📝 Finding the Equation of a Line Using Slope and a Point (43 MCQs)

Determining a Line from One Point and Its Slope

Finding a linear equation from a known point and slope uses point-slope form to establish both the direction and position of a straight line. The slope determines the constant rate of change, while the ordered pair identifies a location through which the line passes. These two quantities uniquely determine a nonvertical line and provide an efficient method of constructing its equation without initially calculating the vertical intercept.

Why Point-Slope Form Avoids Unnecessary Intercept Calculations

When the vertical intercept is unknown, one approach is to determine another point, graph the line, and estimate where it crosses the vertical axis. Such a procedure introduces unnecessary steps and may cause errors involving coordinate scales. Point-slope form uses the given point directly. The resulting equation can subsequently be rearranged into slope-intercept or standard form if another representation is required.

Applying the Point-Slope Relationship Correctly

The general formula is \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\), where \(m\) represents slope and \((x_1,y_1)\) represents the known coordinate. Substitute both coordinates in their correct positions, retaining their signs. Distribute and simplify if necessary. For a vertical line, slope is undefined, so the equation must instead be written \(x=x_1\).

Worked Example Through the Point \((3,7)\) with Slope Two

Find the equation passing through \((3,7)\) with slope \(2\). Substitution gives \(y-7=2(x-3)\). Distributing produces \(y-7=2x-6\), and adding \(7\) gives \(y=2x+1\). Substituting \(x=3\) yields \(y=7\), confirming that the equation contains the original point and has the required slope.

Applications When a Coordinate and Rate Are Known

This method is useful when a quantity is measured at a particular time or location and its constant rate of change is established. For example, a balance observed on a particular date can be modeled alongside a fixed weekly increase. The method assumes a constant rate and requires a valid nonvertical slope; it does not directly describe relationships with changing rates.

Recognizing Coordinate Substitution and Sign Errors

'The formula uses \(y+y_1\)' changes the required subtraction and produces an incorrect equation. 'The supplied point must be the vertical intercept' is false because any point on the line is suitable. 'A negative coordinate becomes positive automatically' ignores signed substitution; if \(x_1=-4\), the expression becomes \(x-(-4)=x+4\).

Constructing Equations from Given Coordinates

Problems may provide a point and slope and require the equation, an unknown coefficient, or the corresponding graph. Substitute the information before distributing, particularly when coordinates are negative or fractional. Compare equivalent equation forms rather than assuming their appearance must match. Desmos can verify the graph, while substitution confirms that the specified point satisfies the equation exactly.

Terminology of Point-Slope Equations

  • Point-slope form - equation using one point and slope.
  • Slope - ratio of vertical to horizontal change.
  • Ordered pair - two coordinates locating a point.
  • Substitution - replacing variables with known values.
  • Distributive property - multiplication across parentheses.
  • Equivalent equations - equations with identical solution sets.

Q1. A line has a slope of \(3\) and passes through \((2,5)\). Which equation represents the line in point-slope form?

A.\(y-5=3(x-2)\) ✅
B.\(y+5=3(x-2)\)
C.\(y-2=3(x-5)\)
D.\(y-5=-3(x-2)\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Point-slope form is \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\). Here, \(m=3\) and \((x_1,y_1)=(2,5)\). Substitution gives \(y-5=3(x-2)\). The coordinates must remain in their original order.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The given point provides \(x_1=-1\) and \(y_1=4\), while the slope is \(-2\). Substituting into \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\) produces \(y-4=-2(x+1)\).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Expanding the original equation gives \(y=2x-2\). The point \((0,-2)\) lies on this line, and its slope remains 2. Substituting these values produces \(y+2=2(x-0)\), an equivalent equation.

Q43. The equation \(y-p=3(x+2)\) represents a line passing through \((4,13)\). What is the value of \(p\)?

A.\(-8\)
B.\(-5\) ✅
C.\(5\)
D.\(8\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(x=4\) and \(y=13\) to obtain \(13-p=3(4+2)=18\). Rearranging gives \(-p=5\), so \(p=-5\). This value makes the given point satisfy the equation.
