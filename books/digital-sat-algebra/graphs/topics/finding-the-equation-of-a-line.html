📝 Finding the Equation of a Line (36 MCQs)

Representing Straight-Line Relationships Algebraically

Finding the equation of a line means determining an algebraic relationship satisfied by every coordinate point on a particular straight line. This task connects coordinate geometry with linear equations and functions. The required information may include a slope, an intercept, one coordinate point, or two distinct points. Depending on the available information, the relationship can be expressed in slope-intercept form, point-slope form, or standard form.

Why Both Direction and Position Are Needed

A single coordinate point cannot determine a unique straight line because infinitely many lines can pass through the same location. A rate of change supplies its direction, while a point or intercept establishes its position. Different information therefore requires different algebraic procedures. Recognizing which quantities are provided prevents unnecessary calculations and ensures that the resulting equation represents precisely the required line.

Selecting the Appropriate Form of a Linear Equation

Slope-intercept form is \(y=mx+b\), where \(m\) is slope and \(b\) is the vertical intercept. Point-slope form is \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\), using a known point and slope. Standard form is \(Ax+By=C\). Vertical lines cannot be expressed using a finite slope; their equations have the form \(x=a\).

Worked Construction from a Known Slope and Point

Determine the equation of a line with slope \(4\) passing through \((2,3)\). Apply point-slope form to obtain \(y-3=4(x-2)\). Distributing gives \(y-3=4x-8\). Adding \(3\) to both sides produces \(y=4x-5\). Substitution confirms that the equation passes through the specified coordinate and has the required slope.

Linear Equations in Scientific and Practical Models

Linear equations describe relationships involving constant rates, including distance traveled at steady speed, hourly earnings, fixed service charges, and regular changes in measured quantities. They also describe straight boundaries in coordinate geometry. Their mathematical assumptions require a constant rate of change. A vertical line is geometrically linear but does not represent a function of \(x\), so it requires its own equation form.

Distinguishing Line Equations from Their Special Cases

'Every line has a finite slope' overlooks vertical lines, represented by \(x=a\). 'Coordinates can be inserted in any order' confuses the independent and dependent variables; the first coordinate replaces \(x\), and the second replaces \(y\). 'Point-slope form requires a vertical intercept' is incorrect because it uses any known point on the line.

Constructing and Verifying Linear Equations

Problems may provide a graph, a pair of coordinates, a rate, or a contextual relationship and require its equation. Select a suitable form, substitute the available information, and simplify without changing the relationship. Verify the result using the original coordinates. Desmos can compare algebraic equations with their graphs, but direct substitution provides a precise method of checking accuracy.

Terminology of Linear Equation Construction

  • Linear equation - equation describing a straight line.
  • Slope - constant rate of vertical change.
  • Y-intercept - value of \(y\) when \(x=0\).
  • Point-slope form - equation using a point and slope.
  • Standard form - equation \(Ax+By=C\).
  • Vertical line - line represented by \(x=a\).

📝 All Finding the Equation of a Line MCQs

Q1. Which equation is written in standard form for a linear equation in two variables?

A.\(Ax+By=C\) ✅
B.\(\ y=mx+b\)
C.\(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\)
D.\(x^2+y=C\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Standard form places both variables on the left and a constant on the right: \(Ax+By=C\). The tempting \(y=mx+b\) is slope-intercept form, another valid form but not the requested standard form.

Q2. Which equation represents a straight line in a coordinate plane?

A.\(x^2+2y=8\)
B.\(3x-2y=11\) ✅
C.\(xy=12\)
D.\(y=\frac{5}{x}\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A linear equation has variables only to the first power and no variable products or denominators. The tempting \(xy=12\) includes a product of variables, so it does not describe a straight line.

Q3. A school sells adult tickets for \(\$8\) and student tickets for \(\$5\). The total revenue is \(\$100\). Which interpretation correctly describes the solution \((5,12)\) to \(8a+5s=100\)?

A.5 adult tickets and 12 student tickets generate \(\$100\). ✅
B.12 adult tickets and 5 student tickets generate \(\$100\).
C.5 adult tickets and 12 student tickets generate \(\$80\).
D.12 adult tickets and 5 student tickets generate \(\$125\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Substituting \(a=5\) and \(s=12\) gives \(8(5)+5(12)=40+60=100\). Thus, the pair represents five adult tickets and twelve student tickets producing \(\$100\). The reversed pair produces a different total.

Q4. Which equation is equivalent to \(6x+9y=18\) after simplifying all coefficients by their greatest common factor?

A.\(2x+3y=18\)
B.\(3x+2y=6\)
C.\(2x+3y=6\) ✅
D.\(6x+3y=6\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Dividing every term of \(6x+9y=18\) by 3 gives \(2x+3y=6\). The tempting \(2x+3y=18\) divides the left side only, changing the solution set rather than preserving the original line.

Q5. A phone company charges a fixed monthly fee of \(\$25\) plus \(\$4\) for each additional message. If \(C\) represents the total cost and \(m\) represents additional messages, which equation is an equivalent standard-form representation of the cost model?

A.\(C+4m=25\)
B.\(C-25=4\)
C.\(C-4m=25\) ✅
D.\(4C-m=25\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The fixed monthly fee is represented by the constant 25, and the per-message cost contributes \(4m\). Rearranging \(C=25+4m\) gives \(C-4m=25\); the tempting plus sign changes the model.

Q6. A student claims that \(x^2+2y=7\) represents a straight line because the variable \(y\) has an exponent of 1. Which correction is mathematically accurate?

A.Both variable terms must have exponent 1; \(x^2\) makes the equation nonlinear. ✅
B.An equation is linear whenever it contains exactly two variables.
C.Only the variable on the left side must have exponent 1.
D.The constant 7 makes the equation nonlinear.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A linear equation requires every variable term to have exponent 1. The term \(x^2\) violates this condition, so the equation is nonlinear. The tempting argument checks only y and overlooks the squared x-term.

Q7. Which equation is equivalent to \(y=2x+5\) when written in standard form?

A.\(2x-y=-5\) ✅
B.\(2x+y=5\)
C.\(2x-y=5\)
D.\(x-2y=-5\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Subtracting \(y\) from both sides of \(y=2x+5\) and then subtracting 5 gives \(2x-y=-5\). The tempting \(2x+y=5\) changes signs inconsistently and is not equivalent.

Q8. A gym models its monthly membership cost using \(C=12+0.5g\), where \(g\) represents additional training sessions. Which equation expresses this relationship in standard form with integer coefficients?

A.\(2C+g=24\)
B.\(2C-g=24\) ✅
C.\(C-2g=12\)
D.\(C+g=24\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Multiplying \(C=12+0.5g\) by 2 gives \(2C=24+g\), hence \(2C-g=24\). The tempting \(2C+g=24\) changes the sign and does not preserve the original cost model.

Q9. Which standard-form equation is equivalent to \(y=-\frac{3}{4}x+2\)?

A.\(3x-4y=8\)
B.\(4x+3y=8\)
C.\(3x+4y=8\) ✅
D.\(3x+4y=2\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Multiplying \(y=-\frac{3}{4}x+2\) by 4 gives \(4y=-3x+8\). Moving the x-term produces \(3x+4y=8\). The tempting minus sign before \(4y\) reverses the equation's relationship.

Q10. Which slope-intercept equation results from solving \(5x-2y=14\) for \(y\)?

A.\(y=-\frac{5}{2}x+7\)
B.\(y=5x-7\)
C.\(y=-\frac{5}{2}x-7\)
D.\(y=\frac{5}{2}x-7\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Subtracting \(5x\) gives \(-2y=14-5x\). Dividing by \(-2\) produces \(y=\frac{5}{2}x-7\). The tempting negative slope results from failing to reverse both signs during division.

Q11. A student rewrites \(3x+y=9\) as \(y=3x-9\). Which correction produces an equivalent equation?

A.\(y=9+3x\)
B.\(y=-3x+9\) ✅
C.\(y=-3x-9\)
D.\(y=3x+9\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Subtracting \(3x\) from both sides gives \(y=9-3x\), or \(y=-3x+9\). The tempting \(3x-9\) changes the signs of both terms incorrectly. Checking by rearranging confirms the correct equivalent expression.

Q12. The equation \(y=\frac{k}{3}x-4\) represents the same line as \(2x-3y=12\). What is the value of \(k\)?

A.\(-2\)
B.\(2\) ✅
C.\(4\)
D.\(6\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Rearranging \(2x-3y=12\) gives \(y=\frac{2}{3}x-4\). Comparing coefficients gives \(\frac{k}{3}=\frac{2}{3}\), so \(k=2\). The tempting \(-2\) reverses the coefficient's sign unnecessarily.

Q13. A line crosses the x-axis at \((6,0)\) and the y-axis at \((0,3)\). Which equation represents the line?

A.\(2x+y=6\)
B.\(x+3y=6\)
C.\(x+2y=6\) ✅
D.\(2x+3y=6\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The intercepts give \(x/6+y/3=1\). Multiplying by 6 produces \(x+2y=6\). The tempting \(2x+y=6\) reverses the intercept coefficients, resulting in different axis crossings.

Q14. A road is represented on a coordinate map by a straight line with x-intercept \((8,0)\) and y-intercept \((0,-4)\). Which equation represents the road?

A.\(x+2y=8\)
B.\(x-2y=8\) ✅
C.\(2x-y=8\)
D.\(x-4y=8\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Using the intercepts gives \(x/8+y/(-4)=1\). Multiplying by 8 produces \(x-2y=8\). The tempting plus sign would place the y-intercept above the origin instead of at negative 4.

Q15. A line crosses the x-axis at \((-6,0)\) and the y-axis at \((0,3)\). Which equation represents the line?

A.\(x-2y=-6\) ✅
B.\(x+2y=-6\)
C.\(2x-y=-6\)
D.\(x-2y=6\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The intercept relationship is \(x/(-6)+y/3=1\). Multiplying by 6 gives \(-x+2y=6\), or \(x-2y=-6\). The tempting \(x-2y=6\) reverses the intercept locations.

Q16. An artist has a budget that allows either 10 hours of painting or 5 hours of framing if the entire budget is spent. Let \(x\) and \(y\) represent the respective hours. Which equation describes the budget boundary?

A.\(2x+y=5\)
B.\(x+5y=10\)
C.\(x+2y=5\)
D.\(x+2y=10\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The intercepts are \((10,0)\) and \((0,5)\), giving \(x/10+y/5=1\). Multiplying by 10 produces \(x+2y=10\). The tempting right-hand side 5 confuses an intercept with the total equation constant.

Q17. A student finds the intercepts of \(4x+2y=12\) and reports the x-intercept as \((6,0)\). Which method gives the correct intercepts?

A.Set \(x=0\) for the x-intercept and \(y=0\) for the y-intercept.
B.Set \(y=0\) to obtain \((3,0)\), and set \(x=0\) to obtain \((0,6)\). ✅
C.Set \(x=0\) to obtain \((3,0)\), and set \(y=0\) to obtain \((0,6)\).
D.Divide 12 by the coefficient of x for both intercepts.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: To find the x-intercept, set \(y=0\), giving \(x=3\); for the y-intercept, set \(x=0\), giving \(y=6\). The tempting point \((6,0)\) reverses which variable must be zero.

Q18. A line has a y-intercept of 10 and passes through \((5,5)\). What is its x-intercept?

A.\((5,0)\)
B.\((8,0)\)
C.\((10,0)\) ✅
D.\((20,0)\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The y-intercept gives point \((0,10)\). Substituting the other point into the intercept relationship gives \(5/a+5/10=1\), so \(a=10\). The tempting 5 is the given x-coordinate, not the intercept.

Q19. Which equation represents a horizontal line crossing the y-axis at \(-3\)?

A.\(x=-3\)
B.\(y=3\)
C.\(x=3\)
D.\(y=-3\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A horizontal line has the same y-coordinate at every point, so its equation is \(y=-3\). The tempting \(x=-3\) describes a vertical line instead, because its x-coordinate remains fixed.

Q20. Which equation represents a vertical line passing through \((4,7)\)?

A.\(y=4\)
B.\(x=7\)
C.\(y=7\)
D.\(x=4\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A vertical line keeps its x-coordinate constant, regardless of the y-coordinate. Since the point has x-coordinate 4, its equation is \(x=4\). The tempting \(y=7\) describes a horizontal line.

Q21. A horizontal line passes through the points \((2,7)\) and \((5,7)\). Which equation represents this line?

A.\(x=7\)
B.\(y=7\) ✅
C.\(y=x+5\)
D.\(x=2\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Both points have the same y-coordinate, 7, while their x-coordinates differ. Therefore, the line is horizontal and has equation \(y=7\). The tempting \(x=7\) would create a vertical line.

Q22. A vertical line passes through \((-3,2)\) and \((-3,-8)\). Which equation represents the line?

A.\(y=-3\)
B.\(x=2\)
C.\(x=-3\) ✅
D.\(y=3\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Both points share the x-coordinate \(-3\), so the line is vertical and its equation is \(x=-3\). The tempting \(y=-3\) fixes the vertical coordinate instead, describing an unrelated horizontal line.

Q23. A student claims that the equation \(x=6\) has slope zero because the x-coordinate never changes. Which statement correctly explains the mistake?

A.The line has an undefined slope because it is vertical; horizontal lines have slope zero. ✅
B.The line has slope 6 because x is constant.
C.The line has slope zero because every linear equation has a numerical slope.
D.The line has slope 1 because x changes by one unit.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For \(x=6\), the horizontal change is zero, making the slope undefined. A horizontal line has slope zero instead. The tempting zero-slope claim confuses constant x with constant y coordinates.

Q24. A theater installs a level lighting track that stays 2.5 meters above the floor at every horizontal position \(d\). If \(h\) represents its height, which equation models the track?

A.\(d=2.5\)
B.\(h=d+2.5\)
C.\(h=2.5d\)
D.\(h=2.5\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The track's height remains 2.5 meters regardless of the horizontal position, so \(h=2.5\). The tempting \(d=2.5\) fixes the horizontal coordinate and represents a vertical line instead.

Q25. Which equation contains the point \((3,2)\)?

A.\(2x+3y=10\)
B.\(3x+2y=12\)
C.\(2x+3y=12\) ✅
D.\(3x-2y=12\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Substituting \(x=3\) and \(y=2\) into \(2x+3y=12\) gives \(6+6=12\). The tempting \(2x+3y=10\) produces 12 on the left, so the point does not satisfy it.

Q26. A meal order has a total cost represented by \(4x+3y=24\), where \(x\) and \(y\) count two meal types costing \(\$4\) and \(\$3\), respectively. Which order satisfies the equation?

A.3 first-type meals and 4 second-type meals ✅
B.4 first-type meals and 3 second-type meals
C.2 first-type meals and 4 second-type meals
D.3 first-type meals and 3 second-type meals
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For three first-type and four second-type meals, the total is \(4(3)+3(4)=12+12=24\). The tempting reversed quantities give 25 dollars, not the required total of 24 dollars.

Q27. A student tests \((-2,3)\) in the equation \(x-2y=-8\) and calculates \(-2-2(3)=-2+6=4\). What is the correct conclusion?

A.The point does not satisfy the equation because the result is 4.
B.The point satisfies the equation because the correct substitution gives \(-2-6=-8\). ✅
C.The point satisfies the equation because \(-2+6=8\).
D.The point does not satisfy the equation because the result is \(-4\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The student's mistake is changing \(-2(3)\) into positive 6. Correct substitution gives \(-2-6=-8\), so the point satisfies the equation. The tempting result 4 comes from the incorrect sign.

Q28. A graph is represented by the following points: \(x=-2:y=9\) ; \(x=-1:y=6\) ; \(x=0:y=3\) ; \(x=1:y=0\) ; \(x=2:y=-3\). Which equation matches the graph?

A.\(3x+y=3\) ✅
B.\(3x-y=3\)
C.\(x+3y=3\)
D.\(3x+y=-3\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The y-values decrease by 3 for every unit increase in x, and the y-intercept is 3. Therefore, \(y=-3x+3\), equivalent to \(3x+y=3\). The tempting negative constant reverses the intercept.

Q29. A warehouse ramp is represented by the points \((0,6)\), \((1,4)\), \((2,2)\), \((3,0)\), and \((4,-2)\). Which standard-form equation represents the ramp?

A.\(2x-y=6\)
B.\(x+2y=6\)
C.\(2x+y=6\) ✅
D.\(2x+y=-6\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The table shows y decreasing by 2 for each unit increase in x, with y-intercept 6. Thus \(y=-2x+6\), or \(2x+y=6\). The tempting minus sign gives the wrong direction of change.

Q30. What does it mean for an ordered pair \((a,b)\) to represent a point on the line \(3x+2y=12\)?

A.The values \(a\) and \(b\) must satisfy \(3a+2b=12\). ✅
B.The values \(a\) and \(b\) must satisfy \(a+b=12\).
C.The value of \(a\) must equal 12 and \(b\) must equal zero.
D.The values \(a\) and \(b\) must have a product of 12.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A point belongs to a line when substituting its coordinates makes the equation true. The tempting condition \(a+b=12\) ignores the coefficients, which determine how each coordinate contributes to the relationship.

Q31. A school play sells adult tickets for \(\$15\) and student tickets for \(\$9\). The organizers want total revenue of \(\$360\). If \(a\) and \(s\) represent ticket quantities, which simplified equation models this target?

A.\(5a+3s=360\)
B.\(15a+9s=360\)
C.\(5a+3s=120\) ✅
D.\(3a+5s=120\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The revenue equation is \(15a+9s=360\). Dividing every term by 3 gives \(5a+3s=120\). The tempting equation with 360 unchanged divides the coefficients but fails to divide the total.

Q32. A bakery buys \(x\) kilograms of flour costing \(\$4\) per kilogram and \(y\) kilograms of flour costing \(\$7\) per kilogram. The total expenditure is \(\$56\). Which equation represents the purchase?

A.\(7x+4y=56\)
B.\(4x+7y=56\) ✅
C.\(4x+7y=11\)
D.\(x+y=56\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The first flour contributes \(4x\) dollars and the second contributes \(7y\) dollars. Adding these costs gives \(4x+7y=56\). The tempting reversed coefficients assign the wrong price to each flour quantity.

Q33. A fundraiser sells two types of donation packages for \(\$12\) and \(\$20\). The organization wants to collect \(\$400\). If \(x\) and \(y\) represent package quantities, which simplified equation represents the target?

A.\(3x+5y=100\) ✅
B.\(12x+20y=400\)
C.\(5x+3y=100\)
D.\(3x+5y=400\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The total is \(12x+20y=400\). Dividing all terms by 4 gives \(3x+5y=100\). The tempting reversed coefficients assign the wrong donation values to the package quantities.

Q34. An auditorium has \(x\) rows containing 12 seats each and \(y\) rows containing 16 seats each. The auditorium has 200 seats altogether. Which simplified equation represents the seating arrangement?

A.\(4x+3y=50\)
B.\(3x+4y=50\) ✅
C.\(12x+16y=50\)
D.\(3x+4y=200\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The total seating equation is \(12x+16y=200\). Dividing every term by 4 gives \(3x+4y=50\). The tempting right-hand side 200 retains the original total while changing the coefficients.

Q35. A cafeteria sells meal boxes for \(\$6\) and \(\$9\). The total revenue from \(x\) first-type boxes and \(y\) second-type boxes is \(\$90\). A student simplifies \(6x+9y=90\) to \(2x+3y=90\). What is the correct simplification?

A.\(2x+3y=90\)
B.\(3x+2y=30\)
C.\(2x+3y=30\) ✅
D.\(6x+9y=30\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Dividing \(6x+9y=90\) by the common factor 3 gives \(2x+3y=30\). The student's mistake is leaving the right side unchanged. The tempting equation therefore does not preserve the original revenue relationship.

Q36. A production plan models the total cost of two package types using \(4x+ky=40\), where \(x\) and \(y\) are package quantities. The combination \((5,2)\) satisfies the equation. What is the value of \(k\)?

A.\(5\)
B.\(8\)
C.\(12\)
D.\(10\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Substituting \((5,2)\) gives \(4(5)+2k=40\), so \(20+2k=40\). Subtracting 20 and dividing by 2 gives \(k=10\). The tempting 20 is the remaining total before dividing by two packages.
