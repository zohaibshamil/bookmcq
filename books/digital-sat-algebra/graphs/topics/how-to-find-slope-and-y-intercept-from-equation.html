What is How to find slope and y-intercept from equation?

Definition:
Finding slope and y-intercept from an equation means rewriting the equation in slope-intercept form y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b, where the coefficient of xxx is the slope mmm and the constant term is the y-intercept bbb, and if the equation is already in this form, the values are read directly, while if it is in another form such as standard form Ax+By=CAx + By = CAx+By=C, the equation is solved for yyy first, and the resulting coefficient and constant give the slope and y-intercept.

Working:
The procedure works by isolating yyy on one side: for Ax+By=CAx + By = CAx+By=C, subtract AxAxAx from both sides to get By=−Ax+CBy = -Ax + CBy=−Ax+C, then divide by BBB to get y=−ABx+CBy = -\frac{A}{B}x + \frac{C}{B}y=−BA​x+BC​, so the slope is m=−ABm = -\frac{A}{B}m=−BA​ and the y-intercept is b=CBb = \frac{C}{B}b=BC​; for an equation already in y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b, read mmm and bbb directly, and the y-intercept point is (0,b)(0, b)(0,b), which can be verified by substituting x=0x = 0x=0 into the original equation.

Example:
For 4x−2y=84x - 2y = 84x−2y=8, subtract 4x4x4x to get −2y=−4x+8-2y = -4x + 8−2y=−4x+8, divide by −2-2−2 to get y=2x−4y = 2x - 4y=2x−4, so the slope is m=2m = 2m=2 and the y-intercept is b=−4b = -4b=−4; for y=−34x+5y = -\frac{3}{4}x + 5y=−43​x+5, the slope is −34-\frac{3}{4}−43​ and the y-intercept is 5, and substituting x=0x = 0x=0 gives y=5y = 5y=5, confirming the intercept.

Reason:
The reason for ease of grasping how to find slope and y-intercept from an equation is that a single algebraic step, solving for yyy, converts any linear equation into a form where the slope and y-intercept are plainly visible, so the same procedure works for every linear equation and always yields the two values needed for graphing and analysis.
