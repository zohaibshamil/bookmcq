📝 Finding the Equation of a Line Perpendicular to a Given Line (39 MCQs)

Combining Right-Angle Geometry with Linear Equations

Finding the equation of a line perpendicular to a given line combines the geometric definition of a right angle with the algebraic relationship between slopes. Perpendicular lines intersect at \(90^\circ\), and their finite nonzero slopes are negative reciprocals. A specified point determines the position of the required line, while its perpendicular slope determines direction. This relationship makes it possible to construct the equation systematically using coordinate information.

Why Perpendicular Directions Require a Negative Reciprocal

Changing only the sign of a slope does not necessarily produce a right angle, and taking its reciprocal without reversing the sign is equally insufficient. Perpendicular directions require both a reversal of orientation and an exchange of vertical and horizontal changes. This relationship gives a precise algebraic alternative to estimating angles from a graph. Horizontal and vertical lines represent the corresponding special case.

Calculating the Perpendicular Slope and Constructing the Equation

For a given finite nonzero slope \(m\), calculate the perpendicular slope using \(m_{\perp}=-\frac{1}{m}\). Substitute this value and the specified coordinate into \(y-y_1=m_{\perp}(x-x_1)\). When the original line is horizontal, its perpendicular is vertical, and the required equation is \(x=x_1\). A vertical original line has a horizontal perpendicular.

Worked Construction Through a Specified Coordinate

Find the perpendicular line to \(y=\frac{3}{2}x+1\) through \((3,4)\). The original slope is \(\frac{3}{2}\), so the required slope is \(-\frac{2}{3}\). Point-slope form gives \(y-4=-\frac{2}{3}(x-3)\). Simplifying produces \(y=-\frac{2}{3}x+6\), and the slope product is \(-1\).

Applications in Coordinate Geometry and Geometric Design

Perpendicular equations are useful in constructing rectangular boundaries, determining right-angle intersections, and solving geometric problems involving lines through specified locations. They also describe perpendicular directions in coordinate-based design and measurement. The negative reciprocal rule applies to finite nonzero slopes, whereas horizontal and vertical lines require separate handling. A perpendicular slope without a location condition does not determine one unique equation.

Correcting Errors in Perpendicular Slope Calculations

'The perpendicular slope is just the reciprocal' omits the necessary sign reversal; \(\frac{3}{2}\) becomes \(-\frac{2}{3}\). 'A vertical line has zero slope' confuses it with a horizontal line; its slope is undefined. 'Any opposite-sign slopes are perpendicular' ignores magnitude; their product must equal \(-1\) when both slopes are finite and nonzero.

Deriving Perpendicular Equations from Given Conditions

Problems may provide a line and a coordinate, then require the equation of a perpendicular line or an unknown coefficient. Identify the original slope before calculating its negative reciprocal. Handle vertical and horizontal cases separately, and verify the supplied coordinate in the final equation. Desmos can illustrate the intersection, while the slope product establishes the exact right-angle condition.

Terminology of Perpendicular Line Equations

  • Perpendicular lines - lines intersecting at right angles.
  • Negative reciprocal - reciprocal with opposite sign.
  • Slope - ratio of vertical to horizontal change.
  • Point-slope form - equation using a point and slope.
  • Horizontal line - line with zero slope.
  • Vertical line - line with undefined slope.

Q1. A line perpendicular to \(y=2x-5\) passes through \((4,1)\). Which equation represents the perpendicular line?

A.\(y=-\frac{1}{2}x+3\) ✅
B.\(y=\frac{1}{2}x+3\)
C.\(y=-2x+3\)
D.\(y=-\frac{1}{2}x-3\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The original slope is 2, so a perpendicular line has slope \(-\frac{1}{2}\). Using \((4,1)\), \(1=-2+b\), giving \(b=3\). Therefore, the required equation is \(y=-\frac{1}{2}x+3\), containing the given point.

Q2. A line is perpendicular to \(3x+6y=12\) and passes through \((1,4)\). Which equation represents the new line?

A.\(y=-2x+2\)
B.\(y=2x+2\) ✅
C.\(y=2x-2\)
D.\(y=\frac{1}{2}x+4\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Rearranging the original equation gives \(y=-\frac{1}{2}x+2\). Its negative reciprocal slope is 2. Substituting \((1,4)\) into \(y=2x+b\) gives \(b=2\), so the required equation is \(y=2x+2\).

Q3. A construction wall follows the vertical line \(x=5\). A horizontal support crosses it at \((2,-3)\). Which equation represents the support?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The original line \(x=5\) is vertical. A line perpendicular to a vertical line must be horizontal, so its y-coordinate remains constant. Passing through \((2,-3)\) gives the equation \(y=-3\).

Q37. A line passes through \((1,4)\) and \((5,6)\). A perpendicular line passes through \((3,-2)\). Which equation represents the perpendicular line?

A.\(y=2x-8\)
B.\(y=-2x+4\)
C.\(y=-2x-2\) ✅
D.\(y=-2x+1\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The original slope is \(\frac{6-4}{5-1}=\frac{1}{2}\), making the perpendicular slope \(-2\). Substituting \((3,-2)\) gives \(-2=-6+b\), so \(b=4\). The equation is \(y=-2x+4\).

Q38. A line has equation \(y=\frac{k}{2}x+3\) and passes through \((4,7)\). A line perpendicular to it passes through \((2,5)\). Which equation represents the perpendicular line?

A.\(y=-2x+9\) ✅
B.\(y=2x+1\)
C.\(y=-\frac{1}{2}x+6\)
D.\(y=-2x+1\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Substituting \((4,7)\) gives \(7=2k+3\), so \(k=2\) and the original slope is 1. Its perpendicular slope is \(-1\). Through \((2,5)\), the intercept is 7, giving \(y=-x+7\).

Q39. A line \(L\) passes through \((0,6)\) and \((3,0)\). A second line is perpendicular to \(L\) and passes through \((4,2)\). What is the y-coordinate of the second line when \(x=1\)?

A.\(8\)
B.\(6\) ✅
C.\(4\)
D.\(2\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Line L has slope \(\frac{0-6}{3-0}=-2\), so the perpendicular slope is \(\frac{1}{2}\). Using \((4,2)\) gives \(b=0\). At \(x=1\), the perpendicular line therefore has y-coordinate \(\frac{1}{2}\).
