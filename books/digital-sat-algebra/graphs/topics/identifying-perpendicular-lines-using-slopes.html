📝 Identifying Perpendicular Lines Using Slopes (34 MCQs)

Perpendicularity as a Relationship Between Linear Directions

Perpendicular lines are straight lines that intersect at an angle of \(90^\circ\). In coordinate geometry, their relationship can be established through the numerical values of their slopes. For two nonvertical, nonhorizontal lines, perpendicularity requires the slopes to be negative reciprocals. This connection converts a geometric condition involving angles into an algebraic relationship that can be tested directly from equations or coordinate data.

Why a Right Angle Requires Reciprocal Slopes with Opposite Signs

Measuring an angle from a graph can produce inaccurate results because coordinate scales and plotting precision may vary. Slope comparison provides an exact alternative. Perpendicular directions require the vertical and horizontal changes to exchange their roles while reversing the sign. This produces negative reciprocal slopes. Horizontal and vertical lines form the corresponding special case because their slopes are zero and undefined, respectively.

The Negative Reciprocal Condition and Its Mathematical Limitations

For two nonvertical lines with finite slopes \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), the perpendicularity condition is \(m_1m_2=-1\). Equivalently, when \(m_1\ne0\), the second slope is \(m_2=-\frac{1}{m_1}\). Interchange the numerator and denominator of a fractional slope and reverse its sign. A horizontal line is perpendicular to a vertical line, requiring separate treatment.

Worked Verification Using Slopes of Two-Thirds and Negative Three-Halves

Consider one line with slope \(\frac{2}{3}\). A perpendicular line must have slope \(-\frac{3}{2}\). Their product is \(\frac{2}{3}\times-\frac{3}{2}=-1\), satisfying the perpendicularity condition. The vertical intercepts can take different values because they determine position rather than direction. Therefore, any two lines with these slopes intersect at right angles.

Applications of Perpendicular Directions in Geometry

Perpendicular lines are fundamental to rectangular construction, coordinate geometry, right-angle layouts, and geometric relationships involving intersecting paths. Their slopes also simplify the construction of a line perpendicular to a given line through a specified point. The negative reciprocal rule applies to finite nonzero slopes; horizontal and vertical lines require separate treatment. A perpendicular slope alone cannot determine the new line's position.

Resolving Errors in Negative Reciprocal Calculations

'Perpendicular slopes are ordinary reciprocals' omits the sign reversal; \(\frac{2}{3}\) must become \(-\frac{3}{2}\). 'Every perpendicular line has a negative slope' ignores horizontal and vertical pairs. 'Opposite signs guarantee perpendicularity' overlooks magnitude; slopes \(2\) and \(-2\) have a product of \(-4\), not \(-1\).

Establishing Perpendicularity in Algebraic Problems

Problems may ask for a missing coefficient, a perpendicular slope, or the equation of a line meeting another at a right angle. Identify the original slope before calculating its negative reciprocal. Check zero and undefined slopes separately. Desmos can provide a visual confirmation of a right-angle intersection, while the product of two finite nonzero slopes supplies the exact algebraic test.

Terminology of Perpendicular Lines

  • Perpendicular lines - intersecting lines forming right angles.
  • Negative reciprocal - reciprocal with reversed sign.
  • Slope - ratio of vertical to horizontal change.
  • Right angle - angle measuring \(90^\circ\).
  • Horizontal line - line with zero slope.
  • Undefined slope - slope of a vertical line.

📝 All Identifying Perpendicular Lines Using Slopes MCQs

Q1. What is the slope of a line perpendicular to a line with slope \(4\)?

A.\(-\frac{1}{4}\) ✅
B.\(\frac{1}{4}\)
C.\(-4\)
D.\(4\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Perpendicular nonvertical lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals. The negative reciprocal of \(4\) is \(-\frac{1}{4}\). The tempting \(\frac{1}{4}\) is only the reciprocal and keeps the sign unchanged, so the product is positive, not negative one.

Q2. A line has slope \(-\frac{3}{5}\). What slope must a perpendicular line have?

A.\(\frac{3}{5}\)
B.\(-\frac{5}{3}\)
C.\(\frac{5}{3}\) ✅
D.\(-\frac{3}{5}\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Taking the reciprocal of \(-\frac{3}{5}\) gives \(-\frac{5}{3}\), then changing its sign gives \(\frac{5}{3}\). The tempting \(-\frac{5}{3}\) omits the sign change, so the product is positive rather than the required negative one.

Q3. A line has equation \(y=\frac{2}{7}x+3\). What is the slope of a perpendicular line?

A.\(\frac{7}{2}\)
B.\(-\frac{2}{7}\)
C.\(-\frac{7}{2}\) ✅
D.\(\frac{2}{7}\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Because the given slope is \(\frac{2}{7}\), its negative reciprocal is \(-\frac{7}{2}\). The tempting \(\frac{7}{2}\) reverses the fraction but keeps the positive sign, so the lines would not meet at a right angle.

Q4. Which value is the slope of a line perpendicular to a line with slope \(-\frac{1}{2}\)?

A.\(-2\)
B.\(-\frac{1}{2}\)
C.\(\frac{1}{2}\)
D.\(2\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The product must equal \(-1\). Since \(-\frac{1}{2}\cdot2=-1\), slope 2 is perpendicular to the given slope. The tempting \(-2\) produces a positive product, so it does not form a right angle.

Q5. A line has slope \(-4\). Which slope would produce a perpendicular line?

A.\(-\frac{1}{4}\)
B.\(\frac{1}{4}\) ✅
C.\(4\)
D.\(-4\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The slopes of perpendicular nonvertical lines multiply to \(-1\). Here, \(-4\cdot\frac{1}{4}=-1\), confirming the relationship. The tempting \(-\frac{1}{4}\) gives a positive product and therefore does not produce perpendicular lines.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The given line has slope 6, so a perpendicular line must have slope \(-\frac{1}{6}\). The tempting \(\frac{1}{6}\) is the reciprocal without the required sign change, producing a positive slope product.

Q8. Which pair of equations represents perpendicular lines?

D.\(y=4x-6\) and \(y=\frac{1}{4}x+2\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The slopes in choice B are 3 and \(-\frac{1}{3}\), whose product is \(-1\). The tempting choice A has reciprocal slopes with the same sign, producing a positive product instead of the perpendicular condition.

Q9. A line is represented by \(y=-2x+5\). Which slope belongs to a line perpendicular to it?

B.\(2\)

D.\(\frac{1}{2}\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The negative reciprocal of \(-2\) is \(\frac{1}{2}\). Their product is \(-1\), confirming perpendicularity. The tempting \(-\frac{1}{2}\) is the ordinary reciprocal with an unchanged negative sign, so it fails the product test.

Q10. Which pair of slopes describes perpendicular nonvertical lines?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The required relationship is a product of \(-1\). In choice A, \(-2\cdot\frac{1}{2}=-1\). The tempting pair in choice C has a positive product, so those slopes do not describe perpendicular lines.

Q13. The equation of a line is \(3x+2y=8\). What is the slope of a line perpendicular to it?

D.\(-\frac{3}{2}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Solving for y gives \(y=-\frac{3}{2}x+4\), so the original slope is \(-\frac{3}{2}\). Its negative reciprocal is \(\frac{2}{3}\). The tempting \(-\frac{2}{3}\) changes magnitude but not the sign and therefore produces a positive slope product.

Q14. A line is represented by \(5x-4y=12\). Which slope belongs to a perpendicular line?

Q15. A student rewrites \(2x+3y=7\) and \(3x-2y=5\) in slope-intercept form and obtains slopes \(-\frac{2}{3}\) and \(\frac{3}{2}\). What conclusion should the student make?

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Rearranging gives slopes \(-\frac{2}{3}\) and \(\frac{3}{2}\), whose product is \(-1\); therefore the lines are perpendicular. The tempting parallel classification is wrong because parallel lines have equal slopes, not negative reciprocals.

Q16. On a coordinate map, a straight segment passes through \((1,2)\) and \((5,4)\). A second segment crosses it at a right angle. What is the slope of the second segment?

A.\(-\frac{1}{2}\)
B.\(2\)
C.\(-2\) ✅
D.\(\frac{1}{2}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The map segment's slope is \(\frac{4-2}{5-1}=\frac{1}{2}\). A perpendicular segment has slope \(-2\). The tempting \(\frac{1}{2}\) repeats the original direction rather than forming a right angle with the given segment on the map.

Q17. Two hiking trails cross at a right angle. The first trail passes through \((-2,5)\) and \((4,1)\). What is the slope of the second trail?

D.\(\frac{3}{2}\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The trail's slope is \(\frac{1-5}{4-(-2)}=-\frac{2}{3}\). Its negative reciprocal is \(\frac{3}{2}\). The tempting \(-\frac{3}{2}\) takes the reciprocal but fails to reverse the sign, so the slopes' product is positive, not negative one.

Q20. A student is given the line \(4x+2y=6\) and claims that a perpendicular line has slope \(-\frac{1}{2}\). Which correction is accurate?

📖 Explanation: Solving \(4x+2y=6\) gives \(y=-2x+3\), so the original slope is \(-2\) and its negative reciprocal is \(\frac{1}{2}\). The tempting \(-\frac{1}{2}\) changes magnitude without changing the required sign.
