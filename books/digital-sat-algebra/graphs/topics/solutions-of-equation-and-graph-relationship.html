What is Solutions of equation and graph relationship?

Definition:
The relationship between solutions of an equation and its graph is that every ordered pair (x, y) that satisfies the equation corresponds to a point on the graph of the equation, and conversely, every point on the graph represents a solution to the equation; thus, the graph is a visual representation of the solution set, and for linear equations, this solution set forms a straight line, providing a geometric interpretation of algebraic solutions.

Working:
When we graph a linear equation, we are plotting all points that satisfy the equation; the x and y coordinates of each point on the line make the equation true; for example, for \( y = 2x \), the point (1, 2) satisfies \( 2 = 2(1) \), and (2, 4) satisfies \( 4 = 2(2) \); thus, the line is the set of all solutions; this connection is used to solve equations graphically, where the intersection of two graphs gives the solution to a system, and it helps interpret real-world situations.

Example:
A simple example is the equation \( y = x + 1 \); its graph is a line passing through (0, 1), (1, 2), (2, 3); each of these points is a solution, and any point on the line, such as (3, 4) or (-1, 0), is also a solution; this shows the direct correspondence between solutions and the graph, illustrating the relationship.

Reason:
Understanding the relationship between equations and graphs is central to algebra, as it bridges algebraic and geometric thinking, enables solving systems, and helps model and analyze real-world problems, making it a foundational concept in mathematics.

📝 All Solutions of equation and graph relationship MCQs

Q1. A student solves \(2x+6=0\) and obtains \(x=-3\). Which statement best connects this algebraic solution to the graph of \(y=2x+6\)?

A.The graph crosses the \(x\)-axis at \((-3,0)\), because the solution makes \(y=0\). ✅
B.The graph crosses the \(y\)-axis at \((-3,0)\), because \(x\) is the solution.
C.The graph has a slope of \(-3\), because the solution is negative.
D.The graph never reaches the \(x\)-axis because the solution is negative.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The equation \(2x+6=0\) gives \(x=-3\). On the graph of \(y=2x+6\), setting \(y=0\) identifies an \(x\)-intercept. Therefore, the algebraic solution corresponds directly to the point \((-3,0)\) where the graph crosses the \(x\)-axis.

Q2. For an equation written as \(f(x)=0\), what does each real solution represent on the graph of \(y=f(x)\)?

A.An \(x\)-coordinate where the graph intersects the \(x\)-axis ✅
B.A \(y\)-coordinate where the graph intersects the \(y\)-axis
C.The slope of the graph at the solution
D.The highest \(y\)-value of the graph
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When \(f(x)=0\), the corresponding point on \(y=f(x)\) has coordinates \((x,0)\). Thus, every real solution is an \(x\)-coordinate of an \(x\)-axis intersection. This interpretation connects solving an equation with identifying graph intersections.

Q3. A graph of \(y=x^2-5x+6\) crosses the \(x\)-axis at \(x=2\) and \(x=3\). Without factoring the expression, which conclusion is justified?

A.The equation \(x^2-5x+6=0\) has exactly two real solutions, \(2\) and \(3\). ✅
B.The equation has only one real solution because the graph is a parabola.
C.The solutions must be \(x=-2\) and \(x=-3\) because the graph opens upward.
D.The equation has no solutions because the graph does not cross the \(y\)-axis at zero.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An \(x\)-axis intersection occurs where \(y=0\). Since the graph crosses the axis at \(x=2\) and \(x=3\), those values satisfy \(x^2-5x+6=0\). Two distinct intersections therefore indicate exactly two real solutions.

Q4. Two students solve \(x^2-4x+4=0\). Ali says there are two solutions because the equation has degree two. Sara says there is one solution because the graph touches the \(x\)-axis once. Who is correct?

A.Ali, because every quadratic equation must have two distinct real solutions.
B.Sara, because one \(x\)-axis contact corresponds to one real solution. ✅
C.Both, because a touching point represents two different \(x\)-values.
D.Neither, because solutions cannot be identified graphically.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A quadratic can have one repeated real solution. Here the graph touches the \(x\)-axis at a single \(x\)-value rather than crossing it. That single \(x\)-coordinate represents one distinct real solution, even though its multiplicity may be two.

Q5. A company models profit with \(P(x)=x^2-8x+15\), where \(x\) represents a production level. At which production levels is the company breaking even?

A.At \(x=3\) and \(x=5\) ✅
B.At \(x=0\) and \(x=15\)
C.At \(x=4\) only
D.At \(x=8\) only
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Breaking even means profit equals zero, so solve \(P(x)=0\). The expression factors as \((x-3)(x-5)=0\), giving \(x=3\) and \(x=5\). Graphically, these are exactly the two points where the profit graph meets the \(x\)-axis.

Q6. The equation \(3x-9=0\) is represented by \(y=3x-9\). A student claims that \(x=3\) is the \(y\)-intercept because it is the solution. What is the best correction?

A.The solution \(x=3\) gives the \(x\)-intercept \((3,0)\), while the \(y\)-intercept occurs when \(x=0\). ✅
B.The solution \(x=3\) gives the \(y\)-intercept \((0,3)\).
C.The solution must always represent the slope.
D.There is no relationship between a solution and an intercept.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A solution to \(3x-9=0\) makes the function value zero, so it identifies an \(x\)-intercept. Substituting \(x=3\) gives \(y=0\), producing \((3,0)\). The \(y\)-intercept instead requires \(x=0\), giving \((0,-9)\).

Q7. A temperature model is \(T(t)=2t-10\), where \(t\) is time in hours. The graph crosses the horizontal axis at \(t=5\). What does this intersection mean in the model?

A.The temperature is \(0\) at \(t=5\) hours. ✅
B.The temperature reaches \(5^\circ\) at \(t=0\).
C.The temperature increases by \(5^\circ\) every hour.
D.The initial temperature is \(5^\circ\).
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The horizontal axis represents \(T=0\). Therefore, the intersection at \(t=5\) means \(T(5)=0\). In the context of the model, the temperature reaches \(0^\circ\) five hours after the starting time.

Q8. A graph of \(y=f(x)\) stays entirely above the \(x\)-axis. A student concludes that \(f(x)=0\) has a solution because every function must equal zero somewhere. How should the reasoning be evaluated?

A.The reasoning is correct because every graph eventually crosses the \(x\)-axis.
B.The reasoning is incorrect because the graph has no point with \(y=0\). ✅
C.The reasoning is correct if the graph has a positive slope.
D.The reasoning is incorrect only when the graph is nonlinear.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Solutions of \(f(x)=0\) correspond to points where the graph has \(y=0\). If the entire graph remains above the \(x\)-axis, no such point exists. Therefore, the equation has no real solution represented by the graph.

Q9. A student estimates that a graph crosses the \(x\)-axis near \(x=1.8\) and \(x=4.2\). Another student solves the corresponding equation algebraically and obtains \(x=1.79\) and \(x=4.21\). Which conclusion is most reasonable?

A.The methods disagree because graphical estimates can never represent solutions.
B.The graphical and algebraic methods are consistent because the graph gives approximate values. ✅
C.The algebraic answers must be wrong because they are not whole numbers.
D.Only the graph can determine whether the solutions are real.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Graphs often provide approximate coordinates because values are read from a visual scale, while algebraic methods can provide greater precision. Values near \(1.8\) and \(4.2\) agree closely with \(1.79\) and \(4.21\), so the methods support the same solutions.

Q10. A graph intersects the \(x\)-axis at \(x=-4\), touches it at \(x=1\), and intersects it again at \(x=6\). How many distinct real solutions does the equation represented by \(y=f(x)\) have?

A.One
B.Two
C.Three ✅
D.Six
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Every distinct \(x\)-coordinate where the graph has \(y=0\) represents one distinct real solution. The graph has three such \(x\)-values: \(-4\), \(1\), and \(6\). Whether the graph crosses or merely touches the axis does not change the number of distinct solutions.

Q11. A student says, \"If a graph crosses the \(y\)-axis at \(4\) then the equation has the solution \(x=4\)\". Which response best identifies the error?

A.The student confused the \(y\)-intercept with an \(x\)-intercept; a solution of \(f(x)=0\) is identified by an \(x\)-axis intersection. ✅
B.The student should have used the slope instead of the \(y\)-intercept.
C.The student is correct because every intercept is a solution.
D.The student should replace \(x=4\) with \(y=4\) as the only possible solution.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A \(y\)-axis intersection occurs when \(x=0\), so a point such as \((0,4)\) does not mean \(x=4\) is a solution. For \(f(x)=0\), the relevant graph feature is an \(x\)-axis intersection, where the output equals zero.

Q12. Suppose \(g(x)=0\) has no real solutions. What must be true about the graph of \(y=g(x)\), assuming the graph represents all real \(x\)-values?

A.It never intersects the \(x\)-axis. ✅
B.It must cross the \(y\)-axis exactly twice.
C.It must have zero slope everywhere.
D.It must contain the point \((0,0)\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A real solution of \(g(x)=0\) would create a point \((x,0)\) on the graph. If there are no real solutions, the graph cannot contain any point on the \(x\)-axis. It may still intersect the \(y\)-axis elsewhere.

Q13. Consider \(h(x)=(x-2)^2-9\). A graphing approach shows two \(x\)-axis intersections. Which pair of values should be expected, and why?

A.\(x=-1\) and \(x=5\), because setting \(h(x)=0\) gives \((x-2)^2=9\). ✅
B.\(x=2\) and \(x=9\), because these numbers appear in the expression.
C.\(x=-2\) and \(x=2\), because the squared term is centered at \(2\).
D.\(x=3\) and \(x=11\), because the constant is added to \(2\).
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: To find the \(x\)-axis intersections, set \(h(x)=0\): \((x-2)^2-9=0\), so \((x-2)^2=9\). Thus \(x-2=\pm3\), producing \(x=-1\) and \(x=5\). These are the two real solutions represented by the graph.
