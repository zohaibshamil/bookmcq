📝 Checking Solutions to Linear Inequalities in Two Variables (36 MCQs)

Ordered Pairs and Membership in an Inequality's Solution Set

Checking solutions to a linear inequality in two variables requires determining whether a specified ordered pair makes the algebraic comparison true. Unlike a linear equation, which generally describes a straight line, an inequality accepts coordinates throughout a region. Substitution provides an exact test of membership in this solution set. The method connects algebraic evaluation with coordinate geometry and applies equally to numerical and contextual constraints.

Why Direct Substitution Is Preferable to Visual Estimation

A coordinate may appear to lie within a shaded region, but graph scales, line thickness, and plotting precision can make visual judgments unreliable. This difficulty increases when the coordinates contain fractions or the boundary has a steep gradient. Substitution eliminates the need to estimate positions by evaluating the original inequality directly. The resulting true or false statement provides an unambiguous mathematical conclusion.

Substituting Coordinates and Evaluating the Comparison

For an ordered pair \((a,b)\), replace \(x\) with \(a\) and \(y\) with \(b\) in the original inequality. Simplify both sides and evaluate the comparison without changing its direction. A true statement means the point is a solution; a false statement means it is not. Strict inequalities exclude equality, whereas inclusive inequalities accept boundary points.

Worked Verification of the Point \((2,5)\)

Determine whether \((2,5)\) satisfies \(y\le2x+1\). Substitute \(x=2\) and \(y=5\), obtaining \(5\le2(2)+1\), or \(5\le5\). The statement is true, so the point is a solution. For \(y<2x+1\), however, equality is excluded, and the same coordinate does not satisfy the inequality.

Testing Mathematical Constraints in Practical Situations

Substitution determines whether a proposed combination of quantities satisfies restrictions on expenditure, production, time, or available resources. For example, two purchase quantities can be tested against a total-cost inequality to determine whether they remain within a spending limit. The conclusion addresses only the stated mathematical condition; additional requirements, such as nonnegative values or whole-number quantities, must also be checked when specified.

Distinguishing Coordinates, Equality, and Inequality Signs

'The first coordinate replaces \(y\)' reverses the ordered-pair convention; \((a,b)\) means \(x=a\) and \(y=b\). 'Equality always makes a point invalid' ignores inclusive comparisons, namely \(\le\) and \(\ge\). 'A nearby point must satisfy the inequality' relies on visual proximity rather than evaluation; only substitution into the original comparison establishes whether a coordinate is a solution.

Evaluating Candidate Solutions with Algebraic Precision

Problems may ask which coordinate satisfies an inequality, whether a proposed combination meets a restriction, or whether a point belongs to a shaded region. Substitute coordinates in the correct order, simplify arithmetic, and preserve the original inequality sign. A calculator can assist with numerical evaluation, while Desmos provides a graphical check. Direct substitution remains the decisive verification method.

Terminology of Inequality Solution Testing

  • Ordered pair - first coordinate \(x\), second coordinate \(y\).
  • Solution - values satisfying an inequality.
  • Substitution - replacing variables with numerical values.
  • Strict inequality - comparison excluding equality.
  • Inclusive inequality - comparison including equality.
  • Boundary point - coordinate lying on the boundary line.

📝 All Checking Solutions to Linear Inequalities in Two Variables MCQs

Q1. Which ordered pair satisfies the inequality \(y>2x-1\)?

A.\((0,0)\) ✅
B.\((1,1)\)
C.\((2,2)\)
D.\((0,-2)\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Substitute \((0,0)\) into the inequality: \(0>2(0)-1\), giving \(0>-1\), which is true. The remaining pairs fail the comparison. A point is a solution only when its coordinates make the original inequality true.

Q2. Which ordered pair does NOT satisfy \(y\leq -x+4\)?

A.\((0,3)\)
B.\((2,1)\)
C.\((3,2)\) ✅
D.\((-1,4)\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Test each pair in \(y\leq-x+4\). For \((3,2)\), substitution gives \(2\leq-3+4\), or \(2\leq1\), which is false. The other three pairs satisfy the comparison and therefore belong to the solution set.

Q3. Which ordered pair satisfies \(y\geq2x-1\) and lies exactly on its boundary?

A.\((1,1)\) ✅
B.\((2,2)\)
C.\((0,0)\)
D.\((3,4)\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For \((1,1)\), substitution gives \(1=2(1)-1\), so equality holds. The symbol \(\geq\) includes boundary points. The other choices either lie strictly above the boundary or fail the inequality, so they do not meet both requirements.

Q4. Does the ordered pair \((1,3)\) satisfy the strict inequality \(y<2x+1\)?

A.Yes, because both sides equal 3.
B.Yes, because 3 is greater than 2.
C.No, because equality is excluded by the strict inequality. ✅
D.No, because the y-coordinate must always be negative.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Substituting \((1,3)\) gives \(3<2(1)+1\), or \(3<3\). This statement is false because both sides are equal. A strict inequality requires the left side to be strictly smaller, not equal.

Q5. Does \((2,4)\) satisfy \(x+y<7\)?

A.Yes, because \(2+4=6\) and \(6<7\). ✅
B.No, because \(2+4=7\).
C.Yes, because \(2+4=8\).
D.No, because both coordinates must be negative.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(x=2\) and \(y=4\). Their sum is \(2+4=6\), and \(6<7\) is true. Therefore, the ordered pair satisfies the inequality. Both coordinates do not need to be negative for a point to qualify.

Q6. Does the point \((2,1)\) satisfy \(3x-2y\geq4\)?

A.No, because the expression equals 2.
B.No, because the expression equals 4.
C.Yes, because the expression equals 6.
D.Yes, because the expression equals 4 and equality is included. ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Substituting \(x=2\) and \(y=1\) gives \(3(2)-2(1)=6-2=4\). Since \(4\geq4\), the point satisfies the inequality. Equality is permitted by the symbol \(\geq\), so the boundary value is a valid solution.

Q7. A school has a budget of \(\$40\) for notebooks costing \(\$5\) each and folders costing \(\$8\) each. Does purchasing 4 notebooks and 2 folders satisfy \(5x+8y\leq40\)?

A.No, because the total is \(\$44\).
B.Yes, because the total is \(\$36\). ✅
C.Yes, because the total is exactly \(\$40\).
D.No, because the total is \(\$32\).
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(x=4\) and \(y=2\): \(5(4)+8(2)=20+16=36\). Since \(36\leq40\), the purchase satisfies the budget restriction. The remaining budget is 4 dollars, so the total is within the allowed limit.

Q8. Does \((3,2)\) satisfy \(2x+3y>12\)?

A.Yes, because the expression equals 12.
B.Yes, because 12 is greater than 10.
C.No, because the expression equals 12 and the inequality is strict. ✅
D.No, because the expression equals 15.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Substitution gives \(2(3)+3(2)=6+6=12\). The required condition is \(12>12\), which is false. Because the inequality is strict, equality does not count as a solution, even when both sides have identical values.

Q9. Does the ordered pair \((-1,-2)\) satisfy \(4x-y\leq5\)?

A.Yes, because \(-4+2=-2\) and \(-2\leq5\). ✅
B.No, because the expression equals 6.
C.Yes, because the expression equals 5.
D.No, because both coordinates are negative.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(x=-1\) and \(y=-2\): \(4(-1)-(-2)=-4+2=-2\). Since \(-2\leq5\), the ordered pair is a solution. Subtracting a negative y-coordinate correctly changes the second term to positive 2.

Q10. Does \((4,-1)\) satisfy \(x-2y\geq6\)?

A.No, because the expression equals 2.
B.No, because the expression equals 4.
C.Yes, because the expression equals 8.
D.Yes, because the expression equals 6 and equality is allowed. ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(x=4\) and \(y=-1\): \(4-2(-1)=4+2=6\). Since \(6\geq6\), the point satisfies the inequality. The negative y-coordinate changes subtraction into addition, and the inclusive symbol allows equality.

Q11. Which ordered pair satisfies \(y\geq-x+2\)?

A.\((0,1)\)
B.\((2,0)\) ✅
C.\((1,0)\)
D.\((3,-2)\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For \((2,0)\), substitution gives \(0\geq-2+2\), or \(0\geq0\), which is true. The other pairs produce false comparisons. The ordered pair is therefore a valid boundary solution because the inequality includes equality.

Q12. A school awards \(x\) points for mathematics and \(y\) points for science. A student must earn at least 18 points under the condition \(x+2y\geq18\). Does the score pair \((6,6)\) qualify?

A.No, because the weighted score is 12.
B.Yes, because the weighted score is 18. ✅
C.Yes, because the weighted score is 24.
D.No, because the weighted score is 16.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(x=6\) and \(y=6\): \(6+2(6)=18\). The requirement is at least 18, so a total of exactly 18 qualifies. The coefficient 2 means each science point contributes twice its numerical value to the weighted total.

Q13. Does the ordered pair \((3,2)\) satisfy \(4x+3y<18\)?

A.Yes, because the expression equals 15.
B.Yes, because the expression equals 18.
C.No, because the expression equals 18 and equality is excluded. ✅
D.No, because the expression equals 21.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Substitution gives \(4(3)+3(2)=12+6=18\). The inequality requires a value strictly less than 18, so equality does not satisfy it. The point lies on the boundary rather than inside the permitted solution region.

Q14. Does \((-1,1)\) satisfy \(-2x+5y\geq7\)?

A.No, because the expression equals 3.
B.Yes, because the expression equals 7. ✅
C.Yes, because the expression equals 5.
D.No, because the expression equals 5.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Substituting \(x=-1\) and \(y=1\) gives \(-2(-1)+5(1)=2+5=7\). Since \(7\geq7\), the ordered pair satisfies the inequality. The negative x-coordinate produces a positive contribution, and the inclusive symbol accepts equality.

Q15. A student checks whether \((1,-2)\) satisfies \(3x-2y>5\). The student calculates \(3-4=-1\) and concludes that the point is not a solution. What is the correct conclusion?

A.The point is not a solution because the expression equals 1.
B.The point is not a solution because the expression equals 5.
C.The point is a solution because the expression equals 5.
D.The point is a solution because the expression equals 7. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The error is evaluating \(-2(-2)\) as negative 4. The correct substitution is \(3(1)-2(-2)=3+4=7\), and \(7>5\) is true. Thus, the point satisfies the inequality because multiplying two negative values gives a positive result.

Q16. A student claims that \((2,1)\) satisfies \(3x-y<5\) because substituting the coordinates gives \(5\). What is the correct conclusion?

A.The point is a solution because equality is allowed.
B.The point is not a solution because the inequality is strict. ✅
C.The point is a solution because 5 is positive.
D.The point is not a solution because both coordinates are positive.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Substitute to obtain \(3(2)-1=5\). The original condition requires \(5<5\), which is false. A strict inequality excludes equality, so a point producing the boundary value is not a solution.

Q17. A school play has a budget of \(\$80\). Adult tickets cost \(\$12\), and student tickets cost \(\$8\). Does selling 4 adult tickets and 4 student tickets satisfy the budget inequality \(12a+8s\leq80\)?

A.No, because revenue is \(\$72\).
B.Yes, because revenue is \(\$80\). ✅
C.No, because revenue is \(\$88\).
D.Yes, because revenue is \(\$96\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(a=4\) and \(s=4\): \(12(4)+8(4)=48+32=80\). Since \(80\leq80\), the ticket combination satisfies the budget limit exactly. The inclusive inequality allows the entire budget to be used without exceeding it.

Q18. A bakery must use at least 20 kilograms of ingredients. Flour contributes \(2x\) kilograms and sugar contributes \(5y\) kilograms. Does the combination \((5,2)\) satisfy \(2x+5y\geq20\)?

A.No, because the total is 10 kilograms.
B.Yes, because the total is 15 kilograms.
C.Yes, because the total is exactly 20 kilograms. ✅
D.No, because the total is 25 kilograms.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(x=5\) and \(y=2\): \(2(5)+5(2)=10+10=20\). The requirement is at least 20 kilograms, so exactly 20 is acceptable. Both ingredient contributions must be included using their specified coefficients.

Q19. Does the point \((2,4)\) satisfy \(y<\frac{3}{2}x+2\)?

A.Yes, because \(4<5\). ✅
B.No, because \(4>3\).
C.No, because both sides must be equal.
D.Yes, because \(4=5\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Substituting \(x=2\) gives \(\frac{3}{2}(2)+2=5\). Comparing the coordinates gives \(4<5\), which is true. Therefore, the point belongs to the solution set because its y-coordinate is strictly below the calculated boundary value.

Q20. Does \((-2,1)\) satisfy \(-3x+2y\leq8\)?

A.No, because the expression equals 4.
B.No, because the expression equals 10.
C.Yes, because the expression equals 6.
D.Yes, because the expression equals 8 and equality is included. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Substitution gives \(-3(-2)+2(1)=6+2=8\). Because \(8\leq8\) is true, the point satisfies the inequality. The negative x-coordinate contributes positive 6, and the symbol \(\leq\) includes points on the boundary.

Q21. For which smallest integer value of \(y\) does \((3,y)\) satisfy \(2x+y>10\)?

A.\(3\)
B.\(5\) ✅
C.\(4\)
D.\(6\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Substituting \(x=3\) gives \(6+y>10\), so \(y>4\). Since the question asks for the smallest integer satisfying this condition, \(y=5\). A value of 4 is excluded because the original inequality is strict.

Q22. What is the greatest integer value of \(x\) for which \((x,2)\) satisfies \(3x+y\leq11\)?

A.\(2\)
B.\(4\)
C.\(3\) ✅
D.\(5\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Substituting \(y=2\) gives \(3x+2\leq11\). Subtracting 2 and dividing by 3 produces \(x\leq3\). Therefore, the greatest permitted integer is 3, which makes the original expression equal to 11.

Q23. A student tests \((-1,4)\) in \(5x+2y\geq14\) and calculates \(-5+8=13\), concluding that the point satisfies the inequality. What is the correct conclusion?

A.The point satisfies the inequality because the expression equals 14.
B.The point does not satisfy the inequality because the expression equals 3.
C.The point satisfies the inequality because the expression equals 13.
D.The point does not satisfy the inequality because the expression equals 13, which is less than 14. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The substitution is correct: \(5(-1)+2(4)=-5+8\). However, the sum equals 3, not 13. Since \(3\geq14\) is false, the point is not a solution. The arithmetic error changes the comparison and leads to an incorrect conclusion.

Q24. A phone plan costs \(\$12\) monthly plus \(\$0.15\) per message. A customer has a \(\$30\) limit and sends 100 messages. Does the point \((100,27)\) satisfy \(0.15m+12\leq30\)?

A.Yes, because the calculated cost is \(\$27\). ✅
B.No, because the calculated cost is \(\$30\).
C.No, because the calculated cost is \(\$42\).
D.Yes, because the calculated cost is \(\$15\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(m=100\): \(0.15(100)+12=15+12=27\). Since \(27\leq30\), the monthly cost satisfies the spending limit. The fixed fee must be added to the message charge to calculate the actual total.

Q25. Which ordered pair satisfies \(\frac{3}{2}x-\frac{2}{3}y\geq\frac{5}{3}\)?

A.\((0,0)\)
B.\((2,1)\) ✅
C.\((1,2)\)
D.\((2,4)\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For \((2,1)\), substitution gives \(\frac{3}{2}(2)-\frac{2}{3}(1)=3-\frac{2}{3}=\frac{7}{3}\). Since \(\frac{7}{3}\geq\frac{5}{3}\), the pair satisfies the inequality. Keeping fractional coefficients intact prevents errors when comparing the two sides.

Q26. A delivery company has a budget of \(\$170\). Package type X costs \(\$18\), and package type Y costs \(\$25\). Which ordered pair satisfies \(18x+25y\leq170\)?

A.\((4,4)\)
B.\((6,3)\)
C.\((5,3)\) ✅
D.\((5,4)\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: For \((5,3)\), the total cost is \(18(5)+25(3)=90+75=165\). Since \(165\leq170\), this combination satisfies the budget. The other combinations exceed the limit, so they cannot represent permitted purchases.

Q27. For the inequality \(4x-3y\leq19\), what is the greatest integer value of \(t\) that makes the point \((t,-1)\) a solution?

A.\(3\)
B.\(5\)
C.\(6\)
D.\(4\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(x=t\) and \(y=-1\): \(4t-3(-1)\leq19\), so \(4t+3\leq19\). Subtracting 3 and dividing by 4 gives \(t\leq4\). Therefore, the greatest integer solution is 4.

Q28. Which ordered pair satisfies \(5x-2y>x+10\)?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Substitute \((3,-1)\): \(5(3)-2(-1)=17\), while \(3+10=13\). Since \(17>13\), this point satisfies the inequality. Comparing both sides after substitution is essential because the variables appear on both sides.

Q29. Does the ordered pair \((3,2)\) satisfy \(2x+3y<12\)?

📖 Explanation: Substitution produces \(2(3)+3(2)=6+6=12\). The required comparison becomes \(12<12\), which is false. Since the inequality is strict, a point whose coordinates produce exactly 12 lies on the excluded boundary.

Q31. A parking facility charges \(\$3\) per hour and a fixed \(\$5\) entry fee. The total charge cannot exceed \(\$40\). Does the point \((5,5)\) satisfy \(3h+5\leq40\), where \(h\) is the number of hours?

📖 Explanation: Substitute \(h=5\): \(3(5)+5=15+5=20\). Since \(20\leq40\), the parking charge is within the permitted limit. The fixed entry fee is counted once, rather than being multiplied by the number of hours.
