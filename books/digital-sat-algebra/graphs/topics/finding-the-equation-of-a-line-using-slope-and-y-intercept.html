📝 Finding the Equation of a Line Using Slope and Y Intercept (36 MCQs)

Determining a Linear Function from Its Slope and Intercept

Finding the equation of a line from its slope and vertical intercept is a direct application of slope-intercept form. The slope identifies the constant change in the dependent variable for each unit increase in the independent variable. The intercept establishes the value when the independent variable is zero. Together, these quantities determine a unique nonvertical straight line and connect algebraic coefficients with its geometric representation.

Why the Initial Value and Rate of Change Are Sufficient

A straight line contains infinitely many coordinate points, making it unnecessary to calculate and record each one separately. The slope establishes how the dependent variable changes, while the intercept fixes its initial position. Neither quantity alone determines a unique line: many lines share a given slope or intercept. Combining them provides enough information to construct the complete linear relationship directly.

Substituting Known Values into Slope-Intercept Form

Use the general form y=mx+by=mx+by=mx+b. Substitute the given slope for mmm and the vertical intercept for bbb, preserving their signs and numerical values. Simplify the expression when necessary. This procedure applies to nonvertical lines because vertical lines have undefined slopes and cannot be represented by a finite coefficient multiplying xxx.

Worked Example with a Negative Slope and Positive Intercept

A line has slope −3-3−3 and vertical intercept 555. Substituting into y=mx+by=mx+by=mx+b produces y=−3x+5y=-3x+5y=−3x+5. The line crosses the vertical axis at (0,5)(0,5)(0,5) and decreases by three units whenever xxx increases by one unit. At x=2x=2x=2, its corresponding value is y=−1y=-1y=−1.

Modeling Initial Quantities and Constant Rates

This form is particularly useful when a process begins with an established quantity and subsequently changes at a constant rate. Examples include an initial account balance with regular deposits, a starting rental charge plus a fixed hourly cost, and a constant starting distance followed by steady movement. The model becomes unsuitable when the rate varies significantly or the relationship is nonlinear.

Separating the Meaning of the Two Coefficients

'The coefficient of xxx is the intercept' confuses the slope mmm with the constant bbb. 'A negative slope requires a negative intercept' incorrectly links direction with initial position. 'A positive intercept guarantees an increasing graph' ignores the slope; a line can begin above the origin and still decrease when its slope is negative.

Interpreting Initial Values and Rates in Linear Problems

Problems frequently describe a starting quantity and a rate per unit, asking for the corresponding equation or a predicted value. Identify which quantity represents the initial condition and which represents the rate. Maintain consistent units and signs. A graphing calculator can confirm the resulting relationship, while evaluating the equation at x=0x=0x=0 verifies the intercept directly.

Terminology of Slope and Intercept

  • Slope - change in yyy per unit of xxx.
  • Y-intercept - value of yyy at x=0x=0x=0.
  • Slope-intercept form - equation y=mx+by=mx+by=mx+b.
  • Initial value - starting quantity in a model.
  • Rate of change - quantity changed per unit.
  • Coefficient - numerical factor multiplying a variable.
