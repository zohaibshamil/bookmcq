📝 Finding the Equation of a Line Given Two Points (36 MCQs)

Two Coordinate Points as a Complete Specification of a Line

Finding a linear equation from two points uses their coordinate differences to determine the direction and position of a straight line. Two distinct points uniquely determine one line, making this a fundamental application of slope and point-slope form. The method connects ordered pairs with algebraic representations and allows the construction of a complete equation from geometric information without requiring a pre-existing graph.

Why Two Distinct Points Determine a Unique Straight Line

One point alone cannot specify a unique line because infinitely many straight lines can pass through the same coordinate. A second distinct point determines the direction joining their positions. The vertical and horizontal differences provide the slope, which can then be combined with either point to construct the equation. Identical points provide no unique direction and are therefore insufficient information.

Calculating Slope and Selecting an Equation Form

For distinct points (x1,y1)(x_1,y_1)(x1​,y1​) and (x2,y2)(x_2,y_2)(x2​,y2​), calculate m=y2−y1x2−x1m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}m=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​ when x2≠x1x_2\ne x_1x2​=x1​. Substitute this slope and either point into y−y1=m(x−x1)y-y_1=m(x-x_1)y−y1​=m(x−x1​). If x2=x1x_2=x_1x2​=x1​ and the points are distinct, the resulting line is vertical and has equation x=x1x=x_1x=x1​.

Worked Example Through (1,4)(1,4)(1,4) and (3,10)(3,10)(3,10)

Calculate the slope using m=10−43−1=62=3m=\frac{10-4}{3-1}=\frac{6}{2}=3m=3−110−4​=26​=3. Substitute the point (1,4)(1,4)(1,4) into point-slope form to obtain y−4=3(x−1)y-4=3(x-1)y−4=3(x−1). Simplification gives y=3x+1y=3x+1y=3x+1. Substituting x=3x=3x=3 produces y=10y=10y=10, confirming that both original coordinates satisfy the resulting equation.

Applications and Limitations of Two-Point Models

Two observations can establish a linear relationship when a constant rate is mathematically reasonable, such as movement at steady speed or a fixed production rate. The method is also central to finding gradients between plotted positions. Although two distinct points always determine a geometric line, they do not prove that an underlying real-world process maintains a constant rate between or beyond the observations.

Preventing Coordinate Order and Slope Calculation Errors

'The horizontal difference belongs in the numerator' reverses the slope formula; vertical differences must be divided by horizontal differences. 'The subtraction order can change independently' ignores sign consistency; both coordinate differences must follow the same point order. 'A zero denominator produces zero slope' is incorrect because equal horizontal coordinates with different vertical coordinates describe a vertical line with undefined slope.

Deriving and Verifying Equations from Coordinate Data

Problems may require an equation, slope, intercept, or unknown coordinate using two supplied points. Label the coordinates consistently, calculate the slope, and substitute either point into point-slope form. Verify the final equation using both original coordinates. Desmos can display the resulting line, but checking the slope calculation and substitutions provides an exact verification.

Terminology of Equations Through Two Points

  • Slope formula - ratio of coordinate differences.
  • Ordered pair - coordinates of a point.
  • Vertical change - difference between yyy-coordinates.
  • Horizontal change - difference between xxx-coordinates.
  • Point-slope form - equation using a point and slope.
  • Undefined slope - slope of a vertical line.
