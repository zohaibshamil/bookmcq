What is Triangles, Rectangles, and the Pythagorean Theorem?

Definition:
Triangles, rectangles, and the Pythagorean theorem are fundamental geometric concepts where triangles have three sides and angles summing to \( 180^\circ \), rectangles have four sides with opposite sides equal and angles of \( 90^\circ \), and the Pythagorean theorem applies to right triangles, stating that the square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides, expressed as \( a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \), where \( c \) is the hypotenuse.

Working:
For triangles, use the angle sum property \( \angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ \) to find missing angles, and for rectangles, use the perimeter \( P = 2(l + w) \) and area \( A = l \times w \), while the Pythagorean theorem is used to find missing side lengths in right triangles by isolating the variable: \( c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \) for the hypotenuse, or \( a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2} \) for a leg.

Example:
A right triangle has legs of 3 cm and 4 cm, so the hypotenuse is \( c = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5 \) cm, and if a rectangle has length 8 m and width 5 m, its perimeter is \( 2(8 + 5) = 26 \) m and area is \( 8 \times 5 = 40 \) m\(^2\).

Reason:
These concepts are foundational in geometry and are used in construction, architecture, engineering, navigation, and many fields involving spatial reasoning and measurements, making them essential for students and professionals alike.

📝 All Triangles, Rectangles, and the Pythagorean Theorem MCQs

Q1. A triangular garden has a base of \(18\) m and an area of \(126\) m². The owner wants to determine the perpendicular height before ordering fencing for the other sides. What is the height of the garden?

A.12 m
B.14 m ✅
C.16 m
D.18 m
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The area of a triangle is half the product of its base and perpendicular height. Thus \(126=\frac{1}{2}(18)h=9h\), giving \(h=14\) m. The key modeling step is recognizing that the given area and base determine the missing height.

Q2. A rectangular poster has length \(4\) cm greater than its width. Its area is \(96\) cm². Which dimensions correctly describe the poster?

A.8 cm by 12 cm ✅
B.6 cm by 16 cm
C.10 cm by 14 cm
D.4 cm by 24 cm
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Let the width be \(w\), so the length is \(w+4\). The area condition gives \(w(w+4)=96\). Factoring gives \((w+12)(w-8)=0\), so the positive width is \(8\) cm and the length is \(12\) cm.

Q3. Two rectangles have the same perimeter of \(40\) m. Rectangle X is \(6\) m by \(14\) m, while Rectangle Y is \(10\) m by \(10\) m. Which conclusion best explains their areas?

A.X has greater area because its length is greater.
B.Y has greater area because its dimensions are more balanced. ✅
C.Both have equal area because their perimeters are equal.
D.X has greater area because its perimeter is smaller.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Rectangle X has area \(6(14)=84\) m², while Rectangle Y has area \(10(10)=100\) m². Equal perimeter does not imply equal area. For a fixed perimeter, dimensions that are more balanced can enclose more area.

Q4. A triangular sign has side lengths \(9\) m, \(12\) m, and \(15\) m. A student claims it is impossible to use the Pythagorean relationship because no right angle is explicitly marked. Which assessment is most accurate?

A.The claim is correct because a right angle must always be marked.
B.The triangle is right because \(9^2+12^2=15^2\). ✅
C.The triangle is equilateral because \(9+12=21\).
D.The triangle is obtuse because \(15\) is the longest side.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The longest side is \(15\) m. Checking the squared lengths gives \(9^2+12^2=81+144=225=15^2\). Therefore the side lengths are consistent with a right triangle, even without a right-angle symbol being explicitly shown.

Q5. A ladder reaches a point \(15\) ft above the ground while its base is \(8\) ft from the wall. The owner plans to replace it with a ladder \(2\) ft longer. Approximately how high could the longer ladder reach if its base remains \(8\) ft from the wall?

A.15.9 ft
B.16.0 ft
C.17.0 ft ✅
D.18.0 ft
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The original ladder has length \(\sqrt{15^2+8^2}=17\) ft. A replacement ladder is \(19\) ft long. With the same \(8\)-ft horizontal distance, its height is \(\sqrt{19^2-8^2}=\sqrt{297}\approx17.23\) ft, so the closest option is \(17.0\) ft. This requires modeling both situations rather than simply adding \(2\) ft to the height.

Q6. A student solves a rectangle problem by writing \(2L+W=54\) when the perimeter is \(54\) m. The student then finds \(L=20\) and \(W=14\). What is the main error?

A.The student should have multiplied length and width.
B.The perimeter equation must include both lengths and both widths. ✅
C.The dimensions must always be equal.
D.The area should be used instead of perimeter.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A rectangle has two lengths and two widths, so its perimeter is \(2L+2W\), not \(2L+W\). The proposed dimensions have perimeter \(2(20)+2(14)=68\) m, not \(54\) m. The error is an incomplete perimeter model.

Q7. A student determines that a triangle with sides \(7\), \(10\), and \(13\) is right because \(7+10>13\). Why is this reasoning insufficient?

A.Side lengths cannot be used to classify triangles.
B.The comparison \(7+10>13\) only checks whether a triangle can exist, not whether it is right. ✅
C.A right triangle must always have equal sides.
D.The largest side must be smaller than the other two combined.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The inequality \(7+10>13\) verifies that the three lengths can form a triangle, but it does not establish a right angle. For a right triangle, the relevant test compares squared side lengths. Here \(7^2+10^2=149\), which is not \(13^2=169\), so the triangle is not right.

Q8. On a coordinate grid, a rectangular region has vertices \((2,1)\), \((2,7)\), \((10,7)\), and \((10,1)\). A diagonal is drawn from \((2,1)\) to \((10,7)\). Which statement correctly describes the diagonal?

A.It has length \(10\) units.
B.It has length \(12\) units.
C.It has length \(100\) units.
D.It has length \(\sqrt{100}\) units, which equals \(10\) units. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The horizontal change is \(10-2=8\) units and the vertical change is \(7-1=6\) units. The diagonal therefore has length \(\sqrt{8^2+6^2}=\sqrt{100}=10\) units. The coordinate differences provide the two perpendicular components needed for the calculation.

Q9. A triangular piece of land has a base of \(30\) m and height \(18\) m. A rectangular section of \(6\) m by \(10\) m is removed from it. What area remains?

A.180 m²
B.210 m² ✅
C.240 m²
D.270 m²
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The original triangular area is \(\frac{1}{2}(30)(18)=270\) m². The removed rectangular area is \(6(10)=60\) m². Subtracting gives \(270-60=210\) m². The important modeling step is treating the removed portion as an area subtraction rather than changing the triangle's base or height.

Q10. A designer wants a rectangle with a fixed perimeter of \(48\) m but wants to maximize its enclosed area. Which dimensions should be selected?

A.6 m by 18 m
B.8 m by 16 m
C.10 m by 14 m
D.12 m by 12 m ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: For a fixed perimeter, increasing the smaller dimension while decreasing the larger one makes the area larger until the dimensions become equal. The four options all have perimeter \(48\) m, but their areas are \(108\), \(128\), \(140\), and \(144\) m² respectively. Thus \(12\) m by \(12\) m gives the greatest area.
