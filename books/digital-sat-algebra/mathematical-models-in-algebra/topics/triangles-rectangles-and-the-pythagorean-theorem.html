What is Triangles, Rectangles, and the Pythagorean Theorem?

Definition:
Triangles, rectangles, and the Pythagorean theorem are fundamental geometric concepts where triangles have three sides and angles summing to 180∘180^\circ180∘, rectangles have four sides with opposite sides equal and angles of 90∘90^\circ90∘, and the Pythagorean theorem applies to right triangles, stating that the square of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides, expressed as a2+b2=c2a^2 + b^2 = c^2a2+b2=c2, where ccc is the hypotenuse.

Working:
For triangles, use the angle sum property ∠A+∠B+∠C=180∘\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ∠A+∠B+∠C=180∘ to find missing angles, and for rectangles, use the perimeter P=2(l+w)P = 2(l + w)P=2(l+w) and area A=l×wA = l \times wA=l×w, while the Pythagorean theorem is used to find missing side lengths in right triangles by isolating the variable: c=a2+b2c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}c=a2+b2​ for the hypotenuse, or a=c2−b2a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}a=c2−b2​ for a leg.

Example:
A right triangle has legs of 3 cm and 4 cm, so the hypotenuse is c=32+42=9+16=25=5c = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5c=32+42​=9+16​=25​=5 cm, and if a rectangle has length 8 m and width 5 m, its perimeter is 2(8+5)=262(8 + 5) = 262(8+5)=26 m and area is 8×5=408 \times 5 = 408×5=40 m2^22.

Reason:
These concepts are foundational in geometry and are used in construction, architecture, engineering, navigation, and many fields involving spatial reasoning and measurements, making them essential for students and professionals alike.
