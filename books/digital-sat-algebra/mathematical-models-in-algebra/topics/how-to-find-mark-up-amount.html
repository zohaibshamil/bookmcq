📝 How to find mark up amount (14 MCQs)

Calculating the Amount Added to an Original Cost

A markup amount is the additional money placed above an item's original cost to establish a selling price. It is often calculated as a percentage of the cost and used in retail pricing and business models. Algebra expresses the markup as a portion of the original cost, connecting percentage reasoning with practical financial applications in the Digital SAT.

Why Markup Must Be Distinguished from the Final Selling Price

The markup represents the increase above the original cost, not the entire selling price. Calculating only the final price without identifying the added amount can confuse cost, markup, and profit. A percentage markup uses the original cost as its reference base under the standard cost-based definition.

Multiplying Original Cost by the Markup Rate

Convert the markup percentage rrr into a decimal by dividing by 100. Multiply it by the original cost CCC to obtain M=CrM=CrM=Cr, where MMM is the markup amount. Add the markup to the cost to calculate the selling price before taxes or other charges.

Finding the Markup Amount on a Sixty-Dollar Product

A retailer purchases an item for 60 dollars and applies a markup of 35 percent. Convert the rate to 0.350.350.35, then calculate M=60(0.35)=21M=60(0.35)=21M=60(0.35)=21. The markup is 21 dollars, producing a selling price of 81 dollars before additional charges.

Using Markup Calculations in Retail and Business Pricing

Markup calculations help retailers establish selling prices, estimate gross profit amounts, and compare pricing strategies. They are useful when the markup is defined relative to cost. A markup percentage based on selling price is a different measure, so the reference base must be identified before calculating.

Distinguishing Cost-Based Markup from Other Price Percentages

'A 35 percent markup means adding 35 dollars' confuses a rate with a monetary amount. 'Markup equals the selling price' ignores the original cost. 'Markup percentage is always calculated using the selling price' changes the reference base; the conventional cost-based markup uses original cost.

Solving Markup Questions on the Digital SAT

Identify the original cost, convert the markup rate into a decimal, and multiply to find the added amount. Questions may then ask for the final selling price or compare business models. A calculator can verify multiplication, while separating cost, markup, and final price prevents confusion.

Key Terms of Markup Amounts

  • Markup - Amount added above original cost.
  • Original cost - Amount paid to acquire an item.
  • Markup rate - Percentage applied to original cost.
  • Selling price - Price charged to a customer.
  • Profit - Financial gain after relevant costs.
  • Decimal rate - Percentage expressed as a decimal.
