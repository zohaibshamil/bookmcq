What is At most inequality meaning and examples?

Definition:
At most" in inequality problems means the quantity is less than or equal to a certain value represented by \( \le \) and it is synonymous with "no more than indicating a maximum limit, meaning the value cannot exceed the stated amount, and this is often used for constraints like spending limits, time limits, or inventory limits.

Working:
To translate at most use \( \le \) and place the variable accordingly; for example, at most 30" is \( x \le 30 \) and when solving the solution includes all values up to and including the boundary and this is essential for respecting upper bounds and the graph shows a closed circle at the boundary with an arrow pointing left.

Example:
A school can accommodate at most 500 students; if currently 420 students are enrolled and \( x \) new students apply then the inequality is \( 420 + x \le 500 \) so \( x \le 80 \) meaning the school can accept at most 80 new students.

Reason:
"At most" is a common phrase in real-world constraints such as capacity budget and time management and understanding it helps in planning resource allocation and adherence to limits in various professional and personal contexts."

📝 All At most inequality meaning and examples MCQs

Q1. A school club has a budget of $480. Each student attending a workshop costs $32 for materials. Which inequality correctly represents the greatest possible number \(x\) of students who can attend?

A.\(32x<480\)
B.\(32x\le480\) ✅
C.\(32x\ge480\)
D.\(32+x\le480\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The phrase 'at most' means the quantity cannot exceed the stated limit, so the cost must be less than or equal to $480. Since each student costs $32, the model is \(32x\le480\), allowing exactly the maximum budget to be used.

Q2. A delivery service charges a fixed fee of $18 plus $7 per package. A customer can spend at most $74. Which inequality and maximum number of packages correctly model the situation?

A.\(18+7x<74,\ x=7\)
B.\(18+7x\le74,\ x=8\) ✅
C.\(18+7x\ge74,\ x=8\)
D.\(18x+7\le74,\ x=3\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The fixed charge is $18 and the variable cost is \(7x\), while the total may be at most $74. Thus \(18+7x\le74\). Solving gives \(7x\le56\), so \(x\le8\), making 8 packages the greatest feasible whole number.

Q3. A worker earns $15 per hour and wants to work at most 26 hours this week. If \(h\) represents hours worked, which statement best describes the solution set?

A.\(h\ge26\)
B.\(h<26\)
C.\(h\le26\) ✅
D.\(h=26\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: 'At most 26 hours' includes 26 hours but prohibits anything greater. Therefore the correct inequality is \(h\le26\). The distinction matters because using \(h<26\) would incorrectly exclude the allowed endpoint.

Q4. A student solves \(5x+12\le47\) and concludes \(x\le5\). What is the best evaluation of the student's reasoning?

A.Correct, because 12 is subtracted from 47 to get 35 and \(35/5=5\).
B.Incorrect, because \(x\ge5\) after dividing by 5.
C.Incorrect, because the correct result is \(x\le7\). ✅
D.Correct, because 'at most' always produces \(x=5\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Subtracting 12 from both sides gives \(5x\le35\). Dividing by the positive number 5 preserves the inequality direction and gives \(x\le7\). The student's arithmetic incorrectly treats \(35/5\) as 5, causing the wrong boundary.

Q5. A graph represents the inequality \(y\le-2x+6\). Which description should a student use to identify the solution region?

A.The region above a dashed line
B.The region below a solid line ✅
C.The region above a solid line
D.The region below a dashed line
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Because the inequality contains \(\le\), points on the boundary are included, so the boundary must be solid. The symbol also indicates values of \(y\) less than or equal to the line, meaning the shaded region lies below the line.

Q6. A fitness center allows a maximum of 5 visitors per instructor. If 7 instructors are present, a manager claims that 36 visitors are allowed because \(7\times5+1=36\). Which assessment is correct?

A.The manager is correct because one extra visitor is always allowed.
B.The manager is incorrect because the maximum is 35 visitors. ✅
C.The manager is incorrect because the maximum is 34 visitors.
D.The manager is correct because 'at most' means slightly more than the calculated value.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Each instructor can supervise at most 5 visitors, so 7 instructors allow \(7(5)=35\) visitors. Adding one more visitor violates the stated maximum. The phrase 'at most' requires the total to remain less than or equal to the calculated capacity.

Q7. A café sells sandwiches for $6 each and charges a one-time delivery fee of $9. A customer has $65 and wants to buy the greatest possible number of sandwiches. Which modelling step is most important before choosing the answer?

A.Ignore the delivery fee because it is fixed.
B.Set \(6s+9\le65\) and require \(s\) to be a whole number. ✅
C.Set \(6s+9<65\) because the entire budget must not be reached.
D.Set \(9s+6\le65\) because the fee is paid for every sandwich.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The fixed fee must be included once, while the sandwich cost depends on \(s\). Since the customer can spend at most $65, \(6s+9\le65\). Finally, \(s\) must be a nonnegative whole number because partial sandwiches are not meaningful.

Q8. A student says that solving \(40-3x\le16\) gives \(x\le8\). Another student says the answer is \(x\ge8\). Which reasoning is correct?

A.The first student is correct because subtracting 40 never changes an inequality.
B.The second student is correct because dividing by \(-3\) reverses the inequality. ✅
C.Both are correct because \(x=8\) is the only boundary value.
D.Neither is correct because the inequality has no solution.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Subtracting 40 gives \(-3x\le-24\). Dividing by the negative number \(-3\) reverses the inequality, producing \(x\ge8\). The second student's answer correctly accounts for this crucial sign reversal.

Q9. A graph of a feasible region is bounded by \(x=2\), \(y=5\), and \(x+y=9\), with the boundary lines included. Which point is feasible if the conditions require \(x\ge2\), \(y\le5\), and \(x+y\le9\)?

A.\((1,5)\)
B.\((2,5)\) ✅
C.\((3,7)\)
D.\((5,5)\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The point \((2,5)\) satisfies all three conditions: \(2\ge2\), \(5\le5\), and \(2+5=7\le9\). The other choices violate at least one restriction, so interpreting the intersection of the graph's boundaries is essential.

Q10. A mobile data plan allows at most 12 GB per month. A customer has already used 7.5 GB and expects daily use of 0.3 GB for the next \(d\) days. What is the greatest whole number of days that can be predicted without exceeding the limit?

A.12 days
B.14 days ✅
C.15 days
D.16 days
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The model is \(7.5+0.3d\le12\). Subtracting 7.5 gives \(0.3d\le4.5\), so \(d\le15\). Thus 15 days is actually feasible, making option C correct; using 14 would unnecessarily underestimate the maximum.

Q11. A manufacturer must keep the total weight of a package at most 50 kg. The package contains a fixed 8 kg container and identical units weighing 3.5 kg each. A worker calculates \(3.5n+8<50\) and reports \(n=11\). Which correction is most accurate?

A.The strict inequality should be replaced by \(\le\), and the maximum is 12 units. ✅
B.The inequality is correct, and 11 units is the maximum.
C.The fixed weight should be multiplied by \(n\), giving 10 units.
D.The inequality should be \(3.5n+8\ge50\), giving 12 units.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: 'At most 50 kg' means the package may weigh exactly 50 kg, so the correct model is \(3.5n+8\le50\). This gives \(3.5n\le42\), hence \(n\le12\). Therefore 12 whole units are allowed, whereas 11 is not the maximum.

Q12. A competition awards 4 points for each successful task and subtracts 3 points for each failed task. A contestant may attempt at most 10 tasks and must score at least 20 points. Which pair of conditions correctly models \(s\) successes and \(f\) failures?

A.\(s+f\le10,\ 4s-3f\ge20\) ✅
B.\(s+f\ge10,\ 4s+3f\ge20\)
C.\(s+f=10,\ 4s-3f\le20\)
D.\(s-f\le10,\ 4s+3f\ge20\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The phrase 'at most 10 tasks' gives \(s+f\le10\), while 'at least 20 points' gives \(4s-3f\ge20\). Together these constraints capture both the attempt limit and the minimum score, demonstrating how different inequality phrases combine in one model.
