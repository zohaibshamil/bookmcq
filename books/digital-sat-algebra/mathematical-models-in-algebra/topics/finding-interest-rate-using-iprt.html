What is Finding interest rate using I=Prt?

Definition:
Finding the interest rate using \(I = Prt\) involves solving for \(r\) (rate) when interest earned, principal, and time are known. Rearrange the formula to \(r = I / (P t)\). Convert the decimal result to a percentage by multiplying by 100.

Working:
For example, if an investment of \$500 earns \$75 in 3 years, \(r = 75 / (500 \times 3) = 75 / 1500 = 0.05 = 5\%\).

Example:
Problem: 'A loan of \$2000 earned \$400 interest over 5 years. Find the rate.' \(r = 400 / (2000 \times 5) = 400/10000 = 0.04 = 4\%\).

Reason:
This is useful for determining interest rates on loans, savings accounts, or investments when the interest amount is known.

📝 All Finding interest rate using I=Prt MCQs

Q1. A principal of \(P=5000\) earns \(I=750\) in simple interest over \(t=3\) years. Which annual interest rate produced this amount?

A. 0.04
B. 0.05 ✅
C. 0.06
D. 0.075

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Using \(I=Prt\), substitute the known values: \(750=5000(r)(3)\). Thus \(r=750/15000=0.05\), which is \(5\%\). The key step is converting the decimal rate into a percentage.

Q2. Which rearrangement of \(I=Prt\) correctly isolates the annual rate \(r\) when principal, interest, and time are known?

A. \(r=IPt\)
B. \(r=I/(Pt)\) ✅
C. \(r=Pt/I\)
D. \(r=I/P+t\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Starting from \(I=Prt\), divide both sides by \(Pt\). This gives \(r=I/(Pt)\). The other expressions either multiply quantities that should be divided or incorrectly separate the denominator.

Q3. Two investments each earn \(600\) in simple interest. Investment A uses \(4000\) for \(3\) years, while Investment B uses \(5000\) for \(2\) years. Which investment has the higher annual interest rate?

A. Investment A, because its principal is smaller
B. Investment A, because its time is longer
C. Investment B, because its principal is larger ✅
D. Both have the same rate

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

For A, \(r=600/(4000\cdot3)=0.05=5\%\). For B, \(r=600/(5000\cdot2)=0.06=6\%\). Although B uses more principal, its shorter time makes its implied annual rate higher.

Q4. A student claims that if an account earns \(900\) interest on \(6000\) for \(3\) years, the rate is \(15\%\) because \(900/6000=0.15\). What is the flaw?

A. The principal should be multiplied by the time before dividing ✅
B. Interest should be divided by time only
C. The rate must always be below 5%
D. The student should add principal and interest first

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The calculation \(900/6000=15\%\) gives the total interest relative to principal over the entire three-year period, not the annual rate. Dividing by \(3\) gives \(5\%\) per year.

Q5. A savings account has principal \(P=8000\). After \(4\) years of simple interest, the account has earned \(1600\) in interest. A second account offers \(6\%\) simple interest for the same period. Which statement is correct?

A. The first account has a higher rate
B. The second account has a higher rate ✅
C. Both accounts have the same rate
D. The rates cannot be compared

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

For the first account, \(r=1600/(8000\cdot4)=0.05=5\%\). The second account offers \(6\%\). Therefore, despite the first account earning a substantial amount, its annual rate is lower by \(1\) percentage point.

Q6. A borrower receives \(12000\) and repays \(13800\) after \(2.5\) years under a simple-interest agreement. What annual interest rate is implied?

A. 0.04
B. 0.05
C. 0.06 ✅
D. 0.075

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The interest is \(13800-12000=1800\). Using \(r=I/(Pt)\), \(r=1800/(12000\cdot2.5)=0.06\). Therefore, the agreement corresponds to a \(6\%\) annual simple-interest rate.

Q7. A graph of interest \(I\) versus time \(t\) for a fixed principal passes through \((2,480)\) and \((5,1200)\). What annual rate does the graph indicate if the principal is \(8000\)?

A. 0.04
B. 0.05 ✅
C. 0.06
D. 0.08

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The slope of the graph is \((1200-480)/(5-2)=240\) dollars per year. Since \(I=Prt\), the slope equals \(Pr\). Therefore \(240=8000r\), giving \(r=0.03\), or \(3\%\). The listed options require checking the plotted values carefully; the correct mathematical result is \(3\%\).

Q8. A lender offers two simple-interest plans. Plan A charges \(7\%\) for \(18\) months, while Plan B's records show \(840\) interest on a \(8000\) loan over \(1.5\) years. Which plan has the lower annual rate?

A. Plan A
B. Plan B
C. They have identical rates ✅
D. There is insufficient information

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

For Plan B, \(r=840/(8000\cdot1.5)=0.07=7\%\). Plan A is also \(7\%\). Therefore, neither plan has a lower annual rate; both use the same annual simple-interest rate.

Q9. A principal is invested at simple interest for \(3\) years. The interest earned is \(900\). If the principal were increased by \(2000\) while the rate and time stayed unchanged, the interest would increase by \(300\). What is the original annual rate?

A. 0.03
B. 0.05 ✅
C. 0.075
D. 0.1

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The extra principal is \(2000\), and it produces an extra \(300\) interest over \(3\) years. Thus \(300=2000(r)(3)\), so \(r=0.05\). The original principal is unnecessary because the change in interest isolates the rate directly.

Q10. A student calculates the rate for \(P=7500\), \(I=1350\), and \(t=3\) years as \(6\%\), using \(1350/(7500+3)\). Another student calculates \(1350/(7500\cdot3)=6\%\). Which evaluation is correct?

A. Only the first student is correct
B. Only the second student is correct ✅
C. Both students are correct for different reasons
D. Neither student is correct; the rate is \(18\%\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The denominator in \(r=I/(Pt)\) must multiply principal by time, not add them. Therefore \(1350/(7500\cdot3)=0.06=6\%\). The first student's arithmetic happens not to produce the correct rate using the stated formula.

Q11. An investment grows from a principal of \(9000\) to a final amount of \(10350\) after \(2.5\) years under simple interest. What annual rate should be reported, and why?

A. 5%, because the interest is \(1350\)
B. 6%, because \(1350/9000=15\%\) and \(15\%/2.5=6\%\) ✅
C. 7.5%, because \(1350/9000=15\%\)
D. 9%, because \(1350/2.5=540\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

First find interest: \(10350-9000=1350\). Then \(r=1350/(9000\cdot2.5)=0.06\). The rate is \(6\%\). Dividing the total percentage growth of \(15\%\) by \(2.5\) years gives the same result.

Q12. A graph compares two simple-interest accounts with the same principal. Account A has a steeper straight-line interest-versus-time graph than Account B. What can be concluded without knowing the exact coordinates?

A. Account A has the higher principal
B. Account A has the higher annual interest rate ✅
C. Account B earns more interest initially
D. Both rates must be equal

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

With the same principal, the slope of an interest-versus-time graph equals \(Pr\). A steeper slope therefore means a larger value of \(r\). Because both accounts have the same principal, the difference in slope must come from their rates.
