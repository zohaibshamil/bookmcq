What is Solving formulas for a variable?

Definition:
Solving formulas for a variable involves isolating a specific variable in an equation that contains multiple variables. This uses inverse operations (addition/subtraction, multiplication/division) to get the desired variable alone on one side, treating other variables as constants.

Working:
For \(F = \frac{9}{5}C + 32\) (Fahrenheit to Celsius), solve for \(C\): subtract 32: \(F - 32 = \frac{9}{5}C\), multiply by \(5/9\): \(C = \frac{5}{9}(F - 32)\).

Example:
Solve \(A = \frac{1}{2}bh\) for \(h\) (area of triangle). Multiply by 2: \(2A = bh\), divide by \(b\): \(h = 2A/b\).

Reason:
This is a critical skill in STEM fields, allowing conversion between different forms of formulas and solving for any parameter.

📝 All Solving formulas for a variable MCQs

Q1. Given \(A=lw\), which expression correctly isolates \(w\) when \(A\) and \(l\) are known?

A. \(w=A-l\)
B. \(w=Al\)
C. \(w=A/l\) ✅
D. \(w=l/A\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

To isolate \(w\), divide both sides of \(A=lw\) by \(l\), assuming \(l\neq0\). This gives \(w=A/l\). The other choices result from subtracting or multiplying instead of applying the inverse operation correctly.

Q2. The formula \(P=2l+2w\) represents a rectangle's perimeter. Which form isolates \(l\)?

A. \(l=P-2w\)
B. \(l=(P-2w)/2\) ✅
C. \(l=(P+2w)/2\)
D. \(l=P/(2w)\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Starting with \(P=2l+2w\), subtract \(2w\) from both sides to obtain \(P-2w=2l\). Dividing by 2 then gives \(l=(P-2w)/2\), preserving equivalence at every step.

Q3. A formula is \(C=5(F-32)/9\). Which sequence correctly isolates \(F\) without changing the relationship?

A. Multiply by 9, divide by 5, then add 32. ✅
B. Add 32, multiply by 9, then divide by 5.
C. Subtract 32, multiply by 5, then divide by 9.
D. Multiply by 5, divide by 9, then subtract 32.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To isolate \(F\), first multiply by 9, giving \(9C=5(F-32)\). Divide by 5 to obtain \(9C/5=F-32\), then add 32. This produces \(F=9C/5+32\).

Q4. A physics model uses \(v=u+at\). A student wants \(t\) and writes \(t=(v-u)a\). Why is this incorrect?

A. Acceleration must be added instead of multiplied.
B. The entire difference \(v-u\) must be divided by \(a\). ✅
C. Initial velocity must be divided by \(a\) first.
D. Velocity must be multiplied by acceleration.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Subtracting \(u\) from both sides gives \(v-u=at\). Since \(a\) multiplies \(t\), division by \(a\) is required to isolate \(t\). Therefore, \(t=(v-u)/a\), assuming \(a\neq0\).

Q5. A rectangle has area 84 square units and width 7 units. Using \(A=lw\), which expression and value correctly determine its length?

A. \(l=84-7=77\)
B. \(l=84+7=91\)
C. \(l=84/7=12\) ✅
D. \(l=7/84=1/12\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Because area equals length times width, isolate length by dividing the area by the width. Substituting \(A=84\) and \(w=7\) gives \(l=84/7=12\). Subtraction or reversing the division does not preserve the formula.

Q6. A phone plan is modeled by \(T=25+0.08m\), where \(T\) is total cost and \(m\) is the number of messages. Which expression isolates \(m\)?

A. \(m=(T+25)/0.08\)
B. \(m=(T-25)/0.08\) ✅
C. \(m=0.08(T-25)\)
D. \(m=25-0.08T\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Subtracting the fixed charge 25 from both sides gives \(T-25=0.08m\). Dividing by 0.08 isolates \(m\). Thus \(m=(T-25)/0.08\), which correctly separates the variable-dependent charge from the fixed charge.

Q7. A student transforms \(S=2\pi rh+2\pi r^2\) into \(h=(S-2\pi r^2)/(2\pi r)\). Which reasoning justifies the result?

A. Both terms are divided by \(2\pi r\) after the second term is subtracted. ✅
B. The radius can be canceled from only the second term.
C. The entire equation is first multiplied by \(2\pi r\).
D. The \(2\pi r^2\) term is ignored because it contains no \(h\).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To isolate \(h\), subtract \(2\pi r^2\) from both sides, giving \(S-2\pi r^2=2\pi rh\). Dividing the entire equation by \(2\pi r\) then yields the stated expression, assuming \(r\neq0\).

Q8. The equation \(y=mx+b\) can be viewed as a line. If \(y\) and \(b\) are fixed and \(m\neq0\), which rearrangement gives the corresponding \(x\)-coordinate?

A. \(x=(y-b)/m\) ✅
B. \(x=(y+b)/m\)
C. \(x=m(y-b)\)
D. \(x=(b-y)/m\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Subtracting the intercept \(b\) from both sides gives \(y-b=mx\). Dividing by the slope \(m\) isolates \(x\), producing \(x=(y-b)/m\). This also represents how a known vertical value determines the horizontal coordinate.

Q9. On a graph of \(y=4x-12\), a point has \(y=20\). Which rearrangement and calculation correctly find its \(x\)-coordinate?

A. \(x=(20+12)/4=8\) ✅
B. \(x=(20-12)/4=2\)
C. \(x=4(20-12)=32\)
D. \(x=(12-20)/4=-2\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Substituting \(y=20\) into \(y=4x-12\) gives \(20=4x-12\). Adding 12 produces \(32=4x\), and dividing by 4 gives \(x=8\). The point therefore has horizontal coordinate 8.

Q10. Two students isolate \(x\) from \(ax+c=b\). Student 1 writes \(x=(b-c)/a\). Student 2 writes \(x=b/a-c\). Assuming \(a\neq0\), which statement is most accurate?

A. Only Student 2 is always correct.
B. Both are always equivalent.
C. Student 1 is correct because the numerator must be divided as a whole by \(a\). ✅
D. Student 2 is correct because division distributes over subtraction.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Student 1 correctly subtracts \(c\) first, obtaining \(ax=b-c\), then divides the entire right side by \(a\). Student 2's expression generally means \(b/a-c\), which does not equal \((b-c)/a\) unless special values make them coincide.

Q11. A travel formula is \(d=rt\). A driver knows the distance \(d=315\) km and time \(t=4.5\) hours. Which method correctly isolates and calculates the rate?

A. Multiply 315 by 4.5, giving 1417.5 km/h.
B. Divide 315 by 4.5, giving 70 km/h. ✅
C. Divide 4.5 by 315, giving approximately 0.014 km/h.
D. Subtract 4.5 from 315, giving 310.5 km/h.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

From \(d=rt\), divide both sides by \(t\) to isolate \(r\), giving \(r=d/t\). Substituting the measurements gives \(315/4.5=70\), so the driver's average rate is 70 km/h.

Q12. A quantity is defined by \(Q=(x+6)/4\). Another student claims the isolated form is \(x=4Q-6\). How can the claim be verified most efficiently?

A. Substitute \(x=4Q-6\) into the original formula and simplify. ✅
B. Multiply the original formula by 6 only.
C. Compare the coefficients of \(x\) without substitution.
D. Subtract \(Q\) from both sides of the original equation.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Starting with \(Q=(x+6)/4\), multiplying both sides by 4 gives \(4Q=x+6\), and subtracting 6 gives \(x=4Q-6\). Substitution into the original formula returns \(Q\), confirming the rearrangement.

Q13. A model is \(E=mc^2\). If \(m\) is the variable to isolate, which expression is correct, and what key condition is required?

A. \(m=Ec^2\), with \(c=0\)
B. \(m=E/c^2\), with \(c\neq0\) ✅
C. \(m=c^2/E\), with \(E\neq0\)
D. \(m=E-c^2\), with no restriction

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Since \(c^2\) multiplies \(m\), dividing both sides by \(c^2\) gives \(m=E/c^2\). Division requires \(c^2\neq0\), so \(c\neq0\). The other choices either reverse the division or use an invalid operation.
