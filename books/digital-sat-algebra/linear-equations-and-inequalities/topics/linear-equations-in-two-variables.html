What is Linear equations in two variables?

Definition:
Linear equations in two variables are equations that can be written in the form \(Ax + By = C\), where \(A, B, C\) are constants and \(x, y\) are variables. They graph as straight lines on a coordinate plane and have infinitely many ordered pair solutions \((x, y)\) that satisfy them.

Working:
To find solutions, choose a value for one variable and solve for the other. For example, for \(2x + y = 6\), if \(x = 1\), then \(2(1) + y = 6\), so \(y = 4\), giving solution (1,4).

Example:
For \(x - 3y = 9\), if \(y = 0\), then \(x = 9\), solution (9,0). If \(x = 0\), then \(-3y = 9\), so \(y = -3\), solution (0, -3).

Reason:
Understanding two-variable equations is key to graphing, systems of equations, and modeling real-world relationships with two unknowns.

Q1. A student solves \(6x-3y=21\) as follows: \(6x-21=3y\), then \(y=2x-21\). What is the specific error?

A. The student should add 21, not subtract it
B. The student divided only \(6x\) by 3 and failed to divide 21 by 3 ✅
C. The student should divide by 6 first
D. The student incorrectly changed \(3y\) into \(-3y\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

After rearranging, \(3y=6x-21\). Dividing the entire right side by 3 gives \(y=2x-7\). The student's error is failing to divide the constant 21 by 3, a common misconception when isolating a variable.

Q2. Two students solve \(8x+4y=32\). Student A gets \(y=8-2x\), while Student B gets \(y=2x-8\). Which conclusion is correct?

A. Student A is correct because the \(x\)-term is subtracted before division ✅
B. Student B is correct because \(4y\) is positive
C. Both are correct for different values of \(x\)
D. Neither is correct because \(y\) cannot be isolated

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Subtracting \(8x\) gives \(4y=32-8x\). Dividing by 4 produces \(y=8-2x\). Student B reversed the signs incorrectly. Substituting a simple value such as \(x=0\) confirms that \(y=8\), supporting Student A.

Q3. The graph of a linear equation crosses the \(y\)-axis at 6 and passes through the point \((3,0)\). Which equation represents the line in solved-for-\(y\) form?

A. \(y=2x+6\)
B. \(y=-2x+6\) ✅
C. \(y=-\frac{1}{2}x+6\)
D. \(y=\frac{1}{2}x-6\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The \(y\)-intercept is 6. Using the points \((0,6)\) and \((3,0)\), the slope is \((0-6)/(3-0)=-2\). Therefore the equation is \(y=-2x+6\), matching both graph features.

Q4. A company models its remaining inventory with \(4x+y=120\), where \(x\) is the number of boxes sold and \(y\) is inventory remaining. Which interpretation follows from solving for \(y\)?

A. \(y=120+4x\), so inventory rises as boxes are sold
B. \(y=120-4x\), so each additional box sold reduces inventory by 4 units ✅
C. \(y=4x-120\), so inventory starts negative
D. \(y=\frac{120}{4}-x\), so each box reduces inventory by 1

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Rearranging \(4x+y=120\) gives \(y=120-4x\). The intercept 120 represents the starting inventory, while the coefficient \(-4\) means every additional box sold reduces the remaining inventory by four units.

Q5. For \(3x-2y=12\), a learner substitutes \(x=2y\) and obtains \(y=6\). Is this conclusion valid?

A. Yes, because \(3(2y)-2y=12\) gives \(y=6\)
B. No, because substituting \(x=2y\) gives \(4y=12\), so \(y=3\) ✅
C. No, because \(x=2y\) cannot be substituted into a linear equation
D. Yes, because \(x\) and \(y\) must have equal coefficients

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Substituting \(x=2y\) gives \(3(2y)-2y=12\), so \(6y-2y=12\), which simplifies to \(4y=12\) and therefore \(y=3\). The learner's arithmetic conclusion \(y=6\) is incorrect.

Q6. A line has equation \(9x+3y=27\). Another student rewrites it as \(y=9-3x\). Which equivalent form correctly preserves the line and best reveals its slope?

A. \(y=9-3x\) ✅
B. \(y=3-3x\)
C. \(y=9x-9\)
D. \(y=3x-9\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Subtracting \(9x\) from both sides gives \(3y=27-9x\). Dividing every term by 3 produces \(y=9-3x\). Therefore A is mathematically correct, while B incorrectly divides the constant term. The slope is \(-3\).
