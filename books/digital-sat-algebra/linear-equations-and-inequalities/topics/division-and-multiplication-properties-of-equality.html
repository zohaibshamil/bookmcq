📝 Division and Multiplication Properties of Equality

📖 From Digital SAT Algebra • 2. Linear Equations And Inequalities • 17 questions available

What is Division and Multiplication Properties of Equality?

Definition:
The Division and Multiplication Properties of Equality state that multiplying or dividing both sides of an equation by the same nonzero number keeps the equation balanced. For \(a = b\), \(a \cdot c = b \cdot c\) and \(a/c = b/c\) (if \(c \neq 0\)). These are used to undo multiplication or division.

Working:
To solve \(3x = 21\), use the Division Property: divide both sides by 3: \(3x/3 = 21/3\), giving \(x = 7\). To solve \(x/4 = 5\), use the Multiplication Property: multiply both sides by 4: \((x/4) \cdot 4 = 5 \cdot 4\), giving \(x = 20\).

Example:
Solve \(6y = 42\) using Division Property. Divide both sides by 6: \(6y/6 = 42/6\), so \(y = 7\). Verify: \(6(7) = 42\).

Reason:
These properties allow us to isolate the variable when it is multiplied or divided by a constant, making them essential for solving equations of the form \(ax = b\) or \(x/a = b\).

📝 All Division and Multiplication Properties of Equality MCQs

Q1. If \( -5x = 45 \), what operation and property correctly isolate \( x \)?

A. Divide both sides by 5 using the Division Property of Equality
B. Divide both sides by -5 using the Division Property of Equality ✅
C. Multiply both sides by -5 using the Multiplication Property of Equality
D. Multiply both sides by 5 using the Multiplication Property of Equality

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The coefficient of \( x \) is -5. To isolate \( x \), we must perform the inverse operation, which is division by -5. The Division Property of Equality states that dividing both sides of an equation by the same non-zero number keeps the equation balanced. Option A is incorrect because dividing by 5 would leave \( -x = 9 \), not solving for \( x \). Options C and D involve multiplication, which would not undo the multiplication by -5.

Q2. A student solves \( \frac{x}{7} = 12 \) by multiplying both sides by 7 and gets \( x = 84 \). Which property justifies this step?

A. Division Property of Equality
B. Multiplication Property of Equality ✅
C. Additive Inverse Property
D. Commutative Property of Multiplication

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The student multiplied both sides of the equation by the same number (7) to maintain equality. This is a direct application of the Multiplication Property of Equality, which states that if \( a = b \), then \( a \cdot c = b \cdot c \). The Division Property involves dividing, the Additive Inverse involves addition, and the Commutative Property changes order, not balance.

Q3. A recipe requires \( \frac{3}{4} \) cup of sugar for 12 cookies. How many cups are needed for 20 cookies if the relationship is proportional?

A. \( 1 \) cup
B. \( 1.25 \) cups ✅
C. \( 1.5 \) cups
D. \( 2 \) cups

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Set up a proportion: \( \frac{3/4}{12} = \frac{x}{20} \). Cross-multiply: \( 20 \cdot (3/4) = 12x \Rightarrow 15 = 12x \Rightarrow x = 1.25 \). This requires using the Multiplication Property to eliminate the denominator. Option A (1 cup) underestimates, C (1.5) and D (2) overestimate due to incorrect scaling.

Q4. Examine this solution: \( 4x + 6 = 18 \Rightarrow 4x = 12 \Rightarrow x = 48 \). Identify the error.

A. Addition error
B. Division error – divided instead of multiplied
C. Division error – forgot to divide both sides
D. Multiplication error – multiplied instead of divided ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The first step \( 4x = 12 \) is correct after subtracting 6. However, to isolate \( x \), the student should divide both sides by 4, getting \( x = 3 \). Instead, they multiplied 12 by 4 to get 48, incorrectly applying the Multiplication Property when the Division Property was needed. This is a classic misconception of reversing operations.

Q5. The graph of the equation \( y = 3x \) passes through which point? Solve \( 3x = 15 \) and identify the corresponding \( y \)-value on the graph.

A. (5, 15) ✅
B. (15, 5)
C. (5, 3)
D. (3, 15)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Solving \( 3x = 15 \) using the Division Property gives \( x = 5 \). For the equation \( y = 3x \), when \( x = 5 \), \( y = 15 \), so the point is (5, 15). Option B reverses coordinates; C uses \( x \) as \( y \); D misinterprets the slope. This connects algebraic solution to graphical representation.

Q6. Given \( -2x = 14 \), which of the following is NOT a valid application of the properties?

A. Divide both sides by -2 ✅
B. Multiply both sides by \( -\frac{1}{2} \)
C. Divide both sides by 2 and then multiply by -1
D. Multiply both sides by 2 and then divide by -4

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

(Revised) In solving \( -2x = 14 \), a student divides by -2 and gets \( x = -7 \). Another student multiplies by \( -\frac{1}{2} \) and gets \( x = -7 \). Which statement is true? Both are correct because dividing by -2 is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal \( -\frac{1}{2} \). Option A says only division is correct, which is false. So A is the error.

Q7. If \( \frac{3}{5}x = 9 \), what is the value of \( x \) in simplest form?

A. 15 ✅
B. 5
C. 27
D. \( \frac{27}{5} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To solve, multiply both sides by the reciprocal of \( \frac{3}{5} \), which is \( \frac{5}{3} \): \( x = 9 \cdot \frac{5}{3} = 15 \). Option B is \( 9 \div 3 \times 5 \) misapplied; C is \( 9 \times 3 \); D is \( 9 \times \frac{3}{5} \). This tests the concept that multiplying by the reciprocal is equivalent to division.

Q8. A car travels \( d \) miles in \( t \) hours at speed \( r = \frac{d}{t} \). If the speed is 60 mph and time is 2.5 hours, find the distance. Solve \( 60 = \frac{d}{2.5} \).

A. 150 miles ✅
B. 120 miles
C. 100 miles
D. 200 miles

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Multiply both sides by 2.5 using the Multiplication Property: \( d = 60 \times 2.5 = 150 \). Option B assumes \( 60 \times 2 \); C assumes \( 60 \times 1.5 \) misreading 2.5 as 1.5; D uses \( 60 \times 2.5 \) but adds extra. This models a real-world proportional relationship.

Q9. Compare two methods: Method A: \( 0.2x = 10 \Rightarrow x = 50 \) by dividing by 0.2. Method B: Multiply by 5 to get \( x = 50 \). Which is correct?

A. Only A
B. Only B
C. Both A and B ✅
D. Neither

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Dividing by 0.2 is equivalent to multiplying by 5 because \( 0.2 = \frac{1}{5} \). Both methods correctly isolate \( x \) and yield the same answer. Option A or B alone is incomplete; this tests the understanding of equivalent operations and decimal-fraction conversion.

Q10. Solve for \( x \): \( \frac{2x - 4}{3} = 8 \). Which sequence of properties is applied correctly?

A. Multiply by 3, then add 4, then divide by 2 ✅
B. Multiply by 3, then divide by 2, then add 4
C. Add 4, then multiply by 3, then divide by 2
D. Divide by 3, then add 4, then multiply by 2

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

First, multiply both sides by 3 (Multiplication Property) to get \( 2x - 4 = 24 \). Then add 4 (Addition Property) to get \( 2x = 28 \). Finally divide by 2 (Division Property) to get \( x = 14 \). Option B reverses addition/division; C ignores the order of operations; D applies division incorrectly. This requires ordering inverse operations.

Q11. The perimeter of a square is \( 4s = 36 \). If a student writes \( s = 9 \) after dividing by 4, what property did they use?

A. Division Property of Equality ✅
B. Multiplication Property of Equality
C. Substitution Property
D. Symmetric Property

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The student divided both sides of the equation by 4 to isolate \( s \). This is a direct application of the Division Property of Equality, which states that dividing both sides by the same non-zero number keeps the equation balanced. Multiplication would be used if the coefficient were a fraction; substitution and symmetric are not relevant here.

Q12. A student claims that \( \frac{x}{-3} = 7 \) can be solved by multiplying both sides by -3, giving \( x = -21 \). Is this correct? Explain.

A. Yes, because multiplying by -3 cancels the division ✅
B. No, because should divide by -3
C. Yes, but only if you also change the sign
D. No, because -3 times 7 is -21, so it's correct

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The equation is \( \frac{x}{-3} = 7 \). To isolate \( x \), multiply both sides by -3 (the denominator) – this is the Multiplication Property. \( x = 7 \times (-3) = -21 \). So the student is correct. Option B is wrong because division would complicate; C is nonsense; D is a common error in sign. This tests the reciprocal relationship between multiplication and division.

Q13. If \( 8x = 24 \), and you incorrectly divide only the left side by 8, what is the result?

A. \( x = 24 \)
B. \( x = 3 \)
C. \( x = 192 \)
D. \( 8x = 3 \) ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The correct operation is to divide both sides by 8, giving \( x = 3 \). If you only divide the left side, the equation becomes \( x = 24 \), but that's not balanced. Wait: If you divide left side by 8, you get \( x = 24 \)? No, \( 8x / 8 = x \), right side remains 24, so \( x = 24 \). Option A. But let's check: The student divided only the left side: \( 8x \div 8 = x \), right side stays 24, so \( x = 24 \). Option A. But D says \( 8x = 3 \) which would be dividing right only. Let's adjust options. Actually, the correct answer is A. I'll set options: A) \( x = 24 \), B) \( x = 3 \), C) \( x = 192 \), D) \( 8x = 3 \). The error is division on one side only, which violates the property. Answer A.

Q14. The graph of \( y = kx \) is a line through the origin. If the line passes through (6, 18), what is the value of \( k \)? Solve \( 6k = 18 \).

A. 3 ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{3} \)
C. 6
D. 18

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The equation \( y = kx \) gives \( 18 = k \cdot 6 \). To find \( k \), divide both sides by 6 using the Division Property: \( k = 3 \). Option B is the reciprocal; C is the x-coordinate; D is the y-coordinate. This connects algebraic solution to slope interpretation from a graph.

Q15. Solve for \( x \): \( 0.5x + 3 = 7 \). A student multiplies by 2 first to get \( x + 6 = 14 \), then subtracts 6 to get \( x = 8 \). Is this valid?

A. Yes, because multiplying by 2 is a valid operation ✅
B. No, because you must subtract before multiplying
C. Yes, but only if you also divide later
D. No, because the Multiplication Property doesn't apply to addition

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The student multiplied both sides of the entire equation by 2: \( 2(0.5x + 3) = 2(7) \) gives \( x + 6 = 14 \). This is a valid application of the Multiplication Property of Equality because it's applied to both sides. Then subtracting 6 is valid. Option B is wrong because order can vary as long as properties are applied correctly. This tests the flexibility of solving multi-step equations.

Q16. If \( \frac{2}{3}x = \frac{5}{6} \), what is \( x \)? Express as a fraction in lowest terms.

A. \( \frac{5}{4} \) ✅
B. \( \frac{5}{9} \)
C. \( \frac{4}{5} \)
D. \( \frac{9}{5} \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Multiply both sides by the reciprocal \( \frac{3}{2} \): \( x = \frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{3}{2} = \frac{15}{12} = \frac{5}{4} \). Option B is \( \frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \); C is the reciprocal of the answer; D is \( \frac{6}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \). This requires fraction multiplication and simplification, testing deeper understanding of reciprocals and equivalent forms.

Q17. A student solves \( -3x = 21 \) and writes \( x = -7 \). They then check by substituting: \( -3(-7) = 21 \). Which property validates the check?

A. Division Property of Equality
B. Multiplication Property of Equality
C. Substitution Property of Equality ✅
D. Reflexive Property

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The check involves substituting the found value back into the original equation. This is justified by the Substitution Property of Equality, which allows replacing a variable with its equal value. The Division Property was used to solve, but the verification uses substitution. Options A and B are operations; D is about equality of a number to itself. This distinguishes between solving and checking.
