What is Solve application problems (word problems)?

Definition:
Solving application problems involves translating a real-world scenario into an algebraic equation, solving that equation, and then interpreting the solution back in the context of the problem. This process often requires defining variables and setting up equations from given relationships.

Working:
Read the problem carefully, identify unknown quantities and assign variables. Write an equation based on the relationships described, solve it using algebraic methods, and then check if the answer makes sense in the original context. For example, if total cost = price × quantity, set up accordingly.

Example:
Problem: A number plus 6 times the same number equals 28. Find the number. Let the number be \(x\). Equation: \(x + 6x = 28\). Combine: \(7x = 28\), divide: \(x = 4\). The number is 4.

Reason:
This method provides a structured approach to tackling real-life problems, enhancing critical thinking and showing the practical use of algebra.

Q1. A car rental company charges a flat fee of $30 plus $0.20 per mile driven. Another company charges a flat fee of $20 plus $0.30 per mile. A customer has a budget of $80 and needs to drive 150 miles for a trip. Which company is within budget, and how much money will the customer save by choosing the cheaper option within budget?

A. Company A is within budget; saves $5 ✅
B. Company B is within budget; saves $5
C. Both are within budget; Company B saves $10
D. Only Company A is within budget; saves $10

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Company A cost: \(30+0.20(150)=60\), under $80. Company B cost: \(20+0.30(150)=65\), also under $80. Both are within budget. Savings = \(65-60=5\), so Company A saves $5. Option A is correct. Distractor B misidentifies the cheaper company; C miscalculates savings; D misses that B is also within budget.

Q2. A student solves the equation \(2(x-3)+4x=24\) and gets \(x=5\). To check, she substitutes \(x=5\) into the original equation and gets \(2(2)+20=24\), which is true. However, her classmate says the answer should be \(x=6\). Who is correct, and what is the likely error?

A. Student is correct; classmate likely added 6 instead of subtracting ✅
B. Classmate is correct; student likely forgot to distribute the 2
C. Student is correct; classmate likely divided incorrectly
D. Classmate is correct; student likely combined like terms incorrectly

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Solving: \(2x-6+4x=24 \Rightarrow 6x-6=24 \Rightarrow 6x=30 \Rightarrow x=5\). Student is correct. The classmate likely solved \(2x+4x=30\) as \(6x=36\) (added 6 instead of adding 6 to both sides correctly). Option A identifies this. B is wrong because distribution was correct; C and D misattribute the error.

Q3. A rectangular garden has a length that is 5 meters more than twice its width. If the perimeter is 70 meters, which equation correctly models the situation, and what is the width?

A. \(2(w+2w+5)=70\); width = 10 m
B. \(2w+2(2w+5)=70\); width = 10 m ✅
C. \(w+(2w+5)=70\); width = 15 m
D. \(2(w+2w-5)=70\); width = 12 m

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Let width = \(w\), length = \(2w+5\). Perimeter = \(2w+2(2w+5)=70\). Solve: \(2w+4w+10=70 \Rightarrow 6w=60 \Rightarrow w=10\). Option B is correct. A uses incorrect perimeter formula; C omits the factor 2; D uses wrong sign for length.

Q4. A teacher asks students to solve \(3(x-4)+2x=5x-12\). Two students respond: Student 1 says 'all real numbers' and Student 2 says 'no solution.' Which is correct, and why?

A. Student 1: both sides simplify to \(5x-12\), identity ✅
B. Student 2: after subtracting \(5x\), get \(-12=-12\), contradiction
C. Student 1: because \(x\) cancels but constants differ
D. Student 2: because \(x\) cancels and constants equal

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Simplify LHS: \(3x-12+2x=5x-12\). RHS: \(5x-12\). Both sides identical, so the equation is an identity true for all real numbers. Student 1 is correct. Student 2 incorrectly interprets \(-12=-12\) as contradiction; it's actually an identity. Option A explains correctly.

Q5. The graph of \(y=2x+3\) and \(y=-x+9\) intersect at a point. What does the x-coordinate of the intersection represent in a real-world context where \(y\) is cost in dollars and \(x\) is number of items?

A. The number of items where both costs are equal ✅
B. The total cost at the break-even point
C. The profit from selling items
D. The number of items that maximizes profit

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The x-coordinate of the intersection is the value of \(x\) (number of items) where both linear functions give the same \(y\) (cost). Setting \(2x+3=-x+9\) gives \(x=2\). So at 2 items, costs are equal. Option A is correct. B is the y-coordinate, not x. C and D are irrelevant to this simple intersection.

Q6. A student incorrectly solves the equation \(\frac{x}{4}+2=\frac{x}{2}-1\) by first multiplying by 4 to get \(x+8=2x-1\), then solving to \(x=9\). What error did the student make, and what is the correct solution?

A. Error: multiplied only one side by 4; correct x=12
B. Error: forgot to distribute to both terms; correct x=12 ✅
C. Error: added instead of subtracting; correct x=10
D. Error: correct solution is x=9; no error

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Multiplying by 4 correctly: \(4(\frac{x}{4}+2)=4(\frac{x}{2}-1)\) gives \(x+8=2x-4\). Then \(x=12\). The student missed multiplying the constant -1 on RHS by 4. Option B correctly identifies the distribution error. A is wrong because both sides were multiplied; C is off; D denies the error.

Q7. A phone plan offers 500 minutes for $40, plus $0.05 per additional minute. A second plan offers unlimited minutes for $60. A customer estimates they use 450 minutes. Which plan is cheaper, and how much do they save?

A. Plan 1 is cheaper by $17.50 ✅
B. Plan 2 is cheaper by $2.50
C. Plan 1 is cheaper by $20
D. Plan 2 is cheaper by $10

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Plan 1 cost for 450 min: $40 (since 450 ≤ 500). Plan 2: $60. Plan 1 cheaper by $20. Wait, recalc: 450 is within 500, so Plan 1 = $40, Plan 2 = $60, savings = $20. Option C is correct, but the options list C as 'Plan 1 is cheaper by $20'. Yes, C is correct. However, the answer key should be C. Let's correct: The correct option is C. Explanation: Since 450 ≤ 500, no extra charge, so Plan 1 = $40, Plan 2 = $60, save $20. Distractors: A miscomputes extra minutes; B reverses savings; D off.

Q8. A water tank is being filled at a rate of 5 gallons per minute and initially contains 10 gallons. A second tank is being filled at 3 gallons per minute and initially contains 30 gallons. After how many minutes will the tanks have the same amount of water? Solve and check.

A. 10 minutes ✅
B. 5 minutes
C. 8 minutes
D. 12 minutes

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Let \(t\) = minutes. Tank 1: \(10+5t\). Tank 2: \(30+3t\). Set equal: \(10+5t=30+3t \Rightarrow 2t=20 \Rightarrow t=10\). Check: Tank1=10+50=60, Tank2=30+30=60. Option A correct. B from subtracting incorrectly; C from miscalculation; D from adding instead of subtracting.

Q9. A student solves \(4(x+2)-3x=10\) and gets \(x=2\). Another student solves by first distributing and gets \(4x+8-3x=10\), then \(x=2\). A third student suggests that because \(x\) is on both sides, the equation has no solution. Which reasoning is flawed, and what is the correct solution?

A. Third student: equation has solution x=2 ✅
B. First student: should get x=3
C. Second student: distribution error
D. All are correct; x=2

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Solving: \(4x+8-3x=10 \Rightarrow x+8=10 \Rightarrow x=2\). First and second students are correct. Third student incorrectly assumes variables on both sides mean no solution. Correct solution is x=2. Option A identifies flawed reasoning. B and C are wrong accusations; D incorrect because third is wrong.

Q11. A student graphs the equation \(y=3x-2\) and another graphs \(y=-3x+4\). They claim the lines are parallel because they have opposite slopes. Is the claim correct? If not, what is the relationship?

A. Incorrect; they intersect at (1,1) ✅
B. Correct; parallel lines have opposite slopes
C. Incorrect; they are perpendicular
D. Incorrect; they are the same line

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Slopes are 3 and -3, not equal, so not parallel. Opposite slopes do not guarantee perpendicular (perpendicular requires product -1, here 3*(-3)=-9). They intersect. Set \(3x-2=-3x+4\) => 6x=6 => x=1, y=1. So they intersect at (1,1). Option A correct. B is false; C false because product not -1; D false.

Q12. A train leaves station A at 60 mph. Two hours later, a second train leaves station A on a parallel track at 80 mph. When will the second train catch up? A student solves \(60t=80(t-2)\) and gets \(t=8\) hours. Another student solves \(60(t+2)=80t\) and gets \(t=6\) hours. Which student is correct, and what does the variable represent?

A. Second student correct; t = time for faster train ✅
B. First student correct; t = time for slower train
C. Both correct; t represents different starting points
D. Neither; the trains never meet

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Let \(t\) = time in hours after the faster train leaves. Then slower train has traveled \(t+2\) hours. Distance equality: \(60(t+2)=80t\) => 60t+120=80t => 20t=120 => t=6 hours. So second student correct. First student set \(60t=80(t-2)\) assuming t is time for slower, which gives t=8 for slower, meaning faster travels 6 hours—same meeting time. Actually both are correct if interpreted properly: first student's t is time for slower train, so slower travels 8h, faster travels 6h. So both are correct but variables differ. The question asks 'which student is correct'—both are, but option C says 'Both correct; t represents different starting points'—that is true. So answer C. Explanation: First student: t=time for slow train, then faster travels t-2, so 60t=80(t-2) => t=8h for slow, so faster travels 6h. Second: t=time for fast, then slow travels t+2, so 60(t+2)=80t => t=6h for fast. Both give same meeting time. So C.

Q13. The perimeter of a rectangle is 40 cm. The length is 3 cm more than twice the width. A student writes the equation \(2w+2(2w+3)=40\) and solves to \(w=5.67\). Another student writes \(2w+2(2w-3)=40\) and gets \(w=7.67\). Which student is correct, and what error did the other make?

A. First student correct; second used wrong sign for length ✅
B. Second student correct; first mis-wrote the expression
C. Both incorrect; correct width is 6 cm
D. First student correct; second reversed the terms

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Let width = w, length = 2w+3. Perimeter = 2w+2(2w+3)=40 => 2w+4w+6=40 => 6w=34 => w=5.666... So first student correct. Second student used length = 2w-3, which is wrong. So option A. Explanation: The length is '3 more than twice width' so +3, not -3. Option B is false; C is off by rounding; D is incorrect.

Q14. A student claims that the equation \(2(x+1)=2x+2\) is an identity, so it has infinitely many solutions. Another student claims it has exactly one solution, \(x=0\). Who is correct, and how can you verify?

A. First student: simplify to 0=0, identity ✅
B. Second student: because 2=2, x=0
C. Both: x=0 is one of infinite solutions
D. Neither: no solution

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Simplify LHS: \(2x+2\). RHS: \(2x+2\). Both sides identical, so any x works. Identity, infinite solutions. First student correct. Second student incorrectly thinks the constant term 2=2 implies x=0. Verification: substitute any number, e.g., x=5 gives 12=12, true. So option A. B is wrong; C misleading; D false.

Q15. A business has a fixed cost of $500 and variable cost of $2 per unit. They sell each unit for $7. How many units must they sell to break even? A student solves \(500+2x=7x\) and gets \(x=100\). Another solves \(500=7x-2x\) and gets \(x=100\). Which method is correct, and what does the break-even point represent?

A. Both methods are correct; break-even means revenue equals cost ✅
B. Only first is correct; second misses variable cost
C. Only second is correct; first adds fixed cost twice
D. Neither; break-even occurs when profit is zero, so equation is \(7x-2x=500\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Break-even: total cost = total revenue. Cost = 500+2x, Revenue = 7x. So \(500+2x=7x\) => 5x=500 => x=100. Second equation \(500=7x-2x\) is same as \(500=5x\) after subtracting 2x from both sides, so both are correct. Break-even means profit zero (revenue - cost = 0). Option A correct. B and C wrongly dismiss second; D is also correct but not exclusive—both methods are valid.
