What is How to Use interval notation?

Definition:
Interval notation is a concise way to represent the solution set of an inequality using brackets and parentheses. Square brackets \([ \, ]\) indicate inclusion of an endpoint (\(\leq\) or \(\geq\)), while parentheses \(( \, )\) indicate exclusion (\(<\) or \(>\)). Infinity (\(\infty\)) always uses a parenthesis.

Working:
For \(x > 2\), write \((2, \infty)\). For \(x \leq 5\), write \((-\infty, 5]\). For \(-3 < x \leq 4\), write \((-3, 4]\).

Example:
If solution is \(y < -1\) or \(y \geq 3\), interval notation is \((-\infty, -1) \cup [3, \infty)\).

Reason:
Interval notation is compact and widely used in higher mathematics to describe solution sets efficiently.

📝 All How to Use interval notation MCQs

Q1. Which interval notation correctly represents all real numbers greater than -3 but less than or equal to 5?

A.\([-3,5]\)
B.\((-3,5]\) ✅
C.\([-3,5)\)
D.\((-3,5)\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The phrase greater than \(-3\) excludes \(-3\), so a parenthesis is required there. The phrase less than or equal to 5 includes 5, so a bracket is required there. Therefore, \((-3,5]\) is correct.

Q2. A student writes \(x\geq2\) as \((2,\infty)\). What is the most important error in the student's notation?

A.The infinity symbol should be replaced by a bracket
B.The endpoint 2 should use a bracket because it is included ✅
C.The interval must begin at 0
D.The infinity symbol must use a bracket
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Because \(x\geq2\) includes 2, the endpoint must be represented with a bracket. The infinity symbol always uses a parenthesis because infinity is not an actual real number that can be included.

Q3. A temperature-control system allows temperatures from 18°C through 24°C, including both endpoints. Which interval represents the allowed temperatures?

A.\((18,24)\)
B.\([18,24)\)
C.\((18,24]\)
D.\([18,24]\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The wording through 24°C and from 18°C indicates that both endpoint temperatures are permitted. Included endpoints require brackets, so the correct interval is \([18,24]\).

Q4. A solution is described as all values \(x<-1\). Which interval notation correctly captures the solution?

A.\((-\infty,-1)\) ✅
B.\((-\infty,-1]\)
C.\([-1,\infty)\)
D.\((-1,\infty)\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The inequality \(x<-1\) contains every number to the left of \(-1\), but it does not include \(-1\). Thus a parenthesis is required at \(-1\), and infinity also requires a parenthesis.

Q5. A rental company charges a special rate when the rental duration \(t\) satisfies \(2<t\leq7\) days. Which interval should be used to represent the eligible durations?

A.\([2,7]\)
B.\((2,7]\) ✅
C.\([2,7)\)
D.\((2,7)\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The duration must be greater than 2, so 2 is excluded and receives a parenthesis. Since \(t\leq7\), 7 is included and receives a bracket. Therefore, \((2,7]\) is correct.

Q6. A number-line graph has an open circle at -4 and is shaded to the right. Which interval notation matches the graph?

A.\([-4,\infty)\)
B.\((-4,\infty)\) ✅
C.\((-\infty,-4)\)
D.\((-\infty,-4]\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An open circle means the endpoint is excluded, while shading to the right represents numbers greater than the endpoint. Therefore, the graph represents \(x>-4\), written as \((-4,\infty)\).

Q7. A number-line graph shows shading from -2 to 6, with a closed circle at -2 and an open circle at 6. What interval is represented?

A.\((-2,6)\)
B.\([-2,6]\)
C.\([-2,6)\) ✅
D.\((-2,6]\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The closed circle at -2 means -2 is included, while the open circle at 6 means 6 is excluded. Shading between them gives the interval \([-2,6)\).

Q8. A student converts \(x\leq3\) into \((-\infty,3)\). Another converts it into \((-\infty,3]\). Which reasoning best explains why the second conversion is correct?

A.Three is included, so a bracket is required ✅
B.Infinity must be written with a bracket
C.The inequality points toward positive numbers
D.A parenthesis is always required for finite numbers
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The symbol \(\leq\) means that 3 is part of the solution set. Therefore, the endpoint 3 must use a bracket. The interval extends indefinitely left, so infinity uses a parenthesis.

Q9. A machine operates safely when its pressure \(p\) is at least 40 psi but below 75 psi. Which interval represents the safe pressure range?

A.\([40,75)\) ✅
B.\((40,75]\)
C.\([40,75]\)
D.\((40,75)\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: At least 40 means 40 is included, requiring a bracket. Below 75 means 75 is excluded, requiring a parenthesis. Hence the safe range is \([40,75)\).

Q10. Which interval represents \(x<-5\) or \(x\geq2\)?

A.\((-\infty,-5) \cup [2,\infty)\) ✅
B.\((-\infty,-5] \cup (2,\infty)\)
C.\([-5,2)\)
D.\((-5,2]\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The first inequality contains numbers strictly less than -5, giving \((-\infty,-5)\). The second contains 2 and everything greater, giving \([2,\infty)\). Since the regions are separate, union notation combines them.

Q11. A student claims that \([-3,4]\) represents \(x>-3\) and \(x<4\). Which correction is necessary?

A.Both endpoints should be parentheses ✅
B.Only -3 should use a parenthesis
C.Only 4 should use a parenthesis
D.The interval is correct as written
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Both inequalities are strict: \(x>-3\) excludes -3, and \(x<4\) excludes 4. Therefore, both endpoints require parentheses, giving \((-3,4)\), not \([-3,4]\).

Q12. A scholarship requires a test score \(s\) of at least 70 but less than 90. A second program accepts scores from 85 through 100, including 100. Which interval represents scores eligible for either program?

A.\([70,90) \cup [85,100]\) ✅
B.\([70,85) \cup (90,100]\)
C.\((70,90] \cup [85,100)\)
D.\([70,90] \cup (85,100]\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The first program accepts \([70,90)\), while the second accepts \([85,100]\). Because students can qualify for either program, the union of the two intervals represents the complete eligible set. Overlap is allowed.

Q13. A graph represents \(x\geq-1\). A student writes \([-1,\infty]\). What single notation rule makes the student's answer invalid?

A.The finite endpoint cannot use a bracket
B.The infinity endpoint must use a parenthesis ✅
C.Negative endpoints cannot use brackets
D.Intervals cannot contain infinity
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The value \(-1\) is included, so its bracket is correct. However, infinity is not a real endpoint and cannot be included in an interval. Infinity must always be paired with a parenthesis, giving \([-1,\infty)\).

Q14. A delivery service accepts package weights greater than 2 kg and no more than 10 kg. A customer argues that \([2,10)\) is correct because both numbers mark boundaries. What is the best evaluation?

A.Correct, because boundaries always use brackets
B.Correct, because 2 and 10 are both possible weights
C.Incorrect, because 2 is excluded and 10 is included ✅
D.Incorrect, because both endpoints must be excluded
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The phrase greater than 2 excludes 2, requiring a parenthesis. The phrase no more than 10 includes 10, requiring a bracket. Thus the correct interval is \((2,10]\), not \([2,10)\).

Q15. Which interval notation is equivalent to all real numbers except \(x=4\)?

A.\((-\infty,4) \cup (4,\infty)\) ✅
B.\((-\infty,4] \cup [4,\infty)\)
C.\([-\infty,4) \cup (4,\infty]\)
D.\((-\infty,4) \cup [4,\infty)\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: All real numbers less than 4 form \((-\infty,4)\), while all real numbers greater than 4 form \((4,\infty)\). Because 4 itself is excluded, both intervals use parentheses at that point, and union combines them.
