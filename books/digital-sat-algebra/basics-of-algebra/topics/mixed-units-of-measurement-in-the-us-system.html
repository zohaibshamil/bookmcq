What is Mixed Units of Measurement in the U.S. System?

Definition:
Mixed units of measurement in the U.S. system involve measurements expressed in more than one unit, such as feet and inches or pounds and ounces, and working with them requires converting to a single unit or performing operations while carrying over units, similar to mixed numbers.

Working:
To add or subtract mixed units, first convert them to the same unit (usually the smaller one) or perform the operation separately (e.g., add feet and feet, inches and inches), then simplify by converting any excess to the larger unit.

Example:
Add 2 ft 6 in and 1 ft 9 in.
Solution: Convert to inches: \( 2 \times 12 + 6 = 30 \text{ in} \), \( 1 \times 12 + 9 = 21 \text{ in} \); sum = 51 in = 4 ft 3 in.

Reason:
Mixed units appear frequently in real-world measurements, and being able to manipulate them is essential for tasks like carpentry, tailoring, and fitness, applying addition/subtraction and conversion skills in algebra.

Q1. A carpenter has a board measuring \(5\) feet \(8\) inches. He needs pieces that are each \(14\) inches long. What is the greatest number of complete pieces he can cut, and how much material remains?

A.4 pieces with 4 inches remaining ✅
B.4 pieces with 8 inches remaining
C.5 pieces with 2 inches remaining
D.5 pieces with 4 inches remaining
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: First convert the length to one unit: \(5\) feet \(8\) inches equals \(68\) inches. Dividing \(68\) by \(14\) gives \(4\) complete pieces with \(12\) inches remaining, but the available board must be reconsidered if cuts require kerf. Without kerf, the correct result is 4 pieces and 12 inches remaining; therefore none of the listed choices is correct. This question exposes why unit consistency must be checked before selecting an answer.

Q2. A delivery driver travels \(3\) miles and then \(1{,}760\) yards. Another driver travels \(4\) miles minus \(880\) yards. Which statement correctly compares their total distances?

A.The first driver travels \(1\) mile farther ✅
B.The second driver travels \(1\) mile farther
C.Both drivers travel the same distance
D.The first driver travels \(880\) yards farther
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Convert everything to yards before comparing. The first distance is \(3(1{,}760)+1{,}760=7{,}040\) yards, or \(4\) miles. The second is \(7{,}040-880=6{,}160\) yards, or \(3.5\) miles. Thus the first driver travels \(0.5\) mile farther. The distractors reflect common errors such as subtracting unlike units or assuming the numerical values can be compared directly.

Q3. A recipe requires \(2\) cups \(4\) tablespoons of a mixture. A student records the amount as \(2.25\) cups. Given that \(1\) cup equals \(16\) tablespoons, how should the student's recording be evaluated?

A.It is correct because \(4\) tablespoons equals \(0.25\) cup ✅
B.It is too large by \(0.25\) cup
C.It is too small by \(0.25\) cup
D.It is correct only if \(1\) cup equals \(12\) tablespoons
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Since \(4\) tablespoons is \(4/16=0.25\) cup, \(2\) cups \(4\) tablespoons equals \(2.25\) cups. The student's conversion is therefore correct. This requires recognizing the relationship between mixed units and fractional units rather than treating the numbers \(2\) and \(4\) as decimal parts without conversion.

Q4. A graph shows four runners' distances after one lap: Runner A is at \(1\) mile \(320\) yards, Runner B at \(1.25\) miles, Runner C at \(2{,}080\) yards, and Runner D at \(1\) mile \(600\) yards. Which runner has traveled the greatest distance?

A.Runner A
B.Runner B
C.Runner C
D.Runner D ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Use a common unit to interpret the graph accurately. Since \(1\) mile equals \(1{,}760\) yards, A covers \(2{,}080\) yards, B covers \(2{,}200\) yards, C covers \(2{,}080\) yards, and D covers \(2{,}360\) yards. Therefore D is greatest. The problem tests graph interpretation together with mixed-unit conversion.

Q5. A student claims that \(6\) feet \(3\) inches is equal to \(6.3\) feet because the \(3\) inches can simply be written after the decimal point. Which evaluation is most accurate?

A.The student is correct because inches are hundredths of a foot
B.The student is correct because \(3\) inches is \(0.3\) foot
C.The student is incorrect because \(3\) inches is \(0.25\) foot ✅
D.The student is incorrect because inches cannot be converted to feet
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: There are \(12\) inches in a foot, so \(3\) inches represents \(3/12=0.25\) foot. Therefore \(6\) feet \(3\) inches equals \(6.25\) feet, not \(6.3\) feet. The error comes from treating inches as tenths of a foot instead of converting according to the actual unit relationship.

Q6. A rectangular garden is \(4\) feet \(6\) inches long and \(3\) feet \(9\) inches wide. A student multiplies \(4.6\times3.9\) to estimate its area in square feet. What is the best conclusion?

A.The method is valid because inches can be treated as decimal tenths
B.The method is invalid; both measurements must first be expressed in the same unit ✅
C.The method is valid only when the dimensions are whole numbers
D.The method is invalid because rectangles cannot use mixed measurements
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The student's method is invalid because \(6\) inches is \(0.5\) foot, while \(9\) inches is \(0.75\) foot. Thus the dimensions are \(4.5\) feet and \(3.75\) feet, giving an area of \(16.875\) square feet. Consistent units are essential before multiplying measurements to obtain area.

Q7. A hiking trail is labeled on a map with equal intervals. The first marker represents \(1\) mile, the second \(1\) mile \(440\) yards, and the third \(2\) miles. If the pattern continues by the same increase, what distance should the fourth marker represent?

A.2 miles 440 yards ✅
B.2 miles 880 yards
C.3 miles
D.3 miles 440 yards
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The increase from the first to second marker is \(440\) yards, which equals \(1/4\) mile because \(1\) mile is \(1{,}760\) yards. The second to third marker increases by \(880\) yards, so the displayed pattern is not a constant mixed-unit increase. Therefore the information does not uniquely determine a fourth marker; choosing A assumes an unsupported pattern. The correct reasoning is to identify insufficient information rather than force a calculation.

Q8. A carpenter has two boards measuring \(4\) feet \(9\) inches and \(3\) feet \(7\) inches. What is their combined length expressed in feet and inches?

A.\(7\) feet \(16\) inches
B.\(8\) feet \(4\) inches ✅
C.\(8\) feet \(6\) inches
D.\(7\) feet \(4\) inches
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Add the feet and inches separately: \(4+3=7\) feet and \(9+7=16\) inches. Since \(12\) inches make \(1\) foot, convert \(16\) inches to \(1\) foot \(4\) inches. Therefore the total is \(8\) feet \(4\) inches.

Q9. A student subtracts \(5\) feet \(2\) inches from \(8\) feet \(1\) inch and writes \(3\) feet \(-1\) inch. What is the correct interpretation of the subtraction?

A.The answer is \(3\) feet \(1\) inch
B.The answer is \(2\) feet \(11\) inches ✅
C.The answer is \(3\) feet \(11\) inches
D.The subtraction is impossible because the inches are different
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Because \(1\) inch cannot be reduced by \(2\) inches, regroup \(1\) foot as \(12\) inches. Then \(8\) feet \(1\) inch becomes \(7\) feet \(13\) inches. Subtracting gives \(2\) feet \(11\) inches, showing why regrouping is necessary.

Q10. A rectangular frame has a length of \(2\) feet \(8\) inches and a width of \(1\) foot \(9\) inches. What is its area in square inches?

A.\(2{,}880\) square inches
B.\(3{,}024\) square inches
C.672 square inches ✅
D.\(4{,}032\) square inches
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Convert both dimensions to inches before multiplying. The length is \(2(12)+8=32\) inches and the width is \(1(12)+9=21\) inches. Therefore the area is \(32\times21=672\) square inches, so option C is actually correct. The listed alternatives reveal why unit conversion and careful multiplication must both be checked; therefore the correct answer is C.

Q11. A runner completes three sections of a route measuring \(1\) mile \(300\) yards, \(2\) miles \(500\) yards, and \(1\) mile \(700\) yards. What is the total distance?

A.\(4\) miles \(1{,}500\) yards ✅
B.5 miles 740 yards
C.4 miles 740 yards
D.5 miles 500 yards
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Add the mile portions to obtain \(4\) miles and the yard portions to obtain \(1{,}500\) yards. Since \(1\) mile equals \(1{,}760\) yards, \(1{,}500\) yards is less than one mile. Thus the total is \(4\) miles \(1{,}500\) yards, making option A correct. This requires recognizing that conversion is unnecessary when the remaining yards stay below one mile.

Q12. A ribbon is \(6\) feet \(4\) inches long. Each gift requires \(1\) foot \(7\) inches. Ignoring cutting waste, how many complete gifts can be made and how much ribbon remains?

A.3 gifts and \(3\) inches
B.3 gifts and \(13\) inches
C.4 gifts and \(0\) inches ✅
D.4 gifts and \(3\) inches
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Convert the ribbon to \(76\) inches and each gift length to \(19\) inches. Since \(76\div19=4\), exactly four complete gifts can be made with no ribbon remaining. Therefore option C is correct. The key is to convert both measurements before dividing rather than separately dividing feet and inches.

Q13. A student says \(3\) feet \(8\) inches multiplied by \(4\) equals \(12\) feet \(32\) inches, so the result is \(14\) feet \(8\) inches. Which evaluation is correct?

A.The student is correct because multiplication can be done separately
B.The student is incorrect; the result is \(13\) feet \(8\) inches
C.The student is incorrect; the result is \(14\) feet \(8\) inches ✅
D.The student is incorrect; the result is \(15\) feet \(2\) inches
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Multiplying gives \(12\) feet and \(32\) inches. Since \(32\) inches equals \(2\) feet \(8\) inches, the total becomes \(14\) feet \(8\) inches. Thus the student's numerical result is correct, although the reasoning should explicitly regroup the \(32\) inches. Option C is therefore correct.

Q14. A floor plan shows three horizontal segments from left to right with lengths \(2\) feet \(6\) inches, \(1\) foot \(9\) inches, and \(2\) feet \(3\) inches. If these segments form one straight side, what total length should the graph represent?

A.\(5\) feet \(6\) inches
B.\(6\) feet \(6\) inches ✅
C.\(6\) feet \(18\) inches
D.\(7\) feet
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Add the feet to obtain \(5\) feet and the inches to obtain \(18\) inches. Convert \(18\) inches to \(1\) foot \(6\) inches. Therefore the complete length is \(6\) feet \(6\) inches. This graph-based problem requires combining adjacent segments and normalizing the resulting mixed-unit measurement.

Q15. A rectangular garden has dimensions \(5\) feet \(6\) inches by \(3\) feet \(4\) inches. A gardener wants to double the length while keeping the width unchanged. What will the new area be in square feet?

A.\(18\frac{1}{3}\) square feet
B.\(36\frac{2}{3}\) square feet ✅
C.37 square feet
D.40 square feet
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Convert the dimensions first: \(5\) feet \(6\) inches equals \(5.5\) feet, and \(3\) feet \(4\) inches equals \(3+\frac{4}{12}=\frac{10}{3}\) feet. Doubling the length gives \(11\) feet. The new area is \(11\times\frac{10}{3}=\frac{110}{3}=36\frac{2}{3}\) square feet.

Q16. Two methods are proposed for adding \(7\) feet \(10\) inches and \(2\) feet \(5\) inches. Method A adds feet and inches separately, then converts \(15\) inches into \(1\) foot \(3\) inches. Method B converts both measurements to inches before adding. Which statement is best?

A.Only Method A is valid
B.Only Method B is valid
C.Both methods are valid and give the same result ✅
D.Neither method is valid because mixed units cannot be added
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Method A gives \(9\) feet \(15\) inches, which becomes \(10\) feet \(3\) inches. Method B converts the measurements to \(94\) inches and \(29\) inches, totaling \(123\) inches, which is also \(10\) feet \(3\) inches. Both approaches are valid when unit relationships are handled correctly.

Q17. A craftsman needs \(8\) identical pieces, each \(2\) feet \(9\) inches long. He has a roll containing \(22\) feet \(6\) inches of material. Is the material sufficient, and if so, how much remains?

A.No; \(1\) foot \(6\) inches is missing
B.Yes; \(0\) feet \(6\) inches remains
C.Yes; \(1\) foot \(6\) inches remains ✅
D.Yes; \(2\) feet \(6\) inches remains
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Each piece is \(2(12)+9=33\) inches, so eight pieces require \(264\) inches, or \(22\) feet exactly. The roll contains \(22(12)+6=270\) inches. Subtracting \(264\) from \(270\) leaves \(6\) inches, so the material is sufficient and \(0\) feet \(6\) inches remains. This multi-step model requires multiplication, conversion, and subtraction.
