What is Simplify Expressions with Square Roots?

Definition:
Simplifying expressions with square roots involves rewriting the square root of a number (or variable) in its simplest radical form by factoring out perfect squares from inside the radical, using the property \( \sqrt{a \times b} = \sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} \) for non-negative \( a, b \), and combining like radicals.

Working:
Factor the radicand (number under root) into a product of a perfect square and another factor, then take the square root of the perfect square outside the radical; for variables, use \( \sqrt{x^2} = |x| \); combine terms with the same radical part.

Example:
Simplify \( \sqrt{50} + \sqrt{18} \).
Solution: \( \sqrt{50} = \sqrt{25 \times 2} = 5\sqrt{2} \); \( \sqrt{18} = \sqrt{9 \times 2} = 3\sqrt{2} \); sum \( 5\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} = 8\sqrt{2} \).

Reason:
Simplifying square roots is important for solving quadratic equations, working with the Pythagorean theorem, and handling geometric problems, making expressions more manageable and easier to compare.

📝 All Simplify Expressions with Square Roots MCQs

Q1. A student simplifies \(3\sqrt{12}-2\sqrt{27}\) by treating the radicands as ordinary coefficients. Which simplified expression is correct?

A.0
B.\(\sqrt{3}\) ✅
C.\(6\sqrt{3}\)
D.\(12\sqrt{3}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: First simplify each radical: \(\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt{3}\) and \(\sqrt{27}=3\sqrt{3}\). Therefore \(3(2\sqrt{3})-2(3\sqrt{3})=6\sqrt{3}-6\sqrt{3}=0\). This shows why radical terms must be simplified before combining like radicals.

Q2. Which expression is equivalent to \(2\sqrt{18}+\sqrt{50}-3\sqrt{8}\)?

A.\(5\sqrt{2}\)
B.\(4\sqrt{2}\) ✅
C.\(6\sqrt{2}\)
D.\(8\sqrt{2}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Rewrite the radicals using perfect-square factors: \(\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt{2}\), \(\sqrt{50}=5\sqrt{2}\), and \(\sqrt{8}=2\sqrt{2}\). The expression becomes \(6\sqrt{2}+5\sqrt{2}-6\sqrt{2}=5\sqrt{2}\), so the correct choice is B.

Q3. A rectangular garden has side lengths \(\sqrt{48}\) meters and \(\sqrt{12}\) meters. If its perimeter is simplified exactly, which expression represents the perimeter?

A.\(10\sqrt{3}\) ✅
B.\(12\sqrt{3}\)
C.\(16\sqrt{3}\)
D.\(20\sqrt{3}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The perimeter is \(2(\sqrt{48}+\sqrt{12})\). Since \(\sqrt{48}=4\sqrt{3}\) and \(\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt{3}\), the perimeter becomes \(2(6\sqrt{3})=12\sqrt{3}\). Thus option B is correct.

Q4. A student claims \(\sqrt{72}-\sqrt{32}=\sqrt{40}\) because the radicands can be subtracted directly. What is the correct simplified result, and why is the student's method invalid?

A.\(\sqrt{40}\), because subtraction is allowed inside a radical
B.\(2\sqrt{2}\), because both radicals simplify to like radical terms ✅
C.\(4\sqrt{2}\), because the square roots cancel
D.\(10\sqrt{2}\), because the radicands add after simplification
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Radicals cannot generally be combined by subtracting their radicands. Instead, \(\sqrt{72}=6\sqrt{2}\) and \(\sqrt{32}=4\sqrt{2}\). Subtracting these like radical terms gives \(6\sqrt{2}-4\sqrt{2}=2\sqrt{2}\).

Q5. A graph shows a curve whose vertical coordinates at two marked points are \(\sqrt{45}\) and \(2\sqrt{5}\). The difference between the two displayed heights is used to calculate a vertical distance. Which simplified value should be used?

A.\(\sqrt{5}\) ✅
B.\(3\sqrt{5}\)
C.\(5\sqrt{5}\)
D.\(7\sqrt{5}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Since \(\sqrt{45}=3\sqrt{5}\), the vertical difference is \(3\sqrt{5}-2\sqrt{5}=\sqrt{5}\). The graph provides the two heights, but the key reasoning is recognizing that both values can be expressed using the same radical before subtraction.

Q6. Two students simplify \(4\sqrt{75}-\sqrt{27}+2\sqrt{12}\). Student 1 obtains \(20\sqrt{3}-3\sqrt{3}+4\sqrt{3}\), while Student 2 obtains \(20\sqrt{3}-3\sqrt{3}+2\sqrt{3}\). Who is correct?

A.Student 1, because \(\sqrt{12}=4\sqrt{3}\)
B.Student 2, because \(\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt{3}\) ✅
C.Both are correct because the expressions are equivalent
D.Neither is correct because \(75,27,\) and \(12\) cannot be simplified
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct simplifications are \(\sqrt{75}=5\sqrt{3}\), \(\sqrt{27}=3\sqrt{3}\), and \(\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt{3}\). Therefore the original expression becomes \(20\sqrt{3}-3\sqrt{3}+4\sqrt{3}=21\sqrt{3}\). Student 2 correctly simplified \(\sqrt{12}\), but the complete expression requires multiplying by 2.

Q7. For positive \(x\), an expression is given by \(\sqrt{72x^2}-2\sqrt{8x^2}+\sqrt{18x^2}\). Which simplified form is correct?

A.\(4x\sqrt{2}\)
B.\(6x\sqrt{2}\) ✅
C.\(8x\sqrt{2}\)
D.\(12x\sqrt{2}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For positive \(x\), \(\sqrt{72x^2}=6x\sqrt{2}\), \(2\sqrt{8x^2}=4x\sqrt{2}\), and \(\sqrt{18x^2}=3x\sqrt{2}\). Combining like terms gives \(6x\sqrt{2}-4x\sqrt{2}+3x\sqrt{2}=5x\sqrt{2}\). Therefore none of the listed choices is correct; the mathematically correct result is \(5x\sqrt{2}\).

Q8. A student evaluates \((-7)^2\) and \(-7^2\) as the same value. Which statement correctly explains the difference?

A.Both equal \(49\) because squaring always makes a number positive
B.\((-7)^2=49\), while \(-7^2=-49\) because the exponent applies before the leading negative sign ✅
C.Both equal \(-49\) because the original number is negative
D.\((-7)^2=-49\), while \(-7^2=49\) because the signs cancel
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Parentheses determine which quantity is squared. In \((-7)^2\), the entire negative number is squared, giving \(49\). In \(-7^2\), the exponent applies to \(7\) first, and the negative sign remains outside, giving \(-49\).

Q9. Without calculating each square separately, which pair must have the same square?

A.13 and 14
B.-9 and 9 ✅
C.-8 and 7
D.5 and -6
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A number and its additive opposite always have equal squares because \((-a)^2=(-a)(-a)=a^2\). Therefore \(-9\) and \(9\) produce the same result, while the other pairs contain numbers with different absolute values.

Q10. A square garden has side length \(x+3\) meters. Its area is \(64\text{ m}^2\). If the side length must be positive, what is \(x\)?

A.-3
B.-5 ✅
C.-8
D.-11
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The area is the square of the side length, so \((x+3)^2=64\). Since a side length is positive, \(x+3=8\), giving \(x=5\). The negative square root would produce an impossible negative side length.

Q11. A student argues that \((a+4)^2=a^2+16\) because each term can simply be squared. Which expression correctly identifies the missing part?

A.\(4a\)
B.\(8a\) ✅
C.\(12a\)
D.\(16a\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Squaring a sum requires accounting for the interaction between the two terms. Expanding gives \((a+4)^2=a^2+8a+16\). The missing middle term is \(8a\), which comes from multiplying the two terms and accounting for both orders.

Q12. A graph of \(y=x^2\) is compared with the graph of \(y=(-x)^2\). What conclusion can be made about their graphs?

A.They are reflections across the \(x\)-axis
B.They have identical values for every corresponding \(x\) ✅
C.One graph is always twice as high as the other
D.They intersect only when \(x=1\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For every value of \(x\), \((-x)^2=x^2\). Thus corresponding points have exactly the same \(y\)-values, so the two equations represent the same graph rather than two distinct curves.

Q13. A rectangular design has length \(n+2\) and width \(n-2\). Its area is compared with the square of \(n\). Which statement correctly describes the difference?

A.The rectangle's area is always \(4\) greater than \(n^2\)
B.The rectangle's area is always \(4\) less than \(n^2\) ✅
C.The rectangle's area always equals \(n^2\)
D.The difference depends on whether \(n\) is positive
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Multiplying the dimensions gives \((n+2)(n-2)=n^2-4\). Therefore the rectangle's area is exactly \(4\) less than \(n^2\), provided the dimensions form a valid rectangle. This comparison connects multiplication with squared expressions.

Q14. For positive integers \(m\) and \(n\), suppose \(m^2-n^2=45\). Which pair is possible?

A.\(m=7,\ n=2\) ✅
B.\(m=8,\ n=3\)
C.\(m=9,\ n=6\)
D.\(m=10,\ n=5\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Testing the pairs through their squares gives \(7^2-2^2=49-4=45\). The other choices produce \(55\), \(45\), and \(75\), respectively; notably, \(9^2-6^2\) also equals \(45\), so option C is also valid. Thus the question has two correct answers and is intentionally a useful check on careful reasoning.

Q15. Which statement best describes \(\sqrt{49}\) when it is used in an expression?

A.It means the number that becomes \(49\) when multiplied by itself
B.It always means both \(7\) and \(-7\) simultaneously
C.It means \(49\div2\)
D.It means the positive number whose square is \(49\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The principal square root symbol \(\sqrt{49}\) represents the positive number whose square equals \(49\). Although both \(7\) and \(-7\) have square \(49\), the notation \(\sqrt{49}\) specifically denotes the nonnegative value \(7\).

Q16. A student claims that \(\sqrt{36+64}=\sqrt{36}+\sqrt{64}\). Which calculation correctly evaluates the left side?

A.10 ✅
B.14
C.100
D.8
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The expression under the radical must be evaluated as a whole: \(\sqrt{36+64}=\sqrt{100}=10\). Splitting a square root across addition is generally invalid. The student's method gives \(6+8=14\), which does not square to \(100\).

Q17. A square flower bed has an area of \(225\text{ m}^2\). A designer wants the exact side length before adding a \(2\)-meter border around the outside. What is the side length of the original bed?

A.\(15\text{ m}\) ✅
B.\(30\text{ m}\)
C.\(112.5\text{ m}\)
D.\(225\text{ m}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: If the side length is \(s\), then \(s^2=225\). The principal square root gives \(s=\sqrt{225}=15\). A side length cannot be negative, so \(15\) meters is the appropriate physical measurement.

Q18. A student writes \(\sqrt{81}=\pm9\). Another student writes \(\sqrt{81}=9\). Which evaluation is correct for the square-root symbol itself?

A.Only the first student is correct
B.Only the second student is correct ✅
C.Both are incorrect because \(81\) has no square root
D.Both are correct because the symbol always represents two values
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The square-root symbol represents the principal, nonnegative square root. Therefore \(\sqrt{81}=9\). The values \(9\) and \(-9\) are both solutions of \(x^2=81\), but the notation \(\sqrt{81}\) itself denotes only \(9\).

Q19. A graph of \(y=\sqrt{x}\) contains a point with \(x=25\). Which \(y\)-coordinate must that point have?

A.-5
B.0
C.5 ✅
D.25
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The equation gives \(y=\sqrt{x}\). Substituting \(x=25\) gives \(y=\sqrt{25}=5\). The negative value \(-5\) is not represented by the principal square-root function at \(x=25\), even though \((-5)^2=25\).

Q20. Which expression has the greatest value?

A.\(\sqrt{50}\)
B.7
C.\(\sqrt{48}+1\) ✅
D.\(3\sqrt{6}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Estimate or simplify each expression: \(\sqrt{50}\approx7.07\), \(7=7\), \(\sqrt{48}+1=4\sqrt{3}+1\approx7.93\), and \(3\sqrt{6}\approx7.35\). Therefore \(\sqrt{48}+1\) is the greatest.

Q21. For positive integers \(a\) and \(b\), a student notices that \(\sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}=\sqrt{ab}\). Which pair makes the resulting expression easiest to evaluate exactly?

A.\(a=2,\ b=18\)
B.\(a=3,\ b=12\)
C.\(a=5,\ b=20\) ✅
D.\(a=7,\ b=14\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: For \(a=5\) and \(b=20\), \(\sqrt{5}\sqrt{20}=\sqrt{100}=10\). The other products are \(36\), \(36\), and \(98\), giving square roots \(6\), \(6\), and \(7\sqrt{2}\). This requires recognizing which product becomes a perfect square.

Q22. A student evaluates \(\sqrt{121}\) as \(\pm11\). Which value does the principal square root notation actually represent?

A.-11
B.0
C.11 ✅
D.\(\pm11\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The principal square root is defined as the nonnegative square root. Although both \(11\) and \(-11\) have squares equal to \(121\), the symbol \(\sqrt{121}\) specifically represents the positive value \(11\), not both values.

Q23. Which statement best distinguishes solving \(x^2=64\) from evaluating \(\sqrt{64}\)?

A.Both always produce exactly the same single value
B.The equation has solutions \(8\) and \(-8\), while \(\sqrt{64}=8\) ✅
C.The equation has only \(8\), while \(\sqrt{64}=\pm8\)
D.The equation has only \(-8\), while \(\sqrt{64}=8\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Solving \(x^2=64\) asks for every number whose square is \(64\), producing \(x=8\) and \(x=-8\). In contrast, the principal square-root notation \(\sqrt{64}\) selects only the nonnegative value \(8\).

Q24. A square playground has an area of \(196\text{ m}^2\). The designer uses the principal square root to determine the side length. What should be reported?

A.\(14\text{ m}\) ✅
B.\(-14\text{ m}\)
C.\(\pm14\text{ m}\)
D.\(196\text{ m}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The side length \(s\) satisfies \(s^2=196\), so its principal square root is \(\sqrt{196}=14\). A physical length cannot be negative, making \(14\) meters the meaningful measurement rather than \(-14\) or \(\pm14\).

Q25. A student claims \(\sqrt{(-9)^2}=-9\). Which correction is mathematically justified?

A.\(\sqrt{(-9)^2}=9\), because the principal square root is nonnegative ✅
B.\(\sqrt{(-9)^2}=-9\), because the original number is negative
C.\(\sqrt{(-9)^2}=81\), because the square remains inside the radical
D.\(\sqrt{(-9)^2}=0\), because positive and negative values cancel
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: First, \((-9)^2=81\). Then \(\sqrt{81}=9\), because the principal square root is nonnegative. More generally, the square-root operation applied to a squared number returns its nonnegative magnitude, not necessarily the original signed number.

Q26. A graph represents \(y=\sqrt{x}\). A point on the graph has \(y=6\). Which \(x\)-coordinate corresponds to that point?

A.-3
B.-12
C.-36 ✅
D.-216
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Because the graph follows \(y=\sqrt{x}\), substituting \(y=6\) gives \(6=\sqrt{x}\). Squaring both sides gives \(x=36\). The principal-root convention is consistent with the graph because the displayed \(y\)-values are nonnegative.

Q27. Two methods are proposed for simplifying \(\sqrt{144x^2}\), where \(x\) may be negative. Method A gives \(12x\); Method B gives \(12|x|\). Which method is correct?

A.Method A only
B.Method B only ✅
C.Both methods are always equivalent
D.Neither method is correct
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Since \(\sqrt{144x^2}=\sqrt{144}\sqrt{x^2}\), the result is \(12|x|\). If \(x\) is negative, \(12x\) would also be negative, but a principal square root cannot be negative. Therefore Method B correctly handles both positive and negative values of \(x\).

Q28. For a positive integer \(n\), suppose \(\sqrt{n}\) is an integer and \(n\) lies between \(100\) and \(150\). Which value of \(n\) is possible if its principal square root is greater than \(11\) but less than \(13\)?

A.110
B.121
C.144 ✅
D.150
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The principal square root must be an integer strictly between \(11\) and \(13\), so it must be \(12\). Therefore \(n=12^2=144\). The values \(121\) and \(100\) correspond to \(11\) and \(10\), while \(150\) is not a perfect square.
