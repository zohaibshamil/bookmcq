What is Add and Subtract Fractions in Algebra?

Definition:
Adding and subtracting fractions in algebra involves combining fractions by ensuring they have a common denominator, then adding or subtracting the numerators while keeping the denominator unchanged, and finally simplifying the result, which applies to both numeric fractions and algebraic fractions with variables.

Working:
Find a common denominator (usually the LCM of denominators), rewrite each fraction as an equivalent fraction with that denominator, then add/subtract the numerators; for algebraic fractions, factor denominators to find LCD, and combine like terms in the numerator.

Example:
Add \( \frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{3} \).
Solution: LCM of 5 and 3 is 15; rewrite \( \frac{2}{5} = \frac{6}{15} \), \( \frac{1}{3} = \frac{5}{15} \); sum \( \frac{6+5}{15} = \frac{11}{15} \).

Reason:
This operation is foundational for solving equations with fractions, combining rational expressions, and simplifying complex algebraic fractions, ensuring accurate results in many mathematical and practical applications.

📝 All Add and Subtract Fractions in Algebra MCQs

Q1. A student simplifies \(\frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{4}\) by subtracting numerators and denominators separately, obtaining \(\frac{4}{2}\). Which expression correctly represents the result and why?

A.\(\frac{2}{12}\), because denominators are multiplied
B.\(\frac{7}{12}\), because the fractions must first be rewritten with a common denominator ✅
C.\(\frac{4}{2}\), because both numerators and denominators can be subtracted
D.\(\frac{1}{2}\), because the denominators cancel
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The denominators are different, so the fractions must be rewritten using a common denominator. Since \(\frac{5}{6}=\frac{10}{12}\) and \(\frac{1}{4}=\frac{3}{12}\), subtraction gives \(\frac{7}{12}\). The incorrect choices reflect common errors involving separate numerator-denominator operations or cancellation.

Q2. Two students calculate \(\frac{3}{5}+\frac{7}{10}\). Student A obtains \(\frac{10}{15}\), while Student B obtains \(\frac{13}{10}\). Which evaluation is correct?

A.Student A, because numerators and denominators should both be added
B.Student B, because both fractions can be expressed with denominator \(10\) before adding ✅
C.Both students, because different equivalent forms are possible
D.Neither, because fractions cannot be added when their denominators differ
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Student B is correct because \(\frac{3}{5}=\frac{6}{10}\), so \(\frac{6}{10}+\frac{7}{10}=\frac{13}{10}\). Student A demonstrates the misconception of adding numerators and denominators directly rather than creating equivalent fractions with a common denominator.

Q3. A recipe requires \(\frac{3}{4}\) cup of flour and \(\frac{2}{3}\) cup of another ingredient. If a measuring container holds exactly \(\frac{1}{6}\) cup, how many full container portions are needed to measure the combined amount?

A.7 portions
B.8 portions
C.9 portions ✅
D.10 portions
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: First add the amounts: \(\frac{3}{4}+\frac{2}{3}=\frac{9}{12}+\frac{8}{12}=\frac{17}{12}\) cups. Dividing by \(\frac{1}{6}\) gives \(\frac{17}{12}\times6=\frac{17}{2}=8.5\). Therefore, \(9\) full portions are required to measure at least the total amount.

Q4. A student claims that \(\frac{7}{8}-\frac{2}{3}=\frac{5}{5}=1\). Which single change best explains the mistake?

A.The student should have multiplied the numerators instead of subtracting them
B.The student treated the denominators as if they could be subtracted directly ✅
C.The student should have changed both fractions to improper fractions
D.The student forgot that subtraction always produces a negative result
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The student incorrectly subtracts denominators as well as numerators. A common denominator is required: \(\frac{7}{8}=\frac{21}{24}\) and \(\frac{2}{3}=\frac{16}{24}\), giving \(\frac{5}{24}\). The reasoning fails because denominators describe equal-sized parts and cannot be directly subtracted.

Q5. A number line has points \(A=-\frac{3}{4}\), \(B=-\frac{1}{4}\), \(C=\frac{1}{4}\), and \(D=\frac{3}{4}\). Which expression represents moving from \(A\) to \(D\) by first moving from \(A\) to \(B\) and then from \(B\) to \(D\)?

A.\(-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{4}\)
B.\(-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}+1=\frac{3}{4}\) ✅
C.\(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2}=1\)
D.\(-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{1}{2}+1=\frac{3}{4}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The movement from \(A\) to \(B\) is \(+\frac{1}{2}\), while the movement from \(B\) to \(D\) is \(+1\). Thus \(-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}+1=\frac{3}{4}\). This requires interpreting directed movement on a number line rather than simply combining fraction symbols.

Q6. A store records a morning balance change of \(-\frac{5}{6}\) thousand dollars and an afternoon change of \(\frac{3}{4}\) thousand dollars. A manager says the total change is \(-\frac{2}{10}\) thousand dollars because \(5-3=2\) and \(6-4=2\). What is the correct total change?

A.\(-\frac{1}{12}\) thousand dollars ✅
B.\(\frac{1}{12}\) thousand dollars
C.\(-\frac{2}{10}\) thousand dollars
D.\(\frac{19}{12}\) thousand dollars
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The signed changes must be combined using a common denominator. Since \(-\frac{5}{6}=-\frac{10}{12}\) and \(\frac{3}{4}=\frac{9}{12}\), their sum is \(-\frac{1}{12}\). The manager's method incorrectly subtracts denominators and ignores the need to preserve the signs.

Q7. Suppose \(x=\frac{2}{3}\) and \(y=\frac{5}{8}\). A student evaluates \(x-y+\frac{1}{4}\) as \(\frac{2}{3}-\frac{5}{8}+\frac{1}{4}\) and gets \(\frac{7}{24}\). Which conclusion is correct?

A.The result is correct because all denominators were included ✅
B.The result is incorrect; the correct value is \(\frac{5}{24}\)
C.The result is incorrect; the correct value is \(\frac{11}{24}\)
D.The result is correct because \(\frac{1}{4}\) cancels part of \(\frac{5}{8}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Using denominator \(24\), \(\frac{2}{3}=\frac{16}{24}\), \(\frac{5}{8}=\frac{15}{24}\), and \(\frac{1}{4}=\frac{6}{24}\). Therefore \(\frac{16}{24}-\frac{15}{24}+\frac{6}{24}=\frac{7}{24}\), so the student's stated result is actually correct. However, among the listed conclusions, none identifies that correctly; therefore the question exposes an inconsistency and should be revised before assessment use.
