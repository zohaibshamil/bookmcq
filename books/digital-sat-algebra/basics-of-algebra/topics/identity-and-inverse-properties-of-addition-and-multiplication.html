What is Identity and Inverse Properties of Addition and Multiplication?

Definition:
The identity properties state that adding 0 0 0 to a number leaves it unchanged (additive identity: a+0=a a+0=a a+0=a) and multiplying by 1 1 1 leaves it unchanged (multiplicative identity: a×1=a a \times 1 = a a×1=a); the inverse properties state that adding the opposite gives 0 0 0 (additive inverse: a+(−a)=0 a+(-a)=0 a+(−a)=0) and multiplying by the reciprocal gives 1 1 1 (multiplicative inverse: a×1a=1 a \times \frac{1}{a} = 1 a×a1​=1, for a≠0 a \neq 0 a=0).

Working:
Use identity properties to simplify expressions by recognizing +0 +0 +0 or ×1 \times 1 ×1 as neutral; use inverse properties to cancel terms or factors when solving equations, such as adding/subtracting to isolate variables or multiplying/dividing to remove coefficients.

Example:
Solve x+5=0 x + 5 = 0 x+5=0 and 3x=6 3x = 6 3x=6.
Solution: For first, add inverse of 5: x=−5 x = -5 x=−5; for second, multiply by inverse of 3: x=2 x = 2 x=2.

Reason:
These properties are essential for solving linear equations, as they allow us to isolate the variable by undoing" operations, and they form the basis for algebraic manipulation in all areas."
