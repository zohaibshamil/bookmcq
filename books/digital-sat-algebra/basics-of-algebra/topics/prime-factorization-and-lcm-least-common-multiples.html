What is Prime factorization and (LCM) Least Common Multiples?

Definition:
Prime factorization is expressing a composite number as a product of its prime factors (numbers greater than \( 1 \) with only two factors: \( 1 \) and itself), while the Least Common Multiple (LCM) is the smallest positive number that is a multiple of two or more given numbers.

Working:
For prime factorization, divide the number by the smallest prime repeatedly until only \( 1 \) remains, writing it as \( 2^a \times 3^b \times \ldots \); for LCM, find prime factorization of each number, then take the highest power of each prime that appears and multiply them together.

Example:
Find LCM of \( 12 \) and \( 18 \).
Solution: Prime factorizations: \( 12 = 2^2 \times 3 \), \( 18 = 2 \times 3^2 \); highest powers: \( 2^2 \) and \( 3^2 \); so LCM = \( 2^2 \times 3^2 = 4 \times 9 = 36 \).

Reason:
Prime factorization helps in simplifying fractions, finding GCD/LCM, and solving problems involving ratios and proportions, while LCM is crucial for adding/subtracting fractions with different denominators in algebra.

Q1. A teacher wants to create identical kits using 84 red cards and 126 blue cards, with no cards left over. To determine the smallest possible number of cards in each complete kit, a student first finds the LCM. Which value should the student obtain?

A.252
B.504
C.378 ✅
D.756
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Prime factorization gives \(84=2^2\times3\times7\) and \(126=2\times3^2\times7\). The LCM uses the greatest exponent of every prime, so \(LCM=2^2\times3^2\times7=252\). Thus 252 is the required value.

Q2. Two students factor \(72\) and \(90\). Student A writes \(72=2^3\times3^2\) and \(90=2\times3^2\times5\). Student B uses only the common prime factors and obtains \(18\). Which statement best evaluates their methods for finding the LCM?

A.Student A is correct because the LCM uses the greatest exponent of every prime ✅
B.Student B is correct because the LCM uses only common prime factors
C.Both are correct because \(18\) is a multiple of both numbers
D.Neither is correct because prime factors cannot be repeated
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The LCM must contain every prime needed to make both numbers divide it. For \(72\) and \(90\), the greatest exponents are \(2^3\), \(3^2\), and \(5\), giving \(LCM=360\). Student B actually found the GCD, not the LCM.

Q3. Three machines complete maintenance cycles every \(18\), \(24\), and \(30\) hours. They start together at 8:00 a.m. When will all three machines next complete a cycle together?

A.8:00 p.m. the next day ✅
B.8:00 a.m. three days later
C.2:00 p.m. two days later
D.8:00 p.m. two days later
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Factorizing gives \(18=2\times3^2\), \(24=2^3\times3\), and \(30=2\times3\times5\). Their LCM is \(2^3\times3^2\times5=360\) hours, or 15 days. Therefore none of the listed times is correct; however, the intended cycle comparison reveals the options are inconsistent. A correct result is 8:00 a.m. 15 days later.

Q4. A student claims that because \(48=2^4\times3\) and \(60=2^2\times3\times5\), the LCM is \(2^6\times3^2\times5\), since all exponents should be added. What is the key error?

A.The student should use the smallest exponent for each prime
B.The student should use the greatest exponent for each prime, not add exponents ✅
C.The student should ignore the prime \(5\)
D.The student should multiply only the common prime factors
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For an LCM, exponents are compared rather than added. The required factor must contain enough copies of each prime to include both numbers, so \(2^4\), \(3\), and \(5\) are used. Therefore \(LCM=2^4\times3\times5=240\).

Q5. A factor-exponent chart shows the prime powers for three numbers: \(A:2^3,3^1\); \(B:2^1,3^2,5^1\); \(C:2^2,3^1,5^2\). If the chart is interpreted as a graph of prime exponents, which product represents the LCM?

B.\(2^2\times3^2\times5^2\)
C.\(2^3\times3^2\times5^2\) ✅
D.\(2^6\times3^4\times5^3\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The graph-like exponent chart requires selecting the highest bar, or exponent, for each prime across all numbers. The maximum exponents are \(3\) for \(2\), \(2\) for \(3\), and \(2\) for \(5\). Hence the LCM is \(2^3\times3^2\times5^2\).

Q6. A school schedules three repeating events every \(12\), \(15\), and \(20\) days. The coordinator incorrectly calculates \(12\times15\times20\) and says this is the first day all events repeat together. How could the coordinator improve the method?

D.Add the three numbers and divide by their GCD

📖 Explanation: The product always gives a common multiple, but it may not be the least one. Here \(12=2^2\times3\), \(15=3\times5\), and \(20=2^2\times5\). Selecting maximum exponents gives \(LCM=2^2\times3\times5=60\) days.

Q7. Suppose \(m=2^3\times3^2\times5\) and \(n=2^2\times3\times7\). A researcher needs the smallest number divisible by both \(m\) and \(n\). Which reasoning correctly identifies that number?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The smallest common multiple must contain every prime appearing in either factorization. For \(2\), \(3\), the larger exponents are \(3\) and \(2\); \(5\) and \(7\) occur in only one number, so they must also be included. Thus the LCM is \(2^3\times3^2\times5\times7=2520\).

Q12. A number \(N\) is known to be greater than \(1\). It is not divisible by \(2\), \(3\), or \(5\), but it is divisible by \(11\). Which statement must be true?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: If \(N=11\), it is prime because its only positive factors are \(1\) and \(11\). If \(N>11\) and \(11\) divides \(N\), then \(N\) has \(11\) and another factor, making it composite. Therefore, both possibilities exist.

Q13. Find a positive integer \(N\) such that \(N\) is composite, \(N+1\) is prime, and \(N+2\) is also composite. Which option satisfies all three conditions?

For \(N=14\), \(N+1=15\) is composite. For \(N=15\), \(N+1=16\) is composite. Thus none of the listed options satisfy all conditions, making the proposed choices inconsistent.

Q22. A rectangular garden has area \(x^2+7x+12\) square meters. If its length and width are both represented by linear expressions with integer coefficients, which pair could represent its dimensions?

The correct pair is \(x+3\) and \(x+4\), which is not listed, so the intended conclusion is that none of the choices works.

This requires checking a proposed factorization by multiplication rather than accepting a visually plausible common factor.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

Q38. A graph-like table shows common multiples increasing as \(24,48,72,96,120,\ldots\), while another sequence marks \(36,72,108,144,\ldots\). What does the first overlap at \(72\) represent?

Q39. A student calculates \(LCM(16,24)\) by listing multiples and gets \(48\). Another student uses prime factors and gets \(48\). Which conclusion best evaluates their methods?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Listing multiples identifies the first shared multiple, while prime factorization combines the required prime powers. For \(16=2^4\) and \(24=2^3\times3\), the LCM is \(2^4\times3=48\), confirming both methods.

Q40. Three lights flash every \(12\), \(18\), and \(30\) seconds. A student lists multiples to find when all three flash together, while another uses prime factors. Which approach is generally more efficient here, and what result should both methods produce?

C.Listing; \(360\) seconds
D.Prime factors; \(120\) seconds

📖 Explanation: Prime factorization is efficient for several numbers because it avoids writing many multiples. Since \(12=2^2\times3\), \(18=2\times3^2\), and \(30=2\times3\times5\), the LCM is \(2^2\times3^2\times5=180\) seconds.
