What is Prime factorization and (LCM) Least Common Multiples?

Definition:
Prime factorization is expressing a composite number as a product of its prime factors (numbers greater than 1 1 1 with only two factors: 1 1 1 and itself), while the Least Common Multiple (LCM) is the smallest positive number that is a multiple of two or more given numbers.

Working:
For prime factorization, divide the number by the smallest prime repeatedly until only 1 1 1 remains, writing it as 2a×3b×… 2^a \times 3^b \times \ldots 2a×3b×…; for LCM, find prime factorization of each number, then take the highest power of each prime that appears and multiply them together.

Example:
Find LCM of 12 12 12 and 18 18 18.
Solution: Prime factorizations: 12=22×3 12 = 2^2 \times 3 12=22×3, 18=2×32 18 = 2 \times 3^2 18=2×32; highest powers: 22 2^2 22 and 32 3^2 32; so LCM = 22×32=4×9=36 2^2 \times 3^2 = 4 \times 9 = 36 22×32=4×9=36.

Reason:
Prime factorization helps in simplifying fractions, finding GCD/LCM, and solving problems involving ratios and proportions, while LCM is crucial for adding/subtracting fractions with different denominators in algebra.
