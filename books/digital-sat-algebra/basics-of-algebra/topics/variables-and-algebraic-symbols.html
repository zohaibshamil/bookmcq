What is Variables and algebraic symbols?

Definition:
Variables are symbols, usually letters like \( x, y, \) or \( z \), that represent unknown or changing numerical values in algebraic expressions, while algebraic symbols include operators like \( +, -, \times, \div \), and grouping symbols such as parentheses, which together form mathematical statements.

Working:
A variable can take any value, and we manipulate these symbols using arithmetic operations; for example, in \( 3x + 2 \), \( x \) is the variable, \( 3 \) is the coefficient, and \( +2 \) is the constant term, and we can substitute different numbers for \( x \) to evaluate the expression.

Example:
If \( y = 4 \), evaluate \( 2y - 5 \).
Solution: Substitute \( y = 4 \): \( 2(4) - 5 = 8 - 5 = 3 \).

Reason:
Variables allow us to generalize arithmetic, write formulas, and solve equations for unknown quantities, making them essential for modeling real-world problems and describing relationships in science and engineering.

Q1. A school club charges a fixed registration fee of \(150\) rupees plus \(40\) rupees for each workshop attended. Which expression represents the total cost for a student attending \(w\) workshops, and what does \(40w\) represent?

A. \(150w+40\); the registration cost
B. \(150+40w\); the workshop cost ✅
C. \(190w\); the total fixed cost
D. \(150+40\); the number of workshops

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The fixed registration fee is paid once, so it is represented by \(150\). Each workshop adds \(40\) rupees, so \(w\) workshops cost \(40w\). Therefore, \(150+40w\) correctly models the situation.

Q2. A student writes \(3x+5=20\) to represent a number increased by \(5\), then multiplied by \(3\), but the intended situation is that the number is first increased by \(5\) and the result is multiplied by \(3\). Which equation correctly represents the situation?

A. \(3x+5=20\)
B. \(3(x+5)=20\) ✅
C. \(x+15=20\)
D. \(3(x-5)=20\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Parentheses are essential because the entire quantity \(x+5\) must be multiplied by \(3\). The expression \(3x+5\) multiplies only \(x\), which changes the order of operations and models a different situation.

Q3. A delivery service charges \(80\) rupees for the first kilometer and \(25\) rupees for every additional kilometer. If \(d\) is the total number of kilometers traveled, which expression correctly models the cost for \(d\ge1\)?

A. \(80+25d\)
B. \(80d+25\)
C. \(80+25(d-1)\) ✅
D. \(105d\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The first kilometer is already included in the \(80\)-rupee charge. Only the remaining \(d-1\) kilometers receive the \(25\)-rupee charge, giving \(80+25(d-1)\). This avoids charging the first kilometer twice.

Q4. A graph shows a straight line passing through \((0,50)\) and \((4,130)\), where \(x\) represents hours and \(y\) represents total savings. Which algebraic expression best represents the relationship?

A. \(y=20x+50\) ✅
B. \(y=50x+20\)
C. \(y=80x+50\)
D. \(y=20x+130\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The initial value is \(50\), so the constant term is \(50\). The rate is \((130-50)/(4-0)=20\), meaning savings increase by \(20\) per hour. Thus, \(y=20x+50\).

Q5. A student claims that \(4(a+3)\) and \(4a+3\) are equivalent because the \(4\) is next to both expressions. Which statement best evaluates the student's reasoning?

A. The student is correct because multiplication affects only the first term

D. The student is incorrect because \(4(a+3)=a+12\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The factor \(4\) multiplies every term inside the parentheses. Therefore, \(4(a+3)=4a+12\). The student's mistake is failing to distribute the multiplier to both \(a\) and \(3\).

Q6. Two students model the number of pencils in \(n\) identical boxes. Student A writes \(6n\), while Student B writes \(n+6\). Each box contains \(6\) pencils. Which conclusion is most accurate?

A. Student A is correct because \(6\) is multiplied by the number of boxes ✅
B. Student B is correct because \(6\) pencils are added once
C. Both are always equivalent
D. Neither expression uses a variable correctly

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

If each of \(n\) boxes contains \(6\) pencils, multiplication is required: \(6\times n=6n\). The expression \(n+6\) would describe adding six to the number of boxes, which is not the intended situation.

Q7. A rectangular garden has length \(x+4\) meters and width \(x\) meters. A student says its perimeter is \(2x+4\). Which expression gives the correct perimeter and best explains the error?

📖 Explanation:

Perimeter is twice the sum of the length and width: \(2[(x+4)+x]\). Simplifying gives \(4x+8\). The student's \(2x+4\) fails to double both dimensions and therefore underestimates the perimeter.

Q9. A student claims that in \(7x+14\), both \(7\) and \(14\) are variables because they are numbers appearing in an algebraic expression. Which response best evaluates the student's reasoning?

The symbol \(x\) can represent changing values, so it is the variable. The numbers \(7\) and \(14\) have fixed values in the expression, making them constants. Their numerical size does not determine whether they are variables.

Q10. A taxi company models a fare using \(F=80+25d\), where \(d\) is the distance traveled in kilometers. If the distance changes from \(4\) km to \(9\) km, which statement correctly identifies what changes and calculates the new fare?

A. Only \(80\) changes, and the fare becomes \(305\).
B. Only \(25\) changes, and the fare becomes \(225\).
C. \(d\) changes, while \(80\) and \(25\) remain fixed; the new fare is \(305\). ✅
D. All three quantities change because the fare changes.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The variable \(d\) represents distance and changes from \(4\) to \(9\). The values \(80\) and \(25\) are constants in this model. Substituting \(d=9\) gives \(F=80+25(9)=305\), so option C is correct.

Q11. A learner evaluates \(3n+40\) for \(n=6\) and writes \(3(6)+40=58\). Another learner says the answer should be \(3+6+40=49\) because every symbol and number must be added. Which analysis is correct?

A. The first learner is correct because multiplication must be performed before addition. ✅
B. The second learner is correct because variables always represent addition.

D. Neither learner is correct because \(40\) should be treated as a variable.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The notation \(3n\) means \(3\times n\), not \(3+n\). Substituting \(n=6\) gives \(3\times6+40=18+40=58\). The second learner has confused multiplication with addition, a common algebraic misconception.

📖 Explanation:

The graph begins at \(y=50\) when \(x=0\), showing a fixed starting amount of \(50\). Each increase of one item raises the cost by \(20\), so \(20\) represents the changing rate while \(50\) remains constant.

Q13. Two expressions are proposed for the cost of renting bicycles: Model A is \(300+75h\), while Model B is \(375h\), where \(h\) is the number of hours. For a one-hour rental both give the same cost. Which reasoning best explains why Model A is more informative for multiple hours?

Although both models produce \(375\) when \(h=1\), they behave differently for other values. Model A includes a fixed \(300\)-rupee charge plus \(75\) per hour. Model B incorrectly assumes the entire \(375\) changes with every hour.

Q14. A number \(k\) is selected so that the expression \(5k+12\) has the same value as \(47\). A student argues that \(12\) must also change because otherwise the expression cannot reach \(47\). Which conclusion correctly challenges this argument?

A. The constant \(12\) stays fixed; solving \(5k+12=47\) gives \(k=7\). ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A constant can participate in an operation without changing its own value. Subtracting \(12\) from both sides gives \(5k=35\), and dividing by \(5\) gives \(k=7\). Thus only the variable \(k\) needs a suitable value.

The multiplication operation is evaluated before subtraction when no grouping symbols change the order. Therefore, \(3\times4=12\), and then \(18-12=6\). The student's error comes from reversing the usual operation priority.

Q16. A shop charges a fixed delivery fee of \(120\) rupees plus \(35\) rupees for each item. If a customer orders \(6\) items, which expression correctly models the total cost?

Q18. A graph shows four points on a number line representing \(x-3\), \(x+3\), \(3-x\), and \(x\times3\). For \(x=2\), which expression corresponds to the point located at \(-1\)?

Q20. A number machine follows the rule: start with a number, multiply it by \(4\), subtract \(6\), then divide the result by \(2\). Which single expression represents the machine's output for an input \(x\), and what output does it produce when \(x=5\)?

The student's mistake is treating the boundary as part of the solution. The symbol \(>\) means strictly greater than, so \(5\) itself is excluded. Values such as \(5.1\), \(6\), and \(10\) satisfy \(x>5\), but \(5\) does not.

Q35. A student evaluates \(18-[6-(2+5)]\) as \(18-6-2+5=15\). Which statement best identifies the error and gives the correct value?

Q36. A rectangular garden has \(x\) meters of length and \(x+4\) meters of width. A path of \(2\) meters is added to both dimensions. Which expression correctly represents the new area?

Q37. Consider the expression \(24\div[3(2+2)]\). Two students disagree. Student A gets \(2\), while Student B gets \(16\). Which evaluation is correct, and why?

📖 Explanation:

First evaluate the parentheses: \(2+2=4\). Then the bracket contains \(3(4)=12\). Finally, \(24\div12=2\). Student B correctly preserves the grouping structure and evaluates the expression from the inside outward.

Q40. A learner simplifies \(7-\{3-[2-(x-4)]\}\) to \(7-3-2-x-4\). Which change would best correct the reasoning?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The learner loses track of sign changes when crossing nested grouping levels. First simplify the innermost group \(2-(x-4)\), then account for the negative sign before the bracket, and finally the negative sign before the braces. Careful distribution preserves each sign correctly.

Q41. For the expression \(12-[4-\{2+(x-3)\}]\), a student says that changing the braces to parentheses makes the expression invalid because braces have a special arithmetic operation. Which conclusion is strongest?

A. The student is correct because braces always mean multiplication.
B. The student is correct because only parentheses can contain variables.
C. The student is incorrect because braces, brackets, and parentheses can all indicate grouping; their shape mainly clarifies nesting. ✅
D. The student is incorrect only when the expression contains subtraction.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Grouping symbols do not represent separate arithmetic operations merely because their shapes differ. Parentheses, brackets, and braces can all indicate that a set of terms should be treated together. Different shapes are especially useful when several levels of grouping are nested.

Q42. A school club charges a fixed registration fee of \(120\) rupees plus \(35\) rupees for each meeting attended. Which equation represents the total cost \(C\) for \(m\) meetings, and what is the cost for \(4\) meetings?

A. \(C=120+35m\), so \(C=260\) ✅
B. \(C=155m\), so \(C=620\)
C. \(C=35+120m\), so \(C=515\)
D. \(C=120m+35\), so \(C=515\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The fixed fee is paid once, so it is the constant \(120\), while \(35\) is multiplied by the number of meetings. Substituting \(m=4\) gives \(120+35(4)=260\). The distractors reverse the roles of the fixed and variable quantities.

Q43. Two students simplify \(3(x+4)-2x\). Student A obtains \(x+12\), while Student B obtains \(x+4\). Which evaluation best explains the disagreement?

A. Student A is correct because \(3\) must be multiplied only by \(4\)
B. Student B is correct because \(3x-2x=x\) and the \(12\) cancels ✅
C. Both are correct because equivalent expressions can have different constant terms
D. Neither is correct because the simplified expression should contain \(2x\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Distributing \(3\) across the parentheses gives \(3x+12\). Combining like terms then gives \(3x-2x+12=x+12\). Therefore Student A is correct. Student B correctly combines the variable terms but incorrectly drops the constant \(12\).

Q44. A taxi company models a fare with \(F=80+25k\), where \(k\) is the number of kilometers traveled. A passenger has \(230\) rupees. What is the greatest whole number of kilometers the passenger can travel without exceeding the budget?

A. 5 km
B. 6 km ✅
C. 7 km
D. 8 km

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Set the fare equal to the available budget: \(80+25k\le230\). Subtracting \(80\) gives \(25k\le150\), so \(k\le6\). Thus \(6\) kilometers is the greatest whole-number distance. Choosing \(7\) would produce a fare of \(255\) rupees.

Q45. A student solves \(4x+7=23\) by subtracting \(7\) and writing \(4x=23\), then concludes \(x=23/4\). What is the student's key error?

A. The student should divide by \(7\) first
B. The student forgot to subtract \(7\) from \(23\) ✅
C. The student should multiply \(4\) by \(7\) before solving
D. The student should subtract \(4\) from both sides

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The constant \(7\) must be removed from both sides, giving \(4x=16\), not \(4x=23\). Then dividing by \(4\) gives \(x=4\). The error is failing to apply the subtraction to the numerical side of the equation.

Q46. A graph of \(y=2x+1\) and a horizontal line \(y=9\) intersect at one point. Without calculating every value, which ordered pair must represent their intersection?

A. \((3,9)\)
B. \((4,9)\) ✅
C. \((5,9)\)
D. \((9,4)\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

At the intersection, both expressions have the same \(y\)-value. Setting \(2x+1=9\) gives \(2x=8\), so \(x=4\). Therefore the intersection is \((4,9)\). The distractors reflect common confusion between the slope, intercept, and coordinate order.

Q47. A rectangular garden has length \(x+5\) meters and width \(x\) meters. Its perimeter is \(34\) meters. Which equation correctly models the situation and gives the garden's dimensions?

A. \(x+(x+5)=34\), giving \(x=14.5\)
B. \(2x+5=34\), giving \(x=14.5\)
C. \(2[x+(x+5)]=34\), giving \(x=6\) ✅
D. \(x(x+5)=34\), giving \(x=4\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Perimeter requires adding all four sides, so \(2[x+(x+5)]=34\). Simplifying gives \(4x+10=34\), hence \(4x=24\) and \(x=6\). The dimensions are \(6\) m by \(11\) m. The other choices confuse perimeter with a partial sum or area.

Q48. A number \(x\) satisfies \(3(x-2)+4=2x+9\). A student claims there is no solution because the coefficients of \(x\) on the two sides are different. Which conclusion is correct?

A. There is no solution because \(3\ne2\)
B. There is exactly one solution, \(x=11\) ✅
C. There is exactly one solution, \(x=7\)
D. Every real number is a solution

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Expanding gives \(3x-6+4=2x+9\), so \(3x-2=2x+9\). Subtracting \(2x\) gives \(x-2=9\), hence \(x=11\). The student's reasoning is incorrect because different coefficients do not prevent an equation from having a unique solution.
