What is Variables and algebraic symbols?

Definition:
Variables are symbols, usually letters like x,y, x, y, x,y, or z z z, that represent unknown or changing numerical values in algebraic expressions, while algebraic symbols include operators like +,−,×,÷ +, -, \times, \div +,−,×,÷, and grouping symbols such as parentheses, which together form mathematical statements.

Working:
A variable can take any value, and we manipulate these symbols using arithmetic operations; for example, in 3x+2 3x + 2 3x+2, x x x is the variable, 3 3 3 is the coefficient, and +2 +2 +2 is the constant term, and we can substitute different numbers for x x x to evaluate the expression.

Example:
If y=4 y = 4 y=4, evaluate 2y−5 2y - 5 2y−5.
Solution: Substitute y=4 y = 4 y=4: 2(4)−5=8−5=3 2(4) - 5 = 8 - 5 = 3 2(4)−5=8−5=3.

Reason:
Variables allow us to generalize arithmetic, write formulas, and solve equations for unknown quantities, making them essential for modeling real-world problems and describing relationships in science and engineering.
