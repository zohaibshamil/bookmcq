What is Commutative and Associative Properties in Algebra?

Definition:
The commutative and associative properties in algebra state that for addition and multiplication, the order of numbers does not affect the sum or product (commutative: a+b=b+a a+b=b+a a+b=b+a, ab=ba ab=ba ab=ba), and the grouping of numbers does not affect the result (associative: (a+b)+c=a+(b+c) (a+b)+c=a+(b+c) (a+b)+c=a+(b+c), (ab)c=a(bc) (ab)c=a(bc) (ab)c=a(bc)), allowing flexibility in calculations.

Working:
Use commutative property to reorder terms/factors to make calculations easier (e.g., 7+3=3+7 7 + 3 = 3 + 7 7+3=3+7); use associative property to regroup terms/factors when adding or multiplying multiple numbers, especially to pair numbers that are easy to combine (like (2+8)+5=2+(8+5) (2+8)+5 = 2+(8+5) (2+8)+5=2+(8+5)).

Example:
Simplify 2×5×6 2 \times 5 \times 6 2×5×6 using commutative and associative properties.
Solution: 2×5×6=(2×5)×6=10×6=60 2 \times 5 \times 6 = (2 \times 5) \times 6 = 10 \times 6 = 60 2×5×6=(2×5)×6=10×6=60; or 2×(5×6)=2×30=60 2 \times (5 \times 6) = 2 \times 30 = 60 2×(5×6)=2×30=60.

Reason:
These properties simplify arithmetic and algebra, making it easier to compute, combine like terms, and factor expressions, and they are foundational for understanding more advanced algebraic structures.

D.Changing the order always changes the result unless numbers are equal.
