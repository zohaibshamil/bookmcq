What is Why is water called universal solvent?

Definition:
Water is called the universal solvent because its polarity allows it to dissolve more substances than any other liquid, including ionic compounds, polar molecules, and many biological molecules. It dissolves solutes by surrounding them with water molecules in a process called hydration.

Working:
The δ−\delta^-δ− oxygen attracts positive ions, and the δ+\delta^+δ+ hydrogens attract negative ions. Water molecules surround the ions, separating them from the crystal lattice. For polar molecules, water forms hydrogen bonds with them, dissolving them as well. Nonpolar substances do not dissolve in water.

Example:
NaCl dissolves in water: Na+Na^+Na+ is surrounded by oxygen atoms, and Cl−Cl^-Cl− is surrounded by hydrogen atoms. Solution: the ions separate and disperse throughout the water, forming a solution.

Reason:
Understanding water's solvent ability is important because it explains nutrient transport, waste removal, blood plasma, and the chemistry of life.

📝 All Why is water called universal solvent MCQs

Q1. A researcher places equal amounts of sodium chloride into four liquids. Salt dissolves rapidly in water but remains mostly undissolved in hexane. Which molecular property best explains this difference?

A.Water molecules can form favorable interactions with charged ions, whereas hexane cannot effectively stabilize them ✅
B.Water contains more atoms per molecule than hexane, so it dissolves more substances
C.Hexane molecules are heavier, preventing them from mixing with salt
D.Salt dissolves only in liquids containing hydrogen atoms
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Water is polar, so its partially charged regions orient around ions and stabilize them through ion-dipole interactions. Hexane is nonpolar and cannot provide comparable favorable interactions, making it a poor solvent for ionic substances.

Q2. Two substances are added separately to water. Substance X separates into individual ions, while substance Y remains as intact molecules but becomes evenly distributed. What conclusion is most justified?

A.Only X is soluble because solubility requires ion formation
B.X is likely ionic, while Y can be a polar molecular substance that interacts with water without ionizing ✅
C.Y must be nonpolar because it remains molecular
D.Both substances must have identical interactions with water
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An ionic substance such as a salt can dissociate into ions that become surrounded by water molecules. A polar molecular substance may dissolve without forming ions because its partial charges interact favorably with water.

Q3. A cell is placed in a solution containing a dissolved polar nutrient. The nutrient enters the surrounding water readily but does not enter a nonpolar oil layer. Which prediction best explains the observation?

A.The nutrient is likely interacting favorably with polar water molecules but poorly with nonpolar oil ✅
B.The nutrient must be an ion because all soluble substances are ions
C.The oil layer must contain more hydrogen bonds than water
D.The nutrient cannot interact with any molecules because it remains dissolved
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Polar solutes tend to interact favorably with polar water because their partial charges can form hydrogen bonds or other electrostatic interactions. Nonpolar oil lacks these strong interactions, so the nutrient preferentially remains in water.

Q4. A student claims, If a substance contains hydrogen atoms it will dissolve readily in water. Which example most directly exposes the flaw in this reasoning?

A.A substance containing several nonpolar hydrocarbon groups may contain hydrogen but still interact poorly with water ✅
B.Sodium chloride dissolves even though it contains no hydrogen
C.Oxygen dissolves because it contains hydrogen bonds
D.Water dissolves because every molecule containing hydrogen is ionic
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The presence of hydrogen alone does not determine polarity or solubility. Hydrocarbons contain many hydrogen atoms but have largely nonpolar bonds and interact weakly with water, illustrating why molecular structure matters.

Q5. A pharmaceutical compound has one charged group and a large nonpolar hydrocarbon region. A scientist modifies the molecule by adding another charged group while preserving its nonpolar region. What is the most likely effect on its behavior in water?

A.Its water solubility will generally increase because additional charge allows stronger interactions with water ✅
B.Its solubility must decrease because charged groups repel water
C.Its behavior will become identical to that of a pure hydrocarbon
D.The added charge will prevent all interactions with water
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Adding a charged group generally increases favorable interactions with water because the charged region can attract water's partial charges. Although the nonpolar region may still limit solubility, increasing polarity commonly improves aqueous compatibility.

Q6. A laboratory graph shows the percentage of a polar solute remaining dissolved as temperature increases: 10°C = 82%, 20°C = 86%, 30°C = 91%, 40°C = 95%. A student concludes that temperature is the only reason the solute dissolves. What is the best evaluation?

A.The graph proves that polarity is irrelevant
B.The graph shows a temperature association, but it does not establish that temperature alone causes dissolution ✅
C.The graph proves that all substances become more soluble at higher temperatures
D.The graph demonstrates that the solute must be nonpolar
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The data show that solubility increases with temperature under the tested conditions, but they do not isolate molecular polarity or establish causation by temperature alone. Dissolution depends on interactions among solute and solvent molecules as well.

Q7. A plant root absorbs a mineral ion from moist soil. Nearby soil contains water molecules that orient their partially negative regions toward the positive ion. Why does this arrangement help the ion remain dispersed?

A.The oriented water molecules form a hydration shell that reduces the tendency of the ion to associate with other ions ✅
B.The water molecules remove all electrical charge from the ion
C.The water molecules convert the ion into a nonpolar molecule
D.The hydration shell makes the ion chemically identical to water
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Water's polarity allows its partially charged regions to surround dissolved ions. This hydration shell stabilizes the ions in solution and reduces attractive forces that would otherwise cause oppositely charged ions to reassociate.

Q8. A student predicts that a nonpolar molecule will dissolve better in water if its hydrocarbon portion becomes larger. Why is this prediction generally incorrect?

A.A larger nonpolar surface usually increases unfavorable interactions with water and strengthens the molecule's tendency to associate with other nonpolar molecules ✅
B.Larger molecules always become ionic in water
C.Hydrocarbon groups form stronger hydrogen bonds as their size increases
D.Water cannot interact with any molecule larger than itself
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Increasing a molecule's nonpolar hydrocarbon region generally increases the extent to which water must organize around a surface that cannot form strong favorable interactions. This tends to reduce aqueous solubility unless polar groups compensate sufficiently.

Q9. Three solutes are tested in water. Solute P dissolves as ions, solute Q forms many hydrogen bonds with water, and solute R has an extensive nonpolar surface with few polar groups. Which ranking is most reasonable for expected aqueous compatibility?

A.P and Q are generally more compatible with water than R ✅
B.R is most compatible because nonpolar surfaces attract water strongly
C.P is incompatible because ions cannot interact with polar molecules
D.Q is incompatible because hydrogen bonding prevents dissolution
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Ionic solutes and strongly polar molecules generally interact favorably with water through electrostatic attractions and hydrogen bonding. A molecule dominated by nonpolar surface area lacks comparable favorable interactions and is therefore usually less water-compatible.

Q10. A student says, Because water dissolves many substances every substance that enters water must be chemically broken apart. Which observation provides the strongest counterexample?

A.A molecular sugar can dissolve while remaining composed of intact sugar molecules ✅
B.An ionic salt can separate into ions
C.A charged particle can attract water molecules
D.Water molecules can form hydrogen bonds
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Dissolution does not necessarily mean chemical decomposition. Molecular substances such as many sugars can become dispersed throughout water while retaining their molecular identity, because dissolution can involve physical separation and hydration.

Q11. A mixture contains water, sodium ions, and a neutral polar molecule. After equilibrium is reached, both solutes are uniformly distributed. Which model best explains why both can remain dispersed despite having different chemical forms?

A.Water can stabilize ions through electrostatic interactions and stabilize polar molecules through interactions involving their partial charges ✅
B.Water must chemically convert both solutes into identical particles
C.Only the sodium ions interact with water; the neutral molecule remains suspended mechanically
D.Uniform distribution proves that the solutes have become covalently bonded to water
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Water can accommodate chemically different solutes because its polarity supports multiple favorable interactions. Ions are stabilized by ion-dipole attractions, while polar molecules can interact through dipole-based attractions and hydrogen bonding.

Q12. A researcher compares two drugs. Drug A has several exposed polar groups and a small hydrocarbon region. Drug B has one polar group and a very large hydrocarbon region. Both have similar molecular masses. Which drug would most likely show greater aqueous solubility, assuming no ionization differences?

A.Drug A, because a larger proportion of its surface can form favorable interactions with water ✅
B.Drug B, because hydrocarbon groups strongly hydrogen-bond with water
C.Both must have equal solubility because their masses are similar
D.Drug B, because fewer polar groups reduce competition for water molecules
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A molecule's overall molecular mass does not determine water solubility by itself. Drug A has a greater relative contribution from polar groups capable of favorable interactions with water, whereas Drug B is dominated by a nonpolar region.
