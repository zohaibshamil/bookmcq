What is Cohesion of Water Molecules?

Definition:
Cohesion of water molecules is the attraction between water molecules due to hydrogen bonding, which causes them to stick together. This property creates surface tension and allows water to move as a continuous column in plants.

Working:
Each water molecule forms hydrogen bonds with up to four neighbors. At the surface, molecules have fewer neighbors above, so they bond more strongly with those beside and below, creating surface tension. In narrow tubes, cohesion helps water rise against gravity.

Example:
Water droplets form beads on a waxy surface because cohesion pulls water molecules together. Solution: the droplet minimizes surface area, forming a sphere.

Reason:
Understanding cohesion is important because it explains water transport in plants, surface tension, and the behavior of water in biological systems.

📝 All Cohesion of Water Molecules MCQs

Q1. A water droplet remains rounded on a waxed surface rather than spreading immediately. Which molecular explanation best accounts for this behavior?

A.Water molecules form ionic bonds with the wax surface
B.Hydrogen bonding causes neighboring water molecules to attract one another ✅
C.Water molecules become nonpolar when touching wax
D.Covalent bonds between different water molecules become stronger
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Cohesion results from attraction among water molecules, largely through hydrogen bonding. Because neighboring molecules attract one another, the droplet tends to minimize its exposed surface and remain together instead of spreading freely across a nonpolar waxed surface.

Q2. Two identical water droplets on a clean glass plate merge into one larger droplet when they touch. Which prediction most directly follows from the molecular behavior responsible for cohesion?

A.The molecules at the interface attract neighboring water molecules, reducing separation between the droplets ✅
B.The droplets merge because water molecules lose their polarity when they touch
C.The glass converts hydrogen bonds into covalent bonds between droplets
D.The droplets merge because gravity creates chemical bonds between water molecules
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When two water droplets contact each other, molecules near their surfaces can form attractive hydrogen-bond interactions with molecules in the other droplet. This intermolecular attraction pulls the droplets together and allows them to behave as one continuous body of water.

Q3. A researcher compares two liquids by placing equal-volume droplets on a smooth surface. Liquid X forms a compact droplet, while liquid Y spreads much farther. If surface interactions are otherwise controlled, what is the strongest inference?

A.X necessarily has stronger covalent bonds within each molecule
B.X has stronger attractions among its molecules than Y has ✅
C.Y must contain more atoms per molecule than X
D.Y cannot participate in intermolecular interactions
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A compact droplet indicates that molecules within the liquid strongly attract one another and resist separating across the surface. Water's cohesion is associated with hydrogen bonding, so stronger intermolecular attraction generally favors a more cohesive, compact droplet.

Q4. A narrow glass tube is inserted into water. Water rises inside the tube, although the water level outside remains lower. A student claims that cohesion alone pulls water upward against gravity. What is the best evaluation?

A.Correct, because cohesion always moves water upward regardless of the surface
B.Incorrect, because upward movement involves both cohesion among water molecules and adhesion to the tube ✅
C.Correct, because gravity causes water molecules to repel one another
D.Incorrect, because water cannot interact with glass surfaces
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The student's explanation is incomplete. Cohesion helps water molecules pull neighboring molecules along, but adhesion between water and the glass surface contributes to upward movement in a narrow tube. Gravity opposes this rise, so the observed result reflects competing forces.

Q5. A plant stem contains narrow water-conducting tubes. Suppose hydrogen bonding between water molecules were substantially weakened while adhesion to the tube walls remained unchanged. What would most likely happen to long-distance water movement?

A.Water movement would become easier because molecules could separate without resistance
B.Water would move less effectively as a continuous column because cohesion would decrease ✅
C.Water would become unable to interact with the tube walls
D.Water would spontaneously move upward faster because gravity would weaken
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Long-distance water transport depends on water behaving as a relatively continuous column. Weaker hydrogen bonding would reduce cohesion between adjacent molecules, making the column more vulnerable to separation and disrupting efficient transmission of pulling forces through the water.

Q6. A sealed container is gently shaken and then left undisturbed. Water molecules repeatedly separate slightly and return toward neighboring molecules. Which model best explains why the liquid remains continuous rather than permanently breaking apart?

A.Cohesive intermolecular attractions repeatedly bring nearby molecules together ✅
B.Each molecule is permanently attached to exactly four other molecules
C.Gravity permanently locks all molecules into fixed positions
D.Water molecules have no kinetic energy and therefore cannot separate
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Water molecules are constantly moving, so they can momentarily separate and change neighbors. However, intermolecular attractions, including hydrogen bonding, continually pull nearby molecules toward one another. This balance between motion and cohesion allows water to remain a liquid.

Q7. A graph records the maximum mass of water supported by a thin liquid surface before the surface breaks. Sample A supports 4.0 g, B supports 6.5 g, C supports 9.0 g, and D supports 5.0 g. Which sample shows the strongest cohesive behavior, assuming all other conditions are identical?

A.Sample A
B.Sample B
C.Sample C ✅
D.Sample D
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: If the experimental setup is identical, supporting a larger mass before the surface breaks indicates that the liquid surface resists disruption more strongly. Sample C supports the greatest mass, so it provides the strongest evidence of greater cohesive behavior among the four samples.

Q8. A student observes that a water surface can support a lightweight needle when it is placed carefully, but the needle sinks after the surface is disturbed. Which reasoning best connects the observation to cohesion?

A.Cohesion helps surface molecules resist being pulled apart, but disturbance can disrupt the organized surface interactions ✅
B.The needle becomes chemically bonded to water until movement breaks the bond
C.Water molecules below the needle stop moving completely
D.The needle floats because water becomes less dense exactly at the surface
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: At the water surface, cohesive attractions among molecules create resistance to disruption. A carefully placed needle can be supported by this surface behavior, but disturbing the surface changes molecular arrangements and can cause the supporting surface to fail.

Q9. A scientist compares the maximum height reached by water in two situations. In the first, intact water forms a continuous column in a narrow tube. In the second, cohesion is greatly reduced but adhesion to the tube remains strong. Which outcome is most plausible?

A.The second column would transmit pulling forces less effectively because neighboring molecules interact less strongly ✅
B.The second column would transmit forces more effectively because molecules separate more easily
C.Both columns must behave identically because adhesion determines all water movement
D.The second column would become a solid because weaker cohesion increases molecular packing
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A continuous water column depends on intermolecular attraction between neighboring molecules. If cohesion decreases, pulling forces cannot be transmitted as effectively through the liquid, even if adhesion to the tube remains strong. Thus continuity and force transmission are reduced.

Q10. Two students propose explanations for water's tendency to resist being divided into separate droplets. Student 1 says molecules attract because each water molecule has a permanent charge separation. Student 2 says water molecules attract because they form hydrogen bonds with neighboring molecules. Which conclusion is most accurate?

A.Only Student 1 is correct
B.Only Student 2 is correct
C.Both statements contribute to the explanation, with polarity enabling hydrogen bonding ✅
D.Neither statement is relevant because cohesion depends only on gravity
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Water has a permanent unequal distribution of charge because its bonds are polar and its shape creates partial charges. These properties allow neighboring water molecules to form hydrogen bonds, producing the intermolecular attractions responsible for strong cohesion.

Q11. A researcher gradually increases temperature while measuring the maximum tension a water surface can withstand before breaking. The measured values decrease as temperature rises. Which interpretation best links the graph trend to molecular behavior?

A.Higher temperature generally increases molecular motion and can reduce the persistence of cohesive interactions ✅
B.Higher temperature makes every water molecule permanently covalently bonded to its neighbors
C.Lower surface strength proves that water becomes nonpolar at higher temperature
D.The trend shows that hydrogen bonding becomes a type of ionic bonding
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Increasing temperature increases molecular kinetic energy, causing molecules to move more vigorously and making intermolecular associations less persistent on average. Consequently, cohesive effects at the surface can weaken, producing lower resistance to surface disruption.
