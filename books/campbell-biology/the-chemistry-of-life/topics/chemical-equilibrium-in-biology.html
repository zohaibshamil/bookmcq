What is Chemical Equilibrium in biology?

Definition:
Chemical equilibrium in biology is the state in which the forward and reverse rates of a reversible reaction are equal, so the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant over time. In biological systems, equilibrium is dynamic and is often shifted by changing conditions to favor necessary products.

Working:
For a reversible reaction \( A \rightleftharpoons B \), equilibrium is reached when the rate of \( A \rightarrow B \) equals the rate of \( B \rightarrow A \). The equilibrium constant is \( K_{eq} = \frac{[B]}{[A]} \). Enzymes and concentration changes can shift equilibrium.

Example:
In the blood, \( CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3 \rightleftharpoons H^+ + HCO_3^- \). Solution: when \( CO_2 \) increases, the equilibrium shifts right, producing more \( H^+ \) and lowering pH.

Reason:
Understanding chemical equilibrium is important because it explains how biological systems maintain pH, transport oxygen, and regulate metabolic pathways.

📝 All Chemical Equilibrium in biology MCQs

Q1. A reaction reaches a state in which reactants and products are both still present, but their concentrations remain nearly constant. Which interpretation best explains this observation?

A.The reaction has completely stopped.
B.Reactants and products are reacting at equal forward and reverse rates. ✅
C.All reactant molecules have been converted into products.
D.The concentrations are constant because no molecular collisions occur.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: At equilibrium, molecular reactions continue in both directions. The concentrations remain constant because the forward and reverse reaction rates become equal, so there is no net change in the amounts of reactants and products.

Q2. A student claims that chemical equilibrium means a reaction has stopped because the concentrations of all substances stop changing. What is the strongest correction to this reasoning?

A.Equilibrium means only the reverse reaction continues.
B.Equilibrium means reactant concentration must equal product concentration.
C.Equilibrium is dynamic because forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates. ✅
D.Equilibrium occurs only when every molecule has reacted at least once.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Constant concentrations do not mean molecular activity has stopped. At dynamic equilibrium, particles continue undergoing forward and reverse reactions, but equal rates balance one another and prevent a net macroscopic change.

Q3. In a closed container, reaction A⇌BA \rightleftharpoons B initially contains mostly AA. As time passes, BB increases while AA decreases, and eventually both concentrations become constant. Which sequence best describes the process?

A.The forward rate decreases while the reverse rate increases until they become equal. ✅
B.Both reaction rates remain constant from the beginning.
C.The reverse reaction occurs first, followed by the forward reaction.
D.Both reactions stop when equal concentrations are reached.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Initially, the forward reaction is favored because abundant AA molecules collide frequently. As BB accumulates, the reverse rate increases while the forward rate decreases until both rates become equal at equilibrium.

Q4. A sealed flask contains a reversible reaction. A researcher removes some product while the system is at equilibrium. Which prediction is most reasonable immediately after removal?

A.The reverse reaction becomes faster because less product is available.
B.Both reaction rates become zero.
C.The system can never return to equilibrium.
D.The forward reaction becomes relatively favored until a new equilibrium is established. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Removing product lowers its concentration, reducing the reverse reaction rate immediately. The forward reaction then becomes relatively faster, producing more product until the forward and reverse rates become equal again.

Q5. Two sealed containers undergo the same reversible reaction. Container X has reached equilibrium, while container Y has not. Which observation would provide the strongest evidence that X is at equilibrium?

A.The concentrations of reactants and products remain constant over time while molecular reactions continue. ✅
B.The container contains only products.
C.The temperature must continuously decrease.
D.The reactant and product concentrations must be identical.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Equilibrium is identified by stable macroscopic concentrations in a closed system while microscopic forward and reverse reactions continue. Equal concentrations are not required, and equilibrium does not require a temperature change.

Q6. A graph of concentration versus time shows reactant concentration falling rapidly, then leveling off, while product concentration rises rapidly and then levels off. At the leveling-off region, which conclusion is best supported?

A.The reaction has permanently stopped.
B.The system is approaching a dynamic equilibrium. ✅
C.All reactant molecules have become product molecules.
D.The product concentration must equal the reactant concentration.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The leveling of both concentration curves indicates that their net concentrations are no longer changing significantly. This is consistent with dynamic equilibrium, where forward and reverse processes continue at balanced rates.

Q7. Consider a concentration-time graph for A⇌BA \rightleftharpoons B. Curve 1 for AA decreases from 0.80 M to 0.30 M and levels off. Curve 2 for BB increases from 0.10 M to 0.60 M and levels off. Which statement is most accurate?

A.The equilibrium constant must equal 1 because both final concentrations are 0.60 M.
B.The reverse reaction has completely stopped.
C.The final concentrations indicate equilibrium, but they do not by themselves prove that the reaction rates are zero. ✅
D.The reaction cannot be reversible because both curves eventually become flat.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A flat concentration curve means there is no net concentration change, not that molecular reactions have ceased. Equilibrium can occur with unequal concentrations, and concentration values alone do not establish zero reaction rates.

Q8. A reversible reaction initially has a much faster forward rate than reverse rate. As products accumulate, the reverse rate increases. What is the most likely reason for this change?

A.Product particles become more energetic automatically.
B.Increasing product concentration provides more opportunities for the reverse reaction to occur. ✅
C.Reactants stop colliding with one another.
D.The equilibrium state destroys excess product molecules.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: As product molecules accumulate, collisions involving those molecules become more frequent, increasing the reverse reaction rate. Eventually, the reverse rate can match the forward rate, producing dynamic equilibrium.

Q9. A researcher compares two identical closed systems containing the same reversible reaction. System A reaches equilibrium quickly, while System B reaches equilibrium more slowly. Which statement is safest?

A.System A must contain more products at equilibrium.
B.System B cannot reach equilibrium.
C.System A must have a larger equilibrium constant.
D.The time required to reach equilibrium does not by itself determine the final equilibrium composition. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The speed at which equilibrium is reached describes how rapidly the system approaches equilibrium, whereas equilibrium composition depends on the relative tendencies of the forward and reverse processes under the given conditions.

Q10. A student observes that the concentrations of substances in a reversible reaction remain unchanged and concludes, 'Because nothing changes, molecules are no longer reacting.' Which evidence most directly disproves the conclusion?

A.At equilibrium, forward and reverse reactions can continue at equal rates, producing no net concentration change. ✅
B.At equilibrium, every molecule must remain in its original position.
C.At equilibrium, only reactants continue reacting.
D.At equilibrium, product molecules cannot collide.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Dynamic equilibrium involves ongoing molecular activity. Forward reactions convert reactants into products while reverse reactions convert products into reactants at equal rates, causing concentrations to remain constant despite continuous molecular changes.

Q11. A reaction X⇌YX \rightleftharpoons Y is studied in a closed container. At equilibrium, XX remains at 0.20 M while YY remains at 1.00 M. A student says equilibrium cannot exist because the concentrations are unequal. What is the best response?

A.The student is correct because equilibrium always requires equal concentrations.
B.The student is correct because only gases can reach equilibrium.
C.The student is incorrect because equilibrium requires equal reaction rates, not necessarily equal concentrations. ✅
D.The student is incorrect only if the temperature is zero.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Equilibrium does not require equal concentrations of reactants and products. It requires equal forward and reverse reaction rates. Different equilibrium concentrations are common because the two directions can have different relative tendencies.

Q12. For a reversible reaction, a graph shows the forward reaction rate starting high and steadily decreasing, while the reverse rate starts near zero and steadily increasing. The curves intersect and then remain together. What does the intersection most strongly represent?

A.The instant when all reactants disappear.
B.The point at which the reaction becomes irreversible.
C.The point where product formation permanently stops.
D.The point at which forward and reverse reaction rates become equal. ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The intersection represents equality of the forward and reverse rates. After this point, both processes can continue at the same rate, maintaining constant macroscopic concentrations and producing a dynamic equilibrium.
