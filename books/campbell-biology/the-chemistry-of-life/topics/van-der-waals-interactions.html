What is Van der Waals Interactions?

Definition:
Van der Waals interactions are weak, short-range attractive forces between molecules or between parts of the same molecule that arise from temporary fluctuations in electron distribution, creating transient dipoles. These interactions include dipole-dipole forces, London dispersion forces, and hydrogen bonding, and they are individually weak but collectively strong when many contacts exist. The potential energy varies with distance as \( E \propto -\frac{1}{r^6} \).

Working:
Electrons move constantly, so at any instant a molecule may have an uneven charge distribution, creating a temporary dipole \( \delta^- \) and \( \delta^+ \). This temporary dipole induces a complementary dipole in a neighboring molecule, and the two attract each other. The strength depends on distance and molecular size.

Example:
Geckos can climb smooth surfaces because millions of tiny hairs on their feet make Van der Waals contact with the surface. Solution: although each interaction is weak, the huge number of contacts produces enough force to support the gecko's weight.

Reason:
Understanding Van der Waals interactions is important because they influence molecular shape, protein folding, DNA base stacking, and many biological structures even though they are much weaker than covalent or ionic bonds.

📝 All Van der Waals Interactions MCQs

Q1. Two nonpolar molecules approach one another. At one instant, electrons in one molecule become slightly unevenly distributed. What is the most likely immediate consequence?

A.A permanent ionic bond forms between the molecules
B.A temporary dipole can induce an opposite partial charge pattern in the neighboring molecule ✅
C.Both molecules become permanently charged
D.The molecules repel because nonpolar molecules cannot interact
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A temporary uneven distribution of electrons creates an instantaneous dipole. This dipole can distort the electron distribution of a nearby nonpolar molecule, producing an induced dipole and a weak attractive interaction between the molecules.

Q2. A researcher compares two nonpolar molecules of similar shape. Molecule X has a larger electron cloud than molecule Y. Which prediction is most reasonable when other conditions are similar?

A.Y should always have stronger interactions because smaller molecules move faster
B.X should have stronger temporary attractions because its electron cloud is more easily distorted ✅
C.Both must have identical attractions because neither molecule is polar
D.X cannot experience temporary dipoles because it is nonpolar
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A larger electron cloud generally contains more electrons and is more polarizable. Greater polarizability makes temporary fluctuations in electron distribution more capable of inducing dipoles, strengthening weak attractions between nearby molecules.

Q3. A student says, Nonpolar molecules cannot attract one another because they have no permanent dipoles." Which response best evaluates the claim?"

A.It is correct because only ionic attractions can occur between nonpolar molecules
B.It is correct because electrons never change position within molecules
C.It is incorrect because temporary electron fluctuations can create instantaneous and induced dipoles ✅
D.It is incorrect because every nonpolar molecule actually contains a permanent positive charge
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The claim confuses permanent polarity with temporary polarity. Even nonpolar molecules have moving electrons, so their electron distributions can fluctuate briefly, creating instantaneous dipoles that induce dipoles in neighboring molecules.

Q4. A model represents two nonpolar molecules as electron clouds. In Model 1, the clouds are small and difficult to distort. In Model 2, the clouds are larger and easily distorted. If the molecules are equally close, which model predicts stronger attraction?

A.Model 1, because compact clouds always have stronger temporary dipoles
B.Model 2, because greater polarizability allows larger temporary charge separations ✅
C.Both models predict zero attraction because the molecules are nonpolar
D.Model 1, because electrons in large clouds cannot respond to nearby charges
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The important difference is polarizability, or how readily an electron cloud can be distorted. A larger, more easily distorted cloud can develop stronger instantaneous and induced dipoles, producing greater van der Waals attraction.

Q5. Two samples contain similar nonpolar molecules. Sample A is kept at a temperature where molecules move rapidly, while Sample B is cooler. Which statement best describes the role of van der Waals interactions?

A.They disappear completely in the warmer sample
B.They become covalent bonds in the cooler sample
C.They may still occur in both samples, although increased molecular motion can make sustained close contacts less favorable ✅
D.They occur only when molecules are motionless
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Van der Waals attractions arise from temporary fluctuations in electron distribution and can occur whenever molecules approach closely. Higher thermal motion does not eliminate the interaction, but it can reduce the duration or frequency of favorable close contacts.

Q6. A graph of boiling temperature versus molecular size for a homologous series of nonpolar molecules shows a general upward trend with increasing size. Which explanation best connects the graph to intermolecular interactions?

A.Larger molecules usually have more polarizable electron clouds, increasing temporary dipole attractions ✅
B.Larger molecules become ionic as their mass increases
C.Boiling temperature rises because nonpolar molecules form permanent hydrogen bonds
D.Molecular size has no connection to electron-cloud behavior
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For many related nonpolar molecules, increasing size means more electrons and a more polarizable electron cloud. Stronger temporary dipole interactions require more energy to overcome, contributing to higher boiling temperatures.

Q7. Two nonpolar molecules have the same molecular formula but different shapes. Molecule P is compact, while molecule Q has a more extended shape that allows greater surface contact. Which prediction is most defensible?

A.P must always have stronger interactions because compact molecules contain more electrons
B.Q can have stronger overall van der Waals attraction because greater contact can allow more simultaneous weak interactions ✅
C.Neither can interact because their molecular formulas are identical
D.Q must become polar simply because its shape is extended
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Van der Waals attractions depend not only on electron count but also on how closely molecular surfaces can approach. An extended shape may provide greater contact area, allowing more weak attractions to act simultaneously.

Q8. A student compares methane and a much larger nonpolar hydrocarbon and concludes that the larger molecule must have stronger van der Waals interactions simply because it weighs more. What important reasoning step is missing?

A.The student should consider electron-cloud polarizability and molecular shape rather than mass alone ✅
B.The student should ignore the number of electrons entirely
C.The student should assume both molecules have permanent dipoles
D.The student should compare only their melting points
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Mass alone does not determine the strength of van der Waals interactions. A better analysis considers electron number, polarizability, molecular shape, and surface contact, because these factors influence temporary and induced dipoles.

Q9. A molecular simulation shows that attraction between two nonpolar molecules becomes stronger as their separation decreases, but at extremely short distances the molecules strongly resist further approach. Which interpretation best fits the model?

A.Only repulsive forces exist at every distance
B.Attraction can increase at close range, while electron-cloud and nuclear repulsions become important when molecules are pushed extremely close ✅
C.The molecules form ionic bonds at short distance
D.Van der Waals attraction becomes infinite when molecules touch
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Weak attractions become significant when molecules approach closely enough for their temporary dipoles to interact. However, pushing electron clouds too close causes strong repulsive effects, so the net interaction is not simply attractive at all distances.

Q10. A scientist wants to predict which of two nonpolar substances will require more energy to separate its molecules. Substance A has smaller, less polarizable molecules, while substance B has larger, more polarizable molecules of comparable shape. Which prediction is strongest?

A.Substance A, because smaller molecules always have stronger intermolecular forces
B.Substance B, because greater polarizability can produce stronger temporary dipole attractions ✅
C.They must require equal energy because both substances are nonpolar
D.Substance A, because nonpolar molecules cannot form induced dipoles
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Greater polarizability allows temporary electron fluctuations to produce stronger instantaneous and induced dipoles. If molecular shapes and other relevant factors are comparable, the larger, more polarizable molecules generally experience stronger van der Waals attractions.

Q11. Two scientists explain why a nonpolar liquid can remain condensed at moderate temperature. Scientist 1 says permanent dipoles hold the molecules together. Scientist 2 says temporary dipoles create many weak attractions that collectively stabilize the liquid. Who gives the better explanation?

A.Scientist 1 only, because nonpolar molecules have permanent dipoles
B.Scientist 2 only, because nonpolar molecules can experience collective temporary-dipole attractions ✅
C.Both equally, because permanent and temporary dipoles are identical
D.Neither, because liquids cannot contain nonpolar molecules
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Nonpolar molecules lack permanent dipoles, but their electrons are constantly moving. Temporary fluctuations create instantaneous dipoles, which induce dipoles in neighbors. Many such weak attractions can collectively contribute to cohesion in a liquid.
