📖 The Chemistry of Life

The Chemistry of Life begins by breaking down living organisms into their most basic chemical components, showing that all biological processes ultimately rest on the rules of chemistry and physics. This chapter first explores matter and elements, identifying the essential elements—such as carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and nitrogen—that make up the vast majority of biological molecules. It then dives into atomic structure, explaining how protons, neutrons, and electrons determine an element's properties and how electron configuration drives chemical bonding. Students will learn the difference between covalent, ionic, and hydrogen bonds, and why each type is crucial for building and stabilizing the molecules found inside cells.Moving from atoms to molecules, the chapter introduces the concept of chemical equilibrium and the role of polarity in making water the universal solvent of life. A major focus is placed on the properties of water—cohesion, adhesion, high specific heat, and expansion upon freezing—and how these unusual traits directly support life on Earth. The chapter also covers acids, bases, and pH, explaining the pH scale and why buffers are essential for maintaining the stable internal conditions that enzymes and cellular processes require. By connecting simple chemistry to living systems, this chapter provides the chemical vocabulary and conceptual framework needed to understand every biological structure and function that follows.
