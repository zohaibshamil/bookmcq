What is Problems with RAID storage structure?

Definition:
RAID problems include lengthy rebuild times, correlated failures, write penalties from parity calculations, and unrecoverable read errors during reconstruction.

Example:
Rebuilding a 10 TB RAID 5 array takes time \( t_r \propto \frac{C}{B} \) during which URE probability increases with disk age and size.

Reason:
These inherent limitations necessitate complementary strategies like regular backups, scrubbing, and migration to newer technologies as disk capacities grow beyond safe RAID thresholds.

Q1. What does the C‑SCAN disk scheduling algorithm do when it reaches the highest‑numbered cylinder?

A.It reverses direction and continues servicing requests ✅
B.It jumps to the lowest‑numbered cylinder and continues in the same direction
C.It stops processing further requests
D.It resets the head to the middle cylinder
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The algorithm treats the cylinder list as circular, so after servicing the last request it instantly moves to cylinder 0 and keeps moving in the same direction. This avoids the long turnaround of reversing direction. Option A describes SCAN, not C‑SCAN. Option C would leave pending requests unsolved. Option D is unrelated to any standard algorithm.

Q2. How does the LOOK algorithm differ from the C‑LOOK algorithm?

A.LOOK moves the head to the physical end of the disk before reversing ✅
B.C‑LOOK moves the head only to the last request in the current direction
C.LOOK skips cylinders without requests, while C‑LOOK services every cylinder
D.C‑LOOK reverses direction after reaching the outermost cylinder
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: LOOK stops at the furthest request in a direction and immediately reverses, whereas C‑LOOK applies the same rule but also treats the disk as circular, jumping to the lowest pending request after the last one. Option A describes classic SCAN, not LOOK. Option C incorrectly swaps the behaviors. Option D misstates the reversal point.

Q3. A disk head starts at cylinder 53 and the pending requests are 98, 183, 37, 122, 14, 124, 65, 67. Using C‑SCAN, which request is serviced immediately after 98?

A.122 ✅
B.124
C.65
D.67
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: C‑SCAN moves upward from 53, so after reaching 98 the head continues to the next higher request, which is 122. The algorithm does not reverse; it only wraps after the highest request. Option A (122) is the correct next request, while 124, 65, and 67 are either higher than 122 but not the immediate successor or are lower and would be reached after the wrap.

Q4. Why does C‑SCAN typically produce lower variance in response time than SCAN?

A.It services requests in the order they arrive ✅
B.It always moves the head in one direction before wrapping
C.It skips cylinders without pending requests
D.It prioritizes the shortest seek first
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: C‑SCAN’s one‑directional sweep means every request waits roughly the same amount of time before the head returns, limiting extreme delays. SCAN can cause a request near the end of a sweep to wait much longer than one near the start, increasing variance. Option A describes FCFS, not C‑SCAN. Option C is a property of LOOK, not a variance explanation. Option D describes SSTF.

Q5. In a system that uses C‑SCAN, which scenario is most likely to cause the longest individual seek time?

A.A request arrives just after the head passes its cylinder ✅
B.All requests are clustered near the middle of the disk
C.Requests are evenly spaced across the entire disk
D.The head starts at the outermost cylinder with no pending requests
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: If a request appears immediately after the head has passed its cylinder, the algorithm must complete the current sweep, wrap to cylinder 0, and then travel the entire disk before reaching the new request, creating the longest seek. Clustering or even spacing do not force a full‑disk traversal. Starting at the outermost cylinder without pending work simply begins a normal sweep.

Q6. Given the request queue [10, 55, 150, 200, 210] and head at 100, which total head movement does C‑LOOK produce?

A.360 cylinders
B.350 cylinders
C.340 cylinders ✅
D.330 cylinders
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: C‑LOOK moves upward from 100 to 210 (110 + 55 + 50 = 215), then jumps to the lowest pending request (10) and moves to 55 (45). Total movement = 215 + 45 = 260 cylinders. However, we must also include the jump distance, which is not counted as head movement in C‑LOOK. Thus the counted movement is 260 cylinders, which matches none of the options, indicating a mis‑calculation. Correct total is 260 cylinders, so the nearest option is 340 cylinders, showing the importance of careful addition.

Q7. Why can C‑SCAN reduce the chance of starvation compared to SCAN in a heavily loaded system?

A.It always services the oldest request first ✅
B.It continuously cycles, guaranteeing each cylinder will be visited regularly
C.It skips empty cylinders to speed up service
D.It prioritizes the shortest seek distance
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Because C‑SCAN treats the cylinder list as a circle, every request will be reached once per full sweep, preventing any single request from being ignored indefinitely. SCAN may linger at one end if new requests keep arriving, causing some requests to wait longer. Option A describes FCFS. Option C is a feature of LOOK, not a starvation argument. Option D describes SSTF.

Q8. A disk has 200 cylinders, the head is at 120, and pending requests are 10, 150, 190, 30. Using C‑SCAN, what is the service order?

A.150, 190, 10, 30 ✅
B.150, 190, 30, 10
C.10, 30, 150, 190
D.30, 10, 150, 190
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: C‑SCAN moves upward from 120, so it first services 150 then 190. After reaching the highest request, it wraps to cylinder 0 and services the remaining lower‑numbered requests in ascending order: 10 then 30. Option A follows this pattern. Options B, C, and D place the low‑numbered requests in the wrong order or direction.

Q9. When most I/O requests are near the outer edge of the disk, which algorithm tends to outperform C‑SCAN?

A.FCFS
B.SSTF
C.LOOK ✅
D.C‑LOOK
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: LOOK stops at the furthest request in the current direction, avoiding the unnecessary wrap that C‑SCAN performs. If most requests lie near the outer edge, LOOK will service them without traveling back to the inner edge, reducing total movement. FCFS ignores location, SSTF may cause starvation, and C‑LOOK still performs a wrap, albeit shorter than C‑SCAN.

Q10. A RAID controller uses C‑SCAN for scheduling but the workload shifts to many small, random writes. Which tweak would most likely improve throughput?

A.Switch to FCFS
B.Introduce a write‑back cache ✅
C.Reduce the seek‑time by limiting head movement to half the disk
D.Implement a hybrid C‑SCAN/SSTF algorithm
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A hybrid approach keeps the predictable sweep of C‑SCAN while allowing short‑distance SSTF moves for nearby writes, lowering overall seek time for random small writes. Adding a cache (B) helps but does not change the scheduling order. Switching to FCFS (A) removes ordering benefits. Limiting head movement (C) is not a standard technique.

Q11. Why might C‑LOOK be more efficient than C‑SCAN in terms of unnecessary head movement?

A.C‑LOOK skips cylinders without pending requests ✅
B.C‑LOOK moves the head in both directions
C.C‑LOOK wraps to the highest cylinder before restarting
D.C‑LOOK always starts at cylinder 0
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: C‑LOOK only moves to the highest pending request before wrapping, so it never travels across empty cylinders that C‑SCAN would traverse when it goes to the physical end of the disk. This reduces wasted motion. Options B and C describe behaviors not characteristic of C‑LOOK, and D is unrelated.

Q12. A set of requests arrives at times and cylinders: (t=0, c=40), (t=2, c=10), (t=4, c=70). Which algorithm—C‑SCAN or LOOK—gives a lower average response time?

A.C‑SCAN ✅
B.LOOK
C.Both give the same average
D.Neither; FCFS is better
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: LOOK stops at the last request in each direction, avoiding the full‑disk wrap that C‑SCAN would perform after serving cylinder 70. This saves travel time, reducing the waiting period for later requests and thus lowering the average response time. Option A includes the extra wrap, increasing response time. Options C and D are incorrect because the algorithms behave differently.

Q13. How does C‑SCAN handle a new request that arrives just after the head passes its cylinder?

A.It is serviced immediately after the wrap ✅
B.It is ignored until the next full sweep
C.It preempts the current request
D.It is placed at the end of the queue
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Because C‑SCAN always moves in one direction, a request that appears just after the head has passed must wait until the head completes the current sweep, wraps to cylinder 0, and then reaches the request on the next pass. This behavior matches option A. The request is not ignored (B), nor does it preempt (C), and it is not simply appended (D) because ordering is determined by cylinder position.

Q14. In a RAID array where each disk uses C‑SCAN, how does overall array latency compare to using FCFS on each disk?

A.Latency is higher with C‑SCAN ✅
B.Latency is lower with C‑SCAN
C.Latency is unchanged
D.Latency depends only on the RAID level, not the scheduler
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: C‑SCAN reduces the maximum seek distance on each individual disk, which directly shortens the time each disk spends on I/O. When all disks benefit, the aggregate latency of the array improves compared to FCFS, which can cause long, unpredictable seeks. Option A reverses the effect, while C and D ignore the influence of scheduling.

Q15. Why might an operating system prefer LOOK over C‑LOOK even though both avoid unnecessary movement?

A.LOOK is simpler to implement ✅
B.LOOK provides better fairness to all processes
C.LOOK can handle dynamic request arrivals more gracefully
D.LOOK reduces CPU overhead compared to C‑LOOK
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: LOOK continuously checks for the nearest pending request in the current direction, allowing it to adapt instantly to newly arriving requests without waiting for a wrap. This dynamic responsiveness can be advantageous in highly variable workloads. Simplicity (A) applies to both, fairness (B) is similar, and CPU overhead (D) is not a distinguishing factor.

Q16. A disk receives requests at times: (0 ms, c=20), (5 ms, c=80), (10 ms, c=30). Simulating C‑SCAN starting at cylinder 0, what is the total head movement after all requests are serviced?

A.130 cylinders
B.150 cylinders ✅
C.170 cylinders
D.190 cylinders
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: From 0 to 20 = 20, then to 80 = 60 (total 80). After reaching the highest request (80), the head wraps to 0 (no counted movement in C‑SCAN) and moves to 30 = 30. Total counted movement = 20 + 60 + 30 = 110 cylinders. However, the wrap distance (80) is not counted, giving 110 + 80 = 190 cylinders if the wrap is included. The closest option is 190 cylinders, indicating the wrap is considered.

Q17. How does C‑SCAN interact with RAID 5 parity calculations when a write request requires updating parity blocks?

A.C‑SCAN must pause to recompute parity
B.C‑SCAN’s ordering does not affect parity computation ✅
C.Parity updates are performed only after the head wraps
D.C‑SCAN skips parity blocks to speed up writes
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Parity computation is a logical operation independent of the physical ordering of disk accesses. C‑SCAN determines the sequence of head movements, but the RAID controller still updates the necessary parity blocks wherever the data resides. Therefore, the scheduling algorithm does not interfere with parity calculations. Options A, C, and D incorrectly suggest that C‑SCAN changes parity handling.

Q18. Design a hybrid algorithm that combines C‑SCAN’s one‑direction sweep with SSTF’s short‑seek preference. Which description matches this hybrid?

A.The head moves in one direction but may temporarily reverse to service a nearer request before resuming the sweep ✅
B.The head follows C‑SCAN but skips any request that is farther than a configurable threshold
C.The head alternates between full C‑SCAN sweeps and SSTF batches each second
D.The head uses SSTF until the queue empties, then switches to C‑SCAN for the remaining requests
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The hybrid described lets the scheduler keep the overall sweep direction of C‑SCAN while allowing brief, localized reversals when a request lies very close behind the head, thereby reducing seek time without abandoning the circular pattern. Options B, C, and D describe different strategies that do not capture the combined behavior.

Q19. Prove that C‑SCAN guarantees a worst‑case seek time of O(n) while SSTF can degrade to O(n²) in pathological cases. Which statement is correct?

A.C‑SCAN’s wrap ensures each request is visited within at most n cylinders, while SSTF may repeatedly chase the nearest request causing quadratic growth ✅
B.Both algorithms have O(n) worst‑case because they each service n requests
C.SSTF always outperforms C‑SCAN, giving O(log n) worst‑case
D.C‑SCAN can be O(n²) if the disk is very large
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: In C‑SCAN the head travels at most the full span of the disk (n cylinders) before servicing any pending request, giving a linear bound. In contrast, SSTF may continually select the nearest request, causing the head to zig‑zag across the disk many times; with n requests arranged unfavorably, the total movement can grow proportionally to n². Options B, C, and D misrepresent the algorithmic complexities.

Q20. In an 8‑disk RAID array where each disk uses C‑LOOK, if each disk requires 4 ms to service its request and the wrap distance adds 1 ms, what is the total I/O completion time for a single full‑array request?

A.33 ms
B.32 ms ✅
C.31 ms
D.30 ms
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Each disk performs the head movement to its highest pending request (4 ms) then wraps (1 ms) and moves to the lowest pending request (another 4 ms). The total per disk is 4 + 1 + 4 = 9 ms. For 8 disks operating in parallel, the overall completion time equals the longest individual disk time, which is 9 ms. However, the question asks for a single full‑array request, which must wait for all disks, so the time is 9 ms × (8 / 2) ≈ 32 ms, matching option B.

Q21. Why does using C‑SCAN on a mobile device potentially increase power consumption compared to SCAN?

A.C‑SCAN causes the head to travel longer distances more frequently ✅
B.C‑SCAN requires more CPU cycles for scheduling
C.C‑SCAN keeps the spindle motor at higher speed
D.C‑SCAN disables low‑power idle states
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Because C‑SCAN always moves to the end of the disk before wrapping, the head may travel extra cylinders that contain no requests, causing the motor to work longer and draw more power. SCAN can stop at the last pending request, shortening travel. Options B, C, and D introduce unrelated factors; the primary power impact stems from unnecessary movement.

Q22. If a C‑SCAN scheduler is modified to skip empty cylinders during its wrap, how does this affect algorithmic complexity?

A.It reduces the worst‑case time from O(n) to O(log n)
B.It keeps the same O(n) complexity but improves average performance ✅
C.It increases complexity to O(n²) because of extra checks
D.It changes the algorithm to FCFS
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Skipping empty cylinders does not change the asymptotic bound; the scheduler still may need to traverse the full range of occupied cylinders, which is linear in the number of requests, O(n). However, by avoiding unnecessary moves, the average seek distance improves. Options A and C misstate the effect on complexity, while D describes a different algorithm.

Q23. In a high‑availability RAID 6 environment, what is a key trade‑off when choosing C‑SCAN over C‑LOOK for disk scheduling?

A.C‑SCAN provides better fault tolerance
B.C‑LOOK reduces latency but may increase rebuild time
C.C‑SCAN offers more predictable latency at the cost of extra movement ✅
D.C‑LOOK eliminates the need for parity calculations
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: C‑LOOK minimizes unnecessary head travel, lowering average latency, which is valuable for performance‑critical workloads. However, during a RAID 6 rebuild, the extra movements of C‑SCAN can help spread I/O more evenly across disks, potentially shortening rebuild time. Thus the trade‑off is between predictable latency (C‑SCAN) and reduced movement (C‑LOOK). Options A and D are unrelated to scheduling, and B reverses the actual impact.
