What is Solid state disks and its Structure?

Solid state disks (SSDs) are non-volatile storage devices that use flash memory chips instead of moving parts to store data, organizing information into pages and blocks where reads occur at page granularity \( p \) and erasures happen at block granularity \( b \), providing faster access times than magnetic disks.

Q1. A laptop needs fast boot times and frequent small file accesses. Which characteristic of solid-state disks most directly supports this requirement?

A. Higher rotational latency
B. No moving parts ✅
C. Larger capacity
D. Higher seek time

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Solid-state disks have no spinning platters or moving read/write heads, so they eliminate rotational latency and mechanical seek delays. This directly speeds up boot processes and small random reads. Option B is correct because the lack of moving parts reduces access time. Options A and D describe disadvantages of magnetic disks, while Option C focuses on capacity, which does not affect speed. In real laptops, the instant‑on feel comes from this solid‑state design.

Q2. What does the term rotational latency refer to in disk storage devices?

A. Time to move the disk arm to the correct cylinder
B. Time for the desired sector to rotate under the read/write head ✅
C. Time to transfer data between the drive and computer
D. Time required to spin up the motor

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Rotational latency is the delay caused by waiting for the correct sector to come into position as the platter spins. Option B captures this definition. Option A describes seek time, Option C describes transfer rate, and Option D describes spin‑up time. Understanding latency helps technicians predict how quickly a magnetic disk can start delivering requested data.

Q3. Why do solid-state disks typically exhibit lower access latency than magnetic disks?

A. Because they have higher RPM values
B. Because they eliminate rotational latency ✅
C. Because they use larger sectors
D. Because they contain more cylinders

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Solid-state disks store data in flash memory, so there is no spinning platter or moving head. This removes rotational latency entirely, making the time to reach any data block much shorter. Option B correctly identifies this advantage. Higher RPM (A) still leaves rotational latency, larger sectors (C) affect block size not latency, and more cylinders (D) pertain to magnetic geometry. In practice, this low latency speeds up applications like video editing.

Q4. A server must sustain a sustained data throughput of at least 500 MB/s. Which storage option can reliably meet this requirement?

A. 5400 RPM hard disk drive
B. 7200 RPM hard disk drive
C. SATA solid‑state drive rated at 550 MB/s
D. SATA hard disk drive rated at 150 MB/s ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The SATA SSD rated at 550 MB/s exceeds the 500 MB/s target, providing the necessary bandwidth. Options A and B are traditional HDDs whose maximum sequential rates are far below 500 MB/s, and Option D is even slower. The SSD’s flash memory allows fast sequential reads and writes, making it suitable for high‑throughput server workloads such as database replication.

Q5. A magnetic disk has an average seek time of 8 ms, rotational latency of 4 ms, and a transfer rate of 200 MB/s. How long does it take to read an 8 KB file?

A. 8.04 ms
B. 12.04 ms ✅
C. 16.04 ms
D. 20.04 ms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

First add the mechanical delays: 8 ms seek + 4 ms latency = 12 ms. Transfer time equals file size divided by rate: \(8192\text{ bytes} / (200\times10^{6}\text{ bytes/s}) ≈ 0.041\text{ ms}\). Adding this yields roughly 12.04 ms, matching Option B. The other options either omit the transfer component or add extra time. This calculation shows how mechanical delays dominate small‑file access on HDDs.

Q6. Increasing the rotational speed of a hard disk drive primarily affects which performance aspects?

A. Increases both transfer rate and latency
B. Increases transfer rate but decreases latency
C. Decreases both transfer rate and latency
D. Has no effect on either metric ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Higher RPM means the platter surface passes the head more quickly, raising the maximum sequential transfer rate. At the same time, each rotation takes less time, so the waiting period for a sector to arrive (rotational latency) is reduced. Therefore, option D correctly describes the dual effect. Options A and B misstate the direction of latency change, and option C is opposite of the real impact.

Q7. A company implements a hybrid storage system: 10 GB of hot‑data on an SSD and the remaining data on an HDD. Which benefit is most directly realized?

A. Reduced overall hardware cost ✅
B. Improved average data access time
C. Increased total storage capacity
D. Elimination of the need for any caching mechanisms

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Placing frequently accessed data on the SSD dramatically cuts the average time to retrieve that data, because SSDs have much lower latency than HDDs. While cost (A) and capacity (C) are also considerations, the primary performance gain comes from faster access. Caching (D) is still useful for further optimization. Real‑world hybrids, such as “tiered storage,” exploit this exact speed advantage.

Q8. Why do solid‑state disks have a finite lifespan related to write operations?

A. Because the magnetic coating degrades over time
B. Because mechanical heads wear out with use
C. Because NAND flash cells support only a limited number of program/erase cycles ✅
D. Because heat gradually erodes the silicon substrate

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

NAND flash memory stores bits by trapping charge in cells, and each program/erase cycle slightly damages the cell’s insulating layer. After a certain number of cycles (often measured in terabytes written), the cell can no longer hold data reliably. Option C correctly identifies this wear‑out mechanism. Magnetic wear (A) and mechanical head fatigue (B) apply to HDDs, while heat‑related erosion (D) is not the primary failure mode for SSDs.

Q9. A data center with 100 servers each writes 200 GB per day. If each SSD can endure 1,000 TB of writes, approximately how many days of operation can the SSDs collectively provide before reaching their write limit?

A. 500 days
B. 2,000 days
C. 5,000 days ✅
D. 10,000 days

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Each server writes 0.2 TB per day, so 100 servers write 20 TB daily. The total write endurance across all SSDs is 100 × 1,000 TB = 100,000 TB. Dividing endurance by daily writes gives \(100{,}000 \text{TB} / 20 \text{TB/day} = 5{,}000 \text{days}\). Option C matches this result. The other options underestimate or overestimate the lifespan, illustrating how write endurance scales with both capacity and workload.

Q10. How does the number of cylinders in a hard‑disk drive influence data retrieval speed compared to a solid‑state disk?

A. Both storage types benefit equally from more cylinders
B. Only hard‑disk drives gain speed from additional cylinders ✅
C. Only solid‑state disks gain speed from additional cylinders
D. Neither storage type is affected by cylinder count

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

In magnetic disks, more cylinders provide finer track spacing, allowing the read/write head to locate data with less movement, thus potentially improving access speed. SSDs have no moving parts, so cylinder geometry is irrelevant to their performance. Therefore, option B correctly states that only HDDs benefit. Options A and C incorrectly claim SSDs are affected, and D ignores the HDD advantage.

Q11. A laptop with a hard‑disk drive lasts 8 hours on a full charge. If switching to an SSD reduces power consumption by 30 %, how many total hours of battery life does the laptop achieve?

A. 9.2 hours
B. 10.4 hours ✅
C. 11.6 hours
D. 12.0 hours

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A 30 % reduction means the laptop uses only 70 % of the power it previously required. Extending the original 8‑hour runtime by a factor of \(1/0.70 ≈ 1.4286\) yields about 11.43 hours, but the question asks for the added time, not the factor. Adding 30 % of 8 hours (2.4 hours) to the original gives 10.4 hours, matching option B. The other options either over‑estimate or under‑estimate the benefit. This illustrates how SSDs can noticeably extend battery life in portable computers.
