What is Swap space location in Storage Structure?

Definition:
Swap space location determines whether virtual memory resides in a dedicated partition, file within filesystem, or separate device affecting performance and flexibility.

Example:
A dedicated swap partition avoids filesystem overhead tfs t_{fs} tfs​ compared to a swap file but lacks dynamic resizing capability.

Reason:
Location choice trades off raw I/O performance against administrative flexibility and integration with existing storage management policies.

Q1. A system uses the SCAN algorithm with the head starting at cylinder 53 and a request queue [98, 183, 37, 122, 14, 124, 65, 67]. Which request will be serviced first after the head moves toward higher cylinders?

A.65
B.67 ✅
C.98
D.124
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The head moves upward from 53, so the first cylinder greater than 53 in the queue is 65, making it the first request served. Option B (67) is later, Option C (98) is farther away, and Option D (124) is even farther. This illustrates how SCAN serves requests in order of their physical location, reducing seek time for the nearest upcoming request.

Q2. What is the primary advantage of C‑SCAN over SCAN regarding wait‑time uniformity?

A.Serves requests on the return trip ✅
B.Returns the head instantly without servicing any requests on the return trip
C.Moves the head only in one direction
D.Uses multiple heads to service requests
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: C‑SCAN improves uniformity because when the head reaches the far end it jumps back to the beginning without handling any requests on the way back, giving every request a similar maximum wait time. Option A is false because C‑SCAN does not serve on the return. Option C describes SCAN, not C‑SCAN. Option D is unrelated to the algorithm design.

Q3. Assuming a uniform distribution of requests on a 200‑cylinder disk, which algorithm yields a lower average seek distance: SCAN or C‑SCAN?

A.SCAN ✅
B.C‑SCAN
C.Both have the same average
D.Neither, both are inefficient
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: With uniform requests, SCAN services requests while moving across the disk, so the average seek is about half the full stroke (200/2=100200/2 = 100200/2=100 cylinders). C‑SCAN must also travel the full stroke and then jump back, adding an extra 200200200 cylinders that do not service requests, raising the average. Therefore SCAN gives the lower average. Options B, C, and D are incorrect for the reasons described.

Q4. Why does request density become low near the end of a SCAN sweep?

A.Because the head speeds up at the ends ✅
B.Because those cylinders are empty by design
C.Because they have just been serviced
D.Because of hardware limits on the arm
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When the head reaches an end, the cylinders it just passed have been serviced, so few pending requests remain there, creating low density. Option A is unrelated to request density, Option B is inaccurate—cylinders are not empty by design, and Option D concerns physical constraints, not request patterns.

Q5. A system places its swap space on the outermost cylinders to reduce average seek time under SCAN. If the head starts near the middle, which placement strategy best minimizes average swap‑access time?

A.Outer cylinders ✅
B.Inner cylinders
C.Random placement
D.Evenly distributed across the disk
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Placing swap on outer cylinders means the head will encounter them early when moving outward, reducing the distance it must travel before a swap request is satisfied. Inner cylinders would be reached later, increasing latency. Random or even distribution do not guarantee early access, so they are less optimal.

Q6. What does the acronym C‑SCAN stand for?

A.Circular SCAN ✅
B.Continuous SCAN
C.Central SCAN
D.Composite SCAN
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: C‑SCAN is short for Circular SCAN, reflecting the algorithm’s behavior of moving the head in one direction, then instantly returning to the start of the disk, forming a circular service pattern. The other options are plausible-sounding but do not match the established terminology.

Q7. A disk has 500 cylinders and 20 uniformly random pending requests. Under SCAN starting at cylinder 250 and moving upward, what is the expected number of requests serviced before the head reaches the highest cylinder?

A.8
B.10 ✅
C.12
D.14
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: With a uniform distribution, half of the requests are expected to lie above the starting point (250) and half below. Therefore, the expected number of requests encountered while moving upward to the highest cylinder is 20×0.5=1020 \times 0.5 = 1020×0.5=10. Options A, C, and D deviate from this statistical expectation.

Q8. How does the choice of swap‑space location affect the performance difference between C‑SCAN and SCAN?

A.It has no effect on either algorithm
B.It improves C‑SCAN more than SCAN ✅
C.It improves SCAN more than C‑SCAN
D.It degrades both algorithms
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Placing swap space at a fixed location (e.g., at the start of the disk) benefits C‑SCAN because the algorithm always returns to that point without servicing other requests, giving a predictable, short access time. SCAN may still have to service many intervening requests, so the relative gain is larger for C‑SCAN. Options A, C, and D misstate the impact.

Q9. A system uses SCAN and has its swap area at cylinder 0. If the head is currently at cylinder 400 moving toward 0, what additional seek time is incurred to service a swap request after completing all pending requests at higher cylinders?

A.0 ✅
B.400
C.500
D.600
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Since the head is already moving toward cylinder 0, it will naturally reach the swap area after finishing higher‑cylinder requests. No extra movement is required beyond the planned sweep, so the additional seek time is zero. Options B, C, and D incorrectly add extra distance that the algorithm does not need.

Q10. In disk scheduling, what is meant by “request density” and how does it influence algorithm selection?

A.Number of pending writes per second
B.Speed of the spindle motor
C.Number of pending requests per cylinder range ✅
D.Size of the cache memory
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Request density refers to how many outstanding I/O requests are clustered within a specific range of cylinders. High density regions may benefit from algorithms that scan sequentially (like SCAN) to reduce overall seek distance, whereas low‑density areas might favor algorithms that jump directly (like LOOK). The other options describe unrelated performance factors.
