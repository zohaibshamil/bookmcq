What is Magnetic disks and its Structure?

Definition:
Magnetic disks are non-volatile storage devices consisting of circular platters coated with magnetic material, where data is organized into concentric tracks and sectors accessed by read/write heads on actuator arms.

Example:
A traditional hard disk drive (HDD) stores binary data as magnetic orientations on a spinning aluminum platter at 7200 RPM.

Reason:
This mechanical structure provides high capacity at low cost per gigabyte but introduces latency due to physical head movement and rotational delay.

Q1. What is a sector on a magnetic disk?

A.A circular track
B.The smallest addressable unit ✅
C.A group of platters
D.The spindle motor
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A sector is the smallest addressable unit that can be read or written on a magnetic disk. It is a fixed‑size block of data, typically 512 bytes or a multiple thereof. Option B captures this definition. Option A describes a track, which is a larger circular path. Option C refers to platters, the physical disks. Option D mentions the motor, which rotates the platters but is not a data unit. Understanding sectors is essential for calculating capacity and transfer rates.

Q2. How does the seek time contribute to the overall random‑access time?

A.It is the only component
B.It adds to rotational latency ✅
C.It reduces latency
D.It is unrelated
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Seek time is the interval needed for the actuator arm to move the read/write heads to the correct cylinder. It adds to the total random‑access time together with rotational latency, which is the waiting period for the desired sector to rotate under the head. Option B correctly states this additive relationship. Options A, C, and D misrepresent the role of seek time, either ignoring other components or suggesting it improves performance.

Q4. Why are tracks at the same radius on different platters grouped into a cylinder?

A.Because the heads move together, allowing simultaneous access ✅
B.Because each cylinder stores the same file
C.Because cylinders increase spin speed
D.Because cylinders reduce power consumption
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: All heads are mounted on a single actuator arm, so when the arm positions over a radius, every head aligns with a track at that radius, forming a cylinder. This enables the drive to read or write a full logical block without moving the arm again, improving throughput. Option A describes this mechanical coupling. The other options misstate the purpose of cylinders, confusing logical organization with physical effects.

Q5. A file occupies four consecutive sectors that span two adjacent tracks. Which factor most affects the total access time?

A.Seek time ✅
B.Rotational latency
C.Transfer rate
D.Number of platters
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Moving from one track to the next requires the actuator to reposition the heads, incurring additional seek time. Although rotational latency and transfer rate also play roles, the extra movement between tracks dominates the delay for this scenario. Option A correctly identifies the primary contributor. The other options either describe delays that are already accounted for (latency) or have little impact on crossing tracks.

Q6. Which factor most strongly influences the total time for a large sequential read?

A.Seek time
B.Rotational latency
C.Transfer rate ✅
D.Cylinder count
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: For a long sequential read, the drive settles after an initial seek and latency, then streams data continuously. The streaming speed is governed by the transfer rate, so it dominates the overall time. Option C captures this effect. Seek time and rotational latency are incurred only once at the start, making them far less significant for large transfers. Cylinder count does not directly affect transfer speed.

Q7. How does increasing the spindle speed affect both transfer rate and rotational latency?

A.It increases transfer rate and increases latency
B.It increases transfer rate and decreases latency ✅
C.It decreases transfer rate and increases latency
D.It has no effect on either
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A higher RPM means more surface passes under the head per second, raising the raw data transfer rate. At the same time, each rotation takes less time, so the average rotational latency (half a rotation) becomes shorter. Option B accurately reflects both effects. Options A and C reverse the relationships, and D incorrectly claims no impact.

Q8. Two identical 500 GB disks are configured in a RAID‑0 array. What is the total usable capacity and the expected throughput improvement?

A.1 TB capacity, 2× throughput ✅
B.1 TB capacity, same throughput
C.500 GB capacity, 2× throughput
D.500 GB capacity, same throughput
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: RAID‑0 stripes data across both disks, so their capacities add, giving 500 GB + 500 GB = 1 TB of usable space. Because data is read/written in parallel, the sequential throughput roughly doubles compared with a single disk. Option A correctly states both capacity and performance gains. The other options either forget the capacity addition or ignore the parallel speed benefit.

Q9. Why have solid‑state drives largely replaced magnetic disks for many workloads despite higher cost per gigabyte?

A.Because SSDs have larger storage capacities
B.Because SSDs use magnetic recording like disks
C.Because SSDs provide much lower latency and higher throughput
D.Because SSDs are easier to manufacture ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: SSDs access data electronically with no moving parts, giving access times measured in microseconds versus milliseconds for magnetic disks. This dramatic latency reduction and higher sustained throughput make SSDs preferable for performance‑critical applications, outweighing the higher price per GB. Option D captures this trade‑off. Options A and B are inaccurate; SSDs typically have lower capacities and a different technology, while manufacturing ease is not the primary driver.

Q12. Why is the arm movement synchronized across all platters in a disk drive?

A.Because the heads share the same actuator, moving them together ensures each head aligns with its cylinder simultaneously ✅
B.Because it reduces power consumption
C.Because it increases the number of sectors per track
D.Because it allows different platters to spin at different speeds
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: All read/write heads are mounted on a single actuator arm; when the arm moves, every head moves to the same radial position, forming a cylinder. This synchronization simplifies the mechanics and guarantees that data on any platter can be accessed without additional arm motion. Option A correctly explains the design. Q13. A system frequently reads small 4 KB records randomly. Which disk parameter should be improved first to reduce overall latency?

A.Seek time
B.Rotational speed (higher RPM) ✅
C.Sector size
D.Number of platters
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For small random reads, the time waiting for the correct sector to rotate under the head (rotational latency) is a major component. Raising the spindle speed shortens each rotation, thus lowering average latency. While seek time also matters, increasing RPM typically yields a larger proportional reduction for tiny accesses. Option B captures this priority. Changing sector size or platter count does not directly affect latency for existing geometry.
