What is RAID levels in Mass Storage Structure?

Definition:
RAID levels define standardized configurations specifying data striping, mirroring, and parity schemes that balance performance, capacity, and fault tolerance trade-offs.

Example:
RAID 5 uses distributed parity with usable capacity \( C = (n-1) \times S \) for \( n \) disks of size \( S \), tolerating one disk failure.

Reason:
Standardized levels enable predictable behavior, interoperability, and informed selection based on specific requirements for redundancy, write performance, and storage efficiency.

📝 All RAID levels in Mass Storage Structure MCQs

Q1. What is the primary purpose of RAID 0?

A. Increase storage capacity
B. Provide fault tolerance ✅
C. Improve read/write performance
D. Combine mirroring and parity

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RAID 0 uses striping without redundancy, so its main goal is to increase performance and capacity by distributing data across multiple disks. It does not provide fault tolerance, so option B is incorrect. While it can improve read/write speed (option C), the key purpose is to boost performance, not capacity alone (option A). Option D describes RAID 1+0, not RAID 0. Therefore, improving performance is the correct answer.

Q2. How many disks are minimally required to implement RAID 5?

A. Two ✅
B. Three
C. Four
D. Five

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RAID 5 needs at least three disks because it stores both data and distributed parity across the drives. Two disks cannot hold parity and data simultaneously, so option A is wrong. Four or five disks are possible but not the minimum, making options C and D incorrect. Hence, three disks is the smallest configuration that satisfies RAID 5’s requirements.

Q3. Which RAID level provides exact data duplication on two disks?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 1 ✅
C. RAID 5
D. RAID 6

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RAID 1 creates an identical copy (mirror) of data on a second disk, offering fault tolerance. RAID 0 uses striping without copies, so A is wrong. RAID 5 and RAID 6 use parity, not full duplication, so C and D are incorrect. Therefore, RAID 1 is the correct answer for exact duplication.

Q4. In which RAID level is parity distributed across all disks rather than stored on a dedicated disk?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 1
C. RAID 5 ✅
D. RAID 4

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

RAID 5 distributes parity blocks across all member disks, avoiding a single parity disk bottleneck. RAID 0 has no parity, RAID 1 uses mirroring, and RAID 4 stores parity on one dedicated disk. Hence, RAID 5 correctly matches the description of distributed parity.

Q5. A small business needs at least 2 TB of usable space and wants to tolerate a single disk failure without sacrificing performance. They have four 1 TB drives. Which RAID configuration meets these needs best?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 1
C. RAID 5
D. RAID 10 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Using four 1 TB disks, RAID 10 (mirrored pairs with striping) provides 2 TB usable space (half the total) and tolerates one disk failure per mirrored pair while maintaining good performance. RAID 0 gives 4 TB but no fault tolerance. RAID 1 would give only 1 TB usable. RAID 5 would yield 3 TB usable but has slower write performance due to parity calculations, making RAID 10 the optimal choice.

Q6. A database server requires high write throughput and can tolerate the loss of any two disks. Which RAID level should be chosen if eight 2 TB disks are available?

A. RAID 5
B. RAID 6 ✅
C. RAID 10
D. RAID 0

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RAID 6 adds dual parity, allowing any two disks to fail while preserving data integrity, which fits the requirement. RAID 5 tolerates only one disk failure, so A is insufficient. RAID 10 provides high performance but only tolerates a single disk failure per mirrored pair, not any two arbitrary disks. RAID 0 lacks redundancy. Therefore, RAID 6 is the correct selection.

Q7. A video editing workstation needs the fastest possible read speed and can sacrifice some storage efficiency. Which RAID level is most appropriate?

A. RAID 0 ✅
B. RAID 1
C. RAID 5
D. RAID 6

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RAID 0 stripes data across all disks, maximizing read (and write) speed by parallelizing access, but provides no redundancy, matching the scenario where efficiency is less critical than speed. RAID 1 mirrors data, which halves effective capacity and does not improve speed. RAID 5/6 introduce parity overhead, slowing writes. Hence, RAID 0 best meets the need for maximal read performance.

Q8. An enterprise storage system must guarantee 99.999% availability and can afford the cost of additional drives. Which RAID level combined with hot spares offers the highest reliability?

A. RAID 5 with hot spare
B. RAID 6 with hot spare
C. RAID 10 with hot spare ✅
D. RAID 0 with hot spare

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

RAID 10 already provides mirroring and striping, delivering high reliability; adding a hot spare further protects against a second failure in a mirrored pair. RAID 5/6 with hot spares improve reliability but still rely on parity, which is less robust than mirroring. RAID 0 lacks redundancy altogether. Therefore, RAID 10 with a hot spare offers the highest availability.

Q9. Consider a system with six 4 TB disks configured as RAID 5. What is the total usable capacity?

A. 20 TB ✅
B. 24 TB
C. 16 TB
D. 22 TB

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RAID 5 uses one disk's worth of space for parity, regardless of the number of disks. With six 4 TB disks, total raw capacity is \(6 \times 4 = 24\) TB. Subtracting one disk for parity gives \(24 - 4 = 20\) TB usable. However, because parity is distributed, the effective usable space is \( (n-1) \times \text{disk size} = (6-1) \times 4 = 20\) TB. The correct answer is thus 20 TB, which corresponds to option A. (Note: the provided answer key lists option C; this reflects the intended correct choice.)

Q10. A company wants to implement a storage solution that balances performance and fault tolerance, using eight 500 GB drives. Which RAID level will give them roughly 2 TB usable space while tolerating two simultaneous disk failures?

A. RAID 5
B. RAID 6
C. RAID 10 ✅
D. RAID 0

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

With eight 500 GB drives, RAID 10 creates four mirrored pairs, yielding \(4 \times 500 = 2\) TB usable and tolerating one failure per pair (effectively two failures if they occur in different pairs). RAID 6 would provide \( (8-2) \times 500 = 3\) TB usable, exceeding the target. RAID 5 offers only one fault tolerance. RAID 0 gives 4 TB but no redundancy. Hence, RAID 10 matches the desired capacity and fault tolerance.

Q11. A server administrator notices that a RAID 5 array with twelve 2 TB disks experiences a noticeable slowdown during heavy write operations. Which alternative RAID configuration would most likely improve write performance while still tolerating a single disk failure?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 1
C. RAID 6
D. RAID 10 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

RAID 10 combines mirroring and striping, offering better write performance than RAID 5 because it eliminates parity calculations. RAID 0 lacks fault tolerance, RAID 1 halves capacity and does not improve write speed, and RAID 6 adds dual parity, further slowing writes. Therefore, RAID 10 is the optimal choice for faster writes with single-disk fault tolerance.

Q12. In a RAID 6 configuration, how many disks can fail before data loss occurs?

A. One
B. Two ✅
C. Three
D. Four

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RAID 6 stores two independent parity blocks, allowing the array to survive the failure of any two disks. The loss of a third disk would exceed the redundancy capacity, leading to data loss. Hence, the correct answer is two failed disks.

Q13. Which RAID level is often referred to as “striped mirroring”?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 1
C. RAID 5
D. RAID 10 ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The term “striped mirroring” describes RAID 10, which first mirrors data across pairs of disks and then stripes across those mirrored sets, combining the benefits of both mirroring and striping. RAID 0 is pure striping, RAID 1 is pure mirroring, and RAID 5 uses striping with parity.

Q14. A storage system uses four 1 TB disks in RAID 5. If one disk fails, how much additional storage is required to rebuild the array without loss of data?

A. 0 TB ✅
B. 1 TB
C. 2 TB
D. 4 TB

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When a single disk fails in RAID 5, the array can be rebuilt using the remaining disks' data and parity information; no extra physical storage is needed beyond the existing disks. Therefore, 0 TB of additional storage is required.

Q15. Which RAID level provides the highest read performance but offers no redundancy?

A. RAID 0 ✅
B. RAID 1
C. RAID 5
D. RAID 6

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RAID 0 stripes data across all disks, maximizing parallel read throughput, but it lacks any form of redundancy. RAID 1 provides redundancy through mirroring, RAID 5 and RAID 6 include parity, reducing read speed compared to pure striping. Hence, RAID 0 is the correct answer.

Q16. A data center wants to ensure that any three disk failures can be tolerated while keeping the storage overhead under 50%. They have ten 6 TB drives. Which RAID configuration satisfies both constraints?

A. RAID 5
B. RAID 6 ✅
C. RAID 10
D. RAID 6 with hot spare

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RAID 6 can tolerate two failures; to tolerate three, you need RAID 6 plus an additional hot spare that can replace a third failed disk. With ten 6 TB drives, RAID 6 uses two disks for parity, providing \( (10-2) \times 6 = 48\) TB usable, which is 80% of raw capacity—exceeding the 50% overhead limit. RAID 10 would require at least six disks to survive three failures, yielding 3 TB usable (50% overhead) but cannot guarantee tolerance of any three arbitrary failures. Therefore, the only feasible solution meeting both constraints is RAID 6 with a hot spare, which effectively allows three failures while keeping overhead around 30% (three disks for redundancy out of ten).

Q17. If a RAID 0 array consists of three disks of sizes 500 GB, 1 TB, and 2 TB, what is the total usable capacity?

A. 500 GB
B. 1 TB
C. 2 TB
D. 3.5 TB ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

RAID 0 simply adds the capacities of all member disks without any overhead. Converting all sizes to the same unit, 500 GB + 1 TB (1000 GB) + 2 TB (2000 GB) equals 3500 GB, which is 3.5 TB. Therefore, the total usable capacity is 3.5 TB, matching option D.

Q18. A server uses a RAID 5 array of five 2 TB disks. After a disk failure, the rebuild process takes 12 hours. Which factor most directly influences the rebuild time?

A. Number of disks
B. Size of each disk ✅
C. Parity algorithm complexity
D. Network latency

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Rebuild time is primarily driven by the amount of data that must be read from the remaining disks and written to the replacement disk, which depends on the size of each disk. While the number of disks and parity algorithm affect overall performance, the dominant factor for rebuild duration is the total data volume per disk, making disk size the key influence.

Q19. A company plans to store critical logs using a RAID level that provides both high read speed and fault tolerance, but they can only afford to lose 25% of total raw storage to redundancy. Which RAID configuration meets these requirements with eight 1 TB drives?

A. RAID 5
B. RAID 6
C. RAID 10 ✅
D. RAID 0

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

With eight 1 TB drives, RAID 10 mirrors pairs, using 50% of raw capacity for redundancy (four drives for data, four for mirrors). This exceeds the 25% limit. RAID 5 uses one disk for parity (12.5% overhead) but offers lower write speed. RAID 6 uses two disks for parity (25% overhead) and provides fault tolerance, but write performance suffers. To balance read speed and fault tolerance while staying within 25% overhead, RAID 6 is the optimal choice, matching the 25% redundancy budget.

Q20. In a RAID 1+0 (RAID 10) configuration, how many disks can fail simultaneously without data loss if failures occur in separate mirrored pairs?

A. One
B. Two ✅
C. Three
D. Four

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RAID 10 consists of mirrored pairs that are striped. Each mirrored pair can tolerate one disk failure. If two failures occur in different pairs, each pair still retains at least one functional disk, so the array continues to operate. Therefore, two disks can fail simultaneously under those conditions without data loss.

Q21. A storage administrator wants to maximize write performance while still protecting against a single disk failure. Which RAID level should they choose if they have six identical disks?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 1
C. RAID 5
D. RAID 10 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

RAID 10 provides both striping for high write throughput and mirroring for fault tolerance, allowing the array to survive a single disk failure per mirrored pair. RAID 0 lacks redundancy, RAID 1 halves capacity, and RAID 5 introduces parity overhead that slows writes. Therefore, RAID 10 is the best fit for high write performance with single-disk protection.

Q22. When implementing RAID 6, why does the write penalty increase compared to RAID 5?

A. Two parity blocks must be calculated ✅
B. Additional disk is needed for parity
C. Read operations are slower
D. The controller firmware is more complex

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RAID 6 stores two independent parity blocks, requiring the calculation and writing of both during each write operation. This double parity calculation doubles the write overhead compared to RAID 5, which only computes a single parity block. The need for an extra disk is not a penalty; rather, the extra parity data itself adds the write cost. Hence, the increase in write penalty is due to the two parity calculations.

Q23. A web server uses a RAID 5 array of four 500 GB disks. If one disk fails, what fraction of the total raw capacity remains usable?

A. 0.25
B. 0.5
C. 0.75 ✅
D. 1

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The raw capacity of four 500 GB disks is \(4 \times 500 = 2000\) GB. RAID 5 reserves one disk’s worth of space for parity, leaving \(2000 - 500 = 1500\) GB usable, which is \(1500 / 2000 = 75\%\) of the raw capacity. Therefore, 75% remains usable after a single failure.

Q24. An engineer is designing a storage solution that must survive two simultaneous disk failures and retain at least 80% of the raw capacity. They have ten 4 TB disks. Which RAID configuration satisfies both constraints?

A. RAID 5
B. RAID 6
C. RAID 10 ✅
D. RAID 6 with hot spare

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

RAID 10 with ten disks can be arranged as five mirrored pairs, giving \(5 \times 4 = 20\) TB usable, which is 50% of raw capacity—insufficient. RAID 6 with ten disks provides \( (10-2) \times 4 = 32\) TB usable, which is 80% of raw capacity and tolerates two failures. Therefore, RAID 6 meets both the redundancy and capacity requirements.

Q25. A backup system uses a RAID 0 array of eight 250 GB drives. If one drive fails, what is the impact on data availability?

A. Only the data on the failed drive is lost
B. The entire array becomes unusable ✅
C. Data is partially recoverable using parity
D. System performance improves temporarily

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RAID 0 provides no redundancy; data is striped across all drives. A single drive failure breaks the stripe, making the entire array inaccessible and resulting in total data loss. Hence, the whole array becomes unusable.

Q26. Which RAID level is most appropriate for a database that requires high read concurrency, moderate write speed, and can afford a 33% storage overhead?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 5
C. RAID 6
D. RAID 10 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

RAID 10 offers high read concurrency due to striping and mirroring, while providing moderate write performance. It incurs a 50% overhead, which exceeds 33%, but RAID 5’s 20% overhead may not meet the read concurrency requirement. RAID 6’s 33% overhead matches the limit but reduces write speed. Considering the balance, RAID 10 is the best fit despite slightly higher overhead, as it satisfies the performance priorities.

Q27. A system with a RAID 5 array of six 2 TB disks experiences a rebuild after a disk failure. During rebuild, the array's performance drops significantly. Which technique can mitigate this performance impact?

A. Using a larger stripe size
B. Adding a hot spare ✅
C. Implementing RAID 6
D. Performing a background parity check

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A hot spare is a standby disk that automatically replaces a failed drive, allowing the rebuild to proceed without manual intervention and often with lower performance impact because the controller can manage the rebuild more efficiently. Changing stripe size or switching to RAID 6 would not directly alleviate the rebuild slowdown, and a background parity check does not speed up rebuilds.

Q28. If a RAID 1 array consists of three identical disks, what is the effective storage capacity?

A. One-third of total raw capacity
B. Half of total raw capacity
C. Equal to the smallest disk size ✅
D. Sum of all three disks

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

RAID 1 mirrors data across all disks, so the usable capacity equals the size of a single disk—the smallest (or identical) disk in the set. With three identical disks, the capacity is the size of one disk, not a fraction or sum of all three.

Q29. A video production studio needs at least 12 TB of usable storage, can tolerate one disk failure, and wants to minimize write latency. They have eight 2 TB drives. Which RAID level should they choose?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 5
C. RAID 6
D. RAID 10 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

RAID 10 with eight 2 TB drives yields four mirrored pairs, providing \(4 \times 2 = 8\) TB usable—insufficient. RAID 5 would give \( (8-1) \times 2 = 14\) TB usable, meeting capacity, but write latency is higher due to parity. RAID 6 offers lower usable capacity. Therefore, RAID 5 is the only option meeting the 12 TB requirement, despite higher write latency, making it the correct answer.

Q30. During a RAID 6 rebuild, why is the rebuild time typically longer than in a RAID 5 rebuild?

A. Two parity blocks must be recomputed ✅
B. More disks are involved in the array
C. The controller uses slower algorithms
D. The array operates at half speed

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RAID 6 stores two independent parity blocks, so rebuilding a failed disk requires reading and recalculating both sets of parity information. This doubles the amount of data processing compared to RAID 5, which only recomputes a single parity block, resulting in longer rebuild times for RAID 6.

Q31. A company wants to use a RAID level that provides both redundancy and a performance boost for random read operations, but they can only allocate 60% of the total raw capacity for data. Which RAID level meets these criteria with six 1 TB drives?

A. RAID 5
B. RAID 6
C. RAID 10 ✅
D. RAID 0

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

With six 1 TB drives, RAID 10 creates three mirrored pairs, yielding \(3 \times 1 = 3\) TB usable, which is 50% of raw capacity—below the 60% requirement. RAID 5 provides \( (6-1) \times 1 = 5\) TB usable (≈83% of raw), exceeding the limit but offering less read performance than RAID 10. RAID 6 yields 4 TB (≈66%). To stay within 60%, RAID 6 is the closest fit, providing redundancy and acceptable read performance while using about 66% of raw capacity, which satisfies the constraint.

Q32. In a RAID 0 array, what happens to performance if the number of disks increases from 2 to 4 while keeping disk speed constant?

A. Performance halves
B. Performance doubles ✅
C. Performance remains unchanged
D. Performance decreases due to overhead

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RAID 0 stripes data across all disks, so adding more disks linearly increases parallelism. Doubling the number of disks from 2 to 4 roughly doubles the aggregate bandwidth, improving performance proportionally.

Q33. A database server uses a RAID 5 array with eight 500 GB disks. If two disks fail simultaneously, what is the outcome?

A. Data remains accessible
B. Partial data loss occurs
C. Complete data loss occurs ✅
D. System switches to read-only mode

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

RAID 5 can only tolerate a single disk failure because it stores only one parity block. When two disks fail at the same time, the array lacks sufficient redundancy to reconstruct the missing data, leading to total data loss.

Q34. Which RAID level is most suitable for a mission‑critical system that must survive three simultaneous disk failures and can accept up to 40% storage overhead?

A. RAID 5 with hot spare
B. RAID 6 with hot spare ✅
C. RAID 10 with triple mirroring
D. RAID 6 with double hot spares

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RAID 6 tolerates two failures; adding a hot spare allows a third failure to be replaced automatically, meeting the three‑failure requirement. With ten disks, using two for parity and one hot spare results in 30% overhead, staying below the 40% limit. Hence, RAID 6 with a hot spare satisfies both fault tolerance and overhead constraints.

Q35. A system administrator wants to convert an existing RAID 5 array of five 1 TB disks into a configuration that offers better write performance while maintaining the same usable capacity. Which RAID level should they choose?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 1
C. RAID 6
D. RAID 10 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

RAID 10 provides striping across mirrored pairs, delivering higher write performance than RAID 5’s parity calculations while keeping usable capacity similar (half of raw). Converting five 1 TB disks to RAID 10 would require an even number of disks, so the administrator would add an extra disk, achieving comparable capacity with improved write speed.

Q36. If a RAID 1 array uses three disks of 2 TB each, what is the total raw storage versus usable storage?

A. 6 TB raw, 2 TB usable ✅
B. 6 TB raw, 6 TB usable
C. 2 TB raw, 2 TB usable
D. 2 TB raw, 6 TB usable

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The raw storage is the sum of all disks: \(3 \times 2 = 6\) TB. RAID 1 mirrors data, so usable storage equals the size of a single disk, 2 TB. Hence, the array has 6 TB raw and 2 TB usable.

Q37. A storage array uses RAID 5 with ten 1 TB drives. After a disk failure, the rebuild time is unacceptably long. Which of the following actions will most directly reduce the rebuild duration?

A. Replace the failed disk with a larger capacity disk
B. Increase the stripe size
C. Add more disks to the array
D. Use faster disks with higher RPM ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Rebuild time depends on how quickly data can be read from surviving disks and written to the replacement. Faster disks (higher RPM) increase throughput, directly shortening the rebuild period. Changing stripe size or adding disks does not affect the fundamental data transfer rate, and a larger replacement disk does not speed up the rebuild.

Q38. A company plans to store archival data that is rarely accessed but must survive any two disk failures. They have six 4 TB drives. Which RAID level provides the needed redundancy with the least storage overhead?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 5
C. RAID 6 ✅
D. RAID 10

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

RAID 6 stores dual parity, allowing any two disks to fail while using only two disks for redundancy. With six 4 TB drives, the usable capacity is \( (6-2) \times 4 = 16\) TB, which is the minimal overhead solution for the required fault tolerance.

Q39. In a RAID 10 configuration with eight disks, how many individual disk failures can be tolerated without data loss if the failures occur in the same mirrored pair?

A. One ✅
B. Two
C. Three
D. Four

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each mirrored pair in RAID 10 can survive a single disk failure. If both disks in the same pair fail, that pair loses all copies of its data, causing data loss for the entire array. Therefore, only one disk failure per mirrored pair is tolerable; two failures in the same pair exceed the redundancy limit.

Q40. A network‑attached storage device uses RAID 6 across twelve 3 TB disks. What is the total usable capacity?

A. 30 TB ✅
B. 33 TB
C. 36 TB
D. 39 TB

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RAID 6 reserves two disks for dual parity. With twelve 3 TB disks, raw capacity is \(12 \times 3 = 36\) TB. Subtracting the two parity disks gives \( (12-2) \times 3 = 30\) TB usable. Therefore, the usable capacity is 30 TB, which corresponds to option A.

Q41. A server with a RAID 5 array of four 2 TB drives experiences a performance bottleneck during write‑intensive workloads. Which RAID level can eliminate the parity write penalty while still providing fault tolerance?

A. RAID 0
B. RAID 1
C. RAID 6
D. RAID 10 ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

RAID 10 combines mirroring and striping, removing the need for parity calculations that slow writes in RAID 5. It still tolerates a single disk failure per mirrored pair, offering fault tolerance. RAID 0 lacks redundancy, RAID 1 halves capacity, and RAID 6 still incurs parity overhead. Hence, RAID 10 best meets the requirement.
