📝 C SCAN scheduling in Disk Scheduling

📖 From Operating System • 10. Mass Storage Structure • 8 questions available

What is C SCAN scheduling in Disk Scheduling?

Definition:
Circular SCAN (C-SCAN) moves the arm in only one direction servicing requests, then returns to the beginning without servicing on the return trip to provide uniform wait times.

Example:
After reaching cylinder 199, C-SCAN resets to cylinder 0 with reset time \( t_{reset} \) before continuing upward service.

Reason:
C-SCAN offers more predictable latency distribution than SCAN by treating the disk as circular, benefiting interactive workloads despite slightly lower throughput.

Q1. What does the C‑SCAN disk‑scheduling algorithm stand for?

A. Circular SCAN ✅
B. Constant Scan
C. Concurrent Scan
D. Cylindrical Scan

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Circular SCAN is the correct expansion of C‑SCAN. The other choices are invented terms that do not correspond to the algorithm. Knowing the name helps identify the method, which moves the disk arm in one direction, jumps to the start, and repeats, similar to a circular elevator. This basic fact is often asked in introductory OS quizzes, and recalling it distinguishes C‑SCAN from other scheduling strategies.

Q2. How does C‑SCAN differ from the traditional SCAN algorithm after the disk arm reaches the highest‑numbered request?

A. It reverses direction and services lower‑numbered requests immediately.
B. It continues in the same direction, jumps to the lowest cylinder, and resumes servicing. ✅
C. It stops and waits for new requests before moving again.
D. It randomly selects the next request regardless of cylinder order.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

C‑SCAN differs by keeping the head moving in one direction; when it reaches the highest request, it goes to the outermost cylinder, then jumps to the innermost cylinder without servicing any intermediate requests, and continues upward. This contrasts with SCAN, which reverses direction and services pending lower‑numbered requests. The jump prevents the head from repeatedly traversing the same range, reducing variance in response times.

Q3. Why does C‑SCAN usually provide more uniform wait times than FCFS?

A. It always services the closest request first.
B. It groups requests by process ID.
C. It services requests in a single circular direction, preventing long jumps. ✅
D. It prioritizes reads over writes.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

C‑SCAN moves the head in one consistent direction, servicing all requests encountered before wrapping to the beginning. Because every request is served in the same order each rotation, no request suffers an unusually long wait caused by a far‑away request that might appear early in FCFS. This uniform traversal leads to more predictable, evenly distributed wait times across all processes, unlike FCFS where a single distant request can delay many others.

Q4. Given pending requests at cylinders 55, 58, 18, 90, and 150, with the head currently at 50 moving upward on a 0‑199 cylinder disk, what is the total head movement using C‑SCAN?

A. 167
B. 216
C. 366 ✅
D. 420

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Starting at 50, the head moves upward to 55 (5), then to 58 (3), 90 (32), and 150 (60). It continues to the highest cylinder 199 (49), then jumps to cylinder 0 (199) and finally moves to 18 (18). Adding these distances gives 5+3+32+60+49+199+18 = 366 cylinders traveled. The other totals correspond to incomplete or mis‑ordered calculations, so 366 is the correct total head movement.

Q5. A disk has cylinders 0–199. Requests are at 40, 10, 70, 130, 160, and 190. The head starts at 120 and moves upward. What is the total distance the head travels under C‑SCAN?

A. 338 ✅
B. 300
C. 280
D. 250

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With the head at 120 moving upward, C‑SCAN serves 130 (10), 160 (30), and 190 (30) before proceeding to the last cylinder 199 (9). It then jumps to cylinder 0 (199) and services the remaining lower requests: 10 (10) and 40 (30) and finally 70 (30). Summing these moves: 10+30+30+9+199+10+30+30 = 338 cylinders, which is the correct total distance. All other options miss one or more segments of the journey.

Q6. In a workload dominated by large sequential writes, which advantage makes C‑SCAN preferable to LOOK?

A. It reduces rotational latency by skipping cylinders.
B. It guarantees that each cylinder is visited exactly once per cycle.
C. It minimizes the number of direction changes, lowering mechanical wear. ✅
D. It always chooses the shortest seek distance.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

C‑SCAN keeps the head moving in a single direction, only changing direction when it reaches the outermost cylinder and then instantly jumps to the innermost. This eliminates the frequent reversals that LOOK performs each time the highest pending request is serviced. Fewer direction changes reduce mechanical stress and wear on the actuator, making C‑SCAN especially attractive for workloads with long sequential writes where the head already moves in one direction for extended periods. The other options describe benefits not specific to C‑SCAN.

Q7. For requests at cylinders 95, 180, 34, 11, and 123 with the head at 50 moving upward, which algorithm yields the lower average seek time?

A. FCFS
B. C‑SCAN ✅
C. Both give the same average
D. Cannot be determined without more data

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Under FCFS the head travels 45 + 85 + 146 + 23 + 112 = 411 cylinders, giving an average of 82.2 cylinders per request. Using C‑SCAN, the head serves 95 (45), 123 (28), 180 (57), then moves to 199 (19), jumps to 0 (199), and finally serves 11 (11) and 34 (23), totaling 382 cylinders and an average of 76.4 cylinders. Because C‑SCAN’s average is lower, it provides better overall performance for this set of requests. The other choices either overestimate or claim insufficient information, which is incorrect.

Q8. On a disk with cylinders 0–499, pending requests are at 10, 220, 340, and 470. The head is at 200 moving upward. What is the total head movement using C‑SCAN?

A. 600
B. 720
C. 808 ✅
D. 650

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Starting at 200, the head moves to 220 (20), then to 340 (120), and to 470 (130). It continues to the outermost cylinder 499 (29), jumps to cylinder 0 (499), and finally moves to 10 (10). Adding these distances yields 20+120+130+29+499+10 = 808 cylinders traveled. The other answer choices omit one or more segments of the path, so 808 is the correct total head movement.
