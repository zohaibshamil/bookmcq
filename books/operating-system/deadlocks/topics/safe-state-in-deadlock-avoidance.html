What is Safe State in Deadlock Avoidance?

Definition:
A state is safe if there exists at least one sequence of processes \( \langle P_{\sigma(1)}, P_{\sigma(2)}, ..., P_{\sigma(n)} \rangle \) such that each process can complete using currently available plus held resources.

Example:
With Available \( = 3 \) and processes needing \( \{2, 4, 1\} \), sequence \( \langle P_3, P_1, P_2 \rangle \) is safe because \( P_3 \) finishes first, releasing resources for others.

Reason:
The safe state concept is the core invariant of avoidance algorithms; maintaining safety ensures the system can always progress to completion without entering a deadlock.
