What is Deadlocks and Starvation in Semaphore?

Definition:
Deadlock occurs when two or more processes are permanently blocked, each waiting for a resource held by another. Starvation happens when a process is indefinitely postponed from accessing a resource, often due to unfair scheduling or priority issues. Both are critical issues in semaphore-based synchronization.

Working:
Deadlock arises if processes hold one resource and wait for another in a circular chain. Starvation occurs if the semaphore queue is not managed fairly, allowing some processes to bypass others repeatedly. Proper ordering of resource requests and fair queuing can mitigate these issues.

Example:
Deadlock: P1 holds S1 and waits for S2. P2 holds S2 and waits for S1. Neither can proceed. Starvation: In a priority-based system, low-priority processes may never get signaled if high-priority processes continuously acquire and release the semaphore.

Reason:
Understanding these phenomena is crucial for designing robust systems. Deadlocks halt system progress, while starvation degrades performance and fairness. Recognizing the conditions for deadlock (mutual exclusion, hold and wait, no preemption, circular wait) helps in prevention strategies.

Common MisConceptions:
Students often think deadlocks only happen with semaphores. They can occur with any resource allocation. Also, starvation is sometimes confused with deadlock; in starvation, the system makes progress, but some processes don't, whereas in deadlock, no progress is made.

Exam Preparation Tips: How to prepare this topic
Learn the four necessary conditions for deadlock. Practice identifying potential deadlocks in code. Understand how to break circular wait by ordering resources. Differentiate clearly between deadlock and starvation with examples.

Key Terms:

  • Deadlock - permanent blocking of processes waiting for each other.
  • Starvation - indefinite postponement of a process.
  • Circular Wait - chain of processes each waiting for next.

📝 All Deadlocks and Starvation in Semaphore MCQs

Q1. A semaphore protects a printer. Process P repeatedly acquires and releases it for short tasks, while process Q waits for a long printing job. The scheduler consistently favors P whenever the printer becomes free. What problem may Q experience?

A.Deadlock is guaranteed because P releases the semaphore
B.Starvation, because Q may repeatedly lose access despite the printer becoming available ✅
C.Mutual exclusion failure, because P uses the printer frequently
D.Resource exhaustion, because the semaphore count must increase indefinitely
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The printer becomes available repeatedly, so the system is not necessarily deadlocked. However, if scheduling or semaphore selection continually favors P, Q may never acquire the printer. This is starvation caused by unfair allocation.

Q2. Two processes need semaphores X and Y. P always requests X before Y, while Q always requests Y before X. Which design change most directly removes the circular-wait possibility?

A.Require every process to acquire X before Y
B.Increase both semaphore counts without changing resource ownership
C.Make both processes wait indefinitely before requesting any resource
D.Require all processes to acquire resources in one globally consistent order ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A consistent global resource order prevents one process from holding a later resource while waiting for an earlier resource when another process follows the opposite order. This breaks the circular-wait condition needed for the described deadlock.

Q3. A system contains four processes, each holding one resource and waiting for another resource held by the next process in a cycle. Every resource is non-shareable, and no process releases its resource before obtaining the requested one. Which conclusion is strongest?

A.The circular dependency can produce deadlock even if the CPU remains idle ✅
B.The system cannot deadlock because four processes are involved
C.The system is experiencing starvation only, because resources are non-shareable
D.The scheduler can always resolve the cycle by selecting the oldest process
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Each process waits for a resource held by another process in the cycle. Since resources are non-shareable and held while waiting, no process can advance to release its resource. CPU scheduling alone cannot break this dependency cycle.

Q4. A semaphore implementation wakes the newest waiting process whenever a permit is released. An older process has waited through 30 releases, but newer processes repeatedly acquire the permit first. Which statement best describes the situation?

A.Deadlock must have occurred because the older process is not running
B.The semaphore has necessarily lost its permit
C.Starvation is possible even though permits continue to be released and acquired ✅
D.The older process must eventually win because all waits are atomic
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The system continues making progress because other processes acquire the released permit, so deadlock is not established. The older process may nevertheless wait indefinitely if the wake-up policy consistently favors newer arrivals, demonstrating starvation.

Q5. Process A acquires semaphore S1 and then requests S2. Process B acquires S2 and then requests S1. A timeout eventually forces B to release S2. What is the likely effect of this intervention?

A.It guarantees that A will never be blocked again
B.It breaks the immediate circular wait and may allow one process to progress ✅
C.It proves that no deadlock existed before the timeout
D.It converts both semaphores into counting semaphores
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Forcing B to release S2 can make that resource available to A, breaking the current circular dependency. The intervention may permit progress, although repeated conflicts or poorly designed recovery could still create future deadlocks.

Q6. A developer argues that using a fair FIFO semaphore eliminates every possible deadlock. Which response is most accurate?

A.Correct, because FIFO ordering prevents processes from holding resources while waiting
B.Correct, because every deadlocked process eventually reaches the front of the queue
C.Incorrect, because FIFO fairness cannot resolve a cycle in which each process holds a resource needed by another ✅
D.Incorrect, because FIFO semaphores cannot provide mutual exclusion
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: FIFO ordering can improve fairness among processes waiting for the same semaphore, but it does not eliminate circular dependencies across multiple semaphores. Processes may still hold resources while waiting for one another, so deadlock remains possible.

Q7. A process acquires semaphore A, performs a long computation, and then requests B. Another process holds B and waits for A. Why does the long computation increase the risk of system-wide delay?

A.It guarantees that the first process will release A before requesting B
B.It reduces the number of processes that can wait
C.It extends the time A remains unavailable while another process may be blocked in a dependency cycle ✅
D.It makes the semaphore count independent of resource ownership
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The first process continues holding A throughout the computation. If another process holds B and requests A, the second process remains blocked; once the first requests B, a circular wait can form. Long holding times increase the duration and likelihood of serious blocking.

Q8. A graph records the number of completed jobs per minute. Total throughput stays positive, but job Q remains incomplete for several hours while newer jobs finish. What is the best interpretation?

A.Deadlock is certain because one job has not finished
B.The system may exhibit starvation even while overall progress continues ✅
C.No synchronization problem can exist if throughput is positive
D.Q must be blocked by a hardware failure rather than semaphore policy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Positive throughput shows that at least some jobs continue completing, which argues against a total system deadlock. A particular job can still be repeatedly bypassed by scheduling or resource allocation and remain unfinished, which is consistent with starvation.

Q9. Three processes use semaphores as follows: P holds A and waits for B; Q holds B and waits for C; R holds C and waits for A. No process can release its held semaphore until its next request succeeds. Which change most directly removes this particular deadlock cycle?

A.Increase the CPU time slice for every process
B.Allow each process to retry its wait more frequently
C.Require every process to release all held semaphores before waiting for another one ✅
D.Make semaphore operations non-atomic
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A process that releases held resources before waiting does not maintain the hold-and-wait condition in this scenario. Removing that condition breaks the described cycle, whereas faster scheduling or more frequent retries cannot make unavailable resources appear.

Q10. A semaphore protects a single resource. Process P owns it and is actively executing a finite critical section, after which it will release the semaphore. Process Q waits. Is this situation necessarily a deadlock?

A.Yes, because Q cannot enter immediately
B.Yes, because every semaphore wait creates circular wait
C.No, because P can finish and release the resource, allowing Q to proceed ✅
D.No, because a semaphore cannot block a process
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A blocked process does not imply deadlock. If P continues executing and eventually releases the semaphore, Q can proceed. Deadlock requires a persistent dependency preventing progress, not merely temporary waiting for an occupied resource.

Q11. A program uses semaphores A and B in different orders in two functions. Testing rarely reveals a problem, but under high concurrency the program sometimes freezes with each function waiting for the other semaphore. What is the best diagnosis?

A.A deadlock caused by inconsistent resource acquisition order ✅
B.Starvation caused only by CPU priority
C.A harmless delay caused by atomic operations
D.A counting error that necessarily fixes itself after one signal
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Different acquisition orders can create a circular wait when concurrent executions hold different semaphores and request one another's resources. The intermittent nature is typical of deadlock caused by a particular timing interleaving.

Q12. A semaphore has a FIFO queue, but a high-priority process repeatedly preempts the process that was awakened. The awakened process never receives enough CPU time to finish and release another resource. What can still occur?

A.FIFO queueing guarantees eventual completion regardless of CPU scheduling
B.Starvation due to scheduling can occur despite FIFO semaphore ordering ✅
C.Deadlock is impossible whenever process priorities exist
D.The semaphore must automatically increase its count to compensate
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: FIFO ordering governs selection from the semaphore's waiting queue, not necessarily CPU allocation after a process becomes ready. A scheduler that continually preempts a process can prevent it from making progress, producing starvation despite fair queue ordering.

Q13. A system designer wants to prevent deadlocks among processes that request several semaphores. Which strategy is a prevention technique rather than merely a recovery technique?

A.Terminate a process after a deadlock has been detected
B.Restore a checkpoint after the system freezes
C.Require all resources to be requested in a fixed global order ✅
D.Restart the entire system when no job completes
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A global ordering rule prevents circular wait before it occurs. Termination, rollback, and restart are recovery responses after a problem has occurred, whereas a consistent acquisition order changes the system design to prevent the specified deadlock condition.

Q14. A process holds semaphore S while waiting for a message from another process. The message sender needs S before it can send the message. Neither process has an alternate path. Which condition is central to the resulting deadlock?

A.Circular wait combined with holding a resource while waiting for an event that depends on that resource ✅
B.The presence of a counting semaphore rather than a binary semaphore
C.The use of a message queue instead of shared memory
D.The fact that one process has a lower numerical process identifier
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The holder of S waits for a message that cannot be sent until S becomes available. Since the holder will not release S until the message arrives, the dependency forms a cycle. The key issue is the resource-dependent wait, not the process identifiers.

Q15. A resource manager detects that P and Q are deadlocked. It terminates P, which releases the semaphore P held, allowing Q to continue. What is this action classified as?

A.Deadlock prevention
B.Starvation avoidance
C.Deadlock recovery through process termination ✅
D.Fairness enforcement through FIFO ordering
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Terminating a deadlocked process releases its resources and may break the circular dependency, allowing another process to continue. Because the action is taken after detecting deadlock, it is a recovery measure rather than prevention.

Q16. A process waits repeatedly on a semaphore that is signaled by several competing processes. Every signal is consumed by a different process that arrives later. The semaphore never remains unavailable for long. What is the most precise concern?

A.The semaphore count is necessarily corrupted
B.The waiting process may starve even though the resource is repeatedly released ✅
C.All processes must be deadlocked simultaneously
D.The resource is permanently unavailable to every process
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Repeated release and acquisition demonstrate that the resource is available at times. If the same waiting process continually loses the opportunity to acquire it, the system may be making progress overall while that process suffers starvation.

Q17. A process holds a semaphore for a database transaction and then waits for a second semaphore used by a reporting task. The reporting task holds the second semaphore while waiting for the first. Which change is most likely to reduce the risk without changing the database's results?

A.Increase the duration for which both semaphores are held
B.Make both processes acquire the second semaphore first according to a common ordering rule ✅
C.Remove all signal operations from both processes
D.Allow each process to decrement both semaphores even when unavailable
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A consistent acquisition order prevents the two processes from taking resources in opposite orders. If both acquire the second semaphore before the first, one cannot hold the first while waiting for the second in the conflicting pattern, breaking the described circular wait.

Q18. An engineer proposes giving every process equal CPU time to eliminate starvation in a system that uses multiple semaphores. Why is this insufficient by itself?

A.Equal CPU time cannot guarantee fair access to resources or prevent circular wait among semaphores ✅
B.Equal CPU time always causes deadlock
C.Semaphore queues become unnecessary under round-robin scheduling
D.Equal CPU time guarantees that every wait succeeds immediately
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Fair CPU scheduling can reduce starvation caused by lack of processor time, but a process may still be blocked waiting for a semaphore held by another process. Circular resource dependencies can cause deadlock regardless of how fairly the CPU is shared.

Q19. A process holds semaphore A, waits for B, and is part of a cycle. An administrator increases the priority of that process without changing any resource ownership. What is the most likely result?

A.The deadlock is necessarily resolved because the process runs sooner
B.The process can still remain blocked because higher priority does not make B available ✅
C.The semaphore count becomes positive automatically
D.Every other process is forced to release all semaphores
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Priority affects scheduling only when a process is runnable. A process blocked waiting for B cannot proceed merely because its priority rises, particularly if B is held by another process that is itself waiting in the cycle.

Q20. A log shows P waiting for S1, Q waiting for S2, and R waiting for S3. The ownership records show that S1 belongs to Q, S2 belongs to R, and S3 belongs to P. What does this graph-like dependency information indicate?

A.A circular wait exists among P, Q, and R ✅
B.Only P is starving, while Q and R are guaranteed to progress
C.All three semaphores are free
D.The processes are waiting only for CPU time
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: P waits for S1 held by Q, Q waits for S2 held by R, and R waits for S3 held by P. These dependencies form a closed cycle, so none can obtain its requested semaphore unless an external action breaks the cycle.

Q21. A process repeatedly fails to acquire a semaphore because a stream of higher-priority processes acquires it first. The semaphore is released after every short critical section. Which intervention most directly addresses the stated cause?

A.Use a fair waiting policy or suitable priority management for semaphore acquisition ✅
B.Add more circular dependencies between resources
C.Allow the semaphore owner to signal without acquiring it
D.Increase the number of times the waiting process polls the semaphore
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The stated problem is unfair access under priority competition, not a circular resource dependency. A fair queue or appropriate priority-inheritance or priority-management policy can reduce repeated bypassing, although the best mechanism depends on the operating system and workload.

Q22. A system has two identical resources represented by a counting semaphore initialized to 2. Two processes each acquire one permit and then wait for the other to finish. Neither process requests another resource, and both will release their permit after completing a finite computation. Are they deadlocked?

A.Yes, because both semaphore permits are consumed
B.Yes, because two processes always deadlock when two resources exist
C.No, if both can complete their computations without needing another permit, they can release their resources ✅
D.No, because counting semaphores cannot cause deadlock
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The processes may both hold permits without deadlocking if their remaining computations do not depend on acquiring additional resources. Deadlock requires a blocking dependency that prevents progress, not simply the fact that every available permit is currently in use.

Q23. A circular-wait detector identifies a cycle, but one process in the cycle can release its resource voluntarily without needing another resource. What should the analyst conclude?

A.The cycle alone proves that permanent deadlock must occur
B.The system may escape the cycle if that process actually releases its resource ✅
C.The process cannot release a resource while a cycle exists
D.The detector has proved that starvation is impossible
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A dependency cycle is a strong warning, but the ability of a process to release resources independently can break it. Whether the system is truly deadlocked depends on the processes' actual behavior and resource-release rules, not only on a snapshot of dependencies.

Q24. A system records each process's waiting time for a semaphore. One process's waiting time grows steadily while other processes continue to acquire and release the semaphore. Which measurement would best support a diagnosis of starvation?

A.The total number of completed jobs alone
B.The semaphore's initial value only
C.Evidence that the same process is repeatedly bypassed despite recurring resource availability ✅
D.The number of CPUs in the system without scheduling data
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Starvation is process-specific: a process can wait indefinitely while others continue progressing. Repeated bypassing despite available opportunities provides stronger evidence than overall throughput or the semaphore's initial count, neither of which directly reveals fairness for an individual waiter.

Q25. A system avoids deadlock by imposing a global order on semaphores. A developer then uses a non-FIFO queue for each semaphore. Which statement is correct?

A.The global order prevents every form of starvation automatically
B.The global order can prevent circular wait, but an unfair queue may still permit starvation ✅
C.Non-FIFO queues always create deadlock
D.The ordering rule removes the need to signal semaphores
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A global resource order addresses circular wait and can prevent the described class of deadlock. It does not ensure that each process eventually receives a permit. An unfair semaphore queue can still repeatedly bypass a particular waiter, causing starvation.

Q26. A program detects a deadlock and releases every semaphore held by one selected process without terminating it. The process continues executing as if it still owns those resources. Why is this recovery strategy unsafe?

A.The process may access resources after ownership has been revoked, causing corruption or race conditions ✅
B.The released semaphores can no longer be acquired by any process
C.The process automatically stops executing when a semaphore is signaled
D.The strategy guarantees FIFO scheduling for all waiters
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Releasing a process's resources while allowing it to continue under the assumption that it still owns them breaks the ownership protocol. The process may modify shared state concurrently with new owners. Safe recovery typically coordinates rollback, termination, or explicit resource reacquisition.

Q27. A semaphore waiting-time graph shows process A's wait increasing continuously, process B's wait repeatedly returning to zero, and process C alternating between short waits and execution. What is the most reasonable interpretation?

A.All three processes must be deadlocked
B.Process A may be starving, while B and C continue to make progress ✅
C.Process B is necessarily deadlocked because its waiting time returns to zero
D.The graph proves that the semaphore count is always negative
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A's continually increasing wait while other processes make progress suggests starvation. B's repeated return to zero indicates successful progress, and C's alternating pattern is compatible with intermittent waiting. Waiting-time patterns alone do not prove the semaphore count or a complete deadlock.

Q28. A resource policy prevents circular wait but permits a process to be postponed indefinitely by higher-priority arrivals. Which evaluation best distinguishes the two outcomes?

A.The policy may prevent deadlock while still allowing starvation ✅
B.Preventing circular wait automatically guarantees fairness
C.Starvation and deadlock are identical terms
D.Higher-priority arrivals cannot affect semaphore acquisition
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Preventing circular wait removes one necessary condition for deadlock, but it does not ensure that every waiting process eventually obtains a resource. A process may be repeatedly bypassed by higher-priority arrivals even though the system continues to make progress.

Q29. A process holds semaphore A and requests B. Another process holds B and requests A. The system administrator decides to increase both semaphore counts by one while both processes are blocked. Why is this not a sound general solution?

A.Increasing counts can misrepresent actual resource availability and allow unsafe concurrent access without reliably resolving ownership ✅
B.The counts cannot change while any process is blocked
C.Increasing counts always causes every process to terminate
D.Semaphore values have no relationship to resource availability
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Semaphore counts must represent available permits or follow a well-defined waiting-count convention. Artificially increasing counts does not release the resources held by the processes and may allow unauthorized access. Safe recovery must break the dependency cycle while preserving resource consistency.

Q30. A program uses a semaphore to coordinate a one-time event. The consumer waits first, and the producer later signals after completing the event. In a separate execution, the producer signals before the consumer begins waiting. Assuming a counting semaphore, what is the expected behavior in both executions?

A.The consumer proceeds only in the first execution
B.The consumer can proceed in both executions because a permit can remain available after an early signal ✅
C.The second execution necessarily deadlocks
D.The producer must repeat the signal until the consumer begins waiting
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A counting semaphore retains a signal as an available permit when no process is waiting. Thus, the consumer can consume the permit whether it was already blocked when the producer signaled or began waiting afterward, provided no other operation consumed the permit.

Q31. A process holds semaphore S1 while waiting for an external event. Another process needs S1 to generate that event. An engineer proposes increasing the first process's CPU priority. What is the key flaw in this proposal?

A.The blocked process cannot generate CPU progress merely through priority, and the event producer still cannot acquire S1 ✅
B.Priority increases always reduce semaphore counts
C.The external event will occur automatically when priority changes
D.The proposal converts S1 into a fair semaphore
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Priority changes affect scheduling but do not release held resources. If the event producer needs S1 and the holder waits for that event before releasing S1, the dependency remains circular. The protocol must change so the event can occur without requiring the held resource.

Q32. A semaphore system uses a fair queue, but one process never releases a permit because it waits forever for user input while inside the protected region. What is the most direct problem?

A.Fairness in the waiting queue cannot help other processes while the permit is never released ✅
B.The FIFO queue forces the holder to release its permit automatically
C.The system cannot experience blocking because the queue is fair
D.The semaphore must grant all waiters a permit simultaneously
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Queue fairness determines which waiting process is selected when a permit becomes available. It cannot create a permit if the current owner never releases it. Holding a resource indefinitely can block all other processes and may create a deadlock if the holder's wait depends on them.

Q33. A developer claims that deadlock detection is unnecessary if every semaphore wait has a timeout. Which answer is most accurate?

A.Timeouts guarantee that resource ownership remains consistent after every failure
B.Timeouts can help detect or escape prolonged waits, but recovery and safe resource handling are still required ✅
C.Timeouts ensure that all processes acquire semaphores in FIFO order
D.Timeouts prevent circular wait from forming in the first place
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Timeouts can reveal that progress has stalled and may allow a process to abandon a wait. However, they do not automatically restore consistent shared state, release resources safely, or prevent circular dependencies. Recovery logic is still required.

Q34. Two processes require semaphores A and B. A policy lets a process acquire A only when it can also reserve B atomically; otherwise it acquires neither. What is the principal benefit of this policy?

A.It prevents hold-and-wait for this resource pair by avoiding partial acquisition ✅
B.It guarantees that all processes run at the same speed
C.It makes semaphore signals unnecessary
D.It guarantees that no process can ever be starved
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Acquiring both resources atomically or acquiring neither prevents a process from holding one while waiting for the other. This removes hold-and-wait for the specified pair and can prevent the corresponding deadlock pattern, although fairness still depends on the allocation policy.

Q35. A monitoring system shows no completed jobs for a long interval. Every process is blocked waiting for a semaphore held by another blocked process, and ownership records form a closed cycle. Which diagnosis is best supported?

A.Starvation only, because no process is executing
B.Deadlock, because blocked dependencies form a cycle and no process can release the needed resource ✅
C.Normal contention, because semaphores always cause long waits
D.Successful scheduling, because all processes have equal priority
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The evidence shows that every process is blocked by another process in a closed resource-dependency cycle. Since no process can execute the action needed to release its resource, the system cannot make progress without external intervention or recovery.

Q36. A semaphore's wake-up policy selects waiters randomly. In a long-running test, most processes progress, but one process repeatedly loses the selection and remains blocked. What is the most appropriate conclusion?

A.Random selection guarantees bounded waiting for every process
B.Starvation may occur because random selection does not guarantee that every waiter is selected within a finite time ✅
C.Deadlock is proven because one process remains blocked
D.The semaphore is incorrect unless it uses a negative count
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Random selection may be fair in a probabilistic sense over long periods, but it does not guarantee that a particular process will be selected within a bounded interval. Repeatedly losing selection can leave a process waiting indefinitely while others progress.

Q37. A process requests semaphore A, acquires it, and then waits for semaphore B. Before requesting B, it could have completed a small operation and released A, but it keeps A unnecessarily. What design lesson best applies?

A.Hold resources only as long as necessary, and avoid retaining one while waiting for unrelated resources when safe to release it ✅
B.Always acquire every semaphore twice
C.Use higher process identifiers to indicate ownership
D.Never release a semaphore until the program exits
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Unnecessarily retaining A while waiting for B increases contention and may contribute to hold-and-wait cycles. Releasing A before the wait, when correctness permits, reduces the set of resources held during blocking and can improve concurrency or eliminate a specific deadlock dependency.
