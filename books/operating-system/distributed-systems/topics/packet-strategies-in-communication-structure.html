What is Packet strategies in Communication Structure?

Definition:
Packet strategies govern how data is fragmented, sequenced, and reassembled during transmission, involving packet size \( S_p \) and header overhead \( H \).

Example:
TCP segmenting large file transfers into numbered packets with checksums for reliable delivery.

Reason:
Proper packetization balances efficiency against reliability, ensuring data integrity while optimizing network utilization in distributed communications.

Q1. What is the nominal data transfer rate of a T1 line?

A.1.544 megabits per second ✅
B.45 megabits per second
C.622 megabits per second
D.40 gigabits per second
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A T1 line carries 1.544 Mbps, which is the standard rate for a single T1 circuit. The other options describe faster links (T3, OC‑12, and backbone gigabit speeds). Because the question asks for the nominal rate, the 1.544 Mbps value is correct. Knowing this rate helps students compare it with higher‑capacity links and decide when multiple T1s must be aggregated to meet larger bandwidth needs.

Q2. Why are WAN connections typically slower than LAN connections?

A.Because WANs use older hardware
B.Because LANs have more routers
C.Because WANs cover longer distances and often traverse multiple media ✅
D.Because LANs use higher‑frequency signals
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: WANs span cities or continents, so signals travel longer distances and must pass through many different media (fiber, copper, radio). Each hop adds latency and may introduce lower‑speed segments, whereas LANs are confined to a single building or campus and can use high‑speed, low‑latency media. The other choices either misstate hardware age or router count, which are not the primary cause of speed differences.

Q3. A small business needs a guaranteed 30 Mbps connection. Which of the following choices best meets the requirement with the fewest links?

A.Use a single T3 line (45 Mbps)
B.Aggregate 20 T1 lines
C.Use a single OC‑12 line (622 Mbps)
D.Use a single T1 line ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A single T3 provides 45 Mbps, exceeding the 30 Mbps need while using only one physical circuit, making it the most efficient choice. Aggregating 20 T1s would be costly and complex, and a single OC‑12, though faster, supplies far more capacity than required and is usually more expensive. A single T1 cannot meet the bandwidth demand. The answer demonstrates practical decision‑making based on required throughput and cost.

Q4. How does aggregating several T1 lines increase overall throughput?

A.It reduces latency
B.It adds the individual data rates together ✅
C.It changes the protocol to UDP
D.It creates a higher‑capacity virtual circuit
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When multiple T1 circuits are combined, their capacities sum, so two T1s give roughly 2 × 1.544 Mbps ≈ 3.1 Mbps, and more lines add proportionally. This technique, often called channel bonding or link aggregation, raises the total bandwidth available to applications. The other options describe effects that do not directly result from simple aggregation: latency reduction, protocol changes, or virtual circuit creation are not the primary mechanism for increasing raw data rate.

Q5. In a cellular data network, a user’s device can receive up to 10 Mbps, while the backbone WAN can carry 40 Gbps. Which part of the path is most likely to limit the user’s speed?

A.The backbone WAN
B.The cell tower’s radio interface ✅
C.The core router’s processing speed
D.The user’s operating system
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The radio link between the handset and the tower is typically the bottleneck because wireless spectrum and air‑interface protocols constrain the maximum rate to around 10 Mbps for many devices. The backbone’s 40 Gbps capacity is far higher, and modern routers can process traffic well above 10 Mbps. The operating system may affect performance, but the dominant limitation is the wireless interface, illustrating how the slowest segment dictates overall throughput.

Q6. When transmitting over a T3 link (45 Mbps) or an OC‑12 link (622 Mbps), which packet payload size gives the highest link utilization if each packet incurs an 80‑byte header and the maximum MTU is 1500 bytes?

A.1500 bytes ✅
B.1000 bytes
C.500 bytes
D.200 bytes
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Link utilization is payload ÷ (total size). With an 80‑byte header, a 1500‑byte payload yields 1500 ÷ (1500 + 80) ≈ 0.95, the highest possible ratio. Smaller payloads increase the proportion of overhead, reducing efficiency. The calculation is the same for both T3 and OC‑12 because utilization depends on packet composition, not raw link speed. Therefore, using the maximum MTU of 1500 bytes maximizes throughput on either link.

Q7. What is the primary advantage of packet switching for connecting LANs to WANs?

A.It guarantees fixed delivery times ✅
B.It requires no routers
C.It allows each packet to take a different path, improving flexibility
D.It eliminates the need for error checking
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Packet switching breaks data into independent packets that can travel via different routes, adapting to congestion and failures, which is essential when linking local LANs to wide‑area networks that may have variable conditions. Fixed delivery times (A) are more characteristic of circuit switching, while routers are still needed (B). Error checking (D) remains necessary regardless of switching style. The flexibility of dynamic routing is the key benefit.

Q8. A company wants a private WAN that maximizes reliability. Should it use a single OC‑12 link or two redundant T3 links in parallel? Which option provides greater fault tolerance?

A.Single OC‑12 link
B.Two redundant T3 links ✅
C.Both provide equal reliability
D.Use multiple OC‑12 links
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Two T3 links placed in parallel give redundancy: if one T3 fails, traffic instantly shifts to the other, preserving connectivity. A single OC‑12, while offering higher bandwidth, has no backup; a failure would cut the entire link. Redundant paths are the classic method for increasing reliability, so the dual‑T3 configuration is the more fault‑tolerant choice.
