About 1. Introduction to Operating System

An Operating System (OS) is the most important software that runs on a computer. It acts as a manager for all the hardware parts, like the CPU, memory, and storage devices, and it provides a friendly environment for you to run your programs. Think of it as the boss of the computer; without it, your computer is just a collection of electronic parts that can't do anything useful. Its main job is to make sure that all the different parts work together smoothly and that your applications (like games, browsers, or word processors) get the resources they need to run properly.

The OS also acts as a bridge between you and the complex hardware. Instead of you having to understand difficult technical commands, the OS gives you a simple interface, like a desktop with icons and windows. It hides all the messy details of how the hardware actually works, so you can focus on your tasks. It also makes sure that if one program crashes, it doesn't bring down the whole computer. In short, the OS is the foundational layer of software that makes a computer powerful and user-friendly.
