Q1. A company stores payroll data on a server. An employee accidentally copies the file to a USB drive and loses it. Which type of security violation does this represent?

🔹 A. Breach of confidentiality
🔹 B. Breach of integrity
🔹 C. Breach of availability
🔹 D. Breach of non‑repudiation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The incident exposes payroll information to an unauthorized party, which is a breach of confidentiality. The data itself was not altered, so integrity is unchanged, and the system remained operational, so availability is unaffected. Non‑repudiation deals with proving actions, not relevant here. Thus option A correctly classifies the violation, illustrating how accidental data loss still threatens confidentiality in real situations.

Q2. What does it mean for a system to be secure?

🔹 A. Resources are used and accessed as intended under all circumstances
🔹 B. All data on the system is encrypted at rest
🔹 C. The system has no known vulnerabilities
🔹 D. The system is immune to any future attacks

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A secure system is defined by the proper use and access to its resources in every situation. Encryption, lack of vulnerabilities, and immunity are desirable goals but do not alone guarantee security. The definition focuses on intended usage, which is why option A is correct. Recognizing this helps students evaluate security beyond isolated technical controls.

Q3. Which term describes a person attempting to break into a computer system?

🔹 A. Intruder
🔹 B. Administrator
🔹 C. User
🔹 D. Auditor

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

An intruder is the standard term for someone who tries to gain unauthorized access to a system. Administrators and users have legitimate roles, while auditors inspect systems without breaching them. Therefore option A accurately names the attacker, reinforcing the vocabulary needed to discuss security threats.

Q4. A malicious attacker exploits a buffer overflow to gain root privileges. Which security principle is being violated?

🔹 A. Least privilege
🔹 B. Defense in depth
🔹 C. Confidentiality
🔹 D. Availability

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Exploiting a buffer overflow bypasses multiple protective layers, indicating that the system’s defense in depth failed. While least privilege also relates to limiting rights, the primary issue is that several safeguards (e.g., input validation, OS protections) did not stop the attack. Hence option B is correct, showing how layered defenses are critical in real‑world security.

Q5. Why is accidental misuse generally easier to protect against than malicious misuse?

🔹 A. Because accidental misuse follows intended usage patterns
🔹 B. Because malicious users have more resources
🔹 C. Because accidental misuse is less frequent
🔹 D. Because policies can prevent accidental misuse

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Accidental misuse typically occurs within the bounds of normal system behavior, making it easier to detect and restrict with existing controls. Malicious actors deliberately seek to subvert controls, often employing sophisticated techniques that policies alone cannot stop. Therefore option A best explains the difference, helping students appreciate why preventive measures are more effective for mistakes than for intentional attacks.

Q6. What is the term for the potential for a security violation?

🔹 A. Threat
🔹 B. Attack
🔹 C. Vulnerability
🔹 D. Exploit

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A threat represents the possibility that a security breach could occur, such as the existence of a vulnerability or an adversary’s intent. An attack is the actual execution of harmful activity, while a vulnerability is a specific weakness, and an exploit is a tool used to take advantage of that weakness. Option A correctly identifies the concept of potential risk, a key idea in risk assessment.

Q7. A company discovers that an employee can read files they shouldn't. Which mechanism could prevent this?

🔹 A. Access control lists
🔹 B. Encryption of data at rest
🔹 C. Auditing logs
🔹 D. Physical locks

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Access control lists (ACLs) define which users may access particular files, directly preventing unauthorized reading. Encryption protects data if it is accessed, but does not stop an authorized user from reading it. Auditing logs record activity after the fact, and physical locks guard hardware, not file permissions. Thus option A is the appropriate preventive measure, illustrating how permission systems block confidentiality breaches.

Q8. How does a breach of confidentiality differ from a breach of integrity?

🔹 A. Confidentiality is about unauthorized reading; integrity is about unauthorized modification
🔹 B. Confidentiality concerns data loss; integrity concerns data theft
🔹 C. Confidentiality involves encryption; integrity involves authentication
🔹 D. Confidentiality and integrity are the same concept

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A confidentiality breach occurs when data is read without permission, while an integrity breach occurs when data is altered without authorization. Data loss and theft describe different outcomes, and encryption or authentication are tools rather than definitions. Option A accurately contrasts the two core security properties, helping students distinguish between reading and modifying unauthorized data.

Q9. An organization implements multi‑factor authentication to reduce risk of credential theft. Which type of security violation does this primarily address?

🔹 A. Breach of confidentiality
🔹 B. Breach of integrity
🔹 C. Breach of availability
🔹 D. Breach of non‑repudiation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Multi‑factor authentication adds extra proof of identity, making it harder for attackers to obtain valid credentials and thus preventing unauthorized reading of protected information. It does not directly prevent data alteration, service disruption, or provide proof of actions. Therefore option A correctly identifies the primary protection against confidentiality breaches, showing the practical impact of stronger authentication.

Q10. In the security context, what is the relationship between a \threat\ and an \attack\?

🔹 A. A threat is a potential; an attack is the realization of that threat
🔹 B. A threat is the same as an attack
🔹 C. An attack precedes a threat
🔹 D. A threat is a type of attack

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A threat describes the possibility that a system could be compromised, while an attack is the concrete act that attempts to exploit that possibility. They are not interchangeable; an attack follows a threat, not the other way around. Option A captures this cause‑effect relationship, which is essential for understanding how risk assessments translate into real‑world incidents.

Q12. A system uses discretionary access control (DAC). An attacker gains privileges by convincing a user to run malicious code. Which security principle is compromised?

🔹 A. Principle of least privilege
🔹 B. Principle of need‑to‑know
🔹 C. Principle of accountability
🔹 D. Principle of separation of duties

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

DAC relies on users granting permissions, and when a user runs malicious code, they inadvertently grant more rights than necessary, violating the principle of least privilege. Need‑to‑know, accountability, and separation of duties address different aspects of access control. Option A correctly identifies the broken principle, illustrating how social engineering can undermine technical safeguards.

Q13. Why can total security never be achieved, even with strong mechanisms?

🔹 A. Users may violate policies
🔹 B. Hardware can fail
🔹 C. Attackers adapt
🔹 D. All of the above

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Each listed factor limits absolute security: human error or non‑compliance can bypass controls, hardware failures can expose vulnerabilities, and attackers continuously develop new techniques. Because these elements coexist, no combination of mechanisms can guarantee perfection. Option D captures the comprehensive reason, helping students understand why security is about risk reduction rather than elimination.

🔹 B. Integrity
🔹 C. Availability
🔹 D. Authentication

Confidentiality and integrity concern data secrecy and correctness, while authentication deals with verifying identities. Therefore option C is correct, demonstrating how availability is a critical pillar that attacks can target.

Q16. How does \defense in depth\ help mitigate both accidental and malicious security violations?

🔹 A. By providing multiple layers of protection
🔹 B. By encrypting all data at rest
🔹 C. By limiting user rights to the minimum necessary
🔹 D. By logging every operation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Defense in depth relies on several independent safeguards—such as firewalls, access controls, and monitoring—so that if one layer fails, others remain. This approach reduces the chance that either an accidental mistake or a deliberate attack will succeed. The other options describe single‑layer techniques, making option A the most comprehensive explanation.

Option A correctly identifies the primary impact, highlighting the real‑world danger of input validation failures.

Q27. How does encryption help maintain confidentiality?

🔹 A. By making data unreadable without the key
🔹 B. By preventing modification of data
🔹 C. By ensuring data is always available
🔹 D. By authenticating users

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Encryption transforms plaintext into ciphertext that cannot be understood without the correct decryption key, thereby protecting the data from unauthorized readers and preserving confidentiality. It does not inherently stop changes (integrity), guarantee uptime (availability), or verify identities (authentication). Option A correctly describes the confidentiality benefit of encryption.

Q28. A web app uses role‑based access control (RBAC). A user with the \manager\ role can approve expenses up to $10,000, but an attacker escalates their role to \director\ to approve $100,000. Which security principle is violated?

🔹 A. Least privilege
🔹 B. Need‑to‑know
🔹 C. Separation of duties
🔹 D. Defense in depth

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RBAC is intended to grant only the minimum permissions required for a role. By elevating the attacker’s role, the system no longer enforces the principle of least privilege, allowing excessive authority. Need‑to‑know, separation of duties, and defense in depth address different aspects of security. Thus option A correctly identifies the violated principle, illustrating the risks of role escalation.

Option A correctly captures the multi‑layer failure.
