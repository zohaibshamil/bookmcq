📖 The Second World War 1939-1945

The Second World War: Global Campaigns and Allied Victory

The Second World War transformed international politics, military strategy, economies, and societies between 1939 and 1945. Germany's invasion of Poland initiated a European conflict that expanded through Axis campaigns in Western Europe, North Africa, the Balkans, and the Soviet Union. Japanese expansion brought the United States into the war, while Allied industrial capacity, military coordination, and strategic offensives gradually reversed Axis victories. The Holocaust, nuclear warfare, unconditional surrenders, and destruction of European empires reshaped the international order and established the United States and Soviet Union as superpowers.

Background: Versailles, Fascist Expansion and Appeasement

Students should understand the Treaty of Versailles, the rise of Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy, Japanese imperial expansion, the policy of appeasement, the Munich Agreement, and the Nazi-Soviet Pact of August 1939. Knowledge of the invasion of Poland, the European alliance system, British and French military commitments, and Germany's earlier territorial expansion explains the immediate outbreak of war. Familiarity with the Great Depression, industrial warfare, antisemitism, Nazi racial ideology, and the political development of the Soviet Union is also essential for understanding wartime policies and consequences.

Learning Outcomes: Major Fronts, Turning Points and War's Impact

Students should be able to explain the major military campaigns in Europe, Africa, Asia, and the Pacific, compare Axis and Allied strategies, and identify the turning points at Midway, El Alamein, and Stalingrad. They should analyse the importance of Allied industrial production, naval control, air superiority, and coordination. They should also explain the Holocaust, distinguish intentionalist and functionalist interpretations, assess the use of atomic weapons, and analyse the political, economic, and social consequences of the war.

Campaigns: Poland, the Battle of Britain, Barbarossa and the Pacific War

  • Outbreak and German Victories, 1939–1941 - Poland, the Phoney War, Scandinavia, France, and the Battle of Britain.
  • Axis Expansion and Globalisation, 1940–1941 - North Africa, Greece, Operation Barbarossa, and Pearl Harbor.
  • Turning Points, 1942–1943 - Midway, El Alamein, Stalingrad, Atlantic warfare, and Allied industrial mobilisation.
  • Holocaust and Wartime Atrocities - Nazi racial policies, the Final Solution, and German and Japanese atrocities.
  • Allied Offensive and Axis Defeat, 1943–1945 - Italy's collapse, strategic bombing, D-Day, and Germany's defeat.
  • Final Campaigns and Surrenders, 1945 - Allied invasion of Germany, atomic bombings, and Japan's surrender.
  • Postwar International Transformation - Destruction, social changes, decolonisation, superpowers, and United Nations formation.

Study Sequence: From the Invasion of Poland to the Axis Defeat

Begin with Germany's invasion of Poland and the Phoney War, followed by campaigns in Denmark, Norway, Belgium, the Netherlands, and France. Examine the Battle of Britain before studying Axis expansion into North Africa, Greece, and the Soviet Union. Continue with Pearl Harbor and the expansion of the conflict into a global war. Study naval warfare, Midway, El Alamein, and Stalingrad as major turning points. Then examine the Holocaust, Allied strategic bombing, Italy's collapse, D-Day, and the invasion of Germany. Finish with Japan's defeat, the use of nuclear weapons, wartime consequences, and the formation of the postwar international order.

How Allied Production and Multiple Fronts Reversed Axis Gains

German victories in 1939–1941 expanded the conflict but created increasingly demanding military commitments across several fronts. The invasion of the Soviet Union and attack on Pearl Harbor brought powerful opponents into direct warfare against the Axis. Allied industrial production, naval control, and strategic coordination supported the turning points of 1942–1943. Subsequent offensives liberated occupied territories, defeated Germany, and forced Japan's surrender.

Timeline: Invasions, Turning-Point Battles and Allied Victory

Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939, prompting Britain and France to declare war on 3 September. The Phoney War ended with German attacks on Denmark and Norway on 9 April 1940. Germany invaded France through Belgium on 10 May, and France signed an armistice on 22 June. The Battle of Britain followed from July to October 1940. Germany invaded the Soviet Union on 22 June 1941, while Japan attacked Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941, bringing the United States into the war.

Why World War II Reshaped Superpower Politics and Decolonisation

The conflict explains the collapse of European military dominance, the emergence of American and Soviet superpower influence, and the acceleration of decolonisation. Its military, political, and humanitarian consequences provide essential background for understanding the Cold War, the United Nations, nuclear deterrence, postwar reconstruction, and the changing international order after 1945.

Common Misconceptions: The Phoney War and US Entry

Students sometimes claim that 'the Phoney War was a formal peace agreement'; it describes the relative inactivity on the Western Front before Germany's April 1940 offensives. 'The United States entered the war in 1939' confuses American material support with its formal entry after Pearl Harbor in December 1941. Finally, 'Italy's surrender in 1943 ended the fighting in Italy'; German forces continued resistance against Allied armies.

Exam Focus: Midway, El Alamein, Stalingrad and D-Day

Common questions require arranging campaigns chronologically, matching battles with locations and outcomes, and identifying the significance of Pearl Harbor, Midway, El Alamein, and Stalingrad. Students should compare Axis and Allied military resources, explain the importance of D-Day, and assess strategic bombing. Holocaust questions examine the Final Solution and historical interpretations, while analytical questions address atomic weapons, unconditional surrender, and the political consequences of Allied victory.

Summary: Axis Defeat and the Postwar World

Axis expansion initially produced extensive territorial victories, but simultaneous campaigns against the Soviet Union and the Western Allies stretched German resources. American intervention, Soviet resistance, and Allied industrial superiority gradually reversed the balance. Military defeat destroyed the Axis powers and ended the European and Pacific wars. Mass atrocities, nuclear weapons, economic devastation, and imperial decline transformed global politics and established the foundations of the postwar international system.

Key Operations, Battles and Leaders of World War II

  • Operation Barbarossa - Germany's invasion of the Soviet Union launched on 22 June 1941.
  • Battle of Britain - The 1940 air campaign in which the Royal Air Force resisted German efforts to gain air superiority over Britain.
  • Battle of Midway - The June 1942 naval battle in which American forces inflicted a major defeat on the Japanese carrier fleet.
  • Battle of Stalingrad - The 1942–1943 battle that ended with the surrender of Germany's encircled Sixth Army and marked a major Soviet victory.
  • D-Day and Operation Overlord - The Allied landing in Normandy on 6 June 1944 that opened a major Western Front in France.
  • Final Solution - The Nazi policy of systematically murdering Europe's Jewish population through mass shootings, deportations, and extermination camps.
  • Intentionalist and Functionalist Interpretations - Two historical approaches debating the roles of Hitler's intentions, Nazi ideology, institutions, and wartime radicalisation in the Holocaust.
  • United Nations - The international organisation established in 1945 to promote peace, security, cooperation, and international law.

War Archives, Holocaust Records and Military Collections

  • United States Holocaust Memorial Museum - Explore Holocaust Encyclopedia entries, survivor testimony, Nazi documentation, deportation records, and evidence concerning the Final Solution.
  • Imperial War Museums Collections - Search original photographs, military records, soldiers' diaries, naval documents, aircraft histories, and civilian accounts from the European and Pacific theatres.
  • United States National Archives - Examine Pearl Harbor investigation records, wartime military correspondence, D-Day planning documents, surrender instruments, and atomic bombing records.
  • German Federal Archives - Investigate Nazi government records, military operational documents, occupation policies, and evidence concerning German war crimes.
  • International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg - Study trial transcripts, documentary evidence, indictments, and judgments concerning Nazi aggression and wartime atrocities.
  • Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library - Explore wartime correspondence, Lend-Lease records, Allied conferences, and American decisions concerning intervention and postwar planning.
  • United Nations Charter and San Francisco Conference Records - Examine the original 1945 Charter, founding negotiations, and the establishment of the postwar international organisation.
