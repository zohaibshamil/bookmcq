What is Italian strikes 1919-1920 Biennio Rosso?

The Biennio Rosso, or 'Two Red Years,' was a period of intense social conflict where workers occupied factories and peasants seized land in fear of a communist revolution, which ultimately terrified the middle class and industrialists into supporting Mussolini’s Fascists as a force to restore law and order.

📝 All Italian strikes 1919-1920 Biennio Rosso MCQs

Q1. In which year did the wave of strikes in Italy begin?

A. 1918
B. 1919 ✅
C. 1920
D. 1921

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The strike wave started in the aftermath of World War I, when Italian workers faced inflation and unemployment. Historical records pinpoint 1919 as the initial year when mass walkouts began, setting the stage for the larger unrest that continued into 1920.

Q2. What political party was formed in January 1921 that influenced perceptions of revolution?

A. Italian Socialist Party
B. Italian Communist Party ✅
C. Liberal Party
D. Christian Democratic Party

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Italian Communist Party was officially established in January 1921. Its creation intensified fears among landowners and the bourgeoisie that a Bolshevik‑style revolution was imminent, even though the actual revolutionary potential was already diminishing after the failed strikes.

Q3. In which Italian city did factory councils resembling Russian soviets appear?

A. Milan
B. Rome
C. Turin ✅
D. Naples

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Turin became a focal point for radical labor organization, where workers formed factory councils that mirrored the Russian soviets. These councils attempted to manage production directly, reflecting the influence of revolutionary ideas spreading across Europe after 1917.

Q4. Which sector's workers seized land from wealthy owners in the south?

A. Industrial workers
B. Farmworkers ✅
C. Fishermen
D. Miners

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

In southern Italy, agrarian workers organized into socialist leagues that seized estates from large landowners. By occupying the land and establishing cooperatives, they aimed to address long‑standing inequalities and demonstrate the reach of the broader strike movement beyond industrial centers.

Q5. What was a primary reason the strikes began to fizzle out?

A. Lack of raw materials ✅
B. Overwhelming government support
C. High wages
D. International pressure

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The occupations faltered because essential supplies, especially raw materials, were cut off by external suppliers. Without these inputs, factories could not operate efficiently, and the lack of engineers further hampered any attempt to sustain production under worker control.

Q6. Analyze why the formation of the Communist Party in 1921 reduced the likelihood of a revolution despite increasing fear.

A. It unified left forces
B. It split left forces ✅
C. It gained mass support
D. It received foreign aid

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Although the Communist Party's formation heightened anxieties about a leftist uprising, it also fragmented the previously unified socialist movement. This division diluted collective bargaining power, making coordinated revolutionary action less feasible and thereby reducing the actual likelihood of a successful revolt.

Q7. Which of the following best describes the government's prestige during the strike wave?

A. Rising
B. Stable
C. Declining ✅
D. Unchanged

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

During the 1919‑1920 strike period, the Italian government's reputation suffered dramatically. Its inability to protect private property and maintain order led many observers to view the state as weak, which contributed to a broader loss of confidence among the middle and upper classes.

Q8. If suppliers had continued providing raw materials to occupied factories, which outcome would be most likely?

A. Successful worker control ✅
B. Government collapse
C. Increased foreign investment
D. Return to pre‑strike conditions

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

If suppliers had continued delivering raw materials, workers might have maintained higher output levels, strengthening their claim to factory control. Sustained production could have legitimized the occupations, potentially forcing the government to negotiate more favorable labor concessions.

Q9. How did the strikes impact property owners' perception of political stability?

A. They felt more secure
B. They feared a left‑wing revolution ✅
C. They were indifferent
D. They supported the strikes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The intense unrest convinced many property owners that a radical left‑wing revolution was looming. Their perception shifted from economic concern to political alarm, prompting some to support more authoritarian measures in hopes of restoring order and protecting their assets.

Q10. Which statement accurately reflects the relationship between the strikes and the Italian Socialist Party's strength?

📖 Explanation:

The strikes energized the Italian Socialist Party, providing a platform to mobilize workers and articulate demands. By demonstrating the capacity for organized collective action, the movement bolstered the party's influence within the labor sector and national politics.

Q12. Which factor most directly contributed to the inability of workers to maintain production during occupations?

B. Lack of political support
C. Poor weather
D. High taxes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Engineers and technical specialists were essential for running complex industrial processes. Their absence, combined with shortages of raw inputs, meant that workers could not sustain the intricate production cycles required, leading to a rapid decline in output during occupations.

D. All of the above

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Evidence of looting and rioting points to immediate economic distress among workers, suggesting that material hardship was a primary catalyst. While political ideology played a role, the tangible need for food and wages drove many to participate in the strikes.

Q14. Analyze the long‑term effect of the 1919‑1920 strikes on the rise of Fascism in Italy, considering the weakening of leftist unity.

A. Directly caused Fascism's rise
B. Created power vacuum exploited by Fascists ✅
C. Strengthened democratic institutions
D. Had no impact

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The fragmentation of the left after the Communist Party's creation left a power vacuum that Fascist leaders, notably Mussolini, exploited. By presenting themselves as the only force capable of restoring order, they attracted support from those disillusioned by the ineffective leftist movements.

Q15. If the Italian government had successfully protected property owners during the strikes, how might the political landscape of early 1920s Italy differ?

B. Socialist movements would lose momentum
C. Fascist movement would be less appealing ✅
D. No change

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Had the government successfully defended property owners, the motivation for radical change would have been diminished. A more stable environment could have reduced the appeal of extremist alternatives, making the emerging Fascist movement less attractive to the populace.

Q16. The strikes involved approximately 1.2 million workers. If 25 % were in the north, how many workers participated in northern strikes?

A. \(300{,}000\) ✅
B. \(250{,}000\)
C. \(400{,}000\)
D. \(350{,}000\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With a total of \(1.2\) million participants, calculating 25 % of that figure yields \(0.25 \times 1.2\text{ million} = 300{,}000\) workers involved in northern strikes, highlighting the significant regional concentration of labor unrest.

Q17. Considering that suppliers refused raw materials and workers needed engineers, which economic theory best explains the failure of the occupations?

A. Marxist theory of class struggle
B. Supply chain dependency theory ✅
C. Keynesian demand‑side economics
D. Institutional economics

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The failure of the occupations can be explained by supply chain dependency theory, which emphasizes that production hinges on uninterrupted access to inputs. When suppliers withdrew raw materials, the worker‑run factories could not function, regardless of political intent.

Q18. Imagine the Communist Party had not split the left forces but instead formed a united front with the Socialist Party. Predict the likely impact on the 1919‑1920 strike outcomes.

D. Neither A nor B

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A united front between the Communist and Socialist parties would have consolidated resources and political influence. This cohesion could have forced the government to negotiate reforms while also improving the chances of establishing lasting worker control over factories.
