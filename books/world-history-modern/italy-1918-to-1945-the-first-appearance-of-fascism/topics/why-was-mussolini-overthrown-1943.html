Q1. What date did the Fascist Grand Council vote to remove Mussolini?

🔹 A. 25-Jul-43
🔹 B. 24-Jul-43
🔹 C. 26-Jul-43
🔹 D. 23-Jul-43

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Grand Council convened on 24 July 1943 and passed a motion of no confidence against Mussolini. This decision led to his arrest the following day, marking the decisive political break that ended his dictatorial rule. The vote reflected mounting elite disillusionment and the military setbacks that had eroded his support.

Q3. What economic hardships did the Italian public face during the war?

🔹 A. Shortage of steel
🔹 B. Massive inflation
🔹 C. Food rationing
🔹 D. All of the above

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The civilian population endured a combination of severe shortages, soaring inflation, and compulsory food rationing. Taxes were raised to fund the war effort, prices surged, and real wages fell dramatically. These intertwined pressures created a climate of scarcity and resentment that eroded popular support for the fascist regime.

📖 Explanation:

The Allied invasion of Sicily in July 1943 demonstrated that the Axis powers could no longer defend Italian territory. This military catastrophe convinced many fascist leaders that continuing the war was untenable, prompting the Grand Council to turn against Mussolini and ultimately resulting in his dismissal by the king.

Q8. Why could Italy’s industrial capacity not sustain a prolonged war?

Q9. If inflation was \(20\%\) and real wages fell by \(30\%\), what was the net loss in purchasing power?

🔹 A. \(40\%\)
🔹 B. \(45\%\)
🔹 C. \(50\%\)
🔹 D. \(55\%\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The combined effect of a \(30\%\) decline in real wages and a \(20\%\) inflationary rise means workers retained only \(70\%\) of their earnings, which were then devalued by another \(20\%\). Multiplying \(0.70\) by \(0.80\) yields \(0.56\), indicating a net loss of \(44\%\); rounding to the nearest option gives \(50\%\).

Q18. Calculate the inflation‑adjusted loss in disposable income if taxes rose by \(15\%\), real wages fell by \(30\%\), and inflation was \(25\%\).

🔹 A. \(45\%\)
🔹 B. \(58.75\%\)
🔹 C. \(65\%\)
🔹 D. \(70\%\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Start with a baseline income of 100. A \(15\%\) tax increase reduces net income to 85, and a \(30\%\) wage drop further lowers it to 55. Applying a \(25\%\) inflation erosion gives \(55 \times 0.75 = 41.25\). The overall reduction is \(100 - 41.25 = 58.75\%\), matching option B.

Q21. If war spending was \(\frac{3}{4}\) of GDP and GDP fell by \(10\%\), what proportion of the original GDP is now allocated to war spending?

🔹 A. \(66.7\%\)
🔹 B. \(75\%\)
🔹 C. \(80\%\)
🔹 D. \(83.3\%\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Originally, war expenditure equaled \(0.75\) of GDP. After a \(10\%\) contraction, GDP becomes \(0.9\) of its original size. The share of war spending relative to the reduced GDP is \(0.75 ÷ 0.9 = 0.833\), or roughly \(83.3\%\) of the shrunken economy.
