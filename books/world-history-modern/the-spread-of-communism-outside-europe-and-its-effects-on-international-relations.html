About 8. The Spread of Communism Outside Europe and its Effects on International Relations

After 1949, communism expanded beyond Eastern Europe into Asia, Africa, and Latin America, transforming what was once a Eurocentric ideological struggle into a truly global contest. Revolutionary movements in China, Korea, Vietnam, and Cuba aligned with Marxist-Leninist ideology, often blending it with anti-colonial nationalism and creating new flashpoints that drew in superpower intervention.

This spread forced the US and USSR to adapt their strategies, leading to costly proxy wars and complex alliances with non-aligned or developing nations. It also fractured the monolithic communist bloc as national interests diverged from Moscow’s directives, particularly after the Sino-Soviet split, making international relations more multipolar and unpredictable.
