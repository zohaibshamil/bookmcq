What is Why did Britain and France appease Hitler?

Western leaders pursued appeasement due to traumatic memories of World War I, severe economic depression limiting military spending, widespread pacifist public opinion, and a genuine belief that some of Germany’s territorial claims were justified corrections of Versailles injustices.

📝 All Why did Britain and France appease Hitler MCQs

Q1. In what year did Japan invade Manchuria, marking the first major aggression leading up to World II?

A. 1930
B. 1931 ✅
C. 1932
D. 1933

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Japanese Kwantung Army seized the region in September 1931, establishing the puppet state of Manchukuo. This action exposed the weakness of the League of Nations and set a precedent for unchecked expansion, influencing the appeasement mindset of Britain and France in the following years.

Q2. Which treaty permitted Germany to construct submarines, violating the Versailles restrictions?

A. Treaty of Rapallo
B. Locarno Pact
C. Anglo‑German Naval Agreement ✅
D. Munich Agreement

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Signed in June 1935, the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement allowed Germany to build a navy up to 35 % of the Royal Navy’s tonnage, including U‑boats. This concession was made without consulting France or Italy and signaled a willingness to accommodate German rearmament, undermining collective security.

Q3. What was the primary motive behind Britain’s decision to sign the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement?

A. Desire to strengthen the League of Nations
B. Economic pressure from the Great Depression
C. Fear of a naval arms race with Germany ✅
D. To secure British naval superiority

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

British policymakers feared that a German naval buildup could challenge British sea power and provoke an expensive arms race. By granting limited concessions, they hoped to placate Germany, maintain peace, and avoid costly confrontation while Britain continued to rearm domestically.

Q4. If France had not entered a mutual‑assistance pact with the USSR in May 1935, which of the following outcomes would have been most likely?

A. Germany would have faced a two‑front war earlier ✅
B. Italy would have allied with Britain
C. The League of Nations would have intervened in Manchuria
D. Britain would have withdrawn from the Stresa Front

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Without Soviet backing, France would have been less inclined to confront Germany alone, reducing the strategic calculus that encouraged Britain to pursue the naval agreement. An earlier two‑front scenario for Germany might have deterred further aggression, altering the appeasement trajectory.

Q5. How did the League of Nations’ failure to curb Japanese aggression in Manchuria influence subsequent appeasement policies?

A. It demonstrated that economic sanctions were effective
B. It reinforced belief that collective security could not be relied upon ✅
C. It encouraged the League to adopt harsher penalties later
D. It led to immediate military intervention by Britain and France

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League’s impotence in Manchuria convinced Britain and France that diplomatic mechanisms were weak, prompting them to favor bilateral concessions over multilateral enforcement. This loss of confidence in collective security became a cornerstone of the appeasement approach, as leaders sought more pragmatic, less idealistic solutions.

Q6. Which action by Hitler constituted the first direct breach of the Versailles Treaty?

A. Reintroduction of conscription in March 1935 ✅
B. Annexation of Austria in 1938
C. Signing the Anti‑Comintern Pact in 1936
D. Occupation of Czechoslovakia in 1939

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In March 1935, Hitler announced the reinstatement of compulsory military service, directly contravening the Versailles limit of a 100,000‑man army. This move alarmed neighboring powers and signaled a willingness to flout international agreements, setting the stage for subsequent aggressive expansions.

Q7. What was the significance of the Rome–Berlin Axis formed in October 1936?

A. It created a formal military alliance with the USSR
B. It signaled Italy’s commitment to support Germany’s expansionist aims ✅
C. It established a joint economic plan for Africa
D. It dissolved the Stresa Front

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Rome–Berlin Axis marked Mussolini’s decision to align Italy with Hitler’s Germany, providing diplomatic legitimacy and military cooperation for future aggression. This partnership deepened the isolation of Britain and France, weakening any collective response and reinforcing the perception that appeasement could contain the threat.

Q8. Suppose Italy had refused to join the Anti‑Comintern Pact with Japan in 1936. Which consequence would have been most plausible?

A. The Axis would have collapsed before World II
B. Germany would have pursued a separate pact with the USSR
C. Japan would have withdrawn from its Asian campaigns
D. Italy would have remained neutral in later conflicts ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Italy’s abstention would likely have kept it more independent, reducing the ideological cohesion of the Axis. While the core German‑Japanese alliance might have persisted, Italy’s neutrality could have altered strategic calculations, potentially limiting joint operations in Europe and Africa.

Q9. Why did Britain adopt a policy of appeasement despite clear evidence of German rearmament?

A. Because Britain sought to expand its own empire
B. Due to domestic economic constraints and desire to avoid another war ✅
C. Because Britain believed in the superiority of diplomatic negotiations
D. Because Britain wanted to support the League of Nations

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Britain was still recovering from the Great Depression, with limited resources for immediate large‑scale rearmament. Public opinion favored peace, and political leaders feared the political cost of confronting Germany. These economic and social pressures encouraged a strategy of concession, hoping to buy time for military preparation while avoiding costly conflict.

Q10. In which year did Hitler order the remilitarization of the Rhineland?

A. 1934
B. 1935
C. 1936 ✅
D. 1937

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

On March 7 1936, German troops marched into the demilitarized Rhineland, directly violating the Versailles Treaty. The lack of decisive response from Britain and France emboldened Hitler, demonstrating that aggressive moves could be taken with minimal risk of immediate retaliation.

Q11. Which nation provided material support to the Spanish Republican forces during the Civil War?

A. Italy
B. Germany
C. Soviet Union ✅
D. Japan

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet Union dispatched troops, aircraft, and weapons to aid the Republican side, aiming to counter fascist influence and protect left‑wing interests. This involvement heightened the ideological dimension of the conflict and contributed to the perception that the war was a proxy battleground for competing powers.

Q12. What treaty formalized the anti‑communist alliance between Germany and Japan in 1936?

A. Treaty of Versailles
B. Munich Agreement
C. Anti‑Comintern Pact ✅
D. Molotov‑Ribbentrop Pact

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Signed on November 25 1936, the Anti‑Comintern Pact united Germany and Japan in opposition to communist influence, particularly the Soviet Union. It later expanded to include Italy, solidifying the ideological foundation of the Axis powers and reinforcing their mutual hostility toward the USSR.

Q13. How did the League of Nations react to Italy’s invasion of Abyssinia in October 1935?

A. It imposed comprehensive economic sanctions
B. It launched a military intervention
C. It offered half‑hearted condemnation with limited sanctions ✅
D. It supported Italy diplomatically

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The League issued a mild condemnation and imposed limited sanctions that excluded vital resources like oil and steel, rendering them ineffective. The tepid response highlighted the League’s inability to enforce collective security, encouraging further aggression by Italy and reinforcing appeasement attitudes among other powers.

Q14. What underlying factor primarily motivated the signing of the Anti‑Comintern Pact?

A. Desire for territorial expansion in Africa
B. Opposition to liberal democracy
C. Fear of Soviet communist influence ✅
D. Interest in trade agreements

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The pact was driven largely by mutual suspicion of the Soviet Union’s revolutionary aims. By presenting a united front against communism, Germany, Japan, and later Italy sought to legitimize their own expansionist policies while portraying themselves as defenders against a perceived ideological threat.

Q15. Which lake was the venue for the 1935 Stresa meeting of Britain, France, and Italy?

A. Lake Como
B. Lake Garda
C. Lake Maggiore ✅
D. Lake Geneva

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Stresa Conference took place on Lake Maggiore in northern Italy, where the three powers discussed a joint response to Hitler’s rearmament. The meeting ultimately produced a short‑lived diplomatic front that collapsed after Britain’s unilateral naval agreement with Germany.

Q16. How did the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement undermine the cohesion of the Stresa Front?

A. It strengthened the Front by adding naval cooperation
B. It led to Italy withdrawing from the agreement
C. It was negotiated without consulting France or Italy, causing distrust ✅
D. It forced Britain to leave the League of Nations

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

By signing the agreement independently, Britain signaled a willingness to accommodate German ambitions, betraying the collective stance of the Stresa Front. France and Italy felt excluded, eroding trust and dismantling the unified diplomatic pressure that might have deterred further German violations.

Q17. If the Stresa Front had remained intact, could it have deterred German aggression in the late 1930s?

A. Yes, because a united front would have forced Germany to negotiate
B. No, because Germany’s internal politics would have overridden external pressure
C. Possibly, but only if the Front had coordinated economic sanctions ✅
D. It would have had no impact on German decisions

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A cohesive Stresa Front could have coordinated stronger economic measures, presenting a credible threat that might have slowed German expansion. However, Hitler’s ideological drive and domestic support suggest that even a united front would have faced significant challenges in altering his strategic objectives.

Q18. Which three powers dominated international affairs between 1933 and 1939?

A. Britain, France, USA
B. Japan, Italy, Germany ✅
C. Soviet Union, China, USA
D. Spain, Portugal, Belgium

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Japan, Italy, and Germany emerged as the primary aggressive actors during this period, each pursuing expansionist policies that destabilized the international order and ultimately led to the outbreak of World II.

Q19. What economic problems contributed to the decline of the Locarno spirit after 1925?

A. Hyperinflation in Germany and France
B. The Great Depression and high unemployment ✅
C. Trade surpluses in Britain and Italy
D. Currency devaluation in the USSR

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The worldwide economic downturn of the early 1930s, marked by soaring unemployment and reduced trade, strained diplomatic goodwill. Nations turned inward, prioritizing domestic recovery over collective security, which weakened the cooperative atmosphere fostered by the Locarno agreements.

Q20. To what extent did appeasement provide Britain with time to rearm before World II?

A. It gave Britain exactly three years of preparation
B. It allowed a modest increase in naval production but not a full rearmament ✅
C. It delayed rearmament due to continued reliance on diplomacy
D. It had no impact; Britain rearmed at the same pace regardless

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Appeasement postponed direct confrontation, granting Britain a limited window to expand its navy and modernize its army. However, the policy also fostered complacency, and rearmament progressed slower than it might have under a more urgent threat perception, making the time gained only partially effective.

Q21. In which year did Italy launch its invasion of Abyssinia?

A. 1933
B. 1934
C. 1935 ✅
D. 1936

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Italy began its campaign against Abyssinia on October 3 1935, seeking to expand its colonial empire. The invasion proceeded with little effective opposition from the League of Nations, underscoring the weaknesses of collective security.

Q22. Which organization, founded by Lenin in 1919, aimed to promote worldwide communist revolutions?

A. The Red Army
B. The Comintern ✅
C. The Bolshevik Party
D. The Soviet Council

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Communist International, or Comintern, was established to coordinate communist parties globally, encouraging revolutionary activity. Its existence heightened fears among Western powers, influencing alliances such as the Anti‑Comintern Pact.

Q23. Imagine the League of Nations had imposed strict sanctions on Japan after the Manchurian incident. Which of the following outcomes is most plausible?

A. Japan would have withdrawn from Manchuria immediately
B. Japan would have sought diplomatic negotiations with China
C. Japan’s expansion would have been delayed, altering its later aggression in Asia ✅
D. Japan would have allied with the Soviet Union

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Effective sanctions could have constrained Japan’s resources, slowing its military advances and possibly preventing the rapid consolidation of Manchukuo. A delayed or limited expansion might have altered the timeline of Japanese aggression, influencing the broader strategic environment of the late 1930s.

Q24. Which treaty did France sign with the USSR in May 1935?

A. Treaty of Versailles
B. Treaty of Rapallo
C. Franco‑Soviet Mutual Assistance Treaty ✅
D. Munich Agreement

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Franco‑Soviet Mutual Assistance Treaty pledged that each nation would support the other if attacked by Germany, aiming to create a deterrent against further German violations of Versailles.

Q25. Which country condemned Hitler’s reintroduction of conscription at the Stresa Conference?

A. Spain
B. Italy
C. France ✅
D. Belgium

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

France expressed strong disapproval of Germany’s conscription policy at Stresa, viewing it as a direct breach of the Versailles Treaty and a threat to European stability.

Q26. Evaluate the claim that appeasement was driven more by fear of communism than by a desire for peace. Which statement best reflects scholarly consensus?

A. Fear of communism was the sole motive for appeasement
B. Desire for peace outweighed anti‑communist concerns
C. Both anti‑communist anxiety and the wish to avoid war influenced policy, with the latter being primary ✅
D. Appeasement was unrelated to either fear of communism or pursuit of peace

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Historians generally agree that appeasement stemmed from a combination of factors: the urgent desire to prevent another catastrophic war and genuine apprehension about Soviet communism. While anti‑communist sentiment played a role, the predominant driver was the pursuit of peace and the avoidance of immediate conflict.
