What is International relations 1933 to 1939 path to WW2?

This period describes the gradual breakdown of global peace as aggressive actions by Japan, Italy, and Germany went unchecked by the League of Nations, creating a chain reaction of territorial expansion and failed diplomacy that made a second world war inevitable.

📝 All International relations 1933 to 1939 path to WW2 MCQs

Q1. In which year did Japan invade Manchuria, marking its first major aggression in the 1930s?

A.1929
B.1931 ✅
C.1933
D.1935
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Japan's invasion of Manchuria began in 1931, following the Mukden Incident. This aggressive move demonstrated the League of Nations' weakness and set a precedent for future expansionist policies by other powers, contributing directly to the escalating tensions that led to World War II.

Q2. What policy did Hitler announce in March 1935 that directly violated the Versailles Treaty?

A.Reintroduction of conscription ✅
B.Abolition of the Treaty of Versailles
C.Formation of the Nazi Party
D.Signing of the Munich Agreement
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: In March 1935, Hitler publicly declared the reintroduction of conscription for German men, a clear breach of the Versailles restrictions that limited the size of Germany's army. This action alarmed neighboring states and signaled the Nazi regime's willingness to defy international agreements, heightening regional insecurity.

Q3. Where was the Stresa Front conference held, where Britain, France, and Italy condemned German rearmament?

A.Rome
B.Berlin
C.Lake Maggiore ✅
D.Vienna
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Stresa Front met on the shores of Lake Maggiore, specifically at the town of Stresa in northern Italy. Representatives from Britain, France, and Italy gathered there in April 1935 to issue a joint protest against Hitler's remilitarization efforts, aiming to preserve the post‑World War I order.

Q4. Which agreement permitted Germany to build submarines, further breaching the Versailles Treaty?

A.Treaty of Rapallo
B.Locarno Pact
C.Munich Agreement
D.Anglo-German Naval Agreement ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Anglo‑German Naval Agreement of June 1935 was signed by Britain and Germany, allowing the latter to construct a fleet of submarines within limits that nonetheless exceeded the Versailles provisions. This unilateral concession undermined French trust and illustrated Britain's policy of appeasement toward Hitler's military ambitions.

Q5. In what year did Italy launch its invasion of Abyssinia (Ethiopia)?

A.1935 ✅
B.1936
C.1937
D.1938
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Italy, under Mussolini, commenced its full‑scale invasion of Abyssinia in October 1935. The campaign was marked by the use of chemical weapons and brutal tactics, provoking condemnation from the League of Nations but eliciting only limited sanctions, thereby exposing the League's impotence.

Q6. What was the name of the alliance formed between Germany and Italy in October 1936?

A.Munich Pact
B.Rome–Berlin Axis ✅
C.Pact of Steel
D.Anti-Comintern Pact
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Rome–Berlin Axis, concluded in October 1936, formalized the political and military cooperation between Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy. This partnership deepened the isolation of democratic powers and set the stage for coordinated aggression that would later culminate in the outbreak of World War II.

Q7. Which pact, signed in November 1936, united Germany and Japan against communist influence?

A.Molotov‑Ribbentrop Pact
B.Treaty of Versailles
C.Anti-Comintern Pact ✅
D.Munich Agreement
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Anti‑Comintern Pact, signed on 25 November 1936 by Germany and Japan, pledged mutual opposition to the Communist International. Though initially bilateral, it later attracted other right‑wing regimes, creating an ideological bloc that reinforced anti‑Soviet sentiment and foreshadowed the Axis coalition.

Q8. Which side in the Spanish Civil War received direct military assistance from the Soviet Union?

A.Nationalists
B.Falange
C.Carlist forces
D.Republicans ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: During the Spanish Civil War (1936‑1939), the Soviet Union provided weapons, advisors, and limited air support to the Republican government, which defended the elected left‑wing administration. This aid contrasted with the German and Italian support for Franco's Nationalists, turning Spain into a proxy battleground for competing ideologies.

Q9. Why did Britain choose to sign the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement despite French objections?

A.To strengthen the League of Nations
B.To prevent a naval arms race with Italy
C.To appease Germany and maintain peace ✅
D.Because France demanded it
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: British leaders believed that granting Germany limited naval construction would satisfy Hitler's ambitions and thus avoid a costly confrontation. This policy of limited concession, rooted in a desire to preserve peace, ignored French concerns that such a concession would erode collective security and embolden German aggression.

Q10. If the League of Nations had imposed strict economic sanctions on Italy after the Abyssinia invasion, how might the Axis formation have been affected?

A.It would have accelerated the Axis alliance
B.It would have forced Italy to seek Soviet aid ✅
C.It could have weakened Italy's resolve, delaying the Rome‑Berlin Axis
D.It would have had no impact
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Strict sanctions could have crippled Italy's war economy, pushing Mussolini to look for alternative partners, possibly aligning more closely with the Soviet Union rather than Nazi Germany. Such a shift would have altered the diplomatic landscape, potentially preventing the formal Rome‑Berlin Axis and changing the trajectory toward global conflict.

Q11. Calculate the interval between Hitler's reintroduction of conscription (March 1935) and the remilitarization of the Rhineland (March 1936).

A.\(6\) months
B.\(12\) months ✅
C.\(18\) months
D.\(24\) months
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The two events are exactly one year apart, as both occurred in March of consecutive years. This twelve‑month interval underscores the rapid pace at which Hitler escalated Germany's military capabilities, moving from domestic policy changes to overt territorial violations within a single year.

Q12. How did the collapse of the Stresa Front influence French diplomatic strategy in the late 1930s?

A.France turned to isolationism
B.France deepened its alliance with the Soviet Union
C.France pursued appeasement with Germany
D.France focused on colonial expansion ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: After the Stresa Front fell apart, France became increasingly wary of relying on unreliable partners and shifted its focus toward consolidating its colonial empire and reinforcing its own military preparedness. This strategic reorientation, however, left France without a strong continental ally against German expansion.

Q13. Evaluate the effect of the Spanish Civil War on British foreign policy toward Germany.

A.It hardened British opposition to Hitler ✅
B.It reinforced British neutrality and non‑intervention
C.It led Britain to support the Nationalists
D.It prompted Britain to join the League of Nations sanctions
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Spanish conflict highlighted the willingness of Germany to intervene militarily abroad, prompting Britain to adopt a more cautious stance that eventually evolved into a policy of appeasement. While Britain maintained formal neutrality, the war demonstrated the risks of German aggression, influencing later diplomatic calculations.

Q14. What was the primary reason the League of Nations failed to stop Japanese aggression in Manchuria?

A.Lack of military forces
B.Divided opinions among member states
C.Strong economic ties with Japan ✅
D.Absence of a unified command structure
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The League's impotence largely stemmed from the reluctance of major powers to jeopardize trade and diplomatic relations with Japan. Economic considerations outweighed collective security concerns, leading to tepid responses that emboldened further Japanese expansion and undermined the League's credibility.

Q15. If Germany had respected the Versailles limits on its military, what alternative diplomatic path might Britain have pursued?

A.Form a defensive alliance with Italy
B.Encourage a French‑German reconciliation ✅
C.Adopt a policy of strict isolationism
D.Support a stronger League of Nations
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Had Germany adhered to the imposed limits, Britain would likely have felt less threatened and could have facilitated a rapprochement between Paris and Berlin. Such a diplomatic thaw might have strengthened collective security mechanisms and reduced the impetus for Germany to seek alliances outside the League.

Q16. Compare the ideological motivations behind Japan's and Italy's expansions in the 1930s.

A.Both sought economic resources only
B.Japan pursued racial superiority, Italy sought religious goals
C.Both were driven by nationalist and imperialist ideologies
D.Japan aimed at spreading communism, Italy opposed it ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Japan's expansion was justified by the doctrine of Pan‑Asianism and a belief in racial superiority, whereas Italy's actions were framed by Fascist nationalism and a desire to resurrect a Roman empire. Despite differing cultural rationales, both regimes shared an imperialist drive to secure resources and prestige.

Q17. In what way did the Anti‑Comintern Pact influence later alliances?

A.It led directly to the formation of the Axis Powers ✅
B.It caused the Soviet Union to join the League of Nations
C.It isolated Germany from other European powers
D.It resulted in a peace treaty with France
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Anti‑Comintern Pact created an ideological bloc opposed to communism, attracting like‑minded regimes and laying the groundwork for the later formal Axis alliance between Germany, Italy, and Japan. This shared anti‑Soviet stance deepened cooperation and coordinated aggression across continents.

Q18. Suppose France had not signed the mutual assistance treaty with the USSR in May 1935; predict the likely impact on the balance of power in 1938.

A.Germany would have faced a two‑front war earlier
B.The Soviet Union would have withdrawn from Europe
C.France would have been more isolated, encouraging German aggression ✅
D.Poland would have allied with Italy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Without the Franco‑Soviet pact, France would have lacked a crucial eastern ally, making it more vulnerable to German pressure. This isolation could have emboldened Hitler to pursue territorial ambitions more aggressively, accelerating the path toward war in 1938.

Q19. Assess why Britain adopted a policy of appeasement toward Germany after 1936.

A.To maintain colonial dominance
B.To avoid another costly war and preserve peace ✅
C.Because Britain favored German economic models
D.Due to strong pro‑German sentiment in Parliament
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: British leaders, scarred by World War I casualties, prioritized avoiding another large‑scale conflict. They believed that conceding limited demands—such as permitting German rearmament—would satisfy Hitler's ambitions and preserve continental stability, a miscalculation that ultimately encouraged further German aggression.

Q20. Critically assess how the failure of the League of Nations after Manchuria set a precedent for later aggression by Italy and Germany.

A.It showed that economic sanctions were effective
B.It demonstrated that the League could enforce peace
C.It revealed the League's inability to act, encouraging further violations
D.It proved the League's authority over colonial matters ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The League's impotence in Manchuria signaled to Italy and Germany that the international community lacked resolve and mechanisms to enforce its rulings. This precedent emboldened both regimes to pursue territorial ambitions—Italy in Abyssinia and Germany in the Rhineland—knowing that punitive action was unlikely.

Q21. Using a cause‑and‑effect framework, trace how the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement contributed to the erosion of collective security.

A.It strengthened the League's authority ✅
B.It isolated Britain from France
C.It encouraged Germany to violate other treaties
D.It led to immediate disarmament of both navies
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: By granting Germany permission to expand its submarine fleet, Britain effectively legitimized a Versailles breach, undermining the principle of collective security. This concession signaled to other powers that unilateral agreements could override multilateral commitments, weakening the League's cohesion and encouraging further treaty violations.

Q22. Imagine the Soviet Union had intervened militarily in the Spanish Civil War on behalf of the Republicans; hypothesize possible repercussions for the 1939 Molotov‑Ribbentrop Pact.

A.The pact would have been stronger
B.The pact would have been delayed or altered
C.The Soviet Union would have allied with Germany ✅
D.There would be no impact on the pact
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A Soviet military presence in Spain would have deepened antagonism between Moscow and Berlin, making a non‑aggression agreement between the two powers less palatable. Consequently, the Molotov‑Ribbentrop Pact might have been postponed, renegotiated, or possibly never concluded, altering the strategic calculus at the war's outset.

Q23. Analyze the strategic significance of the Rome–Berlin Axis for the eventual outbreak of World War II.

A.It isolated Britain diplomatically
B.It created a unified front against the Soviet Union ✅
C.It coordinated military planning and deterrence against the Allies
D.It dissolved the League of Nations
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Rome–Berlin Axis formalized cooperation between two revisionist powers, enabling coordinated political and military strategies that challenged the existing balance. This partnership facilitated joint aggression, such as Italy's support for Germany during the invasion of France, and signaled to other nations that a formidable bloc opposed to the status quo was emerging.

Q24. Evaluate the extent to which economic crises of the early 1930s influenced the nationalist policies of Japan, Italy, and Germany.

A.Crises had minimal impact on policy
B.Economic hardship drove aggressive expansion to secure resources
C.Only Germany was affected by the crises
D.All three countries prioritized domestic welfare over foreign aggression ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Severe economic downturns heightened public discontent and created fertile ground for extremist ideologies. In each country, nationalist leaders promised revitalization through territorial conquest—Japan sought raw materials in China, Italy aimed for African colonies, and Germany pursued Lebensraum—linking economic recovery to expansionist ambition.

Q25. Which diplomatic mistake by Britain most directly enabled Hitler's aggressive foreign policy?

A.Refusing to sign the Treaty of Versailles ✅
B.Signing the Munich Agreement
C.Signing the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement
D.Ignoring the Stresa Front
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Britain's decision to overlook Germany's early treaty violations, epitomized by the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement, signaled a willingness to accommodate Hitler's ambitions. This diplomatic misstep undermined collective security, encouraged further German breaches, and ultimately paved the way for unchecked expansion that culminated in war.

Q26. Apply the concept of “balance of power” to explain why France's alliance with the USSR failed to deter German expansion.

A.The alliance was too strong for Germany
B.The Soviet Union withdrew its support in 1937
C.The alliance lacked mutual strategic interests and credible military coordination ✅
D.France prioritized its colonial empire over European security
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: While theoretically a counterweight, the Franco‑Soviet pact suffered from divergent strategic priorities, limited joint planning, and logistical challenges. Without a clear, coordinated response, Germany perceived the alliance as weak, allowing Hitler to pursue aggressive moves with little fear of a unified front.

Q27. Synthesize the events of 1933‑1939 to argue whether World War II was inevitable or could have been avoided through different international actions.

A.Inevitable due to economic determinism
B.Avoidable if the League had enforced early sanctions ✅
C.Inevitable because of ideological fanaticism
D.Avoidable only with a different British government
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The cascade of unchecked aggressions—Manchuria, Abyssinia, Rhineland—demonstrates that stronger collective enforcement, particularly decisive League sanctions, could have constrained revisionist powers. While deep‑seated ideologies played a role, decisive early international action might have altered the trajectory, suggesting that war was not strictly inevitable.
