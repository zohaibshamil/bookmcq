Q1. In which year did Italy commence its invasion of Abyssinia?

🔹 A. \(1934\)
🔹 B. \(1935\)
🔹 C. \(1936\)
🔹 D. \(1937\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Italian invasion began in October \(1935\). This action marked a clear breach of the League of Nations' principles and demonstrated Mussolini’s willingness to pursue aggressive expansion despite international objections, setting a precedent for later Axis collaborations with Germany.

Q2. What was the primary purpose of the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement signed in June \(1935\)?

🔹 A. To limit German submarine construction
🔹 B. To permit Germany to build a navy up to 35% of the Royal Navy
🔹 C. To enforce the Treaty of Versailles
🔹 D. To create a joint British‑German naval fleet

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The agreement allowed Germany to construct a navy whose tonnage could not exceed 35 % of the British fleet, effectively relaxing Versailles restrictions. Britain hoped the concession would satisfy German ambitions and maintain peace, but it unintentionally legitimized German rearmament and alienated France and Italy.

Q3. Which treaty did France sign with the USSR in May \(1935\) as a response to German rearmament?

🔹 A. Treaty of Versailles
🔹 B. Treaty of Rapallo
🔹 C. Treaty of Mutual Assistance
🔹 D. Treaty of Locarno

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

France, fearing Germany’s breach of Versailles through conscription, sought security by signing a mutual‑assistance pact with the Soviet Union. This move aimed to create a deterrent against further German aggression, though it also contributed to the complex web of alliances that would later shape the diplomatic landscape preceding World War II.

Q4. Scenario: If the League of Nations had imposed economic sanctions on Italy in \(1935\), how might this have altered the Axis alliance formation?

📖 Explanation:

Strong sanctions could have crippled Italy’s war effort, forcing Mussolini to reconsider aggressive policies. Economic pressure might have driven Italy back toward Britain or France for aid, reducing the likelihood of a formal Rome‑Berlin Axis and potentially delaying coordinated aggression by the three fascist powers.

Q5. Which event directly demonstrated the League of Nations’ inability to enforce collective security prior to the Abyssinian crisis?

This precedent emboldened other revisionist states, including Italy and Germany, to pursue expansionist policies, knowing that the League lacked the will or mechanisms to impose meaningful consequences.

📖 Explanation:

This diplomatic protest signaled collective unease, yet the alliance quickly unraveled due to divergent national interests, especially after Britain’s unilateral naval agreement with Germany later that year.

Q7. Analytical: Assess the impact of the Rome‑Berlin Axis (October \(1936\)) on the balance of power in Europe. Which statement best captures its effect?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Rome‑Berlin Axis formalized cooperation between two revisionist powers, creating a bloc that combined Italy’s Mediterranean ambitions with Germany’s continental objectives. This partnership heightened strategic anxiety in Britain and France, prompting them to reconsider their own alliances and ultimately contributing to the diplomatic drift toward confrontation that culminated in World War II.
