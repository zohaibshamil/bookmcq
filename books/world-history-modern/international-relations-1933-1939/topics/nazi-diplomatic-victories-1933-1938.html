What is Nazi diplomatic victories 1933-1938?

Through a combination of bluffing, exploiting Allied divisions, and presenting each demand as his last, Hitler successfully achieved rearmament, remilitarization of the Rhineland, annexation of Austria, and acquisition of the Sudetenland without facing military opposition.

📝 All Nazi diplomatic victories 1933-1938 MCQs

Q1. In which year did Hitler announce the reintroduction of conscription, breaching the Versailles Treaty?

A.1934
B.1935 ✅
C.1936
D.1937
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The announcement came in March \(1935\), directly violating the disarmament clauses of the Treaty of Versailles. This move alarmed Britain, France, and Italy, prompting the brief Stresa Front reaction and signaling Germany's willingness to disregard post‑World War I constraints.

Q2. Which agreement permitted Germany to construct submarines, further contravening Versailles provisions?

A.Anglo‑German Naval Agreement ✅
B.Treaty of Versailles
C.Locarno Pact
D.Stresa Front
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Signed in June \(1935\), the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement allowed Germany to build a submarine fleet up to a specified percentage of the British navy. The pact shocked France and Italy, undermining the Stresa Front and illustrating Britain's policy of appeasement toward German rearmament.

Q3. Which country launched the invasion of Abyssinia (Ethiopia) in October \(1935\)?

A.Italy ✅
B.Germany
C.Japan
D.Spain
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Fascist Italy, under Benito Mussolini, invaded Abyssinia in October \(1935\) seeking imperial expansion and prestige. The League of Nations responded weakly, exposing its inability to enforce collective security and encouraging further aggression by Germany and Japan.

Q4. The Stresa meeting was held on which Italian lake?

A.Lake Como
B.Lake Garda
C.Lake Maggiore ✅
D.Lake Geneva
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Stresa Conference convened in August \(1935\) on Lake Maggiore, bringing together Britain, France, and Italy to condemn Hitler’s rearmament. Though short‑lived, the meeting highlighted attempts to maintain a united front against German violations of the post‑war order.

Q5. What was the name of the alliance formalized between Germany and Italy in October \(1936\)?

A.Rome–Berlin Axis ✅
B.Anti‑Comintern Pact
C.Munich Agreement
D.Molotov‑Ribbentrop Pact
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Rome–Berlin Axis, declared in October \(1936\), signified a political and military alignment between Hitler and Mussolini. It solidified their cooperation, especially against perceived communist threats, and laid groundwork for deeper collaboration during the ensuing global conflict.

Q6. Which region did Japan successfully invade in \(1931\), marking its first major aggression?

A.Manchuria ✅
B.Korea
C.Vietnam
D.Mongolia
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Japan’s seizure of Manchuria in September \(1931\) established a puppet state, Manchukuo, and demonstrated the League of Nations’ impotence. This early expansion encouraged both Germany and Italy to pursue their own territorial ambitions, believing the international community would not intervene decisively.

Q7. In which month was the Anti‑Comintern Pact signed between Germany and Japan?

A.July
B.August
C.September
D.November ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Anti‑Comintern Pact was concluded on 25 November \(1936\), uniting Germany and Japan in opposition to the Communist International. This agreement deepened ideological bonds among Axis powers and signaled a coordinated stance against Soviet influence.

Q8. Which two authoritarian states provided military assistance to Franco during the Spanish Civil War?

A.Germany and Italy ✅
B.Germany and Japan
C.Italy and Japan
D.Britain and France
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Both Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy dispatched aircraft, troops, and equipment to support General Franco’s Nationalist forces. Their involvement turned the Spanish conflict into a proxy war, while the democracies of Britain and France adhered to a non‑intervention policy, further weakening collective security.

Q9. Why did Britain sign the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement without consulting France and Italy?

A.Fear of immediate war
B.Desire to appease Germany
C.Economic interests
D.All of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: British policymakers feared that confronting Germany could trigger conflict, while also hoping to control naval expansion through a bilateral pact. Economic considerations, such as protecting trade routes, intertwined with appeasement motives, leading to a decision made unilaterally and alienating traditional allies.

Q10. If the Stresa Front had remained intact, how might the German remilitarization of the Rhineland have been handled?

A.No German action would have occurred
B.A collective military response would likely have been mounted ✅
C.Germany would have negotiated a compromise
D.The Front would have ignored the violation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A cohesive Stresa Front could have presented a united diplomatic and possibly military deterrent, compelling Germany to reconsider its breach of the demilitarized zone. Historical evidence suggests that unified opposition often restrained early Nazi aggression, making a collective response plausible.

Q11. Which event most directly demonstrated the League of Nations' inability to enforce its resolutions?

A.Japanese invasion of Manchuria
B.Italian invasion of Abyssinia
C.German rearmament
D.All of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Each of these aggressions—Japan’s 1931 Manchuria incursion, Italy’s 1935 Abyssinia campaign, and Germany’s systematic violations—met with tepid League responses. The cumulative failure to impose meaningful sanctions highlighted the organization’s structural weakness and encouraged further unilateral actions by the Axis powers.

Q12. How did the breach of Versailles by reinstating conscription influence French foreign policy in 1935?

A.It pushed France to seek an alliance with the USSR ✅
B.It led France to adopt isolationism
C.It caused France to increase its own conscription
D.It resulted in France withdrawing from the League
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The perceived German threat after the conscription announcement prompted France to negotiate a mutual assistance treaty with the Soviet Union in May \(1935\). This alignment aimed to counterbalance German militarization and demonstrated France’s shift toward a more security‑focused diplomatic posture.

Q13. Under the Anglo‑German Naval Agreement, Germany was permitted to build submarines up to what percentage of the British fleet’s tonnage?

A.0.15
B.0.25
C.0.35 ✅
D.0.45
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The 1935 agreement stipulated that Germany could possess a submarine force not exceeding 35% of the total tonnage of the Royal Navy. This limitation was intended to keep German naval growth within a manageable scope, yet it nonetheless facilitated a rapid expansion of U‑boat capabilities.

Q14. What was the primary strategic purpose of the Rome–Berlin Axis formed in 1936?

A.Mutual defense against the Allies
B.Joint colonial expansion
C.Coordinated anti‑communist policy ✅
D.Economic integration
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Axis primarily served as a platform for coordinated opposition to the Communist International, aligning Germany and Italy against perceived Soviet influence. While it later facilitated broader military cooperation, its initial emphasis was ideological, reinforcing each regime’s anti‑communist credentials.

Q15. How did Japan’s success in Manchuria influence Mussolini’s decision to invade Abyssinia?

A.It showed the League’s impotence ✅
B.It provided raw materials for Italy
C.It created a military alliance with Japan
D.It diverted attention from Europe
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Japan’s unchallenged occupation of Manchuria convinced Mussolini that the League of Nations could not enforce collective security. Observing this weakness encouraged Italy to pursue its own imperial ambitions in Abyssinia, anticipating minimal international resistance.

Q16. If Britain had strictly enforced Versailles terms in \(1935\), what would likely have been the impact on German naval buildup?

A.It would have slowed the buildup ✅
B.It would have driven it underground
C.It would have had no effect
D.It would have provoked war
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Enforcement of treaty limits would have constrained Germany’s ability to expand its surface fleet and submarine forces, forcing the regime either to comply or to develop clandestine programs. Historical patterns suggest that external pressure would have at least delayed overt naval growth.

Q17. How did the Anti‑Comintern Pact affect diplomatic relations between Germany and the Soviet Union between \(1936\) and \(1939\)?

A.It deepened mutual cooperation
B.It isolated the USSR diplomatically
C.It heightened hostility and suspicion ✅
D.It led to a formal alliance
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The pact explicitly positioned Germany against the Communist International, intensifying ideological antagonism toward the USSR. This hostility limited any possibility of rapprochement, contributing to the climate of distrust that culminated in the eventual German invasion of Soviet territory in 1941.

Q18. If the League of Nations had imposed strict economic sanctions on Italy after the Abyssinia invasion, how might the Axis formation have been altered?

A.The Axis would have been delayed or prevented
B.Italy would have aligned with Britain ✅
C.Germany would have withdrawn from the Axis
D.The sanctions would have had no effect
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Severe sanctions could have weakened Italy’s war effort, making Mussolini more receptive to British diplomatic overtures. A financially strained Italy might have refrained from deepening ties with Germany, potentially postponing or averting the formal Rome–Berlin Axis.

Q19. Why was the reoccupation of the Rhineland strategically advantageous for Germany despite the lack of immediate Allied response?

A.It secured Germany’s western defensive frontier
B.It forced the Allies into a peace treaty
C.It provided economic resources
D.It was merely symbolic ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: By marching troops into the demilitarized Rhineland, Germany bolstered its defensive depth, boosted national morale, and demonstrated the Allies’ reluctance to enforce treaty provisions. This success encouraged further violations, such as the annexation of Austria and the Sudetenland, accelerating the path to war.

Q20. In terms of international law, which German action between \(1935\) and \(1938\) constituted the most blatant violation of the Versailles Treaty?

A.Reintroduction of conscription
B.Anglo‑German Naval Agreement
C.Remilitarization of the Rhineland ✅
D.All of the above
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: While each action breached Versailles, the direct military occupation of the Rhineland in March \(1936\) flagrantly violated the treaty’s demilitarization clause, representing the most overt and provocative infringement that directly altered the balance of forces in Western Europe.

Q21. If the Soviet Union had not signed the mutual assistance treaty with France in May \(1935\), how would the balance of power in Central Europe likely have shifted?

A.Germany would have faced less opposition, encouraging earlier aggression ✅
B.France would have sought a separate peace with Germany
C.The USSR would have aligned with Italy
D.Poland would have become the dominant power
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Without Soviet backing, France’s deterrent capability would have been weakened, reducing the strategic cost for Germany to pursue further violations. This environment could have prompted Berlin to accelerate its expansionist agenda, potentially leading to earlier conflicts.

Q22. Comparing the diplomatic outcomes, which invasion—Manchuria or Abyssinia—provided the aggressor with greater long‑term strategic advantage?

A.Japanese invasion of Manchuria ✅
B.Italian invasion of Abyssinia
C.Both offered equal advantage
D.Neither yielded lasting benefit
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Japan’s control of Manchuria secured vital raw materials, a strategic buffer, and a foothold for further expansion into China, whereas Italy’s conquest of Abyssinia strained its economy and yielded limited strategic value, making the former more advantageous.
