What is Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand 1914?

The murder of the Austro-Hungarian heir in Sarajevo by a Serbian nationalist served as the direct spark for war because Austria-Hungary used the event as justification to crush Serbian independence, triggering a chain reaction of alliance obligations that pulled all major European powers into combat within weeks.

📝 All Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand 1914 MCQs

Q1. On what date did the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand occur?

A. 28-Jun-14 ✅
B. 28-Jul-14
C. 01-Aug-14
D. 04-Aug-14

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The assassination occurred on 28 June 1914 in Sarajevo. This event was the immediate trigger for Austria-Hungary's declaration of war on Serbia, which escalated into World War I through the alliance system and mobilization decisions.

Q2. Where did the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand take place?

A. Vienna
B. Belgrade
C. Sarajevo ✅
D. Berlin

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The assassination took place in Sarajevo, the capital of Bosnia. This location was significant because Bosnia had been annexed by Austria-Hungary in 1908 despite Serbian nationalist aspirations, making it a flashpoint for Austro-Serbian tensions.

Q3. What was the relationship of Archduke Franz Ferdinand to Emperor Franz Josef?

A. Son
B. Brother
C. Nephew and heir ✅
D. Cousin

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Archduke Franz Ferdinand was the nephew and heir to Emperor Franz Josef. His assassination therefore had dynastic significance for the Habsburg Empire and gave the assassination particular political weight as an attack on the imperial succession.

Q4. Who assassinated Archduke Franz Ferdinand?

A. A Serbian soldier
B. Gavrilo Princip ✅
C. Young Turk rebels
D. Russian nationalist

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Gavrilo Princip, a Serb terrorist, shot and killed Archduke Franz Ferdinand and his wife. Princip was a member of the Black Hand organization, which sought to unite all Serbs into a Greater Serbia and opposed Austrian rule in Bosnia.

Q5. What was the immediate cause of Austria-Hungary's declaration of war on Serbia?

A. The Balkan Wars
B. The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand ✅
C. The Agadir Crisis
D. The Bosnian Crisis

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand was the immediate cause of Austria-Hungary's declaration of war on Serbia. The Austrians blamed the Serb government for the assassination and used the incident as an excuse to crush Serbian nationalism.

Q6. What did the Austrians do after the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand?

A. They accepted Serbian denials
B. They sent a harsh ultimatum to Serbia ✅
C. They immediately declared war
D. They appealed to Russia

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Austrians blamed the Serb government and sent a harsh ultimatum. This ultimatum was deliberately designed to be unacceptable to Serbia, giving Austria a pretext for war while appearing to seek a diplomatic solution.

Q7. What was Serbia's response to the Austrian ultimatum?

A. They rejected all demands
B. They accepted most demands ✅
C. They declared war on Austria
D. They appealed to Germany

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Serbia accepted most of the demands in the Austrian ultimatum. However, the Austrians, with German support, were determined to use the incident as an excuse for war and were not satisfied with Serbia's conciliatory response.

Q8. Which power promised to support Austria-Hungary before it declared war on Serbia?

A. Russia
B. France
C. Germany ✅
D. Britain

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Germany promised to support Austria-Hungary before it declared war on Serbia. This blank check" gave Austria confidence to pursue a hard line against Serbia, knowing that Germany would back them if the conflict escalated and drew in other powers."

Q9. On what date did Austria-Hungary declare war on Serbia?

A. 28-Jun-14
B. 28-Jul-14 ✅
C. 01-Aug-14
D. 04-Aug-14

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia on 28 July 1914. This declaration of war triggered the alliance system and led to Russia's mobilization, Germany's declarations of war, and ultimately the outbreak of World War I.

Q10. Why did Russia order general mobilization on 29 July 1914?

A. To attack Germany
B. To support Serbia ✅
C. To defend itself
D. To join the war

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Russia ordered general mobilization because they were anxious not to let the Serbs down again. Having failed to support Serbia effectively during the Bosnia Crisis of 1908, Russia was determined to show support this time, despite the risk of war with Austria-Hungary and Germany.

Q11. What did Germany demand from Russia on 31 July 1914?

A. That Russia declare war on France
B. That Russia cancel its mobilization ✅
C. That Russia ally with Germany
D. That Russia abandon Serbia

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Germany demanded that Russia cancel its general mobilization on 31 July 1914. When the Russians failed to comply with this demand, Germany used it as a pretext to declare war on Russia, beginning the European war.

Q12. When did Germany declare war on Russia?

A. 28-Jul-14
B. 31-Jul-14
C. 01-Aug-14 ✅
D. 03-Aug-14

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Germany declared war on Russia on 1 August 1914. This declaration of war followed Germany's demand for Russia to cancel its mobilization and the failure of that demand, escalating the conflict from an Austro-Serbian to a European war.

Q13. When did Germany declare war on France?

A. 01-Aug-14
B. 03-Aug-14 ✅
C. 04-Aug-14
D. 06-Aug-14

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Germany declared war on France on 3 August 1914. Germany anticipated that war with Russia would lead to French intervention under the Franco-Russian alliance, so Germany declared war on France first to implement the Schlieffen Plan against Russia's western ally.

Q14. Why did Britain enter World War I?

A. To support Russia
B. To defend Belgian neutrality ✅
C. To support France
D. To defeat Germany

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Britain entered the war because Germany invaded Belgium on its way to attack France. Britain had promised to defend Belgian neutrality in 1839 and demanded Germany's withdrawal. When this demand was ignored, Britain declared war on Germany on 4 August 1914.

Q15. On what date did Britain enter World War I?

A. 03-Aug-14
B. 04-Aug-14 ✅
C. 06-Aug-14
D. 01-Aug-14

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Britain entered the war on 4 August 1914. Germany's invasion of Belgium, which violated Belgian neutrality, was the casus belli that brought Britain into the conflict, as Britain had treaty obligations to protect Belgian neutrality dating back to 1839.

Q16. When did Austria-Hungary declare war on Russia?

A. 01-Aug-14
B. 03-Aug-14
C. 04-Aug-14
D. 06-Aug-14 ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Austria-Hungary declared war on Russia on 6 August 1914. This followed Russia's mobilization in support of Serbia and Germany's declaration of war on Russia. Austria's declaration completed the alignment of the Central Powers against the Entente in the early stages of the war.

Q17. What promise had Britain made regarding Belgian neutrality?

C. In 1878 at the Congress of Berlin
D. In 1904 in the Entente Cordiale

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Britain had promised in 1839 to defend Belgian neutrality. This treaty commitment was the legal basis for Britain's entry into World War I when Germany invaded Belgium, allowing Britain to declare war with moral justification and public support.

Q18. What was the German strategy that involved invading Belgium?

A. The Bismarck Plan
B. The Schlieffen Plan ✅
C. The Weltpolitik Plan
D. The Ostpolitik Plan

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Germany invaded Belgium as part of the Schlieffen Plan, which aimed to quickly defeat France by attacking through Belgium and then focus on Russia. This strategy required violating Belgian neutrality, which brought Britain into the war.

C. Germany gained territory
D. Germany became democratic

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Germany was soon to be displaced, for a time at least, from its mastery of Europe. The war ended German dominance of the continent and Europe never quite regained its dominant position in the world, with the center of global power shifting across the Atlantic.

Q20. What was the effect of World War I on Europe's global position?

B. Europe never quite regained its dominant position in the world ✅
C. Europe remained the world's leader
D. Europe expanded its empire

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Europe never quite regained its dominant position in the world after World War I. The war devastated European economies, populations, and prestige, accelerating the rise of non-European powers like the United States and shifting the global balance of power.

📖 Explanation:

Emperor Franz Josef was the Austrian Emperor and King of Hungary. Archduke Franz Ferdinand was his nephew and heir, making the assassination a direct attack on the Habsburg succession and adding to Austria's determination to respond forcefully.

📖 Explanation:

Russia's support for Serbia was significant because it led to general mobilization and war. Russia's determination not to abandon Serbia as it had in 1908 resulted in mobilization that Germany interpreted as a threat, leading to Germany's declaration of war and the expansion of the conflict.

Q25. What was Germany's response to Russia's mobilization?

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Germany demanded that Russia's mobilization be cancelled and, when Russia failed to comply, declared war on Russia. Germany interpreted Russian mobilization as an act of aggression requiring a military response, despite mobilization being defensive in nature.

Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Russia had declared war before Britain entered. Austria declared war on Serbia (28 July), Germany on Russia (1 August) and France (3 August), and Austria on Russia (6 August), with Britain entering on 4 August after Germany's invasion of Belgium.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The assassination triggered the alliance system, leading to general war. Austria's declaration of war on Serbia brought in Russia, Germany, France, and Britain through their respective alliance obligations and strategic interests, turning a local Balkan conflict into a world war.

Sarajevo was the capital of Bosnia, which Austria had annexed in 1908 despite Serbian nationalist aspirations. The location symbolized the conflict between Austrian control and Serbian nationalism, making it a fitting and explosive site for the assassination.

Britain demanded German withdrawal from Belgium and, when this demand was ignored, declared war on 4 August. The violation of Belgian neutrality allowed Britain to enter the war with public support and moral justification under its 1839 treaty obligations.

📖 Explanation:

Europe lost its dominant position in the world as a result of World War I. The war devastated European economies, destroyed populations, and exhausted the great powers, leading to the emergence of the United States as a global power and shifting the center of world influence.

B. Only Austria and Serbia
C. Germany, France, and Britain

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The immediate declarations of war in 1914 involved Serbia, Austria-Hungary, Russia, Germany, France, and Britain. The sequence of declarations—Austria on Serbia, Germany on Russia and France, Austria on Russia, and Britain on Germany—escalated the conflict to a general war within days.

Q35. What was the Austrian goal in declaring war on Serbia?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Austria's goal was to end Serbian nationalism and its threat to the empire. Serbia's nationalism and territorial ambitions directly threatened the Habsburg Empire's stability by encouraging nationalist movements among its diverse populations, making the destruction of Serbia a priority.

Q36. What was the German strategy for war after Russia's mobilization?

B. Declare war on Russia and France first ✅
C. Negotiate with Britain
D. Attack only Russia

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Germany's strategy was to declare war on Russia and France first, implementing the Schlieffen Plan to defeat France quickly before turning to Russia. This required invading Belgium, which brought Britain into the war. The strategy reflected German fears of a two-front war.

A. It was irrelevant

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of 1839 committed Britain to defend Belgian neutrality. This treaty obligation provided the legal and moral basis for Britain's declaration of war after Germany invaded Belgium, allowing Britain to enter the conflict as a defender of international law and small nations.

B. Through direct war between Austria and Serbia only
C. Through German aggression alone
D. Through Russian expansion

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The assassination led to World War I through the operation of the alliance system. Austria's war on Serbia brought in Russia, Germany's support of Austria brought in France, and Germany's invasion of Belgium brought in Britain, demonstrating how a local conflict could escalate into a general war through interlocking alliances.

The international community responded with limited protests but no effective action. While some powers urged moderation, no major power was willing or able to prevent Austria from proceeding with its ultimatum and subsequent declaration of war, as each was focused on its own strategic interests.

Austria-Hungary was multi-national and threatened by nationalism before the assassination. Serbia's goal was to create a Greater Serbia (Yugoslavia) by uniting all Serbs and Croats. This nationalist ambition directly threatened Austria-Hungary because many Serbs and Croats lived within the Habsburg Empire, making Serbian nationalism an existential threat to Austrian stability.

The events of July 1914 demonstrated that diplomatic efforts failed to stop the escalation. 📖 Explanation:

Nationalism was the driving force behind the assassination. Gavrilo Princip was motivated by Serbian nationalist ideology seeking to free Bosnian Serbs from Austrian rule. Serbian nationalism also drove Austria's determination to crush Serbia, demonstrating how nationalism was a primary cause of the war.

Q46. Why did Austria receive German support before declaring war?

B. Germany wanted to demonstrate alliance solidarity ✅
C. Germany sought to expand its empire
D. Germany wanted to weaken Russia

📖 Explanation:

Germany supported Austria to demonstrate alliance solidarity and maintain the credibility of the Triple Alliance. By providing unconditional support, Germany showed it would stand by its ally, but this encouragement made Austria more aggressive and made war more likely.
