What is First Balkan War 1912 causes and results?

Driven by nationalist ambitions and the decline of Ottoman power, the Balkan League successfully expelled Turkey from most of its European territories, but the uneven distribution of conquered lands created bitter resentment among victors and set the stage for immediate future conflict in the region.

📝 All First Balkan War 1912 causes and results MCQs

Q1. In what year did the First Balkan War begin?

A.1911
B.1912 ✅
C.1913
D.1914
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The First Balkan War began in 1912 when Serbia, Greece, Montenegro, and Bulgaria (the Balkan League) launched attacks on Turkey. This conflict marked a significant shift in Balkan power dynamics and increased tensions among European powers.

Q2. Which countries formed the Balkan League in 1912?

A.Serbia, Greece, Montenegro, and Bulgaria ✅
B.Serbia, Romania, Bulgaria, and Turkey
C.Greece, Serbia, Austria, and Bulgaria
D.Montenegro, Serbia, Turkey, and Greece
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Balkan League consisted of Serbia, Greece, Montenegro, and Bulgaria. These former Turkish territories united to attack the weakened Ottoman Empire and acquire more land at Turkey's expense.

Q3. What was Turkey commonly referred to as by other European powers in 1912?

A.The Rising Sun
B.The Sick Man of Europe ✅
C.The Eastern Power
D.The Ottoman Giant
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Turkey was regarded by the other powers as 'the Sick Man of Europe' because of its military weakness and internal decline. This perception encouraged Balkan states to attack and seize Turkish territory in Europe.

Q4. Why did the Balkan League attack Turkey in 1912?

A.Turkey attacked them first
B.Turkey was weak and they wanted to acquire land ✅
C.Germany encouraged them
D.Britain ordered them to
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Balkan League attacked Turkey because Turkey was weak and they wanted to acquire more land at Turkey's expense. All these countries had once been part of the Turkish Empire and saw an opportunity to regain territory.

Q5. What was the military result of the First Balkan War for Turkey?

A.Turkey defeated the Balkan League
B.Turkey captured new territories
C.Turkey lost most of its remaining European territory ✅
D.The war ended in stalemate
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Turkey lost most of its remaining European territory to the Balkan League. The combined forces of Serbia, Greece, Montenegro, and Bulgaria overwhelmed the weakened Ottoman forces, dramatically reducing Turkey's European possessions.

Q6. Who arranged the peace conference to end the First Balkan War?

A.Sir Edward Grey and Germany ✅
B.France and Russia
C.Austria-Hungary and Italy
D.The United States
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Sir Edward Grey, the British Foreign Secretary, together with the German government, arranged a peace conference in London. This cooperation was notable as it demonstrated that Britain and Germany could work together diplomatically.

Q7. Where was the peace conference for the First Balkan War held?

A.Paris
B.Vienna
C.London ✅
D.Berlin
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The peace conference was held in London. Sir Edward Grey arranged this conference with the German government to end the fighting and prevent the conflict from spreading to involve the major European powers.

Q8. Why was Sir Edward Grey anxious to end the First Balkan War?

A.To help Turkey
B.To avoid the conflict spreading and demonstrate Anglo-German cooperation ✅
C.To weaken Serbia
D.To support Austria
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Grey was anxious to avoid the conflict spreading and also to demonstrate that Britain and Germany could still work together. This reflected British concerns about maintaining European stability and testing the possibility of cooperation with Germany.

Q9. What did the London peace settlement do with former Turkish lands?

A.Returned them to Turkey
B.Divided them among the Balkan states ✅
C.Gave them to Austria
D.Made them independent
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The London settlement divided up the former Turkish lands among the Balkan states. This territorial redistribution reflected the military realities of the war but created new tensions among the Balkan powers over their respective shares.

Q10. Which territory did Serbia want but failed to gain in the peace settlement?

A.Macedonia
B.Albania ✅
C.Bosnia
D.Kosovo
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Serbia wanted Albania, which would have given them an outlet to the sea. However, Austria, with German and British support, insisted that Albania should become an independent state, denying Serbia access to the Adriatic.

Q11. Why did Austria insist on Albanian independence?

A.To help Albania
B.To prevent Serbia becoming more powerful ✅
C.To support Turkey
D.To please Britain
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Austria insisted on Albanian independence as a deliberate move to prevent Serbia becoming more powerful. Access to the sea would have strengthened Serbia economically and strategically, threatening Austrian interests and influence in the Balkans.

Q12. Which powers supported Austria's position on Albanian independence?

A.France and Russia
B.Germany and Britain ✅
C.Italy and Turkey
D.Serbia and Greece
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Austria was supported by Germany and Britain in insisting that Albania should become an independent state. This alliance of powers against Serbian ambitions demonstrated the complex diplomatic alignments in the Balkans.

Q13. Why were the Bulgarians dissatisfied with the peace settlement?

A.They received no territory
B.They wanted Macedonia but most was given to Serbia ✅
C.They lost territory to Turkey
D.They wanted Albania
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Bulgarians were dissatisfied because they had been hoping for Macedonia, but most of it had been given to Serbia. This territorial dispute led Bulgaria to attack Serbia, starting the Second Balkan War.

Q14. What action did Bulgaria take after the First Balkan War settlement?

A.They accepted the settlement
B.They attacked Serbia ✅
C.They allied with Austria
D.They withdrew from the Balkans
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Bulgaria attacked Serbia because they were dissatisfied with their gains from the peace settlement and blamed Serbia for receiving most of Macedonia. This attack triggered the Second Balkan War.

Q15. Which countries supported Serbia when Bulgaria attacked?

A.Greece, Romania, and Turkey ✅
B.Austria, Germany, and Italy
C.France, Britain, and Russia
D.Montenegro only
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Greece, Romania, and Turkey rallied to support Serbia when Bulgaria attacked. This coalition defeated Bulgaria, demonstrating that Bulgaria had misjudged the balance of power and the willingness of other states to oppose its aggression.

Q16. What was the outcome of Bulgaria's attack on Serbia?

A.Bulgaria was victorious
B.The war ended in stalemate
C.Bulgaria was defeated and forfeited most of its gains ✅
D.Serbia surrendered
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Bulgaria was defeated when Greece, Romania, and Turkey supported Serbia. By the Treaty of Bucharest (1913), Bulgaria forfeited most of its gains from the First Balkan War, significantly reducing its territorial expansion.

Q17. What treaty ended the Second Balkan War?

A.Treaty of London
B.Treaty of Berlin
C.Treaty of Bucharest ✅
D.Treaty of Versailles
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Treaty of Bucharest in 1913 ended the Second Balkan War. Under this treaty, Bulgaria forfeited most of its gains from the first war, reflecting its military defeat by the Serbian-led coalition.

Q18. What was the Bulgarian result of the Treaty of Bucharest?

A.They gained all of Macedonia
B.They forfeited most of their gains from the first war ✅
C.They became the dominant Balkan power
D.They allied with Austria
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: By the Treaty of Bucharest, Bulgaria forfeited most of its gains from the First Balkan War. The treaty punished Bulgaria's aggression and reduced its territorial expansion to pre-war levels, leaving Serbia as the primary beneficiary of the Balkan Wars.

Q19. What seemed to have prevented escalation of the First Balkan War?

A.French military intervention
B.Anglo-German influence restraining Austria ✅
C.Russian threats to Austria
D.American mediation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Anglo-German influence seemed to have prevented an escalation of the war by restraining the Austrians, who were itching to support Bulgaria and attack Serbia. This demonstrated the potential for Anglo-German cooperation in maintaining European stability.

Q20. What was Austria's position during the Second Balkan War?

A.They remained neutral
B.They wanted to support Bulgaria and attack Serbia ✅
C.They supported Serbia
D.They mediated peace
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Austria was itching to support Bulgaria and attack Serbia during the Second Balkan War. Only the restraining influence of Britain and Germany prevented Austria from intervening, which would have escalated the conflict significantly.

Q21. What consequence of the Balkan Wars involved Serbia?

A.Serbia was weakened
B.Serbia was strengthened and determined to stir trouble among Serbs in Austria ✅
C.Serbia lost territory
D.Serbia allied with Austria
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Serbia had been strengthened by the Balkan Wars and was determined to stir up trouble among the Serbs and Croats living inside Austria-Hungary. This aggressive nationalism directly threatened the stability of the Habsburg Empire.

Q22. What was Austria's reaction to Serbia's strengthened position?

A.They accepted Serbia's power
B.They were equally determined to end Serbia's ambitions ✅
C.They allied with Serbia
D.They withdrew from the Balkans
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Austrians were equally determined to put an end to Serbia's ambitions. The Balkan Wars had strengthened Serbia and its nationalist aspirations, which Austria viewed as an existential threat to its multi-national empire.

Q23. How did Germany interpret Britain's cooperation during the Balkan Wars?

A.As a sign of lasting alliance
B.As a sign that Britain could be detached from France and Russia ✅
C.As a sign of British weakness
D.As a sign of German dominance
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Germans took Grey's willingness to cooperate as a sign that Britain was prepared to be detached from France and Russia. This misinterpretation of British diplomacy contributed to German miscalculations about British intentions in 1914.

Q24. What did Serbia want Albania to provide?

A.An outlet to the sea ✅
B.A buffer against Austria
C.Agricultural land
D.Military bases
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Serbia wanted Albania to provide an outlet to the sea. Access to the Adriatic would have given Serbia strategic and economic advantages, but Austrian opposition with British and German support prevented this.

Q25. What did the Balkan League countries have in common?

A.They were all allies of Germany
B.They had all been part of the Turkish Empire ✅
C.They were all democracies
D.They were all naval powers
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: All the Balkan League countries—Serbia, Greece, Montenegro, and Bulgaria—had at one time been part of the Turkish (Ottoman) Empire. This shared history gave them common cause in attacking the weakened Ottoman state.

Q26. What was the significance of the London peace conference?

A.It ended all Balkan tensions
B.It divided former Turkish lands ✅
C.It created lasting peace
D.It led to war
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The London peace conference divided the former Turkish lands among the Balkan states. While it ended the First Balkan War, the settlement created new tensions over territorial distribution that would lead to the Second Balkan War.

Q27. How did the Balkan Wars affect relations between Britain and Germany?

A.They worsened them
B.They showed potential for cooperation ✅
C.They had no effect
D.They led to alliance
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Balkan Wars showed potential for Anglo-German cooperation, as Grey and Germany worked together to arrange the peace conference and restrain Austria. However, Germany's misinterpretation of this cooperation as a sign of British detachment from its allies ultimately undermined this potential.

Q28. What was the Austrian perspective on Serbian ambitions?

A.They supported Serbian expansion
B.They viewed it as a threat to their empire ✅
C.They were indifferent
D.They wanted to join Serbia
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Austria viewed Serbian ambitions as a direct threat to its empire. Serbia's desire to unite Serbs and Croats (including those inside Austria-Hungary) and its strengthened position after the Balkan Wars made it a serious danger to Habsburg stability.

Q29. What role did Romania play in the Second Balkan War?

A.They remained neutral
B.They supported Serbia against Bulgaria ✅
C.They supported Bulgaria against Serbia
D.They attacked Turkey
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Romania rallied to support Serbia when Bulgaria attacked. This support helped defeat Bulgaria and contributed to Bulgaria's territorial losses under the Treaty of Bucharest.

Q30. What was the German interpretation of Grey's cooperation during the Balkan Wars?

A.Germany and Britain were now allies
B.Britain could be detached from France and Russia ✅
C.Britain supported German expansion
D.Britain was weak
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Germans interpreted Grey's willingness to cooperate as a sign that Britain was prepared to be detached from France and Russia. This misreading of British policy led Germany to underestimate British commitment to the Entente when the 1914 crisis erupted.

Q31. How did the Balkan Wars affect Bulgaria's territorial position?

A.It was strengthened
B.It was significantly reduced ✅
C.It remained unchanged
D.It became a major power
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Bulgaria's territorial position was significantly reduced by the Second Balkan War. The Treaty of Bucharest forced Bulgaria to forfeit most of its gains from the First Balkan War, making it the loser of both Balkan conflicts.

Q32. What was the significance of Albania's independence?

A.It helped Serbia
B.It prevented Serbian sea access ✅
C.It strengthened Austria
D.It weakened Turkey
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Albania's independence prevented Serbia from gaining an outlet to the sea. This was a deliberate Austrian move to limit Serbian power and maintain Austrian influence in the region by denying Serbia strategic and economic advantages.

Q33. What was the condition of Turkey's European territories after the First Balkan War?

A.They were mostly lost to the Balkan League ✅
B.They expanded
C.They remained unchanged
D.They were gained by Austria
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Turkey's European territories were mostly lost to the Balkan League during the First Balkan War. The war dramatically reduced Ottoman holdings in Europe, leaving Turkey with minimal territory on the continent.

Q34. Which powers restrained Austria from attacking Serbia during the Balkan Wars?

A.Britain and Germany ✅
B.France and Russia
C.Italy and Turkey
D.The United States
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Britain and Germany restrained Austria from attacking Serbia. Anglo-German influence prevented Austrian intervention in the Second Balkan War, which would have escalated the conflict and potentially drawn in the major European powers.

Q35. What did Serbia gain from the First Balkan War?

A.Territory but not Albania ✅
B.Albania
C.Nothing
D.All of Macedonia
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Serbia gained territory from the First Balkan War but not Albania. While Serbia received significant territorial gains including parts of Macedonia, it was denied Albania by Austrian opposition, preventing the sea access it desired.

Q36. What was the relationship between the Balkan Wars and World War I?

A.They prevented the war
B.They contributed to tensions that led to war ✅
C.They had no connection
D.They ended European rivalries
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Balkan Wars contributed to tensions that led to World War I. Serbia was strengthened and became more aggressive in its nationalism, while Austria became more determined to crush Serbia. The wars also demonstrated Anglo-German cooperation potential but created miscalculations about British intentions.

Q37. What was the British government's view on conflict spreading during the Balkan Wars?

A.They wanted it to spread
B.They were anxious to avoid it spreading ✅
C.They were indifferent
D.They encouraged it
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The British government, particularly through Sir Edward Grey, was anxious to avoid the conflict spreading. This concern motivated Grey to arrange the peace conference and work with Germany to restrain Austria from intervening in the Balkans.

Q38. What role did Turkey play in the Second Balkan War?

A.They remained neutral
B.They supported Serbia against Bulgaria ✅
C.They supported Bulgaria against Serbia
D.They attacked Serbia
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Turkey rallied to support Serbia when Bulgaria attacked. Despite having lost territory to the Balkan League in the first war, Turkey joined the coalition against Bulgaria, contributing to Bulgaria's defeat under the Treaty of Bucharest.

Q39. What was Serbia's nationalist goal after the Balkan Wars?

A.To join Austria
B.To stir trouble among Serbs in Austria ✅
C.To ally with Turkey
D.To remain peaceful
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Serbia was determined to stir up trouble among the Serbs and Croats living inside Austria-Hungary. This nationalist goal reflected Serbia's ambition to unite all Serbs into a Greater Serbia, directly threatening Austrian territorial integrity.

Q40. What was Austria's determination after the Balkan Wars?

A.To accept Serbian power
B.To end Serbia's ambitions ✅
C.To ally with Serbia
D.To leave the Balkans
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Austria was equally determined to put an end to Serbia's ambitions. The Balkan Wars had shown that Serbia was growing stronger and more aggressive in its nationalism, making it an urgent threat that Austria believed must be dealt with before it became more powerful.

Q41. What did the Treaty of Bucharest (1913) require Bulgaria to do?

A.Expand its territory
B.Forfeit most of its gains from the first war ✅
C.Gain access to the sea
D.Join the Triple Alliance
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Treaty of Bucharest (1913) required Bulgaria to forfeit most of its gains from the First Balkan War. Bulgaria's defeat in the Second Balkan War resulted in significant territorial losses that reduced it to pre-war boundaries.

Q42. How did Germany view Britain's role in the Balkan peace conference?

A.As a sign of British weakness
B.As a sign of Anglo-German cooperation ✅
C.As a threat to German interests
D.As an insult to Germany
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Germany viewed Britain's role as a sign of Anglo-German cooperation. However, this positive interpretation led to the misjudgment that Britain could be detached from its Entente partners, an assumption that would prove disastrously wrong in 1914.

Q43. What was the strategic importance of Albania to Serbia?

A.Military defense
B.Access to the sea ✅
C.Agricultural land
D.Mountain passes
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Albania was strategically important to Serbia because it would provide access to the sea. Serbia's landlocked position limited its economic and strategic options, making Albanian ports crucial for trade and potential military operations.

Q44. Which Balkan state was the primary beneficiary of the Balkan Wars?

A.Bulgaria
B.Greece
C.Serbia ✅
D.Turkey
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Serbia emerged as the primary beneficiary of the Balkan Wars. While Bulgaria lost territory and Greece made gains, Serbia's territory expanded significantly, its military prestige grew, and it gained confidence in pursuing nationalist ambitions that would lead to confrontation with Austria.

Q45. What was the British approach to the Balkan crisis?

A.Support Austria
B.Work with Germany to maintain peace ✅
C.Support Serbia
D.Remain neutral
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Britain, through Sir Edward Grey, worked with Germany to maintain peace in the Balkans. This demonstrated British interest in European stability and willingness to cooperate with Germany, but also reflected British concern about preventing any single power from dominating the region.

Q46. What did the Balkan Wars reveal about the European alliance system?

A.It prevented war
B.It was flexible but creating tensions ✅
C.It had no effect
D.It caused the wars
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Balkan Wars revealed that the alliance system was flexible but created tensions. Anglo-German cooperation restrained Austria temporarily, but the underlying rivalries remained. The wars demonstrated the potential for conflict escalation while showing the limits of cooperation.

Q47. Why did the Serbs view Albanian independence as a setback?

A.They lost territory
B.They were denied sea access ✅
C.They wanted Albanian resources
D.They lost allies
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Serbs viewed Albanian independence as a setback because they were denied access to the sea. The Austrian-backed decision to create independent Albania prevented Serbia from gaining an Adriatic port, which would have strengthened Serbia's position considerably.

Q48. What was Germany's perception of British policy during the Balkan Wars?

A.Britain was anti-German
B.Britain could cooperate with Germany ✅
C.Britain was weak
D.Britain dominated Europe
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Germany perceived British policy as showing that Britain could cooperate with Germany. This perception, however, led to a dangerous miscalculation that Britain might be willing to abandon its Entente partners, influencing German decisions in 1914.

Q49. What was the Austrian goal in supporting Bulgarian interests?

A.To strengthen Bulgaria
B.To weaken Serbia ✅
C.To gain Bulgarian territory
D.To support Germany
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Austria's goal in supporting Bulgarian interests was to weaken Serbia. By backing Bulgaria against Serbia, Austria hoped to contain Serbian power and ambitions, preventing Serbia from becoming strong enough to threaten Austrian interests in the Balkans.

Q50. What was the overall impact of the Balkan Wars on Europe?

A.Reduced tensions
B.Maintained peace
C.Increased tensions and contributed to World War I ✅
D.Created lasting stability
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Balkan Wars increased tensions and contributed to World War I. They strengthened Serbia and its nationalist ambitions, made Austria more determined to destroy Serbia, and created miscalculations about alliance commitments that would prove fatal in 1914.
