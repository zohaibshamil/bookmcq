About 21. Communism in Korea and south east Asia

Communism took root in Korea and Southeast Asia through anti-colonial struggles and Cold War interventions, leading to the Korean War and prolonged conflicts in Vietnam, Laos, and Cambodia. These movements combined Marxist ideology with nationalist aspirations, often receiving support from China and the USSR while facing fierce opposition from the US and local anti-communist forces.

The outcomes varied widely: North Korea became a hereditary totalitarian state, Vietnam unified under communism after defeating the US, and Cambodia suffered genocide under the Khmer Rouge before Vietnamese intervention. These experiences show how communism adapted to local conditions and how superpower rivalry amplified regional conflicts with lasting consequences.
