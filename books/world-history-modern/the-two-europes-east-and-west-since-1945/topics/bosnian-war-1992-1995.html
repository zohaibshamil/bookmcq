What is Bosnian War 1992-1995?

Bosnia-Herzegovina descended into brutal three-way conflict among Bosniaks, Croats, and Serbs following independence referendum in 1992, marked by genocide at Srebrenica, siege of Sarajevo, and ethnic cleansing campaigns until NATO bombing and Dayton Peace Agreement established complex power-sharing arrangement.

Q1. What percentage of Bosnia's population was Muslim at the start of the war?

Demographic data from 1992 indicates that Muslims made up about 44%44% of the total population, the largest single ethnic group, which shaped the political claims of the new government and contributed to tensions with Serb and Croat minorities.

Q2. Which city was declared a UN safe area but later fell to Serb forces in 1995?

A. Sarajevo
B. Banja Luka
C. Srebrenica ✅
D. Mostar

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Srebrenica was designated a UN safe area in 1993, yet in July 1995 it was overrun by Bosnian Serb forces, leading to the massacre of thousands of Bosniak men and boys, a stark example of the UN's inability to protect designated zones without sufficient troops.

Q3. Who was the Muslim president of Bosnia when it declared independence in March 1992?

A. Alija Izetbegović ✅
B. Radovan Karadžić
C. Franjo Tuđman
D. Slobodan Milošević

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Alija Izetbegović was elected as the first president of the newly independent Bosnia and Herzegovina, representing the Bosniak (Muslim) majority, and his leadership was contested by Bosnian Serb leaders who rejected the new constitution and sought autonomy for Serb-majority areas.

Q4. What was the primary objective of the Bosnian Serbs regarding the eastern strip of Bosnia?

A. To join Serbia ✅
B. To create an independent Croat state
C. To establish a UN protectorate
D. To merge with Croatia

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Bosnian Serb leadership aimed to carve out a contiguous corridor in eastern Bosnia that bordered Serbia, hoping to annex this territory to Serbia, thereby strengthening Serb dominance and creating a land bridge that would facilitate political and military integration with the Serbian state.

📖 Explanation:

In 1993 the United Nations designated Srebrenica, Zepa, and Gorazde as safe areas, intending to protect Bosniak civilians from Serb attacks, but insufficient troop deployments meant these enclaves could not be effectively defended when the Bosnian Serb army launched offensives.

The European Community quickly recognized Bosnia and Herzegovina's independence, mirroring its earlier stance on Croatia, yet it failed to secure guarantees for minority rights, a lapse that contributed to escalating ethnic tensions and the outbreak of war.
