What is Kosovo War 1998-1999?

Ethnic Albanian Kosovo Liberation Army fought Serbian forces seeking autonomy for Kosovo province, escalating to humanitarian crisis with mass expulsions that prompted NATO air campaign against Yugoslavia in 1999, ending Serbian control and placing Kosovo under UN administration pending final status resolution.

📝 All Kosovo War 1998-1999 MCQs

Q1. In which year did non‑violent protests begin in Kosovo?

A.1985
B.1989 ✅
C.1991
D.1993
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The non‑violent protests started in 1989, led by Albanian intellectuals who sought greater autonomy within Yugoslavia. This early movement set the stage for later resistance but was initially ignored by the Yugoslav authorities, contributing to growing frustration among Kosovo Albanians.

Q2. Who led the non‑violent protests that started in Kosovo?

A.Slobodan Milošević
B.Ibrahim Rugova ✅
C.Vojislav Šešelj
D.Radovan Karadžić
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Ibrahim Rugova emerged as the symbolic leader of peaceful resistance, advocating for dialogue and autonomy. His approach contrasted sharply with the later militant tactics of the KLA, highlighting a shift from diplomatic to armed struggle as frustration grew.

Q3. Which organization emerged as the radical Albanian element during the Kosovo conflict?

A.KLA ✅
B.UNMIK
C.NATO
D.ICTY
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Kosovo Liberation Army (KLA) formed as a militant response when peaceful protests failed to achieve results. It became the primary armed group fighting Serbian forces, escalating the conflict into a near‑civil war by the late 1990s.

Q4. In what year did NATO commence bombing attacks against Serbia over Kosovo?

A.1995
B.1997
C.1999 ✅
D.2001
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: NATO began its air campaign in March 1999 after diplomatic efforts stalled. The bombing aimed to halt Serbian repression of Kosovo Albanians and force Milošević to accept a peace settlement, marking a major international intervention.

Q5. Approximately how many peacekeeping troops did NATO and the UN initially deploy to Kosovo?

A.10000
B.20000
C.40000 ✅
D.60000
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Following the cease‑fire, a joint NATO–UN force of roughly 40,000 personnel entered Kosovo to enforce peace, protect civilians, and stabilize the region after the intensive bombing campaign.

Q6. By 1997, Yugoslavia consisted of which two republics?

A.Slovenia and Croatia
B.Serbia and Montenegro ✅
C.Bosnia and Herzegovina
D.Macedonia and Kosovo
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: After the breakup of the Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, only Serbia and Montenegro remained unified as the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, providing Milošević a platform to retain power despite internal political constraints.

Q7. Which tribunal indicted Slobodan Milošević for crimes related to Kosovo?

A.International Criminal Court
B.International Court of Justice
C.International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia ✅
D.European Court of Human Rights
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY) issued an indictment in May 1999, charging Milošević with responsibility for war crimes committed by Yugoslav forces in Kosovo, marking a historic move against a sitting head of state.

Q8. What primary factor drove public opinion against Milošević in 2000?

A.Military victories
B.Economic hardships ✅
C.Cultural reforms
D.Foreign aid
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Widespread shortages of food, fuel, and the collapse of the economy created daily hardships for citizens, eroding support for Milošević and fueling mass protests that ultimately contributed to his political downfall.

Q9. Which UN mission was tasked with administering Kosovo after the conflict?

A.UNPROFOR
B.UNMIK ✅
C.UNIFIL
D.UNOSAT
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The United Nations Mission in Kosovo (UNMIK) was established to oversee civil administration, rebuild institutions, and ensure security until local governance structures could function independently.

Q10. By the end of 2003, how many peacekeeping troops remained in Kosovo?

A.5000
B.10000
C.20000 ✅
D.40000
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: After the initial deployment, troop numbers were gradually reduced, but about 20,000 peacekeepers stayed on the ground by late 2003 to continue stabilizing the region and supporting UNMIK’s mandate.

Q11. If the initial peacekeeping deployment was 40,000 troops and later reduced to 20,000, what proportion of the original force remained?

A.0.25
B.0.33
C.0.5 ✅
D.0.75
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The remaining force represents half of the original number, calculated as \( \frac{20,000}{40,000}=0.5\), which corresponds to 50 % of the initial deployment, indicating a significant but still substantial presence.

📖 Explanation: The text notes that rather than capitulating, Milošević became more determined, launching an ethnic‑cleansing campaign. This reaction demonstrates how external military pressure can sometimes harden a leader’s stance instead of prompting immediate compliance.

Q13. Assume the KLA had 5,000 fighters while Serbian security forces deployed 20,000. What is the ratio of KLA fighters to Serbian forces?

📖 Explanation: Dividing 5,000 by 20,000 yields \( \frac{5,000}{20,000}=0.25\), which simplifies to a 1 to 4 ratio, illustrating the numerical disadvantage faced by the KLA during the height of the conflict.

Q14. If 150,000 Albanian refugees fled to Albania, Macedonia, and Montenegro in equal numbers, how many refugees went to each country?

A.30000
B.45000
C.50000 ✅
D.60000
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Dividing 150,000 by three yields \( \frac{150,000}{3}=50,000\) refugees per country, highlighting the sizable displacement burden placed on each neighboring state.

Q15. How did the omission of Kosovo from the 1995 US‑led peace negotiations affect later conflict dynamics?

A.It reduced tensions
B.It delayed international response
C.It contributed to escalation ✅
D.It resolved the issue
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Leaving Kosovo out of the 1995 talks meant its grievances were not addressed, allowing resentment to fester. When violence later erupted, the lack of prior diplomatic groundwork made the situation more explosive and harder to contain.

Q16. Which two countries drafted the peace agreement that Milošević eventually accepted?

A.Germany and France
B.Russia and Finland ✅
C.USA and UK
D.Italy and Greece
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The settlement was negotiated by Russia and Finland, reflecting a diplomatic effort that combined Eastern and Western interests to persuade Milošević to withdraw forces and end hostilities.

Q17. UNMIK employed 10,000 civilian staff while 20,000 troops were present. What is the staff‑to‑troop ratio?

📖 Explanation: Dividing the number of civilian staff (10,000) by the number of troops (20,000) gives a ratio of 1 to 2, indicating that for every two soldiers there was one civilian administrator supporting the mission.

Q18. What was a direct demographic consequence of the ethnic cleansing campaign ordered by Milošević?

A.Return of Serb civilians
B.Displacement of Albanians ✅
C.Increase in Serbian birth rates
D.Reduction of refugee flows
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The campaign forced hundreds of thousands of ethnic Albanians to flee Kosovo, dramatically altering the region’s population composition and creating a massive refugee crisis across neighboring countries.

Q19. By 1998, Milošević had already served how many terms as president of Serbia?

A.One
B.Two ✅
C.Three
D.Four
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Milošević was in his second term by 1998, having first assumed the presidency in the late 1980s. Constitutional limits barred a third term, prompting him to seek alternative routes to retain power.

Q20. If Serbia’s GDP before sanctions was \(100\) billion dollars and fell by 30 %, what was the GDP after the decline?

A.\(70\) billion ✅
B.\(80\) billion
C.\(90\) billion
D.\(60\) billion
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A 30 % reduction of \(100\) billion yields \(100 \times (1-0.30)=70\) billion dollars, reflecting the severe economic impact of sanctions and the NATO campaign on the Serbian economy.

Q21. In which city was heavily targeted during the 1999 NATO campaign due to its strategic importance?

A.Belgrade ✅
B.Pristina
C.Novi Sad
D.Niš
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Belgrade, the capital of Serbia, was a primary target because of its political and military significance, aiming to pressure Milošević’s regime into halting operations in Kosovo.

Q22. If 200,000 Albanians were displaced and 60 % returned within a year, how many remained displaced?

A.40000
B.80000 ✅
C.120000
D.160000
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Sixty percent of 200,000 equals 120,000 returning, leaving 40 % still displaced. Multiplying \(200,000 \times 0.40\) gives 80,000 people who remained without homes after one year.

Q23. Which of the following best describes the role of Russia in the 1999 peace agreement?

A.Primary negotiator
B.Passive observer
C.Opponent
D.Mediator ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Russia acted as a mediator alongside Finland, helping to craft a settlement that would be acceptable to both the Serbian government and the international community, illustrating its diplomatic influence in the Balkans.

Q24. Assuming the peace agreement required Serbia to withdraw 10,000 troops from Kosovo, and the total Serbian force was 25,000, what percentage remained?

A.0.3

C.0.6 ✅
D.0.8

📖 Explanation: If 10,000 troops withdrew, 15,000 stayed. The percentage remaining is \( \frac{15,000}{25,000}=0.60\), or 60 %, indicating a substantial military presence persisted despite the agreement.

Q25. What primary challenge did Kosovo face after the return of refugees, according to the passage?

A.Lack of housing ✅
B.Overabundance of jobs
C.Decrease in ethnic tension
D.Increase in tourism
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The influx of returning refugees strained existing infrastructure, creating a housing shortage that, together with poverty and unemployment, fueled public impatience toward UNMIK and the slow pace of reconstruction.

Q26. Critically assess why the international community opted for air strikes rather than a ground invasion in Kosovo.

A.To minimize civilian casualties
B.To avoid NATO casualties
C.To bypass UN Security Council veto
D.All of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: All listed reasons influenced the decision: air strikes reduced the risk to civilians and NATO troops, sidestepped the need for a UN Security Council resolution authorizing a ground operation, and allowed rapid pressure on Milošević while limiting political fallout.

Q27. How does the timeline of the Kosovo conflict compare with the Bosnian peace settlement?

📖 Explanation: The Bosnian war concluded with the Dayton Agreement in 1995, whereas Kosovo’s violence intensified later, particularly after 1998, showing that the latter conflict unfolded in the wake of the earlier settlement and was partly a spill‑over of unresolved tensions.

Q29. What legal significance did indicting a sitting head of state like Milošević have for international law?

A.It set no precedent
B.It reinforced sovereign immunity
C.It established that heads of state can be prosecuted ✅
D.It limited ICC jurisdiction
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The indictment demonstrated that even sitting leaders could be held accountable for war crimes, weakening the doctrine of absolute sovereign immunity and reinforcing the principle that individuals, regardless of rank, are subject to international criminal law.

📖 Explanation: Corruption claims eroded confidence in the UN administration, amplifying existing grievances about unemployment and poverty, and prompting demonstrations demanding faster progress toward self‑governance and better economic conditions.
