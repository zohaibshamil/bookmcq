What is Treaty of Nice problems and tensions?

The Treaty of Nice faced criticism for complex voting arrangements favoring smaller states, insufficient institutional streamlining for 25+ members, unresolved questions about EU’s final borders and constitutional identity, and public skepticism reflected in Irish referendum rejection requiring renegotiation before ratification.

📝 All Treaty of Nice problems and tensions MCQs

Q1. Which leader advocated a strong European government with more power to the Commission and Council?

A. Gerhard Schröder ✅
B. Tony Blair
C. John Major
D. Jacques Chirac

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Gerhard Schröder, the German Chancellor at the time, championed a federal‑style EU with enhanced authority for the European Commission and the Council of Ministers, seeking a constitution that would centralise decision‑making, unlike the more intergovernmental stance of the United Kingdom.

Q2. Which three countries supported Schröder’s vision of a stronger federal Europe?

A. Belgium, Finland, Luxembourg ✅
B. France, Italy, Spain
C. Poland, Hungary, Czech Republic
D. Denmark, Portugal, Greece

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Schröder found allies in Belgium, Finland and Luxembourg, all of which favoured deeper integration and a more powerful EU structure, aligning with his federal aspirations while many other members preferred a looser association.

Q3. What major event on \(11\) September 2001 threw the EU into confusion?

A. The fall of the Berlin Wall
B. The terrorist attacks in the USA ✅
C. The signing of the Treaty of Nice
D. The enlargement of the EU in \(2004\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The terrorist attacks on the United States on \(11\) September 2001 created a security crisis that the EU was unprepared for, prompting quick expressions of solidarity but revealing gaps in collective defence and foreign‑policy mechanisms.

Q4. Which country felt that political integration had gone far enough and opposed further loss of national powers?

A. Germany
B. France
C. United Kingdom ✅
D. Netherlands

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United Kingdom argued that the EU should not transfer additional authority from national governments to a federal body, insisting that cooperation should occur between sovereign states rather than through a centralised European administration.

France and Germany advocated for a diplomatic solution, emphasizing the need for United Nations‑mandated weapons inspections and a cautious approach, contrasting sharply with the more immediate military preferences of the United Kingdom, Italy and Spain.

The split between pro‑intervention states and those favouring diplomatic channels revealed fundamental disagreements, slowing the development of a cohesive foreign policy and demonstrating that consensus on security matters was still elusive despite constitutional ambitions.

📖 Explanation:

A binding decision‑making process would likely have forced member states to align with a collective stance on Iraq, reducing unilateral national actions and potentially preventing the split between the UK‑Italy‑Spain bloc and the France‑Germany bloc.

📖 Explanation:

Rumsfeld’s remark attacks the character of the opposition rather than addressing their substantive arguments, constituting an ad hominem fallacy that diverts attention from the legitimate concerns about UN authority and diplomatic solutions.

The United Kingdom, historically wary of ceding sovereignty to EU institutions, would likely resist a newcomer’s demand for a blanket veto, fearing it could further limit the ability of the EU to act decisively in security matters.
