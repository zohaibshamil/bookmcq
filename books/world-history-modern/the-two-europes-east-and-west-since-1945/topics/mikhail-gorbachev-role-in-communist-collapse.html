📝 Mikhail Gorbachev role in communist collapse

📖 From World History: Modern • 10. The Two Europes: East and West since 1945 • 33 questions available

What is Mikhail Gorbachev role in communist collapse?

Soviet leader Mikhail Gorbachev’s policies of glasnost (openness) and perestroika (restructuring), combined with his refusal to intervene militarily in Eastern Europe, inadvertently unleashed forces that dismantled communist regimes by removing the threat of Soviet force and encouraging reform movements across the bloc.

📝 All Mikhail Gorbachev role in communist collapse MCQs

Q1. In which year did Mikhail Gorbachev become the leader of the USSR?

A. 1985 ✅
B. 1991
C. 1975
D. 1980

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Gorbachev assumed the position of General Secretary after the death of Konstantin Chernenko in March 1985, marking a new era of attempted reforms within the Soviet Union. His ascension was seen as a potential turning point, as he promised to address systemic problems and modernize the economy.

Q2. Which Soviet policy introduced by Gorbachev is best described as 'openness'?

A. Perestroika
B. Glasnost ✅
C. Demokratizatsiya
D. Leninism

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Glasnost, meaning openness, was a cornerstone of Gorbachev’s reform agenda. It allowed greater freedom of speech, reduced censorship, and encouraged public discussion of social and political issues, contrasting sharply with previous periods of strict information control. This policy aimed to expose problems and stimulate corrective action.

Q3. Which policy aimed at economic and social reform?

A. Glasnost
B. Perestroika ✅
C. Khrushchev Thaw
D. Brezhnev Doctrine

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Perestroika, literally meaning restructuring, was Gorbachev’s strategy to revitalize the Soviet economy and society. It sought to introduce market mechanisms, reduce central planning rigidity, and encourage enterprise autonomy, while also attempting to modernize social institutions. The policy was intended to halt stagnation and increase productivity.

Q4. Which country’s hard‑line communist leadership was Gorbachev NOT directly involved in overthrowing?

A. Czechoslovakia
B. East Germany
C. Romania
D. United States ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

While Gorbachev’s diplomatic efforts influenced changes in several Eastern Bloc nations, there is no evidence of direct involvement in the overthrow of the United States’ leadership. His influence was primarily confined to Soviet satellite states, where he encouraged reformist movements without direct military intervention.

Q5. Gorbachev hoped to save which ideology by revitalizing it?

A. Communism ✅
B. Capitalism
C. Socialism
D. Fascism

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Gorbachev believed that by reforming and modernizing the existing system, he could preserve communism and prevent its disintegration. He argued that openness and restructuring would address inefficiencies without abandoning the ideological foundations, aiming to demonstrate that socialism could adapt to contemporary challenges.

Q6. How did glasnost change the nature of public criticism within the Soviet Union?

A. It banned all criticism
B. It allowed criticism of the system but not the Party ✅
C. It encouraged criticism of the Party
D. It had no effect

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Glasnost permitted citizens to discuss and critique shortcomings of the Soviet system, such as economic mismanagement and bureaucratic corruption, while still drawing a line at direct attacks on the Communist Party itself. This limited scope gave people a voice, yet maintained the party’s core authority.

Q7. If Gorbachev had decided to send Soviet troops to suppress the Romanian uprising, what would have been the most likely immediate impact on his reform agenda?

A. It would have accelerated reforms
B. It would have caused widespread support
C. It would have undermined his credibility and slowed reforms ✅
D. It would have had no effect

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Deploying troops would have contradicted Gorbachev’s image as a reformer, alienating reform‑supporting citizens and hard‑liners alike. The perception of a heavy‑handed response would likely have eroded trust, making further political and economic liberalization far more difficult to implement.

Q8. Calculate the number of years between Gorbachev’s rise to power in \(1985\) and the dissolution of the USSR in \(1991\).

A. \(6\) years ✅
B. \(5\) years
C. \(7\) years
D. \(8\) years

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The interval from March 1985, when Gorbachev became General Secretary, to December 1991, when the Soviet Union formally ceased to exist, spans six calendar years. This period encompassed the introduction of glasnost and perestroika, escalating unrest, and ultimately the breakup of the Soviet state.

Q9. Which recent military engagement most limited Gorbachev’s willingness to use force in Eastern Europe?

A. Withdrawal from Afghanistan ✅
B. Invasion of Poland
C. Conflict in the Baltic
D. War in Chechnya

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The costly and unpopular Soviet withdrawal from Afghanistan, completed in 1989, exhausted the military and demonstrated the limits of Soviet power abroad. Having just ended a prolonged conflict, Gorbachev was reluctant to commit troops elsewhere, preferring diplomatic solutions to avoid further domestic backlash.

Q10. Why did perestroika fail to prevent the collapse of the Soviet system despite its economic goals?

A. It was implemented too late
B. It focused solely on agriculture
C. Structural inefficiencies and political resistance outweighed reforms ✅
D. It increased foreign debt

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Perestroika encountered entrenched bureaucratic inertia, chronic shortages, and a lack of market expertise. Simultaneously, political resistance from hard‑liners stifled meaningful change, while the reforms themselves could not quickly generate the productivity needed to offset systemic deficits, leading to a loss of legitimacy.

Q11. Suppose perestroika had raised Soviet industrial output by \(10\%\) each year for five consecutive years. Approximately how much larger would the total output be after five years compared with the baseline, assuming compounding growth?

A. About \(50\%\) increase
B. About \(61\%\) increase ✅
C. About \(100\%\) increase
D. About \(150\%\) increase

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Compounding a 10 % annual increase yields a factor of \(1.1^{5}\approx1.61\), meaning output would be roughly 61 % greater than the original level after five years. This calculation illustrates the potential impact of sustained growth, though achieving such rates in a centrally planned economy proved unrealistic.

Q12. How does Gorbachev’s glasnost differ from Khrushchev’s Thaw in terms of the scope of political openness?

A. Glasnost was less extensive
B. Glasnost allowed broader public debate and media criticism ✅
C. Thaw allowed only cultural openness
D. Both were identical

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Glasnost expanded beyond the cultural liberalization of Khrushchev’s Thaw by explicitly sanctioning open discussion of political matters, encouraging investigative journalism, and exposing historical abuses. While the Thaw relaxed artistic constraints, glasnost directly challenged censorship and invited scrutiny of governmental policies, creating a more expansive arena for public discourse.

Q13. Which satellite state’s leadership was most resistant to Gorbachev’s reforms?

A. Romania ✅
B. East Germany
C. Czechoslovakia
D. Bulgaria

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Romania, under Nicolae Ceaușescu, maintained a rigid Stalinist line and rejected Gorbachev’s calls for openness and restructuring. Ceaușescu’s regime clung to centralized control, making it the most obstinate among Eastern Bloc governments and ultimately leading to a violent overthrow in December 1989.

Q14. In which year did Gorbachev officially announce the policy of glasnost?

A. 1985
B. 1986 ✅
C. 1987
D. 1988

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The policy of glasnost was formally introduced at the 27th Party Congress in February 1986. Gorbachev used this platform to signal a break from past secrecy, pledging greater transparency, reduced censorship, and the encouragement of public debate as part of his broader reform agenda.

Q15. What primary economic problem did Gorbachev identify as causing shortages in the USSR?

A. Waste and inefficiency ✅
B. High inflation
C. Unemployment
D. Trade deficit

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Gorbachev argued that chronic waste, bureaucratic mismanagement, and systemic inefficiency were the chief reasons for persistent shortages of consumer goods, despite the USSR’s status as a leading producer of steel, fuel, and energy. He believed addressing these flaws was essential for economic revitalization.

Q16. How did the absence of Soviet troop intervention affect the speed and character of revolutions across Eastern Europe?

A. It slowed them
B. It caused violent clashes
C. It accelerated peaceful transitions ✅
D. It had no impact

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Without the threat of Soviet military suppression, opposition movements were emboldened, leading to rapid, largely non‑violent protests that toppled regimes in a cascade. The vacuum of force removed a major deterrent, allowing civil society to organize openly and hastening the dissolution of communist governments.

Q17. What term best describes Gorbachev’s policy of political openness?

A. Glasnost ✅
B. Perestroika
C. Demokratizatsiya
D. Novaya Ekonomika

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Glasnost, directly translating to “openness,” encapsulated Gorbachev’s effort to reduce censorship, promote transparency, and encourage public dialogue about societal issues. This policy was a key component of his broader reform strategy, aiming to expose problems and foster a more responsive governance structure.

Q18. What was the general public reaction in the USSR to the perception that Gorbachev’s reforms were proceeding too slowly?

A. Strong support
B. Indifference
C. Growing criticism ✅
D. Celebration

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Many Soviet citizens grew impatient, viewing the gradual pace as insufficient to alleviate everyday hardships. This sentiment manifested in increasing criticism of both the reforms and the Communist Party, fueling demands for faster change and contributing to the erosion of the regime’s legitimacy.

Q19. If Gorbachev had postponed perestroika until 1990, would the Soviet Union likely have survived longer? Provide a plausible analysis.

A. Yes, because reforms would have been more effective
B. No, because economic decay would have been irreversible ✅
C. It would have made no difference
D. It would have caused immediate collapse

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Delaying perestroika would have allowed the already deteriorating economy to worsen, reducing the state’s capacity to fund any later reforms. The entrenched inefficiencies and mounting external pressures would likely have accelerated collapse, making a later start insufficient to reverse systemic decline.

Q20. Gorbachev succeeded which Soviet leader as General Secretary?

A. Konstantin Chernenko ✅
B. Leonid Brezhnev
C. Yuri Andropov
D. Nikita Khrushchev

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Following the brief tenure of Konstantin Chernenko, who died in March 1985, Mikhail Gorbachev was elected General Secretary of the Communist Party. His succession marked a generational shift and set the stage for the introduction of glasnost and perestroika.

Q21. Which of the following was NOT a direct outcome of perestroika?

A. Increased foreign investment
B. Greater political freedom
C. Strengthening of central planning ✅
D. Reduction of censorship

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Perestroika aimed to decentralize economic decision‑making and introduce market elements, which unintentionally weakened central planning rather than strengthening it. While it fostered some foreign investment and reduced censorship, the policy did not reinforce the existing centrally planned structure.

A. It provided extra resources
B. It drained fiscal capacity ✅
C. It had negligible impact
D. It increased foreign aid

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The sustained expenditure of about ten billion dollars annually strained the Soviet budget, diverting resources from domestic priorities and limiting fiscal flexibility. This financial pressure forced Gorbachev to seek economic restructuring and reduced defense spending, but also constrained the funds available for comprehensive reforms.

Q23. The term ‘people power’ in the context of 1989–1991 best reflects which outcome?

B. Triumph of authoritarianism
C. Triumph of communism
D. Triumph of neutrality

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Massive, non‑violent demonstrations across Eastern Europe demonstrated the effectiveness of popular mobilization, leading to the fall of communist regimes and the emergence of democratic governments. This phenomenon epitomized the concept of “people power,” where citizen activism drove political transformation.

Q24. What was Gorbachev’s official stance on deploying Soviet troops to suppress protests in the Eastern Bloc?

A. He would deploy them
B. He would not deploy them ✅
C. He was undecided
D. He supported local forces

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Gorbachev publicly declared that the USSR would not intervene militarily to crush dissent in its satellite states, reflecting a shift away from the Brezhnev‑era doctrine of using force to maintain control. This policy signaled a willingness to allow internal reforms, even if they threatened Soviet influence.

Q25. Which city was the focal point of the largest anti‑communist demonstration in 1989 that signaled the decline of Soviet influence?

B. Prague ✅
C. Warsaw
D. Leipzig

📖 Explanation:

The massive student-led protests in Prague, culminating in the Velvet Revolution, highlighted the weakening grip of Soviet authority. Demonstrators gathered in Wenceslas Square, demanding democratic reforms, and their success inspired similar movements throughout the Eastern Bloc.

📖 Explanation:

When the larger weight is placed on economic factors, the analysis concludes that systemic inefficiencies—such as chronic shortages, low productivity, and fiscal strain—were the dominant cause of the Soviet Union’s disintegration, outweighing the influence of political liberalization.

Q27. What does the Russian word ‘perestroika’ translate to in English?

A. Rebuilding ✅
B. Openness
C. Equality
D. Revolution

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The term perestroika literally means “restructuring” or “rebuilding,” reflecting Gorbachev’s intent to overhaul the Soviet economic and administrative systems. It signified a shift toward greater efficiency, decentralization, and modernization within the socialist framework.

Q28. Which earlier Soviet policy focused on de‑Stalinization and preceded Gorbachev’s reforms?

A. Khrushchev Thaw ✅
B. Glasnost
C. Brezhnev Stagnation
D. Yeltsin Reforms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Khrushchev Thaw of the late 1950s and early 1960s marked a period of de‑Stalinization, reduced censorship, and limited liberalization. This earlier attempt at reform set a historical precedent that Gorbachev later expanded upon with glasnost and perestroika.

An increase of fifteen percent in tax revenue would have supplied the state with extra capital, enabling broader implementation of perestroika projects, such as modernizing factories and supporting small enterprises, thereby potentially accelerating economic transformation.

By 1991, all former Soviet satellite states had experienced significant political change, with East Germany reunified, Czechoslovakia undergoing the Velvet Revolution, and Romania overthrowing its communist regime. Q32. Considering just war theory, what ethical argument can be made about Gorbachev’s decision to refrain from military intervention in Eastern Europe?

By abstaining from force, Gorbachev avoided disproportionate use of violence against largely civilian protestors, aligning with the principle of proportionality in just war theory. This restraint prevented unnecessary bloodshed and respected the moral requirement to limit harm to non‑combatants.
