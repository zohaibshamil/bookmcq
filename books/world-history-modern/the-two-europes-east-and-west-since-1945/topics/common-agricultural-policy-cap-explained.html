What is Common Agricultural Policy CAP explained?

The Common Agricultural Policy consumed most of the EC budget by guaranteeing farm prices and subsidizing production to ensure food security and rural livelihoods, but faced criticism for creating surpluses, distorting global trade, and benefiting large landowners disproportionately until successive reforms introduced environmental and market-oriented measures.

Q1. When were maximum production quotas first introduced under the CAP?

A. 1980
B. 1982
C. 1984 ✅
D. 1986

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The EC introduced its first set of maximum production quotas in 1984 as a response to mounting surpluses. This policy aimed to limit output and curb the growing budgetary pressure caused by unchecked agricultural production, marking a significant shift in CAP strategy.

Q2. Which three countries pressed for a limit on CAP subsidies before 1984?

A. France, Italy, Spain
B. Britain, Netherlands, West Germany ✅
C. Germany, Belgium, Denmark
D. Greece, Portugal, Ireland

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

British, Dutch and West German governments were the primary voices demanding a cap on subsidies, fearing fiscal unsustainability. Their collective lobbying reflected concerns about national budgets and the fairness of the subsidy system, especially as other members benefited heavily.

Q4. Which commodity formed the infamous “wine lake” in the 1980s?

A. Wine ✅
B. Cheese
C. Butter
D. Milk

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The term “wine lake” referred to the massive overproduction of wine that accumulated as a result of CAP incentives. Excess vines and guaranteed prices led to volumes far beyond market demand, creating a symbolic pool of unsold wine across the Community.

📖 Explanation:

While the EC exported surplus to the USSR, incinerated some outdated butter, and gave away excess to needy citizens, it never responded by raising subsidies further. Raising subsidies would have exacerbated overproduction, contrary to the goal of curbing surpluses.

Q20. Which economic principle was most compromised by the CAP’s subsidy system?

B. Comparative advantage
C. Fiscal neutrality
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

CAP subsidies interfered with free market pricing, distorted comparative advantage by encouraging production where it was not naturally efficient, and violated fiscal neutrality by allocating public funds preferentially. Thus, all listed principles were affected.
