Q1. In which year did Stalin expel Yugoslavia from the Cominform?

🔹 A. 1945
🔹 B. 1947
🔹 C. 1948
🔹 D. 1949

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Stalin removed Yugoslavia from the Cominform in 1948, marking a decisive break between Moscow and Tito's government. The expulsion was intended to isolate Yugoslavia economically and politically, but it instead highlighted the growing independence of Yugoslav communism and foreshadowed future splits within the Eastern bloc.

Q2. Who was the Yugoslav leader that openly defied Moscow in the late 1940s?

🔹 A. Josip Broz Tito
🔹 B. Nikita Khrushchev
🔹 C. Leon Trotsky
🔹 D. Vladimir Lenin

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Josip Broz Tito, the wartime resistance hero, became Yugoslavia's premier after World War II. By 1948 he rejected Stalin's directives, championing an autonomous form of socialism. Tito's refusal to submit to Soviet control made him the first satellite state leader to openly challenge Moscow's authority.

Q3. If Yugoslavia had accepted the Soviet economic plan, what would most likely have happened to its trade with the West?

🔹 A. Trade would increase substantially
🔹 B. Trade would decrease substantially
🔹 C. Trade would remain unchanged
🔹 D. Yugoslavia would face immediate sanctions

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Accepting a Soviet‑directed plan would have aligned Yugoslavia's economy with Warsaw Pact priorities, limiting market reforms and making western partners wary. Consequently, western nations would have reduced imports and investment, shrinking Yugoslavia's ability to diversify its trade and weakening its economic independence.

Q4. Which feature of Yugoslav workers' councils directly contradicted traditional Soviet centralization?

🔹 A. State ownership of all enterprises
🔹 B. Centralized five‑year planning
🔹 C. Worker self‑management of enterprises
🔹 D. Strict price controls

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Yugoslavia introduced workers' councils that gave employees direct control over production decisions, resource allocation, and profit distribution. This self‑management model opposed the Soviet tradition of top‑down planning, where the state dictated all economic activity without input from individual workers or local enterprises.

Q5. How could over‑employment in Yugoslav communes lead to inflation, using the relation \( \text{inflation} = \frac{\text{excess demand}}{\text{aggregate supply}} \times 100\% \)?

🔹 A. By raising excess demand while supply stays constant
🔹 B. By reducing excess demand relative to supply
🔹 C. By increasing aggregate supply faster than demand
🔹 D. By eliminating both demand and supply

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When too many workers are employed, wages rise but productivity does not, creating excess demand for consumer goods. If aggregate supply cannot keep pace, the numerator in the formula grows while the denominator remains static, causing the fraction—and thus the inflation rate—to increase sharply.

After being expelled from the Cominform, Yugoslavia turned to the International Monetary Fund for financial assistance. This move signaled a willingness to engage with Western financial institutions, further distancing the country from Soviet economic influence and demonstrating Tito's pragmatic approach to securing resources for his nation.

Q7. If a Yugoslav factory paid workers double wages, causing a 30% profit drop, what percentage of the original profit remains?

🔹 A. 0.7
🔹 B. 0.5
🔹 C. 0.3
🔹 D. 0

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A 30% reduction in profit means the factory retains 70% of its original earnings. This simple calculation illustrates how generous wage policies, while improving workers’ standards of living, can sharply cut profitability if not matched by corresponding gains in productivity or output.

Q8. Compare the political consequences of the 1948 Tito–Stalin split with the 1956 Hungarian uprising regarding Soviet willingness to use force.

🔹 A. Both led to immediate Soviet invasions
🔹 B. Tito’s split resulted in no invasion, while Hungary saw a full‑scale intervention
🔹 C. Both were resolved through diplomatic negotiations

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Tito–Stalin split demonstrated Soviet restraint; despite expelling Yugoslavia, Stalin avoided a costly invasion because of Yugoslavia’s terrain and Tito’s domestic legitimacy. In contrast, the Hungarian uprising threatened Soviet control over Central Europe, prompting a swift military crackdown to re‑assert Moscow’s dominance.

Yugoslavia structured its rural economy around communes, each comprising several thousand families. These communes elected assemblies that managed local economic, educational, health, and cultural affairs, embodying a decentralized approach that contrasted sharply with the Soviet reliance on large, state‑run collective farms.

Q11. If Yugoslavia’s IMF loan required a 5% annual interest, how much would a 100 million‑dinar loan cost after one year?

🔹 A. 5 million dinars
🔹 B. 10 million dinars
🔹 C. 15 million dinars
🔹 D. 20 million dinars

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A 5% interest on a 100 million‑dinar principal yields \(100{,}000{,}000 \times 0.05 = 5{,}000{,}000\) dinars. This modest cost allowed Yugoslavia to obtain foreign capital while maintaining a manageable debt burden, illustrating the pragmatic financial ties it cultivated beyond the Soviet sphere.

📖 Explanation:

Stalin underestimated Tito’s popular legitimacy, built on his wartime heroism and the appeal of workers’ self‑management. This internal strength, combined with Yugoslavia’s ability to secure Western trade and aid, meant that external pressure alone could not topple the regime, prompting Stalin to avoid a costly invasion.

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet approach concentrated decision‑making in Moscow, with ministries dictating production across the bloc. Yugoslavia, in contrast, devolved authority to workers’ councils and commune assemblies, encouraging local input and reducing the bureaucratic hierarchy that characterized Soviet economics.

Q18. If Yugoslavia’s trade with the West grew by 12% annually while Soviet aid fell by 8% annually, what is the net change after two years?

🔹 A. 0.08
🔹 B. 0.04
🔹 C. 0.2
🔹 D. 0

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each year the net effect is \(12\% - 8\% = 4\%\). Over two years the cumulative net change is \(4\% \times 2 = 8\%\). This simple additive approach shows how Yugoslavia could improve its overall economic position despite losing Soviet assistance.

Q19. The claim that “workers’ unwillingness to sack colleagues undermined Yugoslav productivity” illustrates which economic principle?

🔹 A. Moral hazard
🔹 B. Principal‑agent problem
🔹 C. Comparative advantage
🔹 D. Economies of scale

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When workers are reluctant to dismiss underperforming peers, the employer (principal) cannot enforce efficient labor practices, creating a principal‑agent problem. This misalignment of incentives reduces overall productivity because the agents (workers) prioritize job security over optimal performance.

Q22. If a Yugoslav commune’s budget is 200 million dinars, 60% is spent on wages, 25% on materials, and the remainder on infrastructure, how much is allocated to infrastructure?

🔹 A. 30 million dinars
🔹 B. 40 million dinars
🔹 C. 45 million dinars
🔹 D. 50 million dinars

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Infrastructure receives the leftover portion: \(100\% - 60\% - 25\% = 15\%\). Thus, \(200{,}000{,}000 \times 0.15 = 30{,}000{,}000\) dinars are dedicated to infrastructure projects, reflecting the commune’s commitment to public works despite limited resources.

Q26. Assume a Yugoslav factory employs 1 000 workers but over‑employment reduces productivity by 15%. If each worker originally produced 10 units, what is the new total output?

🔹 A. 8 500 units
🔹 B. 9 000 units
🔹 C. 9 500 units
🔹 D. 10 000 units

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Original output equals \(1{,}000 \times 10 = 10{,}000\) units. A 15% productivity decline reduces output to \(10{,}000 \times (1 - 0.15) = 8{,}500\) units, illustrating how over‑employment can erode efficiency and lower total production despite a larger workforce.

Q29. Using the equation \( \text{GDP growth} = \text{investment} \times \text{multiplier} \), if Yugoslavia’s investment fell by 20% and the multiplier is 2, what is the percentage change in GDP growth?

🔹 A. -0.4
🔹 B. -0.2
🔹 C. 0
🔹 D. 0.2

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

If investment declines to 80% of its former level, GDP growth becomes \(0.8 \times 2 = 1.6\) versus the original \(1 \times 2 = 2\). This represents a \( \frac{1.6-2}{2} \times 100 = -20\% \) change, indicating a proportional decrease in economic expansion due to reduced capital formation.
