Q1. In which year did the EU’s 2004 enlargement take place, expanding membership to ten new countries?

The 2004 enlargement was a landmark event that added ten Central and Eastern European nations to the Union, dramatically increasing its geographic scope and population. This expansion reinforced the EU’s role as a peace‑keeping and economic bloc, setting the stage for future integration efforts.

🔹 A. Military navigation
🔹 B. Civilian positioning
🔹 C. Weather forecasting
🔹 D. Telecommunications

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Galileo was conceived as a civilian‑focused global navigation satellite system, offering high‑precision positioning services for transport, logistics, and personal devices. By providing a non‑military alternative to the U.S. GPS, it underscores European technological autonomy and supports a wide range of commercial and scientific applications.

Q30. Critically assess why the EU’s Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) has been criticized for encouraging high production at the expense of quality. Which economic principle does this illustrate?

🔹 A. Comparative advantage
🔹 B. Opportunity cost
🔹 C. Supply‑side distortion due to price supports
🔹 D. Economies of scale

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The CAP’s price‑support mechanisms artificially raise farm incomes, encouraging producers to maximize output regardless of market demand. This supply‑side distortion leads to excess production, lower quality, and inefficiencies, exemplifying how price supports can misallocate resources away from optimal market outcomes.
