What is European Community institutions and structure?

The European Community operated through key institutions including the Commission (executive), Council of Ministers (national representatives), European Parliament (directly elected legislature), and Court of Justice (legal arbiter), creating a unique supranational governance structure that balanced national sovereignty with collective decision-making.

📝 All European Community institutions and structure MCQs

Q1. Which institution was responsible for the day‑to‑day work of the Community?

A. European Commission ✅
B. European Parliament
C. Council of Ministers
D. European Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The European Commission acted as the executive arm, handling daily administration, drafting proposals and implementing policies. It was the only body with the authority to manage routine operations, distinguishing it from the legislative and judicial institutions.

Q2. In which city was the European Commission headquartered?

A. Luxembourg
B. Brussels ✅
C. Frankfurt
D. Paris

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Brussels served as the seat of the Commission, housing its secretariat and senior officials. This location facilitated close interaction with other EU bodies and member‑state delegations, reinforcing Brussels' role as the political centre of the Community.

Q3. How many founding members did the European Community have?

A. Four
B. Five
C. Six ✅
D. Seven

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The original Community was formed by six countries—Belgium, France, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, and West Germany—creating a framework for economic cooperation that later expanded into the European Union.

Q4. Where did the European Parliament meet during the period described?

A. Luxembourg City
B. Berlin
C. Brussels
D. Strasbourg ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Strasbourg hosted the parliamentary sessions, providing a symbolic link to the Council of Europe while the Parliament debated legislation and offered recommendations to the other institutions.

Q5. What change occurred in 1979 regarding the selection of Parliament members?

A. They were elected directly by the citizens of the Community ✅
B. They were appointed by the Commission
C. They were selected by the Council of Ministers
D. They continued to be nominated by national parliaments

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The 1979 reform introduced direct elections, allowing citizens to vote for their representatives, thereby enhancing democratic legitimacy and reducing reliance on national parliamentary appointments.

Q6. Who composed the Council of Ministers?

A. Judges from the European Court of Justice
B. Civil servants of the Commission
C. Government representatives from each member state ✅
D. Members of the European Parliament

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Council consisted of ministers responsible for national economic and foreign policies, enabling them to exchange information, coordinate strategies, and approve legislation proposed by the Commission.

Q7. Which body could discuss issues and make recommendations but lacked control over the Commission or Council?

A. European Court of Justice
B. European Commission
C. European Parliament ✅
D. European Economic Community Council

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Parliament's role was consultative; it could debate proposals and suggest changes but did not possess the authority to bind the Commission or the Council, limiting its influence on policy implementation.

Q8. What was the primary function of the Council of Ministers?

A. Drafting legal codes for the Community
B. Exchanging information about economic policies and coordinating them ✅
C. Judging disputes between member states
D. Electing the President of the Commission

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Council acted as a forum where national ministers aligned their economic strategies, ensuring policy coherence across the six states and facilitating decisions that required collective approval.

Q9. Which institution possessed strong powers to resist criticism from member‑state governments?

A. Council of Ministers ✅
B. European Parliament
C. European Commission
D. European Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Commission was endowed with extensive executive authority, enabling it to propose legislation and enforce rules even when facing opposition from national governments, though its decisions still required Council endorsement.

Q10. Who staffed the European Commission during the period covered?

A. Military officers and diplomats
B. Civil servants and expert economists ✅
C. University professors only
D. Local government officials

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Commission's workforce combined professional civil servants with specialists in economics, providing the technical expertise needed to design policies, negotiate agreements, and manage the Community's complex administrative tasks.

Q11. If the Commission proposes a new regulation and the Council objects, what is the next procedural step?

A. The regulation is automatically adopted
B. The Commission must withdraw the proposal
C. The Council must vote to approve or reject the proposal ✅
D. The European Parliament decides the outcome

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Council of Ministers holds the decisive power to endorse or reject Commission proposals. Even though the Commission drafts regulations, final acceptance depends on a Council vote, reflecting the balance between executive initiative and member‑state oversight.

Q12. A member state argues that a Commission rule infringes on its sovereignty. Which institution can mediate the dispute?

A. European Parliament
B. Council of Ministers
C. European Court of Justice ✅
D. European Commission itself

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The European Court of Justice interprets Community law and resolves conflicts between institutions and member states, ensuring that Commission actions comply with treaty obligations and protecting national interests within the legal framework.

Q13. During a period of high friction, the Commission continues to issue numerous regulations while the Council seeks tighter coordination. Which outcome best reflects the institutional balance?

A. The Council overrides all Commission proposals
B. The Commission submits all proposals for parliamentary approval first
C. The Council and Commission negotiate compromises before Council voting ✅
D. The Parliament gains veto power over Commission rules

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The typical dynamic involves the Council negotiating with the Commission to shape proposals before voting, preserving both the Commission's initiative and the Council's coordinating role, while avoiding unilateral dominance by either body.

Q14. A a new economic policy is proposed by the Commission, but the Council demands an impact assessment. Which document would most likely satisfy the Council’s request?

A. A political manifesto
B. A budgetary annex
C. A scientific feasibility study
D. A cost‑benefit analysis using ΔGDP \Delta GDP ΔGDP estimates ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Council expects quantitative evidence of economic effects. A cost‑benefit analysis that quantifies changes in gross domestic product (ΔGDP \Delta GDP ΔGDP) provides the necessary data to evaluate the policy’s impact, aligning with the Council’s coordination mandate.

Q15. If the European Parliament adopts a recommendation that conflicts with a Commission proposal, what is the legal status of the recommendation?

A. It becomes binding law
B. It must be incorporated into the Commission’s draft
C. It remains non‑binding and advisory ✅
D. It forces the Council to reject the Commission proposal

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Parliamentary recommendations do not have legislative force; they serve to influence the Commission and Council but cannot compel changes unless the Commission chooses to incorporate them voluntarily.

Q16. Consider a case where the Commission’s rule imposes a tariff that harms a member’s agriculture sector. Which principle allows the member to challenge the rule?

A. Principle of subsidiarity
B. Principle of proportionality ✅
C. Principle of mutual recognition
D. Principle of free movement of goods

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The principle of proportionality requires that EU measures not exceed what is necessary to achieve objectives, permitting a member state to contest a rule that disproportionately impacts its sector.

Q17. In a hypothetical negotiation, the Council proposes a compromise that reduces the Commission’s original budget by 15 %. Which of the following mechanisms would formalize that change?

A. A joint declaration
B. A Council decree without Commission input
C. A revised Commission proposal approved by the Council ✅
D. A parliamentary amendment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Budgetary adjustments require the Commission to submit a revised proposal, which the Council then approves, ensuring that both institutions participate in the financial decision‑making process.

Q18. A member state wishes to accelerate the adoption of a new environmental regulation. Which institutional pathway offers the fastest implementation?

A. Direct approval by the European Parliament
B. Council of Ministers voting after a Commission proposal ✅
C. Commission unilateral adoption without Council consent
D. Referendum in each member state

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Commission drafts the regulation, but the Council’s vote is required for adoption; once the Council approves, the rule can be enacted swiftly, provided there are no procedural delays.

Q19. If the Commission’s decision is contested for violating treaty provisions, which article of the Treaty of Rome would be invoked?

A. Article 12 on customs duties
B. Article 53 on competition policy
C. Article 113 on legal supremacy ✅
D. Article 87 on the free movement of persons

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Article 113 establishes the primacy of Community law over national legislation, allowing the Court of Justice to annul Commission acts that contravene treaty obligations.

Q20. The Commission drafts a regulation on agricultural subsidies, but the Council argues it exceeds the Community’s competence. Which legal test determines competence?

A. The test of legislative intent
B. The test of economic necessity
C. The test of treaty‑based competence ✅
D. The test of political feasibility

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Competence is assessed by examining whether the subject matter falls within powers expressly granted by the treaties; if the regulation exceeds those limits, it can be struck down for lack of legal basis.

B. Allowing the Council to draft legislation independently
C. Mandating direct elections for Commission commissioners
D. Requiring a joint Commission‑Council committee to set the agenda ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Creating a joint committee would distribute agenda‑setting power, fostering greater collaboration and transparency, thereby enhancing democratic oversight while preserving the Commission’s expertise.

Q22. Assume the Commission proposes a rule that increases the average price of a product by 5%5\%5%. The Council requests an elasticity analysis. Which economic concept must the Commission calculate?

A. Price elasticity of demand ✅
B. Cross‑price elasticity of supply
C. Income elasticity of demand
D. Arc elasticity of import demand

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Price elasticity of demand measures how quantity demanded responds to price changes; calculating it helps the Council assess market impact of the proposed price increase and determine whether the rule is proportionate.

Q23. In a dispute over the interpretation of a regulation, the Commission, the Council, and the Parliament each claim authority. Which institution ultimately resolves the interpretive conflict?

A. European Parliament
B. Council of Ministers
C. European Commission
D. European Court of Justice ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Court of Justice has the exclusive competence to interpret Community law, providing authoritative rulings that bind all institutions and member states, thus settling disputes over regulatory meaning.

Q24. If a Commission decision is found to violate the principle of proportionality, what is the likely remedy by the Court of Justice?

A. Automatic amendment of the decision
B. Partial or total annulment of the decision ✅
C. Imposition of a fine on the Commission
D. Transfer of authority to the Council

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Court typically annuls the offending provision, either wholly or partially, requiring the Commission to revise the measure to conform with proportionality requirements.

Q25. A The Council approves a regulation, but a member state refuses to implement it, citing national law conflicts. Which legal doctrine determines the outcome?

A. Doctrine of subsidiarity
B. Doctrine of mutual recognition
C. Doctrine of supremacy of Community law ✅
D. Doctrine of diplomatic immunity

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The doctrine of supremacy asserts that Community law overrides conflicting national legislation, obligating the member state to comply or face infringement proceedings.

A. Lack of expertise in the Commission staff
B. Divergent national economic priorities ✅
C. Overwhelming support from the Parliament

📖 Explanation:

When Council members represent distinct national interests, differing priorities can prolong negotiations, especially on economically sensitive issues, leading to delays in finalizing Commission proposals.

Q27. If the European Parliament’s recommendation aligns with a Commission proposal, but the Council rejects it, what does this illustrate about institutional power?

A. Parliament’s dominance over the Commission
B. Commission’s inability to act without Parliament
C. Council’s veto power over legislation ✅
D. Equal sharing of decision‑making authority

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Council’s capacity to reject proposals, even when both the Commission and Parliament support them, demonstrates its pivotal role as the final legislative gatekeeper within the Community’s institutional framework.

Q28. A member state argues that a Commission‑imposed rule exceeds the Community’s budgetary limits defined in the treaty. Which article would be examined to assess compliance?

A. Article 87 on free movement of goods
B. Article 106 on the budgetary framework ✅
C. Article 113 on legal supremacy
D. Article 38 on customs duties

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Article 106 outlines the Community’s financial provisions, setting limits on expenditures; any rule that would breach these constraints can be challenged for violating the treaty’s budgetary provisions.

Q29. During a policy review, the Commission proposes a rule that would harmonize tax rates across members, but the Council fears loss of fiscal sovereignty. Which principle could the Council invoke to resist the rule?

A. Principle of proportionality
B. Principle of subsidiarity ✅
C. Principle of mutual recognition
D. Principle of free competition

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The subsidiarity principle reserves decision‑making to the lowest appropriate level, allowing member states to retain control over areas like taxation unless harmonization is necessary for Community objectives.

Q30. If the Commission’s decision is challenged on the grounds that it lacks a clear legal basis, which test will the Court apply?

A. The test of procedural fairness
B. The test of legal certainty
C. The test of treaty‑based competence ✅
D. The test of proportionality

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Court examines whether the decision is grounded in a specific treaty article granting the Commission authority, ensuring that actions are legally justified and not arbitrary.

Q31. A The Council approves a regulation, but the European Parliament issues a strong dissenting opinion. Which outcome is most likely?

A. The regulation is automatically revised
B. The Parliament’s dissent triggers a mandatory review by the Commission
C. The regulation proceeds unless the Council decides otherwise ✅
D. The dissent forces a joint Committee to renegotiate

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Parliamentary opinions, while politically significant, do not have veto power; the regulation will continue unless the Council chooses to amend or withdraw it, reflecting the limited legislative influence of the Parliament at that time.
