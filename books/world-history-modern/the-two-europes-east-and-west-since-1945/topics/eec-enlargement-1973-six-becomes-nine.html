What is EEC enlargement 1973 six becomes nine?

The first EEC enlargement in 1973 expanded membership from six founding states to nine by admitting Britain, Ireland, and Denmark, demonstrating the Community’s growing appeal while introducing new challenges regarding budget contributions, agricultural policy adjustments, and institutional adaptation.

Q1. Which three countries formed the Benelux Customs Union after World War II?

A.Belgium, Netherlands, Luxembourg ✅
B.France, Italy, West Germany
C.Spain, Portugal, Greece
D.Norway, Sweden, Denmark
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Benelux Customs Union was created by Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg. These three neighboring states cooperated to remove tariffs and promote trade after the war, laying groundwork for later European integration. Their joint effort demonstrated the benefits of customs cooperation and inspired broader regional projects.

Q2. In which year was the Benelux Customs Union put into operation?

A.\(1945\)
B.\(1947\) ✅
C.\(1950\)
D.\(1952\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The union became operational in \(1947\), after the three governments negotiated the removal of customs duties. This early post‑war step helped revive trade among the members and set a precedent for the removal of trade barriers in later European initiatives.

Q3. Who was the driving force behind the creation of the Benelux Customs Union?

A.Robert Schuman
B.Paul‑Henri Spaak ✅
C.Konrad Adenauer
D.Winston Churchill
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Paul‑Henri Spaak, a Belgian socialist and prime minister from \(1947\) to \(1949\), championed the idea of a customs union among the three nations. His vision for economic cooperation without tariffs was instrumental in turning the Benelux plan into a functioning reality.

Q4. Which treaty added Britain and France to the Benelux collaboration in 1948?

D.Treaty of Maastricht

📖 Explanation: The Treaty of Brussels, signed in \(1948\), extended the Benelux partnership to include Britain and France, pledging military, economic, social and cultural cooperation. This broader alliance paved the way for later supranational institutions such as NATO and the European Coal and Steel Community.
