📖 From World History: Modern • 10. The Two Europes: East and West since 1945 • 33 questions available

What is European Community evolution to EU?

The European Community began as the European Coal and Steel Community in 1951 and expanded into the European Economic Community in 1957, gradually deepening integration through successive treaties until the Maastricht Treaty in 1993 formally transformed it into the European Union with broader political and monetary ambitions.

Q1. In which year was the Benelux Customs Union put into operation?

A. 1945
B. 1946
C. 1947 ✅
D. 1948

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Benelux Customs Union became operational in 1947, following the planning undertaken by the governments-in-exile during World War II. This date marks the first concrete step toward free trade among Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg, eliminating tariffs and customs barriers.

Q2. Who was the principal architect behind the creation of the Benelux Customs Union?

A. Paul‑Henri Spaak ✅
B. Robert Schuman
C. Jean Monnet
D. Konrad Adenauer

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Paul‑Henri Spaak, a Belgian socialist leader who later served as prime minister, championed the Benelux Customs Union. His vision for economic cooperation among the three neighbouring countries drove the agreement that was implemented in 1947.

Q3. The Treaty of Brussels (1948) extended cooperation to which of the following areas?

A. Military, economic, social and cultural collaboration ✅
B. Only military cooperation
C. Only economic cooperation
D. Only cultural collaboration

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The 1948 Treaty of Brussels was a broad agreement that pledged military, economic, social and cultural collaboration among Britain, France, and the three Benelux states. This comprehensive scope laid the groundwork for later institutions such as NATO and the ECSC.

Q4. Which two future institutions can be directly traced to the military and economic clauses of the Treaty of Brussels?

A. NATO and the EEC
B. NATO and the ECSC ✅
C. EUROSTAT and the EEC
D. European Parliament and NATO

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The military clause of the Treaty of Brussels evolved into the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO), while the economic clause paved the way for the European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC), the first step toward deeper European economic integration.

Q5. How many countries originally joined the European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC) in 1951?

📖 Explanation:

The ECSC was founded by six founding members: France, West Germany, Italy, Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg. These nations agreed to remove duties on coal and steel, creating a common market for these strategic materials.

Q6. Which French minister is credited as the brain‑child of the ECSC?

Robert Schuman, serving as France’s Foreign Minister from 1948 to 1953, proposed the idea of a supranational coal and steel community. His plan aimed to bind France and West Germany economically, thereby reducing the risk of future conflict.

The ECSC’s core purpose was to eliminate duties and restrictions on the trade of coal, iron, and steel among the six founding members. By doing so, it sought to boost efficiency, foster cooperation, and lay the foundation for a broader European market.

British reluctance stemmed from concerns about ceding control of its steel industry. Had Britain joined, the High Authority would have needed to reconcile differing national policies, likely complicating governance and potentially delaying the swift reforms that characterized the early ECSC years.

Q14. A non‑member country wishes to trade with the EEC in 1960 and faces tariffs. If the average tariff rate applied was 12 %, what would be the effective tariff on a shipment worth €100 000?

A. €10 000
B. €12 000 ✅
C. €14 000
D. €16 000

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The effective tariff equals the shipment value multiplied by the tariff rate: \(100{,}000 \times 0.12 = 12{,}000\). Therefore, the non‑member would pay €12 000 in duties, illustrating how the EEC protected its market while still allowing some external trade.

Q18. If the Benelux Customs Union removed all tariffs between its members, what economic effect would you expect on intra‑Benelux trade volume, assuming price elasticity of demand is \(1.5\)?

A. Trade volume would decrease
B. Trade volume would remain unchanged
C. Trade volume would increase by more than 50 % ✅
D. Trade volume would increase by exactly 50 %

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

With a price elasticity of \(1.5\), a 100 % reduction in tariffs (effectively a price drop) would lead to a trade‑volume increase of \(1.5 \times 100\% = 150\%\). Hence, intra‑Benelux trade would rise by more than 50 %, illustrating the strong responsiveness to lower costs.

Q20. In 1952, a steel company in West Germany exported 200 000 tons of steel to Belgium under the ECSC. If production rose by 50 % over the next five years, how many tons would it export by 1957, assuming the same market share?

A. 300 000 tons ✅
B. 350 000 tons
C. 400 000 tons
D. 450 000 tons

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A 50 % increase on 200 000 tons yields \(200{,}000 \times 1.5 = 300{,}000\) tons. However, the question asks for the export amount assuming the same share of the total increase, which would be \(200{,}000 + 200{,}000 \times 0.5 = 300{,}000\) tons. The correct answer is 300 000 tons, but the option list includes 300 000 tons as A, so the correct answer is A. (Note: the answer key reflects the correct option.)

Q33. If the EEC’s common market led to a 12 % increase in intra‑community trade in its first year, and the baseline trade volume was €80 billion, what was the new trade volume?

A. €84 billion
B. €88 billion
C. €89.6 billion ✅
D. €92 billion

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The increase equals \(80{,}000{,}000{,}000 \times 0.12 = 9{,}600{,}000{,}000\). Adding this to the original €80 billion yields €\(80{,}000{,}000{,}000 + 9{,}600{,}000{,}000 = 89{,}600{,}000{,}000\), or €89.6 billion, which matches option C.
