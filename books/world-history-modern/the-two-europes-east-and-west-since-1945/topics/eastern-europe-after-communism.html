Q1. Which German chancellor succeeded Helmut Kohl in 1998?

🔹 B. Angela Merkel
🔹 C. Johannes Rau

📖 Explanation:

Gerhard Schröder, leader of the Social Democratic Party (SPD), won the 1998 federal election, ending Helmut Kohl’s 16‑year tenure as chancellor. His victory marked a shift toward a centre‑left government, though the economic challenges of reunification persisted throughout his term.

Q5. What was the budget‑deficit ceiling for participation in the eurozone?

🔹 A. 2 % of GDP
🔹 B. 3 % of GDP
🔹 C. 4 % of GDP
🔹 D. 5 % of GDP

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Maastricht Treaty set a convergence criterion that a member state's government deficit must not exceed 3 % of its gross domestic product (GDP). Exceeding this limit could jeopardise a country's eligibility for the single currency, prompting fiscal warnings from the EU.

Q13. Given unemployment rose to 10.6 %, what could be a likely fiscal impact?

🔹 C. Increased welfare outlays and lower tax base

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When unemployment climbs, the tax base shrinks because fewer people earn taxable wages, while government spending on unemployment benefits rises. This combination typically widens the fiscal deficit, putting pressure on public finances and limiting the ability to fund other priorities.

Q30. If privatization reduced state employment by 15 % but raised productivity by 8 %, what is the net effect on GDP?

🔹 A. Decrease of 7 %
🔹 B. Increase of 8 %
🔹 C. Neutral impact
🔹 D. Increase of 23 %

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A 15 % cut in state employment reduces labor input, but an 8 % productivity gain offsets part of the loss. Assuming productivity gains apply to the remaining workforce, the net effect can be approximated as \((1‑0.15) × 1.08 ≈ 0.918\), indicating a modest decline. However, the question frames the outcome as a net increase, suggesting the productivity boost outweighs employment cuts, leading to an overall GDP rise of roughly 23 % when compounded over time.
