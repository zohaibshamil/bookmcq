Q1. By what year was a completely free and common market intended to be achieved?

🔹 A. 1990
🔹 B. 1992
🔹 C. 1995
🔹 D. 2000

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The 1986 agreements set a target to eliminate all internal barriers by the end of the decade, aiming for a fully integrated market by \(1992\). This date was chosen to give member states sufficient time to adjust national regulations and to coordinate the removal of tariffs, quotas, and other restrictions, ensuring a smooth transition to a single market.

Q2. Which of the following was NOT a change introduced in 1986?

🔹 A. Majority voting in the Council
🔹 B. Increased EC control over health and safety
🔹 C. Introduction of the Euro currency
🔹 D. More powers for the European Parliament

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Euro currency was not part of the 1986 reform package; it was introduced later under the Maastricht Treaty. The 1986 changes focused on deepening economic integration, expanding policy areas such as health, safety, and consumer protection, and strengthening parliamentary procedures, but monetary union came after the 1990s.

Q3. Which two member states expressed strong concerns about national sovereignty?

🔹 A. Britain and Denmark
🔹 B. France and Italy
🔹 C. Spain and Portugal
🔹 D. Germany and Greece

📖 Explanation:

British Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher and Danish leaders were vocal opponents of measures they perceived as eroding national sovereignty. Their objections centered on the fear that increased EC powers, particularly in legislative and regulatory domains, would limit the ability of national parliaments to control domestic affairs.

Q4. Which of the following describes the intended status of internal trade after 1992?

🔹 A. Fully regulated

🔹 D. Prohibited

📖 Explanation:

The 1986 reforms aimed to create a completely free internal market by \(1992\), removing all customs duties, quantitative restrictions, and other barriers. This ambition was central to the vision of a single European market, allowing goods, services, capital, and people to move unhindered across member‑state borders.

Q6. The move to a free and common market by 1992 implied removal of which type of barriers?

The 1986 package called for the elimination of both tariffs and quantitative restrictions (quotas) to achieve a seamless internal market. By removing these trade barriers, the EC intended to promote competition, lower consumer prices, and stimulate cross‑border investment among the twelve member states.

Q7. If the EC introduced majority voting in the Council of Ministers, how would the probability of a single state vetoing a measure change, assuming all states have equal voting weight?

🔹 A. It would increase
🔹 B. It would decrease
🔹 C. It would stay the same
🔹 D. Cannot be determined

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Under a unanimity rule, any one state could block a proposal, giving each a 100 % veto power. Switching to majority voting reduces that power because a measure can pass as long as a sufficient number of states agree, meaning the chance that a single state can stop the measure falls dramatically.

Q8. Suppose a backward region receives an EC fund that raises its GDP by 5 % annually, while a non‑backward region grows 2 % annually. Over 3 years, what is the cumulative growth difference?

🔹 A. ≈9.6 %
🔹 B. ≈7.2 %
🔹 C. ≈5.0 %
🔹 D. ≈12.0 %

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The cumulative growth for the backward region is \((1+0.05)^3=1.1576\) (≈15.8 % increase). For the non‑backward region it is \((1+0.02)^3=1.0612\) (≈6.1 % increase). The difference is about \(1.1576-1.0612=0.0964\), i.e., roughly a 9.6 % higher growth over the three‑year period.

Q10. A member state fears loss of sovereignty. If the EC passes a regulation that overrides national law, which principle justifies this action?

The principle of EU supremacy holds that Community law takes precedence over conflicting national legislation. This doctrine ensures uniform application of EU rules across all member states, even when a national parliament objects, thereby safeguarding the integrity of the single market and other collective objectives.

Q13. If majority voting requires a 55 % majority of member states representing at least 65 % of the EU population, how many of the 12 members must vote in favour, assuming each state has equal population?

Q16. Assume the EC allocates research funding proportionally to each member state's GDP. If Country X has 10 % of total EU GDP and receives €2 billion, what is the total EC research budget?

📖 Explanation:

If €2 billion represents 10 % of the total budget, the full amount is calculated as \(\frac{€2\text{ billion}}{0.10}=€20\text{ billion}\). This proportional allocation method ensures that each country's contribution to the research programme reflects its economic weight within the Union.

Q22. Which voting system was introduced in the Council of Ministers by the 1986 changes?

🔹 B. Qualified majority
🔹 C. Simple majority
🔹 D. Weighted voting

📖 Explanation:

The 1986 reforms replaced the unanimity requirement with qualified majority voting (QMV) for many issues, allowing decisions to be adopted when a sufficient number of states and a defined share of the EU population support the proposal, thereby reducing the chance of a single veto.

Q23. If a member state opposes a measure under majority voting, can it still block the measure?

🔹 C. Only with a veto

Under qualified majority voting, a single member state cannot unilaterally block a proposal. The measure passes once the required number of supporting states and the population threshold are met, meaning opposition by one state does not constitute a veto.
