About 17. The USSR and Stalin 1924 to 1953

Under Joseph Stalin, the Soviet Union transformed from a backward agrarian society into an industrial and military superpower through forced collectivization, five-year plans, and brutal political purges. This rapid modernization came at enormous human cost, including famines, mass executions, and the Gulag system, as Stalin eliminated rivals and enforced absolute obedience to build “socialism in one country.”

Stalin’s leadership also defined the USSR’s role in WWII and the early Cold War, securing victory over Nazism but imposing communist regimes across Eastern Europe. His death in 1953 ended the most repressive phase of Soviet history, leaving a legacy of both superpower status and profound trauma that continued to shape the USSR until its collapse.
