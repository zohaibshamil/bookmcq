What is Lenin Bolshevik consolidation of power 1917?

After seizing power in October 1917, Lenin consolidated Bolshevik control by dissolving the Constituent Assembly, banning opposition parties, creating the Cheka secret police, and using propaganda to present Bolshevik rule as the only path to peace and socialism.

📝 All Lenin Bolshevik consolidation of power 1917 MCQs

Q1. When did the Bolsheviks seize control of Petrograd during the October Revolution?

A.Oct 25, 1917 ✅
B.Oct 26, 1917
C.Oct 27, 1917
D.Oct 28, 1917
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Bolshevik uprising began on Oct 25 (Julian calendar), which corresponds to Nov 7, 1917 Gregorian. This decisive day marked the seizure of key institutions in Petrograd, leading to the collapse of the Provisional Government and establishing Bolshevik authority.

Q2. Which political party initially enjoyed the greatest support among Russian peasants before the Bolsheviks consolidated power?

A.Bolsheviks
B.Mensheviks
C.Social Revolutionaries ✅
D.Kadets
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Social Revolutionaries (SRs) championed agrarian reform and presented themselves as the peasants' champions. Their platform resonated with rural voters, who viewed the SRs as the most reliable advocates for land redistribution, making the SRs the preferred choice over the industrial‑focused Bolsheviks.

Q3. Which treaty did the Bolshevik government sign to withdraw Russia from World War I?

A.Treaty of Brest‑Litovsk ✅
B.Treaty of Versailles
C.Treaty of Trianon
D.Treaty of Berlin
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Treaty of Brest‑Litovsk, signed on Mar 3, 1918, formally ended Russia’s participation in the war. Although the terms were harsh, the Bolsheviks prioritized peace to address internal crises, fulfilling their promise of “peace” and allowing them to focus on consolidating domestic power.

Q4. Why did the Bolshevik takeover of Moscow take longer than that of Petrograd?

A.Moscow had a larger Red Guard presence
B.The city’s garrison was initially loyal to the Provisional Government ✅
C.Petrograd’s workers were less organized
D.Moscow’s infrastructure hindered communication
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Moscow’s regular army units and many officials remained loyal to the Provisional Government, creating fierce resistance. Unlike Petrograd, where workers and soldiers quickly aligned with the Bolsheviks, Moscow required a week of street fighting before the Soviets could assert control, illustrating the uneven nature of the revolution.

Q5. Which policy contradiction most directly fueled opposition to the Bolsheviks among peasants?

A.Prioritizing industrial workers over land redistribution ✅
B.Promising immediate peace but delaying negotiations
C.Implementing War Communism while pledging land reform
D.Nationalizing all factories without compensation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Bolsheviks emphasized the proletariat and industrial development, often seeming to neglect the peasants’ immediate demand for land. This mismatch between rhetoric (“land to the peasants”) and practice (focus on factories) heightened rural discontent and encouraged support for rival parties such as the Social Revolutionaries.

Q6. Which later economic measure was introduced to relax the strict controls of War Communism?

A.New Economic Policy (NEP)
B.Five‑Year Plan ✅
C.Collectivization
D.State Monopoly of Trade
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The New Economic Policy, launched in 1921, re‑introduced limited market mechanisms, allowing small‑scale private trade and agriculture. By easing the harsh requisitioning of War Communism, the NEP aimed to revive production and placate peasants, demonstrating a strategic shift from pure socialist control toward a mixed economy.

Q7. If a provincial peasant council demanded immediate land redistribution but the Bolshevik central committee delayed implementation by \(\) months to focus on the war, how would this likely affect the council’s loyalty?

A.The council would become more supportive of the Bolsheviks
B.The council would demand higher taxes to fund the war
C.The council would seek alliance with the Social Revolutionaries
D.The council would abandon political participation altogether ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Delaying land reform undermines the Bolsheviks’ core promise to peasants, eroding trust. Faced with unmet expectations, the council would likely become disillusioned, reducing political engagement and possibly turning to alternative movements, thereby weakening Bolshevik authority in the countryside.

Q8. Evaluate the claim that the Bolsheviks’ consolidation of power was mainly due to superior military organization. Which piece of evidence most challenges this assertion?

A.The Red Guard’s rapid mobilization in Petrograd
B.The prolonged armed resistance in Moscow and other cities
C.The Bolsheviks’ control of the telegraph network ✅
D.The presence of foreign troops supporting the Bolsheviks
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: While the Bolsheviks did possess organized militias, their dominance also stemmed from political maneuvers, propaganda, and opportunistic alliances. Control of communications, especially the telegraph, allowed them to coordinate actions and disseminate decrees, indicating that organizational advantages extended beyond pure military capability.

Q9. Calculate the interval between the October Revolution (Nov 7, \(1917\)) and the signing of the Treaty of Brest‑Litovsk (Mar 3, \(1918\)). How many months passed?

A.3 months
B.4 months ✅
C.5 months
D.6 months
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: From Nov 7, \(1917\) to Mar 3, \(1918\) spans just under four calendar months (November → December, December → January, January → February, February → early March). Therefore, the interval is approximately four months, reflecting the rapid transition from revolutionary upheaval to formal peace negotiations.

Q10. Why was the slogan “peace, land, and bread” insufficient to secure lasting support among countryside peasants?

A.It ignored the need for industrialization
B.It failed to address the peasants’ desire for political representation
C.It did not promise immediate tax relief
D.It overlooked the peasants’ demand for direct land ownership and autonomy ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Peasants sought concrete, immediate control over the land they worked. While “peace, land, and bread” promised broad reforms, the Bolsheviks’ early policies often delayed actual redistribution and imposed requisitions, betraying the promise of autonomy and prompting many to question the regime’s commitment to their core interests.
