📝 All Nicholas II attempts to stabilize Russia 1905 MCQs

Q1. Why did Nicholas II initially survive the revolution of \(1905\)?

🔹 A. His opponents were united under a single leader
🔹 B. He had no opposition in the army
🔹 C. His opponents were not united and there was no central leadership
🔹 D. He promised immediate democratic reforms

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The 1905 uprising was fragmented; lacking a cohesive leadership and with most of the army still loyal, the Tsar could rely on force. By issuing the October Manifesto he bought time, offering concessions that appeased moderate liberals while the revolutionary momentum waned.

Q2. Which of the following concessions was NOT included in the October Manifesto?

🔹 A. Universal suffrage for Duma elections
🔹 B. Freedom of speech and assembly
🔹 C. Cancellation of redemption payments
🔹 D. Basic civil liberties

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Manifesto granted civil liberties and introduced an elected Duma with universal suffrage, but it did not address the peasants' redemption payments. Those payments remained a source of peasant discontent, as the government continued to demand annual sums despite the abolition of serfdom.

Q3. What was the primary reason the First Duma was dissolved after ten weeks?

🔹 A. It passed a law outlawing the monarchy
🔹 B. It demanded confiscation of large estates and control over ministerial appointments
🔹 C. It supported the Tsar’s war policies
🔹 D. It refused to convene any sessions

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The First Duma, elected in \(1906\), pushed for radical reforms such as land confiscation and the right to approve ministers, threatening Nicholas’s authority. Unable to tolerate this challenge, he ordered the army to disperse the Duma, ending its brief existence.

Q4. How did Nicholas change the electoral system after the Second Duma?

🔹 A. He introduced proportional representation
🔹 B. He expanded voting rights to women
🔹 C. He deprived peasants and urban workers of the vote
🔹 D. He allowed only nobles to vote

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Following the dissolution of the Second Duma in \(1907\), Nicholas altered the voting law to favor the landed and middle classes, effectively stripping peasants and industrial workers of their voting power. This manipulation ensured more compliant, conservative Dumas in the future.

Q5. Which Duma is known for being the most conservative and longest‑lasting?

🔹 A. First Duma
🔹 B. Second Duma
🔹 C. Third Duma
🔹 D. Fourth Duma

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Third Duma, elected after the 1907 electoral reform, was dominated by nobles and bureaucrats who supported the autocracy. Its conservative composition allowed it to operate until \(1912\), making it the longest‑lasting of the early Russian parliaments.

Q6. What key demand of moderate liberal reformers was partially satisfied by the October Manifesto?

🔹 A. Abolition of the death penalty
🔹 B. Right to strike
🔹 C. Creation of an elected parliament (Duma)
🔹 D. Land redistribution

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Manifesto introduced the Duma, providing a legislative body elected by a broad electorate, thereby meeting the liberals’ primary demand for a constitutional element within the autocratic system.

Q7. Which of the following best describes Nicholas II’s attitude toward the October Manifesto?

🔹 A. He saw it as a permanent solution
🔹 B. He embraced it enthusiastically for reform
🔹 C. He accepted it only because he had no choice
🔹 D. He intended to expand its promises further

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Historical evidence suggests Nicholas signed the Manifesto under pressure, lacking genuine commitment to its reforms. He later ignored many of its provisions, indicating his acceptance was tactical rather than ideological.

Q8. Why were redemption payments a source of peasant unrest after 1861?

🔹 A. They were voluntary contributions to the church
🔹 B. They were compulsory annual payments for land ownership
🔹 C. They funded industrial expansion in cities
🔹 D. They were taxes on imported goods

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Redemption payments required peasants to pay the state for the land they received after serf emancipation. These ongoing financial obligations kept many rural families in poverty, fueling resentment toward the regime and contributing to revolutionary sentiment.

Q9. A peasant delegation petitions the Tsar to cancel redemption payments. Which response aligns with Nicholas’s actual policy after the Manifesto?

🔹 A. Immediately cancel all payments
🔹 B. Offer a temporary reduction
🔹 C. Maintain the payments unchanged
🔹 D. Introduce a new tax on urban workers

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Nicholas largely maintained the redemption system, viewing it as a fiscal necessity. Although the Manifesto hinted at reforms, the Tsar’s government did not abolish these payments, demonstrating his reluctance to alter revenue streams despite peasant grievances.

Q10. How did the composition of the First Duma undermine its ability to enact reforms?

🔹 A. It consisted mostly of aristocrats supportive of the Tsar
🔹 B. It was dominated by industrial workers demanding radical change
🔹 C. It was heavily weighted toward landowners and the middle class due to a rigged electoral system
🔹 D. It excluded all rural representatives

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The electoral law favored property owners, skewing representation toward landowners and the bourgeoisie. Consequently, while the Duma could voice reformist ideas, its limited popular base weakened its legitimacy and made it vulnerable to suppression by the autocracy.

Q11. After the Second Duma’s dissolution, a foreign journalist asks why the Tsar altered voting rules. Which explanation best reflects the government's motive?

🔹 A. To broaden democratic participation

🔹 C. To ensure a more compliant, conservative legislature
🔹 D. To transition toward a constitutional monarchy

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The 1907 electoral reform was designed to reduce the influence of peasants and workers, thereby creating a Duma that would support the Tsar’s policies. This manipulation aimed at stability through conservatism, not democratization.

Q12. Which of the following was NOT a demand of the First Duma?

📖 Explanation:

While the First Duma pursued extensive reforms, it did not champion women's suffrage. Its agenda focused on land redistribution, ministerial control, and civil liberties, leaving gender voting rights outside its primary concerns.

📖 Explanation:

Even though the later Dumas existed, their members were selected from conservative circles that tended to cooperate with the autocracy. Combined with the Tsar’s direct control over the executive branch and security apparatus, the Dumas could offer little real opposition.

Q14. A historian argues that the October Manifesto created a “breathing space” for reform. Which evidence supports this claim?

🔹 C. The continuation of redemption payments
🔹 D. The increase in military censorship

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Manifesto’s promises of an elected Duma, civil freedoms, and universal suffrage temporarily opened political space, allowing moderate reformers to engage with the regime. This period of relative openness is what scholars refer to as a “breathing space,” despite later backsliding.

Q15. Which event directly led to the issuance of the October Manifesto?

🔹 A. The assassination of Alexander II
🔹 B. The defeat in the Russo‑Japanese War
🔹 C. The Bloody Sunday massacre of \(1905\)
🔹 D. The coronation of Nicholas II

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The massacre on “Bloody Sunday” in \(1905\), where peaceful protesters were shot by imperial troops, ignited mass unrest across the empire. Facing widespread strikes and uprisings, Nicholas issued the Manifesto to quell dissent and prevent revolution.

Q16. How did the lack of a central revolutionary leadership affect the Tsar’s ability to negotiate?

🔹 C. It made negotiations impossible
🔹 D. It led to immediate armed conflict

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Without a unified opposition, Nicholas could selectively address moderate demands while isolating more radical elements. This divide‑and‑rule approach enabled him to issue limited concessions without threatening the core of autocratic power.

Q17. If the First Duma had been genuinely democratic, which outcome would have been most likely?

🔹 A. Immediate dissolution by the army
🔹 B. Implementation of land reform and ministerial oversight
🔹 C. Continuation of autocratic rule unchanged
🔹 D. A civil war between peasants and nobles

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A truly representative Duma would have possessed the legitimacy to push for substantive reforms like land redistribution and control over the cabinet, challenging the Tsar’s absolute authority and potentially reshaping Russian governance.

Q18. What was the main purpose of the October Manifesto’s clause that “no law could begin to operate without the approval of the Duma”?

🔹 A. To give the Duma full legislative authority
🔹 B. To symbolically limit the Tsar’s power while preserving his control
🔹 C. To empower the secret police
🔹 D. To abolish the monarchy

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The clause suggested a check on imperial power, but in practice it was a symbolic concession. The Tsar retained the ability to bypass the Duma, using emergency powers and dissolving the body when it became inconvenient, thereby preserving autocratic dominance.

Q19. Why did foreign observers express surprise at Nicholas’s ability to dismiss the first two Dumas without sparking another general strike?

📖 Explanation:

The Tsar’s selective adherence to the Manifesto, combined with a strong secret police and limited popular support for the Dumas, allowed him to curb parliamentary opposition without provoking mass unrest, contrary to expectations that such dismissals would reignite revolutionary fervor.

Q20. Which of the following best explains why the revolutionary impetus subsided after the early 1900s?

📖 Explanation:

Repressive measures, including the dissolution of Dumas, censorship, and police action, dampened overt revolutionary activity. While many grievances remained, the state's coercive capacity limited organized resistance, leading to a temporary decline in overt revolutionary momentum.

Q21. A political scientist compares the Russian Duma to the British Parliament of the same era. Which key difference would they highlight?

🔹 D. Both were elected by universal suffrage

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The British Parliament operated under constitutional monarchy with parliamentary sovereignty, whereas the Russian Duma, despite its elected status, remained subject to the Tsar’s authority, who could dissolve it and disregard its decisions, reflecting a fundamental power imbalance.

Q22. How did the continuation of redemption payments influence Nicholas’s decision to limit electoral reforms?

📖 Explanation:

Redemption payments provided a steady income for the treasury. Expanding the electorate to include more peasants threatened this revenue by empowering a class that might demand abolition. Thus, Nicholas preserved the fiscal status quo by restricting voting rights, aligning economic interests with political control.

Q23. Which statement accurately reflects Nicholas’s use of the October Manifesto as a political tool?

🔹 C. He believed it would eliminate all opposition
🔹 D. He planned to expand its promises over time

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Manifesto was a tactical response to the 1905 crisis, offering limited reforms to pacify moderate opposition. Nicholas never intended to fully implement its provisions, using it primarily as a stopgap measure to buy time and maintain autocratic rule.

Q24. Imagine the Tsar had fully implemented the Manifesto’s promises. Which long‑term outcome is most plausible?

🔹 B. The immediate collapse of the Russian Empire
🔹 C. A resurgence of peasant revolts due to economic hardship
🔹 D. Continued autocratic rule with minor reforms

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Full implementation would have established a representative Duma with real legislative power, curtailed autocratic authority, and addressed civil liberties, setting Russia on a path toward constitutional monarchy, similar to other European powers that successfully limited monarchical power.

Q26. Which factor most directly enabled Nicholas to avoid another general strike after dissolving the first two Dumas?
