Q1. In which year did Peter Stolypin become prime minister of Russia?

🔹 A. 1906
🔹 B. 1910
🔹 C. 1908
🔹 D. 1912

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Peter Stolypin was appointed prime minister in 1906, a period marked by attempts to stabilize the empire after the 1905 revolution. His tenure lasted until 1911, during which he introduced both repressive measures and limited reforms aimed at consolidating the regime’s authority.

Q2. Approximately how many people were executed under Stolypin’s repressive policies in the three years following his appointment?

🔹 A. \(2000\)
🔹 B. \(4000\)
🔹 C. \(6000\)
🔹 D. \(1000\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The government’s crackdown resulted in roughly \(4000\) executions, reflecting the harsh tactics employed to suppress dissent. This number underscores the regime’s willingness to use extreme force while simultaneously pursuing selective reforms to placate other segments of society.

Q3. By 1916, how many peasants had purchased their own land under Stolypin’s agrarian reforms?

🔹 A. \(1\) million
🔹 B. \(3\) million
🔹 C. \(2\) million
🔹 D. \(5\) million

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The reforms encouraged private land ownership, leading approximately \(2\) million peasants to buy their own plots by 1916. This created a new class of relatively prosperous peasant proprietors, known as kulaks, who the government hoped would act as a bulwark against revolutionary activity.

Q4. Which of the following reforms was intended to foster a class of kulaks who could support the regime?

🔹 A. Introduction of workers’ sickness insurance
🔹 B. Universal education programme
🔹 C. Increase of factory inspections
🔹 D. Abolition of redemption payments and encouragement of land purchase

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

By abolishing redemption payments and promoting private land acquisition, Stolypin aimed to develop a prosperous peasant class. These kulaks were expected to be loyal to the government and help deter revolutionary sentiments, thereby strengthening the regime’s social base.

Q5. Why did the government consider the newly formed kulaks reliable supporters against revolutionary movements?

🔹 A. They were industrial workers with high wages
🔹 B. They owned land and had a vested interest in maintaining order
🔹 C. They were members of revolutionary parties
🔹 D. They were exempt from taxes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Ownership of land gave kulaks a personal stake in preserving the existing social order, as any upheaval threatened their property. Their economic prosperity made them more inclined to back the regime, providing a counterweight to revolutionary forces among the peasantry.

Q6. If workers’ sickness insurance covered 10% of employees and industrial profits rose by 15% annually, what is the most likely effect on worker morale?

🔹 A. Morale improves significantly due to better security and rising wages
🔹 B. Morale remains unchanged because benefits are limited
🔹 C. Morale declines because profit gains do not reach workers
🔹 D. Morale fluctuates unpredictably

📖 Explanation:

The combination of enhanced social protection and rising profitability signals a more favorable work environment. Employees are likely to perceive the regime as responsive, leading to a noticeable boost in morale, even though only a fraction directly benefits from the insurance scheme.
