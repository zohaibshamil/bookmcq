📝 All Was Russian Revolution a coup or mass uprising MCQs

Q1. Which interpretation emphasizes that the Bolshevik takeover was driven by broad popular support from workers, peasants, and soldiers?

🔹 A. Soviet interpretation
🔹 B. Traditional liberal interpretation
🔹 C. Libertarian interpretation
🔹 D. Revisionist interpretation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet interpretation portrays the October Revolution as a mass movement, arguing that the majority of workers, peasants, and soldiers were attracted by Bolshevik promises such as peace, land redistribution, and workers’ control, thereby providing the regime with legitimacy rooted in popular support.

Q2. According to the traditional liberal interpretation, the Bolsheviks were primarily...

🔹 A. A charismatic mass leader
🔹 B. A minority group of professional revolutionaries
🔹 C. A coalition of peasants
🔹 D. A foreign‑backed army

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Traditional liberal historians reject the Soviet view, insisting that the Bolsheviks were a small, professional revolutionary cadre that exploited the chaos of 1917 rather than enjoying widespread popular backing, and that their success derived from organization and ruthlessness rather than mass support.

Q3. Which historian argued that the Bolsheviks hijacked a popular revolution that was already underway?

🔹 A. Adam Ulam
🔹 B. Richard Pipes
🔹 C. Alexander Berkman
🔹 D. Sheila Fitzpatrick

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Alexander Berkman, writing from a libertarian perspective, contended that the October Revolution was a genuine popular uprising and that the Bolsheviks merely appropriated its momentum, pretending to share the masses’ aims while suppressing the independent power of shop and factory committees.

Q4. In how many of the 97 major centres did the Bolsheviks have to use force to assert authority?

🔹 A. 5
🔹 B. 10
🔹 C. 21
🔹 D. 16

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Soviet historians note that only sixteen of the ninety‑seven major centres required direct Bolshevik force, indicating that the majority of towns and cities accepted Bolshevik authority without armed coercion, a fact used to bolster the claim of widespread popular legitimacy.

Q5. Who most recently restated the traditional interpretation that the October Revolution was driven mainly by Lenin’s overwhelming desire for power?

🔹 A. Richard Pipes
🔹 B. Adam Ulam
🔹 C. Alexander Berkman
🔹 D. Sheila Fitzpatrick

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Richard Pipes, a leading traditional liberal scholar, has argued that the October Revolution was essentially the product of Lenin’s personal ambition for power, portraying the Bolsheviks as a small, determined group that seized key points in Petrograd to install themselves as the new government.

Q6. Revisionist interpretations primarily concentrate on which aspect of the October Revolution?

🔹 A. International diplomacy
🔹 B. Political awareness among ordinary people
🔹 C. Economic policies of the Bolsheviks
🔹 D. Military strategies

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Revisionist scholars focus on the heightened political consciousness of ordinary Russians, emphasizing how trade‑union activity, soviet participation, and local agitation reflected a broad base of engagement that shaped the revolutionary process, rather than attributing change solely to elite maneuvering.

Q7. Which group did the Bolsheviks promise land redistribution to?

🔹 A. Industrialists
🔹 B. Urban merchants
🔹 C. Peasants
🔹 D. Foreign investors

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Bolshevik platform pledged to transfer land from large estates to the peasantry, a promise that resonated deeply with Russia’s vast rural population and helped secure mass support for the October Revolution among those yearning for agrarian reform.

Q8. After the revolution, the Bolsheviks became widely known as what?

🔹 A. Social Democrats
🔹 B. Anarchists
🔹 C. Marxists‑Leninists
🔹 D. Communists

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Following their seizure of power, the Bolsheviks were commonly referred to as Communists, a label that reflected both their ideological commitment to a classless society and the way the new regime presented itself to domestic and foreign audiences.

Q9. What was a key promise made to soldiers and sailors by the Bolsheviks?

🔹 A. Peace
🔹 B. Increased wages
🔹 C. Land ownership
🔹 D. Political autonomy

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

One of the Bolsheviks’ most compelling promises to war‑weary soldiers and sailors was an immediate end to Russia’s involvement in World War I, a pledge that appealed to exhausted troops and contributed significantly to their willingness to back the October uprising.

Q10. In a city where 80 % of workers supported soviets but only 20 % were Bolshevik members, which interpretation best explains the Bolshevik takeover?

🔹 A. Traditional liberal
🔹 B. Soviet
🔹 C. Libertarian
🔹 D. Revisionist

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet interpretation would argue that the overwhelming worker support for soviets indicates a genuine mass movement, allowing a relatively small Bolshevik minority to assume leadership and implement their program, thereby legitimizing the takeover as an expression of popular will.

Q11. How do Adam Ulam’s and Richard Pipes’s views differ regarding the agency behind the October Revolution?

🔹 A. Both deny any Bolshevik agency
🔹 B. Ulam credits Lenin alone
🔹 C. Ulam says any determined group could have acted while Pipes stresses Lenin’s personal drive
🔹 D. Both emphasize foreign intervention

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Adam Ulam maintains that any organized group could have seized power in the chaotic 1917 environment, whereas Richard Pipes emphasizes Lenin’s personal ambition as the decisive factor, highlighting a nuanced divergence between a structural versus a leader‑centric explanation.

Q12. Which of the following is NOT cited by the Soviet interpretation as a source of legitimacy for the October Revolution?

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet narrative stresses internal legitimacy derived from mass support, land redistribution, and worker control, but it does not invoke external military intervention as a basis for the revolution’s authority, making that option inconsistent with the Soviet viewpoint.

Revisionists cite the extensive involvement of ordinary citizens in trade unions and soviets as concrete evidence that large segments of the population were politically conscious and actively shaping revolutionary outcomes.
