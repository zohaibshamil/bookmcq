What is Red Terror Bolshevik violence 1918-1921?

The Red Terror was a systematic campaign of arrests, executions, and intimidation carried out by the Cheka against perceived enemies of the revolution, including former officials, clergy, and political opponents, to eliminate resistance and enforce Bolshevik authority during the Civil War.

Q1. What was the official name of the Soviet secret police created in December 1917?

A.Extraordinary Commission for Combating Sabotage and Counter-Revolution (Cheka) ✅
B.People's Commissariat for Internal Affairs (NKVD)
C.State Security Committee (KGB)
D.Federal Security Service (FSB)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Cheka, formally the Extraordinary Commission for Combating Sabotage and Counter‑Revolution, was created in December 1917 to detect and eliminate counter‑revolutionary activity. It quickly became the instrument of state terror, laying the foundation for later security organs such as the GPU, NKVD, and KGB.

Q2. Which decree issued by Lenin’s government nationalized all land, including former crown estates and church property, without compensation?

A.Decree on Peace
B.Decree on Land ✅
C.Decree on Nationalization of Industry
D.Decree on Workers' Control
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Decree on Land, issued by Lenin’s government in October 1917, abolished private ownership of all land, including former crown estates and church property, without offering compensation. By redistributing the land to the peasantry, the Bolsheviks hoped to secure peasant support and undermine the old social order.

Q3. Who served as the first head of the Cheka, overseeing its early operations?

A.Leon Trotsky
B.Vladimir Lenin
C.Felix Dzierzynski ✅
D.Joseph Stalin
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Felix Dzierzynski, a former member of the Social‑Democratic Labour Party of Poland, was appointed head of the Cheka. Under his leadership, the organization employed ruthless tactics to suppress counter‑revolutionary activity, setting a precedent for the use of political violence as a tool of state policy.

Q4. How many seats did the Bolsheviks secure in the Constituent Assembly elections of 1917?

A.250
B.400
C.600
D.175 ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: In the November 1917 elections for the Constituent Assembly, the Bolsheviks secured only 175 seats out of roughly 700 total. This modest representation reflected limited popular support and foreshadowed the party’s decision to dissolve the Assembly when it proved hostile to their agenda.

Q5. What was the maximum length of the working day set by Bolshevik labour decrees?

A.Six hours
B.Eight hours ✅
C.Ten hours
D.Twelve hours
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Bolshevik labor decrees mandated an eight‑hour workday in factories, aiming to improve workers’ conditions and increase productivity. This limit was a radical departure from pre‑revolutionary long hours and served as a key element of the new socialist labor policy.

Q6. Why did Lenin agree to allow some Left Socialist Revolutionaries to serve as junior partners in the government?

A.To appease the military elite
B.To secure foreign diplomatic recognition
C.Because the Left SRs had far greater support among peasants ✅
D.To fulfill a constitutional requirement
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Lenin allowed Left Socialist Revolutionaries to participate as junior partners because the SRs commanded substantial peasant support, especially in rural areas where the Bolsheviks were weak. By integrating them, Lenin hoped to broaden the coalition, legitimize Bolshevik rule, and prevent a potential peasant uprising that could threaten the nascent Soviet government.

Q7. What effect did the decree on workers' control have on industrial unrest in early Soviet factories?

A.It eliminated all strikes immediately ✅
B.It reduced the frequency of strikes but created new managerial tensions
C.It increased production without affecting labor relations
D.It had no measurable impact on industrial unrest
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The decree on workers’ control gave laborers supervisory authority over managers, which initially reduced overt strikes by involving workers in decision‑making. However, it also introduced new tensions as managers resisted ceding power, leading to managerial‑worker conflicts that sometimes disrupted production, illustrating the mixed outcomes of the policy.

Q10. How did the Bolshevik decree on land redistribution differ from the Provisional Government’s approach to agrarian reform?

A.Both offered compensation to former owners
B.The Bolsheviks nationalized land without compensation, while the Provisional Government planned gradual reform with compensation
C.The Provisional Government seized all land immediately, whereas the Bolsheviks delayed redistribution ✅
D.Both denied peasants any ownership rights
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Provisional Government pursued a cautious, incremental approach to agrarian reform, proposing compensation for former owners and gradual redistribution. In contrast, the Bolshevik Decree on Land immediately nationalized all land without compensation, redistributing it to peasants. This radical break accelerated land reform but alienated former landowners and intensified class antagonisms.

C.It had no impact on ethnic relations
D.It forced all groups to adopt Russian as the official language

While intended to appease minority populations, the policy risked fragmenting the union, especially in regions with strong ethnic identities, thereby creating additional challenges for central authority.

This centralization facilitated the allocation of funds for the Red Army, but it also disrupted existing commercial networks and could provoke resistance from former owners and foreign creditors.

By using the Cheka to suppress dissent, Lenin prioritized revolutionary security over genuine electoral freedom, revealing a pragmatic willingness to override democratic ideals when they conflicted with the consolidation of Bolshevik power.

C.It selectively canceled only domestic debts, avoiding foreign impact

While domestically popular, the decree risked alienating foreign governments and investors, potentially isolating the new state economically and inviting diplomatic retaliation.

C.The policy was neutral, as both metrics improved equally
D.Success cannot be measured without considering political factors

📖 Explanation: The policy’s primary aim was to reduce industrial unrest by empowering workers. Although output rose by 15 %, the concurrent 10 % increase in managerial conflicts indicates heightened tension rather than diminished unrest. Therefore, the decree cannot be deemed successful in achieving its core objective of stabilizing labor relations.
