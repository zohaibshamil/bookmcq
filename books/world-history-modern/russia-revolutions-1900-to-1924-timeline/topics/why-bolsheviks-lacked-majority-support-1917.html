📝 Why Bolsheviks lacked majority support 1917

📖 From World History: Modern • 16. Russia revolutions 1900 to 1924 timeline • 22 questions available

What is Why Bolsheviks lacked majority support 1917?

Despite winning control of major cities and soviets, the Bolsheviks never won a national popular mandate; they received only about 24 percent of votes in the Constituent Assembly election because peasants, who formed the vast majority, largely supported the Socialist Revolutionaries instead.

Q1. Which decree nationalized all land, including former crown estates and church property, without compensation?

A. The Decree on Land ✅
B. The Decree on Workers' Control
C. The Decree on Nationalization of Banks
D. The Decree on Eight‑Hour Workday

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The decree that transferred ownership of all land to the state, eliminating private and ecclesiastical holdings, was known as the Decree on Land. It was intended to win peasant support by redistributing property, a key step in Bolshevik strategy after the October Revolution.

Q2. What primary purpose did the decree on workers' control serve?

A. To reduce wages
B. To give industrial workers authority over managers ✅
C. To increase export quotas
D. To centralize command under the Party

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The workers' control decree was designed to empower factory laborers by granting them direct authority over their managers. This was meant to alleviate industrial unrest, improve morale, and align production with workers' interests, thereby strengthening Bolshevik legitimacy among the urban proletariat.

Q3. Which organization was commonly known as the Cheka?

A. The Red Army
B. The Supreme Soviet
C. The Extraordinary Commission for Combating Sabotage and Counter‑Revolution ✅
D. The People's Commissariat of Finance

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The secret police established by the Bolsheviks was officially titled the Extraordinary Commission for Combating Sabotage and Counter‑Revolution, abbreviated as Cheka. It was created to suppress counter‑revolutionary activity, enforce Bolshevik policies, and protect the nascent regime from internal and external threats.

Q4. How many seats did the Bolsheviks win in the Constituent Assembly?

A. \(250\)
B. \(300\)
C. \(175\) ✅
D. \(400\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In the November 1917 elections, the Bolsheviks secured only \(175\) seats out of roughly \(700\) total. This relatively small share demonstrated their limited popular backing, especially compared with the Social Revolutionaries, and foreshadowed the political conflict that followed.

Q5. Which party held the largest number of seats in the Constituent Assembly?

A. Bolsheviks
B. Mensheviks
C. Social Revolutionaries ✅
D. Kadets

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Social Revolutionaries (SRs) won the most seats, obtaining approximately \(370\) out of the total. Their dominance reflected strong rural support, contrasting sharply with the Bolsheviks' urban base, and highlighted the fragmented nature of Russian political allegiances after the revolution.

Q6. Who was the leader of the Cheka?

A. Felix Dzierzynski ✅
B. Leon Trotsky
C. Vladimir Lenin
D. Alexander Kerensky

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Felix Dzierzynski, a former member of the Social‑Democratic Party, was appointed head of the Cheka. Under his direction, the organization carried out arrests, executions, and censorship, becoming a central instrument for consolidating Bolshevik power and eliminating opposition.

Q7. What maximum working day was established by the Bolshevik decree?

A. Six hours
B. Eight hours ✅
C. Ten hours
D. Twelve hours

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Bolshevik government introduced a decree limiting the working day in factories to eight hours. This measure aimed to improve labor conditions, reduce exploitation, and gain the support of industrial workers who had previously endured longer, more arduous shifts.

Q8. A peasant receives a plot of land after the nationalization decree. Which decree enabled this redistribution?

D. Decree on Eight‑Hour Workday

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Decree on Land transferred ownership of former crown and church estates to the state, allowing the redistribution of land to peasants. By reallocating property, the Bolsheviks hoped to secure the loyalty of the rural population, a crucial demographic for their continued rule.

Q9. Why did Lenin consider holding elections risky for the Bolsheviks?

A. Because elections would be free of intimidation ✅
B. Because the Bolsheviks were unlikely to obtain a majority
C. Because elections would be postponed indefinitely
D. Because foreign powers would intervene

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Lenin feared that free elections would reveal the Bolsheviks' lack of broad popular support. With the Social Revolutionaries expected to dominate, an electoral defeat could undermine Bolshevik legitimacy and force them to cede power, contradicting their revolutionary objectives.

📖 Explanation:

When the SRs sought to establish a government based on their majority, the Bolsheviks ordered Red Guard troops to disperse the Assembly. This decisive move prevented the SRs from exercising authority and ensured that the Bolshevik regime retained control over the political system.

📖 Explanation:

By proclaiming the right of each nation to self‑determination, the Bolsheviks attempted to appeal to non‑Russian peoples who desired autonomy. This policy was meant to undermine nationalist opposition and attract minority support, thereby broadening the revolutionary coalition across the former empire.

📖 Explanation:

The Bolshevik regime justified the suppression of dissenting press by invoking the authority of the Cheka, the extraordinary commission tasked with combating sabotage and counter‑revolution. This legalistic framing allowed the government to silence opposition while portraying the action as a defensive measure.

This numerical advantage underscores the Bolsheviks' reliance on coercive measures to maintain power despite limited popular legitimacy.

Q14. A factory manager resists the eight‑hour workday limit. Which decree supports the workers' claim?

The decree limiting the working day to eight hours expressly protects workers from excessive labor demands. When a manager attempts to exceed this limit, employees can cite this legislation as the legal basis for demanding compliance, reinforcing the Bolsheviks' promise of improved labor conditions.

Q15. Lenin argued that Bolshevik rule represented \the highest form of democracy.\ Which principle underlies this claim?

A. Direct popular voting
B. Vanguard party doctrine ✅
C. Universal suffrage
D. Multi‑party coalition

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Lenin's assertion rests on the vanguard party concept, which holds that a revolutionary elite, aware of the proletariat's true interests, can guide society without the need for formal parliamentary mechanisms. This ideology posits that the Party's guidance supersedes conventional democratic processes, justifying the suppression of elected bodies.

📖 Explanation:

By incorporating Left Social Revolutionaries into the government, the Bolsheviks tapped into the SRs' substantial rural following. This alliance helped to legitimize Bolshevik authority among peasants, mitigate potential rebellion, and create a façade of broader coalition support.

Q17. How did nationalizing banks, large factories, and mines affect Bolshevik economic control?

B. It decentralized production

D. It eliminated all private property

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

State ownership of banks, major factories, and mines granted the Bolsheviks direct control over credit, production, and distribution channels. This centralization enabled the regime to direct economic activity according to socialist objectives, suppress capitalist opposition, and fund the war effort without reliance on private capital.

📖 Explanation:

The Bolsheviks' share is calculated as \(\frac{175}{700}\times 100 = 25\%\). This relatively low proportion highlights their minority status within the assembly, reinforcing why Lenin deemed the body illegitimate and opted for its dissolution despite the democratic outcome.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

The Cheka functioned as an instrument of state security, employing arrest, censorship, and execution to eliminate perceived enemies. 📖 Explanation:

Had the Assembly continued, the most plausible scenario would involve a coalition between the Bolsheviks and the Social Revolutionaries, reflecting their combined legislative strength. Such an arrangement could have moderated radical policies, introduced broader representation, and potentially altered the trajectory of Soviet governance.

📖 Explanation:

Lenin interpreted the Bolsheviks' minority status as evidence that parliamentary democracy could thwart revolutionary progress. Consequently, he justified dissolving the Assembly to prevent the SR majority from overturning Bolshevik reforms, prioritizing the preservation of revolutionary gains over democratic inclusivity.
