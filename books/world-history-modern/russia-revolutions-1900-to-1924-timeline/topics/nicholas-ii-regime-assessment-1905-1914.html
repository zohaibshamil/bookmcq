What is Nicholas II regime assessment 1905-1914?

Between 1905 and 1914 the regime achieved superficial economic growth and limited political concessions, yet its failure to address fundamental social grievances and its continued authoritarianism left Russia vulnerable to collapse when war arrived.

Q1. When did the Bolshevik general strike end in July 1914?

A. 12-Jul-14
B. 13-Jul-14
C. 15-Jul-14 ✅
D. 20-Jul-14

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The strike organized by the Bolsheviks concluded on 15 July 1914, just days before the outbreak of World War I. This date is explicitly mentioned in the passage, marking the end of the street demonstrations, shootings, and barricades that had taken place in St Petersburg.

Q2. According to George Kennan and Leopold Haimson, what would have happened to the tsarist regime without World War I?

A. It would have strengthened and lasted longer
B. It would have collapsed sooner or later ✅
C. It would have transformed into a constitutional monarchy
D. It would have remained unchanged

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Kennan and Haimson argue that the tsarist regime was structurally unstable and would have collapsed eventually, even without the external shock of World War I. Their assessment emphasizes internal weaknesses such as political repression, economic strain, and social unrest, suggesting an inevitable downfall independent of the war.

Q3. If the July 1914 strike had continued for an additional two weeks, which outcome is most consistent with the passage’s analysis?

A. The regime would have solidified its power
B. The strike would have weakened the regime further, making collapse more likely ✅
C. International intervention would have resolved the conflict
D. The Bolsheviks would have abandoned revolutionary goals

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Extending the strike by two weeks would have prolonged mass mobilization and heightened confrontation between workers and state forces. This added pressure could have strained the regime’s capacity to maintain order, potentially accelerating its collapse. Historians note that prolonged unrest often erodes governmental legitimacy, making the prospect of a revolutionary breakthrough more plausible, especially in a context already marked by fragility.

Q4. What does Sheila Fitzpatrick claim about the vulnerability of the tsarist regime?

A. It could survive any jolt
B. It was hard to imagine it could survive long even without war ✅
C. It was immune to external shocks
D. It would have reformed peacefully

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Fitzpatrick contends that the tsarist regime was so vulnerable that any significant disturbance could have precipitated its downfall. She emphasizes that, even without World War I, the regime’s inherent weaknesses made its long‑term survival unlikely, highlighting the precarious nature of pre‑war Russian politics.

Q5. Which statement best reflects the common ground between Christopher Read and Robert Service?

A. Both think war made revolution inevitable
B. Both think the regime could have survived without war ✅
C. Both think the regime was already doomed
D. Both think war had no impact on the revolution

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Both Christopher Read and Robert Service share the view that the Russian state was not doomed to revolution without the war. Read stresses that the pre‑1914 situation could have persisted indefinitely, while Service describes the regime as ‘general brittleness’ but insists that the exhausting war made a radical break possible, implying contingency rather than inevitability.

Q6. Soviet historians argued that the revolution was historically inevitable because:

A. The revolutionary upsurge was reaching a climax in 1914 ✅
B. The war caused the revolution
C. Economic factors were the sole cause
D. Cultural movements sparked the uprising

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Soviet historians maintained that revolution was historically inevitable, arguing that the revolutionary upsurge had reached its climax by 1914. In their interpretation, the outbreak of war did not cause the revolution but merely delayed its inevitable occurrence, reflecting a deterministic view of historical forces leading to 1917.

Q8. What does the phrase ‘general brittleness’ imply about pre‑1914 Russian society?

A. Widespread fragility ✅
B. Economic growth
C. Political stability
D. Military strength

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The term ‘general brittleness’ describes a widespread fragility within Russian society, indicating that multiple social, economic, and political structures were vulnerable to disruption. It suggests that the empire lacked resilience, making it susceptible to crises such as war or internal dissent, rather than implying strength or stability.

Q9. According to Christopher Read, if World War I had been avoided, what would likely have happened to the Russian monarchy?

A. It would have continued indefinitely ✅
B. It would have been overthrown immediately
C. It would have undergone gradual reforms
D. It would have faced foreign intervention

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Read argues that, absent the war, the pre‑1914 conditions could have continued without leading to revolution. He envisions a scenario where the tsarist regime maintained power indefinitely, implying that the war was the catalyst that transformed existing tensions into a full‑scale revolutionary upheaval.

Q10. Which historian emphasizes that the exhausting nature of World War I made the 1917 revolution possible?

A. Christopher Read
B. Sheila Fitzpatrick
C. Robert Service ✅
D. George Kennan

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Robert Service posits that the extensive, exhausting nature of World War I created the conditions necessary for the 1917 revolution. He emphasizes that while Russia was already ‘brittle,’ it was the prolonged conflict that turned latent discontent into a decisive revolutionary movement.

Q11. Calculate the number of days between the strike’s end on 15 July 1914 and the start of World War I on 28 July 1914.

A. 10 days
B. 13 days ✅
C. 15 days
D. 20 days

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

World War I began on 28 July 1914, while the strike ended on 15 July 1914. The interval between these dates is calculated as \(28 July - 15 July = 13\) days. This short period underscores how quickly the broader European conflict followed the domestic unrest, potentially influencing subsequent political developments.

Q12. What overarching theme does the passage illustrate about historiography of the Russian Revolution?

A. Inevitability versus contingency of revolution ✅
B. Economic determinism
C. Role of foreign policy
D. Cultural factors

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The passage presents a historiographical debate between those who view the 1917 revolution as inevitable, given long‑standing societal pressures, and those who argue it was contingent on the specific circumstances of World War I. This dichotomy captures the central tension between deterministic and contingent interpretations of Russian history.
