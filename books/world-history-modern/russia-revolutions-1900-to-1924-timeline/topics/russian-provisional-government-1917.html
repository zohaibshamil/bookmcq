What is Russian Provisional Government 1917?

The Provisional Government was a temporary liberal administration formed after the Tsar’s abdication that promised democratic elections and civil liberties but lacked real authority because it shared power with the Petrograd Soviet and failed to end the war or redistribute land.

📝 All Russian Provisional Government 1917 MCQs

Q1. Who was the first prime minister of the provisional government?

A.Prince George Lvov ✅
B.Alexander Kerensky
C.Vladimir Lenin
D.Nicholas II
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Prince George Lvov was appointed prime minister when the provisional government was formed after the February Revolution. His liberal background and lack of strong political base made his tenure brief, and he was replaced by Alexander Kerensky in July 1917.

Q2. Which decision most directly caused a collapse of army morale?

A.Continuing the war ✅
B.Promising land reform
C.Raising taxes
D.Closing factories
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The provisional government chose to keep Russia in World War I, launching the ill‑fated June Offensive. The disastrous results shattered confidence in the army, leading to massive desertions and a breakdown of discipline, which directly weakened the government's ability to command the military.

Q3. If the provisional government had organized elections for a Constituent Assembly in early 1917, what would most likely have happened to its legitimacy?

A.It would have been strengthened ✅
B.It would have been weakened because of war
C.It would have had no effect
D.It would have caused more chaos
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Holding elections while the country was still at war could have demonstrated a commitment to democratic reform, granting the provisional government a clearer mandate from the populace. This legitimacy boost might have reduced radical opposition and provided a political outlet for grievances.

Q4. During the July Days, which groups united in protest against the provisional government?

A.Workers
B.Soldiers
C.Sailors
D.All of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The July Days saw a massive demonstration in which workers, soldiers, and sailors marched together, demanding an end to the war and more radical reforms. Their joint action highlighted the growing alliance between urban labor and the military, putting additional pressure on the provisional authorities.

Q5. What was the name of the offensive launched by Kerensky that ended disastrously?

A.June Offensive ✅
B.Spring Offensive
C.Autumn Offensive
D.Winter Offensive
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Kerensky's June Offensive aimed to revive Russian morale on the front, but the operation failed spectacularly. The attack resulted in heavy casualties, a rapid loss of territory, and a further decline in confidence among both troops and civilians, accelerating the government's loss of support.

Q6. Why did sharing power with the Petrograd Soviet undermine the provisional government's control over the army?

A.The Soviet ordered soldiers to obey only the Soviet ✅
B.The government kept full control
C.The Soviets had no influence
D.The army ignored both
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Petrograd Soviet, elected before the Tsar's abdication, claimed authority over soldiers, instructing them to follow Soviet orders exclusively. This split loyalty meant the provisional government could no longer guarantee troop obedience, eroding its capacity to enforce policies or maintain order.

Q7. Imagine the provisional government had withdrawn from World War I in early 1917. How would this likely have affected soldiers' support?

A.Support would have increased ✅
B.Support would have decreased
C.Support would have stayed the same
D.The army would have split
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A premature peace would have relieved soldiers from the grueling front lines, likely restoring confidence in the provisional authorities. Many troops were eager to return home, so an early withdrawal could have bolstered popular support for the government among the military ranks.

Q8. Assess the impact of Lenin’s return via a sealed train on the provisional government's stability.

A.It intensified revolutionary activity ✅
B.It had little effect
C.It caused the government to gain support
D.It led to immediate collapse
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Lenin’s arrival on a German‑facilitated sealed train sparked a surge of Bolshevik propaganda and mass rallies. His April Theses called for ending the war and transferring power to the soviets, which energized discontented workers and soldiers, further destabilizing the provisional government and paving the way for its overthrow.

Q9. Which offensive did Kerensky initiate that failed and contributed to the provisional government's downfall?

A.June Offensive ✅
B.Spring Offensive
C.Autumn Offensive
D.Winter Offensive
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Kerensky’s decision to launch the June Offensive was intended to boost morale, but the operation collapsed, causing massive casualties and a sharp drop in army discipline. The failure reinforced public perception that the provisional government could not manage the war, accelerating its loss of authority.

Q10. Was delaying elections for a Constituent Assembly justified given wartime constraints?

A.Justified and preserved stability
B.Not justified and eroded trust ✅
C.Neutral impact
D.Beneficial for war effort
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Postponing elections was presented as a wartime necessity, yet it broke a key promise to the population. The delay alienated many who expected democratic reform, fostering skepticism and driving support toward parties—especially the Bolsheviks—who promised immediate change, thereby weakening the provisional regime.
