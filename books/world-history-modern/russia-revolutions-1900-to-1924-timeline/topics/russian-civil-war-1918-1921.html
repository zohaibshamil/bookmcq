What is Russian Civil War 1918-1921?

The Russian Civil War was a brutal multi-sided conflict between the Bolshevik Red Army and various anti-Bolshevik White forces, foreign interventionists, and nationalist movements that resulted in millions of deaths and ultimately secured Bolshevik control over most of the former empire.

📝 All Russian Civil War 1918-1921 MCQs

Q1. Who comprised the anti‑Bolshevik forces known as the Whites?

A. Only Social Revolutionaries
B. Only Mensheviks
C. Only ex‑tsarist officers
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Whites were a heterogeneous coalition that included Social Revolutionaries, Mensheviks, former tsarist officers, and any groups opposed to Bolshevik rule, making the option “All of the above” the correct description.

Q2. Where did the Allied troops first land in Russia during the intervention?

A. Murmansk
B. Archangel
C. Vladivostok
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The United States, Japan, France, and Britain sent forces that landed at the Arctic ports of Murmansk and Archangel as well as the Far‑East port of Vladivostok, so the comprehensive answer is “All of the above.”

Q3. What was the famous Bolshevik slogan promising local control?

A. All power to the Soviets ✅
B. Long live the Soviets
C. Workers of the world unite
D. Power to the people

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Bolsheviks pledged “All power to the Soviets,” a slogan that implied each town would manage its own affairs through elected councils.

Q4. Who established a White government in Siberia?

A. Admiral Kolchak ✅
B. Leon Trotsky
C. General Denikin
D. Vladimir Lenin

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Admiral Alexander Kolchak, a former Black Sea Fleet commander, set up a White government in Siberia, becoming the most prominent anti‑Bolshevik leader in that region.

Q5. Approximately how many men composed the Czechoslovak Legion?

A. \(20,000\)
B. \(40,000\) ✅
C. \(60,000\)
D. \(80,000\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Czechoslovak Legion consisted of about \(40,000\) soldiers who controlled large sections of the Trans‑Siberian Railway, posing a serious strategic challenge to the Bolsheviks.

Q6. Which country did NOT send troops to intervene in the Russian Civil War?

📖 Explanation:

Historical records show that the United States, Japan, France, and Britain deployed forces, whereas Italy did not participate in the 1918‑1919 interventions.

Q7. What was the Bolshevik stance on world revolution?

B. They opposed it
C. They were neutral
D. They ignored it

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Bolsheviks openly promoted a worldwide proletarian revolution, seeking to spread communist ideology beyond Russia’s borders.

Q8. What primary grievance did peasants have against the Bolshevik government?

A. Food‑procurement policies ✅
B. High taxes
C. Conscription into the Red Army
D. All of the above

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Peasants resented the Bolshevik food‑procurement system, which requisitioned grain and other produce, leading to widespread discontent in the countryside.

Q9. If a peasant in 1918 protested the food‑procurement policy, which slogan would they most likely adopt?

A. All power to the Soviets
B. Long live the Soviets and down with the commissars ✅
C. Workers of the world unite
D. Power to the people

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Discontented peasants rallied behind the counter‑slogan “Long live the Soviets and down with the commissars,” reflecting their desire for local soviet authority without central commissar control.

Q10. Why was the Czechoslovak Legion’s control of the Trans‑Siberian Railway strategically problematic for the Bolsheviks?

A. It disrupted Red supply lines
B. It encouraged Allied support for the Whites
C. Both of the above ✅
D. Neither of the above

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Legion’s dominance hampered the movement of troops and materiel for the Red Army while also providing the Allies with a foothold to aid White forces, creating a dual strategic threat.

Q11. Suppose the Allies withdrew after Germany’s defeat. How might the outcome of the Russian Civil War have changed?

📖 Explanation:

Without continued Allied backing, the Whites would lose crucial supplies and morale, allowing the Bolsheviks to consolidate control over key territories and diminish the opposition’s effectiveness.

C. Whites aimed for a military dictatorship, Bolsheviks wanted local self‑government
D. Both wanted immediate peace with Germany

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Whites generally advocated a democratic government modeled on Western systems, whereas the Bolsheviks aimed to establish a socialist state with the long‑term goal of worldwide revolution.

Q13. How did appointing commissars over local soviets affect Bolshevik legitimacy?

A. It increased popular support
B. It decreased legitimacy by centralising power ✅
C. It had no effect
D. It varied regionally

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Replacing locally elected soviet members with centrally appointed commissars was perceived as a betrayal of the promised decentralisation, eroding the Bolsheviks’ legitimacy among many citizens.

Q14. When Kolchak advanced toward Moscow in early 1919, which logistical challenge was most severe?

B. Hostile terrain

D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Kolchak’s march required long, vulnerable supply routes, traversed difficult terrain, and occurred during a severe Russian winter, making every factor a critical obstacle to his campaign.

Q15. Which faction within the White coalition was most likely to oppose a restoration of the monarchy?

Mensheviks, as moderate socialists, opposed a return to autocratic rule and thus were the group most likely to reject a monarchic restoration.

Q16. Why did the opponents adopt the slogan “Long live the Soviets and down with the commissars”?

📖 Explanation:

The slogan reflected peasants’ and workers’ frustration that the promised local soviet authority was being overridden by centrally appointed commissars, highlighting a demand for genuine grassroots governance.
