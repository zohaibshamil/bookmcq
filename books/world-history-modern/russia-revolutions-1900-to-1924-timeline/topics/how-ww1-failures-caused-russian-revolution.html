What is How WW1 failures caused Russian Revolution?

World War I devastated Russia through massive casualties, food shortages, inflation, and military defeats that destroyed public confidence in the Tsar, turning wartime hardship into revolutionary anger against the entire autocratic system.

📝 All How WW1 failures caused Russian Revolution MCQs

Q1. In which year did Nicholas appoint himself supreme commander of the Russian army?

A.1914
B.1915 ✅
C.1916
D.1917
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Norman Stone notes that Nicholas took personal command in August \(1915\). This decision placed him directly responsible for subsequent defeats, eroding confidence in his leadership and contributing to the growing disillusionment that fueled revolutionary sentiment.

Q2. What primary factor caused bread scarcity in Russian cities during the war?

A.Food rationing
B.Military monopolizing trains ✅
C.Poor harvest
D.Export bans
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The military commandeered the majority of railway capacity, diverting transport resources away from civilian needs. Consequently, foodstuffs could not reach urban markets, driving up prices and creating acute shortages that heightened public unrest and pressure for political change.

Q3. If 70% of trains were allocated to the military and only 30% remained for civilian supply, what was the likely impact on food distribution?

A.No impact
B.Slight delay
C.Severe shortages ✅
D.Improved efficiency
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: With \(70\%\) of logistical capacity dedicated to war efforts, civilian shipments were dramatically reduced, leading to severe shortages in cities. The limited remaining transport could not meet demand, causing hunger, inflation, and resentment that helped destabilize the autocratic regime.

Q4. Why did Nicholas’s self‑appointment as supreme commander undermine Brusilov’s 1916 offensive success?

A.He lacked military experience
B.He diverted resources
C.He made tactical blunders ✅
D.He resigned
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Nicholas’s hands‑on approach introduced strategic mistakes that squandered the gains achieved by Brusilov’s offensive. His poor tactical decisions reversed momentum, resulting in renewed setbacks that tarnished the army’s reputation and intensified calls for regime change.

Q5. General Krimov’s secret meeting implied a coup. If the Duma had 200 members and 60% supported a coup, how many members favored it?

A.80
B.100
C.120 ✅
D.140
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Calculating \(200 \times 0.60\) yields 120 members. This sizable backing indicated deep institutional disaffection with the Tsar, suggesting that even traditional political elites were prepared to endorse radical action to prevent a broader societal upheaval.

Q6. Which social group began to turn against Nicholas by January 1917?

A.Peasants
B.Aristocracy ✅
C.Clergy
D.Merchants
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The aristocracy, many of whom held seats in the Duma, grew wary of the war’s devastation and Nicholas’s mismanagement. Their shift away from unconditional support signaled a critical erosion of elite backing, weakening the autocratic foundation and encouraging revolutionary momentum.

Q7. Why did the army’s reasonable performance not stop the revolution?

A.High casualties
B.Lack of supplies
C.Political discontent
D.All of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Although the army performed competently, it suffered heavy losses, inadequate equipment, and was plagued by political disillusionment. These combined factors eroded morale and loyalty, making soldiers more susceptible to mutiny and aligning them with broader revolutionary currents.

Q8. Who authored the analysis that the Russian army performed reasonably well?

A.Norman Stone ✅
B.John Smith
C.Robert Wilson
D.Alexander Petrov
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Norman Stone’s scholarly work highlighted that, despite overall setbacks, the Russian forces displayed competence in several operations, including Brusilov’s 1916 offensive, thereby providing a nuanced view of military effectiveness amid political turmoil.

Q9. If the war death rate rose by 15% after Nicholas assumed command and initially 1,000,000 soldiers died, what is the new total?

A.1150000 ✅
B.1200000
C.1250000
D.1300000
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An increase of \(15\%\) on the original figure of one million yields an additional 150,000 deaths, bringing the total to 1,150,000. This spike intensified public grief and amplified criticism of Nicholas’s leadership, fueling revolutionary fervor.

Q10. How did the shortage of equipment contribute to troop mutiny?

A.It lowered morale
B.It caused desertion
C.It led to insubordination
D.All of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Insufficient arms and ammunition demoralized soldiers, prompting desertion and open insubordination. The lack of essential supplies undermined confidence in the war effort, turning disciplined troops into a volatile force that could no longer be relied upon to uphold the autocracy.

Q11. Which 1916 offensive is noted as an impressive Russian success?

A.Kerensky Offensive
B.Brusilov Offensive ✅
C.Gallipoli Offensive
D.Verdun Offensive
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Brusilov Offensive of 1916 stands out for its innovative tactics and initial breakthroughs against Austro‑Hungarian forces. Its early triumph temporarily boosted morale but was later squandered by subsequent strategic errors from the Tsar’s leadership.

Q12. What was the main effect of poor transport organization during the war?

A.Faster supply
B.Delayed arms ✅
C.Increased morale
D.Reduced taxes
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Inefficient logistics meant that weapons, ammunition, and other essential materiel reached the front lines far slower than needed, impairing combat effectiveness and contributing to the broader perception of governmental incompetence.

Q13. If each train could carry 500 tons of supplies and the military used 80% of 100 trains, how many tons remained for civilian use?

A.10000 ✅
B.5000
C.2000
D.0
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: With 80 of the 100 trains allocated to the military, only 20 trains were free for civilian transport. Multiplying 20 trains by 500 tons each yields \(20 \times 500 = 10,000\) tons of supplies available for non‑military distribution.

Q14. Why did industrialists consider sacrificing the Tsar to avoid a worse revolution?

A.To protect profits
B.To maintain social order ✅
C.To gain political power
D.To align with Allies
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Industrialists feared that continued war mismanagement would precipitate a chaotic overthrow that could dismantle the existing economic framework. By supporting the removal of the Tsar, they hoped to preserve stability and protect their commercial interests.

Q15. What does the Duma’s contemplation of a coup suggest about the autocracy’s legitimacy?

A.Strengthening
B.Eroding ✅
C.Stabilizing
D.Expanding
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When a legislative body entertains a coup, it signals a profound loss of confidence in the ruling system. This erosion of legitimacy indicates that the autocratic regime no longer commanded the essential support needed to govern effectively.

Q16. In which month of 1915 did Nicholas become supreme commander?

A.March
B.August ✅
C.December
D.June
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Nicholas assumed the role of supreme commander in August \(1915\), a move that placed him at the helm of military strategy and directly linked him to subsequent defeats, thereby accelerating the loss of confidence among both the military and civilian population.

Q17. If food was sufficient country‑wide but only 40% reached cities, what proportion was lost?

A.0.2
B.0.4
C.0.6 ✅
D.0.8
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: When only \(40\%\) of available food arrived in urban areas, the remaining \(60\%\) failed to reach the populace, creating acute shortages and inflating prices, which amplified public discontent and contributed to revolutionary pressures.

Q18. How did the combination of military failures, logistical problems, and political missteps create a revolutionary environment?

A.They were unrelated
B.They compounded pressures ✅
C.They only affected the army
D.They improved governance
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The intertwining of battlefield defeats, inadequate supply chains, and poor leadership decisions amplified societal frustration. Each factor reinforced the others, generating a climate where calls for radical change became increasingly compelling and ultimately inevitable.
