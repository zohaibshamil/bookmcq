About 4. International relations 1919-33

The period from 1919 to 1933 was characterized by a fragile attempt to stabilize international relations through treaties, disarmament conferences, and economic cooperation like the Dawes Plan. Nations sought to move past wartime animosities and integrate Germany back into the international community, resulting in a brief era of optimism known as the "Locarno spirit" where diplomatic dialogue seemed to replace confrontation.

However, this stability was heavily dependent on American loans and a functioning global economy, making it vulnerable to external shocks. The onset of the Great Depression in 1929 shattered international cooperation as countries turned inward toward protectionism and nationalism, causing the collapse of the post-war diplomatic framework and paving the way for aggressive authoritarian regimes.
