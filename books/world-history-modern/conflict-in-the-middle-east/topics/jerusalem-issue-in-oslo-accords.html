Q1. What was the original United Nations intention regarding the status of Jerusalem when Israel was created?

🔹 A. It would be under Israeli control
🔹 B. It would be under Jordanian control
🔹 C. It would be placed under international control
🔹 D. It would be divided between Israel and Jordan

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN, in its founding plans, envisioned Jerusalem as a city administered by an international regime to ensure neutral access for all faiths. This concept was meant to prevent exclusive claims by any single party, reflecting the city's profound religious and cultural significance to multiple communities.

Q2. Which war resulted in Israel capturing East Jerusalem from Jordan?

🔹 A. The 1948 Arab‑Israeli War
🔹 B. The 1956 Suez Crisis
🔹 C. The 1967 Six‑Day War
🔹 D. The 1973 Yom Kippur War

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In June \(1967\), the Six‑Day War dramatically reshaped the map of the Middle East. During this conflict, Israeli forces seized East Jerusalem, along with the West Bank, from Jordanian control. The annexation of these territories has remained a central point of contention in subsequent peace negotiations and diplomatic discussions.

Q3. When did the Knesset pass the Jerusalem Law declaring the city the capital of Israel?

🔹 A. 1975
🔹 B. 1980
🔹 C. 1985
🔹 D. 1990

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Israeli parliament, known as the Knesset, enacted the Jerusalem Law in \(1980\). This legislation proclaimed Jerusalem, complete and unified, as the capital of Israel, prompting international criticism and a United Nations Security Council resolution that condemned the move as a violation of prior UN resolutions concerning the city's status.

Q4. Which summit marked the first detailed discussion of Jerusalem’s status after the Oslo Accords?

🔹 A. Madrid Conference 1991
🔹 B. Camp David Summit 2000
🔹 C. Wye River Memorandum 1998
🔹 D. Sharm El‑Sheikh Summit 2005

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The first in‑depth negotiations on Jerusalem after the Oslo framework took place at the Camp David Summit in July \(2000\). Led by U.S. President Bill Clinton, the talks aimed to resolve final‑status issues, including the city’s future, but ultimately ended without agreement, leading to renewed violence in the region.

Q5. What is the Muslim name for the sacred area that includes the Temple Mount?

🔹 A. Al‑Quds
🔹 B. Al‑Harām al‑Sharīf
🔹 C. Al‑Masjid al‑Aqṣā
🔹 D. Al‑Maqām al‑Mubārak

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Muslims refer to the entire compound that houses the Temple Mount as Al‑Harām al‑Sharīf, meaning “the Noble Sanctuary.” This area holds the Al‑Aqṣā Mosque and the Dome of the Rock, both revered sites. Its religious significance stems from the belief that Prophet Muhammad ascended to heaven from this location during the Night Journey.

Q6. Which event triggered the abandonment of the idea of a divided Jerusalem by Israeli leadership?

🔹 B. The assassination of Yitzhak Rabin
🔹 C. The Second Intifada
🔹 D. The Oslo II Accords

📖 Explanation:

Yitzhak Rabin’s assassination in November \(1995\) profoundly altered Israeli politics. Following his death, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu distanced himself from the notion of sharing Jerusalem, opting instead for a united Israeli capital. This shift disappointed Palestinian negotiators, who had seen the division concept as a potential compromise in peace talks.

Q7. A diplomat argues that international control of Jerusalem could reduce conflict. Which UN resolution supports this view?

🔹 A. Resolution 242
🔹 B. Resolution 338
🔹 C. Resolution 478
🔹 D. Resolution 2334

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

UN Security Council Resolution \(478\) (1980) reaffirmed the principle that Jerusalem should remain under international administration, condemning Israel’s legislative actions that claimed the whole city as its capital. The resolution reflects the longstanding international consensus that external oversight could help mitigate competing sovereignty claims and maintain the city’s shared religious heritage.

📖 Explanation:

These deep‑seated beliefs make compromise on sovereignty, access, and administration particularly difficult within a two‑state framework.

Q10. Which international legal principle does the 1980 Jerusalem Law contravene, according to UN resolutions?

🔹 A. The principle of self‑determination
🔹 B. The principle of non‑intervention
🔹 C. The principle of territorial integrity
🔹 D. The principle of international administration of holy sites

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

UN resolutions, particularly \(478\), emphasize that Jerusalem should be placed under international governance to ensure equitable access to its holy sites. By unilaterally declaring the entire city as Israel’s capital, the Jerusalem Law violates this principle, prompting condemnation and calls for the city’s status to be resolved through multilateral negotiation.

🔹 C. 1981
🔹 D. 1982

Q18. Considering international law, why is the construction of new Israeli settlements in East Jerusalem deemed illegal by many UN bodies?

🔹 A. Because they violate the principle of self‑determination
🔹 B. Because they contravene the Fourth Geneva Convention’s provisions on occupied territories
🔹 C. Because they conflict with the UN Charter’s environmental clauses
🔹 D. Because they breach maritime law

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

International legal scholars and UN committees argue that establishing settlements in East Jerusalem breaches the Fourth Geneva Convention, which prohibits an occupying power from transferring its civilian population into occupied territories. This interpretation underpins numerous UN resolutions condemning settlement activities as violations of international law.
