What is Sharon intifada and Gulf War connections?

Ariel Sharon’s controversial visit to the Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif in September 2000 sparked the Second Intifada, occurring partly because post-Gulf War frustrations over stalled peace, ongoing occupation, and perceived betrayal of Oslo promises created volatile conditions that his provocative act ignited into widespread violence.

Q1. On what date did Ariel Sharon make his highly publicized visit to the Temple Mount?

A. 28-Sept-00 ✅
B. 28-Sept-99
C. 28-Sept-01
D. 28-Sept-02

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The visit occurred on 28 September 2000, a date that marked a turning point in Israeli‑Palestinian relations. The timing was significant because it followed the collapse of the Camp David talks and preceded the eruption of the al‑Aqsa intifada, intensifying tensions across the region.

Q2. Which political party did Ariel Sharon represent at the time of his Temple Mount visit?

A. Labor Party
B. Likud Party ✅
C. Kadima Party
D. Meretz Party

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Sharon was the leader of the opposition Likud party, a right‑wing faction in Israeli politics. His affiliation with Likud shaped his approach to sovereignty claims over Jerusalem and influenced the subsequent Israeli response to the intifada, reflecting the party’s hard‑line stance on security and territorial issues.

Q3. What name was given to the uprising that followed Sharon's visit?

A. First Intifada
B. Second Intifada
C. Al‑Aqsa (Jerusalem) Intifada ✅
D. Gaza War

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The uprising that erupted after the September 2000 visit is commonly referred to as the al‑Aqsa (Jerusalem) intifada, also known as the Second Intifada. This term emphasizes the religious significance of the Temple Mount (Al‑Aqsa) and distinguishes it from the earlier popular uprising of the early 1990s.

Sharon won the February 2001 elections, defeating Ehud Barak and becoming Prime Minister. His victory marked a shift toward a more confrontational policy toward the Palestinian territories, as he promised to halt negotiations while violence persisted, thereby shaping the next phase of the intifada.
