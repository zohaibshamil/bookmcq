Q1. What year did Iraqi forces invade Kuwait, triggering the Gulf War?

🔹 A. 1990
🔹 B. 1991
🔹 C. 1989
🔹 D. 1992

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The invasion occurred in August 1990 when Iraqi troops crossed into Kuwait. This action marked the beginning of the Gulf War and led to a rapid international response. The date is a fundamental fact that situates the conflict within the broader timeline of early 1990s Middle Eastern events.

Q2. Which of the following was NOT a stated motive for Saddam Hussein’s invasion of Kuwait?

🔹 A. Control of oil wealth
🔹 B. Historical claim of Iraqi territory
🔹 C. Desire to spread democracy
🔹 D. Desire to secure financial resources after the Iran‑Iraq War

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Saddam’s declared motives centered on historical claims and the lucrative oil fields of Kuwait, especially after the costly Iran‑Iraq conflict. He did not invoke the spread of democracy; that was never part of his public justification, making option C the incorrect motive.

Q3. How did the United Nations initially respond to Iraq’s occupation of Kuwait?

🔹 A. Authorized immediate military action
🔹 B. Imposed trade sanctions and demanded withdrawal
🔹 C. Recognized Kuwait as part of Iraq
🔹 D. Issued a cease‑fire without conditions

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The UN responded by imposing comprehensive trade sanctions on Iraq, cutting off its oil exports, and issuing an ultimatum demanding withdrawal by 15 January 1991. This diplomatic pressure was intended to compel Iraq to leave Kuwait without immediate use of force, illustrating the UN’s reliance on economic leverage.

Q16. What was the primary legal basis for the coalition’s use of force against Iraq?

🔹 D. NATO Charter
