What is Reasons for poverty in developing countries?

Poverty in developing countries has historically stemmed from colonial legacies that extracted resources, unequal global trade terms, political instability, lack of infrastructure, rapid population growth, and dependence on volatile primary commodity exports rather than diversified industrial economies.

📝 All Reasons for poverty in developing countries MCQs

Q1. What commodity did Ghana primarily export during the colonial era?

A.Cocoa ✅
B.Gold
C.Oil
D.Timber
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Cocoa was Ghana’s main export, providing most foreign‑exchange earnings. This reliance meant that when cocoa prices fell, national income dropped sharply, limiting resources for other sectors. Gold, oil, and timber were not the dominant exports at that time, so they are incorrect choices. Understanding Ghana’s export focus explains why a single‑commodity economy can be fragile when global prices change.

Q2. In the 1970s, which global commodity experienced a dramatic price decline affecting many Southern economies?

A.Wheat
B.Copper ✅
C.Rice
D.Sugar
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Copper prices collapsed in the 1970s, hurting countries like Zambia that depended heavily on copper exports. Wheat, rice, and sugar did not see the same dramatic worldwide price drop during that decade, making those options inaccurate. Recognizing copper’s price fall shows how a single‑product export base can expose economies to volatile market forces.

Q3. Why did reliance on a single export commodity make Southern economies vulnerable?

A.It limited revenue sources and amplified impact of price swings ✅
B.It increased domestic food production
C.It encouraged foreign investment
D.It diversified the labor market
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When an economy depends on one export, any global price change directly reduces national income, restricting funds for imports and development. The other options describe benefits not typically associated with single‑commodity dependence. This vulnerability explains why price drops in cocoa, copper, or coffee caused severe economic distress in many Southern nations.

Q4. How did neocolonial trade patterns keep Southern countries dependent on the North?

A.By providing equal market access
B.By imposing high tariffs on Southern goods
C.By channeling raw material exports to the North while encouraging purchase of Northern manufactured goods ✅
D.By subsidizing Southern industrialization
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Neocolonial arrangements often required the South to supply raw materials and buy finished products from the North, creating a one‑way flow of wealth. High tariffs on Southern goods and subsidies for Southern industry were not typical features, making those choices incorrect. This pattern reinforced economic dependence and limited Southern industrial growth.

Q5. What role did government cash shortages play in preventing diversification?

A.They forced investment in education
B.They limited ability to fund new industries and infrastructure ✅
C.They increased foreign debt levels
D.They boosted agricultural yields
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Insufficient cash meant governments could not invest in factories, training, or infrastructure needed to shift away from a single export. While education and debt may be affected, the direct barrier to diversification is the lack of investment capital. Therefore, option B correctly identifies the primary obstacle to economic diversification.

Q6. How does a fall in world price of cocoa affect food security in a cocoa‑dependent country?

A.It raises domestic cocoa consumption
B.It reduces export earnings, limiting funds for food imports
C.It increases agricultural productivity
D.It eliminates need for foreign aid ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: When cocoa prices drop, export earnings shrink, leaving governments with fewer resources to purchase food for their populations, potentially causing shortages. Higher domestic consumption or productivity gains are not typical outcomes of a price decline, and foreign aid may be needed rather than eliminated. Thus, reduced import capacity is the key impact.

Q7. Why might importing expensive food become necessary after a commodity price crash?

A.Because domestic production becomes insufficient to meet demand ✅
B.Because the government wants to increase trade surplus
C.Because foreign markets demand more food
D.Because food prices globally drop
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A price crash in the main export reduces national income, limiting funds for domestic agriculture and forcing reliance on costly imports to feed the population. The other options misrepresent economic incentives or global price trends. This explains how a single‑commodity shock can create food insecurity despite earlier self‑sufficiency.

Q8. In what way did the expectation that the South buy manufactured goods from the North hinder industrial development?

A.It created high demand for Southern factories
B.It diverted capital away from local manufacturing toward imported goods
C.It encouraged Southern innovation ✅
D.It reduced transportation costs
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The expectation encouraged Southern consumers to purchase Northern products, meaning less capital was available for building local factories and developing technology. High demand for Southern factories would actually promote growth, which contradicts the historical outcome. Therefore, the diversion of capital best explains the hindrance.

Q9. What is neocolonialism in the context of economic relations between North and South?

A.A system where Southern nations dominate global trade
B.A policy of equal exchange between regions ✅
C.A continuation of colonial‑style economic control through trade, investment, and aid
D.A cultural exchange program
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Neocolonialism describes how former colonial powers maintain influence by structuring trade and investment so the South remains dependent on the North. It is not about dominance, equal exchange, or cultural programs. Recognizing this definition helps explain persistent poverty despite formal political independence.

Q10. If Zambia could shift 20% of copper revenue into a textile industry, what long‑term effect on its trade balance is most likely?

A.Worsening deficit due to higher imports
B.Improved balance as textiles replace some imports
C.No change because textiles are not exportable ✅
D.Immediate increase in copper exports
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Investing revenue into textiles creates a new export sector, potentially reducing reliance on copper and diversifying income. The most plausible long‑term outcome is an improved trade balance as the new industry begins to generate export earnings. The other options either ignore the diversification benefit or misstate immediate effects.

Q12. Suppose the South negotiates a trade agreement to export raw materials and import finished goods at equal value. Which outcome best describes the impact on its GDP?

A.GDP will double instantly
B.GDP will shrink due to loss of export surplus
C.GDP will remain unchanged because trade value balances
D.GDP will increase modestly as value‑added processing grows ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: When imports equal exports in monetary terms, the country does not lose income, but if it begins adding value through processing before export, GDP rises modestly. An instant doubling is unrealistic, and a shrinkage would require a deficit. Therefore, modest growth from value‑added activities best captures the likely effect.

Q13. If a government invests 10%10\%10% of its export earnings into agricultural diversification, what immediate effect on food imports is expected?

A.A reduction in food imports ✅
B.No change in food imports
C.An increase in food imports
D.A complete elimination of food imports
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Allocating a portion of export earnings to diversify agriculture can boost domestic food production, lowering the need for imports. The other options either assume no impact or unrealistic outcomes. This demonstrates how reinvesting export income can improve food self‑sufficiency.

Q14. Given a hypothetical scenario where a southern country’s main export price falls by 40%, which fiscal policy would most effectively stabilize its economy?

A.Increase government spending on luxury projects
B.Cut taxes on imported goods
C.Implement a temporary export‑revenue‑linked stimulus ✅
D.Raise interest rates sharply
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A stimulus tied to export revenue can cushion households and businesses from the sudden income loss, supporting consumption and investment. Luxury spending would waste scarce resources, tax cuts on imports would worsen the trade deficit, and higher interest rates would depress borrowing. Hence, the targeted stimulus is the most stabilizing measure.

Q15. A development bank offers a loan contingent on the country reducing its reliance on a single commodity by 30% over five years. Which strategy aligns best with the condition?

A.Boosting production of the same commodity
B.Investing in unrelated service sectors ✅
C.Developing a secondary export like processed cocoa
D.Increasing tariffs on all imports
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Reducing dependence means expanding other sectors; investing in services creates new revenue streams unrelated to the dominant commodity. Boosting the same commodity or raising tariffs does not diversify, and processing the same commodity still relies on it. Therefore, the service‑sector investment best meets the loan requirement.

Q16. If a southern nation increases tariffs on imported manufactured goods from the North, what is the most probable short‑term effect on its trade deficit?

A.The deficit will narrow because imports fall
B.The deficit will widen due to higher import costs
C.The deficit will remain unchanged
D.The deficit will disappear completely ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Higher tariffs make imported goods more expensive, reducing their volume and potentially narrowing the deficit. However, the most immediate and measurable effect is a reduction in imports, which directly lowers the trade deficit, matching option D. The other choices either ignore the tariff impact or overstate its effect.

Q17. Imagine a regional coalition of southern states creates a joint processing plant for cocoa beans. How would this affect each member’s export earnings compared to raw cocoa export?

A.Earnings would decline because processing is costly
B.Earnings would stay the same
C.Earnings would increase due to added value ✅
D.Earnings would be unpredictable
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Processing cocoa adds value, allowing members to sell a higher‑priced product, thus increasing export earnings. While costs rise, the net effect is generally positive. Earnings would not stay the same or decline solely because of processing, and while exact figures vary, the direction of change is an increase.

Q18. A southern economy with a 25% unemployment rate implements a public works program funded by a 5% levy on export profits. Which macro‑economic outcome is most likely?

A.Reduced unemployment and higher aggregate demand ✅
B.Higher inflation without employment change
C.Lower GDP due to reduced export profits
D.Increased trade surplus
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The levy reduces export profit marginally but funds public projects that create jobs, lowering unemployment and boosting aggregate demand. Inflation may rise modestly, but the primary effect is employment improvement. A lower GDP or higher surplus are less direct outcomes of this policy.

Q19. If global demand for cotton rises while its price remains stable, how would a cotton‑dependent southern country’s balance of payments be affected, assuming no change in other sectors?

A.It would improve because export volume rises ✅
B.It would worsen due to higher import costs
C.It would stay unchanged
D.It would become negative
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Higher demand leads to larger export quantities even if price stays constant, increasing foreign‑exchange earnings and improving the balance of payments. Import costs are unrelated to cotton demand, and the balance would not remain unchanged or turn negative under these conditions.
