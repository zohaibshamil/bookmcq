What is Russia economy 2012 global position?

In 2012, Russia’s economy was heavily dependent on oil and gas exports, making it a significant energy supplier and middle-income nation whose global economic position fluctuated with commodity prices, limiting diversification and leaving it vulnerable to external shocks despite substantial natural resource wealth.

📝 All Russia economy 2012 global position MCQs

Q1. In 2012, Russia ranked second worldwide in the production of which of the following?

A. Oil ✅
B. Steel
C. Armaments
D. Natural gas

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is Armaments because Russia was the world’s second largest producer of military equipment after the United States in 2012. Oil, steel, and natural gas are all significant sectors for Russia, but they did not hold the second‑place ranking in armaments. Recognizing this fact helps students link Russia’s defense industry strength to its broader economic profile.

Q2. Why did the 2008 plunge in oil prices trigger a liquidity crisis for Russian banks?

A. Because oil companies defaulted on loans
B. Because the government cut spending
C. Because foreign credit dried up ✅
D. Because inflation rose

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is that foreign credit dried up. When oil prices fell from about \(140\) to \(40\) dollars per barrel, oil‑related revenues collapsed, causing banks that relied on foreign borrowing to lose access to needed funds. The other options describe unrelated effects: loan defaults may follow later, but the immediate cause was the loss of foreign credit, not a spending cut or inflation surge.

Q3. If the price of oil rebounds to \(120\) dollars per barrel, which effect is most likely on Russia’s budget deficit?

A. It will increase sharply ✅
B. It will shrink
C. It will stay unchanged
D. It will become a surplus

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is that the deficit will shrink. Higher oil prices boost government revenue from oil exports, allowing the state to cover more of its expenses and reduce the shortfall. An increase in oil price does not automatically create a surplus because other spending obligations remain, and the deficit does not stay unchanged when a major revenue source improves.

Q4. How did diversification into IT and nuclear‑power industries help Russia reduce its reliance on oil revenues?

A. By creating new export markets
B. By lowering oil production
C. By increasing tax rates ✅
D. By reducing foreign investment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is that diversification created new export markets. Expanding IT services and building nuclear reactors for export gave Russia alternative sources of foreign currency, lessening the impact of oil price volatility. The other choices either misstate the effect (lowering production or raising taxes) or describe unrelated dynamics (foreign investment). Understanding this shows how economic breadth can buffer commodity shocks.

Q5. Russia’s central bank used one‑third of its \(600\) billion‑dollar reserve to support the rouble. How much money was deployed?

A. \(200\text{ billion}\) ✅
B. \(150\text{ billion}\)
C. \(300\text{ billion}\)
D. \(250\text{ billion}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is \(200\) billion dollars because one‑third of \(600\) billion equals \(600/3 = 200\). The other numbers represent either a larger or smaller fraction of the total reserve. This calculation demonstrates how the bank’s intervention size was determined, illustrating the scale of policy action needed during a currency crisis.

Q6. What primary economic risk does a country face when it depends heavily on a single commodity like oil?

A. Increased diversification
B. Reduced employment
C. Exposure to price shocks ✅
D. Improved trade balance

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is exposure to price shocks. Relying on one commodity makes the economy vulnerable to global price swings, as seen when oil prices fell dramatically in 2008, causing revenue loss and liquidity problems. The other options describe opposite outcomes: diversification reduces risk, while reduced employment and trade‑balance changes are consequences, not the core risk.

Q7. If Russia’s labor force is 70 million and it wants unemployment below 5 %, what is the maximum number of unemployed people it can have?

A. 3.5 million ✅
B. 4 million
C. 5 million
D. 2 million

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is 3.5 million because 5 % of 70 million equals \(0.05 \times 70{,}000{,}000 = 3{,}500{,}000\). The other figures either overstate or understate the allowable unemployment level. This calculation helps students see how unemployment targets translate into concrete population numbers, linking macroeconomic goals to demographic data.

Q8. Russia’s steel export is \(30\) billion dollars and aluminum export is \(20\) billion dollars. If total export revenue is \(150\) billion dollars, what percentage of total exports do steel and aluminum together represent?

A. 0.2
B. 0.33 ✅
C. 0.4
D. 0.5

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is 33 % because \((30+20)/150 = 50/150 = 0.333\), or 33 %. The other percentages result from incorrect arithmetic. Knowing this proportion shows the relative importance of these metal sectors within Russia’s overall export portfolio, illustrating how to interpret trade composition data.
