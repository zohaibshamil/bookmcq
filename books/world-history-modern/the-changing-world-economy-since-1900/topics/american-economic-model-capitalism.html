Q1. In which year was John Rawls' *A Theory of Justice* published?

🔹 A. 1970
🔹 B. 1973
🔹 C. 1975
🔹 D. 1978

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is 1973 because Rawls released his seminal work that year, establishing his theory of justice as fairness. The other years are incorrect dates for this publication. Knowing the publication year helps situate Rawls' ideas within the broader debate on American welfare policy, showing when his arguments entered public discourse and influenced later reforms.

Q2. Which president launched the New Deal?

🔹 A. Herbert Hoover
🔹 B. Franklin D. Roosevelt
🔹 C. Dwight Eisenhower
🔹 D. John Kennedy

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Franklin D. Roosevelt introduced the New Deal to address the Great Depression, making him the correct choice. Hoover, Eisenhower, and Kennedy did not create these programs. Recognizing Roosevelt’s role clarifies how the American economic model shifted toward more active government intervention during crises, illustrating the contrast with earlier laissez‑faire attitudes.

Q3. What principle did Robert Nozick emphasize regarding property rights?

🔹 A. Redistribution
🔹 B. Minimal government
🔹 C. Social contract
🔹 D. Collective ownership

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Nozick championed minimal government, arguing that the state should interfere as little as possible with private property. Redistribution, social contract, and collective ownership reflect opposing views. Understanding Nozick’s stance reveals why later Republican administrations pursued tax cuts and reduced welfare, aligning with his philosophy of limited state power.

Q4. How does the concept of 'rugged individualism' influence Republican tax policy?

🔹 A. Increases taxes to fund welfare
🔹 B. Decreases taxes to protect property
🔹 C. Maintains high tariffs
🔹 D. Encourages nationalization

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Republican policymakers often lower taxes to safeguard private property and personal initiative, embodying rugged individualism. Raising taxes for welfare, keeping tariffs high, or nationalizing industries contradict this principle. This link explains why tax‑cut legislation is common in Republican platforms, reflecting a belief that lower taxes foster economic freedom and prosperity.

Q5. If a state cuts welfare benefits by 15% while also reducing the top marginal tax rate from 35% to 28%, which outcome aligns with Nozick's philosophy?

🔹 A. Increased income inequality
🔹 B. Greater economic growth with less redistribution
🔹 C. Higher government spending
🔹 D. More social safety net

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Nozick advocated minimal taxation and limited redistribution, so a lower top tax rate paired with reduced welfare fits his view that the economy will grow when the state steps back. Increased inequality might occur, but his theory predicts growth rather than focusing on distribution. Higher spending or a stronger safety net would contradict his minimal‑state stance.

Q6. A multinational company lobbies the US government to avoid entering a debt relief agreement with a developing nation. Which American economic principle is being invoked?

🔹 A. Social contract
🔹 B. Neo‑conservatism
🔹 C. Free trade
🔹 D. Interventionism

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The company's argument reflects neo‑conservatism, which prioritizes limited government involvement abroad and resists using US resources for foreign welfare. Social contract and free‑trade ideas involve broader cooperation, while interventionism suggests active engagement. This scenario shows how neo‑conservative ideology can shape foreign‑policy decisions, emphasizing national self‑interest over global assistance.

Q7. Why did the Great Society programs represent a shift from pure 'rugged individualism'?

🔹 A. They increased military spending
🔹 B. They introduced universal healthcare
🔹 C. They expanded federal welfare
🔹 D. They reduced taxes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Great Society initiatives broadened federal welfare, moving away from the idea that individuals must rely solely on themselves. Military spending, universal healthcare, and tax cuts do not capture this shift. Expanding welfare illustrates a move toward a social contract where the government assumes responsibility for basic needs, highlighting a pivotal change in American economic thought.

Q8. Suppose the US adopts a policy of a flat tax of 20% on all incomes. Which of the following statements best predicts the impact on middle‑class taxpayers according to Rawlsian fairness?

🔹 A. It will improve fairness
🔹 B. It will reduce fairness
🔹 C. It will have no effect
🔹 D. It will increase redistribution

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Rawls argued that fairness requires progressive taxation to address inequalities; a flat tax treats everyone alike, which he would view as less fair for the middle class. The other options misrepresent his stance: he would not claim it improves fairness or has no effect, and a flat tax does not increase redistribution. This highlights the tension between equality of outcome and equality of opportunity.

Q9. Compare Rawls' 'justice as fairness' with Nozick's 'entitlement theory' regarding the role of taxation.

🔹 A. Both support high taxes
🔹 B. Rawls supports redistribution, Nozick opposes taxes
🔹 C. Rawls opposes taxes, Nozick supports redistribution
🔹 D. Both reject taxes

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Rawls contends that taxation is a tool for achieving a fair distribution of resources, while Nozick maintains that taxes infringe on property rights and should be minimal. The other pairings invert their positions or claim mutual support, which is inaccurate. This contrast explains why liberal administrations favor progressive taxes whereas conservative ones push for tax cuts, reflecting deep philosophical divides.

Q10. A state proposes a welfare program funded by a 5% wealth tax on assets over $1 million. According to Nozick's minimal state view, what is the most likely criticism?

🔹 A. It violates property rights
🔹 B. It encourages savings
🔹 C. It reduces government size
🔹 D. It promotes equality

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Nozick would argue that taxing wealth above a threshold infringes on individuals' rightful holdings, violating property rights. Encouraging savings, reducing government size, or promoting equality are not central to his critique; he focuses on the illegitimacy of compulsory redistribution. This illustrates why minimal‑state advocates resist wealth taxes, seeing them as government overreach.

Q11. What was the main political ideology behind the Reagan administration's reduction of taxes and welfare?

🔹 A. Progressivism
🔹 B. New Right
🔹 C. Populism
🔹 D. Isolationism

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Reagan's policies embodied the New Right, emphasizing limited government, lower taxes, and reduced welfare—core tenets of this ideology. Progressivism and populism generally support more active government roles, while isolationism relates to foreign policy, not domestic economic choices. Recognizing the New Right's influence clarifies the ideological roots of the tax cuts and welfare rollbacks of the 1980s.

Q12. If the US were to increase its foreign aid by 10% while simultaneously cutting domestic welfare by 5%, which criticism rooted in the American economic model would be most likely voiced by neo‑conservatives?

🔹 A. It undermines national security
🔹 B. It violates rugged individualism
🔹 C. It promotes global warming
🔹 D. It encourages trade deficits

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Neo‑conservatives would argue that reducing domestic welfare while expanding foreign aid betrays the principle of rugged individualism, which stresses self‑reliance and limited government assistance at home. The other options address unrelated concerns. This response shows how domestic policy choices are evaluated against core American values of personal responsibility.

🔹 A. *Anarchy, State and Utopia*
🔹 B. *A Theory of Justice*
🔹 C. *The Road to Serfdom*
🔹 D. *Capitalism and Freedom*

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Nozick's 1974 work is *Anarchy, State and Utopia*, where he outlines his libertarian arguments. The other titles belong to different authors or years. Knowing this publication helps differentiate Nozick's libertarian perspective from Rawls's egalitarian ideas, clarifying the intellectual foundations of the New Right.

Q14. How does the idea of a 'social contract' justify government intervention in the economy?

🔹 A. By asserting that citizens consent to basic welfare provision
🔹 B. By demanding absolute freedom
🔹 C. By eliminating property rights
🔹 D. By promoting anarchism

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The social contract theory holds that individuals agree to grant the state limited powers, including providing essential welfare, in exchange for protection and order. This does not call for absolute freedom, the removal of property rights, or anarchism. Understanding this rationale explains why democratic societies accept taxes and public services as part of the mutual agreement between citizens and government.

Q15. A policy analyst argues that reducing corporate tax rates from 35% to 25% will attract $50 billion in foreign direct investment. Which assumption reflects the American model's view of taxation?

🔹 A. Taxes are revenue for welfare
🔹 B. Taxes deter investment
🔹 C. Taxes have no effect
🔹 D. Taxes are primarily for security

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The analyst assumes that lower corporate taxes make the US more attractive to investors, reflecting the belief that high taxes can discourage investment—a key tenet of the American model favoring minimal taxation to stimulate growth. The other statements mischaracterize the role of taxes in this context, showing why tax cuts are often promoted as pro‑business policies.

Q16. Why does the principle of 'property rights' underpin the American emphasis on limited government?

🔹 A. It requires extensive regulation
🔹 B. It mandates redistribution
🔹 C. It limits state interference
🔹 D. It encourages collectivism

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Strong property rights mean individuals own assets free from undue state control, which inherently restricts government power. This does not imply heavy regulation, forced redistribution, or collective ownership. By protecting private holdings, the American model justifies a limited governmental role, reinforcing the cultural value of personal autonomy and economic freedom.

Q17. A city implements a universal basic income of $1,000 per month funded by a 2% carbon tax. Which of the following best aligns with Rawlsian justice?

🔹 A. Supports fairness and redistribution
🔹 B. Violates individual liberty
🔹 C. Increases property rights
🔹 D. Reduces welfare

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Rawlsian justice emphasizes fairness and equitable distribution; a UBI financed by a carbon tax redistributes wealth to ensure a basic standard of living, matching his principles. The other options conflict with his view: it does not infringe liberty, does not focus on property rights, and certainly does not reduce welfare. This illustrates how policy design can reflect Rawlsian ideas.

Q18. What role does national security play in the American economic model's view of government functions?

🔹 A. Primary economic driver
🔹 B. Secondary to welfare
🔹 C. Core function limiting other interventions
🔹 D. Irrelevant

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The model sees the federal government's main purpose as safeguarding national security, which then constrains the extent of domestic economic regulation and welfare programs. It is not primarily an economic driver, nor is it secondary to welfare, and it is certainly not irrelevant. This priority helps explain why many Americans accept a strong defense apparatus while preferring limited domestic intervention.

Q19. If a Republican senator proposes eliminating the estate tax, arguing it protects family property, which philosophical tradition does this reflect?

🔹 A. Rawlsian
🔹 B. Nozickian
🔹 C. Marxist
🔹 D. Keynesian

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Eliminating the estate tax to preserve inherited assets aligns with Nozick's emphasis on protecting property rights and minimizing government interference. Rawlsian theory would favor the tax for redistribution, while Marxist and Keynesian perspectives focus on broader economic restructuring or fiscal policy, not solely on protecting private property. This demonstrates how contemporary policy proposals often draw directly from libertarian philosophical roots.
