What is Industrialization divide in developing countries?

Developing nations diverged significantly in their industrial progress, with some successfully building manufacturing sectors through strategic investment and export policies while others remained dependent on agriculture or raw materials, creating an internal hierarchy of development within the Global South.

Q1. If a newly industrialized country lowers its average wage from \( $5 \) per hour to \( $3 \) per hour, which immediate effect is most likely on foreign firms considering relocation?

A. Increase in product quality
B. Reduction in production costs ✅
C. Decrease in export volume
D. Increase in domestic consumption

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B because lower wages directly cut the cost of labor, which is the biggest expense for manufacturing firms. A reduction in production costs makes the location more attractive for firms looking to maximize profit. Option A is wrong; cheaper wages do not automatically improve product quality. Option C is incorrect because lower wages tend to boost export competitiveness, not reduce volume. Option D is irrelevant since the question concerns foreign firms, not domestic consumption. This aligns with how the Pacific tiger economies attracted Northern firms.

Q2. A multinational corporation receives tax incentives and low labor costs in Country X and reports a 12 % profit margin increase after five years. Which factor most directly contributed to this rise?

A. Higher wages
B. Improved technology
C. Lower production costs due to cheap labor ✅
D. Increased domestic market demand

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is C. Lower production costs from cheap labor directly raise profit margins, as labor is a major input cost. Higher wages (A) would reduce margins, not increase them. Improved technology (B) can affect productivity but the scenario highlights cost factors, not tech upgrades. Increased domestic market demand (D) might boost sales but does not explain a margin rise when the firm operates abroad. This mirrors how the tiger economies' low‑wage environments boosted foreign investors' profits.

Q3. Malaysia offers a wage of \( $2.5 \) per hour for a 40‑hour week, while the Philippines offers \( $2.2 \) per hour for a 48‑hour week. Which country is more likely to win a garment contract if the buyer prioritizes total labor cost?

A. Malaysia ✅
B. Philippines
C. Both equal
D. Neither

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is A. Total labor cost per worker is calculated as wage multiplied by hours. Malaysia’s cost is \(2.5 \times 40 = 100\) monetary units, while the Philippines’ cost is \(2.2 \times 48 = 105.6\). Therefore Malaysia offers the lower total cost, making it more likely to win the contract when the buyer focuses on labor expense. Options B and C are wrong because they ignore the cost calculation, and D is irrelevant to the specific comparison.

Q4. A developing country raises labor costs by 15 % after adopting ILO standards. Its main export has a price elasticity of demand of \(-0.8\). If the world price falls by 10 %, will the country's export revenue increase or decrease?

A. Increase
B. Decrease ✅
C. Remain unchanged
D. Cannot determine

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B. With a price elasticity of \(-0.8\), a 10 % fall in world price leads to a quantity increase of \(-0.8 \times (-10\%) = +8\%\). Revenue change equals the sum of percentage price change and quantity change: \(-10\% + 8\% = -2\%\). Hence export revenue declines despite higher volume. Option A is wrong because the negative net effect outweighs the quantity gain. Option C is incorrect because revenue does change, and D is unnecessary since the elasticity provides enough information. This reflects how higher labor standards can affect competitiveness.

Q5. A 'Pacific tiger' economy grows its GNP by 3 % annually while wages rise 1 % per year. In North America, wages grow 4 % annually. Over a five‑year horizon, which location becomes relatively cheaper for producing a widget?

A. Pacific tiger economy ✅
B. North America
C. Both equal
D. Depends on exchange rates

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is A. Over five years, wages in the Pacific tiger economy grow by \((1.01)^5 \approx 1.051\) (about 5.1 %), while North American wages grow by \((1.04)^5 \approx 1.217\) (about 21.7 %). The slower wage increase makes the tiger economy relatively cheaper for producing the widget, assuming other costs remain similar. Option B is wrong because its higher wage growth raises costs. Option C is inaccurate; the calculations show a clear difference. Option D may matter in reality, but the question isolates wage growth as the decisive factor.

Q6. What is the primary reason that the 'Pacific tiger' economies could attract manufacturing firms from the North during the 1990s?

A. Higher technology levels
B. Cheaper labor ✅
C. Larger domestic markets
D. Better infrastructure

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B. The main attraction for Northern manufacturing firms was the availability of much cheaper labor in the Pacific tiger economies, which allowed firms to lower production costs and compete on price. Higher technology levels (A) were not the primary draw at that time, and larger domestic markets (C) or superior infrastructure (D) were secondary factors. This explains why firms shifted production to these economies, creating the rapid industrialization described.

Q7. How did the emergence of lower‑cost labor markets in Indonesia and China affect the earlier 'tiger' economies?

A. Strengthened their export dominance
B. Caused them to lose some manufacturing jobs ✅
C. Had no impact
D. Led to higher wages in the tigers

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B. As Indonesia and China entered the global market with even lower labor costs, some manufacturing jobs moved from the earlier tiger economies to these newer low‑cost locations. This shift reduced the tigers’ share of certain export industries. Option A is wrong because competition weakened rather than strengthened dominance. Option C is incorrect; the emergence of cheaper competitors had a clear impact. Option D misstates the effect; wages in the tigers did not rise because of this competition.

Q8. Which of the following best describes Jacques Chirac’s concern about developing countries competing with Europe?

A. They should increase tariffs
B. They must maintain high wages and labor rights ✅
C. They should focus on agricultural exports
D. They need to adopt free‑market policies

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B. Jacques Chirac warned that developing nations should not compete by offering miserable wages and poor working conditions, emphasizing the need for basic human rights such as fair wages, collective bargaining, and the abolition of forced and child labour. Raising tariffs (A) does not address labor standards. Focusing on agricultural exports (C) misses the labor‑rights point. Adopting free‑market policies (D) is unrelated to his call for protecting workers. This reflects the ethical concerns tied to industrialization.

Q9. Which four economies are collectively known as the 'Pacific tiger' economies?

A. Singapore, Taiwan, South Korea, Hong Kong ✅
B. Japan, China, Thailand, Malaysia
C. Singapore, India, Brazil, Mexico
D. South Korea, Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is A. The four economies commonly called the 'Pacific tiger' economies are Singapore, Taiwan, South Korea, and Hong Kong. Japan and China (B) are not part of this specific grouping, nor are India, Brazil, Mexico (C), or Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand (D). Knowing these four names is essential for identifying the early success stories of rapid industrialization in East Asia.
