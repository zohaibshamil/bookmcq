What is American economic system in practice?

In practice, the American economic system blends free-market principles with selective government intervention through monetary policy, regulatory agencies, subsidies, and social programs like Social Security, demonstrating that even capitalist economies require state involvement to maintain stability and address market failures.

Q1. In what year did the United States impose import duties on foreign steel to protect its domestic industry?

A.1999
B.2002 ✅
C.2005
D.2010
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The steel tariffs were introduced by the United States in March 2002 to protect domestic producers. The correct year is 2002, while the other years are incorrect. Knowing the date helps place the policy within the Bush administration’s broader trade agenda. This temporal context shows how the measure fit into a pattern of protectionist actions that later faced WTO pressure, illustrating the cause‑and‑effect relationship between policy timing and international response.

Q2. Which satellite navigation system did the European Union develop to counter the United States’ dominance in space communications?

A.GPS
B.GLONASS
C.Galileo ✅
D.BeiDou
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The EU created the Galileo system as a strategic response to the high proportion of American satellites in orbit. Galileo is the correct answer; the other options refer to other nations’ navigation constellations. Understanding this choice highlights the EU’s desire for autonomous capabilities and reduces reliance on U.S. technology. The initiative reflects broader geopolitical competition in space, showing how satellite programs serve both civilian and strategic purposes.

Q3. Why did the United States use the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade in 1994 to open European voice communications to competition?

A.To promote free trade globally
B.To increase U.S. telecom companies’ profits ✅
C.To reduce the European Union’s security
D.To comply with United Nations mandates
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The U.S. leveraged GATT to force the EU to open its voice communications market, primarily to expand American telecom profits. The other options misrepresent the motive: free trade was not the primary goal, security concerns were not central, and UN mandates were unrelated. Recognizing the profit motive explains how trade rules can be used to serve national economic interests, illustrating a classic case of policy used for commercial advantage.

Q4. How did U.S. influence over the International Monetary Fund affect policy decisions in Latin American countries during the 1990s?

A.It provided unconditional financial aid
B.It forced adoption of policies approved by the United States ✅
C.It increased debt forgiveness for poor nations
D.It eliminated all foreign debt obligations
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: U.S. control of the IMF allowed Washington to dictate economic reforms, meaning Latin American governments had to follow American‑favored policies. The other choices are inaccurate: aid was not unconditional, debt forgiveness was not emphasized, and debts were not erased. This influence demonstrates how financial institutions can become tools for political leverage, shaping national strategies to align with donor preferences.

Q5. What was the underlying motive behind the United States overriding HIV/AIDS drug patents to allow cheaper medicines in affected African nations?

A.Gain access to African oil reserves ✅
B.Humanitarian concern for patients
C.Increase profits for U.S. pharmaceutical firms
D.Reduce domestic health‑care costs
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The official rationale cited humanitarian reasons, but the strategic goal was to secure African oil and establish military bases, making oil access the correct answer. The other options do not match the documented ulterior motive. Recognizing this hidden agenda shows how health policy can be intertwined with geopolitical objectives, illustrating the complexity of policy decisions that appear altruistic on the surface.

This opposition underscores a pattern of using financial institutions to sustain power, illustrating how economic tools can be wielded to shape international policy outcomes.

Understanding this consequence highlights how unilateral trade actions can provoke reciprocal measures, emphasizing the interconnected nature of modern trade systems.

Q9. Suppose the United States decides to reimpose steel tariffs today. Based on the 2002 example, which international response would most likely occur?

A.The European Union would impose retaliatory duties ✅
B.The World Trade Organization would immediately sanction the U.S.
C.U.S. allies would publicly support the tariffs
D.There would be no significant response from trading partners
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: History shows that similar tariffs prompted the EU to threaten and later impose retaliatory duties, making that the most probable reaction. Immediate WTO sanctions did not occur, allies did not uniformly back the move, and the lack of response is unrealistic given past trade disputes. This prediction underscores how past actions inform expectations of future diplomatic and trade dynamics.

Q10. A country dependent on U.S. satellite communications wishes to lessen that reliance. Which strategy mirrors the EU’s approach with the Galileo project?

A.Launch its own constellation of satellites ✅
B.Negotiate cheaper access with U.S. providers
C.Develop extensive fiber‑optic terrestrial networks
D.Abandon communications services entirely
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Creating an independent satellite system directly reduces dependence, just as the EU did with Galileo. Negotiating cheaper access does not provide autonomy, while fiber networks address different infrastructure, and abandoning services is impractical. This choice illustrates how strategic investment in space technology can serve national security and economic independence goals.

Q11. If U.S. IMF conditions cause an economic contraction in a Latin American nation, which macroeconomic indicator would most directly reflect this contraction?

A.Increase in GDP growth rate
B.Rise in unemployment levels ✅
C.Decrease in inflation rate
D.Rise in trade surplus
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A contraction typically leads to higher unemployment as output falls, making the rise in unemployment the most direct sign. GDP growth would likely decline, not increase; inflation could fall or rise depending on other factors; trade surplus changes are less immediate. This linkage shows how policy‑induced downturns manifest in labor market statistics, reinforcing the real‑world impact of financial conditionality.

Q12. When the United States uses its WTO influence to require 70 countries to open telecom markets, what is the most likely effect on global telecom service prices?

A.Prices fall due to increased competition ✅
B.Prices rise globally as firms gain market power
C.Prices remain unchanged because of regulation
D.Prices become subject to strict governmental control
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Opening markets introduces more competitors, driving prices down as firms vie for customers. An increase in prices or unchanged rates would contradict basic market dynamics, and strict control is not typical of WTO‑mandated liberalization. This outcome demonstrates how trade rules can reshape industry pricing structures, benefiting consumers through heightened competition.

Q13. A policy analyst claims that the 2002 U.S. steel tariffs were justified to protect national security. Which economic theory best supports this justification?

A.Comparative advantage theory
B.Infant industry argument
C.Strategic trade theory ✅
D.Heckscher‑Ohlin model
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Strategic trade theory posits that governments may protect industries critical for security or future growth, aligning with the national‑security rationale. Comparative advantage and Heckscher‑Ohlin advocate free trade, while the infant industry argument focuses on emerging sectors, not established ones like steel. This connection highlights how specific economic frameworks are invoked to legitimize protectionist measures.

B.They provide leverage for negotiation ✅
C.They force immediate compliance with WTO rules

It does not show total ineffectiveness, nor does it compel instant compliance without negotiation, and it certainly had an impact. This case exemplifies how dispute mechanisms can shape outcomes by creating economic incentives for countries to adjust policies.

Q16. When the United States overridden HIV/AIDS drug patents, leading to cheaper generics in Africa, and later gained access to African oil, which concept best describes this sequence?

A.Trade creation
B.Rent seeking
C.Conditional aid
D.Strategic reciprocity ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The pattern reflects strategic reciprocity: the U.S. offered health benefits to secure future resource access, linking two distinct policy moves. Trade creation and rent seeking do not capture the conditional exchange, and conditional aid lacks the mutual strategic element. This explanation highlights how health policy can be leveraged for broader geopolitical gains.

Q17. A developing nation is negotiating with the United States for satellite launch services. Based on the 2002 data that 174 of 180 commercial satellites were American, what bargaining power does the United States possess?

A.Low bargaining power
B.Moderate bargaining power
C.High bargaining power ✅
D.No bargaining power
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: With the overwhelming majority of commercial satellites owned by U.S. firms, the United States holds strong leverage, making high bargaining power the correct choice. Low or moderate power would underestimate its market dominance, and no power is inaccurate. This scenario shows how market share translates into negotiation strength in high‑technology sectors.

Q18. If the European Union successfully launches its Galileo system, how might this affect the market share of U.S. satellites?

B.Decrease the U.S. share from 174 to about 120 satellites ✅
C.Leave the U.S. share unchanged
D.Cause the United States to abandon space activities
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

An increase to 80% contradicts the expected competition, unchanged share ignores the new entrant, and abandoning space is unrealistic. This outcome illustrates how competition can shift market balances in the satellite industry.

Q19. How does the United States’ pattern of using trade policy to achieve geopolitical goals, such as steel tariffs and telecom access, relate to the concept of “soft power”?

A.It demonstrates pure soft power
B.It demonstrates pure hard power
C.It blends both soft and hard power elements ✅
D.It undermines the notion of soft power
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The U.S. employs economic tools (soft power) while also imposing coercive measures (hard power), creating a blend of influence strategies. Pure soft power would rely solely on attraction, and pure hard power would involve direct force; undermining soft power does not capture the dual nature. This analysis shows how trade actions can simultaneously attract and coerce, embodying a hybrid approach to international influence.
