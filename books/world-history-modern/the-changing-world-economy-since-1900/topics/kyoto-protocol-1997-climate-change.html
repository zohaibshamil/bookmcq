What is Kyoto Protocol 1997 climate change?

The 1997 Kyoto Protocol was the first major international treaty to set legally binding emission reduction targets for industrialized nations, recognizing that developed countries bore primary responsibility for historical emissions, though its effectiveness was limited by the absence of key participants like the United States.

Q1. If a country currently emits 12 tons of carbon per person per year and must achieve a 7 % reduction by 2012, what is the target emission per person?

A.12.84 tons
B.11.16 tons ✅
C.10.80 tons
D.13.00 tons
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The target is found by keeping 93 % of the original level because a 7 % cut leaves 100 % – 7 % = 93 %. Multiplying 12 tons by 0.93 gives \(12 \times 0.93 = 11.16\) tons, which matches option B. Options A and D increase the figure, and option C uses an incorrect reduction factor (0.90). This calculation shows how percentage cuts translate into concrete emission goals, a key step for any nation planning to meet Kyoto targets.

Q2. Why did the United States receive a larger percentage reduction target than France under the Kyoto commitments?

A.Because the United States had a larger land area
B.Because the United States emitted more carbon per capita than France ✅
C.Because France refused to sign the protocol
D.Because the protocol favored countries with higher GDP
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: France already relied heavily on nuclear power, so its per‑capita emissions were relatively low. The United States, however, had a much higher per‑capita emission rate, requiring a steeper cut to meet the same overall target. Option B correctly identifies the emissions gap as the reason for a larger reduction percentage, while the other options refer to unrelated factors such as land size or political decisions.

Q3. In which year was the Kyoto Protocol signed by 86 nations?

A.1997 ✅
B.1998
C.2001
D.2005
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The international agreement that set binding emission‑reduction targets was concluded in 1997, when 86 countries adopted the Kyoto Protocol. Option A states the correct year. The other years correspond to later events: 1998 was a record‑hot year, 2001 saw the US President’s rejection, and 2005 marks the entry into force of the protocol after sufficient ratifications.

Q4. Country X emits 10 tons of carbon per person per year. To meet the 1990 global average of 8.5 tons, what percentage reduction must Country X achieve?

A.0.12
B.0.15 ✅
C.0.2
D.0.25
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: To reach the 1990 benchmark, Country X must lower its emissions from 10 tons to 8.5 tons. The reduction required is \((10-8.5)/10 = 0.15\), or 15 %. Option B reflects this calculation. Option A uses a smaller fraction, while options C and D assume larger cuts than necessary. Understanding the percentage change helps policymakers set realistic national targets aligned with global baselines.

Q5. Which policy instrument most directly contributed to Japan’s relatively low per‑capita emissions before the Kyoto Conference?

A.Heavy taxation on power and petrol ✅
B.Large subsidies for wind farms
C.Mandatory vehicle retirement
D.Import bans on coal
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Japan lowered its carbon output primarily by imposing high taxes on electricity and gasoline, which discouraged the use of carbon‑intensive fuels and encouraged efficiency. This fiscal approach is captured in option A. The other options describe different policy tools that were not the main drivers of Japan’s emissions decline during that period.

Q6. The United States emitted an average of 19 tons of carbon per person. A 7 % cut is required. How many tons of carbon must each person reduce?

A.0.97 tons
B.1.12 tons
C.1.33 tons ✅
D.1.45 tons
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A 7 % reduction of 19 tons equals \(19 \times 0.07 = 1.33\) tons, which matches option C. Options A and B underestimate the reduction, while option D overestimates it. This straightforward multiplication illustrates how national targets translate into per‑capita reductions, a useful figure for assessing the scale of domestic policy changes needed.

Q7. What was the primary reason the Kyoto Protocol produced limited immediate impact on global emissions despite many signatories?

A.The protocol set unrealistically low targets
B.Major emitters such as the United States did not ratify it ✅
C.It required only voluntary reporting
D.It focused solely on deforestation
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The protocol’s effectiveness was hampered because the United States, one of the world’s largest emitters, chose not to ratify the agreement, reducing overall compliance pressure. Option B identifies this key obstacle. The other options misrepresent the protocol’s design: the targets were ambitious, reporting was mandatory for parties, and the agreement addressed a broad range of sectors, not just deforestation.

Q8. After 2003, malaria deaths increased by 20 % due to warmer climates. If the original death toll was 100 000, how many additional deaths occurred?

A.10 000
B.15 000
C.20 000 ✅
D.25 000
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A 20 % rise on a base of 100 000 deaths adds \(100\,000 \times 0.20 = 20\,000\) deaths, which is option C. Options A and B represent smaller increases, while option D is too large. Quantifying the health impact of climate change helps illustrate the human cost of rising temperatures and guides public‑health planning.

Q9. How does heavy taxation on electricity and gasoline encourage a country to lower its carbon emissions?

A.It forces companies to import more coal
B.It raises the price of carbon‑intensive energy ✅
C.It reduces government revenue for environmental programs
D.It eliminates the need for renewable energy research
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Higher taxes make electricity and gasoline more expensive, prompting consumers and businesses to seek less carbon‑intensive alternatives, improve efficiency, or shift to cleaner energy sources. Option B correctly describes this economic incentive. The other options either misstate the effect or suggest negative consequences that are not the primary mechanism for emission reductions.

Q10. The European Union averaged 8.5 tons of carbon per person. If the EU decides to cut emissions by 10 % by 2012, what will be the new average per‑person emission?

A.7.65 tons ✅
B.8.0 tons
C.8.5 tons
D.9.35 tons
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A 10 % cut reduces the average from 8.5 tons to \(8.5 \times 0.90 = 7.65\) tons, which matches option A. Options B and C either underestimate or leave the figure unchanged, while option D suggests an increase. This calculation demonstrates how percentage targets translate into concrete per‑capita values for policy planning.

Q11. What is the significance of returning global emissions to 1990 levels by 2012 in the context of climate policy?

A.It establishes a baseline that reflects pre‑industrial emissions
B.It aims to reverse the upward trend observed in the 1990s ✅
C.It guarantees that all countries will have identical per‑capita emissions
D.It eliminates the need for future international agreements
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Returning emissions to 1990 levels was intended to halt the rising trajectory of greenhouse gases that characterized the 1990s, effectively stabilizing atmospheric concentrations. Option B captures this goal. Option A confuses 1990 with pre‑industrial baselines, while options C and D overstate the uniformity and permanence that the protocol did not provide.
