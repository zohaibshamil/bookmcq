What is Brandt Report 1980 North South divide?

The 1980 Brandt Report highlighted the stark economic inequality between the wealthy industrialized Global North and the poorer developing Global South, arguing that this divide threatened global stability and calling for increased aid, fairer trade, and debt relief to bridge the gap.

Q1. Who chaired the 1980 international report on global poverty?

A. Edward Heath
B. Willi Brandt ✅
C. Margaret Thatcher
D. Nelson Mandela

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Willi Brandt was the chairman of the 1980 report that examined worldwide poverty. The other listed figures were prominent leaders, but only Brandt led the specific initiative. Recognizing the chairperson helps students link the report to its political origins and understand how leadership can shape development agendas. This knowledge is useful when evaluating who influences major policy documents today.

Q2. What primary factor explains why the North's GNP per capita was far higher than the South's in the early 1990s?

A. Higher agricultural output
B. Greater industrialization and technology ✅
C. Larger land area
D. More natural resources

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Industrialization and advanced technology generate high‑value goods and services, raising average income per person. Agriculture alone cannot produce the same monetary output, and land size or resource abundance does not guarantee wealth without productive use. Understanding this causal link clarifies why the North stayed richer, guiding analysts to focus on industrial policy when addressing economic gaps.

Q3. How does the disparity in calorie intake per person relate to overall health outcomes in developing nations?

A. No relation
B. Higher calories cause obesity
C. Low calories lead to malnutrition and higher death rates ✅
D. Calorie intake only affects economic growth

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When people regularly consume fewer calories than needed, especially lacking proteins and vitamins, they suffer from malnutrition, weakened immunity, and increased mortality. The other options misinterpret the relationship: excess calories can cause obesity, but the key issue in many poor regions is insufficient intake, directly influencing health statistics and policy priorities.

Q4. Why did the Brandt Report conclude that the North was getting richer while the South was getting poorer, despite some South countries showing economic growth?

A. Population growth outpaced income growth in the South ✅
B. Exchange rate fluctuations
C. Decrease in agricultural productivity worldwide
D. Increase in foreign aid

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Rapid population growth in many Southern nations diluted per‑capita gains, so even when total output rose, average wealth fell relative to the North. Exchange rates and aid affect finances but do not explain the systematic widening gap. Recognizing the demographic pressure helps policymakers design interventions that target both income and population control to close the disparity.

Q5. Country X has a GNP per capita of \(2,500\) USD, while Country Y has \(20,000\) USD. If both countries aim to raise average calorie intake by 10%, which country would need a larger absolute increase in food imports, assuming equal population?

A. Country X
B. Country Y ✅
C. Both equal
D. Cannot determine

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The absolute amount of food needed depends on the size of the population, not on GNP. Since the populations are equal, the richer Country Y will have to import more calories to achieve the same 10% rise because its baseline consumption is higher. The other options ignore the baseline difference or claim insufficient data, which is inaccurate given the equal‑population assumption.

Q6. A development agency can allocate $5 million to either improve protein intake in Country A (population 10 million) or increase overall calories in Country B (population 2 million). Which allocation yields a higher per‑capita nutritional gain if the cost per person is the same?

A. Country A
B. Country B
C. Both equal ✅
D. Need more data

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When the cost per individual is identical, each dollar spent benefits the same number of people regardless of total population. Thus the per‑capita nutritional improvement will be the same for both countries. The other choices incorrectly assume that larger populations automatically receive more benefit per person or that additional information is required.

Q7. If the average GNP per capita in the South rises from \$300 to \$600 over a decade, but the population grows from 1 billion to 1.5 billion, what happens to total GNP?

A. Doubles
B. Increases by 50%
C. Remains the same
D. Triples ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Initial total GNP = 300 × 1 billion = 300 billion. New total = 600 × 1.5 billion = 900 billion, which is three times the original amount. The other options underestimate or misstate the change. Recognizing how simultaneous income and population growth multiply total output is essential for evaluating economic progress in rapidly growing regions.

Q8. Suppose a South country improves its GNP per capita from \$400 to \$800 while its calorie intake rises from 2,000 kcal/day to 2,200 kcal/day. If a neighboring North country has GNP per capita \$20,000 and calorie intake 3,200 kcal/day, calculate the ratio of GNP per capita to calorie intake for each country and determine which has a higher productivity index.

A. South higher
B. North higher ✅
C. Both equal
D. Cannot compute without more data

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

South ratio = 800 / 2,200 ≈ 0.36; North ratio = 20,000 / 3,200 ≈ 6.25. The North's ratio is far larger, indicating more economic output per calorie consumed. The other answers either ignore the calculation or claim insufficient data, which is unnecessary because all required numbers are provided.

Q9. If a family in a South country consumes 1,800 kcal per day and a family in a North country consumes 3,200 kcal per day, how many more calories does the North family consume each day?

A. 1200 ✅
B. 1400
C. 1600
D. 2000

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Subtracting the South family's intake from the North family's intake gives 3,200 − 1,800 = 1,200 kcal. The other options add extra numbers or assume different baselines. Knowing the simple difference helps students grasp the magnitude of nutritional inequality between regions.

Q10. A policy analyst argues that reducing the North–South GNP gap by 10% requires the South to increase its per‑capita GNP by \$2,000 while the North reduces its per‑capita GNP by \$1,000. If the current average GNP per capita is \$25,000 for the North and \$500 for the South, does this policy achieve the desired 10% reduction?

A. Yes ✅
B. No, because South increase insufficient
C. No, because North reduction insufficient
D. Cannot tell without population data

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Current gap = 25,000 − 500 = 24,500. Desired 10% reduction = 2,450. New gap after changes = (25,000 − 1,000) − (500 + 2,000) = 24,000 − 2,500 = 21,500, a reduction of 3,000 (> 2,450). Therefore the proposal exceeds the target, making the answer Yes. The other choices misinterpret the arithmetic or request unnecessary data.

Q11. What role does GNP per capita play in assessing economic development disparities between regions?

A. Measures total population
B. Indicates average wealth per person ✅
C. Shows trade balance
D. Reflects only export volumes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

GNP per capita divides a country's total economic output by its population, giving a clear picture of average wealth and living standards. It does not count people, trade balances, or export figures alone. Understanding this metric lets analysts compare how prosperous different regions are on a per‑person basis, which is central to evaluating development gaps.

Q12. Why might calorie intake per person be a more immediate indicator of poverty than GNP per capita in some Third World contexts?

A. Food consumption directly impacts health ✅
B. GNP per capita is unrelated to living standards
C. Calories are easier to measure
D. GNP per capita always overestimates poverty

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Low calorie intake quickly translates into malnutrition, disease, and higher mortality, which are tangible signs of poverty. While GNP per capita reflects economic potential, it may not capture how that wealth translates into everyday food security. The other statements misrepresent the relationship between income and well‑being, making the health‑focused answer the most accurate.

Q13. Which of the following best describes the 'North–South divide' as used in development studies?

A. Geographic latitude differences
B. Economic disparity between industrialized and developing regions ✅
C. Political alliance split
D. Climate variation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The term refers to the economic and development gap between the wealthy, industrialized nations of the North and the poorer, often agrarian nations of the South. It is not about latitude, politics, or climate. Recognizing this definition helps students discuss global inequality without confusing it with other geographic or political concepts.

Q14. An NGO plans to allocate resources to improve protein intake in two countries. Country M has a GNP per capita of \$150 and average protein consumption of 30 g/day. Country N has a GNP per capita of \$1,200 and protein consumption of 55 g/day. Which country shows a larger gap between income and protein intake, suggesting higher marginal benefit from intervention?

A. Country M ✅
B. Country N
C. Both equal
D. Insufficient data

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Compute protein per dollar: M = 30 g / 150 = 0.20 g per dollar; N = 55 g / 1,200 ≈ 0.046 g per dollar. The lower ratio in Country N indicates less efficient conversion of income into protein, but the larger absolute shortfall relative to income is in Country M, implying a greater potential gain from aid. The other answers ignore the ratio analysis or claim missing data.

Q15. If the average GNP per capita in a South region rises by 5% annually for five years, what is the approximate cumulative increase? (Assume compounding.)

A. 0.25
B. 0.276 ✅
C. 0.3
D. 0.33

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Compounding 5% over five years gives (1.05)^5 ≈ 1.276, which is a 27.6% increase. The other percentages either ignore compounding or use linear addition. Understanding compound growth is vital for projecting economic trends, especially when evaluating how quickly developing regions can narrow the North–South gap.
