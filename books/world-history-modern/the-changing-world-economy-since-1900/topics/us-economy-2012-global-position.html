Q1. If a fiscal stimulus increased GDP by 4%4\%4% over two years and the unemployment rate fell from 15% to 13%, which factor best explains the modest reduction in unemployment?

A. The stimulus only boosted supply‑side productivity
B. GDP growth does not automatically create enough jobs to match the drop in unemployment ✅
C. Unemployment statistics are delayed and do not reflect immediate changes
D. The stimulus caused only inflation without real job gains

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

GDP can rise without a proportional rise in employment because businesses may adopt more efficient technologies that require fewer workers. In this case the 4 % boost in output likely reflected higher productivity rather than a large hiring spree, so the unemployment rate only fell modestly. Option B captures this mismatch; the other options misstate the role of supply‑side effects, timing of data, or confuse growth with inflation.

Q2. Why would ending tax breaks for the very wealthy be expected to reduce income inequality in the United States?

A. It would lower the overall tax burden on all citizens
B. It would shift a larger share of taxes onto high‑earners, narrowing the after‑tax income gap
C. It would increase government spending on social programs directly ✅
D. It would encourage the wealthy to invest more in the stock market

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Progressive tax policy works by taking a higher percentage of income from those who earn more, thereby compressing the distribution of after‑tax earnings. When tax breaks for the richest are removed, their effective tax rate rises, which reduces the gap between rich and poor. Option C correctly identifies this mechanism; the other choices either describe the opposite effect or unrelated fiscal actions.

Q3. House prices fell 10 % in real terms over two years while mortgage rates stayed low. Which policy would most directly help first‑time homebuyers enter the market?

A. Raise the federal funds rate to increase borrowing costs
B. Offer a targeted tax credit for first‑time buyers ✅
C. Increase subsidies for large‑scale construction projects
D. Reduce property tax rates for existing homes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A tax credit for first‑time buyers lowers the effective purchase price, making homes affordable even when prices are depressed. Raising rates would worsen affordability, construction subsidies address supply but not immediate buyer costs, and lower property taxes help owners rather than new entrants. Therefore option B directly tackles the affordability barrier faced by new buyers.

A. 2010
B. 2011
C. 2012
D. 2013 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

This timing is central to debates about budget tightening at the close of that year. Options A and B are too early, while option D occurs after the scheduled deadline, leaving option C as the only correct year.

Options A, C, and D use incorrect time frames, so they do not match the official definition.

Q7. Why can real personal incomes fall even when GDP is growing?

A. GDP growth is often concentrated in high‑profit sectors that do not raise wages for most workers ✅
B. Inflation automatically reduces the purchasing power of all incomes
C. Tax cuts reduce disposable income for everyone
D. Government spending cuts directly lower personal income levels

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When GDP expands, the gains may accrue mainly to capital owners or high‑skill industries, leaving average workers with stagnant or declining wages. Consequently, real personal income—adjusted for inflation—can drop despite overall economic growth. Options B, C, and D describe mechanisms that either affect all incomes uniformly or are not directly linked to the observed divergence, making option A the most accurate explanation.

Q8. If gasoline prices rise by 20 %, which of the following outcomes is most likely in the United States?

A. Vehicle miles traveled will increase as people drive more to avoid higher costs later
B. Public transit usage will decline because people prefer cheaper personal vehicles
C. More consumers will shift toward electric or fuel‑efficient vehicles and increase public‑transport use
D. Energy consumption will stay unchanged because price changes have no effect ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A substantial fuel price increase creates incentives for multiple behavioral changes: drivers cut mileage, many turn to public transportation, and consumers accelerate purchases of electric or more efficient cars. The combined effect is a broad reduction in gasoline consumption, making option D, which captures the overall downward pressure on energy use, the most comprehensive answer. The other choices isolate only one effect or predict the opposite reaction.

Q9. Why does the United States rank higher than most OECD members in income inequality despite its overall wealth?

A. Because the U.S. has higher education costs that limit upward mobility
B. Because the U.S. provides a smaller social safety net compared with other OECD nations
C. Because cultural preferences in the U.S. favor wealth accumulation
D. Because wealth is more concentrated and the tax system is less progressive than in many OECD countries ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The United States exhibits a pronounced concentration of wealth among top earners and a tax structure that is less progressive than many peer economies, leading to a wider gap between rich and poor. While education costs and safety‑net differences play roles, they are secondary to the core issue of wealth concentration and tax policy. Option D directly addresses these structural factors; the other options either misplace the cause or oversimplify cultural influences.
