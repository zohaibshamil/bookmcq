📖 From World History: Modern • 27. The Changing World Economy Since 1900 • 6 questions available

What is Oil wealth and division in Third World?

Oil-rich developing nations experienced vastly different economic trajectories than their non-oil counterparts, with petroleum revenues creating wealth for some but also causing corruption, conflict, and economic imbalance, thereby deepening divisions within the Third World based on natural resource endowments.

Q1. In which year did Colonel Gaddafi take power in Libya?

A. 1965 ✅
B. 1967
C. 1969
D. 1971

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is 1969 because Colonel Gaddafi seized power in that year, marking start of his rule. Option A (1965) and B (1967) are earlier and incorrect; D (1971) is after the actual coup. Knowing the correct year helps place Libya’s oil‑driven development timeline, showing that oil profits could be used for modernization only after Gaddafi assumed leadership. This date is a key reference for studying resource‑driven economic change.

Q2. What was the primary motive behind OPEC's decision to raise oil prices in 1973?

A. To conserve oil supplies
B. To increase revenue for member states ✅
C. To punish non‑OPEC countries
D. To fund renewable energy projects

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B because OPEC aimed to capture a larger share of the global oil market value, boosting income for its members after the 1973 oil crisis. Option A is wrong; price hikes do not directly conserve supply. Option C misrepresents intent; OPEC sought profit, not retaliation. Option D is inaccurate; the revenues were used for national development, not renewable projects. Understanding this motive clarifies how oil wealth enabled countries like Libya to fund welfare programs.

Q3. If a newly oil‑rich country adopts Libya’s strategy of allocating a large portion of oil profits to agricultural and industrial development, which short‑term economic effect is most likely?

A. Rapid growth in non‑oil sectors ✅
B. Immediate increase in consumer spending only
C. Decline in oil production
D. Surge in foreign debt

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is A because investing oil revenues in agriculture and industry creates jobs, improves infrastructure, and diversifies the economy, leading to quick expansion of non‑oil sectors. Option B is partially true but focuses only on consumption, neglecting productive investment. Option C is false; oil output would not drop merely because profits are reinvested. Option D is incorrect; using oil money for development reduces the need for borrowing, not increases debt. This scenario shows how strategic reinvestment can transform resource wealth into broader economic growth.

Q4. Suppose OPEC had not increased oil prices in 1973. How would that have likely impacted Libya’s GNP in 1989 compared to Greece?

A. Libya’s GNP would be higher than Greece
B. Libya’s GNP would be roughly equal to Greece
C. Libya’s GNP would be lower than Greece ✅
D. No effect on Libya’s GNP

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is C because without the 1973 price hike, Libya would have earned less from oil, limiting the funds available for its development projects and keeping its per‑capita GNP below that of Greece. Option A is wrong; higher GNP would require higher revenues, not lower. Option B is unlikely because the revenue gap would still exist. Option D is incorrect; oil price changes directly affect national income. This analysis illustrates how global oil pricing decisions can shape long‑term economic standings of oil‑exporting nations.

Q5. What role did Libya’s use of oil revenues for welfare and development play in its economic comparison with the poorest members of the European Community?

A. Oil profits allowed Libya to surpass all EC members
B. Investment in welfare reduced income inequality making GNP comparable to Greece and Portugal ✅
C. Oil revenue had no impact on GNP
D. Libya relied solely on foreign aid

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B because Libya channeled oil earnings into agriculture, industry, and social programs, which lifted living standards and narrowed the gap with the poorest EC nations, reflected in a GNP of \(5460\) pounds in 1989. Option A exaggerates; Libya did not exceed every EC country. Option C contradicts evidence that oil revenue drove growth. Option D ignores the domestic use of oil funds and overstates dependence on aid. Recognizing this link shows how resource wealth, when managed wisely, can produce measurable economic gains comparable to more diversified economies.

Q6. A developing country plans to allocate 30% of its oil profits to infrastructure. Based on Libya’s experience, which potential challenge should policymakers be most wary of?

A. Overreliance on oil leading to volatility
B. Corruption and misallocation of funds ✅
C. Insufficient infrastructure to absorb funds
D. International sanctions

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B because Libya’s history shows that even with large oil revenues, poor governance can divert money away from productive projects, limiting long‑term benefits. Option A describes a generic risk but does not directly reflect the specific challenge of ensuring funds are spent wisely. Option C is less likely; the problem is not a lack of capacity but how that capacity is utilized. Option D is unrelated to internal policy decisions. This answer highlights that transparent management of oil income is essential to avoid the pitfalls that have hindered many resource‑rich nations.
