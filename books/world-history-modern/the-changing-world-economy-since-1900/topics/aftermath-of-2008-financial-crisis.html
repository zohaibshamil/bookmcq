What is Aftermath of 2008 financial crisis?

The aftermath of the 2008 crisis included massive government bailouts, unprecedented monetary stimulus, new financial regulations like Dodd-Frank, prolonged economic recovery, rising national debts, austerity measures in Europe, and a fundamental reshaping of global banking practices and public attitudes toward capitalism.

Q1. Who was the U.S. president when the $787 billion fiscal stimulus was implemented?

A.George W. Bush
B.Barack Obama ✅
C.Bill Clinton
D.Donald Trump
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Barack Obama is the correct answer because the stimulus was enacted after he took office in January 2009. George W. Bush left office in 2009, before the stimulus. Bill Clinton and Donald Trump served at different times, so they could not have authorized this specific package. Knowing the president links the policy to the political context of the crisis, reinforcing the timeline of events.

Q3. Why does a drying up of the money supply typically lead to higher unemployment?

A.Reduced consumer spending lowers demand for labor ✅
B.Increased investment creates more jobs
C.Higher wages attract workers
D.Lower taxes boost hiring
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When the money supply contracts, households have less cash to spend, causing demand for goods and services to fall. Businesses respond by cutting production and laying off workers, which raises unemployment. Increased investment, higher wages, or lower taxes would generally stimulate hiring, not reduce it. This relationship shows how monetary contraction can directly affect the labor market during a recession.

Q5. What is the primary risk associated with massive state‑guaranteed bank loans during a crisis?

A.Moral hazard ✅
B.Inflation
C.Deflation
D.Currency appreciation
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: State guarantees can encourage banks and borrowers to take excessive risks, knowing losses will be covered—this is moral hazard. Inflation, deflation, and currency appreciation are macro‑economic outcomes that may arise from other policies but are not direct consequences of guaranteed lending. Recognizing moral hazard explains why policymakers worry about long‑term financial stability after large emergency loan programs.

Q6. How did the Maastricht Agreement affect EU member states' ability to respond to the crisis?

A.It limited their borrowing capacity ✅
B.It encouraged borrowing
C.It had no effect
D.It required austerity measures
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Maastricht Agreement set fiscal rules, including limits on budget deficits and public debt, which constrained how much each member could borrow. During the crisis many countries needed to exceed these limits to fund stimulus, creating tension between rule compliance and economic recovery. The other options mischaracterize the agreement, which was designed to enforce fiscal discipline rather than promote borrowing or austerity directly.

Q7. Why did near‑zero interest rates fail to stimulate investment during the downturn?

A.Uncertainty and low confidence discouraged spending ✅
B.High taxes reduced profitability
C.Currency controls limited capital flow
D.Overproduction made new investment unnecessary
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Even with cheap borrowing costs, firms were hesitant to invest because they were unsure about future demand and profitability. High taxes, currency controls, or overproduction could also affect investment, but the dominant factor in a deep recession is confidence. This explains why monetary policy alone was insufficient and why fiscal measures were introduced to revive spending.

Q8. What role does the unemployment rate play in assessing the depth of a recession?

A.It signals labor market slack and economic weakness ✅
B.It directly measures inflation trends
C.It determines exchange‑rate movements
D.It reflects the effectiveness of monetary policy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A rising unemployment rate indicates that many workers have been laid off, reflecting reduced output and demand—key signs of a deep recession. While unemployment can influence inflation, exchange rates, and monetary policy, its primary use is as a labor‑market indicator. Understanding this helps policymakers gauge how severe the downturn is and whether additional stimulus is needed.

Q9. If a country experiences a 15 % GDP drop and unemployment rises to 12 %, which policy is most likely to restore growth?

A.Large fiscal stimulus ✅
B.Tight monetary policy
C.Increase tariffs on imports
D.Reduce government spending
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A large fiscal stimulus injects demand directly into the economy, counteracting the sharp GDP contraction and high unemployment. Tight monetary policy would further restrict credit, raising tariffs would hurt trade, and cutting spending would deepen the decline. Hence, aggressive government spending is the most effective tool to jump‑start growth after such a severe shock.

Q12. A U.S. firm postpones investment despite near‑zero interest rates. Which macro concept best explains this behavior?

A.Liquidity trap
B.Expectation of future demand ✅
C.Phillips curve
D.Purchasing power parity
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When interest rates are at or near zero, monetary policy loses its ability to encourage borrowing, creating a liquidity trap where excess cash does not translate into investment. The firm's hesitation reflects broader uncertainty about future demand, not the Phillips curve (inflation‑unemployment trade‑off) or purchasing power parity (exchange‑rate theory). Recognizing a liquidity trap clarifies why fiscal measures were needed.

Q14. If a European country reduces its borrowing from 8 % to 5 % of GDP after a 3 % GDP decline, how does this affect its Maastricht compliance?

A.Improves compliance ✅
B.Worsens compliance
C.Has no effect
D.Triggers default
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Lowering the debt‑to‑GDP ratio brings the country closer to the Maastricht limits on public debt, thereby improving compliance. Reducing borrowing does not worsen the situation, nor does it automatically cause default. This illustrates how fiscal tightening can help nations meet EU fiscal rules after a recession, even though it may limit short‑term stimulus.

A.0.09 ✅
B.0.1
C.0.08

Q16. A nation's exports fall by 20 % and exports account for 30 % of its GDP. What is the estimated impact on overall GDP?

A.6 % decline ✅
B.20 % decline
C.30 % decline
D.0 % change
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The effect on total GDP is the product of the export share and the export decline: 0.30 × 0.20 = 0.06, or a 6 % reduction. The other options either ignore the weighting or assume the entire economy is export‑dependent. This illustrates how sectoral shocks translate into broader economic impacts.
