Q1. In which year did China and India contribute tens of billions of dollars to the IMF emergency fund for the European Union?

A. 2009
B. 2010
C. 2012 ✅
D. 2014

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The contribution occurred in 2012, making that the correct year. 2009 and 2010 are too early, as the eurozone crisis intensified later, and 2014 is after the initial emergency response. Knowing the exact year helps place the event in the broader timeline of global financial cooperation. This date shows how China began playing a larger role in international financial stability during the early 2010s.

Q2. What primary risk arises when a country maintains total investment at roughly 49%49\%49% of its GDP for several years?

A. Overcapacity in infrastructure and housing ✅
B. Significant increase in export earnings
C. Lower inflation rates
D. Higher consumer spending

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Keeping investment at almost half of GDP can create more buildings, roads, and facilities than the economy can absorb, leading to overcapacity. An overbuilt sector can stall, cause price drops, and burden the government with under‑used assets. Higher export earnings, lower inflation, or higher consumer spending are not direct risks of such a high investment share; they are possible outcomes but not the main concern.

Q3. If China’s export revenues fall by 10%10\%10% while investment remains at 49%49\%49% of GDP, what is the most likely effect on economic growth?

A. Growth will accelerate
B. Growth will stagnate
C. Growth will experience a modest decline ✅
D. Growth will cause rapid inflation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A drop in export income reduces a key source of foreign exchange and demand, while maintaining a very high investment level does not automatically offset the loss. Therefore growth is expected to decline modestly, not accelerate or stagnate completely. Rapid inflation is unlikely because reduced export earnings typically dampen demand rather than push prices up.

Q4. How does shifting from an export‑led growth model to one focused on domestic consumption affect a country's vulnerability to global financial crises?

A. It increases vulnerability because domestic markets are less stable
B. It reduces vulnerability by relying less on external demand ✅
C. It has no impact on vulnerability
D. It makes the economy completely immune to external shocks

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Moving toward domestic consumption lessens dependence on foreign markets, so a downturn abroad hurts less. This reduces, not increases, vulnerability. The shift does not render the economy immune, as other factors can still cause instability, nor does it leave vulnerability unchanged because external demand is a major growth driver in an export‑led model.

Q5. A developing nation mimics China’s 2008‑2011 investment surge, reaching 48%48\%48% of GDP, while its housing market shows clear signs of oversupply. Which policy is most prudent?

A. Continue high‑level investment to sustain growth
B. Reduce investment to prevent a bubble burst ✅
C. Increase export incentives to balance the economy
D. Lower taxes to stimulate consumer spending

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

With an oversupplied housing market, maintaining such a high investment rate risks inflating a construction bubble, similar to past crises in other countries. Cutting back investment helps avoid a sudden collapse and aligns resources with actual demand. Raising export incentives or lowering taxes may not address the core oversupply problem, and continuing investment could exacerbate the bubble.

Q6. Why is an unsustainable building bubble, similar to those that burst in the USA, Spain, and Portugal, a concern for policymakers?

A. It signals excess supply that can lead to falling property prices ✅
B. It guarantees long‑term economic growth
C. It attracts more foreign direct investment
D. It reduces unemployment permanently

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A building bubble means too many structures relative to demand, which can cause property values to plummet when the market corrects. This can undermine financial stability and erode household wealth. The other options are inaccurate: a bubble does not ensure growth, does not necessarily draw foreign investment, and does not provide lasting employment benefits.
