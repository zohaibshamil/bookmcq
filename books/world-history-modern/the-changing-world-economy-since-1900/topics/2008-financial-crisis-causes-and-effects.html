What is 2008 financial crisis causes and effects?

The 2008 financial crisis resulted from risky mortgage lending, excessive leverage, complex derivatives, and regulatory failures, triggering a global recession marked by bank collapses, mass unemployment, housing market crashes, and lasting damage to public trust in financial institutions and government oversight.

📝 All 2008 financial crisis causes and effects MCQs

Q1. Who was the Chairman of the Federal Reserve who gave the June 2007 speech about the financial system?

A.Ben S. Bernanke
B.Alan Greenspan ✅
C.Janet Yellen
D.Paul Volcker
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is Ben S. Bernanke because he was the Fed chair at that time and delivered the speech. Alan Greenspan, Janet Yellen, and Paul Volcker held the position earlier or later, so they are incorrect. Knowing the chair helps understand who guided monetary policy before the crisis, linking leadership to later decisions.

Q2. Which investment bank was taken over by J.P. Morgan with Federal Reserve assistance in March 2008?

A.Lehman Brothers
B.Bear Stearns ✅
C.Merrill Lynch
D.Goldman Sachs
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Bear Stearns is the correct answer because it was the bank that faced a liquidity crisis and was acquired by J.P. Morgan using Fed funds. Lehman Brothers collapsed later without a rescue, Merrill Lynch was bought by Bank of America, and Goldman Sachs survived without such a takeover. Recognizing this event shows how a specific bank’s failure prompted emergency action.

Q3. If a bank with $220 billion in bad debts is left to fail, what is the most likely effect on credit markets?

A.Expansion of credit
B.Slight tightening of credit
C.Severe tightening of credit ✅
D.No change in credit conditions
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Severe tightening of credit is correct because massive losses erode confidence, leading lenders to restrict new loans. Slight tightening and expansion are unlikely given the scale of the problem, and “no change” ignores typical market reactions to large defaults. This illustrates how a single large failure can ripple through the broader economy, constraining borrowing.

Q4. If a government adopts a policy that no bank is “too big to fail,” how would that most likely affect risk‑taking by large banks?

A.It would increase risk‑taking
B.It would reduce risk‑taking ✅
C.It would have no effect
D.It would encourage more leverage
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Reducing risk‑taking is correct because banks would know they cannot rely on bailouts, prompting more prudent behavior. Increasing risk, no effect, or encouraging leverage contradict the incentive structure created by a no‑bailout rule. This scenario shows how policy can shape corporate behavior and potentially lower systemic risk.

Q5. A country experiencing a housing bubble similar to the U.S. pre‑2008 should implement which regulatory measure to most directly prevent a comparable crash?

A.Lowering interest rates
B.Stricter mortgage‑lending standards ✅
C.Implementing tax cuts
D.Increasing the size of banks
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Stricter mortgage‑lending standards is correct because they limit risky loans that often trigger defaults. Lower rates encourage borrowing, tax cuts affect fiscal policy, and larger banks do not address loan quality. By tightening underwriting rules, regulators can curb the formation of unsustainable home‑price growth, directly targeting the bubble’s root cause.

Q6. An investor assumes a major bank will always be rescued. How does moral hazard influence his portfolio choice?

A.He avoids risky assets
B.He favors higher‑yield risky assets ✅
C.He chooses only government bonds
D.He increases cash holdings
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Favoring higher‑yield risky assets is correct because the expectation of a rescue reduces perceived downside, encouraging riskier investments. Avoiding risk, choosing only safe bonds, or hoarding cash would be opposite reactions. This illustrates moral hazard: the safety net changes behavior, leading investors to seek greater returns despite higher underlying risk.

Q7. During a crisis, the Fed provides liquidity to a failing bank. What is the immediate impact on that bank’s short‑term funding?

A.It improves liquidity, preventing immediate default ✅
B.It increases the bank’s default risk
C.It reduces the bank’s capital ratios
D.It triggers immediate bankruptcy
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Improving liquidity is correct because fresh funds allow the bank to meet short‑term obligations and avoid collapse. Increasing default risk, reducing capital, or causing bankruptcy are opposite outcomes that would worsen the situation. This immediate effect stabilizes the institution, buying time for longer‑term solutions and limiting panic in the financial system.

Q8. If the Fed’s indirect bailout of Bear Stearns created moral hazard, which outcome would most likely appear in subsequent years?

A.Other banks reduce risk dramatically
B.Other banks take greater risks expecting bailouts ✅
C.No change in banks’ risk profiles
D.All banks exit the market
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Other banks taking greater risks is correct because they may infer that future troubles will be rescued, encouraging riskier behavior. Reducing risk or no change contradict the hazard concept, and exiting the market is unrealistic. This scenario demonstrates how a single rescue can alter industry incentives, potentially sowing seeds for future instability.

Q9. Imagine a bank loses \(1.6\) billion from hedge‑fund failures and \(220\) billion from bad debts. What ratio of bad‑debt loss to hedge‑fund loss does this represent?

A.\(137.5\) to 1 ✅
B.\(1.4\) to 1
C.\(2200\) to 1
D.\(10\) to 1
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: \(137.5\) to 1 is correct because dividing \(220\) by \(1.6\) yields \(137.5\). The other ratios are mathematically inconsistent with the given figures. Understanding this magnitude highlights how bad‑debt losses dwarf other losses, emphasizing why the bank’s solvency was severely threatened.

Q10. A policymaker argues that bailouts undermine market discipline. Which economic principle best supports this view?

A.Comparative advantage
B.Moral hazard
C.Opportunity cost
D.Law of supply and demand ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Moral hazard is correct because it describes how safety nets encourage risky behavior, weakening discipline. Comparative advantage, opportunity cost, and supply‑demand law relate to different concepts and do not explain the erosion of market checks. Recognizing moral hazard helps explain why policymakers worry that rescues may create future vulnerabilities.

Q11. A government imposes a “living‑will” requirement on large banks. What is the primary goal of this regulation?

A.To increase bank profits
B.To ensure an orderly resolution without taxpayer funds ✅
C.To reduce taxes on banks
D.To expand market share of banks
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Ensuring an orderly resolution without taxpayer funds is correct; living wills outline how a bank can be wound down safely, limiting the need for public bailouts. Increasing profits, reducing taxes, or expanding market share are unrelated objectives. This regulation aims to protect the public finances while preserving confidence in the banking system.

Q12. After a major bank’s collapse, credit spreads widen dramatically. What does this signal about investor perception?

A.Decreased perceived risk
B.Increased perceived risk ✅
C.A stable economic outlook
D.Higher inflation expectations
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Increased perceived risk is correct because wider spreads reflect higher compensation demanded for lending, indicating investors view the market as riskier. Decreased risk or stability would narrow spreads, while inflation expectations affect yields differently. The widening spreads illustrate how a single failure can reshape market risk assessments.

Q13. If a central bank raises the discount rate during a financial crisis, what is the likely effect on banks’ willingness to borrow from the Fed?

A.Banks will increase borrowing
B.Banks will decrease borrowing ✅
C.There will be no effect on borrowing
D.Banks will be forced to lend more
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Banks decreasing borrowing is correct because a higher discount rate makes borrowing more expensive, discouraging reliance on the central bank for liquidity. An increase in borrowing or no effect would contradict basic cost‑of‑funds logic, and being forced to lend more is unrelated. This illustrates how monetary policy tools can influence bank behavior during stress.

Q14. Why did the belief that no crisis could happen contribute to the 2008 crash?

A.It encouraged complacency and lax risk controls ✅
B.It strengthened financial regulation
C.It improved overall liquidity
D.It reduced loan demand
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Encouraging complacency is correct; when decision‑makers think a crisis impossible, they may relax oversight, allowing risky practices to grow unchecked. Strengthening regulation, improving liquidity, or reducing loan demand would mitigate rather than exacerbate problems. This shows how overconfidence can erode safeguards, setting the stage for systemic failure.

Q15. How does diversification of risk domestically and globally affect financial‑system resilience?

A.It concentrates risk in one area
B.It spreads risk, reducing systemic impact ✅
C.It eliminates all risk
D.It increases market volatility
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Spreading risk is correct because diversification lessens the chance that a single shock will cripple the whole system. Concentrating risk, eliminating risk entirely, or increasing volatility do not capture the protective effect of diversification. This concept explains why a broad risk pool can help absorb shocks during crises.

Q16. What is the main reason governments intervene to prevent the collapse of a systemically important bank?

A.To avoid contagion across the financial system ✅
B.To increase bank profits
C.To lower corporate taxes
D.To promote competition among banks
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Avoiding contagion is correct because the failure of a large institution can trigger a chain reaction, threatening the entire economy. Raising profits, lowering taxes, or fostering competition are not primary motives for emergency intervention. This rationale underpins many rescue decisions during the 2008 crisis.

Q17. How does moral hazard arise from the expectation of government bailouts?

A.Investors take less risk
B.Investors take more risk expecting a safety net ✅
C.Banks become smaller
D.Markets become more stable
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Investors taking more risk is correct; when they anticipate a rescue, the perceived downside diminishes, encouraging riskier behavior. Taking less risk, shrinking banks, or stabilizing markets contradict the incentive distortion created by bailouts. This explains why policymakers worry about long‑term effects of rescue policies.

Q18. In what way did the loss of \(1.6\) billion from hedge‑fund failures exacerbate Bear Stearns’ problems?

A.It signaled liquidity strain and eroded confidence ✅
B.It improved the bank’s confidence
C.It reduced the bank’s total debt
D.It increased the bank’s capital reserves
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Signaling liquidity strain is correct because the loss reduced cash availability and shook investor trust, worsening the crisis. Improving confidence, reducing debt, or increasing capital are opposite effects that did not occur. This illustrates how even relatively smaller losses can trigger broader panic when combined with larger issues.

Q19. What role did the Federal Reserve’s provision of funds to J.P. Morgan play in stabilizing the financial system?

A.It provided liquidity enabling the acquisition ✅
B.It took over the bank completely
C.It closed the bank permanently
D.It ignored the problem entirely
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Providing liquidity is correct; the Fed’s funds allowed J.P. Morgan to purchase Bear Stearns, preventing a disorderly collapse. Taking over the bank, closing it, or ignoring it would have deepened turmoil. This demonstrates how targeted central‑bank assistance can preserve market confidence.

Q20. How did the concept of “too big to fail” influence regulatory policy after 2008?

A.It led to stronger oversight and higher capital requirements ✅
B.It reduced regulatory oversight
C.It encouraged deregulation of large banks
D.It had no effect on policy
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Stronger oversight and higher capital requirements is correct because regulators responded by imposing tougher rules to curb the risks of large institutions. Reducing oversight, encouraging deregulation, or having no effect contradict the observed post‑crisis reforms. This shows how the crisis reshaped the regulatory landscape to limit future systemic threats.

Q21. Why is a deep and liquid financial system important for economic growth?

A.It enables efficient capital allocation and risk sharing ✅
B.It increases unemployment rates
C.It decreases overall economic growth
D.It reduces investment opportunities
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Enabling efficient capital allocation and risk sharing is correct because liquidity lets funds move quickly to productive uses while spreading risk, fostering growth. Increasing unemployment, decreasing growth, or reducing investment are opposite outcomes. This connects the health of financial markets to broader prosperity.

Q22. What is the primary difference between a commercial bank and an investment bank that affected the bailout approach?

A.Investment banks lack deposit insurance, making bailouts politically sensitive ✅
B.Commercial banks have no loans
C.Investment banks are government‑owned
D.Commercial banks cannot issue stocks
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Lacking deposit insurance is correct; because investment banks do not hold insured deposits, rescuing them directly raises political concerns, leading to indirect methods. The other statements are factually inaccurate. Understanding this distinction explains why the government used a different strategy for Bear Stearns compared with commercial banks.
