Q1. What was India's current account deficit in June 2011?

A. $30 billion
B. $49 billion ✅
C. $72 billion
D. $100 billion

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is $49 billion because official data reported the deficit at that level for June 2011. The other figures either underestimate or overestimate the actual shortfall. Knowing the exact deficit helps understand fiscal pressure on the economy. A $30 billion deficit would suggest a milder imbalance, while $72 billion matches a later estimate, and $100 billion is unrealistic. This factual knowledge is essential for basic economic analysis.

Q2. Why would a weaker rupee potentially boost Indian exports but also increase the cost of living for residents?

A. It makes imports cheaper
B. It reduces foreign demand for Indian goods
C. It lowers export prices while raising import prices ✅
D. It has no impact on trade

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is that a weaker rupee lowers export prices while raising import prices. A cheaper rupee makes Indian goods more competitive abroad, encouraging export sales, but the same depreciation makes foreign goods costlier for Indian consumers, raising household expenses. Options A and B describe opposite effects, and D claims no impact, which contradicts basic trade theory. Understanding this dual effect explains why currency movements can have mixed outcomes for an economy.

Q3. If India's current account deficit reaches about 4 % of GDP, which policy is most likely to be advised by the International Monetary Fund?

A. Increase government spending to stimulate growth
B. Decrease interest rates to boost borrowing
C. Implement measures to curb import demand ✅
D. Raise the rupee's value through foreign exchange intervention

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The IMF typically advises countries with large current‑account deficits to curb import demand, because reducing imports helps narrow the deficit and restores external balance. Increasing spending or lowering interest rates would worsen the deficit, while intervening to raise the rupee could be costly and unsustainable. Therefore, option C, which calls for measures to limit imports, aligns with standard IMF recommendations. This policy aims to improve financial stability and confidence among investors.

Q4. Which factor was a primary reason rating agencies lowered India's sovereign rating to ‘negative’?

A. Large current‑account deficit ✅
B. High inflation
C. Low foreign‑exchange reserves
D. Strong fiscal surplus

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The primary reason rating agencies moved India’s sovereign rating to ‘negative’ was the large current‑account deficit, which signaled external vulnerability and potential balance‑of‑payments stress. High inflation or low reserves could also affect ratings, but the deficit size was the most immediate trigger. A strong fiscal surplus would improve ratings, not downgrade them. Thus, option A correctly identifies the deficit as the key factor, reflecting concerns about debt sustainability.

Q5. If Indian exports fall by 3 % and the rupee depreciates 10 % against the USD, with an import‑price elasticity of 0.5, what is the net effect on the trade balance?

A. Trade balance improves by 2 % ✅
B. Trade balance worsens by 2 %
C. No change in trade balance
D. Trade balance worsens by 8 %

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is that the trade balance improves by about 2 %. A 10 % rupee depreciation raises import prices, and with an import‑price elasticity of 0.5 the import volume falls by \(0.5 \times 10\% = 5\%\). Meanwhile exports decline by 3 % in volume. The net effect on the balance is \(-3\% + 5\% = +2\%\). Options B and D suggest worsening balances, and C claims no change, which contradicts the calculation.

Q6. How do corruption and excessive bureaucracy affect foreign direct investment (FDI) in India?

A. They attract more investors seeking high returns ✅
B. They have no measurable impact
C. They deter investors by increasing costs and uncertainty
D. They only affect domestic firms

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Corruption and excessive bureaucracy raise the cost and uncertainty of doing business, which discourages foreign investors. When firms face unpredictable bribes or lengthy procedures, they anticipate lower returns and higher risks, leading them to seek cleaner environments. Option A is opposite, suggesting attraction; option B denies impact, which contradicts numerous investment studies; option D limits the effect to domestic firms only. Therefore, option C correctly captures the deterrent effect on FDI.
