What is International relations 1919-1933 summary?

The interwar era demonstrates how unresolved grievances from World War I, combined with economic instability and the failure of collective security institutions, created a volatile environment where short-term diplomatic successes could not prevent the eventual return to global conflict.

📝 All International relations 1919-1933 summary MCQs

Q1. What was the primary objective of the 1922 Genoa Conference as pursued by Lloyd George?

A. To reconcile France and Germany ✅
B. To isolate the Soviet Union
C. To enforce reparations on Italy
D. To promote British colonial expansion

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Genoa Conference was convened to ease Franco‑German tensions by discussing reparations and economic cooperation, reflecting Lloyd George’s belief that a stable German economy would benefit British trade. It did not aim at isolating the USSR or imposing new colonial policies, making reconciliation the central goal.

Q2. Which event directly triggered the French occupation of the Ruhr in 1923?

A. Germany’s refusal to pay reparations in cash ✅
B. The Italian seizure of Fiume
C. The Japanese invasion of Manchuria
D. The British refusal to lend money to France

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

France occupied the Ruhr to force Germany to deliver reparations in kind after Berlin stopped paying cash. The move was intended to extract coal and steel, but it backfired by causing hyperinflation and worsening Franco‑German relations, rather than being linked to Italian or Japanese actions.

Q3. How did the United States influence European economic recovery after World War I?

A. By demanding full repayment of war debts ✅
B. By providing unconditional financial aid to all nations
C. By leading the League of Nations in peacekeeping
D. By mediating the Treaty of Versailles

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The U.S. leveraged its economic power by insisting that European allies repay war debts in full, which pressured countries like Britain and France to extract payments from Germany. This policy shaped post‑war finance, even though the U.S. remained politically isolationist and did not directly manage League affairs.

Q4. Which of the following best describes Italy’s foreign policy under Mussolini before 1935?

A. Aggressive revisionism aimed at territorial expansion ✅
B. Commitment to strict neutrality in European affairs
C. Alliance with the Soviet Union against Western powers
D. Support for the League of Nations’ disarmament agenda

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Mussolini’s early regime pursued revisionist aims, exemplified by the seizure of Fiume and the Corfu Incident, seeking to revise the post‑war settlement and expand Italy’s influence. This stance contrasted with neutrality, Soviet alignment, or disarmament support, which were not characteristic of early Fascist foreign policy.

Q5. What was the main cause of tension between Britain and France regarding German recovery in the early 1920s?

A. Differing views on whether Germany should be weak or strong economically ✅
B. Disagreement over colonial possessions in Africa
C. Competing naval armament programs
D. Opposing positions on the League of Nations’ authority

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Britain favored a robust German economy to revive trade and purchase British goods, while France preferred a weak Germany to prevent future aggression. This fundamental disagreement over economic strength created diplomatic friction, unrelated to colonial disputes, naval competition, or League authority.

Q6. Which newly created state after World War I faced the most internal ethnic division?

A. Yugoslavia ✅
B. Poland
C. Czechoslovakia
D. Austria

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Yugoslavia amalgamated Serbs, Croats, Slovenes, and other groups, leading to significant internal ethnic tensions. While Poland, Czechoslovakia, and Austria also dealt with minorities, Yugoslavia’s diverse composition made its internal division the most pronounced among the new post‑war states.

Q7. What was the outcome of the French occupation of the Ruhr on the German currency?

A. It caused hyperinflation and collapse ✅
B. It stabilized the Mark
C. It led to a new gold standard
D. It had no significant impact

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The occupation disrupted production and intensified reparations pressure, contributing to the rapid devaluation of the German Mark and eventual hyperinflation. This economic crisis weakened the Weimar Republic and fueled political extremism, rather than stabilizing the currency or establishing a new monetary regime.

Q8. Scenario: A French diplomat in 1924 must advise on whether to support the Locarno Treaties. Which argument best reflects the diplomatic rationale for French support?

A. The treaties would guarantee Germany’s western borders and reduce the risk of future invasion ✅
B. The treaties would allow France to annex the Rhineland without resistance
C. The treaties would isolate the Soviet Union diplomatically
D. The treaties would enable Britain to dominate European trade

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Supporters argued that the Locarno Treaties secured Germany’s western frontiers, providing France with a framework for collective security and reducing the likelihood of future German aggression. This diplomatic calculus emphasized stability over territorial ambition, contrasting with notions of annexation, Soviet isolation, or British trade dominance.

Q9. Analytical: How did the League of Nations’ failure in Manchuria influence subsequent British foreign policy toward collective security?

A. It prompted Britain to rely more on bilateral agreements rather than multilateral institutions ✅
B. It led Britain to increase its military presence in East Asia
C. It caused Britain to withdraw from all League activities
D. It resulted in Britain championing a new, stronger League framework

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League’s inability to curb Japanese aggression convinced many British policymakers that collective security mechanisms were ineffective, steering them toward bilateral treaties and power‑balancing strategies. This shift reduced enthusiasm for multilateral enforcement, rather than prompting direct military deployment, total withdrawal, or immediate League reform.

Q10. Scenario: In 1925, Gustav Stresemann must decide whether to accept a French proposal for a customs union with Germany. Which factor would most likely persuade him to accept?

A. The promise of French investment to revitalize German industry ✅
B. The requirement to cede the Saar to France permanently
C. The obligation to adopt the French franc as legal tender
D. The condition of limiting German rearmament to 50,000 troops

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Stresemann, focused on economic recovery, would view French investment as a means to rebuild industry and stabilize the German economy. Ceding territory, changing currency, or restricting rearmament would be politically untenable, making financial support the most compelling incentive for acceptance.

Q11. Which policy best illustrates Britain’s shift from punitive to conciliatory attitudes toward Germany in the mid‑1920s?

A. The Dawes Plan’s loan arrangements ✅
B. The imposition of the Young Plan’s harsher reparations
C. The enforcement of the Treaty of Versailles without amendment
D. The withdrawal from the League of Nations

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Dawes Plan, introduced in 1924, restructured reparations and provided loans to Germany, reflecting a conciliatory approach aimed at economic stabilization. This contrasted with the more punitive Young Plan, strict treaty enforcement, or League withdrawal, marking a clear policy shift toward cooperation.

Q12. Analytical: Evaluate the impact of the 1929 Wall Street Crash on European diplomatic efforts to contain German militarism. Which statement is most accurate?

A. The crash reduced American financial leverage, weakening support for European diplomatic restraint ✅
B. The crash led to increased American military aid to Britain and France
C. The crash caused the League of Nations to adopt stricter sanctions against Germany
D. The crash had no effect on European diplomatic strategies

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The 1929 crash curtailed U.S. credit flows, diminishing American influence over European economies and limiting the financial backing that had underpinned diplomatic restraint. Consequently, European powers found it harder to pressure Germany economically, rather than increasing aid, imposing harsher sanctions, or remaining unaffected.

Q13. Scenario: A Polish envoy in 1931 must argue for a defensive alliance with France against potential German aggression. Which point most strongly supports his case?

A. Germany’s re‑armament and violation of the Treaty of Versailles ✅
B. Poland’s desire to expand into Soviet territory
C. France’s reluctance to engage in Eastern European affairs
D. The success of the League of Nations in preventing war

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

By 1931, Germany’s re‑armament and breach of Versailles provisions signaled a clear threat, giving the Polish envoy a compelling justification for a defensive alliance with France. Poland’s expansionist aims, French hesitance, or League successes were less persuasive than the direct German military resurgence.

Q14. What was a direct consequence of the 1923 French occupation of the Ruhr for French domestic politics?

A. Increased criticism of the government’s handling of reparations ✅
B. A surge in popularity for the French monarchy
C. A rapid decline in French industrial output
D. Strengthening of the French Communist Party

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The occupation provoked domestic backlash, as many French citizens blamed the government for exacerbating economic hardship and inflaming international tensions, leading to political criticism. It did not revive the monarchy, cause industrial decline, or specifically empower the Communist Party.

Q15. Which treaty formally ended the Allied occupation of the Rhineland in 1930?

A. The Treaty of Versailles
B. The Locarno Pact
C. The Young Plan Agreement ✅
D. The Kellogg‑Briand Pact

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Young Plan, adopted in 1929, included provisions that scheduled the end of the Allied occupation of the Rhineland, which was completed in 1930. The Treaty of Versailles established the occupation, while the Locarno Pact dealt with borders, and the Kellogg‑Briand Pact addressed war renunciation.

Q16. Analytical: How did the Corfu Incident of 1923 influence Italy’s later decision to invade Abyssinia in 1935?

A. It demonstrated the limitations of League enforcement, encouraging unilateral action ✅
B. It solidified Italy’s commitment to collective security under the League
C. It caused Italy to adopt a policy of strict non‑intervention
D. It led Italy to seek a formal alliance with Britain before any aggression

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Corfu Incident exposed the League’s weak response to Italian aggression, reinforcing Mussolini’s belief that Italy could act unilaterally without meaningful consequences. This perception encouraged the later Abyssinian invasion, rather than fostering commitment to collective security or non‑intervention.

Q17. What was the primary purpose of the Kellogg‑Briand Pact of 1928?

A. To renounce war as a means of national policy ✅
B. To establish a new economic union in Europe
C. To create a military alliance against Germany
D. To divide colonial territories between powers

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Kellogg‑Briand Pact was a multilateral treaty in which signatory nations pledged not to use war to resolve disputes, aiming to promote peace. It did not address economic integration, form a military alliance, or allocate colonies, making war renunciation its central objective.

Q18. Scenario: In 1932, a British diplomat must assess whether to support a French request for a joint military exercise with Britain to deter German expansion. Which factor most undermines the proposal?

A. Britain’s economic depression limiting defense spending ✅
B. France’s unwillingness to share intelligence
C. Germany’s complete compliance with the Treaty of Versailles
D. The strength of the League of Nations in enforcing sanctions

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Britain’s severe economic downturn constrained its ability to fund additional military activities, making joint exercises financially untenable despite strategic concerns. French secrecy, German compliance, or League enforcement were less decisive than Britain’s fiscal limitations.

Q19. How did the 1924–1929 period’s political leadership changes in France and Germany affect bilateral relations?

A. They facilitated rapprochement through moderate leaders like Briand and Stresemann ✅
B. They escalated tensions due to extremist governments taking power
C. They caused France to withdraw from the League of Nations
D. They led to a formal alliance against the Soviet Union

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The emergence of moderate figures such as France’s Briand and Germany’s Stresemann promoted diplomatic dialogue, easing post‑war animosities and fostering cooperation. This contrasted with extremist escalation, League withdrawal, or anti‑Soviet alliances, highlighting the role of centrist leadership in improving relations.

Q20. Analytical: Assess the effectiveness of the Dawes Plan in stabilizing the German economy. Which statement best captures its impact?

A. It temporarily restored confidence by restructuring reparations and providing loans, but dependence on foreign capital made it vulnerable to external shocks ✅
B. It permanently solved Germany’s inflation problem and eliminated all reparations
C. It caused immediate hyperinflation and economic collapse
D. It had no effect on German political stability

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Dawes Plan re‑organized reparations payments and infused foreign loans, briefly stabilizing the German currency and market confidence. However, the reliance on external financing left the economy exposed to global crises, such as the 1929 crash, limiting the plan’s long‑term effectiveness.

Q21. What was the significance of the 1929 Young Plan for the Treaty of Versailles?

A. It reduced Germany’s reparations and set a schedule for ending the occupation of the Rhineland ✅
B. It increased reparations and extended the occupation of the Rhineland
C. It abolished the League of Nations
D. It mandated German disarmament to 10,000 troops

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Young Plan lowered the total reparations burden and established a timeline for concluding the Allied occupation of the Rhineland, thereby easing some of the Treaty of Versailles’ harshest provisions. It did not raise reparations, dissolve the League, or impose extreme disarmament limits.

Q22. Scenario: A Soviet diplomat in 1933 must argue against joining a collective security pact with Britain and France. Which argument most plausibly reflects Soviet concerns?

A. Fear that the pact would be used to contain Soviet influence while ignoring German aggression ✅
B. Belief that the League of Nations would enforce Soviet territorial claims
C. Desire to expand Soviet influence into Western Europe through the pact
D. Confidence that Germany would honor the Locarno Treaties

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet Union feared that a Western‑led collective security arrangement would prioritize containing Soviet expansion rather than confronting German re‑armament, making the pact politically disadvantageous. This concern outweighed expectations of League enforcement, territorial ambitions, or reliance on German compliance.

Q23. How did the failure of the League of Nations to stop Japanese aggression in Manchuria affect European attitudes toward collective security?

A. It reinforced skepticism, prompting many European powers to favor bilateral agreements over multilateral enforcement ✅
B. It led to a surge in enthusiasm for expanding League powers
C. It caused Europe to adopt isolationist policies and withdraw from all diplomatic engagements
D. It resulted in immediate military alliances against Japan

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League’s inability to restrain Japan’s Manchurian invasion convinced European states that multilateral mechanisms were ineffective, encouraging a shift toward bilateral deals and national security strategies. This outcome did not generate enthusiasm for the League, total isolation, or rapid anti‑Japanese alliances.
