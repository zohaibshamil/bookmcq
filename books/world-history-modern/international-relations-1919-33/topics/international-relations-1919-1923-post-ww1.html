What is International relations 1919-1923 post WW1?

These years were defined by instability and resentment as nations struggled with war debts, hyperinflation in Germany, territorial disputes from peace treaties, and failed attempts at international cooperation before stabilization efforts began.

📝 All International relations 1919-1923 post WW1 MCQs

Q1. Which of the following best describes Turkey’s attitude toward the post‑World War I peace settlement?

A.It fully accepted the terms and sought cooperation with the League of Nations.
B.It was dissatisfied and prepared to defy the settlement. ✅
C.It remained neutral and did not engage in diplomatic negotiations.
D.It advocated for a new alliance with Italy against the Allies.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Turkey felt the treaty unfairly limited its sovereignty, especially over territories like Smyrna. Consequently, Turkish leaders, under Mustafa Kemal, pursued a policy of non‑compliance, preparing to reject the settlement’s constraints and to renegotiate borders through diplomatic and, if necessary, military means.

Q2. What was the immediate outcome of Italy’s seizure of Fiume in 1919?

A.The city was returned to Yugoslavia after a League of Nations mandate.
B.Italy annexed Fiume permanently, expanding its coastline.
C.Fiume was occupied temporarily, prompting an international dispute that ended with the city’s return to Yugoslavia. ✅
D.The seizure led to a coalition of Allied powers invading Italy.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The occupation of Fiume by Italian nationalist forces sparked a diplomatic crisis. Although Italy claimed the port city, the League of Nations and the major powers pressured Italy, resulting in the temporary occupation and eventual return of Fiume to Yugoslavia, highlighting Italy’s early post‑war irredentist ambitions.

Q3. The Corfu Incident of 1923 was triggered by which of the following events?

A.A Greek officer’s murder of an Italian soldier on Greek soil. ✅
B.An Italian naval blockade of the Dodecanese Islands.
C.A dispute over fishing rights in the Adriatic Sea.
D.A diplomatic misunderstanding during a League of Nations conference.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The incident began when an Italian officer was killed by Greek soldiers on the island of Corfu. Italy used the incident as a pretext to bombard and occupy the Greek island, escalating tensions and exposing the League of Nations’ limited capacity to enforce its decisions in regional conflicts.

Q4. In 1923, how did Turkey demonstrate its willingness to confront the peace settlement?

A.By signing a new treaty with Britain without Allied approval.
B.By withdrawing from the League of Nations voluntarily.
C.By launching a military campaign into Thrace to challenge the borders set by the treaty.
D.By accepting the terms of the Treaty of Lausanne without protest. ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Turkey’s decisive move came when it advanced militarily into Thrace, directly challenging the territorial allocations imposed by the post‑war settlement. This action signaled a clear refusal to accept the imposed borders and forced the Allies to reconsider their diplomatic approach toward the emerging Turkish Republic.

Q5. In which year did Benito Mussolini become Prime Minister of Italy, marking the start of Fascist rule?

A.1920
B.1922 ✅
C.1924
D.1925
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Mussolini’s March on Rome culminated in his appointment as Prime Minister on 31 October 1922. This event marked the beginning of a Fascist regime that would reshape Italy’s domestic and foreign policies, including aggressive actions such as the seizure of Fiume and later expansionist ambitions in Africa.

Q6. Which country led the occupation of the Ruhr industrial region in January 1923?

A.Germany
B.United Kingdom
C.France ✅
D.Belgium
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: France, supported by Belgium, moved troops into the Ruhr to enforce reparations payments after Germany defaulted on its obligations. The occupation aimed to extract coal and industrial output, but it instead triggered massive passive resistance, economic turmoil, and a severe devaluation of the German mark.

Q7. At which conference did British Prime Minister Lloyd George attempt to reconcile French and German positions in 1922?

A.Genoa Conference ✅
B.Paris Peace Conference
C.Locarno Conference
D.Versailles Conference
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The 1922 Genoa Conference gathered representatives from major powers, including Britain, France, and Germany, to discuss economic reconstruction and reparations. Lloyd George hoped to bridge the gap between French demands for stringent payments and German capacity to pay, but the talks collapsed, deepening post‑war tensions.

Q8. During the early 1920s, Britain’s preferred approach to German reparations was to:

A.Limit reparations to keep Germany weak.
B.Insist on full payment regardless of economic impact.
C.Encourage German re‑armament as leverage against France.
D.Prefer a moderate reparations schedule to keep German trade. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: British policymakers believed that a thriving German economy would generate demand for British exports, thereby stabilizing Europe’s overall trade balance. Consequently, they advocated for a reparations regime that allowed Germany to recover economically while still meeting its obligations, contrasting sharply with France’s more punitive stance.

Q9. What was the most significant outcome of the 1922 Genoa Conference?

A.It resolved reparations and led to lasting peace.
B.It failed to reconcile French and German positions, leaving tensions unresolved. ✅
C.It resulted in a new alliance between Britain and Italy.
D.It caused the League of Nations to dissolve.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Genoa Conference ended without a consensus on reparations, reinforcing the divide between France, which demanded strict payments, and Germany, which could not meet those demands. This failure perpetuated economic instability in Germany and foreshadowed the hyperinflation crisis that would erupt later in the decade.

Q10. How did the French occupation of the Ruhr in 1923 affect the German currency?

A.It stabilized the mark by increasing foreign reserves.
B.It caused a modest depreciation but no inflation.
C.It precipitated hyperinflation, collapsing the German mark. ✅
D.It had no significant effect on the currency.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The occupation disrupted coal production and triggered widespread strikes, causing the German government to print money to finance passive resistance. This surge in the money supply, combined with loss of industrial output, drove the mark into hyperinflation, rendering it virtually worthless and destabilizing the German economy.

Q11. If you were a German industrialist during the Ruhr occupation, which course of action would most likely protect your factory’s operations?

A.Continue full production despite French demands. ✅
B.Close the plant and join the passive resistance movement.
C.Shift production to other regions and seek foreign investment.
D.Collaborate openly with French authorities to secure supplies.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An industrialist seeking to preserve his enterprise would likely maintain production covertly, hoping to avoid French seizure while minimizing losses. However, many chose passive resistance, halting output to protest the occupation, which often led to economic ruin and contributed to the broader hyperinflation crisis.

Q12. The United States insisted on full payment of European war debts primarily to:

A.Punish European economies for their wartime actions.
B.Ensure that Europe remained dependent on American loans. ✅
C.Encourage European nations to settle their financial obligations promptly.
D.Promote a policy of isolationism.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: American policymakers viewed the repayment of war debts as essential to sustaining U.S. financial interests abroad. By demanding full payment, they aimed to keep European economies tied to American capital markets, thereby preserving the United States’ economic influence even as it remained politically isolated from European affairs.

Q13. Why did Western powers view Bolshevik Russia with suspicion after 1918?

A.Because Russia offered generous trade agreements to the West.
B.Because the Bolsheviks promoted democratic reforms that threatened monarchies.
C.Because several Western nations, along with Japan, intervened in the Russian Civil War to oppose the Bolsheviks.
D.Because Russia withdrew from the League of Nations voluntarily. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Bolshevik Revolution introduced a communist regime that challenged capitalist norms, prompting Western powers to fear the spread of revolutionary ideas. Consequently, nations such as Britain, France, and Japan supported anti‑Bolshevik forces during the civil war, reinforcing distrust and shaping early Cold‑War‑like tensions.

Q14. Which of the following best describes the primary problems faced by the new states created after World War I (e.g., Yugoslavia, Czechoslovakia, Austria, Hungary, Poland)?

A.They all enjoyed stable borders and strong economies.
B.They suffered from internal ethnic divisions and contested borders.
C.They were immediately admitted to the League of Nations without reservation. ✅
D.They formed a unified economic bloc that rivaled Germany.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The successor states inherited diverse populations with competing nationalist aspirations, leading to border disputes and internal tensions. These challenges undermined political stability, hindered economic development, and created a volatile environment that the League of Nations struggled to manage effectively.

Q15. Why did France prefer a weak Germany in the early 1920s?

A.To encourage German re‑armament against Soviet threats. ✅
B.To ensure Germany could become a reliable trading partner.
C.To prevent any future German aggression that could threaten French security.
D.Because France wanted to share its colonial possessions with Germany.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: French strategists, still scarred by the devastation of World War I, believed that a militarily and economically weakened Germany would be less capable of launching future attacks. This perspective drove policies such as strict reparations and the occupation of the Ruhr, which aimed to curtail German resurgence.

Q16. How did the French occupation of the Ruhr affect British export markets?

A.It opened new opportunities for British goods in the occupied region.
B.It reduced British exports by disrupting continental trade routes. ✅
C.It had little impact on British trade due to existing colonial markets.
D.It forced Britain to increase tariffs on German products.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The disruption of industrial output in the Ruhr curtailed the supply of German raw materials, which in turn limited the production of goods destined for British markets. Consequently, British exporters faced reduced demand, highlighting how punitive measures against Germany could unintentionally harm allied economies.

Q17. Why was the League of Nations largely ineffective in curbing Japanese aggression in Manchuria during the early 1930s?

A.Because the League lacked a standing army and relied on member cooperation.
B.Because Japan withdrew from the League before the crisis began.
C.Because the League imposed severe economic sanctions that Japan easily circumvented.
D.Because the League successfully mediated the conflict, preventing further aggression. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Although the League’s inability to stop Japanese expansion became evident later, its early weaknesses—such as the absence of enforcement mechanisms and reliance on unanimous member action—already limited its capacity to respond decisively to any unilateral aggression, foreshadowing later failures.

Q18. As a League delegate in 1923, which proposal would most effectively address the Ruhr crisis while preserving European stability?

A.Recommend immediate French withdrawal and a joint German‑French reparations committee.
B.Advise the League to impose economic sanctions on France.
C.Suggest that Germany default on reparations to end resistance. ✅
D.Encourage the League to support Italy’s expansionist aims as a distraction.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A balanced solution would involve diplomatic mediation, encouraging France to withdraw troops while establishing a joint reparations committee. This approach could ease German resistance, restore industrial productivity, and maintain the League’s credibility, thereby fostering a more stable European environment without escalating tensions further.

Q19. How does Italy’s later aggression against Abyssinia (1935) relate to its earlier actions in Fiume and the Corfu Incident?

A.Both demonstrate Italy’s consistent policy of peaceful cooperation with neighbors. ✅
B.They illustrate a pattern of using diplomatic negotiations to resolve disputes.
C.They reflect a continuity of irredentist and expansionist ambitions that began with early post‑war actions.
D.They show that Italy abandoned expansionism after the 1920s.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Italy’s early 1920s confrontations, such as the seizure of Fiume and the Corfu Incident, revealed a willingness to employ force to achieve territorial claims. This irredentist mindset persisted, culminating in the 1935 invasion of Abyssinia, indicating a consistent trajectory of aggressive expansionism.

Q20. What characterized the economic relationship between the United States and Europe after World War I?

A.The U.S. withdrew all financial support, leaving Europe to rebuild independently.
B.European nations relied heavily on American loans and investment to finance reconstruction. ✅
C.Both continents pursued isolationist policies, avoiding any trade.
D.The United States imposed strict trade barriers on European goods.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: American capital flowed into Europe through loans, investments, and purchases of war bonds, fueling reconstruction and stabilizing economies. This financial dependence reinforced U.S. influence over European fiscal policies, even as political isolation persisted, shaping trans‑Atlantic economic dynamics throughout the interwar period.

Q21. How did the link between reparations demands and German hyper‑inflation manifest in 1923?

A.Reparations were reduced, preventing inflation from rising.
B.The German government printed money to meet reparations, causing the mark to lose value rapidly.
C.Germany paid reparations in gold, stabilizing the currency.
D.Hyper‑inflation occurred independently of reparations. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Faced with overwhelming reparations, the German government resorted to printing vast quantities of money to finance both the reparations and passive resistance to the Ruhr occupation. This massive increase in the money supply triggered hyper‑inflation, eroding the mark’s value and plunging the German economy into chaos.

Q22. What impact did the French occupation of the Ruhr have on domestic French politics?

A.It strengthened the popularity of the French government and its leaders.
B.It caused widespread protests and weakened support for the ruling coalition.
C.It led to France abandoning its reparations policy entirely. ✅
D.It had no noticeable effect on French public opinion.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The costly occupation strained French resources and provoked criticism from opposition parties and the public, who questioned the efficacy of aggressive enforcement of reparations. This domestic discontent contributed to political instability and weakened the governing coalition, illustrating how foreign policy actions could reverberate at home.

Q23. Comparing Turkey’s and Italy’s dissatisfaction with the post‑war settlement, which statement best captures their respective motivations?

A.Both sought to expand colonial empires in Africa. ✅
B.Turkey aimed to preserve territorial integrity, while Italy pursued nationalist‑driven irredentism.
C.Italy wanted to join the League of Nations, whereas Turkey rejected it.
D.Both countries were content with the settlement but opposed its implementation.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Turkey’s primary concern centered on safeguarding its sovereignty and revising territorial losses imposed by the treaty, whereas Italy’s grievances stemmed from nationalist ambitions to reclaim perceived historic lands, exemplified by actions in Fiume and later in Abyssinia, reflecting divergent motivations despite shared dissatisfaction.
