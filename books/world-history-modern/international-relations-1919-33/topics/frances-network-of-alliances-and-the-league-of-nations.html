What is France's Network of Alliances and the League of Nations?

Seeking security against German resurgence, France built a system of military alliances with Eastern European states like Poland and Czechoslovakia while simultaneously supporting the League, attempting to balance bilateral defense guarantees with collective international security.

📝 All France's Network of Alliances and the League of Nations MCQs

Q1. Which event highlighted the League of Nations' inability to curb aggression in the early 1920s?

A.Italian invasion of Abyssinia
B.Japanese seizure of Manchuria ✅
C.German occupation of the Ruhr
D.Soviet intervention in Poland
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League's failure became evident when Japan occupied Manchuria in 1931, exposing its weak enforcement mechanisms and undermining confidence in collective security among its members.

Q2. The Treaty of Versailles primarily imposed what type of burden on Germany after World War I?

A.Territorial losses only
B.Military restrictions only
C.Economic reparations only ✅
D.Political isolation only
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The treaty demanded substantial economic reparations, requiring Germany to pay large sums to the Allied powers, which strained its economy and fueled political resentment.

Q3. What was the immediate economic consequence of the French occupation of the Ruhr in 1923?

A.Increase in German exports ✅
B.Stabilization of the German mark
C.Hyperinflation and currency collapse
D.Rise in British investment
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The occupation disrupted coal and steel production, leading to a loss of revenue and triggering hyperinflation, which dramatically devalued the German mark.

Q4. Mussolini's seizure of which city in 1919 was an early sign of Italian dissatisfaction with post‑war settlements?

A.Trieste
B.Fiume ✅
C.Venice
D.Genoa
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Fiume was seized by Italian nationalist forces, reflecting Italy's resentment over not receiving the promised territories, and it set a precedent for future aggressive actions under Mussolini.

Q5. Why did the 1922 Genoa Conference fail to reconcile French and German positions?

A.It excluded the United States
B.It focused on colonial issues only
C.It did not address reparations adequately ✅
D.It was canceled due to a strike
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The conference attempted to address economic cooperation but avoided the contentious reparations issue, leaving French and German tensions unresolved.

Q6. How did the United States influence European economies in the 1920s despite political isolation?

A.By providing military alliances
B.By demanding full payment of war debts ✅
C.By joining the League of Nations
D.By annexing colonies
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The U.S. pressured European nations to honor war debts, using its financial clout to shape economic policies without direct political involvement.

Q7. Which new state formed after World War I faced significant internal ethnic divisions?

A.France
B.Italy
C.Czechoslovakia ✅
D.United Kingdom
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Czechoslovakia comprised Czech, Slovak, German, and Hungarian minorities, leading to political instability and challenges to its cohesion in the interwar period.

Q8. Why did Britain favor a economically strong Germany in the early 1920s?

A.To counter Soviet influence
B.To secure a market for British exports ✅
C.To enforce the Treaty of Versailles
D.To support French security
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: British policymakers believed a prosperous Germany would purchase British goods, stabilizing the European economy and reducing the risk of radicalism, even if it meant compromising on strict reparations enforcement.

Q9. How did the change in French leadership in 1924 affect international relations?

A.It led to a harsher stance toward Germany
B.It resulted in isolationist policies
C.It promoted diplomatic rapprochement and cooperation ✅
D.It caused France to withdraw from the League
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The new leadership under Briand and Herriot pursued reconciliation, signing treaties like Locarno, which eased tensions and encouraged a more collaborative European environment.

Q10. If Germany had defaulted completely on reparations in 1923, what would likely have been the immediate diplomatic response?

A.Allied military intervention
B.League-imposed sanctions
C.Negotiated debt restructuring ✅
D.No response due to economic chaos
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Allies, particularly France and Britain, would have sought a negotiated settlement to prevent total economic collapse, leading to plans such as the Dawes Plan that eventually restructured payments.

Q11. What impact did the Locarno Treaties of 1925 have on French security perceptions?

A.They eliminated the need for an alliance with Britain
B.They guaranteed German borders in the west ✅
C.They forced France to cede territory to Germany
D.They increased French reliance on the League
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: By recognizing the existing western borders, the treaties reduced French fears of German aggression and fostered a sense of stability, though they also limited France's ability to act unilaterally.

Q12. Which mechanism of the League failed during the Italian Corfu Incident of 1923?

A.Economic sanctions were never imposed ✅
B.Military troops were deployed without approval
C.The League council refused to meet
D.The dispute was resolved through arbitration
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League lacked the authority to enforce economic sanctions, allowing Italy to act with impunity and demonstrating the organization's limited capacity to deter aggression.

Q13. If the League had imposed effective sanctions on Italy after the Corfu Incident, could the later invasion of Abyssinia have been prevented?

A.Yes, because sanctions would have crippled Italy's economy
B.No, because Mussolini's ambitions were independent of economic pressure
C.Possibly, but other factors such as nationalism would still drive aggression ✅
D.Uncertain, as sanctions were never tested in practice
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: While sanctions could have raised the cost of aggression, Mussolini's ideological drive and domestic politics might have sustained the decision to invade, making the outcome uncertain.

Q14. How did British and French diplomatic approaches to Germany differ in the early 1920s?

A.Both prioritized strict enforcement of reparations
B.Britain sought economic integration, France demanded security guarantees ✅
C.France advocated for League membership, Britain opposed it
D.Both ignored German domestic politics
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Britain emphasized trade and economic recovery, whereas France focused on maintaining a weak Germany to protect its own borders, leading to divergent policies.

Q15. What effect did Western suspicion of the Soviet Union have on interwar alliance formation?

A.It encouraged a strong anti‑Soviet coalition
B.It led to cooperation with the USSR against Germany
C.It caused isolation of the Soviet Union and limited alliance options ✅
D.It resulted in the Soviet Union joining the League
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Western powers viewed the Bolshevik regime as a threat, reducing willingness to form alliances with the USSR and limiting the pool of potential partners in the evolving diplomatic landscape.

Q16. If the United States had become a full member of the League in the 1920s, how might alliance patterns have changed?

A.European powers would have reduced bilateral treaties ✅
B.The League would have become militarized
C.The US would have led a collective security force
D.No significant change would have occurred
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: American membership could have encouraged multilateral security arrangements, diminishing the need for separate bilateral alliances and potentially strengthening collective action mechanisms.

Q17. How did economic interdependence among European nations between 1924 and 1929 influence the likelihood of war?

A.It increased competition for resources
B.It created mutual deterrence through trade ties ✅
C.It had no effect on diplomatic relations
D.It led to isolationist policies
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Growing trade relationships raised the cost of conflict, as nations risked losing markets and revenues, thereby contributing to a temporary period of relative peace.

Q18. Why did the League's structure hinder effective collective security?

A.It lacked a standing army and relied on unanimous decisions ✅
B.It had too many permanent members
C.It focused solely on economic issues
D.It excluded major powers by design
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The requirement for unanimity and the absence of an enforcement arm meant that decisive action could be blocked by any member, rendering the League unable to respond swiftly to aggression.

Q19. What were the strategic objectives of Italy and Germany in the 1930s, and how did their alliance affect Europe's balance of power?

A.Both sought colonial expansion; their alliance isolated Britain
B.Italy wanted Mediterranean dominance, Germany sought Lebensraum; their alliance shifted power toward the Axis ✅
C.Both focused on economic recovery; their alliance had little impact
D.Italy aimed for neutrality, Germany pursued disarmament; their alliance destabilized the League
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Italy pursued control of the Mediterranean while Germany aimed for territorial expansion in Eastern Europe; their cooperation created a bloc that challenged the existing balance and encouraged revisionist policies.

Q20. How did Hitler's rise in 1933 disrupt existing interwar alliance networks?

A.It strengthened the Locarno system
B.It led to the renewal of the Treaty of Rapallo and isolation of traditional allies
C.It caused France to abandon its alliances ✅
D.It resulted in immediate League membership by Germany
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Hitler's aggressive agenda prompted France to seek new security arrangements while traditional alliances eroded, leading to a fragmented diplomatic environment and increasing the risk of conflict.

Q21. Identify three mechanisms by which the League's failure contributed to the outbreak of World War II.

A.Economic sanctions, military alliances, diplomatic recognition
B.Collective security, arbitration, disarmament
C.Failure to enforce sanctions, inability to prevent aggression, lack of credible deterrence ✅
D.League expansion, universal membership, peacekeeping forces
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The League's inability to impose effective sanctions, its lack of a standing force to deter violations, and the absence of a credible collective security framework all eroded confidence and emboldened aggressor states.

Q22. Evaluate the claim that a stronger League could have prevented World War II using counterfactual reasoning.

A.A stronger League would have guaranteed peace through economic integration
B.Even a robust League would have struggled against determined totalitarian regimes ✅
C.A stronger League would have forced all nations into disarmament
D.The League's failure was unrelated to its size or authority
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: While a more empowered League might have deterred minor aggressions, the ideological drive of Nazi Germany and the willingness of major powers to appease likely would have limited its effectiveness, making war probable despite a stronger institution.
