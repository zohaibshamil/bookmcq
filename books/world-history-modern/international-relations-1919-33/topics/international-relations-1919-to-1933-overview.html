What is International relations 1919 to 1933 overview?

This period covers the fragile peace between World War I and the rise of aggressive dictatorships, characterized by initial post-war chaos, a brief era of diplomatic cooperation and economic recovery in the mid-1920s, and a final collapse into protectionism and conflict caused by the Great Depression.

📝 All International relations 1919 to 1933 overview MCQs

Q1. Which treaty formally ended the First World War?

A.Treaty of Versailles ✅
B.Treaty of Trianon
C.Treaty of Saint-Germain
D.Treaty of Paris
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Treaty of Versailles, signed in 1919, officially concluded World War I. It imposed heavy territorial losses and reparations on Germany, reshaped borders in Europe, and created the League of Nations, setting the diplomatic framework for the interwar period.

Q2. Which country occupied the Ruhr industrial region in 1923 to enforce reparations?

A.Italy
B.France ✅
C.United Kingdom
D.United States
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In 1923, French troops moved into the Ruhr, Germany's key industrial heartland, to compel payment of reparations that Germany was defaulting on. The occupation sparked massive passive resistance and contributed to the hyperinflation crisis that crippled the Weimar economy.

Q3. The League of Nations was founded in which year?

A.1918
B.1920
C.1922 ✅
D.1919
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The League of Nations was established in 1922 as part of the post‑war settlement, aiming to provide a forum for international dispute resolution and to prevent future conflicts, though it struggled with enforcement and the absence of major powers like the United States.

Q4. Who became Chancellor of Germany in January 1933?

A.Heinrich Brüning
B.Franz von Papen
C.Kurt von Schleicher
D.Adolf Hitler ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Adolf Hitler was appointed Chancellor on 30 January 1933, marking the end of the Weimar Republic's democratic governance and the beginning of the Nazi regime, which rapidly moved to consolidate power and pursue aggressive foreign policies.

Q5. The Corfu Incident of 1923 involved Italy and which other country?

A.Turkey
B.Greece ✅
C.Yugoslavia
D.Albania
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In August 1923, Italian forces bombarded and occupied the Greek island of Corfu after a diplomatic dispute over Albanian immigration, prompting a brief crisis that was resolved through League mediation and highlighted Italian assertiveness under Mussolini.

Q6. Which conference attempted to reconcile France and Germany over reparations in 1922?

A.Genoa Conference ✅
B.Lausanne Conference
C.Paris Peace Conference
D.Locarno Conference
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Genoa Conference of 1922 brought together representatives from many nations, including France and Germany, to discuss economic reconstruction and the reparations issue; however, it failed to produce a lasting settlement, leaving tensions unresolved.

Q7. Which newly created state after World War I had its capital in Prague?

A.Yugoslavia
B.Poland
C.Czechoslovakia ✅
D.Austria
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Czechoslovakia emerged from the disintegration of Austria‑Hungary, establishing Prague as its capital. The new republic sought to combine Czech and Slovak lands, but faced ethnic minorities and border disputes that would affect its stability throughout the interwar years.

Q8. Why did Britain favor a strong German economy after the war?

A.To prevent Soviet expansion
B.To ensure reparations
C.To strengthen alliance with France
D.To create a market for British goods ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: British policymakers believed that a prosperous Germany would be a reliable buyer of British manufactured exports, helping to revive the British economy. They argued that excessive economic weakness in Germany could foster political instability, which might threaten broader European security.

Q9. Scenario: If France had not occupied the Ruhr in 1923, what economic outcome would Germany most likely have experienced?

A.German economy would have recovered faster ✅
B.Inflation would have worsened
C.Germany would have defaulted on reparations
D.No change
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Without the disruptive French occupation, German industrial production could have continued, providing tax revenue and foreign exchange that might have mitigated the hyperinflation spiral. A more stable economy would have lessened social unrest and possibly eased the political pressures that facilitated extremist movements.

Q10. Compare the diplomatic approaches of Italy and Turkey to the post‑war settlement.

A.Both were cooperative
B.Italy pursued aggression while Turkey negotiated ✅
C.Turkey engaged in aggression while Italy was conciliatory
D.Both rejected the settlement equally
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Italy, dissatisfied with territorial allocations, resorted to unilateral actions such as the seizure of Fiume, reflecting a confrontational stance. In contrast, Turkey, under Mustafa Kemal, negotiated the Treaty of Lausanne, securing sovereignty while largely accepting the new order, demonstrating a more pragmatic approach.

Q11. What was the main purpose of the 1922 Genoa Conference?

A.To discuss war reparations
B.To negotiate a peace treaty with Japan
C.To address Soviet economic relations ✅
D.To plan League of Nations expansion
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Genoa Conference aimed to foster economic cooperation between the Soviet Union and Western powers, seeking to integrate the Soviet economy into the global market and to ease tensions over reparations. It was a diplomatic experiment that ultimately failed to reconcile competing interests.

Q12. Which policy did Gustav Stresemann pursue to stabilize the German currency?

A.Signing of the Locarno Treaties
B.Rejection of reparations
C.Imposition of high tariffs
D.Introduction of the Rentenmark ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Stresemann introduced the Rentenmark in late 1923, a temporary currency backed by industrial and agricultural assets, which replaced the hyperinflated Papiermark. This decisive monetary reform restored confidence, halted inflation, and laid groundwork for later economic recovery.

Q13. Assess the impact of the United States' insistence on full payment of European war debts on French‑German relations.

A.It strained French‑German ties ✅
B.It improved cooperation
C.It had no effect
D.It led to US loans
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The U.S. demand that European nations honor their war‑debt obligations placed pressure on France to extract payments from Germany, worsening Franco‑German antagonism. France's insistence on reparations conflicted with American expectations of fiscal responsibility, creating diplomatic friction and limiting reconciliation efforts.

Q14. In 1924, which French foreign minister advocated for reconciliation with Germany?

A.Georges Clemenceau
B.Aristide Briand ✅
C.Édouard Herriot
D.Raymond Poincaré
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Aristide Briand, serving as foreign minister, promoted a policy of rapprochement with Germany, championing the Locarno Treaties and encouraging diplomatic dialogue. His approach reflected a shift from punitive post‑war attitudes toward a more cooperative European framework.

Q15. Evaluate the role of the League of Nations during the Manchurian crisis.

A.Successfully imposed sanctions
B.Organized military intervention
C.Provided limited diplomatic pressure ✅
D.Ignored the crisis
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The League issued condemnations and called for a withdrawal of Japanese forces, but lacked enforcement mechanisms and the support of major powers. Consequently, its actions amounted to limited diplomatic pressure, which failed to halt Japanese expansion, exposing the League's weaknesses.

Q16. Which event marked the end of the first phase of interwar diplomacy?

A.Invasion of Manchuria
B.Signing of the Locarno Pact
C.Munich Agreement
D.Hitler's appointment as Chancellor ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The appointment of Adolf Hitler as Chancellor on 30 January 1933 signaled a decisive break from the tentative diplomatic optimism of the 1920s, ushering in a period of overt aggression and the collapse of collective security efforts.

Q17. Scenario: If Mussolini had not seized Fiume, how might Italian foreign policy have differed?

A.More alignment with Britain ✅
B.Less aggressive expansion
C.Stronger ties with France
D.No change
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Without the provocative seizure of Fiume, Italy might have pursued a more conciliatory stance, seeking closer economic and diplomatic ties with Britain rather than emphasizing nationalist expansion. This could have reduced tensions in the Mediterranean and altered Italy's trajectory toward later imperialist ventures.

Q18. Analyze how the differing attitudes of Britain and France toward German reparations influenced the stability of the Weimar Republic.

A.They both destabilized the Weimar Republic equally
B.British support for a strong economy helped stabilize ✅
C.French harshness intensified hyperinflation
D.Neither had significant impact
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: British advocacy for a viable German economy encouraged policies that mitigated fiscal collapse, such as the Rentenmark reform, whereas French insistence on stringent reparations aggravated fiscal strain, contributing to hyperinflation and political volatility that weakened the Weimar system.

Q19. Scenario: Suppose the 1923 Ruhr occupation had been limited to coal‑producing factories; how would that have affected hyperinflation?

A.Hyperinflation would have been worse
B.No effect
C.Inflation would have been less severe ✅
D.Currency would have stabilized immediately
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Restricting the occupation to coal facilities would have preserved much of the Ruhr's broader industrial output, maintaining tax revenues and export earnings. This reduced economic shock could have lessened the rapid devaluation of the Papiermark, making hyperinflation less extreme.

Q20. Evaluate the extent to which the creation of new states (Yugoslavia, Czechoslovakia, etc.) contributed to inter‑war tensions.

A.Minimal impact
B.Major cause
C.Contributed but not primary
D.Contributed significantly to inter‑war tensions ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The fragmentation of former empires produced states with diverse ethnic groups and contested borders, fostering irredentist claims and diplomatic disputes. These internal tensions often spilled into foreign policy, heightening regional instability and creating flashpoints that later facilitated larger conflicts.

Q21. How did Bolshevik Russia's foreign policy affect Western intervention in the Russian Civil War?

A.It provoked intervention ✅
B.It invited cooperation
C.It remained neutral
D.It sought alliance with Japan
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Bolsheviks' revolutionary ideology and support for worldwide communist movements alarmed Western powers, prompting Britain, France, and Japan to intervene militarily to support anti‑Bolshevik forces, thereby deepening the civil war and fostering long‑term mistrust.

Q22. Scenario: If the League of Nations had successfully enforced sanctions on Japan after the Manchurian invasion, what likely trajectory would Japanese aggression have taken?

A.Japan would have withdrawn
B.Japanese aggression would have been curtailed ✅
C.Japan would have escalated elsewhere
D.No effect
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Effective League sanctions would have increased diplomatic and economic costs for Japan, likely compelling a withdrawal from Manchuria and deterring further expansionist moves, at least temporarily, and preserving the credibility of collective security mechanisms.

Q23. Compare the diplomatic strategies of Britain and the United States in the 1920s regarding European economic reconstruction.

A.Britain led with loans
B.US imposed strict conditions
C.Britain used trade while US used loans ✅
D.Both avoided involvement
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Britain emphasized commercial trade, encouraging German purchases of British goods to revive its own economy, whereas the United States leveraged its financial supremacy to provide loans and credit, influencing European recovery through monetary rather than purely commercial channels.
