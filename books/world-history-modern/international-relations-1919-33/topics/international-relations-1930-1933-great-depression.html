What is International relations 1930-1933 Great Depression?

The worldwide economic catastrophe destroyed international trade and trust, leading nations to adopt protectionist tariffs, abandon disarmament efforts, and turn toward aggressive nationalism that undermined the League of Nations and paved the way for war.

📝 All International relations 1930-1933 Great Depression MCQs

Q1. Which month and year saw Adolf Hitler assume the role of Chancellor of Germany, marking a turning point in international relations?

A.Jan-33 ✅
B.Mar-33
C.Nov-32
D.Jul-33
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The appointment of Adolf Hitler as Chancellor on 30 January 1933 signaled the end of the fragile post‑war diplomatic balance. It accelerated the shift from hopeful cooperation to aggressive nationalism, setting the stage for the breakdown of collective security and the eventual outbreak of World War II.

Q2. What was the primary objective of the League of Nations when it was established after World War I?

A.To promote global trade
B.To prevent future wars ✅
C.To facilitate colonial expansion
D.To manage international finance
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League of Nations was created under the Treaty of Versailles with the central aim of preserving peace by providing a forum for diplomatic dispute resolution, collective security arrangements, and disarmament efforts, thereby seeking to prevent another catastrophic conflict like the Great War.

Q3. Which country’s aggression in Manchuria exposed the League of Nations’ inability to enforce its decisions?

A.Italy
B.Germany
C.Japan ✅
D.Soviet Union
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Japan’s invasion of Manchuria in September 1931 demonstrated the League’s weakness, as the organization could not compel Japan to withdraw or impose effective sanctions, undermining its credibility and highlighting the limits of collective security when a major power disregarded international norms.

Q4. The occupation of which German industrial region by French troops in 1923 led to hyperinflation and economic crisis?

A.Saxony
B.Bavaria
C.Hesse
D.Ruhr ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: France’s decision to occupy the Ruhr in January 1923 to enforce reparations payments triggered a massive passive resistance campaign, causing a sharp decline in industrial output and fueling the runaway inflation that devastated the German economy and destabilized the Weimar Republic.

Q5. Which conference in 1922 attempted to reconcile France and Germany but ended in failure?

A.Paris Peace Conference
B.Geneva Conference
C.Genoa Conference ✅
D.Locarno Conference
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The 1922 Genoa Conference, convened to discuss European economic reconstruction and reparations, ended without agreement as French demands for strict payment clashed with German calls for relief, illustrating the deep mistrust that hampered post‑war reconciliation efforts.

Q6. During the early 1920s, which two countries were dissatisfied with the post‑World War I settlement and pursued revisionist policies?

A.France and Britain
B.Turkey and Italy ✅
C.United States and Japan
D.Poland and Czechoslovakia
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Both Turkey, dissatisfied with the loss of territories, and Italy, angered by perceived slight in the territorial awards, pursued revisionist agendas that manifested in actions such as Turkey’s nationalist resistance and Italy’s seizure of Fiume, challenging the new order.

Q7. If Britain had insisted on a weak German economy while France wanted a strong Germany for trade, how would this divergence have affected the stability of the Weimar Republic?

A.It would have intensified political polarization, making extremist parties more attractive. ✅
B.It would have unified German parties around a common economic policy.
C.It would have led to immediate German rearmament.
D.It would have caused Britain to withdraw from European affairs.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: British pressure for a weak Germany would have compounded domestic hardship, feeding resentment and driving voters toward radical movements like the Nazis, while French support for a robust German market would have created diplomatic tension, ultimately destabilizing the fragile democratic regime.

Q8. Which Italian city was seized by nationalist forces in 1919, later becoming a symbol of Mussolini’s early aggression?

A.Trieste
B.Fiume ✅
C.Venice
D.Bari
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The seizure of Fiume by Gabriele D'Annunzio’s nationalist troops in September 1919 became a dramatic showcase of Italian irredentist sentiment, foreshadowing Mussolini’s later expansionist policies and highlighting the volatile post‑war nationalist climate that the new fascist regime would exploit.

Q9. Consider the 1923 French occupation of the Ruhr. Which of the following best explains why this action backfired economically for France?

A.It caused a loss of French export markets due to German retaliation.
B.It triggered hyperinflation in France.
C.It led to the collapse of the German currency, reducing French reparations. ✅
D.It forced France to increase its own military spending.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The French occupation aimed to extract reparations in kind, but the resulting German hyperinflation rendered the seized goods virtually worthless, dramatically decreasing the real value of French payments and undermining the intended economic benefit.

Q10. The Corfu Incident of 1923 involved a dispute between Italy and which nation?

A.Greece ✅
B.Turkey
C.Albania
D.Yugoslavia
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Corfu Incident erupted after Italian forces bombarded Greek islands in response to the murder of an Italian general, leading to a diplomatic crisis that required League mediation and underscored the fragility of Italian‑Greek relations in the early 1920s.

Q11. How did the United States' insistence on full payment of European war debts influence the economic relationship between the US and Europe in the early 1930s?

A.It strengthened European economies by providing capital.
B.It strained relations, limiting trade and deepening the Great Depression. ✅
C.It led to a European coalition to collectively default.
D.It caused the US to abandon the gold standard.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: American demands for complete debt repayment forced European governments to prioritize fiscal austerity over domestic investment, curtailing trade, aggravating unemployment, and deepening the worldwide economic downturn that characterized the early 1930s.

Q12. Which newly created state after World War I faced internal ethnic divisions that affected its foreign policy?

A.France
B.Belgium
C.Yugoslavia ✅
D.Netherlands
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Yugoslavia, formed from the merger of diverse South Slavic peoples, struggled with competing national aspirations among Serbs, Croats, and Slovenes, which complicated its diplomatic posture and made it vulnerable to external manipulation during the interwar period.

Q13. If the League of Nations had successfully intervened in the Japanese invasion of Manchuria, which of the following outcomes would have been most likely?

A.Japan would have withdrawn and pursued diplomatic negotiations. ✅
B.China would have been partitioned among European powers.
C.The League would have been dissolved due to failure.
D.The United States would have entered the war in 1930.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Effective League action—such as imposing sanctions—could have compelled Japan to retreat, opening a diplomatic channel that might have averted further escalation and preserved a tenuous balance of power in East Asia.

Q14. Which year did Adolf Hitler become Führer, consolidating power after becoming Chancellor?

A.1932
B.1933
C.1934 ✅
D.1935
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: After President Hindenburg’s death in August 1934, Hitler merged the offices of Chancellor and President, assuming the title Führer, thereby eliminating any remaining constitutional checks and cementing his totalitarian control over Germany.

Q15. Analyzing the impact of the 1929 Wall Street Crash, why did the economic downturn exacerbate tensions in Europe leading up to 1933?

A.It caused hyperinflation in Germany, prompting radical politics.
B.It reduced American loans, worsening reparations crises. ✅
C.It led to increased British colonial expansion.
D.It forced France to adopt isolationist policies.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The crash sharply cut off American capital flows that many European economies, especially Germany, relied upon to meet reparations and stabilize their currencies, intensifying fiscal strain and fueling extremist political movements that promised relief.

Q16. Which treaty, signed in 1928, was intended to guarantee the borders of Western Europe but failed to prevent later aggression?

A.Treaty of Versailles
B.Treaty of Rapallo
C.Locarno Treaties ✅
D.Treaty of Trianon
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Locarno Treaties aimed to secure the Franco‑German border and promote peaceful arbitration, yet they could not deter later violations by Germany and Italy, illustrating the limits of diplomatic guarantees in the face of rising revanchist ambitions.

Q17. Evaluate the strategic reasoning behind Mussolini’s decision to seize Fiume in 1919 despite the city being awarded to Yugoslavia. Which of the following best captures his motivation?

A.To secure a naval base for Mediterranean expansion.
B.To provoke a war with France.
C.To demonstrate Italy’s commitment to nationalist ideology. ✅
D.To gain control over oil resources.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Mussolini’s precursor, D'Annunzio, seized Fiume to showcase Italian nationalist fervor, signaling a broader fascist agenda that prioritized symbolic victories and the restoration of perceived historical rights over pragmatic strategic considerations.

Q18. Which Soviet leader’s policies were viewed with suspicion by western powers, leading to interventions during the Russian Civil War?

A.Leon Trotsky
B.Vladimir Lenin ✅
C.Joseph Stalin
D.Nikolai Bukharin
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Vladimir Lenin’s Bolshevik regime, with its radical socialist agenda and withdrawal from World War I, alarmed the Western powers, prompting military interventions by Britain, France, Japan, and others to support anti‑communist forces during the Russian Civil War.

Q19. Considering the economic strain of reparations, how might a different French policy—favoring German economic recovery over punitive measures—have altered the political landscape of the Weimar Republic?

A.It would have eliminated extremist parties entirely.
B.It could have reduced hyperinflation, weakening radical nationalist appeal. ✅
C.It would have caused France to lose its colonial holdings.
D.It would have forced Britain to adopt protectionist tariffs.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A French shift toward lenient reparations could have stabilized the German mark, lessening the economic desperation that the Nazis exploited, thereby reducing the appeal of extremist parties and potentially preserving a more moderate democratic trajectory.

Q20. What was the name of the German foreign minister who pursued a policy of reconciliation with the Allies in the mid‑1920s?

A.Heinrich Brüning
B.Gustav Stresemann ✅
C.Konrad Adenauer
D.Franz von Papen
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Gustav Stresemann, serving as Foreign Minister from 1923 to 1929, championed a policy of rapprochement known as “fulfilment,” which aimed to normalize Germany’s diplomatic standing and ease the burden of reparations through constructive engagement with the victorious powers.

Q21. If the League of Nations had imposed economic sanctions on Japan after the Manchurian Incident, which of the following chain reactions would most plausibly have occurred by 1935?

A.Japan would have accelerated its militarization to compensate for sanctions.
B.Japan would have sought an alliance with Germany earlier.
C.Japan would have withdrawn from China, preserving regional stability. ✅
D.The League would have expanded its membership to include the United States.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Effective sanctions could have pressured Japan to reconsider its costly expansion, prompting a withdrawal from Manchuria and reducing the likelihood of further aggression, thereby contributing to a more stable East Asian environment in the mid‑1930s.

Q22. Which British Prime Minister attempted to mediate between France and Germany at the 1922 conference?

A.Stanley Baldwin
B.Ramsay MacDonald
C.David Lloyd George ✅
D.Winston Churchill
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: David Lloyd George, the former British Prime Minister, led the British delegation at the 1922 Genoa Conference, seeking to bridge French demands for reparations with German economic realities, though his efforts ultimately failed to produce a lasting settlement.
