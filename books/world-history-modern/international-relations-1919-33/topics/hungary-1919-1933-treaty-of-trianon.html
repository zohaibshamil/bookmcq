📝 All Hungary 1919-1933 Treaty of Trianon MCQs

Q1. When was the Treaty of Trianon signed?

🔹 A. June 28, 1918
🔹 B. June 28, 1919
🔹 C. June 28, 1920
🔹 D. June 28, 1921

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Trianon was concluded on 28 June 1920. It formally ended Hungary’s involvement in World War I and imposed severe territorial reductions, creating lasting political and social repercussions that shaped the country's interwar foreign policy.

Q2. Which country gained Transylvania from Hungary after the Treaty of Trianon?

🔹 A. Romania
🔹 B. Czechoslovakia
🔹 C. Yugoslavia
🔹 D. Austria

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Transylvania was transferred to Romania as part of the post‑war settlement. The loss of this ethnically mixed region fueled Hungarian revisionist ambitions and contributed to strained relations with Romania throughout the interwar period.

Q3. What was the capital of the newly formed Hungarian state after World War I?

🔹 A. Budapest
🔹 B. Vienna
🔹 C. Prague
🔹 D. Bratislava

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Budapest remained the capital of Hungary after the empire's dissolution. Despite losing large territories, the city continued as the political, cultural, and economic center of the reduced nation throughout the 1920s and early 1930s.

Q4. Which treaty formally ended Hungary's participation in World War I?

🔹 A. Treaty of Versailles
🔹 B. Treaty of Trianon
🔹 C. Treaty of Saint‑Germaine
🔹 D. Treaty of Locarno

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Trianon, signed on 28 June 1920, formally concluded Hungary’s role in the Great War. It redrew borders, ceded about two‑thirds of Hungary’s pre‑war territory, and created a sense of national grievance that dominated its interwar diplomacy.

Q5. Which Hungarian political figure served as foreign minister and advocated for border revision in the 1920s?

🔹 A. István Bethlen

🔹 D. Károly Khuen‑Héderváry

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

István Bethlen, prime minister and former foreign minister, pursued a diplomatic agenda aimed at revising the Trianon borders. His moderate approach sought support from the League of Nations and the great powers while keeping Hungary’s internal stability.

Q6. What form of government characterized Hungary between 1920 and 1933?

🔹 A. Constitutional monarchy
🔹 B. Republic
🔹 C. Regency
🔹 D. Socialist state

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Hungary was governed as a regency under Admiral Miklós Horthy, who acted as head of state without a king. This arrangement combined elements of authoritarian rule with limited parliamentary institutions, shaping the nation’s foreign and domestic policies during the interwar years.

Q7. Approximately what percentage of its pre‑war population did Hungary lose under the Treaty of Trianon?

🔹 A. ≈30 %
🔹 B. ≈40 %
🔹 C. ≈50 %
🔹 D. ≈60 %

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty reduced Hungary’s population from roughly 10.5 million to about 7.6 million, a loss of around 28‑30 %. Historians commonly cite a loss of roughly 40 % when including ethnic Hungarians now living outside the new borders, underscoring the demographic shock.

Q8. How did the French occupation of the Ruhr in 1923 indirectly affect Hungary’s trade balance?

🔹 A. It boosted Hungarian exports to Germany
🔹 B. It reduced imports of German raw materials
🔹 C. It caused inflation that hurt Hungarian consumers
🔹 D. It led to a surge in British investment in Hungary

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Ruhr occupation destabilized the German economy, leading to hyperinflation that spread to neighboring markets. Hungarian exporters faced reduced demand, while the influx of cheap German goods increased competition, ultimately straining Hungary’s trade balance and domestic price stability.

Q9. If Hungary had retained its pre‑war area of 93 000 km² instead of the post‑Trianon 28 000 km², how would its 1930 population density compare?

🔹 A. Approximately 45 inhabitants/km²
🔹 B. Approximately 70 inhabitants/km²
🔹 C. Approximately 100 inhabitants/km²
🔹 D. Approximately 130 inhabitants/km²

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Using the 1930 population of about 7.6 million, the retained area would give a density of \( \frac{7.6\,\text{million}}{93\,000}\approx81\) inhabitants/km². The actual post‑Trianon density was \( \frac{7.6\,\text{million}}{28\,000}\approx271\) inhabitants/km², showing a dramatic increase in concentration after territorial loss.

Q10. Which description best captures Hungary’s diplomatic strategy in the 1920s?

🔹 C. Isolation from all great powers
🔹 D. Cooperation with the Soviet Union

📖 Explanation:

Hungary pursued a revisionist policy that relied on the League of Nations to negotiate border changes. While dissatisfied with the outcomes, it used diplomatic channels and bilateral talks rather than outright alliances, hoping to gain support from sympathetic powers such as Italy and later Germany.

Q15. If Hungary’s reparations were set at 100 million gold marks and it could pay 5 % each year, how many years to settle the debt?

🔹 A. 10 years
🔹 B. 12 years
🔹 C. 15 years
🔹 D. 20 years

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Paying 5 % of 100 million each year equals 5 million annually. Dividing the total debt by the yearly payment gives \( \frac{100}{5}=20\) years to fully repay the amount, assuming no interest or additional penalties.

Q19. Assuming Hungary’s industrial output was 60 % of its pre‑war level in 1925 and grew at 4 % per year, in which year would it reach 80 % of the pre‑war level?

🔹 A. 1930
🔹 B. 1931
🔹 C. 1932
🔹 D. 1933

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Using compound growth: \(60(1.04)^{n}=80\). Solving gives \( (1.04)^{n}=1.333\) and \( n\approx7.2\) years. Adding 7.2 years to 1925 places the threshold in 1932, making that the correct year.
