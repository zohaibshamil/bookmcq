What is Austria 1919-1933 First Republic?

Reduced to a small rump state after empire dissolution, Austria suffered chronic economic weakness, political polarization between socialists and conservatives, and persistent debates over union with Germany, leading to increasing authoritarian tendencies by the early 1930s.

📝 All Austria 1919-1933 First Republic MCQs

Q1. In which year did the Republic of Austria emerge from the Austro‑Hungarian Empire?

A. 1918
B. 1919 ✅
C. 1920
D. 1921

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The empire dissolved after World War I, and the new Austrian state was proclaimed on \(12\) October \(1918\), but it was internationally recognized as a republic in \(1919\) through the Treaty of Saint‑Germaine, marking the official birth of the Republic of Austria.

Q2. Which treaty formally defined Austria's post‑World War I borders?

A. Treaty of Saint‑Germaine ✅
B. Treaty of Versailles
C. Treaty of Trianon
D. Treaty of Locarno

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Saint‑Germaine, signed on \(10\) September \(1919\), delineated Austria's boundaries, assigning territories such as South Tyrol to Italy and confirming its status as a separate nation, thereby shaping the geopolitical map of Central Europe after the collapse of the Austro‑Hungarian Empire.

Q3. What is the capital city of Austria?

A. Graz
B. Salzburg
C. Vienna ✅
D. Innsbruck

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Vienna, known in German as Wien, has served as the political, cultural, and economic heart of Austria since the Habsburg monarchy, and it continued in this role throughout the interwar period, hosting the government and diplomatic missions of the newly formed republic.

Q4. An Austrian businessman in 1923 faced hyperinflation; which of the following best describes the inflation multiplier experienced that year?

A. \(10^2\)
B. \(10^4\)
C. \(10^6\)
D. \(10^8\) ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Austrian kronen suffered a catastrophic loss of value after the Ruhr occupation, with price levels soaring by roughly eight orders of magnitude, i.e., an inflation factor of about \(10^{8}\), compelling citizens to use foreign currencies and prompting monetary reforms.

Q5. How did Austria's membership in the League of Nations in \(1920\) affect its diplomatic standing?

A. It enhanced Austria's legitimacy and facilitated trade agreements ✅
B. It isolated Austria further due to collective security obligations
C. It had no measurable impact on Austria's foreign relations
D. It forced Austria to adopt a neutral foreign policy

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Joining the League of Nations provided Austria with a platform to assert its sovereignty, gain recognition from the great powers, and negotiate economic assistance, which in turn improved its diplomatic credibility and opened channels for trade, despite lingering resentment from neighboring states over territorial adjustments.

Q6. What was the primary economic consequence for Austria of the French occupation of the Ruhr in \(1923\)?

A. Increased export demand to the United States
B. A surge in domestic industrial production
C. A sharp decline in German‑Austrian trade revenues ✅
D. Stabilization of the Austrian kronen

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Ruhr occupation disrupted the flow of raw materials and manufactured goods between Germany and Austria, leading to a substantial drop in Austrian export earnings, exacerbating the already fragile post‑war economy and contributing to the hyperinflation crisis that plagued Austria in the early 1920s.

Q7. Compared to Hungary, how did Austria's post‑war minority policy differ regarding its German‑speaking population?

A. Austria granted full cultural autonomy, whereas Hungary imposed assimilation
B. Austria limited minority rights, while Hungary provided extensive protections ✅
C. Both states pursued identical minority frameworks
D. Austria prioritized minority education, unlike Hungary

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While Austria, as a successor state, often emphasized a homogeneous national identity and restricted political representation for German minorities, Hungary, under the Treaty of Trianon, instituted more comprehensive minority rights, including language and educational provisions, reflecting divergent approaches to ethnic diversity.

Q8. Who served as Austria's foreign minister from \(1920\) to \(1922\)?

A. Karl Renner
B. Johann Schober
C. Ignaz Seipel
D. Otto Bauer ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Otto Bauer, a leading figure of the Social Democratic Party, held the foreign minister portfolio during the early years of the republic, navigating the complex diplomatic landscape of post‑war Europe and representing Austria at international conferences such as the Genoa meeting in \(1922\).

Q9. If Austria had entered a customs union with Germany in \(1925\), which outcome would have been most likely?

A. A rapid recovery of the Austrian kronen ✅
B. Increased political dependence on Berlin
C. Strengthening of Austria's ties with Italy
D. Isolation from the League of Nations

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A customs union would have integrated Austrian markets with the larger German economy, facilitating trade, stabilizing prices, and likely leading to a swift improvement in the value of the Austrian kronen, though it would also have raised concerns about sovereignty and external influence.

Q11. What role did the Austrian Social Democrats play in the 1919‑1923 political landscape?

B. They advocated for immediate annexation to Germany
C. They promoted a republican constitution and workers' rights ✅
D. They remained politically inactive

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Social Democrats were instrumental in drafting the 1919 constitution, championing democratic principles, social welfare policies, and labor protections, thereby shaping Austria's early republican identity and counterbalancing conservative and nationalist pressures during a turbulent formative period.

Austria, preoccupied with its own economic challenges and wary of entangling alliances, issued a diplomatic protest against the Japanese incursion but refrained from endorsing collective League measures, reflecting its limited capacity to influence distant geopolitical conflicts.

Q22. If Austria's foreign reserves fell from \(500\) million shillings to \(200\) million shillings during the 1931 banking crisis, what was the percentage decrease?

B. \(45\%\)
C. \(60\%\) ✅
D. \(75\%\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The decline from \(500\) to \(200\) represents a loss of \(300\) million, which is \( \frac{300}{500}\times100 = 60\% \). This sharp contraction underscored the severity of the crisis and limited Austria's capacity to import essential goods.
