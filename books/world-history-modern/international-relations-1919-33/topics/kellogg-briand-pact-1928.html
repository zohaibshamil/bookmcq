📝 All Kellogg Briand Pact 1928 MCQs

Q1. In which year was the Kellogg‑Briand Pact signed?

🔹 A. \(1928\)
🔹 B. \(1929\)
🔹 C. \(1930\)
🔹 D. \(1931\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The pact was concluded on August 27, \(1928\) in Paris. It was the result of a joint U.S.‑French initiative to outlaw war as a tool of national policy, and the date is a basic factual point for any introductory study of the agreement.

Q2. Which two officials are most closely associated with initiating the pact?

🔹 A. Woodrow Wilson and Georges Clemenceau
🔹 B. Frank B. Kellogg and Aristide Briand
🔹 C. David Lloyd George and Benito Mussolini
🔹 D. Winston Churchill and Léon Blum

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The agreement bears the names of U.S. Secretary of State Frank B. Kellogg and French Foreign Minister Aristide Briand, reflecting their diplomatic leadership in proposing a multilateral renunciation of war.

Q3. What was the primary purpose declared by the signatories of the pact?

🔹 A. To promote free trade across borders
🔹 B. To establish a collective security organization
🔹 C. To renounce war as an instrument of national policy
🔹 D. To create a new league of nations

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The text of the pact explicitly states that the parties condemn recourse to war for the solution of international controversies and renounce it as an instrument of national policy

Q4. Which of the following countries did NOT sign the original pact in 1928?

🔹 A. Australia
🔹 B. Brazil
🔹 C. Japan
🔹 D. Mexico

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

When the pact was opened for signature, 15 nations, including Australia, Brazil, Japan, and Mexico, joined. Mexico delayed its accession until 1929, so it was not among the original signatories in August \(1928\).

Q5. How many nations initially signed the Kellogg‑Briand Pact?

🔹 A. 15
🔹 B. 20
🔹 C. 25
🔹 D. 30

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The original act was signed by fifteen sovereign states, a modest yet symbolically powerful coalition that reflected broad, though not universal, support for the principle of outlawing war.

Q6. Which U.S. official is credited with drafting the text of the pact?

🔹 D. Charles Evans Hughes

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Frank B. Kellogg, serving as Secretary of State, authored the language of the treaty and guided its diplomatic negotiations, giving his name lasting association with the agreement.

Q7. In which city was the pact formally signed?

🔹 A. London
🔹 B. Berlin
🔹 C. Paris
🔹 D. Vienna

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The agreement was concluded in Paris on August 27, \(1928\); the French capital hosted the diplomatic ceremony that gave the pact its historic name.

Q8. A nation invades another claiming self‑defence. Under the pact, how should the international community evaluate this claim?

🔹 A. Accept it if the invader can prove an imminent threat
🔹 B. Reject it unless the United Nations authorizes force
🔹 C. Treat the claim skeptically, as the pact forbids war except in self‑defence against aggression
🔹 D. Allow it if the invading country has a democratic government

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The pact permits war only in cases of self‑defence against armed attack. An unsubstantiated claim of self‑defence is therefore viewed with suspicion, and any use of force must meet the strict criteria of necessity and proportionality under customary international law.

Q9. Which of the following best describes the pact's effect on the frequency of formal war declarations after 1928?

🔹 A. War declarations increased dramatically
🔹 B. There was a noticeable decline in formal declarations, though conflicts continued
🔹 C. The pact eliminated all armed conflicts worldwide
🔹 D. Declarations remained unchanged but were less publicized

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Statistical records show a drop in the number of official war declarations after \(1928\); however, the absence of formal declarations did not stop hostilities, as many aggressions were pursued under alternative justifications, revealing the pact's limited practical impact.

Q10. If a signatory violated the pact, what legal mechanism existed to enforce compliance?

🔹 A. Automatic economic sanctions by the League of Nations
🔹 B. Referral to the International Court of Justice
🔹 C. No direct enforcement; reliance on moral pressure and diplomatic protest
🔹 D. Collective military response by the United Nations

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The pact lacked an enforcement clause; it relied on the goodwill of states and moral condemnation. Violations could be protested diplomatically, but there was no built‑in sanction system, making compliance largely dependent on political pressure rather than legal compulsion.

Q11. Why did the pact ultimately fail to prevent the outbreak of World War II?

🔹 A. Because it was never ratified by any nation
🔹 B. Because major powers ignored its provisions and pursued expansionist policies
🔹 C. Because it mandated disarmament that was impossible to achieve
🔹 D. Because it was superseded by the Treaty of Versailles

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Despite its aspirational language, the pact could not restrain the aggressive agendas of Germany, Italy, and Japan. The absence of enforcement mechanisms and the willingness of signatories to act unilaterally rendered the treaty ineffective in curbing the militaristic ambitions that led to World II.

Q12. Consider a scenario where Country X claims a pre‑emptive strike is necessary to protect its borders. According to the pact, how should this action be judged?

🔹 A. It is permissible if approved by the United Nations Security Council
🔹 B. It is unlawful unless an armed attack has already occurred
🔹 C. It is acceptable if the strike is limited to defensive assets
🔹 D. It is justified if it follows a regional arms control treaty

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The pact expressly forbids war except in self‑defence against an actual armed attack. A pre‑emptive strike, lacking an ongoing attack, does not meet the legal threshold for self‑defence, and therefore would be deemed a violation of the pact's core principle.

Q13. What proportion of the original signatories later joined the Axis powers during World II?

🔹 A. \(20\%\)
🔹 B. \(33\%\)
🔹 C. \(40\%\)
🔹 D. \(60\%\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Out of the fifteen original signatories, nine remained neutral or Allied, while six—Germany, Italy, Japan, Spain, Portugal, and Hungary—aligned with the Axis. This yields a proportion of \( \frac{6}{15}=0.40\) or \(40\%\), illustrating the pact's limited predictive power regarding future alliances.

Q14. Which article of the United Nations Charter reflects the spirit of the Kellogg‑Briand Pact?

🔹 A. Article 2(4) on the prohibition of the use of force
🔹 B. Article 51 on the right of self‑defence
🔹 C. Article 24 on the Security Council's responsibilities
🔹 D. Article 73 on decolonisation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Article 2(4) embodies the same principle of prohibiting force, echoing the pact's goal. While the Charter expands the framework with collective security and self‑defence clauses, the foundational prohibition of war as a policy tool directly mirrors the Kellogg‑Briand ideal.

Q15. If a 1935 treaty added economic sanctions for breaches of the Kellogg‑Briand Pact, how would this align with the original agreement?

🔹 A. It would reinforce the original prohibition by adding tangible penalties
🔹 B. It would contradict the pact’s emphasis on moral suasion only
🔹 C. It would be irrelevant because economic sanctions were already prohibited
🔹 D. It would undermine the pact by introducing coercive measures

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Adding sanctions creates a concrete deterrent that the original pact lacked, thereby strengthening compliance mechanisms. Though the original text relied on moral pressure, supplemental sanctions would complement the prohibition of war with enforceable consequences.

Q16. Which statement best captures a limitation of the Kellogg‑Briand Pact?

🔹 A. It applied only to European powers
🔹 B. It lacked a verification or enforcement body
🔹 C. It required all signatories to disarm completely
🔹 D. It mandated immediate withdrawal from colonial territories

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The most cited shortcoming is the absence of an institutional framework to monitor or punish violations. Without a verification system, the pact depended on voluntary adherence, making its anti‑war pledge largely symbolic.

Q17. From a legal perspective, how is the Kellogg‑Briand Pact classified under international law?

🔹 A. A unilateral declaration of intent
🔹 B. A non‑binding political statement
🔹 C. A treaty with binding obligations
🔹 D. A customary norm without treaty status

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Scholars debate its nature, but most regard it as a treaty because it was signed and ratified by sovereign states, creating obligations under the law of treaties. However, its vague enforcement provisions give it characteristics of a political declaration, highlighting the hybrid legal status of such instruments.

Q18. In a 1932 border dispute, how could a signatory invoke the pact to resolve the conflict?

🔹 A. By demanding the other state withdraw its troops immediately
🔹 B. By filing a complaint with the International Court of Justice under the pact's jurisdiction
🔹 C. By emphasizing that any use of force would breach the pact, encouraging diplomatic negotiation
🔹 D. By invoking a collective security clause to call for UN intervention

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The pact does not provide a dispute‑resolution mechanism, but a party could point to the prohibition of war to pressure the opponent into peaceful negotiations, leveraging the moral authority of the agreement to avoid armed confrontation.

Q19. How did the pact influence the doctrine of \just war\ in the interwar period?

🔹 A. It reinforced the traditional criteria of jus ad bellum
🔹 B. It eliminated the concept of just war entirely
🔹 C. It introduced a new category of humanitarian war
🔹 D. It weakened the moral justification for any use of force

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By declaring war illegal except in self‑defence, the pact challenged the classic just‑war framework that allowed wars for reasons such as self‑defence, protection of the innocent, or restoring peace. The treaty thus shifted scholarly debate toward stricter limitations on the legitimacy of armed conflict.

Q20. If \(60\%\) of the original fifteen signatories had democratic governments, how many were democracies?

🔹 A. \(9\)
🔹 B. \(10\)
🔹 C. \(12\)
🔹 D. \(15\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Calculating \(0.60 \times 15 = 9\) shows that nine of the fifteen original signatories were democratic regimes, illustrating the pact's broad appeal across different political systems despite varying internal structures.

Q21. What role did American public opinion play in the creation of the pact?

🔹 A. It was largely indifferent, allowing policymakers to act without pressure
🔹 B. It strongly opposed any treaty limiting sovereign use of force
🔹 C. It favored isolationism but supported moral initiatives against war
🔹 D. It demanded a collective security alliance before any peace treaty

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Domestic sentiment in the United States during the 1920s favored a return to normalcy and isolationism, yet there was a moral undercurrent that opposed war. This ambivalence allowed leaders like Kellogg to pursue a symbolic anti‑war pact without committing to collective security obligations.

Q22. How does the Kellogg‑Briand Pact differ from the Hague Conventions of 1907 regarding enforcement?

🔹 A. The Kellogg‑Briand Pact includes a standing arbitration court, whereas the Hague Conventions do not
🔹 B. The Hague Conventions provided explicit mechanisms for dispute settlement, while the Kellogg‑Briand Pact lacked any enforcement provisions
🔹 C. Both instruments rely solely on moral persuasion without any legal mechanisms
🔹 D. The Kellogg‑Briand Pact imposed economic sanctions, unlike the Hague Conventions

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Hague Conventions established detailed procedures for arbitration and the use of war tribunals, creating a concrete system for resolving disputes. In contrast, the Kellogg‑Briand Pact contained no such mechanisms, relying instead on the goodwill of states, which limited its practical enforceability.

Q23. If a modern state cited the Kellogg‑Briand Pact in a UN Security Council debate today, what arguments could be made for its continued relevance?

🔹 A. It established a precedent that war is illegal, supporting contemporary norms against aggression
🔹 B. It is obsolete because it has been superseded by the UN Charter
🔹 C. It can be invoked to impose economic sanctions on aggressors
🔹 D. It provides a legal basis for collective military action without UN approval

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Proponents might argue that the pact’s historic articulation of the illegality of war laid groundwork for later legal instruments, reinforcing the principle that aggression is unlawful. Critics, however, would contend that the UN Charter now wholly governs the use of force, rendering the older pact largely ceremonial.
