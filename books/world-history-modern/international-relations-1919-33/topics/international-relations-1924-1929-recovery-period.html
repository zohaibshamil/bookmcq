What is International relations 1924-1929 recovery period?

Often called the 'Locarno Era,' this timeframe saw improved diplomatic relations, American loans stabilizing European economies through plans like Dawes, and a general spirit of optimism and multilateral cooperation before the global financial crash.

📝 All International relations 1924-1929 recovery period MCQs

Q1. In which year were the Locarno Treaties signed?

A.1925 ✅
B.1927
C.1929
D.1930
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Locarno Treaties were concluded in October 1925 at a conference in Locarno, Switzerland. They guaranteed Germany’s western borders with France and Belgium, and they facilitated Germany’s return to the League of Nations. Historians consistently date the agreement to 1925, making it a cornerstone of interwar diplomacy.

Q2. Which French foreign minister advocated reconciliation with Germany in 1925?

A.Aristide Briand
B.Georges Clemenceau ✅
C.Raymond Poincaré
D.Léon Blum
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Aristide Briand, serving as France’s foreign minister in 1925, promoted a policy of reconciliation and collective security, which culminated in the Locarno Treaties. His diplomatic efforts contrasted with earlier French hardline positions and reflected a broader desire to stabilize Europe after the upheavals of the early 1920s.

Q3. How did the Dawes Plan of 1924 affect Germany's ability to meet reparations obligations?

A.It reduced the total reparations amount
B.It restructured payments with foreign loans ✅
C.It increased the annual payments
D.It eliminated reparations altogether
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Dawes Plan introduced a system of staggered reparations payments backed by foreign loans, primarily from the United States. By providing credit and adjusting the schedule, it eased the immediate fiscal burden on Germany, allowing the Reichsmark to stabilize and creating conditions for modest economic recovery during the mid‑1920s.

Q4. What was the main purpose of the Kellogg‑Briand Pact of 1928?

A.To outlaw war as a national policy ✅
B.To establish a mutual defense alliance
C.To regulate colonial possessions
D.To create a new League of Nations
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Kellogg‑Briand Pact, signed in 1928 by numerous states including the United States and France, declared war an illegal instrument of national policy. Although it lacked enforcement mechanisms, the pact reflected the widespread hope that diplomatic means could replace armed conflict, influencing later international law developments.

Q5. What economic effect did the Young Plan of 1929 have on Germany?

A.It increased annual reparations payments
B.It reduced the total reparations and spread them over a longer period
C.It required immediate lump‑sum payment of all reparations
D.It had no impact on reparations ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Young Plan, adopted in 1929, further reduced Germany’s reparations burden and extended the payment schedule to 1988. While it eased short‑term fiscal pressure, the plan still obligated Germany to continue payments, which later became politically contentious as the global economy slid into the Great Depression.

Q6. Which event most directly demonstrated the failure of the League of Nations to curb Japanese aggression in Manchuria?

A.The Mukden Incident of 1931 ✅
B.The invasion of Ethiopia in 1935
C.The Corfu Incident of 1923
D.The occupation of the Ruhr in 1923
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Mukden Incident, a staged railway explosion in September 1931, gave Japan a pretext to occupy Manchuria. The League of Nations condemned the action but could not enforce any sanctions, exposing its inability to restrain major powers and foreshadowing future conflicts in the Asia‑Pacific region.

Q7. Who served as German Chancellor and Foreign Minister, promoting a policy of fulfillment between 1923‑1929?

A.Gustav Stresemann
B.Wilhelm Marx
C.Franz von Papen
D.Heinrich Bruning ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Gustav Stresemann held the dual roles of Chancellor (1923) and Foreign Minister (1923‑1929). He pursued a policy of “fulfillment,” cooperating with the Allies to meet reparations obligations while simultaneously working to revise the Treaty of Versailles. His diplomatic skill restored Germany’s standing in Europe and earned him the Nobel Peace Prize in 1926.

Q8. If Britain pursued a policy of economic cooperation with Germany in the mid‑1920s, which British minister’s stance does this reflect?

A.Winston Churchill
B.Arthur Balfour
C.Lord Cavendish‑Bentinck ✅
D.Neville Chamberlain
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Lord Cavendish‑Bentinck, as Chancellor of the Exchequer in the mid‑1920s, advocated for trade and financial links with Germany to stimulate British exports. His liberal economic outlook aligned with the broader policy of encouraging a stable German economy, which British industry believed would create a healthier market for British goods.

Q9. Assess the impact of the 1925–1926 French–German customs union proposal on bilateral relations.

A.It was accepted and led to a permanent customs union
B.It was rejected, causing heightened tensions ✅
C.It led to a temporary trade agreement without political implications
D.It had no effect on diplomatic ties
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The French‑German customs union proposal, put forward by Foreign Minister Aristide Briand, was ultimately rejected by both domestic constituencies. Its failure deepened mistrust, as French industrialists feared economic domination while German politicians viewed it as an infringement on sovereignty, contributing to the fragile interwar diplomatic climate.

Q10. Which treaty guaranteed borders between Germany and France after World War I?

A.The Locarno Treaties ✅
B.The Treaty of Versailles
C.The Treaty of Saint‑Germaine‑en-Laye
D.The Rapallo Treaty
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Locarno Treaties of 1925 guaranteed the Franco‑German border as defined by the Treaty of Versailles, while also establishing a mutual‑guarantee system among the signatories. By confirming the existing boundaries, the treaties reduced the likelihood of territorial disputes and contributed to a period of relative stability in Western Europe.

Q11. Which country joined the League of Nations in 1926?

A.Spain
B.Brazil ✅
C.Japan
D.Italy
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Brazil became a member of the League of Nations in 1926, reflecting its growing diplomatic engagement on the world stage. The country's admission was part of the League’s broader effort to include more Latin American nations, thereby enhancing its global representation despite the organization’s limited enforcement powers.

Q12. How did the stabilization of the German currency in 1924 influence foreign investment?

A.It discouraged foreign investors due to continued volatility
B.It attracted significant foreign capital, especially from the United States
C.It had no measurable effect on investment flows ✅
D.It led to a capital flight back to Britain
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The introduction of the Rentenmark in late 1923 and its stabilization in 1924 restored confidence in the German economy. However, many foreign investors remained cautious, fearing a relapse into hyperinflation. While some U.S. loans arrived under the Dawes Plan, overall foreign direct investment grew modestly rather than exploding.

Q13. Which French prime minister in 1928 supported the spirit of the Locarno agreements?

A.Édouard Herriot
B.Raymond Poincaré
C.Aristide Briand ✅
D.Paul Painlevé
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Aristide Briand, serving as foreign minister and often acting as prime minister in 1928, was a principal architect of the Locarno spirit. He championed collective security and reconciliation, encouraging France to accept the borders established after World War I and to promote peaceful dispute resolution across Europe.

Q14. What was the main outcome of the 1924 Genoa Conference regarding Soviet participation?

A.The Soviet Union was admitted to the League of Nations
B.Soviet delegates were expelled from the conference
C.The conference recognized the Soviet government as a legitimate participant in economic discussions ✅
D.No Soviet delegation attended
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Genoa Conference of 1922‑1924 invited Soviet representatives to discuss economic cooperation, marking a rare diplomatic engagement with the USSR. Although political tensions persisted, the conference acknowledged the Soviet government’s legitimacy in economic matters, paving the way for limited trade exchanges.

Q15. Evaluate the significance of the 1929 Wall Street Crash on European diplomatic relations.

A.It led to immediate military alliances against the United States
B.It caused European powers to prioritize economic recovery over territorial disputes ✅
C.It had no impact on diplomatic policies
D.It accelerated the formation of the Axis powers
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The 1929 Wall Wall Street Crash triggered a worldwide economic downturn, forcing European governments to focus on domestic recovery and fiscal stability. Consequently, foreign policy priorities shifted toward economic cooperation and trade negotiations, temporarily de‑emphasizing territorial ambitions and reducing the immediacy of diplomatic crises.

Q16. Which country’s foreign policy shift in 1925 led to improved Franco‑Italian relations?

A.Germany
B.Spain
C.Turkey ✅
D.Hungary
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Turkey’s policy of rapprochement with Western powers in 1925, exemplified by the Treaty of Lausanne, helped ease regional tensions and indirectly facilitated a more conciliatory atmosphere between France and Italy, as both powers sought stability in the Mediterranean and Balkans.

Q17. What was the name of the agreement that ended the occupation of the Rhineland in 1925?

A.Treaty of Versailles
B.Treaty of Locarno ✅
C.Treaty of Rapallo
D.Treaty of Neuilly
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Locarno Treaties of 1925, particularly the Rhineland Pact, formally ended the Allied occupation of the Rhineland. By guaranteeing the western borders of Germany, the agreements allowed the withdrawal of troops and symbolized a step toward normalizing Franco‑German relations.

Q18. Analyze why the League’s inability to enforce the Treaty of Sèvres contributed to later tensions in the Near East.

A.It caused immediate military conflict between Turkey and Greece
B.It led to the successful implementation of the treaty’s provisions
C.It undermined the League’s credibility, encouraging nationalist movements ✅
D.It resulted in the League expanding its powers
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The League’s failure to enforce the Treaty of Sèvres, which partitioned Ottoman territories, emboldened nationalist leaders like Mustafa Kemal Atatürk. This weakness eroded confidence in the League’s authority, fueling resentment and later conflicts such as the Turkish War of Independence, which reshaped the Near Eastern political landscape.

Q19. Which British economist’s ideas influenced the 1924‑1929 economic policies in the UK?

A.John Maynard Keynes ✅
B.Friedrich Hayek
C.Alfred Marshall
D.David Rutherford
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: John Maynard Keynes, whose early works on unemployment and fiscal policy gained prominence in the mid‑1920s, influenced British policymakers seeking to address post‑war economic stagnation. Although full implementation of Keynesian stimulus came later, his ideas began to shape debates on public spending and monetary management during this period.

Q20. In 1927, which city hosted the International Conference on the Settlement of the Eastern Question?

A.Vienna
B.Geneva ✅
C.Paris
D.Constantinople
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Geneva hosted the 1927 International Conference on the Settlement of the Eastern Question, bringing together diplomats from Europe and the Near East to discuss territorial adjustments and minority rights following the collapse of the Ottoman Empire. The conference sought to reconcile competing claims but achieved limited concrete outcomes.

Q21. If a historian argues that the 1925 Locarno agreements were a diplomatic success because they reduced French military expenditures, which evidence would support this claim?

A.A decrease in French defence budget from 1925 to 1927 ✅
B.An increase in French troop numbers on the Rhine
C.A rise in French warship construction after 1925
D.No change in French defence spending
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Statistical records show that France’s defence budget contracted noticeably between 1925 and 1927, reflecting the confidence engendered by the Locarno guarantees. This fiscal reduction aligns with the argument that the agreements lessened perceived threats, allowing France to reallocate resources from military to domestic priorities.

Q22. Which 1928 treaty sought to outlaw war as a tool of national policy?

A.The Kellogg‑Briand Pact ✅
B.The Treaty of Versailles
C.The Geneva Convention
D.The Munich Agreement
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Kellogg‑Briand Pact, signed in 1928 by major powers including the United States, France, and Germany, explicitly renounced war as an instrument of national policy. While lacking enforcement provisions, the pact embodied the idealistic belief that diplomatic agreements could replace armed conflict, influencing later developments in international law.

Q23. Which political party in Italy gained significant parliamentary representation in the 1924 elections?

A.National Fascist Party ✅
B.Socialist Party
C.Liberal Party
D.Christian Democratic Party
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The 1924 Italian general election saw the National Fascist Party (Partito Nazionale Fascista) secure a substantial share of seats, consolidating Benito Mussolini’s power. Although the party did not achieve an outright majority, its parliamentary presence, combined with intimidation tactics, paved the way for the establishment of a fascist dictatorship.
