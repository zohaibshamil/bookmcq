Q1. How did the French occupation of the Ruhr affect German inflation in the subsequent months?

🔹 A. It stabilized the currency
🔹 B. It caused hyperinflation
🔹 C. It had no impact
🔹 D. It led to deflation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Ruhr occupation disrupted coal and steel production, reducing export earnings and forcing the German government to print money to meet reparations. This exacerbated the already volatile currency, accelerating hyperinflation that peaked later in 1923 and undermining public confidence in the Weimar Republic.

Q2. If France wanted a weak Germany while Britain preferred a strong German economy, what diplomatic tension most likely arose?

🔹 A. Cooperation over reparations
🔹 B. Competition for influence in Europe
🔹 C. Agreement on trade tariffs
🔹 D. Unified support for the League of Nations

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The divergent goals created a rivalry: France pushed for strict reparations to keep Germany weak, whereas Britain advocated for economic recovery to sustain its export markets. This split hindered unified policy making and contributed to inconsistent enforcement of the Treaty of Versailles.

Q3. What was the primary outcome of Lloyd George’s 1922 Genoa Conference attempt at reconciliation?

🔹 A. Successful debt forgiveness
🔹 B. Creation of a new alliance
🔹 C. Failure to achieve agreement
🔹 D. Establishment of a joint reparations fund

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Genoa Conference ended without a consensus, as French officials rejected any reduction in reparations and German delegates demanded relief. Lloyd George’s diplomatic effort collapsed, reinforcing the entrenched mistrust between France and Germany and foreshadowing future crises.
