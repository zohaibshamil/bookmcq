Q1. Which treaty imposed reparations on Germany after World War I?

🔹 A. Treaty of Versailles
🔹 B. Treaty of Trianon
🔹 C. Treaty of Brest-Litovsk
🔹 D. Treaty of Locarno

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Versailles (1919) required Germany to pay reparations totaling roughly \( \) billions of gold marks. This punitive clause created economic strain and political resentment, fueling later efforts by Britain and France to manipulate German strength in the interwar period.

Q2. In 1923, which German industrial region was occupied by French troops?

🔹 A. Saar
🔹 B. Ruhr
🔹 C. Bavaria
🔹 D. Saxony

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

French forces entered the Ruhr in January 1923 to enforce reparations payments. The Ruhr was Germany's industrial heartland, producing coal and steel, and its occupation triggered a passive resistance campaign that crippled output and precipitated hyperinflation.

Q3. Who was the German chancellor who pursued a policy of reconciliation with France in the mid‑1920s?

🔹 A. Wilhelm Marx
🔹 B. Hermann Müller
🔹 C. Gustav Stresemann
🔹 D. Heinrich Brüning

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Gustav Stresemann, serving as foreign minister and later chancellor, sought rapprochement with France through diplomatic initiatives such as the Locarno Treaties, aiming to stabilize Europe while quietly rebuilding Germany’s economy.

Q4. The 1922 conference where Lloyd George attempted to reconcile France and Germany was held in which city?

🔹 A. Paris
🔹 B. London
🔹 C. Berlin
🔹 D. Genoa

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Genoa Conference of 1922 brought together the United Kingdom, France, Germany, and other powers to discuss economic reconstruction and reparations. Lloyd George’s proposals failed, leaving tensions over German payments unresolved.

Q5. Which country seized Fiume in 1919, leading to tensions with Yugoslavia?

🔹 A. Spain

🔹 C. France
🔹 D. Britain

📖 Explanation:

Italian nationalist forces, later supported by Mussolini’s government, occupied the city of Fiume (now Rijeka, Croatia) in 1919, defying the post‑war settlement and creating diplomatic friction with the newly formed Kingdom of Yugoslavia.

Q6. Why did Britain prefer a economically strong Germany in the early 1920s?

🔹 A. To buy British exports
🔹 B. To maintain a balance of power
🔹 C. To prevent Soviet influence
🔹 D. To secure colonies

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

British policymakers believed that a revitalized German economy would become a reliable market for British manufactured goods, helping to absorb surplus production and stabilize the European trade system after the war.

Q7. If France had continued occupying the Ruhr beyond 1925, what economic impact would Germany most likely have faced?

🔹 A. Currency stabilization
🔹 B. Increased exports
🔹 C. Unemployment rise
🔹 D. Inflation surge

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Prolonged occupation would have deepened the disruption of coal and steel supplies, reducing national output and triggering a surge in prices, which historically manifested as hyperinflation during the early 1920s.

Q9. During 1924‑1929, which French political leaders contributed to an improved international atmosphere?

🔹 A. Charles de Gaulle and Jean Monnet
🔹 B. Edouard Herriot and Aristide Briand
🔹 C. François Mitterrand and Jacques Chirac
🔹 D. Napoleon III and Louis Philippe

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Herriot, as prime minister, and Briand, as foreign minister, pursued policies of reconciliation, supporting treaties such as Locarno that eased Franco‑German tensions and fostered a more cooperative European climate.

Q10. Suppose the USA had not insisted on full payment of European war debts; how might this have altered German economic recovery?

🔹 B. Prolonged recession
🔹 C. Increased inflation
🔹 D. No change

Without the pressure to channel reparations and other debts back to the United States, Germany could have redirected resources toward domestic investment, accelerating industrial revitalization and reducing the fiscal strain that contributed to the 1923 crisis.

Q11. The occupation of which region led to the collapse of the German currency in 1923?

🔹 A. Saar
🔹 B. Rhineland
🔹 C. Bavaria
🔹 D. Ruhr

📖 Explanation:

When French and Belgian forces entered the Ruhr to enforce reparations, German workers responded with a passive‑resistance strike, halting production and prompting the government to print money to cover expenses, which caused the currency to collapse.
