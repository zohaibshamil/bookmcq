What is League of Nations 1919-1933?

Established to maintain world peace through collective security and arbitration, this international organization achieved some humanitarian successes but ultimately failed to stop aggression due to the absence of the United States, lack of enforcement power, and member states prioritizing national interests over global stability.

📝 All League of Nations 1919-1933 MCQs

Q1. Which event marked the first major test of the League of Nations' ability to handle aggression?

A. Japanese invasion of Manchuria ✅
B. Italian invasion of Abyssinia
C. Italian seizure of Fiume
D. Corfu Incident

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Japanese occupation of Manchuria in 19311931 was the League's earliest significant test of collective security, exposing its inability to enforce decisions when a major power refused to comply with its own resolutions.

Q2. In what year did Adolf Hitler become Chancellor of Germany?

A. 1932
B. 1933 ✅
C. 1934
D. 1935

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Adolf Hitler was appointed Chancellor on January 30, 19331933, a turning point that shifted the international climate from hopeful cooperation to increasing tension, ultimately undermining the League's prospects for maintaining peace.

Q3. How did differing British and French economic goals for Germany influence the Ruhr occupation?

A. Britain wanted a weak Germany, leading to harsh sanctions
B. France sought to boost German trade, preventing occupation
C. France aimed to force reparations, prompting troops to occupy the Ruhr ✅
D. Both nations agreed on a joint economic plan, avoiding conflict

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

France's desire to extract reparations pushed it to occupy the Ruhr in 19231923, while Britain preferred a strong German market for its exports, creating diplomatic friction that weakened coordinated League action.

Q4. If the League had imposed economic sanctions on Italy after the Corfu Incident, which outcome would have been most likely?

A. Italy would have withdrawn from Corfu peacefully
B. Italy would have escalated to full war with Greece
C. The sanctions would have had little effect due to Italy's self‑sufficiency
D. The League's credibility would have risen, deterring later aggression ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Imposing sanctions would have demonstrated League resolve, likely enhancing its reputation and possibly curbing Mussolini's future expansionist policies, even though Italy's economy could have mitigated immediate pressure.

Q5. Evaluate the impact of the 1922 Genoa Conference on Anglo‑French‑German relations, citing specific diplomatic failures.

A. It strengthened cooperation among the three powers ✅
B. It resolved reparations disputes permanently
C. It deepened mistrust, especially after Germany left early
D. It had no significant effect on later events

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Genoa Conference failed to bridge British and French differences over German recovery, as Germany walked out in protest, reinforcing French suspicion and British frustration, which later contributed to the Ruhr occupation and weakened the League's diplomatic foundation.

Q6. Which treaty formally ended World War I?

A. Treaty of Versailles ✅
B. Treaty of Trianon
C. Treaty of Saint‑Germain
D. Treaty of Paris

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Versailles, signed in 19191919, officially concluded the Great War, imposing territorial losses, military restrictions, and reparations on Germany, thereby setting the geopolitical context in which the League of Nations was created.

Q7. What was the primary reason the United States remained politically isolated after WWI despite its economic influence?

A. Fear of entanglement in European wars
B. Desire to join the League as a full member ✅
C. Support for Bolshevik Russia
D. Alliance with Japan in Asia

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

American isolationism stemmed from a widespread belief that involvement in European alliances would drag the nation into future conflicts, prompting the Senate to reject League membership while still leveraging economic leverage such as debt repayment enforcement.

Q8. Assume the League had successfully mediated the Turkish‑Greek population exchange in 1923. Predict how that might have altered the League’s credibility in the 1930s.

A. It would have been seen as ineffective
B. It would have boosted confidence, encouraging more mediation
C. It would have caused resentment among Balkan states ✅
D. It would have had no impact on later events

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A successful mediation could have shown the League's capacity for resolving deep ethnic disputes, increasing trust among smaller nations, and potentially fostering broader cooperation that might have tempered the rise of authoritarian aggression in the 1930s.

Q9. Why did the League of Nations fail to prevent Japanese expansion in Manchuria?

A. Because Japan withdrew voluntarily
B. Because Britain and France provided military support to Japan
C. Because the League lacked enforcement mechanisms and consensus
D. Because the Soviet Union blocked action ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The League's inability stemmed from its reliance on unanimous decisions and lack of armed forces; member states, notably Britain and France, were unwilling to impose sanctions or use force, allowing Japan to consolidate control over Manchuria.

Q10. Which country was NOT a successor state created after the Austro‑Hungarian Empire's dissolution?

A. Yugoslavia
B. Czechoslovakia
C. Finland ✅
D. Poland

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Finland emerged from the Russian Empire, not from the breakup of Austria‑Hungary, whereas Yugoslavia, Czechoslovakia, and Poland were all formed from territories formerly belonging to the Austro‑Hungarian realm.

Q11. The League’s Covenant granted the ability to enforce decisions through which mechanism?

A. Military force
B. Economic sanctions ✅
C. Diplomatic isolation
D. No mechanism

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Article 10 of the Covenant envisioned collective security enforced primarily through economic sanctions, reflecting the League’s reliance on non‑military pressure to compel compliance, though the lack of a standing army limited its practical effectiveness.

Q12. Compare the League’s response to the Italian invasion of Abyssinia in 1935 with its earlier response to the Manchurian Crisis. What does this reveal about its consistency?

A. Both responses were swift and decisive ✅
B. Both were indecisive, showing a pattern of weakness
C. The Abyssinia response was harsher, indicating improved resolve
D. The Manchurian response was harsher, showing bias toward Asian powers

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In both cases the League hesitated to impose meaningful sanctions, revealing a consistent inability to confront aggression by major powers, which eroded its authority and highlighted the gap between principle and practice.

Q13. Which French Prime Minister in the 1920s advocated for reconciliation with Germany?

A. Aristide Briand ✅
B. Georges Clemenceau
C. Léon Blum
D. Raymond Poincaré

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Aristide Briand championed a policy of rapprochement, promoting treaties such as Locarno and encouraging diplomatic engagement with Germany, contrasting with more punitive approaches favored by other French leaders.

Q14. The city of Fiume was claimed by which country after WWI?

A. Italy ✅
B. Yugoslavia
C. Austria
D. Hungary

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Italy laid claim to Fiume, asserting that it was awarded to it under the Treaty of Rapallo, leading to the occupation by nationalist forces and a diplomatic dispute that the League attempted, but failed, to resolve.

Q15. Assess how the economic depression of the early 1930s affected the League’s ability to maintain collective security.

A. It strengthened cooperation among members
B. It had no effect on the League’s functions
C. It weakened member commitment, reducing willingness to enforce sanctions
D. It caused the League to expand its membership ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Great Depression intensified nationalist pressures, prompting countries to prioritize domestic recovery over international obligations, which led to a decline in collective‑security initiatives and an inability to fund or support sanctions against aggressors.

A. The League would have ignored German violations
B. The League would have taken a stronger anti‑German position ✅
C. The League would have been paralyzed by Soviet‑German rivalry
D. The League would have dissolved sooner

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Soviet membership could have bolstered the League’s collective‑security framework, providing a powerful counterbalance to German militarization and potentially prompting firmer diplomatic or economic actions against Berlin's violations.

Q17. Which of the following was a core principle of the League of Nations?

A. Collective security ✅
B. Mutual defense pact
C. Nuclear deterrence
D. Economic isolationism

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Collective security, the idea that an attack on one member would be considered an attack on all, was the foundational principle guiding the League’s efforts to prevent war through mutual cooperation.

A. They undermined the League by creating separate alliances
B. They reinforced the League by guaranteeing borders and promoting peace
C. They had no connection to the League’s work ✅
D. They led to immediate disarmament of Germany

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Locarno Treates, while aimed at stabilizing Western Europe, were negotiated outside the League’s formal mechanisms, illustrating the organization’s limited role in major diplomatic breakthroughs and foreshadowing reliance on bilateral accords.

Q19. Which event directly caused the League to lose credibility among European powers in 1933?

A. Hitler’s rise to power ✅
B. Manchurian invasion
C. Italian invasion of Abyssinia
D. Spanish Civil War

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Hitler’s appointment as Chancellor in 19331933 signaled a shift toward aggressive nationalism, prompting many states to doubt the League’s capacity to restrain a rearming Germany, thereby eroding its perceived legitimacy.

Q20. The League’s headquarters were located in which city?

A. Geneva ✅
B. Paris
C. London
D. New York

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League’s secretariat was based in Geneva, Switzerland, chosen for its neutral status and central European location, allowing it to serve as a hub for diplomatic activity among member nations.

Q21. Imagine the League had enforced the Treaty of Versailles' disarmament clauses on Germany after 1925. Predict the likely impact on the outbreak of WWII.

A. It would have prevented WWII entirely
B. It would have delayed but not stopped the war
C. It would have caused Germany to seek alliances elsewhere
D. It would have increased German resentment, accelerating conflict ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Strict enforcement could have intensified German nationalist resentment, possibly prompting earlier rearmament in secret or seeking alliances, thereby not averting war but potentially hastening its onset due to heightened tensions.

Q22. How did the reparations issue influence British‑French relations in the early 1920s?

A. It united them against Germany
B. It created tension as Britain favored leniency while France demanded strict payment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The reparations debate strained Anglo‑French ties, with Britain advocating for a viable German economy to sustain trade, while France pressed for full payments, resulting in diplomatic friction that weakened coordinated League initiatives.

Q23. Critically evaluate the statement: ‘The League of Nations was a futile experiment because it lacked enforcement power.’ Use at least two historical examples to support your argument.

A. Agree, citing Manchuria and Abyssinia failures
B. Disagree, noting successes in the 1920s and in resolving minor disputes ✅
C. Partially agree, acknowledging both successes and limitations
D. Strongly disagree, arguing enforcement was not its primary goal

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While the League’s lack of a standing army limited its ability to coerce aggressors, it achieved notable diplomatic successes such as the 1925 Geneva Protocols and the resolution of minor border disputes, indicating that its impact, though constrained, was not wholly futile.
