What is Successor states of post WW1 Europe?

Newly independent nations created from collapsed empires faced immense challenges including border disputes, ethnic minorities, economic fragmentation, and political instability, making them vulnerable to external pressure and internal authoritarianism throughout the interwar period.

📝 All Successor states of post WW1 Europe MCQs

Q1. Which successor state was formed primarily from territories of the former Ottoman Empire after World War I?

A. Yugoslavia
B. Czechoslovakia
C. Turkey ✅
D. Poland

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Turkey emerged as a successor state largely from the former Ottoman lands, establishing a secular republic in 1923 after the collapse of the empire and the Treaty of Lausanne, distinguishing it from the Central European states listed.

Q2. In the early 1920s Italy seized the city of Fiume, claiming it should belong to Italy. Which principle did this action most directly violate?

A. Self-determination of peoples ✅
B. Collective security under the League of Nations
C. Territorial integrity of Yugoslavia
D. All of the above

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The seizure of Fiume by Italy disregarded the principle of self‑determination, which the post‑war settlement emphasized for newly created nations, especially since the city had been assigned to Yugoslavia by the Paris Peace Conference.

Q3. When French troops occupied the Ruhr in 1923 to enforce reparations, what immediate economic effect did this have on Germany?

A. Increased foreign investment
B. Deflation of the German mark
C. Hyperinflation and loss of confidence in the currency ✅
D. Stabilization of the banking system

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The occupation disrupted coal and steel production, leading to a loss of revenue and a rapid devaluation of the German mark, culminating in hyperinflation that eroded savings and caused massive social unrest.

Q4. Prime Minister Lloyd George attempted to reconcile France and Germany at the 1922 Genoa Conference. Which of the following best describes the outcome?

A. A lasting Franco‑German alliance was formed
B. Germany agreed to full reparations payments
C. The conference failed to produce a workable agreement ✅
D. Britain withdrew from the League of Nations

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Genoa Conference collapsed without achieving a consensus on reparations or economic cooperation, highlighting the deep mistrust between France and Germany and foreshadowing further instability in interwar diplomacy.

Q5. The United States, while politically isolated, exerted economic pressure on Europe by insisting on full payment of war debts. Which policy best illustrates this approach?

A. The Dawes Plan
B. The Young Plan
C. The Hoover Moratorium
D. The demand for cash settlement of Allied war debts ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The U.S. insisted that European Allies repay their World War I debts in cash, a stance that strained economies of successor states and limited their ability to recover from post‑war hardships.

Q6. After the Bolshevik Revolution, western powers viewed Soviet Russia with suspicion. Which action did Japan take that reflected this distrust?

A. Signing a mutual defense treaty with the USSR
B. Intervening in the Russian Civil War on the anti‑Bolshevik side ✅
C. Providing economic aid to the Soviet government
D. Recognizing the Soviet Union diplomatically in 1922

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Japan, alongside several Western powers, sent troops to support the White forces during the Russian Civil War, demonstrating its distrust of the new communist regime and its desire to curb Soviet influence in the Far East.

Q7. The new states created after World War I—Yugoslavia, Czechoslovakia, Austria, Hungary, and Poland—faced internal divisions. Which of the following was a common source of tension?

A. Religious heterogeneity ✅
B. Uniform ethnic composition
C. Lack of natural resources
D. Absence of a central government

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

These successor states contained multiple ethnic groups and religious minorities, leading to frequent internal disputes over autonomy, language rights, and political representation, which complicated their nation‑building efforts.

Q8. If Britain wanted a strong German economy to purchase British exports, which policy would it most likely support?

A. Strict limitation of German industrial output
B. Encouragement of German re‑armament
C. Relaxed reparations and access to foreign loans ✅
D. Isolation of German markets

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

British policymakers feared a weak Germany would reduce demand for British goods, so they advocated for more lenient reparations and facilitated foreign credit, believing this would revive German industry and sustain export markets.

Q9. Why did the League of Nations fail to curb Japanese aggression in Manchuria?

A. The League lacked a standing army and unanimous member support ✅
B. Japan withdrew from the League before the crisis
C. The League imposed economic sanctions that Japan easily circumvented
D. The League successfully mediated the conflict but was ignored

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League's inability to enforce decisions stemmed from the absence of a collective security force and the necessity of unanimous votes, which Japan exploited to continue its expansion without effective opposition.

Q10. The French occupation of the Ruhr in 1923 caused the German currency to collapse. Which monetary measure could have mitigated this outcome?

A. Printing more paper money to stimulate the economy
B. Implementing a fixed exchange rate to the gold standard ✅
C. Introducing price controls on essential goods
D. Austerity measures and fiscal consolidation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Adopting a fixed gold standard would have anchored the mark to a stable value, limiting hyperinflation, though political constraints made such a policy difficult to implement at the time.

Q11. France and Britain held differing attitudes toward German recovery after World War I. Which statement best captures this contrast?

A. Both sought to keep Germany economically weak to prevent future wars
B. France favored a strong German economy to aid its own recovery, while Britain preferred a weak Germany
C. Britain wanted a robust German market for its exports, whereas France aimed for a demilitarized, economically weak Germany ✅
D. Both nations supported unlimited German re‑armament

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

British policymakers emphasized the need for a thriving German market to sustain British exports, while French officials prioritized security, advocating for a weakened German economy to reduce the risk of renewed aggression.

Q12. The improvement in international relations from 1924 to 1929 was partly due to changes in political leadership. Which pair of leaders correctly corresponds to France and Germany during this period?

A. Edouard Herriot and Gustav Stresemann ✅
B. Aristide Briand and Adolf Hitler
C. Georges Clemenceau and Friedrich Ebert
D. Raymond Poincaré and Wilhelm II

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Edouard Herriot served as French Prime Minister and worked with Gustav Stresemann, the German Foreign Minister, both promoting reconciliation and economic cooperation that helped stabilize interwar Europe.

Q13. How did the internal divisions of the new successor states influence their foreign policies between 1919 and 1933?

A. They promoted aggressive expansion to unify ethnic groups
B. They prioritized internal cohesion over external alliances
C. They sought collective security through the League despite internal tensions ✅
D. They withdrew from all diplomatic engagements

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Even with deep ethnic fractures, many successor states joined the League of Nations, hoping collective security would protect them from external threats while they worked to manage internal disputes.

Q14. The Corfu Incident of 1923 heightened tensions between Italy and which other nation?

A. France
B. Greece ✅
C. Turkey
D. Yugoslavia

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Corfu Incident arose after Italian forces bombarded Greek islands, leading to a diplomatic crisis between Italy and Greece that required League mediation.

Q15. Which event is commonly regarded as marking the end of the first phase of interwar international relations?

A. The signing of the Locarno Treaties in 1925
B. The Japanese invasion of Manchuria in 1931
C. Hitler's appointment as Chancellor in 1933 ✅
D. The Munich Agreement of 1938

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The accession of Adolf Hitler to the German Chancellorship in 1933 signaled a decisive shift away from diplomatic attempts at peace, ushering in a more confrontational international climate.

Q16. If the Ruhr occupation caused the German mark to collapse, which fiscal policy might have lessened the impact?

A. Increasing tariffs on foreign goods
B. Reducing government spending and balancing the budget
C. Nationalizing major industries
D. Implementing large-scale public works financed by foreign loans ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Financing public works through foreign loans would have injected capital into the economy, supporting employment and stabilizing the currency while avoiding excessive money printing.

Q17. Assess the impact of the failed 1922 Genoa Conference on Franco‑German relations. Which outcome best reflects the aftermath?

A. It led to a lasting alliance between France and Germany
B. It deepened mutual suspicion and hardened reparations demands ✅
C. It resulted in immediate German economic recovery
D. It prompted Britain to withdraw from European affairs

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The conference's collapse reinforced French distrust of German intentions, prompting stricter enforcement of reparations and contributing to the later occupation of the Ruhr, while German resentment grew.

Q18. Considering the differing objectives of France and Britain regarding German reparations, which diplomatic proposal could have satisfied both parties in 1923?

A. A joint French‑British military occupation of the Ruhr with no economic concessions
B. A staggered reparations schedule tied to German industrial output, coupled with British trade incentives ✅
C. Full cancellation of reparations in exchange for German disarmament
D. A permanent League‑mandated control of German finances

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A proposal linking reparations to measurable industrial production would address French security concerns while allowing Britain to maintain trade ties, offering a compromise that balanced economic and strategic interests.

Q19. Analyze the role of the successor states in the Central European balance of power between 1919 and 1933. Which statement most accurately reflects their influence?

A. They collectively formed a bloc that deterred German aggression
B. Their internal conflicts weakened collective security, facilitating German expansion ✅
C. They remained neutral and uninvolved in great‑power politics
D. They aligned closely with the Soviet Union to counter Western powers

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The successor states, plagued by ethnic disputes and fragile economies, were unable to present a united front, diminishing the effectiveness of collective security mechanisms and creating openings that Germany later exploited for territorial ambitions.

Q20. Given Hitler's rise in 1933, which successor state was most vulnerable to German expansionist aims and why?

A. Poland, because of its large German minority and strategic location
B. Hungary, due to its alliance with Italy
C. Czechoslovakia, because of its border with the Sudetenland and economic ties ✅
D. Austria, because of shared language and cultural affinity

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Czechoslovakia contained the ethnically German Sudetenland, whose annexation provided Hitler a pretext for expansion, and its strategic position in Central Europe made it a prime target for German territorial ambitions.

Q21. Evaluate how U.S. policies demanding full repayment of European war debts affected the stability of the successor states. Which effect was most significant?

A. It bolstered their economies by encouraging fiscal discipline
B. It strained their finances, leading to currency instability and social unrest ✅
C. It prompted them to form a monetary union to pool resources
D. It caused the United States to join the League of Nations

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The insistence on cash repayment forced many successor states to divert scarce resources to debt service, exacerbating inflation and undermining economic recovery, which in turn heightened political volatility and weakened democratic institutions.

Q22. Synthesize the causes of the Ruhr occupation, the collapse of the German mark, and the rise of extremist politics. Which factor was most pivotal in linking these events?

A. The Allied decision to increase reparations demands in 1923
B. The German government's refusal to accept the Treaty of Versailles
C. The lack of a coordinated League of Nations response
D. The hyperinflation that eroded public confidence in the Weimar Republic ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Hyperinflation devastated savings and eroded faith in democratic governance, creating fertile ground for extremist parties like the Nazis, while the Ruhr occupation and reparations demands accelerated the economic collapse that fed this political radicalization.

Q23. If you were a diplomat from Czechoslovakia in 1930, how would you navigate increasing tensions between Germany and the Western powers? Which strategy best protects your nation's interests?

A. Seek a bilateral alliance with Germany to avoid confrontation
B. Advocate for stronger collective security through the League and a mutual defense pact with France and Britain ✅
C. Remain completely neutral and avoid all diplomatic engagements
D. Request mediation from the United States to broker a peace settlement

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By championing collective security and forging mutual defense agreements with France and Britain, a Czechoslovak diplomat could reinforce deterrence against German aggression while maintaining the legitimacy of the League, thereby safeguarding national sovereignty.
