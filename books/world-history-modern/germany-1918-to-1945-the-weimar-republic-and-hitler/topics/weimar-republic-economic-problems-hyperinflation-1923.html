Q1. What was the official exchange rate between the German mark and the US dollar at the end of World War I?

🔹 A. 10 marks per dollar
🔹 B. 20 marks per dollar
🔹 C. 30 marks per dollar
🔹 D. 40 marks per dollar

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The post‑war official rate was set at \(20\) marks for one US dollar. This figure is frequently cited in economic histories of the period and illustrates the relatively strong position of the mark before the hyperinflation began.

Q2. In which month and year did French troops occupy the Ruhr industrial region?

🔹 A. January 1923
🔹 B. February 1923
🔹 C. March 1923
🔹 D. April 1923

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

French forces moved into the Ruhr in January 1923 to enforce reparations. The occupation disrupted production, provoked passive resistance, and accelerated the hyperinflation that devastated the German economy in the following months.

Q3. What new currency was introduced in Germany in 1924 to end hyperinflation?

🔹 A. Goldmark
🔹 B. Reichsmark
🔹 C. Rentenmark
🔹 D. Weissmark

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Rentenmark, launched in November 1924, was backed by mortgage‑secured assets and replaced the worthless Papiermark. Its introduction stabilized prices and restored confidence, marking a turning point in the Weimar Republic’s economic crisis.

Q4. How much money did the Dawes Plan provide as an immediate loan to Germany?

🔹 A. £10 million
🔹 B. £20 million
🔹 C. £30 million
🔹 D. £40 million

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The 1924 Dawes Plan included a loan of £40 million from the United States. This infusion of capital helped Germany meet reparations obligations, rebuild its industrial base, and halt the runaway inflation that had plagued the nation.

Q5. Who chaired the conference that produced the Dawes Plan?

🔹 D. General Douglas MacArthur

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The plan is named after General Charles G. Dawes, an American banker and former military officer who led the committee that negotiated the reparations schedule and financial assistance for Germany.

Q6. Which social group largely benefited from the economic crisis of the early 1920s?

🔹 A. Urban workers
🔹 B. Middle‑class savers
🔹 C. Landowners and industrialists
🔹 D. Small farmers

Landowners and industrialists retained ownership of tangible assets such as farms, mines, and factories. While wages fell and savings evaporated for many, these groups maintained wealth and even increased influence over the German economy.

Q7. Which factor most directly contributed to the hyperinflation that peaked in 1923?

🔹 A. Increased tax revenue
🔹 B. Stable reparations payments
🔹 C. A rise in gold reserves
🔹 D. Mass printing of money

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The government resorted to printing vast quantities of paper money to meet reparations and domestic expenses. This unchecked expansion of the money supply caused the mark’s value to collapse, leading to the infamous hyperinflation.

Q8. What was the immediate economic effect of the German policy of passive resistance in the Ruhr?

🔹 A. Industrial output doubled
🔹 B. Unemployment fell sharply
🔹 C. Industrial production fell dramatically
🔹 D. Exports increased

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Passive resistance meant workers halted production and refused to cooperate with occupying forces. As a result, the Ruhr’s factories and mines ceased operating, sharply reducing Germany’s industrial output and worsening the fiscal crisis.

Q9. A worker earned 500 marks on 1 January 1923 when the exchange rate was \(1\) dollar = \(200\) marks. By 1 November 1923 the rate had fallen to \(1\) dollar = \(5{,}000\) marks. How many dollars could the worker buy on 1 November?

🔹 A. 0.1 dollars
🔹 B. 2.5 dollars
🔹 C. 5 dollars
🔹 D. 0.05 dollars

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

At the later rate, each mark is worth \(1/5{,}000\) of a dollar. Multiplying 500 marks by this value yields \(500 ÷ 5{,}000 = 0.1\) dollars, illustrating how rapidly the currency lost purchasing power.

Q10. Why did the French occupation of the Ruhr ultimately fail to achieve its goals?

🔹 A. French troops seized all coal output
🔹 B. German workers continued normal production
🔹 C. The occupation could not extract sufficient goods from factories
🔹 D. France withdrew voluntarily

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Although French forces controlled the territory, German workers adopted passive resistance, halting production and preventing the extraction of coal and steel. This stalemate meant the occupation did not generate the expected reparations revenue.

Q12. How did the economic situation change after the implementation of the Dawes Plan?

The Dawes Plan’s loan and restructured reparations restored confidence, leading to a surge in iron, steel, coal, chemicals, and electrical industries. Unemployment fell, wages began to stabilize, and the previously volatile currency regained value.

Suspending payments would have reduced the need for the government to print money to meet foreign obligations. However, the loss of foreign credit and continued war debts could have forced other fiscal measures, potentially worsening inflation rather than alleviating it.

Q24. If the exchange rate moved from \(20\) marks per dollar to \(5{,}000\) marks per dollar, what approximate percentage increase did the mark experience relative to the dollar?

Q31. Approximately how many marks did the £40 million Dawes loan represent if the exchange rate was \(1\) dollar = \(4\) marks and £1 = $4.86?

🔹 A. ≈ \(7.8 × 10^{8}\) marks
🔹 B. ≈ \(1.0 × 10^{9}\) marks
🔹 C. ≈ \(2.5 × 10^{9}\) marks
🔹 D. ≈ \(5.0 × 10^{9}\) marks

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

First convert £40 million to dollars: £40 million × $4.86/£ = $194.4 million. Then multiply by the mark‑to‑dollar rate: $194.4 million × 4 marks/$ ≈ 777.6 million marks, which is about \(7.8 × 10^{8}\) marks.
