Q1. Who was arrested and later executed for starting the Reichstag fire?

🔹 A. Marinus van der Lubbe
🔹 B. Ernst Röhm
🔹 C. Heinrich Himmler
🔹 D. Joseph Goebbels

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Marinus van der Lubbe, a Dutch communist, was apprehended at the scene of the fire, tried, and executed. Contemporary accounts and trial records consistently identify him as the sole arsonist, despite later debates about possible conspiracies involving other Nazi elements.

Q2. On what date did the Reichstag fire occur?

🔹 A. \(26\) February 1933
🔹 B. \(27\) February 1933
🔹 C. \(28\) February 1933
🔹 D. \(1\) March 1933

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The fire broke out on the night of \(27\) February 1933, damaging the parliamentary building severely. This date is documented in newspaper reports, police records, and eyewitness testimonies, establishing it as the pivotal moment that preceded the March elections and the subsequent political crackdown.

Q3. How many seats were required for an absolute majority in the Reichstag after the election?

🔹 A. 300
🔹 B. 312
🔹 C. \(324\)
🔹 D. 336

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Reichstag consisted of \(647\) seats, so a party needed more than half to command a majority. Half of \(647\) is \(323.5\); rounding up yields \(324\) seats, which became the benchmark that the Nazi Party fell short of despite its strong electoral performance.

Q4. Approximately what proportion of eligible voters participated in the election held on \(5\) March 1933?

🔹 A. 0.7
🔹 B. 0.8
🔹 C. 0.85
🔹 D. 0.9

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Official election statistics show that about ninety percent of the electorate cast a ballot on \(5\) March 1933. This exceptionally high turnout reflected the intense political mobilization of the period and provided the Nazis with a broad, though not majority, mandate to pursue their agenda.

Q5. Which conservative politician's support was essential for Hitler after the fire?

🔹 A. Gustav Stresemann
🔹 B. Franz von Papen
🔹 C. Otto von Bismarck
🔹 D. Wilhelm II

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Franz von Papen, a former chancellor and leader of the conservative bloc, offered crucial backing to Hitler after the fire. His endorsement enabled Hitler to form a coalition government, illustrating how the Nazis relied on established right‑wing figures to legitimize their rise to power.

Q6. How did the Reichstag fire allow Hitler to justify the suppression of political opposition?

🔹 A. By claiming a foreign invasion
🔹 B. By citing economic crisis
🔹 C. By invoking a health emergency
🔹 D. By asserting a communist threat

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Hitler portrayed the fire as evidence of an imminent communist uprising, using it to invoke emergency powers. This narrative enabled the Reichstag Fire Decree, which suspended civil liberties, authorized mass arrests, and paved the way for the Nazis to silence dissenting voices.

Q7. Which of the following statements reflects the most widely accepted view among historians regarding SA involvement in the fire?

🔹 A. The SA directly set the fire
🔹 B. The SA had no knowledge
🔹 C. Evidence is inconclusive but suggests possible complicity
🔹 D. The SA publicly condemned the fire

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Historians generally agree that definitive proof of SA orchestration is lacking, yet circumstantial evidence—such as internal communications and the timing of SA actions—suggests they may have been aware of or indirectly assisted the arson, leaving the issue unresolved.

Q8. What percentage of the total Reichstag seats did the Nazi Party win in the March \(5\) election?

🔹 A. \(44.5\%\)
🔹 B. \(50\%\)
🔹 C. \(35\%\)
🔹 D. \(60\%\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Nazis secured \(288\) seats out of a total of \(647\). Dividing \(288\) by \(647\) yields approximately \(0.445\), or \(44.5\%\). This calculation shows that although the party achieved a substantial share of parliamentary representation, it fell well short of an absolute majority.

Q9. Compare the Nazi vote share of \(44\%\) with their seat share of \(44.5\%\). Which statement best describes the relationship?

🔹 A. Vote share was higher than seat share
🔹 B. Seat share slightly exceeded vote share
🔹 C. Both were identical
🔹 D. Seat share was far lower than vote share

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Nazis received roughly \(44\%\) of the popular vote, which translated into a slightly higher seat proportion of \(44.5\%\). This modest advantage arose from the electoral system's mechanics, indicating a near‑proportional conversion but still insufficient for unilateral governance.

Q10. How many additional seats would the Nazis have needed to reach the required majority of \(324\) seats?

🔹 A. 30
🔹 B. 32
🔹 C. \(36\)
🔹 D. 40

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Subtracting the Nazis' actual count of \(288\) seats from the majority threshold of \(324\) yields \(324 - 288 = 36\) seats. This shortfall forced Hitler to seek coalition partners, notably Papen and Hugenberg, to form a government despite lacking an outright parliamentary majority.

🔹 A. Strengthening democratic institutions
🔹 B. Reducing political violence
🔹 C. Creating a climate of fear and eliminating opposition
🔹 D. Encouraging foreign investment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The mass arrests suppressed organized communist activity, instilled terror among dissenters, and cleared the way for the Nazi regime to consolidate power by removing a primary source of political resistance, thereby facilitating the transition to an authoritarian state.

Q12. If the Reichstag fire had not occurred, which of the following outcomes is most plausible for the March \(5\) election results?

Q13. Which political group did the Nazi regime primarily blame for the Reichstag fire?

🔹 A. Social Democrats

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Nazis swiftly accused the Communist Party of orchestrating the arson, using the accusation to legitimize emergency measures, justify mass arrests, and sway public opinion against left‑wing opponents, thereby consolidating their own authority and marginalizing genuine political rivals.

Q14. Which of the following best characterizes the historiographical debate on SA involvement in the Reichstag fire?

📖 Explanation:

Historians remain divided; some argue that SA members facilitated the fire, citing internal Nazi documents, while others point to lack of conclusive proof. The debate reflects broader tensions about the extent of Nazi internal conspiracies and the reliability of contemporary testimonies.

Q15. Based on primary source analysis, to what extent can the Reichstag fire be considered a pivotal factor in Hitler's consolidation of power?

🔹 A. Minor factor
🔹 B. One of many equal factors
🔹 C. Central catalyst
🔹 D. Irrelevant to consolidation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Primary sources such as the Reichstag Fire Decree, contemporary newspaper reports, and Hitler's speeches emphasize the fire as a justification for suspending civil liberties and suppressing opposition, making it a central catalyst that accelerated the transition from democracy to authoritarian rule.

Q16. If the Nazis had achieved an outright majority, how might their legislative agenda regarding civil liberties have differed from the historical record?

🔹 A. They would have maintained full democratic freedoms
🔹 B. They would have introduced even harsher restrictions
🔹 C. They would have left civil liberties unchanged
🔹 D. They would have delegated civil liberty decisions to the judiciary

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

With a full majority, the Nazis would not have needed to moderate policies to appease coalition partners, likely resulting in even more sweeping legislation that further eroded civil liberties, such as broader censorship, expanded police powers, and accelerated dismantling of independent institutions.

Q17. How did the Reichstag fire facilitate the passage of the Enabling Act within the broader legal transformation of Nazi Germany?

🔹 A. It delayed the Act by causing political turmoil
🔹 B. It created a climate of emergency that justified the Act
🔹 C. It had no impact on the Act's passage
🔹 D. It led to the Act being rejected by the Reichstag

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The fire generated a nationwide sense of crisis, which the Nazis exploited to argue that extraordinary powers were necessary. This atmosphere of fear helped persuade the Reichstag to approve the Enabling Act, granting Hitler legislative authority without parliamentary consent and cementing the dictatorship.
