What is March 1933 German election Hitler?

The March 1933 German election was Hitler's last attempt to gain a legal majority in the Reichstag (parliament), and even though he was already Chancellor, his Nazis used widespread violence, intimidation against opponents, and control over state resources to campaign, yet they still failed to win an absolute majority, forcing Hitler to rely on coalition partners and paving the way for his next power grab.

📝 All March 1933 German election Hitler MCQs

Q1. Who was appointed minister of the interior for Prussia?

A. Hermann Goering ✅
B. Joseph Goebbels
C. Heinrich Himmler
D. Adolf Hitler

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Hermann Goering, a leading Nazi, was appointed minister of the interior for Prussia, giving him control over the state police. This appointment allowed the Nazis to replace senior officers with loyal party members, facilitating the violent tactics described in the campaign leading up to the 5 March 1933 election.

Q2. How many auxiliary policemen were called up?

A. 30000
B. 40000
C. 50000 ✅
D. 60000

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Nazi regime mobilized a large auxiliary force to enforce its agenda during the election. Approximately 50,000 auxiliary policemen, drawn mainly from the SA and SS, were called up, providing the manpower needed to intimidate opponents and secure Nazi dominance at the polls.

Q3. Which groups were allowed to hold meetings without interference?

A. Nazis and nationalists ✅
B. Communists and socialists
C. All parties
D. No one

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Nazis and their nationalist allies were permitted to hold public meetings without police interference, reflecting the regime’s strategic use of state power to amplify their message. In contrast, communist and socialist gatherings were systematically disrupted, demonstrating the selective enforcement that favored right‑wing groups during the March 1933 election.

Q4. Which authority likely turned a blind eye to a disrupted communist meeting?

A. The Prussian police chief
B. The Reichstag
C. The Nazi interior minister ✅
D. The local mayor

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Prussian interior minister, a Nazi official, oversaw the police apparatus and directed its actions. Because the minister was loyal to the Nazi Party, the police often ignored assaults on communists while allowing SA and SS members to act with impunity, illustrating the politicized nature of law enforcement.

Q5. Why did nationalists support Nazi tactics against communists?

A. They wanted to eliminate left-wing opposition
B. They sought Nazi support for a future coalition
C. They were ideologically aligned
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Nationalists saw the Nazis as a tool to eradicate communism, which they regarded as a primary threat. By supporting Nazi violence against left‑wing opponents, they hoped to eliminate political rivals and strengthen a right‑leaning coalition, thereby securing their own influence in the new power structure.

Q6. What impact did Nazi use of radio have on voter turnout?

A. Increased voter turnout for Nazis ✅
B. Decreased overall turnout
C. No effect
D. Only affected rural areas

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Radio broadcasts were a critical instrument for the Nazis to shape public opinion. By saturating the airwaves with propaganda, they mobilized supporters, suppressed dissenting voices, and created an atmosphere that encouraged higher turnout for Nazi candidates, contributing to the decisive victory on 5 March 1933.

Q7. If the SA had not been allowed to use firearms, how might violence differ?

A. It would have been similar
B. It would have decreased significantly ✅
C. It would have increased
D. It would have caused chaos among Nazis

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

If the SA had been prohibited from using firearms, the intimidation tactics would have been less lethal, potentially reducing the overall level of violence. However, the SA’s presence alone could still have exerted pressure, though the threat of gunfire amplified fear and forced compliance among opponents.

Q8. How did the auxiliary police force of 50,000 affect Nazi power consolidation?

A. It was double previous numbers
B. It was half previous numbers
C. It was similar to previous numbers
D. It was unprecedented ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The deployment of an unprecedented auxiliary police force of 50,000 officers marked a stark escalation compared with earlier elections, where police numbers were far lower and politically neutral. This massive mobilization enabled the Nazis to enforce voter intimidation on a scale that effectively suppressed opposition and secured their electoral dominance.

Q9. Which constitutional principle was most violated by Nazi control of press and radio?

A. Freedom of assembly
B. Freedom of speech ✅
C. Right to bear arms
D. Separation of powers

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Nazis’ domination of press and radio directly contravened the constitutional guarantee of freedom of speech. By monopolizing information channels, they eliminated independent reporting, manipulated public perception, and prevented dissenting views from reaching the electorate, thereby undermining a core democratic principle during the 1933 election.

Q10. Which organization originally formed as Hitler’s personal bodyguard?

A. SA
B. SS ✅
C. Gestapo
D. Wehrmacht

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Schutzstaffeln, abbreviated as SS, originated as Hitler’s personal bodyguard unit before expanding into a powerful paramilitary organization. While the SA functioned as the original stormtrooper corps, the SS evolved to enforce ideological conformity and later played a central role in the regime’s repressive apparatus.

Q11. On what date was the election held?

A. 05-Mar-33 ✅
B. 15-Jan-32
C. 10-Apr-33
D. 01-May-32

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The election took place on 5 March 1933, a date that marked the Nazis’ first national vote after Hitler had become Chancellor. This election was pivotal because it allowed the regime to claim a democratic mandate while simultaneously employing coercive tactics to suppress opposition.

Q12. How did replacing senior police officers affect law enforcement?

A. It made police neutral
B. It increased Nazi bias ✅
C. It decreased police presence
D. It had no effect

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Replacing senior police officers with reliable Nazis shifted the policing apparatus from a neutral law‑enforcement body to a partisan instrument. This change ensured that police actions favored Nazi objectives, such as targeting communists, while ignoring or protecting Nazi paramilitary groups, thereby compromising the integrity of public safety.

Q13. Which evidence supports labeling the election a “pseudo‑election”?

A. Use of SA violence ✅
B. High voter turnout
C. International observers
D. Balanced media coverage

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A historian labeling the March 1933 election as a “pseudo‑election” would point to evidence such as state‑sponsored violence by the SA and SS, the manipulation of media, and the systematic suppression of communist and socialist candidates, all of which indicate that the vote was neither free nor fair.

Q14. Which group was given permission to use firearms if necessary?

A. The SA
B. The SS
C. Both SA and SS ✅
D. The regular police

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Both the SA and SS were granted explicit permission to employ firearms when necessary, reflecting the regime’s reliance on paramilitary force to intimidate and eliminate political opponents. This authorization blurred the line between civilian policing and party militias, reinforcing the atmosphere of fear during the election.

Q15. Who were identified as ‘enemies of the state’?

A. Communists and socialists ✅
B. Farmers
C. Industrialists
D. Religious leaders

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In the context of the 1933 campaign, the Nazis identified communists and socialists as the principal ‘enemies of the state.’ These groups were targeted for repression, with their meetings disrupted and members assaulted, illustrating the regime’s strategy of eliminating left‑wing opposition to consolidate power.

Q16. How did nationalist‑Nazi collaboration alter Prussia’s political landscape?

A. It weakened Nazi dominance
B. It solidified a right‑wing coalition ✅
C. It led to immediate democratic reforms
D. It caused civil war

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The alliance between nationalists and Nazis after the election produced a reinforced right‑wing bloc that dominated Prussian politics. This partnership marginalized centrist and leftist forces, facilitated the implementation of authoritarian policies, and paved the way for the eventual dissolution of democratic institutions in the region.

Q17. What best describes the role of the press in the March 1933 election?

A. Independent reporting
B. State propaganda ✅
C. Foreign news coverage
D. Commercial advertising

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

During the March 1933 election, the press functioned primarily as a vehicle for state propaganda rather than independent journalism. By controlling newspaper content and broadcasting party messages, the Nazis shaped voter perceptions, suppressed dissenting opinions, and ensured that the electorate received a one‑sided narrative supporting their candidates.
