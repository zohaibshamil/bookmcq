📝 All Why Hitler and Nazis replaced Weimar Republic MCQs

Q1. Why did the acceptance of the Versailles Treaty undermine the legitimacy of the Weimar Republic?

🔹 A. It boosted Germany’s industrial output
🔹 B. It was seen as a humiliating and unpopular peace
🔹 C. It granted unlimited military expansion
🔹 D. It eliminated all political parties

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty forced Germany to accept harsh war‑guilt clauses, reparations and military limits, which many Germans viewed as a national humiliation. Because the new republic signed the treaty, it became associated with defeat and dishonour, eroding popular support and providing a rallying point for nationalist opposition.

Q2. Which myth blamed the democratic politicians for Germany’s defeat in World War I?

🔹 A. The 'iron fist' legend
🔹 B. The 'golden age' narrative
🔹 C. The 'stab‑in‑the‑back' legend
🔹 D. The 'peaceful transition' story

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ‘stab‑in‑the‑back’ legend claimed that civilian leaders had betrayed the nation by agreeing to the Versailles terms. This narrative suggested that the military was not truly defeated on the battlefield, shifting blame onto democratic politicians and fueling resentment toward the Weimar government.

Q3. In the 1928 Reichstag election, which party held the greatest number of seats?

🔹 A. Social Democratic Party (SPD)
🔹 B. German National Party (DNVP)
🔹 C. Catholic Centre Party (Zentrum)
🔹 D. German Communist Party (KPD)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The SPD won 153 seats, making it the largest parliamentary group in the 1928 election. The DNVP, Zentrum, and KPD each held fewer seats, so the Social Democrats were the dominant force, though still far short of an outright majority.

Q4. What was the main structural weakness of the parliamentary system introduced by the Weimar Constitution?

🔹 A. It used a first‑past‑the‑post electoral system
🔹 B. It allowed the president to rule by decree
🔹 C. It mandated a single‑party state
🔹 D. It relied on proportional representation producing fragmented coalitions

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The constitution’s reliance on proportional representation meant many small parties entered the Reichstag, preventing any single party from achieving a majority. This fragmentation forced coalition governments that were often unstable and short‑lived, undermining effective governance.

Q5. Who actually requested the armistice that ended fighting in 1918, contrary to the popular ‘stab‑in‑the‑back’ story?

🔹 A. General Ludendorff
🔹 B. Kaiser Wilhelm II
🔹 C. Field Marshal Hindenburg
🔹 D. President Friedrich Ebert

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Historical records show that General Erich Ludendorff, not the democratically elected politicians, urged the Kaiser to accept an armistice when the military situation collapsed. This fact contradicts the myth that civilian leaders betrayed the army, yet the narrative persisted to delegitimize the republic.

Q6. If a governing coalition needed at least 200 seats to pass legislation, could the Social Democrats (153 seats) form a stable government without the Nazis (12 seats) by allying only with the Catholic Centre (62 seats) and the German People’s Party (45 seats)?

🔹 A. Yes, because the total exceeds 200
🔹 B. No, because the total is insufficient
🔹 C. Yes, because they could rely on support from left‑wing liberals
🔹 D. No, because they would still need the Nazis to reach a majority

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Adding the SPD’s 153 seats to the Centre’s 62 and the DVP’s 45 yields 260 seats, comfortably above the 200‑seat threshold. Therefore, a coalition without the Nazis could govern, illustrating how the fragmented party system still allowed majority formations when parties combined strategically.

Q7. How did the traditional admiration for the army and officer class weaken democratic norms in the Weimar Republic?

🔹 A. It encouraged soldiers to run for office, increasing military influence
🔹 B. It led to the abolition of the Reichstag
🔹 C. It fostered the belief that civilian politicians were less competent than military leaders, delegitimizing elected bodies
🔹 D. It resulted in the army refusing to defend the borders

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The cultural esteem for the officer corps created a perception that military expertise trumped civilian decision‑making. Consequently, many Germans mistrusted parliamentary politicians, viewing them as weak, which eroded confidence in democratic institutions and opened space for authoritarian alternatives.

Q8. Assuming the Reichstag comprised 500 seats, would a coalition of the Social Democrats (153 seats) and the Centre Party (62 seats) reach the required \\(\\frac{1}{2}\\) (250 seats) to pass ordinary legislation?

🔹 A. Yes, because they would have a majority
🔹 B. No, because their combined total is insufficient
🔹 C. Yes, but only with additional support from smaller parties
🔹 D. No, because they need a majority of three parties

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The combined strength of the SPD and Zentrum equals 215 seats, which falls short of the 250‑seat threshold required for a simple majority in a 500‑seat chamber. Without further partners, the coalition could not pass ordinary legislation, highlighting the need for broader alliances.

Q9. Which factor most directly contributed to the political instability of the Weimar Republic?

🔹 A. Proportional representation producing fragmented parties
🔹 B. Strong presidential powers that bypassed the parliament
🔹 C. Economic prosperity that reduced public interest in politics
🔹 D. One‑party dominance that suppressed dissent

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The use of proportional representation allowed many small parties to enter the Reichstag, making it difficult for any single group to command a majority. This fragmentation led to frequent coalition changes and fragile governments, which were central to the Republic’s chronic instability.

Q10. What percentage of the Reichstag did the Nazis hold after the 1928 election if the chamber had 500 seats?

🔹 A. 0.012
🔹 B. 0.035
🔹 C. 0.024
🔹 D. 0.05

📖 Explanation:

With 12 seats out of a 500‑seat Reichstag, the Nazis occupied \\(\\frac{12}{500}\\times100 = 2.4\\%\\) of the chamber. This modest representation limited their legislative influence but still provided a platform for propaganda and future growth.

Q11. A historian claims the ‘stab‑in‑the‑back’ myth was used to legitimize extremist politics. Which piece of evidence best supports this claim?

🔹 A. The Reichstag fire was blamed on communists
🔹 B. The myth was repeatedly cited in Nazi propaganda
🔹 C. The treaty was never ratified by the German parliament
🔹 D. The army dissolved after the war

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Nazi pamphlets, speeches, and newspaper articles repeatedly invoked the ‘stab‑in‑the‑back’ narrative to portray the democratic government as traitorous. By constantly referencing this myth, the Nazis framed themselves as defenders of national honor, thereby gaining popular support for extremist actions.

Q12. How did the burden of reparations affect the Weimar Republic’s perceived legitimacy among the German middle class?

🔹 A. It increased confidence because reparations funded social programs
🔹 B. It had little impact as the middle class was insulated
🔹 C. It caused hyperinflation that eroded trust
🔹 D. It led to a surge in foreign investment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Reparations strained the economy, contributing to the hyperinflation of 1923, which wiped out savings and destabilized the middle class. The resulting loss of purchasing power diminished faith in the government’s ability to protect citizens, fostering resentment and making extremist solutions more attractive.

Q13. If reparations demanded \\(\\frac{2}{3}\\) of Germany’s coal output and the country produced 100 units, how many units had to be delivered to the Allies?

🔹 A. 66.7 units
🔹 B. 33.3 units
🔹 C. 75 units
🔹 D. 50 units

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Two‑thirds of 100 units equals \\(\\frac{2}{3}\\times100 = 66.666...\\) units, which rounds to 66.7 units. This sizable portion illustrates the heavy economic strain imposed on Germany, contributing to widespread dissatisfaction with the Weimar government.

Q14. Compared with the French Third Republic’s parliamentary system, which feature of the Weimar Constitution most often resulted in short‑lived governments?

🔹 A. Direct election of the president
🔹 B. Mandatory two‑round voting
🔹 C. Proportional representation leading to fragmented parties
🔹 D. Fixed four‑year terms for the cabinet

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Weimar Constitution’s reliance on proportional representation produced a highly fragmented Reichstag, making it difficult to form stable majorities. In contrast, the French Third Republic, while also parliamentary, employed mechanisms that occasionally produced more cohesive blocs, reducing the frequency of government turnover.

Q15. Rank the three factors—Versailles humiliation, army prestige, and proportional representation—in order of influence on the rise of extremist parties, with 1 being most influential.

🔹 A. 1‑Versailles, 2‑Army, 3‑Proportional
🔹 B. 1‑Army, 2‑Versailles, 3‑Proportional
🔹 C. 1‑Proportional, 2‑Versailles, 3‑Army
🔹 D. 1‑Versailles, 2‑Proportional, 3‑Army

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Scholars argue that the prestige of the army created a powerful cultural foundation for authoritarian leaders, while the humiliation of Versailles added a sense of grievance. Proportional representation, though contributory, was less decisive than the other two factors, placing the army at the top of the influence hierarchy.
