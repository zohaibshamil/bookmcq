What is Population growth in Third World developing countries?

Developing countries experienced rapid population expansion in the mid-to-late 20th century as death rates fell quickly due to imported medical technologies while birth rates remained high, leading to a youthful age structure and significant demographic momentum.

Q1. Which South Asian country is noted for experiencing severe overcrowding due to rapid population growth?

A.India ✅
B.China
C.Nepal
D.Sri Lanka
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Providing India as the example links demographic pressure to urban challenges; its large population created land scarcity, pushing many to cities where housing and jobs were insufficient, leading to shanty‑towns. China, Nepal, and Sri Lanka are not identified in this context, making them incorrect choices.

Q2. Why does rapid population growth create housing shortages in urban areas?

A.High birth rates
B.Rural poverty
C.Overcrowding outpacing housing construction ✅
D.Climate change
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Rapid population growth forces large numbers of people to leave rural areas and seek livelihoods in urban centers. When the influx of migrants exceeds the rate at which new houses, infrastructure, and jobs can be built, a shortage of adequate housing emerges. This shortage compels many newcomers to live in informal settlements lacking basic services. Option C captures this cause‑effect relationship, whereas the other choices either describe unrelated factors or misidentify the primary mechanism behind housing deficits.

Q3. If a city in Latin America provides clean water to its informal settlements, which immediate effect is most likely?

A.Reduction in water‑borne diseases ✅
B.Increase in migration
C.Higher property prices
D.Lower employment
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Providing clean water to informal settlements directly improves public health by reducing exposure to contaminated sources that cause diarrheal diseases. When water‑borne illnesses decline, communities experience lower child mortality and better overall productivity. This immediate health benefit is more likely than the other outcomes, which either describe longer‑term economic effects or unrelated consequences. Therefore, option A correctly identifies the most immediate impact, while options B, C, and D misinterpret the primary effect of water provision.

Q4. How does insufficient arable land in densely populated nations influence the pattern of urban migration?

A.Pushes people to remain rural
B.Encourages migration to cities ✅
C.Leads to emigration abroad
D.Reduces birth rates
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When a country lacks sufficient arable land to support its growing population, many people find it increasingly difficult to sustain themselves in rural areas. This scarcity pushes them to seek employment and better living conditions in cities, accelerating urban migration. Option B accurately describes this push factor, whereas option A suggests the opposite effect, option C introduces emigration abroad which is not directly tied to land shortage, and option D incorrectly links land scarcity to reduced birth rates.

Q5. A government plans to limit rural‑to‑urban migration by increasing rural job opportunities. Which unintended consequence is most probable?

A.Slower urban growth
B.Rise in informal settlements ✅
C.Decreased rural fertility
D.Improved urban infrastructure
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: If a government creates more jobs in rural areas to curb migration, the people who still move to cities may find the housing market unable to absorb the extra demand, leading to a rise in informal settlements. While the policy aims to reduce urban crowding, limited housing supply can cause unintended growth of slums. Option B reflects this likely unintended consequence. Option A suggests slower growth, which is possible but less direct; option C assumes demographic changes not guaranteed; option D describes a positive outcome not typically linked to limited migration policies.
