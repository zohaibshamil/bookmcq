What is UN Population Commission role and functions?

The UN Population Commission (now the Commission on Population and Development) monitors global demographic trends, advises the Economic and Social Council on population policies, and promotes international cooperation to address issues like reproductive health, migration, and sustainable development.

📝 All UN Population Commission role and functions MCQs

Q1. In what year was the United Nations Population Commission established as part of the UN?

A.1945 ✅
B.1950
C.1960
D.1970
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The commission was created when the United Nations itself was founded in 1945, making that the correct year. 1950, 1960, and 1970 are later dates that do not correspond to the founding of the UN or its early agencies. Knowing the establishment date helps students place the commission in its historical context, showing how early population issues were recognized globally.

Q2. What was the primary reason the United Nations promoted birth‑control programmes in the 1950s?

A.To reduce urban overcrowding
B.To accelerate economic development by lowering dependency ratios ✅
C.To increase agricultural production
D.To support religious institutions
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The UN encouraged birth‑control mainly to lower the dependency ratio, allowing faster economic growth as fewer dependents meant more productive workers. Overcrowding and agriculture are indirect effects, not the main driver, and supporting religious institutions contradicts the secular, health‑focused agenda. Understanding this motive clarifies why family‑planning became a key UN strategy for development.

Q3. Country X wants to adopt a family‑planning policy similar to China’s two‑child limit but fears cultural resistance. Which strategy aligns best with the UN Population Commission’s historical approach?

A.Mandate legal penalties for a third child
B.Provide free contraceptives and public education ✅
C.Ban all contraceptives
D.Encourage larger families for labor
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The UN historically used education and affordable contraceptives to change behaviour, rather than coercive laws or bans. Legal penalties would likely provoke backlash, while banning contraceptives defeats the goal of reducing fertility. Promoting larger families opposes the commission’s aim. Offering free methods and information mirrors past UN campaigns that achieved gradual acceptance.

Q4. How did the differing maximum‑children policies of African governments and China reflect the UN Population Commission’s influence on national policy?

A.Demonstrated uniform adoption of UN recommendations
B.Showed that UN only suggested, leaving countries to set their own limits
C.Indicated UN provided a framework that countries adapted to local contexts ✅
D.Revealed UN mandated two‑child policy globally
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The UN offered guidelines rather than mandates, allowing each nation to tailor limits to its circumstances. Africa’s three‑child recommendation and China’s two‑child rule both stem from UN encouragement, yet they differ, showing flexibility. The other options misrepresent the UN’s role: it did not enforce uniform rules, nor did it issue global mandates, and it certainly did not leave countries without any guidance.

Q5. Suppose a country with a population of 200 million has a fertility rate of 3.2 children per woman. If the UN target is to reduce the rate to 2.1 within 20 years, what average annual reduction in children per woman is required?

A.0.055 ✅
B.0.065
C.0.075
D.0.085
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The needed reduction is \(3.2-2.1 = 1.1\) children per woman over 20 years, which equals \(1.1 ÷ 20 = 0.055\) per year. Options 0.065, 0.075, and 0.085 represent larger declines that would overshoot the target. Calculating the yearly change helps students apply demographic concepts to policy planning, illustrating how UN goals translate into measurable national objectives.

Q6. If a South American country with strong Roman Catholic influence refuses artificial birth control, which alternative recommended by the UN Population Commission could it adopt to still lower fertility rates?

A.Promote abstinence education
B.Encourage natural family planning methods
C.Provide financial incentives for small families
D.All of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The UN acknowledges cultural and religious constraints and suggests multiple non‑artificial options: abstinence programs, natural family planning, and economic incentives for smaller families. Selecting only one of these would limit the country’s toolkit, while banning all alternatives contradicts the UN’s flexible, health‑focused approach. Therefore, the comprehensive answer includes all viable strategies.

Q7. Which of the following best describes the UN Population Commission’s role in the 1950s?

A.Enforcing mandatory population quotas
B.Coordinating global family‑planning initiatives ✅
C.Providing military assistance
D.Managing international trade
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: During the 1950s the commission primarily organized worldwide family‑planning efforts, encouraging governments to adopt birth‑control measures. It did not impose quotas, nor was it involved in military or trade matters. Recognizing its coordinating function helps students see how the UN facilitated the spread of population policies across diverse nations.
