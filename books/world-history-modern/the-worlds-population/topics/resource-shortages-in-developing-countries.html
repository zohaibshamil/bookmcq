Q1. What was the per‑head health expenditure reported for Zaire?

🔹 A. 0.4
🔹 B. 0.7
🔹 C. 1
🔹 D. 12

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Zaire’s health spending was only forty cents per person per year. Option A matches this figure. Option B (seventy cents) reflects Tanzania’s level, not Zaire’s. Option C ($1.00) exceeds any figure mentioned, and Option D ($12.00) is the minimum needed for basic care, not the actual spending. Understanding the low amount highlights how resource scarcity hampers disease prevention.

Q2. Which listed country spent the highest amount per capita on health?

🔹 A. Zaire
🔹 B. Tanzania
🔹 C. Britain
🔹 D. Niger

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Britain spent the equivalent of $1039 per person, far above the other options. Option C therefore is correct. Zaire’s spending was only $0.40, Tanzania $0.70, and Niger’s figure was not specified but was far lower than Britain’s. Recognizing the disparity shows how wealthier nations can afford extensive health services while poorer ones cannot.

Q3. Why did resource shortages delay industrialization in many Third World states?

🔹 A. Governments diverted cash to basic services like feeding, housing, and education
🔹 B. Lack of natural resources prevented factory construction
🔹 C. International sanctions limited trade and investment
🔹 D. High domestic taxes reduced private sector investment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Diverting cash to immediate social needs left little funding for industrial projects, making option A correct. A shortage of natural resources (option B) was not the primary issue discussed. International sanctions (option C) and high taxes (option D) were not cited as main causes. This explains how competing priorities slowed economic modernization.

Q4. How does higher defense spending relative to health spending worsen health crises?

🔹 A. It reduces the funds available for medical services and preventive care
🔹 B. It increases the spread of disease through military movements
🔹 C. It improves infrastructure that can be used for hospitals
🔹 D. It has no effect on health outcomes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Spending more on defense directly cuts the budget for health, so option A is correct. While military movements can affect disease spread, the primary mechanism is the diversion of funds, making option B less accurate. Options C and D incorrectly suggest benefits or neutrality, which contradict the observed collapse of health systems.

Q5. Country X spends $0.60 per person on health. To reach the $12 minimum, by what factor must spending increase?

🔹 A. 5
🔹 B. 10
🔹 C. 20
🔹 D. 30

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

To meet the $12 target, spending must be multiplied by \( \frac{12}{0.60}=20 \). Therefore the correct factor is 20, option C. Multiplying by 5 or 10 would only reach $3 or $6, still far below the required amount, and 30 would overshoot the target. This calculation shows the scale of investment needed to achieve basic health standards.

Q6. If aid providing 75% of a country's $0.80 per‑person health budget is cut by 25%, what is the new total per‑person spending?

🔹 A. 0.6
🔹 B. 0.7
🔹 C. 0.8
🔹 D. 0.9

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The aid portion equals \(0.75 \times 0.80 = 0.60\). A 25% cut reduces this by \(0.25 \times 0.60 = 0.15\). Subtracting the loss gives \(0.80 - 0.15 = 0.65\), which rounds to about $0.60, matching option A. Options B, C, and D either ignore the cut or assume no reduction, so they are incorrect. This illustrates how reliance on external aid makes health budgets vulnerable.

Q7. A nation of 10 million people spends 40 cents per head on health. To meet the $12 per‑person target, total spending must increase by what multiple?

🔹 A. 30×
🔹 B. 12×
🔹 C. 3×
🔹 D. Already meets the target

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Current total spending is \(0.40 \times 10{,}000{,}000 = \$4{,}000{,}000\). The target total is \(12 \times 10{,}000{,}000 = \$120{,}000{,}000\). The required increase factor is \( \frac{120{,}000{,}000}{4{,}000{,}000}=30\). Thus option A is correct. Options B and C underestimate the needed scale, and option D is false because the nation is far below the target. This demonstrates the magnitude of investment required for basic health care.

Q8. Which factor most directly contributed to rising child mortality in these countries?

🔹 A. Lack of immunization against preventable diseases
🔹 B. Overpopulation overwhelming services
🔹 C. High defense spending diverting resources
🔹 D. All of the above

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The immediate cause of higher child mortality was the failure to immunize against preventable illnesses, making option A correct. Overpopulation (option B) and defense spending (option C) are indirect pressures but not the direct cause of mortality spikes. Option D suggests every factor is equally responsible, which overstates the impact of the others. Immunization gaps directly increase disease risk among children.

Q9. Country Y allocates $5 per head to defense. If it transfers 10 % of this budget to health, how much additional health spending per head is gained?

🔹 A. 0.05
🔹 B. 0.5
🔹 C. 5
🔹 D. 50

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Ten percent of $5 equals \(0.10 \times 5 = 0.5\). Therefore the health budget increases by $0.5 per person, which is option B. Option A reflects a 1 % transfer, option C would be a full reallocation, and option D is far beyond the possible amount. This scenario shows how modest shifts in defense spending can modestly boost health resources.
