Q1. Who was the President of the United States who decided to intervene in Korea in 1950?

🔹 A. Dwight D. Eisenhower
🔹 B. Harry S. Truman
🔹 C. John F. Kennedy
🔹 D. Franklin D. Roosevelt

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

President Harry S. Truman made the decision to intervene after viewing the North Korean invasion as a direct challenge to the United States and a part of a broader communist expansion strategy. His resolve was shaped by concerns over Soviet influence and the desire to uphold the newly formed United Nations.

Q2. Which international organization did Truman aim to support by intervening in Korea?

🔹 A. League of Nations
🔹 B. North Atlantic Treaty Organization
🔹 C. United Nations
🔹 D. European Union

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Truman emphasized the importance of backing the United Nations, which had replaced the ineffective League of Nations after World War II. By supporting the UN, he intended to demonstrate a collective security commitment and avoid repeating past mistakes where great powers failed to enforce peace.

Q3. Who commanded the United Nations forces during the early Korean War operations?

🔹 B. General Douglas MacArthur
🔹 C. General George Patton
🔹 D. General Omar Bradley

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

General Douglas MacArthur was appointed commander of the UN forces, directing the amphibious landing at Inchon and subsequent advances. His leadership reflected the United States' willingness to place its senior military officers in charge of multinational operations to ensure coordinated action against North Korean aggression.

Q4. Which South Korean port remained under UN control before the Inchon landing?

Q31. Calculate the probability that a UN resolution would pass if the Soviet delegation voted against it, assuming 11 members were present and each non‑Soviet member independently supported the US stance with probability \(0.7\).

🔹 A. \(0.7^{10}\)
🔹 B. \(0.7^{9}\)
🔹 C. \(0.7^{11}\)
🔹 D. \(0.7^{8}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

With 11 members voting and the Soviet veto absent, at least 6 affirmative votes are needed for passage. The probability that exactly \(k\) out of 10 non‑Soviet members vote yes follows a binomial distribution \(P(k)=\binom{10}{k}0.7^{k}0.3^{10-k}\). Summing \(P(k)\) for \(k\ge6\) yields approximately \(0.7^{9}\) as the dominant term, indicating a high likelihood of adoption.
