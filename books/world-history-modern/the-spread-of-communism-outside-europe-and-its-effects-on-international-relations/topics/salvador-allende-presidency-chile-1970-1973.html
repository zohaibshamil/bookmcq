Q1. In which year was Salvador Allende elected President of Chile?

🔹 A. 1969
🔹 B. 1970
🔹 C. 1971
🔹 D. 1972

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The presidential election that brought Salvador Allende to power took place in 1970, marking a historic shift toward a socialist government in Chile. This year is widely documented in both Chilean and international histories as the moment when the Unidad Popular won the presidency, ending the centrist rule of Eduardo Frei.

Q2. What was the name of the left‑wing coalition that supported Allende?

🔹 A. Unidad Popular
🔹 B. National Front
🔹 C. Christian Democratic Alliance
🔹 D. Socialist Bloc

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The coalition that backed Allende was called the Unidad Popular, a broad alliance of socialist, communist, and other left‑leaning parties. It functioned as the vehicle for Allende’s campaign, coordinating grassroots mobilization, voter outreach, and policy platforms that emphasized nationalization and social reform.

Q3. By how much did inflation decrease during Eduardo Frei’s early presidency?

🔹 A. from 38% to 25%
🔹 B. from 45% to 30%
🔹 C. from 50% to 35%
🔹 D. from 60% to 40%

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Frei’s administration succeeded in reducing inflation from a peak of 38 percent to 25 percent through fiscal restraint and modest price controls. This reduction was a notable achievement at the time, demonstrating the government’s ability to stabilize the economy without resorting to drastic measures.

Q4. A farmer receives a 2‑hectare plot after land reform, with average productivity of 1.5 tons per hectare. How much wheat can he expect to produce?

🔹 A. 1.5 tons
🔹 B. 2.0 tons
🔹 C. 3.0 tons
🔹 D. 4.5 tons

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Multiplying the area (2 ha) by the productivity per hectare (1.5 tons) yields \(2 \times 1.5 = 3.0\) tons. This straightforward calculation illustrates how land redistribution aimed to increase agricultural output by allocating productive land to previously landless peasants.

Q5. A drought in 1969 caused a loss of one‑third of the harvest. If the original harvest was 900,000 tons, how much was lost?

🔹 A. 200,000 tons
🔹 B. 300,000 tons
🔹 C. 400,000 tons
🔹 D. 500,000 tons

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

One‑third of 900,000 tons is calculated as \( \frac{1}{3} \times 900{,}000 = 300{,}000\) tons. The loss contributed to food shortages, higher import needs, and a resurgence of inflation, exacerbating social tensions ahead of the 1970 election.

Allende’s government nationalized copper mines, textiles, and banks, but agriculture remained largely in private hands. The decision reflected a strategic focus on sectors deemed vital for export earnings and industrial development, while avoiding direct control over the broader agrarian sector.

Q8. Allende introduced a 40 % wage increase while freezing prices. If a worker earned $100 before, what was the new nominal wage and what was the real‑wage change assuming inflation stayed at 30 %?

🔹 B. $140 nominal, 40 % real increase
🔹 C. $130 nominal, 10 % real increase
🔹 D. $130 nominal, 40 % real increase

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The nominal wage rose to $140 (\$100 × 1.40). With inflation at 30 %, the real wage change is approximately 40 % − 30 % = 10 % increase. This demonstrates how nominal gains can translate into modest real improvements when price stability is enforced.
