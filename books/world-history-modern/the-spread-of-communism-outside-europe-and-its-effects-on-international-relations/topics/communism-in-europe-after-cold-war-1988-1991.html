What is Communism in Europe after Cold War 1988-1991?

Communism in Europe collapsed between 1988 and 1991 as Soviet reforms weakened Moscow’s grip, leading to peaceful revolutions in Eastern Bloc countries, the fall of the Berlin Wall, and the dissolution of the USSR, effectively ending communist rule across the continent.

Q1. In which month and year did the Solidarity trade union begin its massive anti‑government strikes in Poland?

A.Aug-88 ✅
B.Jul-88
C.Sept-88
D.Jun-88

📖 Explanation: The strikes started in August 1988, marking the first large‑scale public challenge to communist rule in Poland. This action set off a chain reaction that eventually led to free elections and the weakening of Soviet‑backed regimes across Eastern Europe.

D.Socialist Party

📖 Explanation: Solidarity, the independent trade‑union movement, captured the majority of seats in the June 1989 elections, a historic defeat for the communist establishment and a catalyst for further democratic reforms throughout the Eastern Bloc.

Q3. What was the primary reason for the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989?

Q4. Which treaty, signed in November 1990, declared that NATO and Warsaw Pact nations were no longer adversaries?
