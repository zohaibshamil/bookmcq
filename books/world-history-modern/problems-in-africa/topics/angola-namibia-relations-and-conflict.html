Q1. In which year was Namibia transferred to South African administration after World War I?

🔹 A. 1919
🔹 B. 1921
🔹 C. 1918
🔹 D. 1920

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is 1919 because the League of Nations assigned the former German South West Africa to South Africa that year. Option B (1921) and D (1920) are later dates that do not match the historical record, and option C (1918) predates the war’s end. Knowing the exact year helps students place the mandate in the post‑war context, showing how colonial legacies began shaping Southern Africa’s later conflicts.

Q2. Why did the South African government resist UN orders to grant Namibia independence?

🔹 A. To protect economic interests in Namibian mineral resources
🔹 B. Because it feared a domino effect of decolonization threatening its own minority rule
🔹 C. To support the MPLA in Angola
🔹 D. To promote regional integration with Angola

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Option B is correct because South Africa’s white minority regime feared that granting independence would inspire similar movements at home and elsewhere, undermining its control. Option A suggests economic motives, which were secondary; Option C misstates South Africa’s stance toward the MPLA; Option D is opposite of South Africa’s isolationist policy. Understanding this fear clarifies why the regime delayed Namibian self‑rule.

Q3. How did the MPLA's decision to allow SWAPO bases in southern Angola affect South Africa's foreign policy?

🔹 A. It led South Africa to increase diplomatic ties with the MPLA
🔹 B. It caused South Africa to view the MPLA as a security threat and intensify support for anti‑MPLA forces
🔹 C. It prompted South Africa to withdraw from Namibia
🔹 D. It resulted in South Africa recognizing SWAPO as the legitimate government

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B because the MPLA’s tolerance of SWAPO fighters turned Angola into a rear‑area sanctuary, prompting South Africa to see the MPLA as hostile and to back proxy forces. Option A is opposite; South Africa did not seek diplomatic ties. Option C is inaccurate—South Africa remained engaged in Namibia. Option D is false; South Africa never recognized SWAPO. This answer links policy shifts to the strategic use of Angolan territory.

Q4. What was the strategic significance of southern Angola for SWAPO's guerrilla campaign against South Africa?

🔹 A. It provided access to maritime ports for international aid
🔹 B. It offered a safe rear area beyond South African military reach
🔹 C. It allowed SWAPO to train in desert conditions similar to Namibia
🔹 D. It enabled coordination with the MPLA's conventional forces

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Option B is correct because southern Angola lay outside the immediate reach of South African forces, giving SWAPO a protected base for planning and logistics. Option A overstates port access; SWAPO primarily used land routes. Option C mentions training terrain but the key factor was safety, not similarity. Option D exaggerates MPLA coordination, which was limited. Recognizing the sanctuary role explains why South Africa targeted Angola.

Q5. If South Africa had complied with UN resolutions and transferred power to a black majority government in Namibia in 1975, which outcome would most likely have occurred?

🔹 A. Reduced support for the MPLA in Angola
🔹 B. Increased guerrilla activity by SWAPO within Namibia
🔹 C. Strengthening of South Africa's regional influence
🔹 D. Accelerated independence movements in neighboring colonies

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The best answer is D because an early Namibian transition would have set a precedent, encouraging other colonies to push for self‑determination more quickly. Option A is unlikely; South Africa’s withdrawal would not directly affect MPLA support. Option B contradicts the scenario—granting power would reduce SWAPO’s need for armed struggle. Option C is opposite to the expected loss of influence. This shows how one policy change can ripple through regional decolonization.

Q6. A policy analyst argues that the presence of SWAPO bases in Angola increased the cost of South Africa's military operations. Which calculation best estimates this cost increase if the base logistics added a 15% overhead to an original budget of \$200 million?

🔹 A. \$230 million
🔹 B. \$215 million
🔹 C. \$300 million
🔹 D. \$260 million

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Option A is correct because a 15 % increase on \$200 million equals \(200 \times 0.15 = 30\) million, raising the total to \$230 million. Option B underestimates the increase, Option C applies a 50 % rise, and Option D adds a 30 % increase. Understanding the arithmetic demonstrates how logistical support can quickly inflate defense spending, reflecting the financial strain of cross‑border insurgencies.

Q7. Consider a hypothetical scenario where the MPLA withdrew its support for SWAPO in 1976. How would this likely have altered the regional balance of power in Southern Africa?

🔹 A. SWAPO would have lost its main supply line, leading to a rapid decline in guerrilla operations
🔹 B. South Africa would have increased its military presence in Angola
🔹 C. The Soviet Union would have directly intervened to support SWAPO
🔹 D. Namibia would have achieved independence earlier through internal negotiations

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is A because without MPLA backing, SWAPO would lose critical logistical and safe‑haven support, weakening its campaign. Option B is unlikely; South Africa’s focus would shift away from Angola. Option C exaggerates Soviet involvement beyond historical patterns. Option D misattributes independence to internal talks, which were stalled by external pressures. This scenario highlights how alliances shape insurgent capabilities.

Q8. Given that the UN Security Council passed Resolution 276 calling for Namibian independence in 1970, and assuming each UN member state votes independently with a 70% chance to support the resolution, what is the probability that at least 12 out of 15 members vote in favor?

🔹 A. 0.1
🔹 B. 0.3
🔹 C. 0.5
🔹 D. 0.7

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Option B (approximately 0.30) is correct. Using the binomial formula, the sum of probabilities for 12, 13, 14, and 15 votes in favor is about 0.296, which rounds to 0.30. Option A underestimates the likelihood, Option C overestimates it, and Option D is far too high. This calculation shows how collective voting probabilities can be quantified, illustrating the challenges of achieving broad international consensus on decolonization.
