What is Somalia united independence 1960?

Somalia achieved united independence in 1960 when British Somaliland and Italian Somaliland merged to form a single nation, initially embracing democratic governance and pan-Somali nationalism before clan rivalries and authoritarianism undermined stability.

Q1. In which year did the British and Italian territories unite to form the Republic of Somalia?

A. 1958 ✅
B. 1960
C. 1962
D. 1965

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Republic of Somalia was created in \(1960\) when the British and Italian Somaliland gained independence and merged. This date is widely recorded in historical accounts. Option A, C, and D list incorrect years, so they are wrong. Knowing the exact year helps students place later events, such as the 1969 coup, in the proper chronological context.

Q2. What was the primary reason the 1963 boundary commission recommended transferring the Somali‑populated area bordering Kenya to the new Republic of Kenya?

A. Ethnic homogeneity
B. Colonial administrative boundaries ✅
C. Economic considerations
D. International pressure

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The commission based its recommendation on the borders drawn by colonial powers, which often ignored ethnic realities. Thus, the decision reflected existing administrative lines rather than local demographics. Options A, C, and D describe motives that were not the main factor, making them incorrect. Understanding this helps explain why the recommendation sparked protests in Somalia.

Q3. Why did President Siad Barre introduce socialist reforms after taking power in 1969?

A. To align with Cold War allies ✅
B. To centralize authority and reduce clan rivalries
C. To attract foreign investment
D. To promote religious unity

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Barre’s socialist agenda was intended to strengthen the central government and diminish the influence of autonomous clans, thereby creating a more unified state. Option A focuses on external alignment, which was secondary; C suggests economic motives that were not primary; D mentions religion, which was not a driving factor. Recognizing the internal political goal clarifies the link between ideology and state‑building.
