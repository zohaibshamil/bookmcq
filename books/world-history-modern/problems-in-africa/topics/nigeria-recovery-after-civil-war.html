What is Nigeria recovery after civil war?

Nigeria’s recovery after the civil war involved rebuilding infrastructure, reintegrating former Biafran territories, and using oil revenues to fund development, though reconciliation remained incomplete and regional disparities persisted beneath surface-level national unity.

Q1. How did the rise in oil prices during the mid‑1970s most directly improve Nigeria’s economic position?

A. It caused inflation to rise sharply
B. It increased foreign exchange reserves ✅
C. It reduced unemployment in the agricultural sector
D. It led to a devaluation of the naira

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B because higher oil prices generated large foreign‑exchange earnings, boosting the country's reserves and allowing it to pay for imports and debt. Option A is wrong; inflation may later rise but it is not the direct effect. Option C misstates the sector impacted—oil, not agriculture, created jobs. Option D is opposite; strong reserves usually support a stronger currency, not devaluation. This explains why oil revenue was key to rapid post‑war recovery.

Q2. Why did Gowon create a new federal system of twelve states after the civil war?

A. To centralize authority in the capital
B. To acknowledge and manage ethnic diversity ✅
C. To attract foreign direct investment
D. To increase military recruitment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Answer B is correct because the new state structure was designed to give each major ethnic group a recognized political unit, reducing tensions and fostering unity. Option A is false; the reform dispersed power rather than concentrating it. Option C is unrelated—the motive was internal cohesion, not external investment. Option D is inaccurate; the change was civilian, not a military recruitment drive. Recognizing diversity helped stabilize the nation after conflict.

Q3. If Nigeria had not taken advantage of the oil price boom in the 1970s, which of the following outcomes would most likely have occurred?

A. A sustained trade surplus ✅
B. A balance‑of‑payments deficit
C. Rapid industrialization
D. Increased foreign aid

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct choice is B because without the surge in oil earnings, export receipts would have fallen short of import costs, creating a deficit in the balance of payments. Option A is the opposite of what would happen; a surplus requires excess earnings. Option C is unlikely because industrial growth depends on capital, which oil revenue supplied. Option D is not the primary result; foreign aid is not a direct substitute for lost oil income. This illustrates the pivotal role of oil in Nigeria’s post‑war economy.

Q4. In the absence of revenge‑taking after the civil conflict, what was the primary effect on national stability?

A. Strengthened ethnic tensions
B. Reduced inter‑tribal hostility ✅
C. Decline in oil production
D. Expansion of the military

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Option B is correct because not seeking revenge prevented further ethnic clashes, allowing the country to focus on rebuilding and governance. Option A is the reverse; the lack of revenge actually eased tensions. Option C is unrelated; oil output depends on market factors, not the presence of revenge. Option D is also incorrect; the military did not expand as a direct consequence of reconciliation. The peaceful approach was essential for Nigeria’s swift recovery and later prosperity.

Q5. Oil prices rose from \( \$10 \) to \( \$30 \) per barrel and Nigeria exported one million barrels annually. What was the increase in export revenue?

A. \(20 \times 10^{6}\) dollars ✅
B. \(30 \times 10^{6}\) dollars
C. \(10 \times 10^{6}\) dollars
D. \(40 \times 10^{6}\) dollars

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The increase equals the price difference (\$30‑\$10 = \$20) multiplied by the volume (1,000,000 barrels), giving \$20,000,000, which is expressed as \(20 \times 10^{6}\) dollars, so A is correct. Option B uses the higher price alone, ignoring the initial price. Option C reflects only the original price, not the gain. Option D adds the two prices, which is mathematically incorrect. This calculation shows how the oil boom dramatically expanded Nigeria’s earnings, fueling rapid post‑war growth.

Q6. In which year was General Gowon removed from power?

A. 1973
B. 1975 ✅
C. 1979
D. 1980

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B because historical records show Gowon was ousted in a coup in 1975. Option A (1973) predates the event, while option C (1979) marks the return to civilian rule, not his removal. Option D (1980) is after the democratic transition. Knowing the timeline helps understand the political shifts that accompanied Nigeria’s economic recovery after the war.

Q7. A post‑conflict country relies heavily on a single commodity and faces a sudden price drop. Which policy mirrors Nigeria’s successful recovery strategy?

A. Diversify the economy beyond the commodity ✅
B. Increase military spending to secure markets
C. Reduce all foreign trade
D. Centralize political power in the capital

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Option A is correct because Nigeria later used oil wealth to invest in other sectors, reducing dependence on a single resource and buffering future shocks. Option B misplaces focus on military action, which did not drive economic resilience. Option C is counterproductive; trade openness helped generate revenue. Option D contradicts the federal decentralization that promoted stability. Diversification is the key lesson for any nation seeking sustainable post‑war recovery.
