What is Mengistu Haile Mariam Derg Ethiopia?

Mengistu Haile Mariam led the Marxist Derg regime that overthrew Haile Selassie, implementing radical land reform and nationalization while conducting a brutal Red Terror campaign against opponents, presiding over civil war, famine, and eventual collapse in 1991.

📝 All Mengistu Haile Mariam Derg Ethiopia MCQs

Q1. In which year did Mengistu flee Ethiopia and seek asylum in Zimbabwe?

A.1991 ✅
B.1989
C.1993
D.1995
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Mengistu left Ethiopia in May 1991 as rebel forces closed in on Addis Ababa. The date marks the definitive end of the Derg’s rule. Option A is correct because historical records confirm the flight occurred in 1991. Options B, C, and D refer to years that either precede or follow the actual event, making them inaccurate. Knowing the correct year helps students place the collapse of the Derg within the broader timeline of African political transitions.

Q2. What was the main reason that the Derg’s agricultural policy caused famines similar to those in Stalin’s USSR?

A.The policy increased private ownership of land, boosting production.
B.Forced collectivization disrupted traditional farming practices and reduced productivity. ✅
C.The government provided abundant foreign aid that offset food shortages.
D.Farmers were given high‑tech equipment that modernized agriculture instantly.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Derg forced peasants onto collective farms, breaking long‑standing farming routines and removing incentives to work efficiently. This disruption lowered crop yields, mirroring the famine‑inducing effects of Stalin’s collectivization. Option B captures this causation. Option A is opposite, as the policy reduced private ownership. Option C is inaccurate because aid was insufficient to prevent famine. Option D is false; the regime lacked the resources to supply modern equipment. Understanding this link clarifies why forced collectivization often leads to food crises.

Q3. If a contemporary government implements a forced collectivization system modeled after the Derg’s approach, which immediate economic outcome is most likely?

A.An increase in agricultural yields
B.A decrease in agricultural yields ✅
C.No noticeable change in production
D.A sudden surplus of food supplies
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Forced collectivization typically reduces individual farmers’ motivation and disrupts efficient local practices, leading to lower output. Historical evidence from the Derg and Stalinist periods shows a sharp drop in harvests, so a decrease in yields is the most probable result. Option B reflects this outcome. Option A contradicts observed data, while Options C and D ignore the well‑documented negative impact on productivity. This scenario teaches students to anticipate economic consequences when policies ignore farmer incentives.

Q4. How did Soviet support affect the Derg’s capacity to remain in power, and why did that support ultimately fail to prevent the regime’s collapse?

A.Soviet aid gave the Derg unlimited resources, ensuring its survival indefinitely.
B.Soviet military training improved the army, but the Derg’s internal rebellions and economic collapse overwhelmed those gains.
C.The USSR’s diplomatic backing secured international legitimacy, which stopped domestic opposition.
D.Soviet assistance strengthened the military, yet it could not solve the Derg’s political isolation, famine, and insurgencies, leading to eventual defeat. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Soviet assistance provided weapons and training that bolstered the Derg’s armed forces, allowing it to fight rebel groups more effectively. However, the regime’s isolation, widespread famine, and persistent insurgencies drained resources and eroded legitimacy, so the military boost alone could not sustain power. Option D correctly captures this dual effect. Option A overstates the permanence of aid, Option B ignores the broader political and economic crises, and Option C misrepresents the limited diplomatic impact. This analysis shows why external military help may be insufficient without internal stability.

Q5. Suppose Eritrea had not declared independence in 1993. Which effect would most likely have occurred on the Derg’s stability?

A.The Derg would have faced fewer rebel fronts, possibly extending its rule.
B.The Derg would have lost its primary source of foreign aid, accelerating its downfall. ✅
C.International sanctions would have increased, forcing the Derg to adopt democratic reforms.
D.Ethnic tensions within Ethiopia would have intensified, leading to immediate civil war.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: If Eritrea remained part of Ethiopia, the Derg would have retained control over a strategically important region, but it would also have continued to allocate resources to suppress the Eritrean insurgency. The loss of Eritrean independence would not have removed the rebel threat; instead, the Derg would still have needed to combat the same forces, likely hastening its collapse due to stretched resources. Option B reflects this likely outcome. Options A, C, and D misinterpret the dynamics of the conflict and the Derg’s reliance on external support.

Q6. What ideological framework guided the Derg’s government policies?

A.Capitalism
B.Islamism
C.Marxism‑Leninism ✅
D.Monarchism
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Derg declared a socialist state based on Marxist‑Leninist principles, nationalizing land, industry, and finance. This ideology shaped its policies of state control and class struggle. Option C is correct because it directly names the guiding doctrine. Options A and B describe opposing economic and religious systems, while Option D refers to the pre‑revolutionary monarchy, none of which reflect the Derg’s official orientation. Recognizing the ideological basis helps students link policy choices to broader political theory.

Q7. The World Bank cancelled 70% of Ethiopia’s external debt, which originally amounted to $10 billion. How much debt remained after the cancellation?

A.\( 7 \times 10 = 70 \) billion
B.\( 10 - 7 = 3 \) billion
C.\( 0.3 \times 10 = 3 \) billion ✅
D.\( 10 \times 0.7 = 7 \) billion
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Seventy percent of $10 billion equals $7 billion, leaving 30 % unpaid. Multiplying the original amount by 0.3 gives the remaining debt: \(0.3 \times 10 = 3\) billion. Option C correctly performs this calculation. Option A multiplies instead of applying the percentage, Option B subtracts the percentage value rather than the amount, and Option D computes the cancelled portion rather than the balance. Mastery of simple percentage math is essential for evaluating debt relief outcomes.

Q8. Why did the Derg’s regime lose control over Eritrea and Tigray by 1989?

A.The Soviet Union withdrew military aid, leaving the Derg defenseless.
B.The Derg voluntarily granted autonomy to those regions to promote peace.
C.International sanctions forced the Derg to abandon those territories.
D.Sustained insurgencies exhausted the regime’s resources, and the rebels gained popular support, leading to loss of control. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Derg faced relentless guerrilla warfare from the Eritrean People’s Liberation Front and the Tigray People’s Liberation Front. These movements drew on local support and wore down the regime’s limited military and economic capacity, culminating in the loss of both regions by 1989. Option D accurately describes this process. Option A misstates the timing of Soviet withdrawal, which occurred later. Option B is false because the Derg did not grant autonomy. Option C overstates the impact of sanctions, which were not the primary cause of territorial loss. Understanding the role of insurgent strength explains how internal conflicts can undermine state authority.
