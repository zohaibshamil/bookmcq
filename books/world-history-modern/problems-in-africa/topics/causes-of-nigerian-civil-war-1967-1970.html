📝 Causes of Nigerian Civil War 1967-1970 (11 MCQs)

Nigeria's Fragile Federation After Independence in 1960

The Nigerian Civil War of 1967–1970 developed from political instability, regional rivalry, military coups and ethnic violence following independence from Britain on 1 October 1960. Nigeria's federation contained major regional differences and diverse communities, particularly among the Hausa-Fulani, Yoruba and Igbo populations. Disagreements over political representation, federal authority and the distribution of resources weakened national unity during the First Republic.

The 1966 Coups, Massacres and Breakdown of Trust

The January 1966 coup removed several political leaders and included the killing of senior northern figures. A counter-coup in July brought Lieutenant Colonel Yakubu Gowon to power. Anti-Igbo violence in northern Nigeria during 1966 caused widespread deaths and mass displacement. These events created fear among Igbo communities and deepened disagreements between the federal government and Eastern Region leadership.

Failed Negotiations and the Eastern Region's Secession

In January 1967, Nigerian leaders met at Aburi in Ghana to seek a constitutional settlement, but disagreements over implementing the agreement continued. Gowon announced a reorganisation of Nigeria into twelve states in May 1967, reducing the Eastern Region's territorial and political position. On 30 May 1967, its military governor, Chukwuemeka Odumegwu Ojukwu, declared the independent Republic of Biafra.

The Federal Blockade and the Importance of Oil-Rich Territory

The Eastern Region contained important oil-producing areas, making the struggle over its separation economically significant. Federal forces launched military operations against Biafra in July 1967. The federal government viewed secession as a threat to national unity, while Biafran leaders considered independence necessary for security. The conflict also attracted international concern because blockade and fighting produced a devastating humanitarian emergency.

Political, Humanitarian and Territorial Consequences

The war caused immense civilian suffering, widespread displacement and severe famine in Biafra. It ended with the surrender of Biafran forces in January 1970 and preserved Nigeria's territorial unity. The conflict strengthened federal control, influenced later debates over state creation and oil revenue, and left lasting memories of violence, exclusion and the difficulties of managing Nigeria's diversity.

Misconceptions About the Causes of the Nigerian Civil War

'The war began only because of religious differences' - political breakdown, military coups and communal violence were central. 'Biafra declared independence after the fighting began' - secession occurred on 30 May 1967, before the federal offensive. 'Oil was the sole cause' - security, federal authority and regional grievances were also crucial.

Examining the Road to the Nigerian Civil War

Build a clear chronology from independence in 1960 through the January and July 1966 coups, the Aburi meeting, the twelve-state reorganisation and Biafran secession. Identify Gowon and Ojukwu, and distinguish long-term tensions from immediate triggers. Questions may compare federal unity with regional self-determination and assess the influence of oil, ethnicity and security.

Key Political and Military Terms of the Nigerian Civil War

  • Biafra - Breakaway state declared in 1967.
  • Aburi Agreement - 1967 attempt at constitutional settlement.
  • Secession - Withdrawal from an existing state.
  • Federalism - Division of authority between governments.
  • Yakubu Gowon - Nigerian federal head of state during the war.
  • Ojukwu - Leader of the Biafran secession.

📝 All Causes of Nigerian Civil War 1967-1970 MCQs

Q1. In which month and year did the first military coup that led to the civil war occur?

A.Jan-65
B.Jan-66 ✅
C.May-67
D.Jul-68
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The coup that sparked the conflict happened in January 1966. This event removed the civilian leadership and created a power vacuum that heightened mistrust among ethnic groups. Choosing the wrong date would ignore the historical timeline that directly links the coup to the ensuing violence. Understanding the exact timing helps students see how a single political shock can cascade into widespread war, a pattern seen in other nations as well.

Q2. Which factor most directly increased ethnic tension before the civil war?

A.Tribal differences among north, east, and west ✅
B.Economic recession
C.Foreign intervention
D.Colonial borders
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Tribal differences among the Hausa‑Fulani, Yoruba, and Ibo groups created the most immediate friction, as each region feared domination by the others. Economic problems and external influences mattered, but they amplified rather than originated the ethnic rivalry. Selecting any other option would miss the core driver of mistrust. Recognizing tribal competition clarifies why identity politics can become a flashpoint for conflict in multi‑ethnic societies.

Q3. If a country experiences a 15% price rise and wages stay below the minimum living wage, which policy is most likely to reduce the risk of civil conflict?

A.Increase wages to meet the minimum living wage ✅
B.Centralize political power
C.Suppress opposition groups
D.Ignore the economic issues
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Raising wages to the minimum living wage directly eases the economic strain that fuels public anger, decreasing the likelihood of violent protest. Centralizing power or suppressing dissent may worsen grievances, while ignoring the problem leaves the root cause untouched. By linking economic relief to stability, students see how policy choices can either calm or inflame societal tensions, a lesson applicable to many modern economies.

Q4. How did the arrest of Chief Awolowo contribute to the breakdown of the federation?

A.It signaled bias against the western region ✅
B.It strengthened national unity
C.It improved economic conditions
D.It reduced ethnic tensions
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Arresting a prominent western leader was perceived as targeted oppression, deepening suspicion that the central government favored the north. This action eroded trust between regions, making compromise harder. Options suggesting unity or improvement ignore the political fallout, while reducing tensions is the opposite of what occurred. Understanding this helps learners appreciate how punitive measures against regional elites can destabilize a federal system.

Q5. A regional leader threatens secession after the central government arrests opposition. Which diplomatic step is most likely to prevent war?

A.Negotiate power‑sharing arrangements ✅
B.Impose economic sanctions
C.Deploy troops to the region
D.Ignore the demand
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Negotiating power‑sharing directly addresses the leader’s grievance by offering political inclusion, reducing the incentive to secede. Sanctions and military force typically raise hostility, and ignoring the threat allows resentment to grow unchecked. By choosing negotiation, students learn that flexible political solutions can defuse crises, a strategy repeatedly used in conflict‑prevention efforts worldwide.

Q6. Why did the manipulation of the 1964 election results exacerbate the crisis?

A.It undermined the legitimacy of the central government ✅
B.It increased tax revenue
C.It improved infrastructure
D.It reduced ethnic rivalry
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Faking election outcomes eroded confidence in national institutions, making many groups feel their voices were ignored, which intensified calls for autonomy. Increased revenue or better infrastructure would not explain rising tensions, and reducing rivalry contradicts the observed escalation. Recognizing the importance of perceived legitimacy helps students grasp why electoral fraud can be a catalyst for rebellion in fragile states.

Q7. If an army composed mainly of one ethnic group overthrows a multi‑ethnic government, what is the most probable outcome for minority officers?

A.They are integrated into the new leadership
B.They receive promotions
C.They face marginalization or exile ✅
D.They lead a parallel government
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Minority officers are likely to be sidelined or forced out because the new regime tends to favor its own ethnic base, creating distrust and potential exile. Integration or promotion would contradict the pattern of ethnic bias, while a parallel government implies organized resistance not typical immediately after a takeover. This scenario illustrates how ethnic dominance in the military can deepen divisions and spark further conflict.

Q8. Why did northern soldiers murder General Ironsi after he became head of state?

A.Ethnic retaliation against an Ibo leader ✅
B.Power struggle over economic policy
C.Foreign influence from neighboring states
D.Personal vendetta unrelated to politics
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: General Ironsi’s Ibo background made him a target for northern soldiers who saw his ascent as a threat to their regional influence, leading to retaliatory violence. Economic or foreign motives do not align with the clear ethnic motive, and a personal vendetta would ignore the broader communal context. This answer highlights how ethnic loyalties can drive extreme actions during periods of political instability.

Q9. A federation accused of election corruption wants to restore trust. Which reform directly addresses the root cause of the civil war?

A.Create an independent electoral commission ✅
B.Increase military spending
C.Centralize tax collection
D.Restrict media coverage
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An independent electoral commission tackles the legitimacy problem by ensuring fair voting, directly removing the corruption that sparked distrust. Boosting the military or centralizing taxes does not fix the perception of bias, and media restrictions could worsen suspicion. By focusing on transparent elections, students see how institutional reforms can heal divisions that otherwise fuel separatist movements.

Q10. How did the economic recession interact with ethnic grievances to trigger the civil war?

A.The recession amplified existing ethnic grievances, making conflict more likely ✅
B.The recession solved the grievances by reducing competition
C.The recession was irrelevant to ethnic tensions
D.The recession only affected the northern region
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A falling economy heightened competition for scarce jobs, intensifying resentment among groups already suspicious of each other. This synergy turned latent tension into open conflict. Saying the recession solved grievances or was irrelevant ignores the way material hardship fuels identity‑based anger, while limiting its impact to one region misrepresents the nationwide strain. The explanation shows how economic stress can be the spark that lights an already volatile ethnic powder keg.

Q11. If a leader describes a campaign to retake a seceded region as a 'short surgical police action', which approach does this phrase suggest?

A.A rapid, limited use of force aiming to quickly restore control ✅
B.A prolonged, total war strategy
C.Economic sanctions and diplomatic isolation
D.Negotiated settlement and power‑sharing
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Calling the operation 'short surgical' implies a brief, precise use of force intended to remove the secessionist challenge without a drawn‑out war. A prolonged strategy, sanctions, or negotiations would contradict the emphasis on speed and limited scope. Recognizing this linguistic cue helps students infer policy intentions, illustrating how terminology can signal the expected scale and duration of military actions.
