What is Kofi Busia government Ghana 1969-1972?

The Kofi Busia government in Ghana from 1969 to 1972 attempted to restore civilian democracy and implement market-oriented economic reforms after years of military rule, but faced severe challenges including currency devaluation, public unrest over austerity measures, and ultimately was overthrown by another military coup.

Q1. When was Kofi Busia overthrown by the army?

A.Dec-71 ✅
B.Jan-72
C.Mar-73
D.Jun-74
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is January 1972 because historical records show that the coup occurred while Busia was in London. Option A is wrong because the overthrow did not happen in 1971, and Option C and D are incorrect dates that do not match the event. Knowing the exact timing helps understand the rapid political turnover in Ghana and why the military acted swiftly to replace the civilian government.

Q2. Which factor most directly undermined Kofi Busia's ability to keep political stability?

A.Strong tribal unity
B.High foreign aid
C.Low unemployment
D.Dependence on army support combined with severe economic crises ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Option D is correct because Busia’s legitimacy relied on the army’s consent while he faced soaring unemployment, rising prices, low cocoa revenues, and large debt burdens, creating an unstable environment. Option A is opposite; tribal unity would aid stability. Option B is inaccurate as foreign aid was limited and did not offset fiscal problems. Option C misstates the situation because unemployment was actually rising, not low. The combination of military dependence and economic strain explains why his government collapsed.

Q3. How did the near‑50 % devaluation of Ghana's currency in 1971 affect the government's fiscal capacity?

A.Reduced the real value of export earnings
B.Increased foreign investment ✅
C.Lowered debt repayments
D.Stabilized domestic prices
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is that devaluation reduced the real value of export earnings because the currency lost almost half its value, making foreign‑denominated revenues worth less in local terms. Option B is wrong; devaluation typically discourages investment due to uncertainty. Option C misinterprets the effect; debt obligations remain the same, so repayments become harder, not easier. Option D suggests price stability, but devaluation often fuels inflation. Understanding this fiscal impact shows why Busia’s budget was squeezed.

Q6. A government wants to cut debt payments to 25 % of export profits while keeping import restrictions at 10 %. Which policy would most likely achieve this goal?

A.Increase cocoa price through subsidies ✅
B.Reduce military spending
C.Raise taxes on agriculture
D.Renegotiate debt terms with creditors
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Renegotiating debt terms is the only option that directly lowers the proportion of export profits needed for repayment, allowing the share to fall to 25 %. Raising cocoa prices (A) may raise revenue but does not change the debt proportion. Cutting military spending (B) frees budget space but does not affect debt ratios. Raising taxes on agriculture (C) could reduce growth and export capacity. Therefore, D is the most effective strategy to adjust the debt burden.

Q7. If tribal conflicts cause a 15 % drop in tax revenue, how would this impact the ability to meet a debt obligation that requires 40 % of export profits?

A.No impact because debt is fixed
B.Likely make it harder to meet payments ✅
C.Improves ability due to lower spending
D.Forces increase in export volume
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A 15 % reduction in tax revenue shrinks the overall fiscal resources, meaning the government has less flexibility to allocate funds toward debt service, which still demands 40 % of export earnings. Therefore, meeting the obligation becomes more difficult, making option B correct. Option A is wrong because debt payments depend on available funds, not a fixed amount. Option C incorrectly assumes lower spending helps debt payment, and option D misinterprets the need for higher exports without addressing revenue shortfalls.

Q8. If export composition shifts from 80 % cocoa to 50 % cocoa, what effect does this have on vulnerability to world cocoa price fluctuations?

A.Increases vulnerability ✅
B.No change in vulnerability
C.Decreases vulnerability
D.Turns Ghana into a net importer of cocoa
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Reducing cocoa’s share from 80 % to 50 % spreads export earnings across more products, so a fall in cocoa prices will affect a smaller portion of total revenue, thereby decreasing vulnerability. Option A is opposite, suggesting higher risk. Option B ignores the diversification benefit. Option D misstates the outcome; Ghana would still export cocoa, not become a net importer. Thus, C correctly captures how diversification mitigates price‑shock exposure.
