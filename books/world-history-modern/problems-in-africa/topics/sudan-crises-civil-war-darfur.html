What is Sudan crises civil war Darfur?

Sudan’s crises including the Darfur conflict arose from longstanding marginalization of peripheral regions, ethnic and resource-based tensions, and government-backed militia violence against non-Arab communities, resulting in genocide allegations, mass displacement, and international condemnation.

Q1. Who led the 1989 coup that overthrew the Sudanese government?

A. Ali Mahdi
B. Omar al‑Bashir ✅
C. Hassan al‑Turabi
D. Abdel Fattah al‑Burhan

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Omar al‑Bashir organized a group of army officers and seized power in 1989, becoming Sudan’s president. The other names were prominent Sudanese figures but never headed the coup. Recognizing Bashir’s role helps link the political shift to later events, such as the continuation of authoritarian rule and the eventual peace negotiations that followed.

Q2. In which year did the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement and the Khartoum government sign a peace deal in Nairobi?

A. 2003
B. 2004
C. 2005 ✅
D. 2006

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The agreement was signed in January 2005, ending years of fighting and setting a timeline for Southern Sudan’s autonomy. The other years either precede or follow the actual signing. Knowing the date is crucial because it marks the start of a six‑year autonomy period that later led to a referendum on independence.

Q3. What was the primary effect of the 1983 introduction of fundamentalist Islamic law on relations between northern Arabs and southern African tribes?

A. It unified the nation under a common legal system
B. It reduced ethnic tensions
C. It deepened the north‑south divide ✅
D. It improved economic development

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Implementing strict Sharia law heightened cultural and religious differences, driving a wedge between the Arab‑dominated north and the predominantly Christian and animist south. This deepening divide fueled grievances that later erupted into armed conflict. The other options describe outcomes that did not occur; the law intensified, rather than eased, hostilities.

Q4. Why did the Sudanese government employ the Janjaweed militia in the Darfur conflict?

A. To protect civilian populations from rebel attacks
B. To train them for future national service
C. To carry out ethnic cleansing while maintaining deniability ✅
D. To provide humanitarian assistance to displaced families

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Janjaweed acted as a proxy force, allowing the government to conduct violent campaigns against African communities without being directly blamed. This strategy created plausible deniability, obscuring state responsibility. The other choices misrepresent the militia’s purpose; they were not a protective or aid‑providing force, but a tool for repression.

Q5. How did the lack of infrastructure in Darfur worsen the humanitarian crisis?

A. It made aid delivery easier by concentrating resources
B. It hindered aid delivery, slowing relief efforts ✅
C. It increased trade opportunities with neighboring countries
D. It improved disease control through isolation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Destroyed roads, schools, and hospitals meant that food drops, medical supplies, and clean water could not reach affected populations promptly. The absence of electricity and functional clinics amplified disease spread. Options suggesting improvement are inaccurate because the infrastructure collapse directly impeded relief operations, exacerbating suffering.

Q6. What was the intended role of the African Union peacekeeping mission launched in August 2004?

A. To exploit Sudan’s oil reserves for AU profit
B. To stabilize the region and protect civilians from violence ✅
C. To support Sudanese government forces against rebels
D. To relocate displaced populations to neighboring countries

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The AU mission aimed to provide a neutral security presence that could deter attacks, monitor cease‑fires, and safeguard vulnerable civilians. It was not designed to aid the government militarily or to manage resource extraction. Understanding this role clarifies why international actors emphasized civilian protection as a cornerstone of the mission.

Q7. How do Sudan’s oil resources relate to the country’s internal conflicts?

A. Oil has no impact on political tensions
B. Oil reduces conflict by providing shared wealth
C. Competition over oil control fuels conflict between groups ✅
D. Oil revenues automatically fund peacebuilding initiatives

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Oil wealth became a strategic prize, intensifying rivalry between the central government and rebel factions seeking a share of revenue. Control over oil fields often dictated alliances and fueled violence. The other statements ignore the reality that resource competition can deepen divisions, especially when revenues are unevenly distributed.

Q8. If an NGO can deliver 500 tons of food per air flight while trucks can only move 200 tons, what is the percentage increase in delivery efficiency using air drops?

A. 1.5 ✅
B. 2.5
C. 1
D. 0.75

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Efficiency gain equals \((500-200)/200 = 300/200 = 1.5\), which is a 150 % increase. The other percentages miscalculate the ratio. This shows how air logistics can dramatically improve aid distribution when ground routes are unusable, a common challenge in conflict‑affected Darfur.

Q9. Given an estimated 300 000 deaths and 3.5 million displaced persons in Darfur, what is the approximate ratio of deaths to displaced persons?

A. 1 : 5
B. 1 : 10
C. 1 : 12 ✅
D. 1 : 20

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ratio is \(300{,}000 ÷ 3{,}500{,}000 ≈ 0.0857\), or roughly 1 death for every 12 displaced people. The other ratios are either too high or too low. Understanding this proportion helps gauge the scale of human loss relative to displacement, highlighting the severity of the crisis.

Q10. If Sudan’s fertile Nile‑bank soil could feed 30 million people but current production only feeds 15 million, what percentage increase in agricultural output is required to meet the potential?

A. 0.5
B. 1 ✅
C. 1.5
D. 2

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Doubling the current output from 15 million to 30 million requires a 100 % increase (\((30-15)/15 = 1\)). The other percentages represent smaller or larger changes than needed. Recognizing the needed increase underscores the untapped capacity for food security if infrastructure and farming practices improve.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

Q12. If the Janjaweed militia consists of 5 000 fighters and each is equipped with 2 rifles, how many rifles are in total?

A. 5 000
B. 7 500
C. 10 000 ✅
D. 12 000

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Total rifles equal \(5{,}000 × 2 = 10{,}000\). The other numbers misrepresent the multiplication. This calculation illustrates the scale of armament available to irregular forces, which influences their capacity to inflict violence.

Q13. A drought reduces crop yield by 40 % and a subsequent flood reduces the remaining yield by 30 %. What percentage of the original yield remains after both events?

A. 0.42 ✅
B. 0.3
C. 0.7
D. 0.55

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

After the drought, 60 % of the original remains. The flood then removes 30 % of that, leaving \(60 % × 70 % = 42 %\). The other choices miscalculate one of the steps. This demonstrates how successive shocks can dramatically cut agricultural production, worsening food insecurity.

Q14. If UN aid drops food to 2 million people and each person needs 2 kg per day, how many kilograms of food are required each day?

A. 2 million kg
B. 4 million kg ✅
C. 6 million kg
D. 8 million kg

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Daily requirement is \(2{,}000{,}000 × 2 kg = 4{,}000{,}000 kg\). The other figures either halve or double the correct amount. This quantifies the massive logistical effort needed to sustain displaced populations, emphasizing why air drops are vital.

Q15. If Sudan extracts 100 000 barrels of oil per day, each worth $70, what is the daily revenue from oil production?

A. $7 million ✅
B. $70 million
C. $700 000
D. $7 000

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Revenue equals \(100{,}000 × 70 = 7{,}000{,}000\) dollars, i.e., $7 million per day. The other options misplace a zero or two. Understanding oil earnings helps explain why control over oil fields is a central factor in Sudan’s conflicts.

Q16. A peacekeeping force of 10 000 troops can protect 1 million civilians. Assuming linear scaling, how many civilians could 25 000 troops protect?

A. 2 million
B. 2.5 million ✅
C. 3 million
D. 3.5 million

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

One troop protects 100 civilians; thus 25 000 troops protect \(25{,}000 × 100 = 2{,}500{,}000\) civilians, or 2.5 million. The other numbers ignore the proportional increase. This illustrates how troop numbers translate into protective capacity in fragile settings.

Q17. Rebuilding a destroyed school costs $200 000. If the government allocates $5 000 per month, how many months will it take to complete reconstruction?

A. 20
B. 30
C. 40 ✅
D. 50

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Months required are \(200{,}000 ÷ 5{,}000 = 40\). The other options miscalculate the division. This simple budgeting exercise shows how limited funding can delay essential services, prolonging the impact of conflict on education.

Q18. Disease incidence rises from 5 % to 12 % among 2 million displaced people. How many additional disease cases have occurred?

A. 100 000
B. 140 000 ✅
C. 200 000
D. 240 000

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Initial cases: \(2{,}000{,}000 × 0.05 = 100{,}000\). New cases: \(2{,}000{,}000 × 0.12 = 240{,}000\). Additional cases: \(240{,}000 - 100{,}000 = 140{,}000\). The other numbers represent either the original or total cases. This calculation highlights how health crises can sharply increase in displaced populations.
