📝 Sierra Leone civil war and catastrophe (18 MCQs)

The Collapse of Sierra Leone's Political Stability

Sierra Leone's civil war lasted from 1991 to 2002 and caused widespread destruction, displacement and human rights abuses. The conflict began when the Revolutionary United Front, led by Foday Sankoh, entered eastern Sierra Leone from Liberia. Longstanding corruption, poor governance, youth unemployment and unequal distribution of diamond wealth had weakened the state and created conditions in which armed rebellion could spread.

Diamond Wealth, Political Corruption and the RUF Rebellion

Sierra Leone's diamond industry generated valuable export income but did not deliver broad prosperity. Smuggling and elite control reduced public revenue, while the government under Joseph Saidu Momoh struggled to maintain authority. The RUF used grievances about corruption and exclusion to attract recruits, although it quickly became notorious for violence against civilians and coercive recruitment.

Military Coups, International Intervention and Civilian Suffering

A military coup in 1992 brought the National Provisional Ruling Council to power. The conflict continued through changing governments and armed factions, while the RUF and allied forces carried out killings, amputations, abductions and forced displacement. Nigerian-led regional forces intervened through ECOMOG, and British troops entered in 2000 to support security and the peace process.

The Lomé Peace Agreement and the End of Fighting

The 1999 Lomé Peace Agreement attempted to end the war by offering power-sharing arrangements and amnesty provisions. However, fighting resumed, and RUF forces seized UN peacekeepers in 2000. The United Nations Mission in Sierra Leone strengthened its deployment and supported disarmament. The war was officially declared over in January 2002 after extensive peacebuilding and security operations.

Humanitarian Catastrophe, Justice and Postwar Reconstruction

The war killed and displaced large numbers of people, destroyed infrastructure and damaged social institutions. The Special Court for Sierra Leone prosecuted individuals responsible for serious crimes, including former Liberian president Charles Taylor. Reconstruction improved security and government capacity, but poverty, unemployment and the reintegration of former child soldiers remained major challenges after peace returned.

Misconceptions About the Sierra Leone Civil War

'The war was only a dispute over diamonds' - governance failures, exclusion and regional conflict were also central. 'The 1999 Lomé Agreement ended fighting permanently' - violence resumed after its signing. 'Only foreign armies ended the war' - national disarmament, peacebuilding and local recovery were also essential.

Examining the RUF, Diamond Conflict and Peace Process

Remember the RUF invasion in 1991, the 1992 military coup, the 1999 Lomé Agreement and the formal end of war in January 2002. Identify Foday Sankoh, ECOMOG, UNAMSIL and the Special Court. Questions may assess conflict diamonds, civilian abuses, child soldiers, international intervention and the limits of peace agreements.

Military and Legal Terms of the Sierra Leone War

  • RUF - Revolutionary United Front.
  • Foday Sankoh - Leader of the RUF rebellion.
  • Conflict diamonds - Gems used to finance armed conflict.
  • ECOMOG - Regional military intervention force.
  • UNAMSIL - UN peacekeeping mission in Sierra Leone.
  • Lomé Agreement - 1999 peace settlement.

📝 All Sierra Leone civil war and catastrophe MCQs

Q1. What year did the civil war in Sierra Leone begin?

A.1990
B.1991 ✅
C.1992
D.1993
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is 1991 because the conflict that later became Sierra Leone's civil war started that year with the emergence of the RUF. 1990 is too early; the rebel group was not yet active. 1992 marks the overthrow of Momoh, not the war's start. 1993 is after the war began. Knowing the start date helps place subsequent events such as the 1995 elections in the broader timeline of chaos and catastrophe.

Q2. Who was the leader of the Revolutionary United Front (RUF)?

A.Foday Sankoh ✅
B.Joseph Momoh
C.Johnny Paul Koroma
D.Ahmad Tejan Kabbah
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is Foday Sankoh because he founded and led the Revolutionary United Front, directing its violent campaign. Joseph Momoh was a former president, not a rebel leader. Johnny Paul Koroma became head of state after a 1997 coup, unrelated to rebel leadership. Ahmad Tejan Kabbah later became president through elections, not the RUF commander. Recognizing the rebel leader clarifies why the group pursued coercive tactics, contributing to the chaos.

Q3. If a neighboring country receives 30,000 refugees from a conflict that produced 900,000 internally displaced persons, what percentage of the total displaced does the neighboring country host?

A.0.033 ✅
B.0.1
C.0.3
D.0.33
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is 3.3% because the neighboring country hosts 30,000 refugees out of a total of 930,000 displaced persons (900,000 internal plus 30,000 external). Dividing 30,000 by 930,000 gives \(0.0323\), or about 3.3%. The 10% option vastly overstates the share, while 30% and 33% are unrealistic. This calculation shows how a relatively small proportion of refugees can still strain a neighboring state's resources during a catastrophe.

Q4. Which measure is most likely to raise voter turnout from 60% to 80% in a post‑conflict election?

A.Offering cash incentives
B.Improving security at polling stations ✅
C.Reducing the number of candidates
D.Limiting voting to urban areas
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is improving security at polling stations because fear of violence directly suppresses turnout, and better protection encourages participation. Offering cash incentives may raise turnout but can be seen as vote‑buying and undermine legitimacy. Reducing the number of candidates does not address safety concerns, and limiting voting to urban areas excludes rural voters, decreasing overall turnout. Enhancing security aligns with the need to rebuild confidence after a period of chaos and intimidation.

Q5. A military coup reduces the political stability index by 0.4 points on a 5‑point scale. If the original score was 3.2, what is the new stability score?

A.2.8 ✅
B.2.6
C.3.6
D.4
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is 2.8 because the original stability score of 3.2 reduced by 0.4 points yields 3.2 − 0.4 = 2.8. Option 2.6 assumes a larger drop, while 3.6 and 4.0 represent increases, which contradict the scenario of decreasing stability after a coup. This simple subtraction illustrates how political shocks can be quantified, helping analysts gauge the severity of institutional erosion during chaotic transitions.

Q6. Why can peace agreements fail when a faction splits and fighting resumes, causing a 15% rise in civilian casualties?

A.Lack of inclusion of all armed groups ✅
B.Insufficient international monitoring
C.Economic incentives for war
D.All of the above
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is all of the above because each factor—excluding some armed groups, lacking robust international monitoring, and providing economic incentives for continued fighting—contributes to the fragility of peace accords. A narrow inclusion leaves out key spoilers, weak monitoring allows violations to go unchecked, and war economies can motivate parties to resume hostilities. The other options isolate a single cause, which underestimates the complex interplay that fuels ongoing catastrophe.

Q7. If a country's economy shrinks by 5% each year for three consecutive years due to war, what is the total percentage decline after three years?

A.0.15
B.0.14300000000000002 ✅
C.0.1
D.0.05
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is 14.3% because a 5% annual contraction compounds: \( (1-0.05)^3 = 0.8574\), meaning the economy retains about 85.7% of its original size, a loss of 14.3%. The 15% choice is a simple addition, not compound. 10% and 5% underestimate the cumulative effect. Understanding compound decline helps policymakers anticipate long‑term economic damage in war‑torn societies.

Q8. Which political theory best describes the risk when the army refuses to cede power and fires at civilians at polling stations?

A.Democratic consolidation
B.Civil‑military relations
C.Authoritarian resilience ✅
D.State fragility
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is civil‑military relations because the question asks which theory best describes the risk when the army refuses to cede power and attacks civilians at polling stations. Democratic consolidation focuses on the strengthening of democratic institutions, which is not the immediate issue. Authoritarian resilience describes regimes that survive authoritarian pressure, while state fragility is broader. Civil‑military relations specifically examines the balance of power between armed forces and civilian authority, making it the most accurate lens for this scenario.

Q9. A sanction reduces export revenue by 20%. If pre‑sanction revenue was $500 million, what is the new export revenue?

A.$400 million ✅
B.$450 million
C.$300 million
D.$250 million
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is $400 million because a 20% reduction of $500 million leaves \(500 \times 0.80 = 400\) million. $450 million reflects only a 10% cut, $300 million a 40% cut, and $250 million a 50% cut, all inconsistent with the 20% figure. This straightforward calculation shows how sanctions can quickly shrink a country’s earnings, exacerbating the economic chaos that follows political turmoil.

Q10. What is a likely long‑term consequence of banning political parties to maintain order?

A.Increased foreign aid
B.Democratic backsliding
C.Economic growth
D.Regional integration ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is democratic backsliding because removing political parties undermines pluralism and erodes democratic norms, leading to a regression in democratic development. Increased foreign aid is unlikely when governance deteriorates; economic growth typically stalls under authoritarian measures; regional integration usually requires stable institutions. Thus, the most plausible long‑term effect is a retreat from democratic standards, deepening the country's political catastrophe.

Q11. If former rebel leaders receive 30% of cabinet posts in a 20‑member cabinet, how many positions do they obtain?

A.6 ✅
B.5
C.8
D.4
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is 6 because 30% of 20 positions equals \(0.30 \times 20 = 6\). Five seats would represent 25%, eight seats 40%, and four seats 20%, none matching the stipulated proportion. Accurately translating percentages into seat counts is essential for designing power‑sharing agreements that aim to reduce conflict by giving former rebels a meaningful role in governance.

Q12. What does a 60% turnout achieved under intimidation suggest about political legitimacy?

A.Legitimacy is undermined
B.Legitimacy remains strong
C.Legitimacy is ambiguous ✅
D.Legitimacy is irrelevant
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is that legitimacy is ambiguous because a 60% turnout achieved under intimidation shows that citizens participated but the environment compromised the freedom of choice, making the democratic mandate uncertain. Saying legitimacy is undermined would be too strong, while claiming it remains strong ignores the coercive context. Declaring it irrelevant dismisses the importance of free elections. Therefore, the most nuanced assessment is that legitimacy is ambiguous, reflecting the mixed signals of participation during a chaotic period.

Q13. If a civil war lasts ten years and displaces 900,000 people, what is the average annual displacement assuming a constant rate?

A.90000 ✅
B.100000
C.75000
D.120000
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is 90,000 because dividing the total displaced population of 900,000 by the ten‑year duration yields \(900{,}000 \div 10 = 90{,}000\) people per year on average. The other options either overestimate or underestimate the yearly figure. This average helps humanitarian planners allocate resources consistently across the conflict timeline.

Q14. Why did the overthrow of President Strasser in 1995 undermine the democratic transition?

A.Because it halted elections
B.Because it showed military power ✅
C.Because it increased voter turnout
D.Because it led to economic growth
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is because it showed military power, as the coup demonstrated that the armed forces could still dominate political outcomes, discouraging civilian confidence and destabilizing the fragile democratic process. Halting elections is inaccurate because voting proceeded; increased voter turnout is unrelated; economic growth was not observed. Recognizing the influence of military intervention clarifies why the transition faltered and why subsequent chaos persisted.

Q15. How did the involvement of the army in politics contribute to the cycle of chaos in Sierra Leone?

A.By providing stability
B.By creating power vacuums
C.By fostering corruption ✅
D.By ensuring civilian control
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is fostering corruption because when the army intervenes in politics it often uses state resources for personal gain, erodes accountability, and creates patronage networks that fuel instability. Providing stability would be the opposite effect; creating power vacuums implies withdrawal, not intervention; ensuring civilian control would actually reduce chaos. Thus, the army’s political role directly contributed to the corruption that amplified the country’s catastrophe.

Q16. What is the main reason rebel groups like the RUF use coercion and drugs to recruit followers?

A.To increase morale
B.To ensure loyalty ✅
C.To improve health
D.To attract foreign aid
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is to ensure loyalty because coercion and drug use bind recruits to the group through dependency and fear, making them less likely to defect. Increasing morale is a secondary benefit, but the primary aim is control. Improving health contradicts drug use, and attracting foreign aid is unrelated to internal recruitment strategies. Understanding these methods explains why the RUF could sustain a violent campaign despite external pressures.

Q17. How does suspension from the Commonwealth affect a country's international standing?

A.It enhances diplomatic ties
B.It leads to isolation ✅
C.It improves trade
D.It reduces domestic unrest
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is it leads to isolation because suspension removes diplomatic privileges, reduces access to Commonwealth assistance, and signals international disapproval, thereby isolating the nation. Enhancing diplomatic ties would be the opposite effect; improving trade is unlikely under suspension; reducing domestic unrest is not a direct consequence. Isolation can exacerbate the economic and political chaos already present.

Q18. Which factor most directly links the 1997 coup to the breakdown of the 1996 peace agreement?

A.Abolition of the constitution ✅
B.Ban on political parties
C.Change in leadership
D.All of the above
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is all of the above because the 1997 coup abolished the constitution, banned political parties, and installed new leadership, each of which directly invalidated the 1996 peace agreement’s legal and political foundations, causing its collapse. Selecting only one of these actions would ignore the combined impact that collectively destabilized the agreement and reignited conflict.
