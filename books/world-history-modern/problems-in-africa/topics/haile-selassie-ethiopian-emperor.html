📝 All Haile Selassie Ethiopian Emperor MCQs

Q1. In what year did Haile Selassie become Emperor of Ethiopia?

🔹 A. 1930
🔹 B. 1935
🔹 C. 1941
🔹 D. 1952

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Haile Selassie was crowned in 1930, ending a period of regency and establishing his long reign. The other years relate to later events: 1935 marks the Italian invasion, 1941 the liberation, and 1952 the Eritrea transfer. Knowing the coronation year helps place his rule in the wider timeline of African decolonization, and it shows why his policies spanned several decades.

Q2. Why did Haile Selassie go into exile in 1935?

🔹 A. British invitation
🔹 B. Italian invasion
🔹 C. Internal rebellion
🔹 D. Famine

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Emperor fled because Mussolini’s forces invaded Ethiopia in 1935, forcing the royal court to seek safety abroad. A British invitation was not the cause, nor were internal uprisings or famine the immediate trigger. Understanding that external military aggression caused the exile clarifies how colonial ambitions disrupted Ethiopian sovereignty and set the stage for later resistance and international diplomacy.

Q3. What was the main significance of Haile Selassie's 1952 acquisition of Eritrea for Ethiopia?

🔹 A. Resolved ethnic tensions
🔹 B. Led to immediate economic boom
🔹 C. Provided sea access
🔹 D. Eliminated need for foreign aid

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Securing Eritrea gave Ethiopia a coastline on the Red Sea, ending its landlocked status and opening trade routes. The other options are inaccurate: ethnic tensions remained unresolved, economic growth was gradual, and foreign aid was still required. Sea access was crucial for importing goods and exporting cash crops, influencing later development plans and foreign policy decisions.

Q4. How did the formation of the Derg in 1974 change the structure of political power in Ethiopia?

🔹 A. Established democratic parliament
🔹 B. Created federal system
🔹 C. Restored imperial rule
🔹 D. Replaced monarchy with military council

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Derg was a committee of military officers that overthrew the emperor and took control, replacing the hereditary monarchy with a junta. It did not create a democratic parliament, a federal arrangement, nor restore the imperial system. This shift centralized authority in the armed forces, leading to radical socialist policies and a new era of authoritarian rule that reshaped Ethiopia’s political landscape.

Q5. Which combination of factors most directly contributed to the 1974 revolution that removed Haile Selassie?

🔹 A. Economic stagnation, drought, and lack of political reform
🔹 B. Successful foreign wars
🔹 C. Cultural renaissance
🔹 D. International sanctions

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Widespread poverty, recurring drought, and the emperor’s reluctance to implement substantial political and economic reforms created deep public discontent. Military officers, witnessing the suffering, mutinied and formed the Derg. The other options are misleading: Ethiopia did not enjoy victorious wars, nor was there a cultural boom or external sanctions driving the uprising. These core grievances explain why the revolution succeeded.

Q6. If Ethiopia had not gained control of Eritrea in 1952, which economic outcome would be most likely?

🔹 A. Rapid industrialization
🔹 B. Continued landlocked trade constraints
🔹 C. Increased agricultural exports
🔹 D. Immediate membership in the OAU

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Without Eritrea’s coastline, Ethiopia would have remained landlocked, forcing reliance on neighboring ports and raising transport costs. This would hinder export competitiveness and limit industrial growth. Rapid industrialization and higher agricultural exports would be unlikely without sea access, and OAU membership depends on political factors, not geography. Thus, trade constraints would persist, affecting economic development.

Q7. A neighboring country offers technical assistance to modernize Ethiopian agriculture in the 1960s. Which policy would best align with Haile Selassie's existing approach?

🔹 A. Nationalize all farmland
🔹 B. Replace all traditional practices with mechanized imports
🔹 C. Introduce cooperative farms while retaining imperial oversight
🔹 D. Withdraw from agriculture entirely

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Selassie favored gradual modernization while preserving the monarchy’s authority, encouraging cooperative schemes that respected traditional landholding patterns. Full nationalization would conflict with his cautious style, and wholesale mechanization would ignore local capacities. Abandoning agriculture contradicts Ethiopia’s agrarian base. Cooperative farms allowed state guidance without dismantling existing social structures, fitting his pragmatic reform agenda.

Q8. How many years passed between Haile Selassie's return to Addis Ababa and his acquisition of Eritrea?

🔹 A. 9
🔹 B. 10
🔹 C. 12
🔹 D. 11

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Haile Selassie returned to Addis Ababa after the liberation of Ethiopia in 1941. He secured Eritrea in 1952, which is a span of \(11\) years. The other numeric choices do not match the historical timeline. Calculating the interval helps students practice simple date subtraction and reinforces the sequence of post‑World War II events that shaped Ethiopia’s territorial ambitions.

Q9. Given famine mortality rates of 5% in 1972 and 12% in 1975 after the Derg took power, which inference about the Derg's early policies is most supported?

🔹 A. Policies worsened food security
🔹 B. Policies improved conditions
🔹 C. No impact
🔹 D. Data unreliable

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The rise from 5% to 12% mortality indicates a deterioration in food availability or distribution, suggesting that the Derg’s initial policies failed to alleviate famine and may have exacerbated it. An improvement would show a decline in mortality, while no impact would keep rates stable. Assuming the data are accurate, the increase points to negative consequences of the new regime’s actions.

Q10. If the Derg had not deposed Haile Selassie in 1974, how might Ethiopia's Cold War alignment have differed?

🔹 A. Joined NATO
🔹 B. Remained non‑aligned
🔹 C. Aligned with the Soviet bloc earlier
🔹 D. Became a US protectorate

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Haile Selassie pursued a policy of balanced diplomacy, accepting aid from both the United States and the United Nations while maintaining independence. Without the radical socialist Derg, Ethiopia likely would have continued this non‑aligned stance rather than joining a military alliance or falling under Soviet or American domination. This scenario underscores how internal regime change can redirect a nation's foreign‑policy orientation.

Q11. How does Haile Selassie's 1952 diplomatic success in securing Eritrea illustrate the principle of realpolitik?

🔹 A. He adhered strictly to idealist moral principles
🔹 B. He ignored international law
🔹 C. He leveraged great power interests for national benefit
🔹 D. He used force to annex territory

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Selassie negotiated with the United Nations and the United States, capitalizing on Cold War strategic interests to obtain Eritrea without resorting to war. This pragmatic use of superpower competition for Ethiopia’s advantage exemplifies realpolitik—prioritizing national interests over ideological purity. The other statements mischaracterize his method, which relied on diplomatic leverage rather than moral idealism or force.

Q12. As a policy advisor in 1970 Ethiopia, which reform would most likely have prevented the 1974 military coup?

🔹 A. Increase taxes on the military
🔹 B. Expand secret police
🔹 C. Close all foreign embassies
🔹 D. Implement land reforms and broaden political participation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Land reform would address peasant grievances, while expanding political inclusion would reduce the appeal of radical change among officers and civilians. Heavy taxation or secret police would heighten resentment, and isolating the country diplomatically would cut off beneficial external support. By tackling economic inequities and opening the political system, the government could have mitigated the conditions that prompted the coup.
