What is Olusegun Obasanjo Nigeria civilian rule 1999?

Olusegun Obasanjo’s return to civilian rule in 1999 marked Nigeria’s transition back to democracy after decades of military dictatorship, during which he worked to stabilize the economy, combat corruption, and restore international credibility despite ongoing challenges like ethnic tension and electoral fraud.

Q1. In which year did US President Bill Clinton visit Nigeria to promise aid for infrastructure?

A. 1998
B. 1999
C. 2000 ✅
D. 2001

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is 2000 because Clinton’s visit occurred that year, marking a diplomatic milestone. Options 1998 and 1999 are earlier dates and do not match the event, while 2001 is after the visit. Knowing the exact year helps place the aid promise in the timeline of Nigeria’s civilian transition and shows how international support was timed with early reforms.

B. 12
C. 14 ✅
D. 16

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Twelve states had adopted Sharia law, making 12 the correct figure. The other numbers are either too low or too high and do not reflect the documented spread of Sharia across the northern region. Recognizing the extent of adoption clarifies the scale of religious‑legal change and its relevance to national politics during the early civilian era.

Q3. What was the primary reason President Obasanjo retired many military officers from official posts after taking office?

A. To reduce defense spending ✅
B. To eliminate military influence over civilian governance
C. To increase foreign investment
D. To expand the size of the police force

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The retirement aimed to eliminate military influence, reinforcing civilian control and signaling democratic intent. Option A addresses budgeting, not the political goal; Option C concerns investment, which is unrelated; Option D involves police expansion, which was not the focus. Removing military officials helped improve Nigeria’s image abroad and reduced the risk of coups during the transition.

Q4. Why did the attorney‑general argue that Sharia law infringed on the rights of Muslims?

A. It allowed harsher punishments than for non‑Muslims ✅
B. It denied Muslims the right to vote
C. It removed religious freedom for Christians
D. It forced Muslims to adopt secular courts

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The attorney‑general claimed Sharia imposed punishments more severe than those for comparable offenses among non‑Muslims, thus violating equal treatment. Options B, C, and D describe rights that were not the basis of his argument. Understanding this legal critique explains why the federal leadership was uneasy about Sharia despite regional support.

Q5. A governor in a northern state wants to introduce a new penalty for theft that aligns with Sharia but also wants to avoid international criticism. Which approach best balances local religious expectations and external human‑rights concerns?

A. Apply amputation of the right hand for first offense only ✅
B. Replace corporal punishment with heavy fines
C. Use community service instead of physical punishment
D. Suspend all Sharia punishments for theft

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Community service respects the desire for a moral deterrent while eliminating physical punishment, thus appeasing both local religious expectations and international human‑rights observers. Option A retains a severe corporal penalty, Option B shifts away from any religious basis, and Option D abandons Sharia entirely, which could provoke local backlash.

Raising the defense budget, appointing generals, or expanding oil deals do not convey a clear break from military influence. The retirement helped reassure both Nigerians and the international community that the new government was committed to democratic governance.

Perceived legal inequality—seeing Sharia punishments as harsher for Muslims—fueled resentment among Christians who viewed the system as unfair. Economic competition, climate issues, and language differences were not the primary drivers of the heightened religious tension. Recognizing the sense of unequal treatment clarifies why the legal shift sparked broader inter‑faith violence during the civilian period.

Q8. Imagine a policy analyst advising the Nigerian government in 2003. To reduce ethnic violence in Nasarawa state, which combination of measures would likely be most effective?

A. Deploying more troops and imposing curfews ✅
B. Initiating inter‑tribal dialogue and economic development projects
C. Enforcing Sharia law uniformly across all tribes
D. Relocating the Tiv minority to another state

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Inter‑tribal dialogue paired with development projects addresses root causes—grievances and resource scarcity—while building trust. Military force (Option A) may suppress violence temporarily but can increase resentment. Uniform Sharia enforcement (Option C) ignores ethnic dimensions, and forced relocation (Option D) violates human rights and likely escalates conflict. A combined social‑economic strategy aligns with conflict‑resolution best practices.

C. It eliminates religious influence
D. It simplifies judicial processes

A. It redirects resources to security ✅
B. It boosts tourism
C. It increases foreign direct investment
D. It improves agricultural output

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Violence forces the government to allocate funds and personnel to security rather than development, stalling economic growth. Tourism, investment, and agricultural gains are unlikely when instability disrupts markets and deters investors, making the diversion of resources the most salient impact.

Q22. A policymaker must design a reform that reduces Sharia‑related human‑rights violations while preserving the cultural legitimacy of northern states. Which hybrid solution best meets both criteria?

A. Complete abolition of Sharia
B. Retaining Sharia but substituting corporal punishments with restorative justice programs ✅
C. Enforcing secular law nationwide
D. Allowing each individual to choose their own legal system

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Replacing physical punishments with restorative justice maintains the cultural framework of Sharia while aligning with human‑rights norms, offering a compromise that respects tradition and international standards. Full abolition removes cultural legitimacy, nationwide secular law ignores regional preferences, and individual choice is impractical to implement uniformly.

Q24. International donors threaten to withdraw aid unless Nigeria reforms its punishments for adultery. If the government negotiates a compromise that replaces death by stoning with a maximum prison term, what legal principle does this compromise illustrate?

A. Sovereignty
B. Proportionality ✅
C. Territoriality
D. Reciprocity

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The shift to a prison term reflects the principle of proportionality, ensuring that the punishment fits the offense and aligns with accepted human‑rights standards. Sovereignty, territoriality, and reciprocity do not capture the balance between severity and fairness achieved by the compromise.

Q25. A researcher collects data on the number of people displaced by religious violence before and after the introduction of Sharia in 2000. Which statistical test would best determine if the introduction had a significant effect?

A. Chi‑square test for independence
B. Linear regression
C. Paired t‑test ✅
D. ANOVA

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A paired t‑test compares the same locations before and after the policy change, directly assessing whether displacement levels differ significantly. Chi‑square examines categorical relationships, regression models trends, and ANOVA compares multiple groups, none of which specifically address the before‑after pairing needed for this analysis.
