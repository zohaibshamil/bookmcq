What is Economic underdevelopment in African states?

Economic underdevelopment in African states resulted from colonial legacies of resource extraction, over-reliance on single cash crops or minerals, lack of industrial infrastructure, and unfair global trade terms, which left newly independent nations with weak economies unable to sustain growth or provide basic services.

Q1. Nigeria earned about 80 % of its annual income from oil. If the world price of oil falls by 30 %, approximately how much will Nigeria’s total income change, assuming production volume stays constant?

A.It will decrease by about 24 % ✅
B.It will decrease by about 30 %
C.It will increase by about 30 %
D.It will stay the same
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Because oil makes up 80 % of income, a 30 % price drop reduces that portion by 0.8 × 30 % = 24 %. The remaining 20 % of income is unchanged, so total income falls roughly 24 %. Option A states this correctly. Option B assumes the whole economy falls 30 %, ignoring the 20 % that is not oil‑related. Option C suggests a rise, which contradicts a price drop. Option D says no change, which is impossible when the main revenue source declines. This calculation shows how a single‑commodity dependence makes a country vulnerable to global price swings.

Q2. Country Z exports coffee (60 % of export value) and cocoa (40 %). World coffee prices drop 20 % while cocoa prices rise 5 %. What is the net effect on export earnings?

A.Overall decline of 5 %
B.Overall decline of 10 % ✅
C.Overall increase of 5 %
D.Overall increase of 10 %
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Coffee, which accounts for 60 % of exports, loses 20 % of its price, causing a loss of 0.6 × 20 % = 12 % of total export earnings. Cocoa gains 5 % of its 40 % share, adding 0.4 × 5 % = 2 % to earnings. The net change is a 12 % loss plus a 2 % gain, resulting in a 10 % overall decline. Option B reflects this result. Option A understates the loss, option C incorrectly predicts growth, and option D overstates the gain. The example illustrates how mixed commodity price movements affect overall export performance.

Q4. If 70 % of a country's export market is in Western Europe and the West imposes a 15 % tariff on its main commodity, which of the following is the most likely immediate effect?

A.Export revenue will rise by 15 %
B.Export revenue will fall by about 10 % ✅
C.Domestic consumption will increase dramatically
D.The country will become self‑sufficient
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A 15 % tariff on the main export cuts the earnings from that product by 15 %. Since 70 % of all exports rely on that market, the overall export revenue drops by 0.7 × 15 % ≈ 10.5 %, which is close to a 10 % decline. Option B captures this effect. Option A incorrectly predicts a rise, option C assumes a domestic boom that the tariff does not guarantee, and option D suggests self‑sufficiency, which is unrealistic without structural change. The scenario demonstrates how trade barriers can quickly reduce national income.

Q5. During the Cold War, a Marxist‑leaning African government receives military aid from the Soviet Union. Which neighboring country's reaction is most consistent with historical patterns?

A.It seeks to join the aid program
B.It imposes economic sanctions
C.It invades to prevent Soviet influence ✅
D.It remains neutral
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Historical cases such as Angola show that neighboring states opposed to Soviet‑aligned regimes often responded with direct military action to curb perceived communist expansion. Option C reflects this pattern of invasion. Option A would be unlikely because the neighbor was typically anti‑communist, not collaborative. Option B (sanctions) was less common than armed intervention, and option D (neutrality) ignores the strategic interests that drove regional powers to intervene. This answer highlights the geopolitical tension of the era.

Q6. Why did colonial powers deliberately limit industrial development in African colonies?

A.To preserve local traditions
B.To create a market for imported manufactured goods ✅
C.Because they lacked technology
D.To encourage foreign investment
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Colonial administrations designed economies to supply raw materials and food while importing finished goods, ensuring that the colonies bought products from the metropole. This strategy kept the colonies dependent on the colonizer’s industry. Option B correctly states this motive. Option A misrepresents the economic intent, option C is inaccurate because the colonizers possessed the technology, and option D is opposite to the restrictive policy. Understanding this motive explains why post‑colonial states struggled to build domestic industry.

Q7. How does dependence on a single export commodity increase an economy’s vulnerability?

A.It diversifies income sources
B.It stabilizes government revenue
C.It makes the economy sensitive to price fluctuations ✅
D.It reduces foreign debt
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Relying on one commodity ties national income to that product’s world price. If the price falls, export earnings and government budgets shrink sharply, creating fiscal instability. Option C captures this sensitivity. Option A describes diversification, which is the opposite of dependence. Option B suggests stability, which is false when prices are volatile. Option D incorrectly implies debt reduction, whereas commodity dependence often forces borrowing to cover deficits. This concept explains why many African economies faced crises when commodity prices dropped.

Q8. What is the relationship between a population growth rate exceeding 2 % per year and a shortage of capital and skills in a developing economy?

A.Rapid growth reduces demand for skills
B.High growth amplifies the need for investment, worsening shortages ✅
C.Population growth directly creates capital
D.Growth rate has no impact on skill shortages
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When a population expands faster than 2 % annually, the demand for jobs, education, and infrastructure rises rapidly. Existing capital and skilled labor cannot keep pace, deepening shortages and hindering economic development. Option B accurately describes this dynamic. Option A reverses the effect, option C falsely claims that population automatically generates capital, and option D denies any link, ignoring the pressure that rapid growth places on limited resources. This relationship helps explain persistent under‑development despite abundant labor.

Q9. Which African country relied on oil exports for roughly 80 % of its annual income after independence?

A.Kenya
B.Nigeria ✅
C.Ghana
D.Ethiopia
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Nigeria’s economy was heavily dominated by oil, which accounted for about 80 % of its national revenue following independence. This reliance made the country extremely sensitive to global oil price movements. Option B correctly identifies Nigeria. Option A (Kenya) and option C (Ghana) had more diversified economies, while option D (Ethiopia) relied primarily on agriculture, not oil. Recognizing Nigeria’s oil dependence illustrates the broader pattern of single‑commodity reliance discussed in the context of African under‑development.
