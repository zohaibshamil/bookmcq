Q1. In what year did Burundi hold its first democratic elections that led to the election of a Hutu president?

🔹 A. 1990
🔹 B. 1991
🔹 C. 1992
🔹 D. 1993

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is 1993 because that year marked Burundi’s first competitive elections, resulting in a Hutu president. Options A, B, and C are incorrect as they predate the actual event. Knowing the election year is essential for understanding the timeline of ethnic politics. This factual detail helps students place subsequent coups and peace efforts in the correct chronological context.

Q2. Which Tutsi army officer declared himself president after the July 1996 coup in Burundi?

🔹 A. Léon Mugesera
🔹 B. Pierre Buyoya
🔹 C. Évariste Ndayishimiye
🔹 D. Alphonse Ndimirimana

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Pierre Buyoya is the correct answer; he seized power in July 1996 claiming to protect lives. The other names are either unrelated or refer to later figures, making them wrong choices. Recognizing Buyoya’s role clarifies how military interventions shaped Burundi’s political landscape and why later peace negotiations needed his participation.

Q3. Why did the 1972 mass uprising of Hutus against the Tutsi‑dominated government result in a large number of casualties on both sides?

🔹 A. It was a spontaneous protest with no leadership
🔹 B. The government responded with indiscriminate violence and reprisals
🔹 C. International forces intervened
🔹 D. Economic crisis caused migration

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is that the government’s harsh crackdown and subsequent reprisals caused massive loss of life among Hutus and Tutsis. Options A and D misrepresent the causes, while C suggests external actors that were not present. Understanding this dynamic shows how state repression can amplify ethnic conflict, a key concept for studying Burundi’s history.

Q4. How did the power‑sharing agreement that placed a Tutsi prime minister and a Hutu president aim to reduce ethnic tension in Burundi?

🔹 B. By sharing legislative seats equally
🔹 C. By balancing executive authority between the two groups
🔹 D. By eliminating the army

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Balancing executive authority between a Tutsi prime minister and a Hutu president was intended to create mutual dependency and deter unilateral domination, making C the correct answer. Option A ignores the power‑sharing intent, B describes a legislative arrangement not used, and D is unrealistic. This arrangement illustrates how constitutional design can be used to manage deep‑rooted ethnic divisions.

Q19. Applying just war theory, evaluate the 1996 army coup led by Pierre Buyoya that claimed to 'save lives.' Which criterion of just war is most violated by this action?

🔹 A. Legitimate authority
🔹 B. Right intention
🔹 C. Proportionality
🔹 D. Last resort

A coup lacks the recognized legal authority of a sovereign government, breaching the legitimate‑authority criterion, thus A is correct.
