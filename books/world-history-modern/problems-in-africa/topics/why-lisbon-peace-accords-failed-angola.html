What is Why Lisbon Peace Accords failed Angola?

The Lisbon Peace Accords failed because neither side fully trusted the other to honor the terms, elections in 1992 were disputed, UNITA rejected the results and resumed fighting, and external actors continued supplying weapons, undermining the fragile peace process.

Q1. In what month and year was the final ceasefire that ended the Angolan civil war signed?

A.Apr-02 ✅
B.Oct-94
C.Nov-94
D.Jan-94
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is April 2002 because the ceasefire signed after Savimbi's death marked the formal end of hostilities. The other dates refer to earlier agreements that were either temporary or ineffective. Recognizing the exact timing is vital for understanding how leadership changes can trigger decisive peace actions, illustrating the link between political events and conflict resolution in real‑world settings.

Q2. Who won the 1992 presidential election in Angola with 49.57% of the vote?

A.Jonas Savimbi
B.José Eduardo dos Santos ✅
C.Nelson Mandela
D.Julius Nyerere
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: José Eduardo dos Santos secured the presidency with 49.57% of the vote, making him the election winner. Savimbi, while a strong contender, received a lower share and therefore did not win. The other names are unrelated African leaders. Knowing the election’s outcome clarifies why the MPLA held governmental legitimacy, a factor that shaped subsequent attempts at peace and power‑sharing.

Q3. Why did UNITA reject the 1992 election results despite international monitoring?

A.They believed the vote was fraudulent ✅
B.They were satisfied with the outcome
C.They wanted to join the MPLA government
D.They had already won a majority of seats
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: UNITA claimed fraud, rejecting the results even though 400 UN observers called the election generally free and fair. The other options contradict the historical stance: UNITA was not satisfied, did not seek immediate coalition, and did not hold a parliamentary majority. Understanding this refusal helps explain how perceived legitimacy issues can reignite conflict, a pattern seen in many post‑election crises.

Q6. Why was the 1994 peace agreement initially ineffective in ending the conflict?

C.Savimbi broke the terms by financing his forces through diamonds ✅
D.The UN refused to monitor the agreement

📖 Explanation: Savimbi resumed fighting by using diamond revenues, violating the ceasefire and preventing the agreement from taking hold. This illustrates how a single party's non‑compliance, especially when funded by illicit resources, can derail peace initiatives in real‑world conflicts.

This case shows how leadership changes can open diplomatic windows, a dynamic relevant to many unresolved wars.

Q9. In the context of post‑war Angola, why might a coalition government that includes former rebels be considered a stabilizing strategy?

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

This principle is applied worldwide, where power‑sharing arrangements aim to transform combatants into political actors, thereby enhancing stability.

Q10. If the United Nations had doubled its personnel in Angola in 1993, which of the following outcomes would be most plausible?

A.The civil war would have ended within six months due to increased monitoring
B.Refugee numbers would have immediately dropped to zero
C.The UN could have more effectively enforced the ceasefire, potentially reducing civilian deaths and pressuring UNITA to negotiate ✅
D.The MPLA would have lost its international legitimacy
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Doubling UN staff would have improved monitoring capacity, allowing better reporting of violations and stronger pressure on UNITA to adhere to the ceasefire, likely lowering civilian casualties. Immediate war termination or refugee disappearance is unrealistic, and the MPLA’s legitimacy would not be threatened by UN presence. This scenario underscores how adequate peacekeeping resources can influence conflict dynamics.

Q11. Which UN measure would most directly limit a rebel group's ability to fund its operations through illegal diamond sales?

A.Deploying additional peacekeepers on the front lines
B.Imposing a global diamond certification scheme that blocks conflict diamonds from entering markets ✅
C.Providing humanitarian aid to civilian populations
D.Encouraging foreign investment in the country's oil sector
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A certification system, such as the Kimberley Process, prevents conflict diamonds from reaching legitimate markets, cutting off a major revenue source for rebels. Adding troops does not stop trade, aid does not affect financing, and oil investment is unrelated to diamonds. This answer illustrates how targeted economic sanctions can curb the financial lifelines of armed groups, a tactic used in many resource‑driven conflicts.

This demonstrates how timely international endorsement can diminish incentives for armed opposition.

Q14. When one party violates a ceasefire, which diplomatic response is most likely to restore confidence and reduce escalation?

A.Immediate military retaliation by the opposing side
B.Calling for a new round of negotiations mediated by a neutral third party ✅
C.Ignoring the violation
D.Announcing unconditional surrender
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Inviting neutral mediators to restart talks addresses the breach, rebuilds trust, and creates a platform for accountability, thereby lowering the chance of spiraling violence. Direct retaliation often escalates conflict, ignoring the issue undermines the ceasefire, and surrender is unrealistic. This answer illustrates a standard conflict‑resolution tool used in many peace processes worldwide.
