📝 Nigeria unfulfilled promises after independence (10 MCQs)

Independence and the Expectations of a New Nigerian Nation

Nigeria became independent from Britain on 1 October 1960, creating expectations of representative government, national unity, economic prosperity and improved living standards. Its political leaders promised that independence would allow Nigerians to control national resources and build institutions responsive to local needs. However, regional rivalry, economic inequality and political competition complicated the construction of a stable national state.

Regional Rivalry, Political Competition and Weak Institutions

The First Republic inherited a federal structure divided among powerful regional governments. Competition between the Northern, Western and Eastern regions influenced party politics and resource distribution. Electoral disputes, corruption allegations and disagreements over the national census weakened confidence in public institutions. Colonial administrative arrangements had not produced a widely accepted system for managing the country's political and regional diversity.

Military Coups, Civil War and Interrupted Democratic Development

The January 1966 coup, the July counter-coup and the violence that followed ended the First Republic. Civil war between 1967 and 1970 caused enormous human suffering and disrupted national development. Subsequent military administrations postponed constitutional democracy and concentrated authority in the armed forces. These developments sharply contradicted early expectations of uninterrupted civilian government and national stability.

Oil Wealth and the Persistence of Poverty and Corruption

Nigeria became increasingly wealthy through petroleum exports during the 1970s, but oil income did not produce equal benefits for the population. Poor accountability, corruption, unemployment and unequal infrastructure development limited the impact of economic growth. The 1979 return to civilian government was followed by another military coup in 1983, showing the continuing fragility of democratic institutions.

Evaluating the Unfulfilled Promise of Independence

Nigeria achieved international sovereignty, expanded education and developed major oil and industrial sectors, but political instability and unequal development weakened public expectations. The restoration of civilian rule in 1999 created another opportunity for democratic consolidation. The historical record is therefore mixed: independence succeeded politically, while the promises of accountable government, economic fairness and equal opportunity remained only partly realised.

Misconceptions About Nigeria's Independence Experience

'Independence immediately produced national unity' - regional and political rivalries continued. 'Oil wealth ended poverty' - revenues did not translate into equal living standards. 'Nigeria never returned to democracy after military rule' - civilian government returned in 1979 and again in 1999. These distinctions reveal both achievements and continuing failures.

Examining the Gap Between Independence Promises and Outcomes

Link independence in 1960 to the expectations of unity, democracy and economic development. Revise the First Republic, the 1966 coups, the 1967–1970 civil war and the military governments that followed. Questions may require an assessment of oil wealth, corruption, regional inequality and democratic restoration. Support arguments with dated events rather than general claims about failure.

Political and Economic Terms of Nigeria's Independence Era

  • First Republic - Nigeria's civilian government, 1963–1966.
  • Regionalism - Political organisation around regional interests.
  • Petroleum boom - Rapid growth in oil income.
  • Accountability - Obligation to answer for public decisions.
  • Military rule - Government controlled by armed forces.
  • Democratic consolidation - Strengthening of lasting democratic institutions.

📝 All Nigeria unfulfilled promises after independence MCQs

Q1. In 1986, the world oil price fell to a record low of how many dollars per barrel?

A.23.5
B.30
C.10 ✅
D.45
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is $10 because the global market price dropped to that level, marking a historic low. $23.50 was the price used for budget planning, not the actual price. $30 and $45 are higher than any recorded price that year, so they cannot be correct. Understanding the actual price is crucial because it explains why the government faced a severe revenue shortfall, leading to fiscal crises and political unrest.

Q2. What was the main reason the civilian government was accused of mismanagement?

A.Over‑reliance on oil exports ✅
B.Excessive military spending
C.Ignoring agriculture
D.High foreign aid
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Over‑reliance on oil exports is the main reason the civilian government was accused of mismanagement. Heavy dependence made the economy vulnerable to price swings, and when prices fell, revenues collapsed, exposing fiscal weakness. Excessive military spending, ignoring agriculture, and high foreign aid were not cited as primary causes of mismanagement. Recognizing the reliance on a single commodity helps explain why the government struggled to maintain stability and why critics blamed its economic policies.

Q3. How did the fall in oil prices contribute to political instability in Nigeria during the early 1980s?

A.Increased foreign aid
B.Reduced revenue leading to deficits and coups
C.Improved trade balance
D.Lower unemployment ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The reduction in oil revenue created large budget deficits, which weakened the state's capacity to provide services and pay civil servants, fueling public discontent. This financial strain contributed directly to political instability, as dissatisfied groups and the military saw the government's inability to manage the economy as justification for intervening. Options suggesting increased foreign aid, improved trade balance, or lower unemployment ignore the causal link between falling revenues and the surge in coups and unrest.

Q4. If Nigeria's oil price had remained at $23.50 instead of dropping to $10, which of the following outcomes would be most likely?

A.Immediate economic collapse
B.Stable economy with less pressure for coups ✅
C.Increased foreign debt
D.Rise in inflation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: If oil had stayed at $23.50 per barrel, the government would have retained the projected income, avoiding the large deficit that prompted austerity measures and loss of legitimacy. A stable fiscal situation would likely have reduced pressure on the ruling elite, making coups less attractive. Immediate economic collapse, higher foreign debt, or rising inflation would not be expected under steady revenue, so those options do not follow from the hypothetical price stability.

Q5. A neighboring country relies 80% on a single commodity that drops 60% in price. Based on Nigeria's experience, which policy should the country prioritize to avoid similar instability?

A.Increase military spending
B.Maintain subsidies
C.Diversify agriculture ✅
D.Raise oil taxes
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A country that depends heavily on one commodity and sees its price drop by 60 % should prioritize diversifying its agricultural sector, as Nigeria's experience shows that reliance on oil made the economy fragile. Increasing military spending or keeping subsidies would deepen fiscal strain, while raising oil taxes would be ineffective when prices are low. Diversification spreads risk, creates alternative income streams, and can help stabilize the economy during commodity price shocks.

Q6. Why did Major‑General Babangida introduce a ‘belt‑tightening’ campaign and focus on non‑oil sectors?

A.To reduce dependence on oil ✅
B.To increase military power
C.To please foreign investors
D.Personal agenda
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Babangida’s belt‑tightening campaign and emphasis on non‑oil industries were intended to reduce the nation’s dependence on volatile oil revenues. By promoting rice, steel, and vehicle assembly, the government hoped to build a broader tax base and improve food security. The other options—expanding military power, appeasing foreign investors, or serving personal ambition—do not explain the strategic shift toward economic diversification that was needed after the oil price crash.

Q7. A government budget was prepared assuming oil price \(P = 23.50\) dollars per barrel, but the actual price fell to \(P = 10\) dollars. If the budget projected revenue of \(R = 5\) billion dollars, what percentage shortfall does the government face?

A.0.45
B.0.3
C.0.1
D.0.57 ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The budget assumed a price \(P = 23.50\) dollars per barrel, projecting revenue \(R = 5\) billion dollars. When the actual price fell to \(P = 10\) dollars, revenue falls to \(\frac{10}{23.5}\times 5\) billion ≈ 2.13 billion, leaving a shortfall of about 57 %. Therefore, the correct percentage shortfall is 57 %. The other percentages (45 %, 30 %, 10 %) underestimate the impact, which would misrepresent the severity of the fiscal crisis.

Q8. If a country announces a promised election but later annuls it citing malpractice, which of the following international reactions is most consistent with the described events?

A.Immediate economic recovery
B.Political liberalization
C.Worsening economy ✅
D.Increase in foreign aid
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: When sanctions target oil purchases, a country that relies on oil exports for most of its foreign exchange experiences a sharp decline in income, worsening its economic situation. Nigeria’s experience shows that cutting off oil sales deepened the recession and heightened public hardship. Immediate economic recovery is unrealistic, and political liberalization or increased foreign aid are unlikely outcomes under such sanctions, making worsening economy the most consistent consequence.

Q9. What role did religious tension between the Muslim north and Christian south play in the political crises described?

A.Primary cause
B.Secondary factor ✅
C.Irrelevant
D.Led to immediate coup
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Religious tension between the Muslim north and Christian south acted as a secondary factor that amplified existing economic grievances but did not directly cause the coups. The primary drivers were fiscal collapse and political disputes. Describing the tension as the main cause or as irrelevant would misrepresent its role, while claiming it led to an immediate coup overstates its impact. Recognizing it as a secondary factor helps understand how multiple stresses combined to destabilize the nation.

Q10. If a government annuls a promised election, which international response is most likely?

A.Increased foreign investment
B.Support from the UN
C.No reaction
D.Condemnation and suspension from international bodies ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Annulment of a promised election typically draws international condemnation and may lead to suspension from bodies such as the Commonwealth, mirroring what occurred after the disputed Nigerian vote. Increased foreign investment or UN support would be unlikely responses to a perceived democratic backslide, and no reaction would ignore the global norms that favor electoral integrity. Therefore, condemnation and suspension best reflect the expected international reaction to such a breach.
