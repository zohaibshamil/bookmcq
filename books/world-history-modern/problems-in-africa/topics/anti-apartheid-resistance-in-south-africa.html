📝 All Anti-apartheid resistance in South Africa MCQs

Q1. In what year did the Sharpeville massacre occur?

🔹 A. 1960
🔹 B. 1955
🔹 C. 1963
🔹 D. 1952

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is 1960 because the Sharpeville massacre happened on March 21, 1960, when police opened fire on a crowd protesting pass laws. The other years are incorrect: 1955 marks the Freedom Charter meeting, 1963 is after the launch of armed resistance, and 1952 relates to the earlier protest campaign. Knowing the exact year helps place the event in the broader timeline of resistance, showing how it accelerated the shift toward armed struggle.

Q2. Who was the ANC leader who organized the 1952 protest campaign of work stoppages?

🔹 A. Albert Luthuli
🔹 B. Nelson Mandela
🔹 C. Walter Sisulu
🔹 D. Oliver Tambo

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Albert Luthuli led the 1952 campaign, known as the Defiance Campaign, encouraging black South Africans to stop work on designated days. The other figures were prominent ANC members but did not head this particular effort. Recognizing Luthuli’s role clarifies leadership dynamics within the ANC and illustrates how early non‑violent resistance set the stage for later, more militant actions.

Q3. What was the name of the ANC’s armed wing launched in 1961?

🔹 A. Umkhonto we Sizwe
🔹 B. African National Liberation Front
🔹 C. People’s Liberation Army
🔹 D. South African Freedom Fighters

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The armed wing created in 1961 was called Umkhonto we Sizwe, meaning “Spear of the Nation.” The other names are fabricated or refer to unrelated groups. Understanding this name is essential because it marks the ANC’s transition from peaceful protest to sabotage, reflecting a strategic response to state violence after Sharpeville.

Q4. Why were Africans forbidden to strike under apartheid law?

🔹 A. The government feared economic disruption
🔹 B. Strikes were considered communist activity
🔹 C. Workers were deemed unskilled
🔹 D. There was no legal framework for labor disputes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The apartheid regime labeled any organized labor action as communist, using the Suppression of Communism Act to criminalize dissent. While the government also feared economic disruption, the primary legal justification was the communist label, allowing harsh penalties. Recognizing this motive shows how the regime conflated political opposition with subversion, limiting legitimate labor rights and forcing activists to seek alternative tactics.

Q5. How did the Freedom Charter’s claim that ‘no government can claim authority unless it is based on the will of the people’ challenge apartheid ideology?

🔹 A. It affirmed universal suffrage contrary to racial voting restrictions
🔹 B. It demanded a monarchy to replace the republic
🔹 C. It supported segregation of political parties
🔹 D. It called for a single‑party state

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The statement directly opposed apartheid’s premise that a white minority could govern without the consent of the black majority. By insisting on authority derived from the people's will, the Charter demanded inclusive democracy, undermining the legal basis for racial exclusion. The other options misrepresent the Charter’s inclusive vision, highlighting how the document sought a fundamental shift from minority rule to popular legitimacy.

Q6. What was the significance of the 1957 bus boycott for the anti‑apartheid movement?

🔹 A. It demonstrated mass economic pressure without violence
🔹 B. It led to immediate policy change in education
🔹 C. It caused the ANC to dissolve
🔹 D. It resulted in the arrest of all leaders

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The boycott showed that coordinated, non‑violent economic action could force the government to retract unfair fare increases, proving the power of collective consumer resistance. It did not directly affect education policy, nor did it dissolve the ANC; instead, it reinforced the effectiveness of peaceful mass protest, inspiring later campaigns such as Sharpeville.

Q7. How did the formation of a coalition with Asian and coloured groups in 1955 strengthen the ANC?

🔹 B. It introduced new military tactics
🔹 C. It eliminated the need for international aid
🔹 D. It focused the movement solely on economic issues

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

By joining forces with Asian and coloured activists, the ANC created a multiracial front that challenged the apartheid narrative of separate development. This broadened support made the movement harder for the government to isolate and delegitimize. The coalition did not instantly bring military expertise, nor did it remove reliance on global solidarity, but it underscored the inclusive nature of the struggle.

Q8. Why did the Sharpeville protest mark a turning point from non‑violent to violent resistance?

🔹 A. Police used lethal force, showing peaceful protest could be met with bloodshed
🔹 B. The protest succeeded without any casualties
🔹 C. International bodies praised the protest tactics
🔹 D. The ANC decided to abandon protest altogether

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The police firing on unarmed protesters, killing 67, demonstrated that non‑violent resistance could be violently suppressed, prompting many leaders to consider armed struggle as a necessary alternative. The event did not end peacefully, nor did it receive universal commendation; instead, it galvanized the decision to form Umkhonto we Sizwe, reflecting a strategic shift in response to state brutality.

Q9. In what way did the Suppression of Communism Act impact opposition leaders?

🔹 C. It required them to register with the United Nations
🔹 D. It provided funding for their activities

The act allowed the state to prosecute anyone opposing apartheid by accusing them of communism, effectively silencing leaders through arrests and bans. It did not offer protection, international registration, or financial support. Understanding this mechanism reveals how legal tools were used to suppress legitimate political activism, forcing opposition figures to operate clandestinely or face severe penalties.

Q16. How did the concept that ‘no government can claim authority unless based on the will of the people’ reflect democratic principles?

Q19. Suppose a city implements a \(40\)-hour work week and a minimum wage similar to ANC demands. Predict the impact on unemployment rates over five years.

🔹 A. Unemployment may initially rise slightly then stabilize as job creation spreads
🔹 B. Unemployment will instantly drop to zero
🔹 C. Unemployment will double immediately and remain high
🔹 D. Unemployment will be unaffected because wages do not influence jobs

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Introducing a shorter work week and a wage floor can initially increase labor costs, prompting some firms to adjust staffing, which may cause a modest short‑term rise in unemployment. Over time, higher wages boost consumer spending, encouraging firms to hire more workers, leading to stabilization or modest decline. Immediate eradication or drastic doubling is unrealistic, and wages do affect employment dynamics.
