📝 All Union of South Africa formed 1910 MCQs

Q1. In what year was the Union of South Africa officially created?

🔹 A. A) 1899
🔹 B. B) 1910
🔹 C. C) 1925
🔹 D. D) 1948

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Union was formed in 1910 when the British colonies of Cape, Natal, Transvaal, and Orange Free State were united. 1899 marks the start of the Boer War, not the union. 1925 and 1948 refer to later South African events. Knowing the correct year is essential for placing the Union in the broader timeline of colonial and post‑colonial history.

Q2. Which term describes the Dutch settlers who later became known as Boers?

🔹 A. A) Afrikaners
🔹 B. B) Huguenots
🔹 C. C) Zulu
🔹 D. D) Khoikhoi

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Afrikaners, also called Boers, were Dutch‑origin settlers who farmed the Cape region. Huguenots were French refugees, Zulu a major African ethnic group, and Khoikhoi indigenous peoples. Recognizing the identity of the Boers helps explain their later migration northward and their role in forming the Union.

Q3. Why did many Boer farmers leave the Cape Colony during the 1830s?

🔹 A. A) To escape a drought
🔹 B. B) To avoid British taxes
🔹 C. C) Because British abolition of slavery threatened their labor system
🔹 D. D) To seek gold mines

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The British emancipation of slaves in 1838 undermined the Boers' reliance on cheap labor, prompting them to seek independence through the Great Trek. Drought and taxes were not the primary drivers, and gold mining became a later incentive. Understanding this motive clarifies the socioeconomic pressures that shaped the future Union.

Q4. How did pass laws restrict the movement of black South Africans after 1910?

🔹 A. A) By requiring a passport for travel abroad
🔹 B. B) By mandating a written permit to live or work in a white‑owned area
🔹 C. C) By limiting them to one city per year
🔹 D. D) By banning them from using railways

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Pass laws required a written permit—often called a pass—to live in towns or work for white employers, effectively controlling labor mobility. They were not about international travel, city limits, or railway use. This mechanism entrenched segregation and limited economic opportunities for black people.

Q5. What was the significance of the Great Trek for the later formation of the Union?

🔹 A. A) It created a unified Boer state that directly became the Union
🔹 B. B) It led to the establishment of independent Boer republics that were later incorporated
🔹 C. C) It resulted in the immediate abolition of slavery
🔹 D. D) It caused the British to withdraw from southern Africa

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Great Trek produced the Transvaal and Orange Free State, two independent Boer republics later defeated in the Boer War and incorporated into the Union. It did not create a single Boer state, nor did it end slavery or force British withdrawal. The trek’s legacy shaped the political geography that the Union later unified.

Q6. If the British had not abolished slavery in 1838, which of the following outcomes would be most likely for the composition of the Union?

🔹 A. A) A larger proportion of Dutch‑origin whites
🔹 B. B) Fewer British settlers in the Cape
🔹 C. C) No Great Trek and therefore no Boer republics
🔹 D. D) Immediate inclusion of black voters

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Without emancipation, the Boers would have had less incentive to leave the Cape, likely preventing the Great Trek and the formation of separate Boer republics. This would not directly affect the proportion of Dutch whites, British settlement patterns, or voting rights for blacks, which remained limited.

Q7. Assuming a total population of ten million in 1910, approximately how many people were white?

🔹 A. A) 1.8 million
🔹 B. B) 2.5 million
🔹 C. C) 3.0 million
🔹 D. D) 4.0 million

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

White South Africans comprised about 18 % of the population. Ten million × 0.18 = 1.8 million. The other options represent higher percentages (25 %, 30 %, 40 %) that do not match the historical demographic data.

Q8. A black laborer in 1910 needed a pass to move between farms. What immediate effect did this have on his employment options?

🔹 A. A) He could freely choose any employer
🔹 B. B) He was forced to stay with his current employer unless a new pass was granted
🔹 C. C) He could only work in urban areas
🔹 D. D) He could negotiate higher wages

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The pass system tied a worker to an employer; without a pass from the current employer, he could not legally work elsewhere. This limited bargaining power, confined labor to specific farms, and prevented easy migration to cities or higher‑pay jobs.

Q9. If the reserved areas for black Africans were increased from 7 % to 15 % of total land, which of the following would most likely improve?

🔹 A. A) Urban industrial growth
🔹 B. B) Agricultural self‑sufficiency for black families
🔹 C. C) Export of gold ore
🔹 D. D) British military presence

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Expanding reserves would give black families more land to farm, improving food production and reducing reliance on wage labor. It would not directly affect urban industry, gold exports, or British troops.

Q10. A historian claims that economic interests, not political compromise, drove the Union’s creation. Which piece of evidence best supports this view?

🔹 A. A) The desire to standardize railway gauges across colonies
🔹 B. B) The inclusion of all four colonies regardless of language
🔹 C. C) The adoption of a single national flag
🔹 D. D) The establishment of a bilingual parliament

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Standardizing railway gauges facilitated trade and movement of goods, reflecting economic motives. Language inclusion, flag adoption, and bilingual parliament are political or symbolic actions, not directly tied to economic benefit.

Q11. If the Union had incorporated neighboring Mozambique, how would the demographic percentages likely change?

🔹 A. A) The proportion of black Africans would decrease
🔹 B. B) The proportion of white Europeans would increase
🔹 C. C) The proportion of Asians would double
🔹 D. D) The proportion of mixed‑race ‘coloureds’ would rise

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Mozambique’s population at the time was overwhelmingly African, so adding its people would raise the overall number of black Africans, reducing the relative share of whites and other groups.

Q12. How did the British victory in the Boer War influence the unification process?

🔹 A. A) It forced the Boer republics to join the Union under British terms
🔹 B. B) It led to immediate independence for all colonies
🔹 C. C) It caused the British to abandon southern Africa
🔹 D. D) It resulted in the abolition of all pass laws

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The defeat of the Transvaal and Orange Free State allowed Britain to incorporate those territories with Cape and Natal, creating the Union. Independence, abandonment, or abolition of pass laws did not follow the war.

Q13. What role did British settlers in the Cape Colony play in the formation of the Union?

🔹 A. A) They opposed unification and fought the Boers
🔹 B. B) They provided a British administrative framework that facilitated merger
🔹 C. C) They remained isolated and had no influence
🔹 D. D) They formed a separate state outside the Union

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

British settlers established legal and administrative institutions that were extended to the new Union, easing the integration of the colonies. They did not oppose unification, stay isolated, or create a separate state.

Q14. Explain how early labor exploitation in gold mines foreshadowed later apartheid policies.

🔹 A. A) Both relied on racial segregation of workforces
🔹 B. B) Both encouraged equal voting rights
🔹 C. C) Both eliminated all forms of discrimination
🔹 D. D) Both promoted free land ownership for blacks

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Gold‑mine labor exploited black workers under harsh conditions and restricted movement, establishing patterns of racial hierarchy that later apartheid codified. The other options contradict historical reality, as apartheid denied voting, maintained discrimination, and restricted land ownership.

Q15. Which two Boer republics became part of the Union in 1910?

🔹 A. A) Transvaal and Natal
🔹 B. B) Orange Free State and Cape Colony
🔹 C. C) Transvaal and Orange Free State
🔹 D. D) Natal and Orange Free State

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Transvaal and Orange Free State were the independent Boer republics incorporated into the Union. Natal and Cape Colony were British colonies, not Boer republics.

Q16. Approximately what percentage of the Union’s total population were black Africans?

🔹 A. A) 50 %
🔹 B. B) 60 %
🔹 C. C) 70 %
🔹 D. D) 80 %

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Black Africans made up roughly 70 % of the population, far exceeding the other listed percentages. This demographic dominance shaped the social and economic dynamics of the Union.

Q17. If a black South African in 1910 wanted to purchase land outside a reserve, which legal barrier would they most likely encounter?

🔹 A. A) No available land for sale
🔹 B. B) A law forbidding ownership of land outside reserves by black persons
🔹 C. C) Mandatory military service
🔹 D. D) Requirement to speak Dutch fluently

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Legislation explicitly prohibited black individuals from owning land outside designated reserves, limiting economic autonomy. While land scarcity and language requirements existed, they were not the primary legal obstacles.

Q18. Why could a minority of whites (18 % of the population) dominate political power in the Union?

🔹 A. A) They controlled the majority of the economy and voting rights
🔹 B. B) They were the only literate group
🔹 C. C) They received foreign military aid
🔹 D. D) They were granted exclusive religious authority

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

White minorities held most economic assets, controlled the franchise, and occupied key administrative positions, enabling political dominance despite being a demographic minority. Literacy, foreign aid, and religious authority were not the decisive factors.

Q19. Why were pass laws considered an effective tool of social control?

🔹 A. A) They allowed free movement of workers
🔹 B. B) They tied individuals to specific employers and locations
🔹 C. C) They provided universal healthcare
🔹 D. D) They increased tax revenue directly

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By requiring a pass for each change of employment or residence, the system bound workers to employers and limited mobility, reinforcing segregation and labor exploitation. The other options misrepresent the purpose of pass laws.

Q20. If 60 % of the white population were Dutch, what proportion of the total Union population were Dutch whites?

🔹 A. A) 10.8 %
🔹 B. B) 12 %
🔹 C. C) 15 %
🔹 D. D) 18 %

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

White South Africans were 18 % of the total; 60 % of that group were Dutch. 0.18 × 0.60 = 0.108, or 10.8 % of the overall population. The other percentages do not reflect this calculation.

Q21. How did the creation of the Union set the stage for later segregation laws?

🔹 A. A) It introduced universal suffrage for all races
🔹 B. B) It established a political framework dominated by whites that could later formalize racial discrimination
🔹 C. C) It abolished all land restrictions for black people
🔹 D. D) It created a single, racially integrated education system

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Union’s white‑controlled institutions and limited black franchise created a foundation that later governments expanded into formal apartheid legislation. Universal suffrage, land freedom, and integrated education did not occur at that time.
