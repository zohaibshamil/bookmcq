What is Economic problems and natural disasters in Africa?

Economic problems and natural disasters in Africa compounded each other as droughts, famines, and floods destroyed agricultural livelihoods in already fragile economies, while poor infrastructure and debt prevented effective disaster response and long-term recovery.

Q1. What year did the severe drought in Africa end?

A. 1984
B. 1985
C. 1986 ✅
D. 1987

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The drought that began in \(1982\) and lasted through \(1985\) finally ended in 1986, allowing many regions to recover and achieve record harvests. The correct answer is 1986 because historical records show the drought ceased that year. The other years either precede the end or occur after the drought had already stopped, making them inaccurate. Recognizing the end date helps understand the timing of subsequent economic recovery and policy responses.

Q2. Why did the IMF‑prescribed Economic Structural Adjustment Programme often lead to higher food prices?

A. It increased agricultural productivity
B. It required devaluation and cuts to food subsidies ✅
C. It expanded export markets
D. It lowered interest rates

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The ESAP typically forced countries to devalue their currency and reduce food price subsidies. Devaluation makes imports more expensive, while cutting subsidies removes price supports, both pushing food prices upward. Increased productivity or expanded export markets would tend to lower prices, not raise them. Lowering interest rates is unrelated to food pricing. Understanding this link explains why many households faced higher costs during adjustment periods.

Q3. How did the combination of a debt crisis and severe drought affect unemployment in African nations during the 1980s?

A. It caused unemployment to rise sharply ✅
B. It led to a temporary drop in unemployment
C. It had no noticeable effect on unemployment
D. It reduced unemployment by creating new jobs in aid programs

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The debt crisis reduced government spending and limited investment, while the drought devastated agriculture, the main employer for many. Both factors together eliminated jobs and raised unemployment sharply. A temporary drop or no effect contradicts the loss of income sources, and creating new jobs through aid was insufficient to offset the widespread job losses. This demonstrates how simultaneous economic and environmental shocks can exacerbate labor market distress.

Q4. If a country devalues its currency by 20 % and simultaneously reduces food subsidies, what is the most likely impact on real wages?

A. Real wages increase by 20 %
B. Real wages stay unchanged
C. Real wages fall by roughly 20 %
D. Real wages double ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Devaluation makes imported goods, including food, more expensive, while cutting subsidies removes a price cushion, together raising the cost of living. If nominal wages do not rise, the purchasing power of workers declines, effectively causing real wages to fall. The answer indicating a fall (approximately 20 %) reflects this loss of purchasing power. Options suggesting an increase or doubling ignore the inflationary pressure, and unchanged wages would only be possible with compensating nominal wage hikes, which the scenario does not provide.

Q5. Country X follows the ESAP but decides to keep education spending while cutting health expenditures. Which social indicator is most likely to deteriorate first?

A. Literacy rate ✅
B. Infant mortality rate
C. Primary school enrollment
D. Number of universities

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Health cuts directly affect services such as vaccinations, maternal care, and disease treatment, leading to a rapid rise in infant mortality. Education spending influences literacy and enrollment, which tend to change more slowly. Thus, the infant mortality rate is the indicator most likely to worsen first. Literacy, school enrollment, and university numbers are less immediately sensitive to short‑term health budget reductions, making them less likely to show immediate decline.

Q6. Given a 30 % drop in export revenue, a 40 % reduction in crop output, and debt service equal to 25 % of GDP, which policy combination best stabilizes the economy?

A. Increase military spending and raise taxes
B. Seek debt restructuring and invest in agricultural recovery
C. Cut all public services and adopt a fixed exchange rate ✅
D. Raise interest rates and reduce foreign aid

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Debt restructuring eases the fiscal burden, and investing in agriculture restores food production and export potential, directly addressing the two biggest shocks. Increasing military spending or raising taxes would worsen fiscal strain, while cutting all services would deepen social crises. Raising interest rates could deter investment, and reducing aid would remove essential support. Therefore, the combination of restructuring debt and agricultural investment offers the most balanced path to stability.

Q7. Which factor contributed most directly to the famine experienced in Africa during the early 1980s?

A. Global oil price decline
B. Severe drought causing crop failures ✅
C. Rapid urbanization
D. Technological advancement in farming

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The famine was driven primarily by the severe drought from \(1982\) to \(1985\) that devastated crops, leading to food shortages and starvation. While falling oil prices affected export earnings, they did not cause immediate food scarcity. Urbanization and technological advances relate to long‑term trends but were not the immediate trigger. Recognizing the drought as the main cause highlights the vulnerability of food systems to climate shocks.
