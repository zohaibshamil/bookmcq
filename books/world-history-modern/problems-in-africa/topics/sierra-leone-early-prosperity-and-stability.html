Q1. In what year did Sierra Leone become independent?

🔹 A. \(1960\)
🔹 B. \(1961\)
🔹 C. \(1962\)
🔹 D. \(1963\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Sierra Leone achieved independence in \(1961\). The year \(1961\) marks the moment when the country left British colonial rule and established its own government. Option B is correct because it matches that date. Options A, C, and D list incorrect years that do not correspond to the historic event. Knowing the exact year helps place subsequent political developments, such as the leadership of Sir Milton Margai, in the correct chronological context.

Q2. Why did the All‑People’s Congress (APC) ban all other political parties in \(1978\)?

🔹 A. To reduce electoral competition
🔹 B. To consolidate power and eliminate dissent
🔹 C. To comply with international pressure
🔹 D. To promote multi‑party democracy

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The APC’s decision to outlaw all opposition parties in \(1978\) was primarily a move to consolidate power and silence dissent. By removing legal avenues for criticism, the ruling party could control the political arena without challenge. Option B correctly identifies this motive. Option A suggests a desire for competition, which contradicts the action of banning parties. Option C implies external pressure, but the ban was an internal strategy. Option D claims promotion of democracy, which is the opposite of the authoritarian outcome. This demonstrates how eliminating opposition can erode democratic institutions.

Q3. How did the exhaustion of iron‑ore deposits affect Sierra Leone’s economic stability?

🔹 A. It caused a sharp decline in export revenues
🔹 B. It led to increased foreign investment
🔹 C. It boosted agricultural production
🔹 D. It had no impact on the economy

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The depletion of iron‑ore reserves removed a major source of export earnings, leading to a sharp drop in national income. With fewer minerals to sell, the government faced reduced foreign exchange and budget shortfalls. Option A correctly states this decline in export revenues. Option B suggests increased investment, which is unlikely when a key resource disappears. Option C claims agricultural boost, not directly linked to iron‑ore loss. Option D asserts no impact, contradicting the evident economic contraction. This illustrates how reliance on a single resource can destabilize an economy when that resource runs out.

🔹 C. It attracted more foreign buyers
🔹 D. It eliminated smuggling

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The corruption that infiltrated Sierra Leone’s diamond sector meant that much of the profit was siphoned off by officials and smugglers rather than entering the public treasury. Option B accurately captures this diversion of profits to private hands. Option A incorrectly claims increased transparency, the opposite of the situation. Option C suggests attraction of more foreign buyers, which corruption typically discourages. Option D states that smuggling was eliminated, which contradicts the historical reality of rampant smuggling. Understanding this link shows how corruption can erode state finances even when valuable resources are abundant.

Q5. A neighboring country with similar mineral wealth decides to nationalize diamond mining companies. Which outcome is most likely based on Sierra Leone’s experience?

🔹 A. State treasury will see a large, sustainable increase
🔹 B. Corruption may divert profits, limiting public benefit
🔹 C. International investors will increase funding
🔹 D. Diamond production will double

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Nationalizing diamond mines can concentrate control but also creates opportunities for misuse if oversight is weak. Sierra Leone’s experience shows that without transparent management, state‑owned enterprises often become channels for corruption and smuggling, limiting public benefit. Option B correctly predicts that profits may be diverted, reducing the intended gain for citizens. Option A’s claim of a large, sustainable increase ignores the risk of misallocation. Option C’s expectation of more foreign funding is unrealistic when ownership shifts away from investors. Option D’s idea of production doubling overlooks operational and market constraints. The scenario highlights the need for strong governance when taking over natural‑resource industries.

Q7. Suppose a new leader promises to eliminate smuggling of natural resources. Which policy would be most effective given Sierra Leone’s past issues?

🔹 A. Increase export taxes
🔹 B. Privatize mining companies
🔹 C. Establish strict border controls and transparent licensing
🔹 D. Rely on foreign NGOs

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

To curb smuggling effectively, a government must combine strict border enforcement with clear, transparent licensing that limits opportunities for illegal diversion. Option C reflects this comprehensive approach, targeting both the supply chain and the regulatory environment. Option A’s higher export taxes could encourage further smuggling to avoid costs. Option B’s privatization without oversight might replicate previous corruption. Option D’s reliance on NGOs alone lacks the enforcement power needed. Sierra Leone’s history shows that without robust controls, diamond profits can be siphoned off, so a policy mixing enforcement and transparency offers the best chance of success.
