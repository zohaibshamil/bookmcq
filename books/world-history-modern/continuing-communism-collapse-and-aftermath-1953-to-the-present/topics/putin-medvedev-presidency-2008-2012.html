What is Putin Medvedev presidency 2008-2012?

Dmitry Medvedev’s presidency served as a placeholder during Putin’s constitutionally mandated break, featuring rhetorical modernization and limited liberal gestures, but real power remained with Putin as prime minister; the tandem arrangement preserved regime continuity while managing international perceptions ahead of Putin’s 2012 return.

Q1. What year did Dmitry Medvedev become President of Russia?

A.2008 ✅
B.2009
C.2010
D.2011
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Dmitry Medvedev was elected President in March 2008, succeeding Vladimir Putin. This election marked the first peaceful handover of power in post‑Soviet Russia and initiated the so‑called “tandem” system, where Medvedev held the presidency while Putin served as Prime Minister.

Q2. Which constitutional amendment allowed Vladimir Putin to return to the presidency in 2012?

A.Extension of presidential term limits
B.Resetting term count after a break ✅
C.Introducing a parliamentary system
D.Abolishing the presidency
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The 2008 amendment reset the count of presidential terms after a break, permitting Putin to run again in 2012. This procedural change was justified as a democratic reset, but critics argued it was a legal maneuver to extend his influence beyond the two‑term limit originally set by the 1993 Constitution.

Q3. During Medvedev’s presidency, which major reform aimed to modernize Russia’s economy?

A.Nationalization of oil assets
B.The “Modernisation Program”
C.Introduction of a flat tax ✅
D.Privatization of agriculture
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Medvedev launched the “Modernisation Program” in 2009, focusing on innovation, technology, and infrastructure. While the program emphasized high‑tech development, the most visible fiscal change was the continuation of the flat‑rate personal income tax introduced under Putin, intended to simplify taxation and encourage investment.

Q4. What was the primary purpose of the 2008 “Yukos‑like” asset seizure controversy?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The seizure of assets from Yukos‑affiliated companies was framed as a move to curb the political and economic influence of Russia’s oligarchs. By asserting state control over strategic sectors, the government aimed to reduce the risk of private power challenging federal authority, a theme consistent with earlier Yeltsin‑era reforms.
