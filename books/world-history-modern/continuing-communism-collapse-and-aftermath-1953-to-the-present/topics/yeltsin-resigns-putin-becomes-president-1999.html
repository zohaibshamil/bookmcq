📝 Yeltsin resigns Putin becomes President 1999 (21 MCQs)

The Final Day of Yeltsin’s Presidency

Boris Yeltsin resigned as president of the Russian Federation on 31 December 1999, six months before his elected term was due to end. His departure concluded a decade of post-Soviet leadership marked by market transition, political conflict and economic crisis. Yeltsin announced that Vladimir Putin, then prime minister, would serve as acting president until a new presidential election could be held.

Why Yeltsin Chose to Leave Office Early

Yeltsin’s authority had been weakened by poor health, the 1998 financial crisis, declining popularity and repeated government instability. His administration faced renewed conflict in the North Caucasus after militants entered Dagestan in August 1999. Yeltsin also wanted to influence the succession and secure continuity in the presidency. His early resignation allowed Putin to take advantage of incumbency before the scheduled election.

Putin’s Rapid Rise Through Government Office

Putin had been appointed prime minister on 9 August 1999 after serving in senior security and government positions. He gained public visibility through the renewed Chechen campaign and his image as a decisive leader. Yeltsin’s resignation on 31 December made him acting president under the constitution. The transition gave Putin control of presidential responsibilities and a prominent position in the approaching election.

The March 2000 Presidential Election

Putin won the presidential election on 26 March 2000, receiving approximately 53 percent of the vote in the first round. His victory gave him a full term and ended the uncertainty surrounding the succession. The result reflected public demand for order and effective government after the instability of the 1990s, as well as Putin’s advantages as acting president and his strong public profile.

Political Continuity and the End of the Yeltsin Era

The transition preserved the Russian Federation’s constitutional institutions while changing the style and direction of executive leadership. Yeltsin received legal immunity under a decree issued as he left office, and Putin inherited the challenges of economic recovery, federal authority and the Chechen conflict. The transfer became a defining moment in post-Soviet history because it opened a new period of increasingly centralized presidential rule.

Misconceptions About the 1999 Presidential Transition

'Putin first became president after winning the March 2000 election' overlooks his acting presidency beginning on 31 December 1999. 'Yeltsin was removed by parliament' confuses a voluntary resignation with impeachment or a coup. 'Putin was an unknown outsider in 1999' ignores his recent premiership and earlier senior government and security roles.

Exam Focus: Resignation, Acting Presidency and Election

Distinguish Putin’s appointment as prime minister on 9 August 1999, his accession as acting president on 31 December and his election on 26 March 2000. Explain Yeltsin’s health and political difficulties, the Chechen conflict and the demand for stability. Assess how the early transfer of office benefited Putin’s political position.

Key Terms of the 1999 Succession

  • Resignation - Voluntary departure from office.
  • Vladimir Putin - Yeltsin’s chosen successor.
  • Acting president - Temporary constitutional officeholder.
  • Prime minister - Head of government.
  • Presidential election - National vote for president.
  • Succession - Transfer of political leadership.

Q1. Which position did Boris Yeltsin appoint Vladimir Putin to in August 1999?

A.President
B.Prime Minister ✅
C.Defense Minister
D.Foreign Minister
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The appointment came at a critical moment as Yeltsin prepared for his exit. By naming Putin prime minister, Yeltsin positioned him directly in the line of succession, allowing Putin to assume the acting presidency when Yeltsin stepped down, which ultimately facilitated Putin’s rise to the highest office.

Q2. According to the Russian constitution at the time, what happens if a president retires before the end of his term?

A.The Senate appoints a new president
B.A new election is held immediately
C.The prime minister becomes acting president for three months ✅
D.The vice‑president takes over permanently
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The constitution stipulated that the prime minister would automatically assume the role of acting president for a three‑month period, during which a presidential election must be organized. This provision ensured continuity of governance and gave the acting president a short, defined window to govern before voters chose a successor.

Q3. What percentage of the popular vote did Vladimir Putin receive in the March 2000 presidential election?

A.\(53\%\) ✅
B.\(47\%\)
C.\(61\%\)
D.\(45\%\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Official results showed that Putin secured a clear majority on the first ballot, obtaining \(53\%\) of the votes. This decisive win eliminated the need for a runoff and demonstrated the strong public endorsement he received following his handling of the Chechen crisis and other national issues.

Q4. Given that the Unity bloc won 24 % of Duma seats in the 1999 election, what strategic benefit did its alliance with the Communists provide?

A.It allowed the bloc to control the executive branch
B.It granted a parliamentary majority to pass legislation
C.It ensured control over the judiciary
D.It eliminated opposition parties completely ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Forming an alliance with the Communists enabled the Unity bloc to combine its 24 % share with the Communists’ 25 % share, creating a coalition that held a clear majority in the Duma. This majority was crucial for advancing Putin’s reform agenda, as it reduced resistance and facilitated the passage of key laws during his early presidency.

Q5. What was the outcome for oligarchs Vladimir Gusinsky and Boris Berezovsky after they criticized Putin?

A.Both were promoted to senior government posts
B.Both were removed from their media positions and faced arrest threats ✅
C.Both fled to the United States voluntarily
D.Both became allies of the Unity bloc
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Both Gusinsky and Berezovsky, who owned major television networks and were outspoken critics, were stripped of their media assets and threatened with criminal prosecution for alleged corruption. Facing mounting pressure, they chose exile, which allowed the state to re‑assert control over the broadcasting sector and diminish independent media influence.

Q6. If Yeltsin had not resigned on 31 December 1999, how would the presidential succession likely have proceeded?

A.Putin would have remained prime minister until the next scheduled election ✅
B.The Duma would have elected a new acting president
C.The vice‑president would have assumed the presidency
D.A special election would have been called within 30 days
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Had Yeltsin stayed in office, constitutional rules would have kept him as president until the end of his term, with Putin remaining as prime minister. The next presidential election would then have been scheduled for the regular timeline, delaying Putin’s opportunity to become acting president and potentially altering the political dynamics of the 2000 race.

Q7. How did the alliance between Putin’s Unity bloc and the Communists influence his ability to implement anti‑corruption reforms?

A.It blocked all reform efforts ✅
B.It provided a supportive majority that facilitated passage of reform legislation
C.It forced Putin to compromise on key anti‑corruption measures
D.It had no impact on legislative outcomes
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The coalition created a parliamentary majority that smoothed the legislative pathway for Putin’s anti‑corruption initiatives. With the Communists’ backing, the government could push through bills targeting oligarchic influence and legal reforms, reducing opposition obstruction and allowing swift implementation of measures that might have otherwise stalled in a fragmented Duma.

Q8. In what way did the September 1999 bombings affect public opinion and Putin’s political standing?

A.The public demanded immediate withdrawal from Chechnya
B.Support for the war decreased sharply, harming Putin’s popularity
C.Outrage over the attacks increased support for decisive action, boosting Putin’s image
D.The bombings had no measurable impact on public sentiment ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The bombings generated widespread outrage and a surge in nationalist sentiment, leading many Russians to favor a strong response against perceived terrorist threats. Putin’s decisive attribution of blame to Chechen rebels and his call for an all‑out military campaign resonated with the public, enhancing his reputation as a firm leader ready to protect the nation.

This restriction limited the ability of opposition groups to secure overseas support, thereby consolidating domestic control over political financing and reinforcing the dominance of parties aligned with the Kremlin.

Q12. If the Unity bloc had only 20 % of Duma seats instead of 24 %, could it still have achieved a governing majority through its alliance with the Communists?

By adding a few smaller groups, the coalition could still assemble enough seats to command a legislative majority, demonstrating the importance of flexible alliance‑building in a fragmented parliament.

Although he garnered a substantial share of the vote, his performance was far behind Putin’s \(53\%\) majority, reflecting the electorate’s preference for the new leadership amid the post‑Chechen crisis environment.

Q14. How did the removal of oligarchs like Gusinsky, Berezovsky, and Khodorkovsky contribute to the Kremlin’s later control over Russian media?

📖 Explanation: The ousting of these high‑profile oligarchs eliminated major private owners of television networks, creating a vacuum that the state could fill with entities sympathetic to the Kremlin. Over time, this shift facilitated the consolidation of media under government‑friendly management, reducing independent reporting and enhancing the Kremlin’s ability to shape public discourse.

Q17. If the September 1999 bombings had been blamed on a non‑Chechen group, how might Putin’s subsequent policy have differed?

D.He would have called for immediate peace talks with Chechen leaders

📖 Explanation: Attributing the attacks to a domestic terrorist network unrelated to Chechnya would likely have redirected Putin’s response toward strengthening internal security agencies and legislative counter‑terrorism measures. The emphasis on a Chechen military offensive might have been reduced, altering both public perception and the trajectory of the conflict.

Q19. Why was Yeltsin’s decision to resign on 31 December 1999 strategically important for Putin’s electoral prospects?

A.It forced an early election that favored the incumbent ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: By stepping down at the end of the year, Yeltsin ensured that Putin would become acting president just before the new election cycle. This timing granted Putin the benefits of incumbency—greater media exposure, control over state resources, and a perception of continuity—that proved decisive in securing a first‑round victory with \(53\%\) of the vote.
