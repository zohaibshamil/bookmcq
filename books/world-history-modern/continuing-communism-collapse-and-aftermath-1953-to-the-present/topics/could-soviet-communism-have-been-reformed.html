📝 Could Soviet communism have been reformed

📖 From World History: Modern • 18. Continuing Communism, collapse and aftermath, 1953 to the Present • 22 questions available

What is Could Soviet communism have been reformed?

Most historians argue Soviet communism could not be successfully reformed because its centralized command economy and one-party rule were inherently incompatible with market mechanisms and pluralism; attempts like Gorbachev’s exposed structural contradictions that made gradual transformation impossible without systemic collapse.

Q1. What political reform did Gorbachev introduce before any major economic changes?

A. Glasnost (openness) ✅
B. Perestroika (restructuring)
C. Collectivization
D. Five‑Year Plan

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Gorbachev's first major political reform was glasnost, which aimed to increase transparency and freedom of expression within the Soviet Union. By allowing criticism of the government and the Communist Party, glasnost set the stage for subsequent reforms, but it was introduced before the more substantive economic restructuring known as perestroika.

Q2. Which country pursued economic reforms before altering the Communist Party’s political control?

A. Soviet Union
B. China ✅
C. Vietnam
D. Cuba

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

China began its reform process by liberalizing the economy in the late 1970s while keeping tight political control over the Party. This sequencing allowed market mechanisms to develop without granting political freedoms, contrasting with the Soviet approach that attempted political openness first, which contributed to instability and eventual collapse.

Q3. In Soviet terminology, what does the term “nomenklatura” refer to?

A. Collective farms
B. Secret police
C. Party‑state elite ✅
D. Youth organization

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The nomenklatura denoted the privileged cadre of Party and state officials who occupied key positions across the bureaucracy. Their control over appointments and resources made them a powerful interest group, often resistant to reforms that threatened their status, and their entrenched position was a major obstacle to systemic change.

Q4. Which Soviet leader attempted political reforms prior to Gorbachev’s tenure?

A. Leonid Brezhnev
B. Yuri Andropov
C. Georgy Malenkov
D. Nikita Khrushchev ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Khrushchev introduced a series of de‑Stalinization measures, such as loosening censorship and releasing political prisoners, in an attempt to revitalize Soviet society. Although his reforms were limited and later reversed, they demonstrated an earlier effort to adjust the Party’s rigidity, providing a historical precedent for later attempts by Gorbachev.

Q5. According to Vladimir Bukovsky, what was Gorbachev’s only instrument of power?

A. The Communist Party ✅
B. The KGB
C. The military
D. The media

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Bukovsky argued that Gorbachev relied solely on the authority of the Communist Party to govern, and that his reforms inadvertently weakened that very instrument. By allowing criticism and reducing party discipline, Gorbachev effectively cut the branch he was sitting on, making the Party incapable of enforcing his policies.

This contrast with Gorbachev’s reluctance to use coercion highlights differing strategies for maintaining control during periods of political unrest.

Q18. Assume party legitimacy L depends on economic growth G and political openness O via L = \(0.6G + 0.4O\). If G must be at least \(6\%\) and O at least \(3\%\) to avoid collapse, what minimum combined score is required?

A. 4
B. 4.4
C. 4.8 ✅
D. 5.2

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Plugging the minimum values into the formula gives L = \(0.6\times6 + 0.4\times3 = 3.6 + 1.2 = 4.8\). Therefore the party would need a legitimacy score of at least 4.8 (on the scale used) to maintain stability. Scores below this threshold suggest insufficient public confidence and heightened risk of systemic breakdown.

Q19. In a simple Prisoner’s Dilemma model of reform, what strategy should Gorbachev adopt to maximize party survival if the elite can cooperate or defect?

By rewarding cooperation and punishing betrayal, this strategy can align elite incentives with reform goals, enhancing the likelihood that both the Party and its bureaucratic base work together toward stability.
