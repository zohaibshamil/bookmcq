What is Khrushchev's fall in October 1964?

Khrushchev was removed from power in a bloodless coup led by Brezhnev and other Politburo members who criticized his impulsive decision-making, failed agricultural policies, and diplomatic blunders, marking the first peaceful transfer of Soviet leadership and signaling a return to more collective and cautious governance.

📝 All Khrushchev's fall in October 1964 MCQs

Q1. Who became chairman of the Council of Ministers after Stalin's death?

A. Georgy Malenkov ✅
B. Nikita Khrushchev
C. Lavrentiy Beria
D. Vyacheslav Molotov

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Georgy Malenkov was appointed chairman of the Council of Ministers in the immediate power vacuum following Stalin’s death in 1953. His appointment reflected the collective‑leadership approach adopted by the Politburo, which sought to prevent any single individual from dominating the Soviet state as Stalin had. Malenkov’s role, however, was short‑lived as internal disputes soon emerged.

Q2. What position did Khrushchev hold in the Soviet leadership immediately after Stalin’s death?

A. Party Secretary ✅
B. Chairman of the Praesidium
C. Chief of Secret Police
D. Minister of Defense

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In the post‑Stalin arrangement, Khrushchev was named First Secretary of the Communist Party, effectively making him the Party Secretary. This role gave him control over party apparatus and allowed him to influence policy and personnel decisions, laying the groundwork for his eventual rise to pre‑eminence within the Soviet hierarchy.

Q3. Why was Lavrentiy Beria executed in 1953?

A. He plotted a coup against the Politburo
B. He was blamed for Stalin’s purges ✅
C. He was accused of economic sabotage
D. He voluntarily resigned

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Beria’s execution stemmed primarily from fear among his fellow leaders that his control of the NKVD and his reputation for brutality could enable him to challenge the new collective leadership. By eliminating him, the remaining Politburo members neutralized a potential rival and removed a symbol of Stalinist terror, paving the way for Khrushchev’s consolidation of power.

Q4. Which action best illustrates Khrushchev’s attempt to distance himself from Stalin’s personality cult?

A. Delivering the secret “Silent” speech at the 20th Party Congress ✅
B. Launching the first Soviet satellite
C. Signing a trade agreement with the United States
D. Increasing censorship of Western literature

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Khrushchev’s secret denunciation of Stalin at the 20th Party Congress, famously known as the “Secret Speech,” directly challenged the cult of personality by exposing Stalin’s crimes and criticizing his policies. This bold move signaled a break with the past and aimed to re‑orient Soviet ideology toward collective leadership and a more open Party discourse.

Q5. Evaluate the impact of Khrushchev’s 1956 speech on internal Party dynamics. Which statement is most accurate?

A. It immediately eliminated all Stalinist hardliners
B. It created a split between reformers and conservatives, weakening collective unity
C. It reinforced the absolute authority of the Party over the state ✅
D. It had no measurable effect on Party cohesion

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The speech, while shocking, ultimately reinforced the Party’s dominance by reaffirming its role as the guiding force of Soviet society. Though it exposed Stalinist excesses, the Party retained control over political narratives, and the denunciation was framed as an internal correction rather than a challenge to Party supremacy, preserving overall cohesion.

Q6. In which year did the Twentieth Communist Party Congress, where Khrushchev delivered his secret speech, take place?

A. 1955
B. 1956 ✅
C. 1957
D. 1958

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Twentieth Party Congress convened in February 1956. This gathering provided the platform for Khrushchev’s clandestine critique of Stalin, marking a pivotal moment in Soviet history. The timing was crucial, as it occurred during a period of relative thaw, allowing the leadership to cautiously introduce reforms without provoking immediate backlash from entrenched hardliners.

Q7. Suppose a Politburo member opposed Khrushchev’s de‑Stalinization policies in the late 1950s. Based on precedent, what would most likely happen to that member?

A. They would be promoted to a higher position
B. They would be reassigned to a lesser role
C. They would be sent abroad as an ambassador
D. They would face marginalization or removal from key committees ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Historical patterns show that dissenters who openly resisted Khrushchev’s reforms were often sidelined, losing influence within the Party apparatus. While outright purges became less common after Stalin, political survival increasingly depended on aligning with the prevailing line, and opposition typically resulted in loss of prestigious posts or exclusion from decision‑making bodies.

Q8. Which Soviet leader resigned in 1955 due to disagreement with Khrushchev over industrial policies?

A. Georgy Malenkov ✅
B. Nikita Khrushchev
C. Lavrentiy Beria
D. Nikolai Bulganin

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Georgy Malenkov stepped down as Chairman of the Council of Ministers in 1955 after clashing with Khrushchev over the direction of industrial development. His resignation illustrated the growing tension between those favoring heavy industry and those advocating a shift toward consumer goods and agricultural investment, a debate that would shape Soviet economic strategy in the following decade.

Q9. How did Khrushchev’s personal background differ from many earlier Soviet leaders?

A. He was born into an aristocratic family
B. He had extensive experience as a diplomat
C. He came from a peasant farming background and worked as a miner ✅
D. He was educated abroad in the United States

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Khrushchev’s rise from a modest peasant family, his early work as a farm labourer and coal‑mine mechanic, and his later technical education distinguished him from many contemporaries who often hailed from revolutionary or intellectual elites. This humble origin contributed to his image as a man of the people and informed his emphasis on agricultural and consumer‑focused policies.

Q10. Which statement best reflects Khrushchev’s policy regarding nuclear war compared with Stalin’s approach?

A. He advocated immediate pre‑emptive strikes
B. He emphasized peaceful coexistence to avoid nuclear catastrophe ✅
C. He increased secret nuclear stockpiles without public acknowledgment
D. He withdrew from the nuclear arms race entirely

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Khrushchev introduced the concept of peaceful coexistence, arguing that the superpowers could avoid direct conflict and the devastating consequences of nuclear war. This was a departure from Stalin’s more confrontational stance, which emphasized military buildup and readiness for possible confrontation, reflecting a strategic shift toward diplomatic stability alongside continued arms development.

Q11. If a historian claims Khrushchev’s secret speech was primarily a power‑consolidation move, which piece of evidence best supports this view?

A. The speech was delivered in a public forum
B. Khrushchev cited personal anecdotes about Stalin’s childhood
C. He used the speech to undermine rival leaders associated with Stalinist policies
D. The speech received immediate approval from all Party members ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

By framing the denunciation as a critique of Stalin’s excesses, Khrushchev weakened the standing of rivals who had closely aligned themselves with the former leader’s legacy. This strategic targeting allowed Khrushchev to elevate his own position, marginalize opponents, and present himself as the reformer, thereby consolidating personal authority within the Party hierarchy.

Q12. What was the name given to the Soviet governing body that was referred to as the Praesidium?

A. Politburo ✅
B. Supreme Soviet
C. Council of Ministers
D. Central Committee

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The term Praesidium was used to designate the Politburo, the executive committee of the Communist Party that wielded the highest decision‑making power in the Soviet Union. This body coordinated policy across the Party and state apparatus, and its members, including Khrushchev, played pivotal roles in shaping the direction of Soviet governance during the post‑Stalin era.

Q13. What primary criticism did Khrushchev make about Stalin’s conduct of World War II?

A. He praised Stalin’s strategic brilliance
B. He accused Stalin of delaying the opening of a second front
C. He claimed Stalin mishandled civilian supply lines ✅
D. He alleged Stalin ignored the contributions of allied nations

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Khrushchev highlighted the mismanagement of civilian supply chains, arguing that Stalin’s policies led to unnecessary hardships for the Soviet populace during the war. By drawing attention to these logistical failures, Khrushchev sought to expose the broader inefficiencies and authoritarian decisions that characterized Stalin’s wartime leadership.

Q14. Why did Khrushchev’s denunciation of Stalin pose a personal risk to him?

A. He lacked support from any Politburo members
B. He was directly implicated in Stalin’s purges ✅
C. He challenged a deeply entrenched cult that many still revered
D. He had no prior experience in public speaking

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The speech threatened the legitimacy of a system that had protected many senior officials, including Khrushchev himself, from prosecution. By exposing Stalin’s crimes, Khrushchev risked alienating colleagues who benefited from the previous regime’s patronage and could have been targeted for retribution, making his bold critique a perilous gamble for his political survival.

Q15. Who served as chief of the Soviet secret police before being executed in 1953?

A. Lavrentiy Beria ✅
B. Georgy Malenkov
C. Vyacheslav Molotov
D. Kliment Voroshilov

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Lavrentiy Beria headed the NKVD (later MGB), the Soviet secret police, playing a central role in Stalin’s terror campaigns. After Stalin’s death, his colleagues perceived him as a potential threat due to his control over the security apparatus, leading to his arrest and execution in December 1953, which removed a powerful figure from Soviet politics.

Q16. Which concept did Khrushchev introduce to describe the relationship between the USSR and the West after his 1956 speech?

A. Peaceful coexistence ✅
B. Aggressive expansionism
C. Isolationist policy
D. Detente

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Khrushchev promoted the idea of peaceful coexistence, asserting that the Soviet Union and capitalist nations could compete ideologically without resorting to direct military conflict. This doctrine aimed to reduce the risk of nuclear war and to position the USSR as a responsible superpower willing to engage diplomatically while still pursuing its socialist agenda.

Q17. If Khrushchev had not denounced Stalin, how might his eventual removal from power have differed?

A. He would have been exiled to a foreign country
B. He would have likely faced a smoother transition with less internal opposition
C. He would have been forced to resign due to popular protests ✅
D. He would have been executed by his rivals

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Without the break from Stalinist orthodoxy, Khrushchev would have remained more closely tied to entrenched hardliners. Growing public dissatisfaction with economic stagnation and policy failures could have sparked mass protests, creating pressure that might have compelled him to resign or be removed in a more tumultuous, possibly violent, manner.

Q18. What was the outcome for Malenkov after his resignation regarding his personal freedom?

A. He was executed
B. He was imprisoned for life
C. He was exiled to Siberia
D. He was neither executed nor imprisoned ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Malenkov’s resignation did not lead to the severe reprisals that characterized Stalin’s purges; he avoided execution or long‑term imprisonment. This relatively lenient treatment reflected the new leadership’s desire to avoid repeating past excesses, indicating a shift toward a more restrained, albeit still politically risky, environment for dissenting officials.

Q19. Which piece of evidence contradicts the claim that Khrushchev’s speech marked a shift from a personality cult to true collective leadership?

A. The continued dominance of the Party apparatus
B. The removal of all Stalinist officials ✅
C. The introduction of multi‑party elections
D. The abolition of the Politburo

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Although Khrushchev publicly condemned Stalin’s cult, the Party apparatus remained the central authority, and many former Stalinist officials retained influence. The continued existence of the Politburo and the lack of systemic changes toward genuine power sharing demonstrate that the shift was more rhetorical than structural, limiting the depth of collective leadership reforms.

Q20. In what year did Joseph Stalin die, creating the power vacuum that led to Khrushchev’s rise?

A. 1952
B. 1953 ✅
C. 1954
D. 1955

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Joseph Stalin died on March 5, 1953, ending a three‑decade reign marked by intense centralization, purges, and a pervasive personality cult. His death precipitated a scramble among senior Party members to fill the leadership void, eventually allowing Khrushchev to emerge as a dominant figure within the newly restructured Soviet hierarchy.

Q21. Which of the following individuals was NOT part of the initial post‑Stalin leadership group?

A. Kliment Voroshilov
B. Nikolai Bulganin
C. Mikhail Gorbachev ✅
D. Vyacheslav Molotov

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Mikhail Gorbachev, who would later become the Soviet leader in the 1980s, was not involved in the immediate post‑Stalin power structure of the 1950s. The early collective leadership comprised figures such as Voroshilov, Bulganin, and Molotov, all of whom played significant roles in shaping Soviet policy during the transitional period after Stalin’s death.
