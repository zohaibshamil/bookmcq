📝 All August 1991 coup against Gorbachev MCQs

Q1. When was the union treaty that Gorbachev planned to sign originally scheduled for?

🔹 A. \(19\) August 1991
🔹 B. \(20\) August 1991
🔹 C. \(21\) August 1991
🔹 D. \(22\) August 1991

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty was set for \(20\) August 1991, the day after the coup began. The hard‑liners halted the process, preventing the formal signing of the new voluntary union, which was intended to give the republics greater autonomy while preserving a weakened Soviet structure.

Q2. Which senior official led the coup attempt alongside Gennady Yanayev?

🔹 A. Boris Yeltsin
🔹 B. Alexander Yakovlev
🔹 C. Dmitry Yazov
🔹 D. Valentin Pavlov

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Valentin Pavlov, the former Soviet Prime Minister, was a key conspirator. He worked with Vice‑President Gennady Yanayev to orchestrate the August coup, aiming to reverse Gorbachev’s reforms. Pavlov’s involvement highlighted the depth of hard‑liner resistance within the Soviet leadership.

Q3. What immediate action did Yeltsin take when the coup leaders tried to seize the White House?

🔹 A. He fled the country
🔹 B. He called for a general strike
🔹 C. He stood on a tank and appealed to the public
🔹 D. He negotiated with the coup leaders

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Yeltsin famously climbed onto a tank outside the White House, delivering a televised appeal urging Muscovites to resist the coup. His bold, visible defiance rallied citizens, turning the White House into a symbol of opposition and helping to undermine the coup’s legitimacy.

Q4. Which of the following best describes the state of the Soviet army during the coup?

🔹 A. Uniformly loyal to the coup plotters
🔹 B. Divided, with many units refusing orders
🔹 C. All units remained neutral
🔹 D. Entirely supportive of Yeltsin

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The army was split; some units followed the coup’s directives, but many soldiers were uncertain or sympathetic to reformers. This lack of unified support prevented the coup leaders from using force effectively, contributing to the collapse of their attempt.

Q5. If the coup had succeeded, which policy would most likely have been reinstated?

🔹 A. Perestroika reforms
🔹 B. Glasnost openness
🔹 C. Rapid market liberalization
🔹 D. Centralized command economy

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A successful coup would have restored the traditional Soviet command economy, reversing Gorbachev’s market‑orienting reforms. The hard‑liners aimed to halt political liberalization and re‑impose strict central control, which would have meant a return to a planned economy and tighter party dominance.

Q6. Why did the public believe Gorbachev was ill during the coup?

🔹 A. Official medical reports released
🔹 B. State propaganda claimed he was hospitalized
🔹 C. Yeltsin announced his illness
🔹 D. No one communicated his condition

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The coup plotters spread misinformation, stating that Gorbachev was ill and unable to lead. This narrative was part of their effort to legitimize the emergency committee, but the public quickly saw through the deception as Yeltsin openly challenged the claim.

Q7. Which republic declared independence in April 1991, signaling the USSR’s fragmentation?

🔹 A. Ukraine
🔹 B. Georgia
🔹 C. Kazakhstan
🔹 D. Belarus

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Georgia proclaimed its independence in April 1991, becoming one of the first Soviet republics to break away. This move underscored the accelerating disintegration of the Union and heightened fears among Moscow’s leadership that the central state was rapidly losing its constituent parts.

Q8. How did the Soviet leadership initially try to legitimize the coup after Gorbachev’s arrest?

🔹 A. By holding a public referendum
🔹 B. By declaring a state of emergency
🔹 C. By convening a new parliament
🔹 D. By inviting foreign observers

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The conspirators announced a state of emergency, banning demonstrations and deploying troops around key sites. This legal veneer was intended to give the takeover an appearance of constitutional authority, even though it lacked genuine legitimacy among both citizens and many military units.

Q9. Imagine you are a Soviet journalist in August 1991. Which action would most likely jeopardize your career?

🔹 A. Reporting Yeltsin’s statements accurately
🔹 B. Publishing the official coup proclamation
🔹 C. Criticizing the hard‑liners publicly
🔹 D. Covering the protests neutrally

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Open criticism of the coup plotters would have been deemed anti‑state propaganda, risking censorship, loss of accreditation, or even imprisonment. Journalists faced severe pressure to align with the official narrative, making dissenting commentary a dangerous professional choice.

Q10. What was the main reason the coup leaders failed to capture Yeltsin?

🔹 B. He had a personal guard of elite troops
🔹 C. They underestimated public support for him
🔹 D. Yeltsin was protected by foreign diplomats

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The coup architects miscalculated Yeltsin’s popularity and the willingness of ordinary citizens to defend him. Massive crowds gathered around the White House, and the army hesitated to act against a widely admired figure, preventing the plotters from detaining him.

After the coup’s failure, the Communist Party’s reputation was severely damaged, leading Russian authorities to ban it in the Russian Federation. This action reflected the broader loss of confidence in the party and paved the way for a multiparty political landscape.

📖 Explanation:

The “White House” is the informal name for the Russian parliament building in Moscow. During the August coup, it became the focal point of resistance, as Yeltsin and his supporters defended it against the hard‑liners’ attempts to seize control.

Q13. Which of the following best characterizes the reaction of the Soviet troops surrounding the White House?

The troops were uncertain about whose orders to follow, leading to a hesitation that prevented them from using force against the demonstrators. This indecision was crucial, as it allowed the civilian resistance to grow without a violent crackdown.

Q14. As a historian, you are asked to assess the coup’s impact on the Soviet Union’s eventual dissolution. Which argument is most defensible?

🔹 C. The coup strengthened Gorbachev’s reforms
🔹 D. The coup unified the republics against external threats

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The failed coup delegitimized the Communist Party, exposed the fragility of central authority, and emboldened separatist movements. By demonstrating the inability of hard‑liners to control the state, it hastened the Union’s collapse and paved the way for the independence of the remaining republics.

Q15. What was the official composition of the emergency committee that claimed to govern after the coup began?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The coup plotters announced an eight‑member committee to replace Gorbachev’s authority, presenting it as a provisional governing body. This committee was intended to project an image of continuity, but its legitimacy was quickly challenged by mass protests and the lack of broad support.

Q16. Which of the following republics did NOT declare independence before the August coup?

Moldova remained within the Soviet framework until after the August events. In contrast, Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia had already taken steps toward independence, highlighting the varied pace of secession among the Soviet republics during this turbulent period.

Q17. If you were advising the Soviet leadership before the coup, which strategic mistake would you highlight as most critical?

🔹 D. Ignoring the growing nationalist movements

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Controlling the narrative was essential; the coup’s reliance on misinformation about Gorbachev’s health and the suppression of independent media allowed Yeltsin’s defiant broadcasts to dominate public perception. This media failure eroded the coup’s credibility and galvanized opposition.

Q18. What was the immediate fate of Gennady Yanayev after the coup collapsed?

After the coup failed, Yanayev was detained along with other conspirators. Though he faced charges, he eventually received a relatively light sentence and was pardoned, reflecting the chaotic legal environment and the desire to move past the crisis without prolonged retribution.
