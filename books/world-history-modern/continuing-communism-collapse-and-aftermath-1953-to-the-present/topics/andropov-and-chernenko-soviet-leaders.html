📝 Andropov and Chernenko Soviet leaders

📖 From World History: Modern • 18. Continuing Communism, collapse and aftermath, 1953 to the Present • 22 questions available

What is Andropov and Chernenko Soviet leaders?

Yuri Andropov (1982–1984) briefly attempted anti-corruption campaigns and modest reforms before his death, while Konstantin Chernenko (1984–1985) represented a return to conservative Brezhnev-era policies; both short tenures highlighted the aging leadership crisis and systemic inertia that preceded Gorbachev’s rise.

📝 All Andropov and Chernenko Soviet leaders MCQs

Q1. How long did Yuri Andropov serve as General Secretary?

A. 15 months ✅
B. 24 months
C. 36 months
D. 48 months

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Andropov assumed leadership in November 1982 and died in February 1984, giving him roughly fifteen months in office, a brief tenure that limited the impact of his reform agenda.

Q2. What was Konstantin Chernenko's age at the time of his death?

A. 70
B. 71
C. 72 ✅
D. 73

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Chernenko was born in September 1911 and died in March 1985, making him seventy‑two years old when he passed away, a fact that underscored the frailty of Soviet leadership in its final years.

Q3. Before becoming General Secretary, which position did Yuri Andropov hold?

A. Minister of Defense
B. Head of the KGB ✅
C. Premier
D. Foreign Minister

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Andropov served as the chief of the Soviet security apparatus, the KGB, until May 1982, which gave him insight into internal corruption and positioned him to launch his anti‑corruption drive once he became leader.

Q4. Which Soviet dissident remained in exile throughout Chernenko's rule?

A. Andrei Sakharov ✅
B. Alexander Solzhenitsyn
C. Natan Sharansky
D. Vladimir Bukovsky

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Sakharov, a prominent physicist and human‑rights advocate, had been confined to a Siberian exile since 1980 and continued to be detained during Chernenko's brief tenure, illustrating the regime's intolerance of dissent.

Q5. In what year did Leonid Brezhnev die, prompting the succession of Andropov?

A. 1980
B. 1981
C. 1982 ✅
D. 1983

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Brezhnev's death on November 10, 1982 created a power vacuum that was quickly filled by Yuri Andropov, marking the start of a short period of rapid leadership turnover in the Soviet Union.

Q6. Which governing body selected Konstantin Chernenko as General Secretary?

A. Supreme Soviet
B. Politburo ✅
C. Central Committee
D. Kremlin Council

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Politburo, the executive committee of the Communist Party, convened after Andropov's death and chose Chernenko, a long‑time Brezhnev aide, reflecting the party’s preference for continuity over reform.

Q7. Which of the following was NOT a characteristic of Chernenko's rule?

A. Anti‑corruption drive ✅
B. Conservatism
C. Health decline
D. Short tenure

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Chernenko is remembered for maintaining the status quo, with no major anti‑corruption initiatives; his administration was defined by traditional conservatism, his own deteriorating health, and a brief period in power.

Q8. If Andropov's decentralization reforms had been fully implemented, which sector would likely see the greatest productivity increase?

A. Heavy industry
B. Agriculture
C. Consumer goods
D. Energy ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Decentralizing decision‑making would give individual enterprises more autonomy, particularly in producing consumer goods, where responsiveness to local demand could boost efficiency and output more than in centrally planned heavy‑industry sectors.

Q9. Compare the anti‑corruption approaches of Andropov and Chernenko. Which statement is accurate?

A. Both intensified
B. Andropov intensified, Chernenko relaxed ✅
C. Chernenko intensified, Andropov relaxed
D. Both relaxed

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Andropov launched a vigorous anti‑corruption campaign soon after taking power, whereas Chernenko largely abandoned these efforts, allowing entrenched corrupt practices to continue unchecked.

Q10. Suppose a younger reformist party official replaced a senior official during Andropov's tenure. What impact would that likely have on policy implementation?

A. Slower ✅
B. Faster
C. No change
D. Increased corruption

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Introducing younger personnel could initially slow implementation as they acclimate to the bureaucracy, but the overall effect might be limited by the entrenched nature of Soviet institutions, which often resist rapid change.

Q11. Which of the following was NOT a characteristic of Chernenko's rule?

A. Reformist zeal
B. Stagnation
C. Radical liberalization
D. Militaristic expansion ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Chernenko's period was marked by stagnation and a lack of new initiatives; there was no notable militaristic expansion, making that option the one that does not describe his leadership.

Q12. Volkogonov's reference to 'convulsions to come' is most often linked to which later Soviet development?

A. Perestroika and Glasnost
B. Soviet‑Afghan War ✅
C. Chernobyl disaster
D. Fall of the Berlin Wall

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Many scholars interpret the phrase as anticipating the turmoil of the Soviet‑Afghan War, a conflict that began in 1979 and intensified under subsequent leaders, contributing to the systemic crisis of the 1980s.

Q13. Which statement best summarizes the significance of Andropov's anti‑corruption campaign?

A. It eliminated corruption entirely
B. It was a minor administrative note
C. It focused solely on economic output
D. It exposed systemic corruption but was limited by brief tenure ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Andropov's campaign highlighted deep‑seated corruption within the Party, yet his early death after roughly fifteen months prevented any lasting institutional transformation, illustrating the challenges of reforming a rigid bureaucracy.

Q14. If Chernenko had survived an additional two years, which policy would likely have continued?

A. Anti‑corruption drive
B. Economic decentralization
C. Human‑rights leniency
D. Status‑quo repression ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Given Chernenko's conservative stance and lack of reformist momentum, it is reasonable to assume that the prevailing repressive policies would have persisted, with little change to the human‑rights environment.

Q15. Which organization arrested members of unofficial trade unions during Chernenko's rule?

A. KGB ✅
B. GRU
C. NKVD
D. MVD

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The KGB, still the chief security agency of the USSR, continued its surveillance and repression of independent labor activities, targeting unofficial trade‑union members as part of the broader crackdown on dissent under Chernenko.

Q16. Why did the Politburo favor a terminally ill leader like Chernenko over a reformist candidate?

A. Fear of losing control
B. Desire for rapid change
C. International pressure ✅
D. Economic necessity

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Choosing an ailing and politically compliant figure such as Chernenko allowed the entrenched leadership to maintain dominance, while avoiding the uncertainties and potential challenges that a reform‑oriented successor might introduce.

Q18. Which statement best describes the overall impact of Andropov's anti‑corruption drive?

A. It eliminated corruption completely, ending bureaucratic inertia
B. It highlighted systemic corruption but was limited by Andropov's short tenure, showing the difficulty of altering entrenched structures ✅
C. It focused solely on economic output, ignoring corruption
D. It increased corruption by exposing officials

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The campaign exposed widespread graft and signaled a willingness to confront entrenched interests, yet Andropov’s premature death curtailed any lasting reforms, underscoring how deeply rooted the Soviet bureaucratic inertia was.

Q19. Imagine a counterfactual where Andropov's reforms were continued by Chernenko. Which outcome is most plausible?

A. Rapid economic growth by 1988
B. Stagnation persists
C. Limited liberalization with modest openness to the West ✅
D. Immediate collapse

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Continuing Andropov’s modest reforms under Chernenko would likely have produced only incremental liberalization, offering limited openness to Western engagement without triggering the rapid economic transformation or catastrophic breakdown that more radical changes might have caused.

Q20. In terms of succession, what does the pattern of selecting aged, ill leaders suggest about internal dynamics?

A. Power vacuum and desire for caretaker leaders ✅
B. Commitment to democratic elections
C. Strong youth movement
D. External pressure

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Choosing frail, elderly figures like Andropov and Chernenko reflects a strategic preference for short‑term, caretaker leadership that allowed existing power structures to preserve their influence while avoiding the uncertainties of a more dynamic successor.

Q21. Evaluate the impact of continued repression of human‑rights activists under Chernenko on the Soviet Union's international image.

A. Improved image
B. No effect
C. Damaged credibility, increasing Western criticism ✅
D. Enhanced internal stability

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Maintaining harsh treatment of dissidents such as Sakharov reinforced negative perceptions abroad, prompting heightened criticism from Western governments and human‑rights organizations, and further isolating the USSR on the global stage during an era of growing diplomatic tension.

Q22. If the KGB had not been led by Andropov before his General Secretary role, how might the anti‑corruption drive have differed?

A. More aggressive
B. Less coordinated ✅
C. Focused on foreign espionage
D. No anti‑corruption drive

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Andropov’s intimate knowledge of the security services informed his targeted anti‑corruption efforts; without that background, any campaign would likely have lacked the precise coordination needed to confront entrenched Party networks, reducing its effectiveness.
