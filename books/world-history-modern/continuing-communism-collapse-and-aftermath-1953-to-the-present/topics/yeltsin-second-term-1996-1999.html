What is Yeltsin second term 1996-1999?

Yeltsin’s second term was marked by economic collapse, the 1998 financial crisis, renewed war in Chechnya, and declining health, leading to political instability and frequent prime minister changes; his erratic governance eroded public trust and paved the way for a successor promising order and stability.

📝 All Yeltsin second term 1996-1999 MCQs

Q1. What was the primary goal of Yeltsin’s ‘shock therapy’ initiated in 1992?

A.To increase state subsidies
B.To privatize the economy rapidly ✅
C.To reintroduce price controls
D.To expand agricultural collectives
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The shock‑therapy program was designed to quickly dismantle the command system by lifting price controls and transferring state‑owned enterprises to private hands. The belief was that a rapid market transition would restore efficiency, even though it caused short‑term hardship for many citizens.

Q2. Which vice‑president did Yeltsin appoint to spearhead economic reforms?

A.Viktor Chernomyrdin
B.Mikhail Gorbachev
C.Yegor Gaidar
D.Sergei Kiriyenko ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Yegor Gaidar, a young economist influenced by Western monetarist ideas, was chosen as Yeltsin’s vice‑president. Gaidar advocated for rapid price liberalization and extensive privatization, believing that these measures would lay the foundation for a market economy despite the social turbulence they would provoke.

Q3. By the end of 1992, prices were on average how many times higher than at the start of the year?

A.\(10\) times higher
B.\(20\) times higher
C.\(30\) times higher ✅
D.\(40\) times higher
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: After price controls were lifted, inflation surged dramatically. By the close of 1992, average prices had risen to roughly thirty times their initial level, reflecting the abrupt removal of subsidies and the market’s adjustment to previously artificial price ceilings.

Q4. What immediate social consequence followed the rapid rise in prices after price liberalization?

A.Increase in foreign investment
B.Sharp decline in unemployment
C.Widespread homelessness ✅
D.Rise in agricultural output
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The sudden inflation eroded real wages, leading many workers to lose their jobs as factories cut staff. Consequently, a significant portion of the population became homeless, resorting to tents and relying on humanitarian aid, highlighting the human cost of the economic shock.

Q5. Which sector did NOT receive government subsidies after the shock‑therapy reforms began?

A.Heavy industry
B.Consumer goods ✅
C.Agriculture
D.Public transportation
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The shock‑therapy reforms eliminated subsidies across most sectors, but consumer goods—especially those previously price‑controlled—saw the most immediate removal of state support, causing rapid price spikes and reducing affordability for ordinary citizens.

Q6. How long did Gaidar claim the most painful phase of reforms would last?

A.Six months ✅
B.One year
C.Two years
D.Five years
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Gaidar warned that the hardest adjustments would last about six months, after which the economy would begin to stabilize and the population would start to experience the benefits of a market‑driven system, a timeline that proved overly optimistic for many Russians.

Q7. Which of the following best describes the overall public sentiment toward Yeltsin during his early reform years?

A.Unanimous support
B.Widespread opposition
C.Hugely popular but conditional ✅
D.Indifference
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Yeltsin enjoyed considerable popularity at the outset because many hoped his reforms would improve living standards. However, this support was conditional on tangible improvements; as inflation and unemployment rose, public enthusiasm waned, illustrating the fragile nature of his political capital.

Q8. A factory worker lost his job after the price liberalization. Which macro‑economic policy could have lessened his hardship without abandoning market reforms?

A.Maintaining full price controls
B.Introducing targeted unemployment benefits ✅
C.Re‑nationalizing the factory
D.Doubling subsidies to all industries
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Targeted unemployment benefits would provide a safety net for displaced workers while preserving the core market reforms. By cushioning income loss, the policy could mitigate social fallout, maintain public support for reforms, and avoid the inefficiencies associated with reinstating broad price controls or full nationalization.

Q9. How did the removal of price controls affect supply‑side incentives for producers in the short run?

A.Incentivized increased production
B.Reduced incentives due to price volatility ✅
C.Had no effect on production decisions
D.Encouraged state‑owned enterprises to expand
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When price controls were lifted, producers faced volatile market prices without guaranteed sales, leading many to cut output or halt production. This uncertainty reduced short‑run incentives, as firms could not reliably forecast revenues, contributing to layoffs and the broader economic contraction observed in the early reform period.

Q10. Which of the following describes the sequence of reforms Gaidar advocated?

A.Privatization → price liberalization → subsidy removal
B.Price liberalization → subsidy removal → privatization ✅
C.Subsidy removal → price liberalization → privatization
D.Privatization → subsidy removal → price liberalization
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Gaidar’s plan began with lifting price controls, followed by ending government subsidies, and culminated in a sweeping privatization of state assets. This sequence was intended to quickly create market signals, reduce fiscal burdens, and then transfer ownership to private actors to foster efficiency.

Q11. If prices increased \(30\) times and wages only rose \(5\) times, what is the approximate change in real wages?

A.Real wages increased
B.Real wages unchanged
C.Real wages fell dramatically ✅
D.Real wages rose slightly
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Real wages are calculated as nominal wages divided by price level. With wages growing fivefold while prices grow thirtyfold, the ratio falls, indicating a sharp decline in purchasing power. Hence, real wages deteriorated substantially, reflecting the hardship many Russians faced during the shock‑therapy period.

Q12. Which economic theory most directly influenced Gaidar’s reforms?

A.Keynesian fiscal stimulus
B.Classical trade theory
C.Western monetarism ✅
D.Marxist‑Leninist planning
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Gaidar was heavily influenced by Western monetarist ideas, which emphasize controlling money supply, reducing state intervention, and allowing market forces to determine prices. This ideological foundation shaped his rapid price liberalization and privatization agenda, distinguishing his approach from Keynesian demand‑management or traditional Soviet planning models.

Q13. By early 1993, a significant portion of the population relied on food parcels from abroad. Which policy failure does this most directly illustrate?

A.Failure to attract foreign investment
B.Failure to stabilize the ruble
C.Failure to ensure food security during price liberalization ✅
D.Failure to implement tax reforms
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The reliance on external food aid signals that domestic food distribution systems could not cope with the sudden price spikes and supply disruptions caused by liberalizing prices. This highlights a gap in the reform plan regarding food security and the need for transitional measures to protect vulnerable households.

Q14. What proportion of goods had price controls removed during the initial shock‑therapy phase?

A.0.5
B.0.7
C.0.9 ✅
D.1
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The reform agenda targeted the majority of the market, removing price controls from about ninety percent of goods. This extensive deregulation aimed to unleash market mechanisms but also precipitated rapid inflation, as many consumers were unprepared for the abrupt shift in pricing structures.

Q15. Critical thinking: Which of the following would most likely have reduced the inflationary spike while still moving toward market reforms?

A.Gradual price liberalization over two years ✅
B.Immediate full deregulation of all prices
C.Maintaining subsidies for all industries
D.Re‑nationalizing the banking sector
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A phased approach to price liberalization would allow producers and consumers time to adjust, smoothing the transition and mitigating inflationary pressure. While slower, this method preserves reform momentum without the shock that abrupt deregulation caused, balancing stability with the goal of a market economy.

Q16. How did the privatization program intend to transfer ownership of large state enterprises?

A.Through direct government sales to foreign investors
B.Via joint ownership among workers, managers, and the state ✅
C.By handing assets to regional governments
D.Through national lotteries
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The privatization strategy aimed to create joint ownership structures, often referred to as “voucher” or “employee‑share” schemes, where workers, managers, and the state would hold stakes. This approach was meant to democratize ownership, incentivize productivity, and prevent the concentration of assets in a few hands.

Q17. Evaluate the long‑term political risk of implementing shock therapy without accompanying social safety nets. Which outcome is most plausible?

A.Strengthening democratic institutions due to economic growth
B.Erosion of public trust leading to political instability ✅
C.Rapid convergence with Western economies
D.Increased foreign direct investment despite domestic unrest
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When rapid market reforms are pursued without adequate safety nets, the immediate social pain can erode citizens’ confidence in the government. Over time, this loss of trust may fuel opposition movements, undermine democratic consolidation, and create a fertile ground for authoritarian resurgence or political volatility.

Q18. Using the data that prices rose \(30\) times while wages rose \(5\) times, calculate the percentage change in real wages. (Assume initial real wage index = 1)

A.\( -83\% \) ✅
B.\( -75\% \)
C.\( -66\% \)
D.\( -50\% \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Real wage index after reforms = (5 / 30) = 0.1667. The change = (0.1667 − 1) × 100 ≈ ‑83 %. This large negative shift illustrates the drastic decline in purchasing power experienced by workers during the shock‑therapy period.

Q19. Which of the following best captures the paradox of “price liberalization” leading to both increased consumer choice and reduced affordability?

A.More goods are produced, but wages fall faster than prices
B.Supply chains improve, yet subsidies are cut
C.Consumers can choose from imported goods, but domestic wages stagnate ✅
D.Price controls are removed, causing hyperinflation while markets open
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Liberalizing prices allows a variety of goods—especially imports—to appear on shelves, expanding choice. However, because wages do not keep pace with soaring prices, many households cannot afford these new options, creating a paradox where abundance coexists with reduced real consumption.

Q20. If the government had maintained subsidies for only the poorest 20 % of the population, how might that have affected the overall inflation rate?

A.Inflation would have remained unchanged
B.Inflation would have increased slightly
C.Inflation would have decreased modestly ✅
D.Inflation would have collapsed
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Targeted subsidies can dampen price pressures for a vulnerable segment while leaving market mechanisms largely intact. By cushioning the poorest, demand would be steadier, potentially moderating price escalations and resulting in a modest reduction in the overall inflation rate compared with a blanket subsidy removal.

Q21. Considering the rapid privatization and resulting concentration of assets, which long‑term economic issue is most likely to emerge?

A.Higher competition among firms
B.Increased income inequality ✅
C.Reduction in foreign debt
D.Improved fiscal balance
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When large state assets are privatized quickly, they often end up in the hands of a few well‑connected individuals, leading to significant wealth concentration. This disparity fuels income inequality, hampers broad‑based economic development, and can provoke social discontent, undermining the reform’s intended benefits.
