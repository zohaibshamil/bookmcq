About 1. Outbreak of the First World War: The world in 1914

In 1914, the world was dominated by powerful European empires that were deeply connected through trade but also locked in fierce competition for colonies, military strength, and national pride. This tension created a fragile balance of power where complex alliance systems meant that a conflict between two nations could quickly drag their allies into a much larger war.

The immediate spark for the war was the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo, which triggered a domino effect of mobilizations and declarations of war across Europe. What began as a regional crisis in the Balkans rapidly escalated into a global catastrophe because the great powers were unwilling to back down, believing that a quick victory would secure their dominance and resolve long-standing rivalries.
