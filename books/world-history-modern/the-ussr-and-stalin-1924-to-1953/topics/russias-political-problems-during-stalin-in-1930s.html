Q1. What year did Stalin's purge of dissident party members result in over 800,000 expulsions?

🔹 A. 1932
🔹 B. 1933
🔹 C. 1934
🔹 D. 1935

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The purge that expelled more than eight hundred thousand members began in 1933, as documented in party records. This massive removal was part of Stalin’s effort to eliminate potential opposition before consolidating power through the new constitution, reflecting the intensifying climate of fear within the Party.

Q2. Which document called for a slowdown in industrialization and the removal of Stalin?

🔹 A. Ryutin Platform
🔹 B. Molotov Letter
🔹 C. Khrushchev Speech
🔹 D. Lenin Manifesto

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Ryutin Platform, circulated among Moscow party leaders, explicitly demanded a deceleration of industrialization, more humane treatment of peasants, and the removal of Stalin, whom it labeled the “evil genius of the Revolution.” Its existence alarmed Stalin and contributed to the subsequent wave of purges.

Q3. Who was the Leningrad party boss whose assassination was used as a pretext for the Great Terror?

🔹 A. Leon Trotsky
🔹 B. Sergei Kirov
🔹 C. Nikolai Bukharin
🔹 D. Vyacheslav Molotov

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Sergei Kirov, the influential Leningrad party chief, was assassinated in December 1934. Stalin seized upon the murder to allege a wide‑scale conspiracy, using it as a justification for launching the Great Terror that targeted perceived enemies across the Soviet Union.

Q5. If the government’s popularity fell by twenty percentage points from an initial approval of \(70\%\), what was the new approval rating?

🔹 A. \(50\%\)
🔹 B. \(55\%\)
🔹 C. \(60\%\)
🔹 D. \(65\%\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The decline of twenty percentage points reduces the original approval of \(70\%\) to \(70\% - 20\% = 50\%\). This sharp drop reflected widespread dissatisfaction with collectivization and the hardships of the First Five‑Year Plan, contributing to growing opposition within the Party.

Q6. Why did Stalin prioritize eliminating political opponents before drafting a new constitution?

🔹 B. He wanted to demonstrate personal power
🔹 C. He needed to free resources for industrialization
🔹 D. He was following Lenin’s directives

📖 Explanation:

Stalin feared that dissenting voices could undermine the legitimacy of any new constitutional framework. By first neutralizing opponents, he aimed to ensure that the subsequent legal document would be adopted without resistance, cementing the Party’s dominance and his own authority.

Q7. A historian claims the death of Kirov increased purge arrests by \(40\%\). If there were initially \(400{,}000\) arrests, how many arrests occurred after the increase?

🔹 A. \(520{,}000\)
🔹 B. \(560{,}000\)
🔹 C. \(600{,}000\)
🔹 D. \(640{,}000\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

An increase of \(40\%\) on the original \(400{,}000\) arrests adds \(0.40 \times 400{,}000 = 160{,}000\) more arrests, yielding a total of \(400{,}000 + 160{,}000 = 560{,}000\). This rise illustrates how Kirov’s murder intensified the climate of suspicion and accelerated the scale of Stalin’s purges.

Q8. Which governance principle was most violated during the Great Terror?

🔹 A. Rule of law
🔹 B. Separation of powers
🔹 C. Freedom of speech
🔹 D. All of the above

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Great Terror breached multiple foundational principles: the rule of law was ignored as arbitrary arrests and executions occurred; separation of powers was absent because the Party controlled the judiciary; and freedom of speech was extinguished through censorship and fear, making all listed principles violated.

Q9. If \(800{,}000\) members were expelled in 1933 and the total incarcerated population was \(2{,}000{,}000\), what fraction of the total does the 1933 expulsion represent?

🔹 A. \(0.2\)
🔹 B. \(0.3\)
🔹 C. \(0.4\)
🔹 D. \(0.5\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The proportion is calculated as \(800{,}000 ÷ 2{,}000{,}000 = 0.4\). Thus, the expulsions of 1933 accounted for forty percent of the overall incarcerated population, underscoring the scale of Stalin’s early purge efforts before the broader terror escalated.

Q11. Assuming each purge year from 1933 to 1938 removed an average of \(250{,}000\) party members, and the total expelled over those years was \(1{,}500{,}000\), how many years did the purges last?

🔹 A. 4
🔹 B. 5
🔹 C. 6
🔹 D. 7

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the total number expelled, \(1{,}500{,}000\), by the average yearly removal, \(250{,}000\), yields \(1{,}500{,}000 ÷ 250{,}000 = 6\) years. This calculation aligns with the historical period of intensified purges spanning from 1933 through 1938.
