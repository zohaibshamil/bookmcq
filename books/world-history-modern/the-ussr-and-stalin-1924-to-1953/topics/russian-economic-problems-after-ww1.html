What is Russian economic problems after WW1?

The Soviet Union faced devastated infrastructure, low industrial output, and agricultural backwardness following World War I and the Civil War, creating urgent pressure to modernize rapidly in order to survive against perceived capitalist threats.

📝 All Russian economic problems after WW1 MCQs

Q1. In \(1929\), which sector's output in the Soviet Union was still considerably lower than that of France, Germany, Britain, and the USA?

A.Coal production
B.Steel production
C.Heavy industry output ✅
D.Textile manufacturing
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Heavy industry lagged behind the West because the Soviet economy had not yet completed the transition from a war‑torn, agrarian base to a modern industrial system, leaving it far behind in both volume and technology.

Q2. By early \(1928\), what major problem threatened Soviet cities due to the primitive agricultural system?

A.Industrial worker strikes
B.Food shortages ✅
C.Housing crisis
D.Energy deficit
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The continuation of the NEP-era, low‑productivity agriculture caused grain supplies to dwindle, leading to acute food shortages in urban centers and raising the specter of famine if decisive action was not taken.

Q3. Under the New Economic Policy (NEP), which sector was allowed to continue using private ownership?

A.Heavy industry
B.Agriculture ✅
C.Banking
D.Foreign trade
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: NEP re‑introduced limited private initiative primarily in agriculture, permitting peasants to sell surplus produce on the market while the state retained control over heavy industry and strategic sectors.

Q4. Stalin argued that increasing the number of industrial workers would strengthen the communist state because they were the party's greatest ______?

A.Allies ✅
B.Opponents
C.Bureaucrats
D.Intellectuals
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Stalin believed that a larger proletariat would cement Bolshevik power, viewing industrial workers as the most reliable supporters of the regime compared with the largely rural peasantry.

Q5. Why were foreign investors unwilling to finance Soviet industrial expansion?

A.High taxes
B.Political instability
C.Lack of legal rights
D.Fear of communism ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Capitalists feared the ideological instability of a communist system, doubting that private profit could be secured under state ownership, which made them reluctant to commit funds to Soviet industrial projects.

Q6. What was the primary purpose of producing a food surplus for export in the late 1920s?

A.Feed the army
B.Earn foreign capital ✅
C.Reduce urban prices
D.Support peasants
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Exporting grain allowed the USSR to obtain hard currency and foreign exchange, which could then be used to purchase machinery and technology needed for rapid industrialization.

Q7. The first Five‑Year Plan was launched in which year?

A.1928 ✅
B.1929
C.1930
D.1932
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The inaugural Five‑Year Plan began in 1928, marking a dramatic shift from the NEP to a centrally planned push for massive increases in heavy industry and collective agriculture.

Q8. If the Soviet Union could increase steel production by \(50\%\) annually for five years, by what factor would total output grow relative to the initial level?

A.\(1.2^{5}=2.49\) times
B.\(1.5^{5}=7.6\) times ✅
C.\(1.1^{5}=1.61\) times
D.\(2^{5}=32\) times
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A \(50\%\) yearly increase compounds to \(1.5^{5}\approx7.6\), meaning output would be roughly 7.6 times the original level, illustrating the explosive potential of sustained growth rates.

Q9. Considering Stalin's goal to raise industrial workers relative to peasants, which policy best aligns with this objective?

A.Collectivization ✅
B.NEP
C.Price controls
D.Import substitution
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Collectivization forced peasants onto state farms, freeing labor for factories and boosting the proletariat’s proportion, directly serving Stalin’s aim of a stronger industrial base.

Q10. Suppose a famine reduces urban population by \(10\%\) and industrial output by \(15\%\) in 1928. Which policy would most directly mitigate the output decline?

A.Increase grain exports
B.Invest in heavy machinery
C.Implement collectivized agriculture ✅
D.Raise urban wages
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Collectivized agriculture would aim to raise overall grain production, alleviating famine and restoring the labor force needed for industrial work, thereby addressing the output shortfall.

Q11. Which of the following best explains why Stalin emphasized heavy industry despite limited capital?

A.To attract foreign investors
B.To create military strength ✅
C.To increase consumer goods
D.To reduce urban migration
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Heavy industry was seen as essential for building a modern army capable of defending the USSR against perceived capitalist threats, even if capital was scarce.

Q12. The speech in February 1931 where Stalin justified the Five‑Year Plans was delivered to which governing body?

A.Politburo
B.Central Committee ✅
C.Supreme Soviet
D.Red Army General Staff
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Stalin addressed the Central Committee, using the platform to outline the necessity of rapid industrialization and to rally party leaders behind the ambitious five‑year timetable.

Q13. Compared to the USA, Soviet coal production in 1929 was approximately ____ of the American output.

A.\(0.5\)
B.\(0.2\)
C.\(0.8\)
D.\(0.1\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Historical data show Soviet coal output was roughly one‑tenth of that of the United States in 1929, highlighting the stark industrial gap that Stalin sought to close.

Q14. If the Soviet government allocated \(30\%\) of its budget to heavy industry and \(10\%\) to agriculture, what is the ratio of industry to agriculture spending?

A.0.12569444444444455 ✅
B.0.043749999999999956
C.0.08402777777777781
D.0.043055555555555625
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Dividing the percentages (30 ÷ 10) yields a 3 : 1 ratio, indicating that heavy industry received three times the financial resources devoted to agriculture.

Q15. Which of the following consequences was most likely if food surplus for export failed to materialize?

A.Increased foreign capital
B.Reduced industrial growth ✅
C.Urban food abundance
D.Lower grain prices
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Without export earnings, the state would lack the foreign exchange needed to purchase machinery, slowing industrial expansion and undermining the broader goals of the five‑year plans.

Q16. Assume the Soviet Union needed \(500\) million rubles to fund its first Five‑Year Plan. If foreign investors would provide only \(10\%\) of needed capital, how much would the state need to raise internally?

A.\(50\) million rubles
B.\(450\) million rubles
C.\(500\) million rubles
D.\(550\) million rubles ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Accounting for inefficiencies and unexpected costs, the USSR would have to secure \(550\) million rubles domestically, exceeding the original budget to compensate for the shortfall in foreign financing.

Q17. Evaluate the statement: 'Collectivization was primarily a response to food shortages rather than an ideological drive.' Which evidence most supports this claim?

A.Rapid industrial output increase
B.Grain procurement quotas ✅
C.Peasant resistance
D.Foreign trade balances
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Grain procurement quotas imposed by the state directly targeted food scarcity, indicating that the immediate economic need to secure sufficient harvests drove the policy more than pure ideological motives.

Q18. If collectivization led to a \(30\%\) drop in agricultural productivity, and the USSR required a minimum of \(10\) million tons of grain to avoid famine, what was the pre‑collectivization production level?

A.\(13.0\) million tons
B.\(14.3\) million tons ✅
C.\(12.9\) million tons
D.\(15.0\) million tons
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Let \(x\) be the original output. After a 30 % decline, output equals \(0.7x = 10\) million, so \(x = 10/0.7 \approx 14.3\) million tons.

Q19. Which of the following best illustrates the paradox of Stalin's industrialization policy regarding the need for foreign capital?

A.Reliance on autarky while seeking exports
B.Heavy industry growth with limited capital
C.Simultaneous grain export and famine ✅
D.Investing in consumer goods while neglecting defense
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The USSR needed to export grain to obtain foreign currency for industrial imports, yet the same export pressures often exacerbated domestic food shortages, creating a contradictory situation.

Q20. During the first Five‑Year Plan, output of steel increased from \(5\) million tons to \(18\) million tons. What is the percentage increase?

A.2.6 ✅
B.3
C.2.4
D.2
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The increase is \((18-5)/5 = 13/5 = 2.6\), or a \(260\%\) rise, demonstrating the dramatic expansion targeted by the plan.

Q21. Which of the following was NOT a direct reason for Stalin's focus on heavy industry?

A.Defense against western powers
B.Need for consumer goods ✅
C.Increasing party support
D.Lack of foreign investment
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Stalin’s emphasis on heavy industry was driven by security and political considerations, not by the desire to produce consumer items for the civilian population.

Q22. By the end of the NEP period, the proportion of the population employed in industry had risen to approximately what percent?

A.0.1
B.0.15 ✅
C.0.2
D.0.25
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Industrial employment grew steadily during the NEP, reaching roughly fifteen percent of the total workforce, reflecting early stages of the Soviet shift toward a more industrialized economy.
