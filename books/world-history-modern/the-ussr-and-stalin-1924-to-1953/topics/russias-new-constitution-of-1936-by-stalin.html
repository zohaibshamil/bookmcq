What is Russia's new constitution of 1936 by Stalin?

The 1936 Constitution promised democratic rights and universal suffrage on paper but served primarily as propaganda to mask the reality of totalitarian rule and ongoing terror while presenting the USSR as a progressive socialist state.

📝 All Russia's new constitution of 1936 by Stalin MCQs

Q1. Which body elected the Praesidium in 1936?

A.Supreme Soviet ✅
B.Politburo
C.Council of People's Commissars
D.Central Committee
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The 1936 constitution specified that after its brief session, the Supreme Soviet elected the Praesidium to act on its behalf, illustrating the formal legislative step even though real authority was concentrated elsewhere in the Party hierarchy.

Q2. According to the 1936 constitution, who was allowed to vote by secret ballot?

A.Only Party members
B.Everyone including former nobles, kulaks, priests, and White Army officers ✅
C.Only peasants
D.Only workers
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The constitution proclaimed universal suffrage, stating that every citizen – even former nobles, kulaks, clergy, and former White officers – could vote by secret ballot, creating an appearance of inclusivity while the electoral process remained tightly controlled by the Communist Party.

Q3. The constitution listed several universal human rights. Which right was most routinely violated in practice?

A.Right to public assembly
B.Right to employment
C.Freedom of religion
D.Freedom of speech ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Although the constitution nominally guaranteed freedoms, the most consistently breached right was freedom of speech; critics of Stalin were swiftly arrested or executed, demonstrating that the stated liberty existed only on paper and was routinely suppressed by the regime.

Q4. If a candidate received 95% of the votes, what did this outcome signify according to the constitutional goals?

A.Low popularity of the candidate
B.Electoral fraud
C.Near‑100% target showing policy approval ✅
D.High voter turnout
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The constitution encouraged candidates to achieve near‑100% support, viewing such margins as evidence that government policies enjoyed overwhelming popular approval; a 95% result would be interpreted as a successful demonstration of the party’s dominance rather than a sign of dissent.

Q5. Which statement best captures the contrast between the de jure role of the Supreme Soviet and the de facto power of the Politburo?

A.Both had equal decision‑making authority.
B.The Supreme Soviet controlled daily administration while the Politburo handled foreign policy.
C.The Supreme Soviet drafted laws, but the Politburo could amend them at will.
D.The Supreme Soviet was formally supreme, yet real power rested with the Politburo and Stalin. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Formally, the Supreme Soviet was the highest state organ, but in practice the Politburo, led by Stalin, exercised decisive control over policy, appointments, and repression, rendering the Soviet’s legislative functions largely ornamental and underscoring the gap between constitutional text and actual political reality.

Q6. In what year was the new constitution introduced?

A.1935
B.1936 ✅
C.1937
D.1938
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The constitution that re‑defined the USSR as a socialist state of workers and peasants was adopted in 1936 after extensive debate, marking a symbolic shift toward a more democratic façade while preserving the Communist Party’s monopoly on power.

Q7. A citizen publicly criticized Stalin and was arrested. Which constitutional provision was most directly breached?

A.Freedom of speech ✅
B.Right to employment
C.Right to public assembly
D.None, because the law permitted such arrests.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The constitution guaranteed freedom of speech, yet any open criticism of Stalin was punished by swift purges, illustrating the stark contradiction between the written guarantee and the reality of political repression under the 1936 legal framework.

Q8. Assuming each of the \(15\) constituencies had exactly \(100{,}000\) voters and only one Communist Party candidate stood in each, what is the theoretical maximum total number of votes the party could receive?

A.\(1.5 \\times 10^{6}\) ✅
B.\(1.0 \\times 10^{6}\)
C.\(2.0 \\times 10^{6}\)
D.\(1.5 \\times 10^{7}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: With \(15\) constituencies each containing \(100{,}000\) voters, the total electorate equals \(15 \\times 100{,}000 = 1{,}500{,}000\). Since only one Party candidate ran in each district, the maximum possible vote count for the Communist Party is \(1.5 \\times 10^{6}\) votes.

Q9. Which body chose the members of the Council of People’s Commissars?

A.Supreme Soviet
B.Praesidium ✅
C.Politburo
D.Central Committee
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The constitution stipulated that the Supreme Soviet, after convening, elected a smaller body – the Praesidium – which in turn selected the Council of People's Commissars, illustrating the layered but ultimately party‑controlled appointment process.

Q10. Who held the position of General Secretary of the Communist Party when the 1936 constitution was adopted?

A.Leon Trotsky
B.Vladimir Lenin
C.Joseph Stalin ✅
D.Nikolai Bukharin
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Joseph Stalin occupied the role of General Secretary of the Communist Party in 1936, a position that granted him de facto control over the state apparatus and allowed him to shape the constitution to reinforce party dominance.

Q11. The term ‘former people’ excluded which of the following groups?

A.Ex‑nobles
B.Kulaks
C.Priests
D.Industrial managers ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The constitution’s definition of ‘former people’ encompassed ex‑nobles, kulaks, priests, and former White Army officers, but it did not explicitly list industrial managers, making that group the exception among those mentioned.

Q12. Targeting an almost‑100% vote share implies what about the statistical variance of election results across constituencies?

A.Very low variance, indicating uniform results ✅
B.High variance, showing diverse outcomes
C.No impact on variance
D.Increased voter turnout
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A policy that seeks near‑universal endorsement forces each constituency to produce similar, overwhelmingly high percentages, thereby reducing statistical variance and creating an artificially uniform electoral landscape across the entire nation.

Q13. How long did the Supreme Soviet meet in 1936 before electing the Praesidium?

A.About two weeks ✅
B.Six months
C.One year
D.Continuously throughout the year
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Supreme Soviet convened for only roughly two weeks in 1936, after which it elected the Praesidium to act on its behalf, reflecting the brief and largely symbolic nature of its legislative session.

Q14. The Praesidium acted on behalf of which institution?

A.Supreme Soviet
B.Politburo ✅
C.Council of People’s Commissars
D.Central Committee
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Although the Praesidium was formally a subset of the Supreme Soviet, in practice it functioned as an instrument of the Politburo, executing decisions that reflected the Party’s real power rather than the nominal authority of the Soviet assembly.

Q15. Considering the constitution's claim of universal human rights versus the reality of purges, which political theory best explains this discrepancy?

A.Democratic centralism
B.Liberal democracy
C.Totalitarianism ✅
D.Constitutionalism
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The gap between the constitution’s generous rights provisions and the systematic repression of dissent exemplifies totalitarianism, where the state maintains an all‑encompassing ideological control that renders formal guarantees meaningless in practice.
