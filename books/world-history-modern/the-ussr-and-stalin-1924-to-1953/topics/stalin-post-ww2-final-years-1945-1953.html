Q1. What was the total number of Soviet military personnel who died during World War II?

A. 5.5 million
B. 6.2 million ✅
C. 7.0 million
D. 4.8 million

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Soviet military casualties were staggering, with approximately \(6.2\) million soldiers killed in action. This number exceeds the combined military deaths of many other nations involved, underscoring the immense human cost the USSR endured to achieve victory on the Eastern Front.

Q2. How many Soviet civilians are estimated to have died as a result of the war?

A. 12 million
B. 15 million
C. 17 million ✅
D. 20 million

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Civilian losses in the Soviet Union reached about \(17\) million, a figure that includes deaths from siege conditions, forced labor, and famine. These civilian casualties highlight the broad societal devastation beyond battlefield losses and illustrate the severe humanitarian impact of the conflict on the home front.

Q3. Which Five‑Year Plan was launched to rebuild the Soviet Union after the war?

A. Third Five‑Year Plan
B. Fourth Five‑Year Plan ✅
C. Fifth Five‑Year Plan
D. Sixth Five‑Year Plan

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Fourth Five‑Year Plan (1946–1950) was the official program aimed at reconstructing industry, housing, and infrastructure devastated by the war. It sought to restore production to pre‑war levels and to lay the groundwork for future economic growth, marking a decisive shift from wartime mobilization to peacetime recovery.

Q4. Approximately how many people were deported from western Ukraine during Stalin’s post‑war purges?

A. 150000
B. 300000 ✅
C. 500000
D. 1000000

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Stalin ordered the forced removal of roughly \(300{,}000\) individuals from western Ukraine as part of a broader campaign to suppress nationalist resistance. These deportations were intended to eliminate perceived threats and to reinforce Soviet control over newly annexed territories, reflecting the regime’s ruthless approach to dissent.

Q5. What percentage of the total Soviet war dead does the military death figure of \(6.2\) million represent?

A. 0.2
B. 0.25 ✅
C. 0.3
D. 0.35

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

To find the proportion, divide the military deaths by the total war dead: \(\frac{6.2}{25}\times100\approx24.8\%\). Rounded to the nearest whole number, this yields about 25 %, indicating that a quarter of all Soviet war casualties were soldiers killed in combat.

Q6. What fraction of Red Army soldiers who survived German POW camps were permitted to return home?

A. One third ✅
B. Half
C. Two thirds
D. All

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Out of the \(2.8\) million former POWs, only about one‑third were allowed to resume civilian life, while the remainder faced arrest, execution, or imprisonment in the Gulag. This policy reflected Stalin’s suspicion toward anyone who had endured captivity, treating them as potentially compromised.

Q7. Before the post‑war surge, roughly how many prisoners were held in Soviet labour camps?

A. 1.2 million ✅
B. 1.0 million
C. 1.3 million
D. 1.5 million

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The labour‑camp population doubled to roughly \(2.5\) million after the war, implying an initial figure near \(\frac{2.5}{2}=1.25\) million. The closest listed estimate is \(1.2\) million, indicating the scale of the pre‑war penal system before its massive expansion for reconstruction projects.

Q8. If industrial production was restored to its 1940 level by 1950 after linear growth over four years, what average annual growth rate does this imply?

A. 0.2
B. 0.225
C. 0.25 ✅
D. 0.3

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Assuming production started at zero in 1946 and reached the 1940 level (100 %) by 1950, the growth per year is \(\frac{100\%}{4}=25\%\). This simplified calculation yields an average annual increase of 25 %, illustrating the rapid post‑war industrial recovery demanded by the Fifth Plan.

Q9. Why does the figure of 3 million people sent to labour camps not contradict the post‑war camp population of 2.5 million?

A. The figures refer to different time periods ✅
B. Some individuals were counted twice
C. Foreign prisoners were included in the total
D. It is a statistical error

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The 3 million count refers to a specific group—those who had been in Allied hands—while the 2.5 million figure represents the overall camp population at a later date. Thus, the numbers describe distinct categories and moments, avoiding a direct numerical conflict.

Q10. Where was the Soviet Union’s first successful atomic bomb test conducted?

A. Kazakhstan, August 1949 ✅
B. Moscow, 1950
C. Leningrad, 1948
D. Siberia, 1951

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The inaugural Soviet atomic explosion occurred at the Semipalatinsk Test Site in Kazakhstan in August 1949. This breakthrough demonstrated the USSR’s entry into the nuclear age and marked a pivotal moment in Cold‑War geopolitics, showcasing its capability to rival the United States.

Q11. If 25 million people were homeless after the war and housing was built at a rate of 5 million units per year, how many years would be required to shelter everyone?

A. 3 years
B. 4 years
C. 5 years ✅
D. 6 years

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the total homeless population by the annual construction rate gives \(\frac{25}{5}=5\) years. Assuming each new unit accommodates one family, it would take five years to provide housing for all displaced individuals, highlighting the massive scale of the post‑war reconstruction effort.
