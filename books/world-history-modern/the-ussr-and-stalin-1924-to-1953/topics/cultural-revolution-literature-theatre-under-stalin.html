📝 All Cultural Revolution literature theatre under Stalin MCQs

Q1. Which organization was dissolved by the government before the creation of the All‑Russian Union of Soviet Writers?

🔹 A. All‑Russian Association of Proletarian Writers (RAPP)
🔹 B. All‑Russian Union of Writers (AUW)
🔹 C. All‑Russian Union of Soviet Writers (AUSW)
🔹 D. Union of Soviet Writers

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The All‑Russian Union of Writers (AUW) was officially dissolved as part of the regime’s effort to eliminate independent literary voices, making way for a single, party‑controlled writers' union. This action removed the platform for many pre‑revolutionary authors and forced them to seek admission to the new, ideologically stricter organization.

Q2. A writer who belonged to the AUW in \(1930\) wants to publish a new novel. What is the most likely consequence if he is not accepted into the new union?

🔹 A. He can publish independently
🔹 B. He will be barred from official publishing houses
🔹 C. He will be forced to join RAPP
🔹 D. He will be exiled

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Only members of the officially sanctioned writers' unions were permitted to publish through state‑run presses. If a former AUW member failed to gain admission to the new union, his work would be denied access to the only legal distribution channels, effectively silencing his literary output.

Q3. Why did Stalin disband both RAPP and AUSW in favor of a single writers' organization?

🔹 A. To reduce bureaucratic overlap
🔹 B. To ensure all literature adhered to his personal vision of socialist realism
🔹 C. To promote competition among writers
🔹 D. To appease foreign critics

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Stalin sought complete control over cultural production, demanding that literature reflect his idealized version of Soviet life. By eliminating the two existing bodies and creating a single Union chaired by Maxim Gorky, he could enforce a uniform artistic doctrine—socialist realism—that matched his political objectives.

Q4. Who chaired the Union of Soviet Writers when it was created after \(1934\)?

🔹 A. Andrei Zhdanov
🔹 B. Maxim Gorky
🔹 C. Nikolai Ostrovsky
🔹 D. Mikhail Sholokov

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Maxim Gorky, whose literary reputation Stalin admired, was appointed as the chair of the newly formed Union of Soviet Writers. His leadership symbolized the regime’s endorsement of socialist realism and provided a respected figurehead to guide Soviet authors under strict ideological supervision.

Q5. A state publishing house in \(1934\) wants to release a novel that reflects collectivization. Which title should they choose?

🔹 A. How the Steel Was Tempered

📖 Explanation:

\Virgin Soil Upturned\ directly addresses the collectivization process, portraying the challenges and triumphs of turning individual farms into collective enterprises. Its thematic focus aligns perfectly with the state's desire to showcase the success of collectivization, making it the logical choice for a publishing house seeking official approval.

Q6. A writer submits a manuscript that depicts societal faults. Stalin reads it and adds comments. According to the period's policies, what is the likely outcome for the writer?

🔹 A. The manuscript will be published unchanged
🔹 B. The writer will be praised for honesty
🔹 C. The writer will be instructed to revise according to Stalin's suggestions
🔹 D. The writer will be arrested immediately

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Stalin’s personal involvement in editing meant that authors were expected to incorporate his feedback. Failure to adapt the work to the party line could lead to censure or worse, but the immediate expectation was a revision that conformed to the approved ideological narrative.

Q7. What term was used by communists to dismiss non‑communist writers who wanted politics out of literature?

🔹 A. Trotskyists
🔹 B. Fellow‑travellers
🔹 C. Bourgeois intellectuals
🔹 D. Saboteurs

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Communists labeled writers who resisted incorporating political doctrine as “fellow‑travellers.” The term implied a lack of commitment to the revolutionary cause and was used to marginalize those who preferred artistic independence over party‑directed content.

Q8. An editor must select a manuscript that exemplifies a ‘five‑year plan novel’. Which description fits?

🔹 A. A story criticizing the five‑year plan’s failures
🔹 B. A romance set during industrialization
🔹 C. A tale of an ordinary worker achieving plan targets despite sabotage
🔹 D. A sci‑fi adventure about future technology

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

‘Five‑year plan novels’ celebrated ordinary heroes who overcame obstacles such as wreckers and saboteurs to meet production goals. The narrative emphasized collective effort, perseverance, and the triumph of socialist labor, making the second description the correct representation of the genre.

Q9. Assess the impact of ‘five‑year plan’ novels on Soviet morale. Which statement best reflects scholarly consensus?

🔹 A. They demotivated workers by exposing hardships
🔹 B. They had negligible effect
🔹 C. They boosted morale by providing simple, relatable heroes
🔹 D. They caused widespread literary dissent

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Scholars agree that these novels, though artistically limited, played a crucial role in uplifting morale. By presenting easily understood stories of heroic workers, they reinforced the belief that individual effort could contribute to national success, thereby encouraging greater productivity and loyalty to the state.

Q10. You are a writer in \(1929\) deciding whether to join RAPP or AUW. Which factor would most likely influence your choice?

🔹 A. RAPP's stronger political alignment
🔹 B. AUW's literary freedom
🔹 C. Salary differences
🔹 D. Geographic location of the union's headquarters

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RAPP demanded strict adherence to communist ideology, offering political favor and potential access to state resources. Writers motivated by ideological commitment or seeking party patronage would be drawn to RAPP, whereas those prioritizing artistic independence might consider AUW, but political pressures often tipped the balance toward RAPP.
