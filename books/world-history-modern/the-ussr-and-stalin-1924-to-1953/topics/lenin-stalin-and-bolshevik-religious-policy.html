What is Lenin Stalin and Bolshevik religious policy?

Both Lenin and Stalin pursued aggressive atheism by closing churches, persecuting clergy, and promoting scientific materialism, though Stalin temporarily relaxed restrictions during World War II to harness religious patriotism for the war effort.

Q1. According to the passage, which organization was formed to persecute clergy?

A. League of Militant Godless ✅
B. Red Army
C. Soviet Writers Union
D. People's Commissariat of Education

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League of Militant Godless was created explicitly to attack the clergy and eradicate religious influence. Its formation reflected the Bolshevik commitment to atheism and the belief that religion was a harmful superstition that needed to be eliminated from Soviet society.

Q2. In what year did Stalin allow churches to reopen during World War II?

A. 1939
B. 1942 ✅
C. 1945
D. 1940

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Stalin’s decision in 1942 to permit churches to reopen was driven by wartime exigencies. Facing German advances on Leningrad and Moscow, the regime recognized that religious sentiment could bolster patriotic morale, leading to a temporary relaxation of anti‑religious policies.

Q3. If the number of working priests fell from \(60{,}000\) in 1925 to under \(6{,}000\) by 1941, approximately what percentage decline does this represent?

A. 0.8
B. 0.85
C. 0.9 ✅
D. 0.95

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The drop from \(60{,}000\) priests to under \(6{,}000\) represents roughly a ninety‑percent reduction. This massive loss illustrates the intensity of the Soviet anti‑religious campaign, which aimed to decimate the clergy and suppress organized faith throughout the 1920s and 1930s.

Q4. Considering the Bolsheviks' view that communism was the only religion, which policy best describes their approach toward religious institutions?

A. Total eradication
B. Complete integration
C. No policy
D. Conditional tolerance ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Although the Bolsheviks proclaimed communism as the sole ‘religion’, their practical approach varied over time. Early on they pursued outright eradication, but later, especially during World War II, they adopted a conditional tolerance that allowed limited worship to serve state interests.

Q5. Suppose a rural community had 12 religious leaders before the anti‑religious campaign, and 75 % were eliminated. How many leaders remained?

A. 6
B. 3 ✅
C. 9
D. 0

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Eliminating 75 % of twelve religious leaders leaves 25 % surviving. Twenty‑five percent of twelve equals three individuals. This calculation demonstrates the severe impact of the anti‑religious campaign on local spiritual figures, reducing community leadership dramatically.

Q6. Which Soviet leader launched a savage attack on the Orthodox Church, seizing lands and arresting priests?

A. Trotsky
B. Khrushchev
C. Gorbachev
D. Lenin ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Lenin directed a brutal assault on the Russian Orthodox Church, confiscating its property, shutting down schools, and imprisoning priests. His policies reflected Marxist atheism and aimed to dismantle the church’s social influence, setting the tone for subsequent Soviet anti‑religious actions.

Q7. If only one in \(40\) church buildings remained functional by 1941, what percent of churches does this represent?

A. 0.05
B. 0.025 ✅
C. 0.015
D. 0.1

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

One functional church out of forty corresponds to a fraction of \(1/40\). Converting this to a percentage gives \((1/40) \times 100 = 2.5\%\). This tiny proportion underscores how extensively the Soviet regime crippled religious infrastructure by 1941.

Q8. During World War II, Stalin regarded religion as a force for patriotism. Which outcome best illustrates this policy?

A. Expulsion of clergy
B. Nationalization of church property
C. Reopening of places of worship ✅
D. Ban on religious festivals

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Stalin’s wartime strategy recognized that allowing churches to operate could strengthen national unity against the German invasion. The reopening of churches, mosques, and synagogues provided spiritual support, boosted civilian morale, and demonstrated a pragmatic shift from ideological suppression to patriotic collaboration.

Q9. Which religious groups were permitted to reopen their houses of worship in 1942?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

In 1942 the Soviet government extended permission to Christians, Jews, and Muslims alike to restore their worship spaces. This inclusive policy aimed to harness the motivational power of each faith community, reinforcing a collective front against the external threat posed by the Axis powers.

Q11. If the League of Militant Godless targeted \(120\) churches per year from 1928 through 1932, how many churches would they have attacked in total?

A. 600 ✅
B. 500
C. 700
D. 400

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Targeting \(120\) churches each year over five years (1928‑1932 inclusive) yields \(120 \times 5 = 600\) churches. This figure illustrates the systematic scale of the League of Militant Godless’s anti‑religious campaign, reflecting the Soviet leadership’s determination to diminish religious presence across the nation.

Q12. Which Soviet policy led to the mass killing of priests who opposed collectivization?

A. Industrialization
B. Dekulakization
C. Collectivization ✅
D. Great Purge

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Collectivization forced peasants into state‑run farms, provoking resistance from many clergy who defended traditional village structures. The Soviet response was harsh; priests who opposed the policy were arrested or executed, linking the broader agricultural reforms directly to the violent suppression of religious leaders.

Q13. What term did Marx use to describe religion in his famous quote?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Karl Marx famously described religion as the ‘opium of the masses’, suggesting it served as a comforting illusion that dulled the awareness of material oppression. This phrase appears in the passage to explain why Bolshevik leaders regarded religion as a harmful superstition to be eradicated.

Q14. Analyzing the shift in Stalin’s policy from persecution to tolerance, which factor most directly influenced the change?

B. Economic crisis
C. International pressure
D. Leadership change

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The most immediate catalyst for Stalin’s policy reversal was the severe military setbacks in 1941, especially the sieges of Leningrad and Moscow. Facing a national crisis, the regime leveraged religious sentiment to foster resilience, making the dire wartime situation the decisive factor behind the temporary tolerance.

Q15. If a village had \(5\) churches before 1941 and only one remained functional after the anti‑religious campaign, what percentage of churches survived?

A. 0.25
B. 0.15
C. 0.1
D. 0.2 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

With one remaining church out of five, the survival rate is \(1/5 = 0.20\), or \(20\%\). This calculation highlights the drastic reduction of worship sites caused by the Soviet anti‑religious drive, leaving only a small fraction of the original churches operational.
