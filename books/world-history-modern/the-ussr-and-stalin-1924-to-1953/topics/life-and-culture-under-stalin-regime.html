What is Life and culture under Stalin regime?

Daily life was characterized by constant fear, material shortages, and mandatory participation in state rituals, while culture was strictly regulated to promote socialist realism and suppress any expression deemed politically unreliable.

Q1. In 1933, a married worker in Moscow consumed less than half the amount of bread compared to his counterpart in 1900. Approximately what fraction of the earlier consumption did he have?

A. \( \frac{1}{4} \)
B. \( \frac{1}{2} \)
C. \( \frac{3}{4} \)
D. Full amount ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The passage notes the 1933 consumption was under half of the 1900 level. Interpreting “less than half” as roughly one‑half gives the fraction \( \frac{1}{2} \). This answer directly reflects the comparative statement without requiring additional calculations.

Q2. What was the primary factor that created housing shortages in Soviet cities between 1926 and 1939?

A. Decline in construction materials
B. Rapid increase in urban population ✅
C. Government policy limiting private ownership
D. Decrease in birth rates

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The text attributes housing shortages to a massive influx of people, specifically a 31‑million rise in urban population. This surge outpaced the construction of new dwellings, making the rapid increase in urban population the most direct cause of the shortages.

Q4. If the urban population increased by 31 million between 1926 and 1939, and the increase was steady each year, what was the approximate yearly increase?

A. 1.5 million
B. 2.38 million ✅
C. 3 million
D. 4 million

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Thirteen years separate 1926 and 1939. Dividing 31 million by 13 yields about \( \frac{31}{13}\approx2.38 \) million people per year, which is the closest estimate among the options.

Q5. Given that real wages in 1937 were three‑fifths of 1928 levels, what percentage decrease does this represent?

A. 0.2
B. 0.4 ✅
C. 0.6
D. 0.8

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Three‑fifths of the original amount means the wages fell to 60 % of the 1928 level. The reduction is therefore \(100\%-60\%=40\%\), matching option B.

Q6. A middle‑class family originally occupying a house with 5 rooms must now share it with two other families of equal size. If each family has 4 members, what is the average number of people per room after the rearrangement?

A. 1.2
B. 2.4 ✅
C. 3
D. 4

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Three families each with 4 members give 12 people. With the house still having 5 rooms, the average occupancy is \( \frac{12}{5}=2.4 \) persons per room.

Q9. A worker spends 3 hours each day queuing for bread while a party member receives it at home. If the worker’s productivity is 1 unit per hour of work, what is the loss in productivity due to queuing?

A. 1 unit
B. 2 units
C. 3 units ✅
D. 4 units

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Each hour spent queuing replaces an hour of productive work. Over 3 hours, the worker forfeits \(3\times1=3\) productivity units, directly reflecting the loss caused by the time spent in line.

Q10. If nominal wages rose 10 % from 1928 to 1937 while inflation was 30 %, what is the resulting real‑wage factor, and does it match the three‑fifths figure?

A. Real wage fell to 84.6% of 1928 level ✅
B. Real wage fell to 60% of 1928 level
C. Real wage increased by 10%
D. Real wage unchanged

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Real wage = nominal/(1+inflation) = 1.10 / 1.30 ≈ 0.846, i.e., 84.6 % of the 1928 level. This differs from the three‑fifths (60 %) figure, showing a discrepancy between the calculated change and the historical claim.

Q11. Three families each consume 0.8 kg of flour per week in a communal apartment. What is the total weekly flour consumption, and how does it compare to the 1933 average consumption of a married worker who ate less than half of a 10 kg weekly amount?

A. 1.6 kg
B. 2.4 kg ✅
C. 3.2 kg
D. 4.0 kg

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Total consumption is \(3\times0.8=2.4\) kg per week. The 1933 married worker ate under half of 10 kg, i.e., less than 5 kg. Thus the communal consumption is lower than the worker’s maximum possible intake, illustrating the scarcity.

Q12. If 31 million new urban residents each required 0.5 m² of housing, estimate the total additional housing area needed in square kilometers.

A. 7.75 km²
B. 12.5 km²
C. 15.5 km² ✅
D. 20 km²

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The required area is \(31{,}000{,}000\times0.5=15{,}500{,}000\) m². Converting to square kilometers (\(1\,\text{km}²=1{,}000{,}000\,\text{m}²\)) gives \(15.5\) km², matching option C.

Q16. If a worker’s weekly bread consumption in 1933 was 4 kg, which is less than half of the 1900 consumption of 10 kg, calculate the percentage reduction.

A. 0.3
B. 0.4
C. 0.5
D. 0.6 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The reduction is \(100\%-\frac{4}{10}\times100\%=100\%-40\%=60\%\). Thus the worker’s consumption fell by 60 % relative to the 1900 level.

Q18. Considering the data that real wages were three‑fifths of 1928 levels in 1937, and housing shortages forced evictions, discuss the likely impact on urban migration patterns using economic theory of supply and demand.

A. Increased migration to cities ✅
B. Decreased migration
C. No effect
D. Reverse migration

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Lower real wages reduce purchasing power, while severe housing shortages increase the cost of living. According to supply‑demand theory, a scarcity of affordable housing (low supply) combined with the need for wage‑earning jobs (high demand) would intensify rural‑to‑urban migration, despite deteriorating living standards.

Q19. If a communal apartment has 4 families, each with 3 members, and there are 2 bathrooms shared, what is the average number of people per bathroom?

A. 2
B. 3
C. 4
D. 6 ✅

📖 Explanation:

Total occupants are \(4\times3=12\). With two bathrooms, the average occupancy per bathroom is \(12/2=6\) people, indicating a high level of crowding.

Q21. Suppose the probability of a worker being evicted in 1939 was 0.12, and the city had 500 000 workers. Estimate the expected number of evictions and discuss the social implications.

A. 30000
B. 45000
C. 60000 ✅
D. 75000

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Expected evictions equal probability times population: \(0.12\times500{,}000=60{,}000\). Such a large number would likely cause widespread insecurity, erode trust in authorities, and exacerbate social unrest among the working class.
