Q1. The armistice was signed on 11 November 1918. If the German capture of Poland occurred in December 1917, approximately how many days passed between these two events?

🔹 A. 330 days
🔹 B. 340 days
🔹 C. 350 days
🔹 D. 360 days

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The capture of Poland occurred in December 1917, roughly eleven months before the armistice on 11 November 1918. Counting the days from 1 December 1917 to 11 November 1918 gives about 340 days, allowing for the varying lengths of months. This interval shows how quickly the war concluded after the German setbacks in the east.

Q4. If the British naval blockade reduced German imports by 80 % in 1918, and the pre‑blockade industrial output was 100 units, what output remained after the blockade?

Q10. The Treaty of Versailles demanded reparations of \( \\frac{3}{4} \\) trillion marks. If Germany could pay \( 5 \\times 10^{10} \\) marks annually, how many full years would be needed to settle the debt?

🔹 A. 10 years
🔹 B. 12 years
🔹 C. 15 years
🔹 D. 20 years

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Three‑quarters of a trillion marks equals \\(0.75 \\times 10^{12}=7.5 \\times 10^{11}\\) marks. Dividing this amount by the yearly payment capacity of \\(5 \\times 10^{10}\\) yields \\(7.5 \\times 10^{11} / 5 \\times 10^{10}=15\\). Hence, fifteen complete years of payments would be required, assuming no interest or economic changes.

Q11. Which of the following best explains why many German scholars condemned the Versailles settlement as unjust?

🔹 A. It imposed excessive territorial losses and reparations
🔹 B. It granted Germany new colonies
🔹 C. It recognized German war guilt fully
🔹 D. It allowed Germany to keep its navy

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

German intellectuals argued that the treaty stripped Germany of valuable territories, imposed crippling reparations, and humiliated the nation, fostering resentment. The combination of land cessions and financial burdens was perceived as punitive beyond what the war caused, fueling political instability in the interwar period.
