Q1. Which Central Power entered the war in November \(1914\) on the Eastern Front?

🔹 A. Turkey
🔹 B. Bulgaria
🔹 C. Italy
🔹 D. USA

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Turkey signed the Ottoman Empire's entry into the war in November \(1914\), aligning with Germany and Austria‑Hungary. This brought a new front in the Balkans and added Ottoman forces to the Central Powers, influencing the broader strategic picture of the conflict.

Q2. Which empire faced early defeats by Russia in \(1914\)?

🔹 A. Austro‑Hungarian
🔹 B. Ottoman
🔹 C. German
🔹 D. British

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Austro‑Hungarian Empire suffered a series of setbacks against Russian forces during the opening months of the war, notably in Galicia. These early defeats forced Austria‑Hungary to rely heavily on German support, straining the alliance and shaping the early course of the Eastern Front.

Q3. Russian forces advanced \(120\) km while Austro‑Hungarian troops fell back \(80\) km. What is the total front‑line shift towards Russia?

🔹 A. \(40\) km
🔹 B. \(120\) km
🔹 C. \(200\) km
🔹 D. \(300\) km

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The net movement of the front line equals the sum of the Russian advance and the Austro‑Hungarian retreat: \(120\) km + \(80\) km = \(200\) km. This combined shift illustrates how both offensive and defensive actions contributed to the rapid changes in territorial control during 1914.

Q4. How did German assistance to Austria‑Hungary in 1914 affect their strategic coordination?

🔹 A. It created complete unity, eliminating any friction
🔹 B. It caused Austria‑Hungary to dominate joint decisions
🔹 C. It led to a balanced partnership with equal input
🔹 D. It generated tension, as Germany often dictated plans, straining the alliance

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

German support, while crucial for Austrian survival, often came with conditions that limited Austrian autonomy. German commanders frequently overrode Austrian preferences, leading to strategic disagreements. This tension impacted joint operations, reducing the effectiveness of coordinated actions on the Eastern Front.

Q5. If the Russian army could field \(1.5\) million men and the Austro‑Hungarian could field \(800{,}000\), what was the approximate ratio of Russian to Austro forces?

🔹 A. 1 : 1
🔹 B. 2 : 1
🔹 C. 15 : 8
🔹 D. 3 : 2

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the Russian strength (\(1.5\) million) by the Austro‑Hungarian strength (\(800{,}000\)) yields roughly \(1.875\), which is close to a \(2\) to \(1\) ratio. This approximation is commonly used in historical analyses to highlight the numerical superiority Russia enjoyed at the war’s outset.

Q6. Which battle in \(1914\) marked the first major Russian victory on the Eastern Front?

🔹 A. Battle of Galicia
🔹 B. Battle of Tannenberg
🔹 C. Battle of the Marne
🔹 D. Battle of Verdun

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Battle of Galicia, fought in August \(1914\), resulted in a decisive Russian triumph over Austro‑Hungarian forces. This victory opened large swaths of Galicia to Russian occupation and demonstrated Russia’s early offensive capability, setting the tone for the fluid nature of the Eastern Front in the war’s first year.

Q7. Why did the Russian advance slow after early successes in 1914?

🔹 A. Logistical shortages and harsh winter conditions
🔹 B. Overwhelming German reinforcements alone
🔹 C. A sudden peace treaty with Austria‑Hungary
🔹 D. Loss of morale among Russian troops

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

While logistics and weather played roles, the primary factor was the rapid deployment of German reserves to support Austria‑Hungary, which stiffened resistance and forced Russia onto a more defensive posture. The combined Central Powers pressure exhausted Russian supply lines and limited further offensive momentum.

Q8. If the Austro‑Hungarian army lost \(30\%\) of its \(1{,}000{,}000\) troops in September \(1914\), how many remained?

🔹 A. \(300{,}000\)
🔹 B. \(500{,}000\)
🔹 C. \(700{,}000\)
🔹 D. \(900{,}000\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A loss of \(30\%\) means \(70\%\) of the original force survived. Calculating \(0.70\times1{,}000{,}000\) gives \(700{,}000\) troops remaining. This substantial reduction weakened Austro‑Hungarian offensive capacity and increased reliance on German assistance during the critical early phases of the Eastern Front conflict.

Q9. Which front saw more movement during the first year of World War I?

🔹 A. Eastern
🔹 B. Western
🔹 C. Italian
🔹 D. Balkan

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Eastern Front experienced far greater maneuver warfare in \(1914\) as Russian and Austro‑German forces clashed across vast territories. Unlike the static trench warfare that quickly dominated the Western Front, the East remained fluid, with armies advancing and retreating over hundreds of kilometers.

Q10. In which month did Germany declare war on Russia?

🔹 A. August
🔹 B. September
🔹 C. July
🔹 D. June

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Germany declared war on Russia on 1 August \(1914\), but the formal declaration was delivered on 2 August, following Russia’s mobilization. Some historical sources cite September due to the timing of diplomatic exchanges, but the initial declaration aligns with the August timeline.

Q11. How did the capture of Polish territories in December \(1917\) influence Eastern Front dynamics?

🔹 A. It forced Russia to re‑enter the war
🔹 B. It allowed Germany to redirect troops to the Western Front
🔹 C. It had no strategic impact
🔹 D. It led to immediate peace negotiations

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Seizing Polish lands removed the last significant Russian foothold in the east, permitting Germany to shift divisions westward for the 1918 offensives. Although the capture did not directly cause peace talks, it underscored the collapse of Russian resistance and reshaped the overall strategic balance.

Q12. If the German army required three weeks to reorganize after the Battle of Tannenberg, and each week they could train \(10{,}000\) troops, how many troops were trained?

🔹 A. \(10{,}000\)
🔹 B. \(20{,}000\)
🔹 C. \(30{,}000\)
🔹 D. \(40{,}000\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Multiplying the weekly training capacity (\(10{,}000\) troops) by the three‑week period yields \(30{,}000\) troops. This figure reflects the German army’s ability to quickly replace losses and maintain operational readiness following the intense early engagements of the Eastern Front.

Q14. Discuss the strategic significance of Austro‑German friction over resource allocation in 1914.

🔹 A. It led to a unified command structure
🔹 B. It caused Austria‑Hungary to withdraw from the war
🔹 C. It limited coordinated offensives, reducing overall effectiveness
🔹 D. It had no impact on battlefield outcomes

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The disagreement over supplies and strategic priorities created mistrust, yet both powers continued to fight together. While friction existed, it did not cripple joint operations; instead, it forced compromise, illustrating how alliance politics could coexist with effective military collaboration despite underlying tensions.

Q15. If the British navy sank \(200\) German submarines in 1915, representing \(25\%\) of the total U‑boat fleet, how many submarines did Germany have?

🔹 A. \(400\)
🔹 B. \(800\)
🔹 C. \(1{,}000\)
🔹 D. \(1{,}200\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

If \(200\) submarines constitute \(25\%\) of the fleet, the total equals \(200 ÷ 0.25 = 800\). This calculation shows the scale of the U‑boat arm at that stage and highlights the impact of British anti‑submarine measures on German naval capabilities.

Q16. Which treaty ended fighting on the Eastern Front?

🔹 A. Treaty of Versailles
🔹 B. Treaty of Trianon
🔹 C. Treaty of Brest‑Litovsk
🔹 D. Treaty of Saint‑Germain

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Brest‑Litovsk, signed in March \(1918\), formally ended hostilities between Soviet Russia and the Central Powers, effectively concluding combat on the Eastern Front. This agreement ceded large territories to Germany and its allies, reshaping the geopolitical map of Eastern Europe.

Q17. Which nation suffered a mutiny in its army in \(1917\), weakening its western front?

🔹 A. France
🔹 B. Italy
🔹 C. Russia
🔹 D. Germany

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In 1917, French troops experienced widespread mutinies after the disastrous Nivelle Offensive, leading to a crisis of morale and a temporary halt to major offensives. The mutinies forced the French command to restructure its leadership and adopt more cautious tactics, affecting the overall Allied war effort.

Q18. Assess why the Allied blockade of German ports was less effective on the Eastern Front compared to the Western Front.

🔹 A. Because the Eastern Front relied heavily on overland supply routes
🔹 B. Because the blockade directly cut off all Eastern supplies
🔹 C. Because the Central Powers had abundant naval power in the east
🔹 D. Because the blockade was equally effective everywhere

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The blockade primarily targeted maritime traffic, which was crucial for Western Front logistics. The Eastern Front depended more on rail and overland routes from Russia and the Balkans, making the naval blockade less disruptive there. Consequently, the Central Powers could sustain eastern operations despite Allied maritime pressure.

Q19. If a convoy of \(50\) merchant ships lost \(12\%\) to U‑boat attacks before convoy protection, how many ships were sunk?

🔹 A. \(4\)
🔹 B. \(6\)
🔹 C. \(8\)
🔹 D. \(10\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Calculating \(12\%\) of \(50\) gives \(0.12 \times 50 = 6\). Thus six ships were lost before the convoy system provided protection, illustrating the early vulnerability of Allied shipping to submarine warfare.

Q22. Explain how the failure of the German Schlieffen Plan influenced the Eastern Front's operational tempo.

🔹 A. It caused the Eastern Front to become static

This redistribution slowed the pace of offensives against Russia, compelling the Central Powers to adopt a more defensive posture in the east.

Q23. If the Russian army mobilized \(1.2\) million men in August \(1914\) and increased by \(15\%\) each month, how many troops were available by October \(1914\)?

🔹 A. \(1.4\) million
🔹 B. \(1.59\) million
🔹 C. \(1.7\) million
🔹 D. \(1.8\) million

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Starting with \(1.2\) million, a \(15\%\) rise in September yields \(1.2 \times 1.15 = 1.38\) million. Applying another \(15\%\) increase for October gives \(1.38 \times 1.15 \approx 1.587\) million, rounded to \(1.59\) million. This growth illustrates the rapid expansion of Russian forces during the early war months.
