Q1. In early 1915, the German navy began laying mines in the North Sea. How many ships were sunk by these mines by the end of 1915?

🔹 A. 15
🔹 B. 22
🔹 C. 30
🔹 D. 45

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The mine campaign began modestly, and records show that by the close of 1915 roughly twenty‑two Allied vessels had been lost to German explosive devices, highlighting the early impact of this new form of naval warfare.

Q2. Which German U‑boat type was primarily used for unrestricted submarine warfare in 1917?

🔹 A. U‑31
🔹 B. U‑35
🔹 C. U‑93
🔹 D. U‑151

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

U‑93 was a Type U 93 ocean‑going submarine, and its long range and torpedo capacity made it a central tool in Germany’s 1917 decision to resume unrestricted attacks on merchant shipping.

Q3. If a convoy of 10 merchant ships travels at 10 knots and a German submarine can travel at 15 knots, how many hours does the submarine have to catch up if it starts 30 nautical miles behind?

🔹 A. \(4\) hours
🔹 B. \(6\) hours
🔹 C. \(8\) hours
🔹 D. \(10\) hours

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The relative speed is 5 knots (15 − 10). Covering a 30‑nautical‑mile gap therefore requires 30 ÷ 5 = 6 hours, giving the U‑boat a six‑hour window to close the distance before the convoy reaches its destination.

Q4. What was the primary reason Britain intensified its convoy system in 1917?

🔹 A. To protect against mines
🔹 B. To counter air raids
🔹 C. To reduce losses from U‑boats
🔹 D. To improve trade routes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

By mid‑1917, U‑boat sinkings had reached alarming levels, prompting the British Admiralty to adopt the convoy system as an organized defense that dramatically cut merchant‑ship casualties caused by German submarines.

Q5. During the Battle of Jutland, German mines sank the British cruiser HMS Nottingham. If the mine field contained 12 mines and the probability of any given ship hitting a mine was 0.08, what is the expected number of ships sunk?

🔹 A. 0.5
🔹 B. 0.96
🔹 C. 1.2
🔹 D. 2

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The expected value equals the number of trials multiplied by the success probability: 12 × 0.08 = 0.96. This indicates that, on average, just under one ship would be expected to be lost in that specific minefield.

Q7. If Germany laid a minefield covering an area of \(100 \text{ km}^2\) and each mine occupies \(0.5 \text{ km}^2\) of effective radius, how many mines could be placed without overlap?

🔹 A. 100
🔹 B. 150
🔹 C. 200
🔹 D. 250

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the total area by the area per mine (100 ÷ 0.5) yields 200 mines, showing the theoretical maximum density of mines that could be deployed in that sector without their blast zones intersecting.

Q9. A British convoy of 5 ships is escorted by 2 destroyers. If each destroyer can detect a U‑boat within a radius of 15 nautical miles, what is the combined detection area assuming no overlap?

🔹 A. \(\pi\times225\)
🔹 B. \(2\pi\times225\)
🔹 C. \(\pi\times300\)
🔹 D. \(2\pi\times300\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each destroyer covers an area of \(\pi r^2 = \pi\times15^2 = \pi\times225\). With two ships and no overlap, the total detection region doubles to \(2\pi\times225\), greatly expanding the protective screen around the convoy.

Q14. The German navy deployed 400 mines in the English Channel. If each mine had a 5 % chance of being neutralized by Allied minesweepers, what is the probability that at least one mine remains active after sweeping?

🔹 A. 0.5
🔹 B. 0.9
🔹 C. 0.999
🔹 D. 0.999999999

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The chance that all mines are neutralized is \((0.05)^{400}\), an astronomically tiny figure. Consequently, the probability that at least one mine survives is essentially \(1-(0.95)^{400}\approx0.999999999\), indicating near‑certainty of remaining threats.

Q17. If a minefield consists of 30 mines each with a lethal radius of 100 meters, what total area (in square meters) is covered assuming no overlap?

🔹 A. \(\;3.14\times10^{5}\)
🔹 B. \(\;9.42\times10^{5}\)
🔹 C. \(\;1.5\times10^{6}\)
🔹 D. \(\;2.0\times10^{6}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each mine covers \(\pi r^{2}= \pi\times100^{2}=3.1415\times10^{4}\) m². Multiplying by 30 mines gives \(30\times3.1415\times10^{4}=9.4245\times10^{5}\) m², approximated as \(9.42\times10^{5}\) m².

Q18. During the convoy system, if a ship travels 800 nautical miles at 8 knots, how many days does the journey take?

🔹 B. 4 days

🔹 D. 5 days

📖 Explanation:

Travel time equals distance divided by speed: 800 ÷ 8 = 100 hours. Converting hours to days (100 ÷ 24) yields approximately 4.17 days, which rounds to 4.2 days for practical scheduling purposes.

Q20. If the probability of a mine being detected by a patrol ship is 0.12 and the probability of it being avoided by a ship is 0.08, what is the overall probability that a ship passes the minefield safely?

🔹 A. 0.1
🔹 B. 0.19
🔹 C. 0.3
🔹 D. 0.4

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The chance a ship is hit equals the product of not being detected (0.88) and not being avoided (0.92), giving 0.8096. Subtracting from 1 yields a safe‑pass probability of about 0.1904, rounded to 0.19.

Q21. A German U‑boat fires a torpedo that travels at 40 knots and must reach a target 8 nautical miles away. How many minutes does it take to reach the target?

🔹 D. 24 minutes

📖 Explanation:

Time equals distance divided by speed: 8 ÷ 40 = 0.2 hours. Converting to minutes (0.2 × 60) results in 12 minutes for the torpedo to cover the distance.

Q23. If the German navy deployed 250 mines with a 10 % failure rate (did not detonate), and Allied forces cleared 40 mines, what is the expected number of functional mines remaining?

🔹 D. 225
