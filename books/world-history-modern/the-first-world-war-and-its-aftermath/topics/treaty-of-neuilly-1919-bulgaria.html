📝 All Treaty of Neuilly 1919 Bulgaria MCQs

Q1. What year was the Treaty of Neuilly signed?

🔹 A. 1918
🔹 B. 1919
🔹 C. 1920
🔹 D. 1921

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty was concluded on 27 March \(1919\) at the French suburb of Neuilly‑sur‑Seine, ending Bulgaria’s participation in World War I and formalising the post‑war settlement imposed by the Allied powers.

Q2. Which country lost territory to the Kingdom of Yugoslavia under the treaty?

🔹 A. Romania
🔹 B. Greece
🔹 C. Bulgaria
🔹 D. Turkey

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Bulgaria was forced to cede the western part of its territory, including the region of Strumica, to the newly created Kingdom of Yugoslavia, reshaping the Balkan map and reducing Bulgaria’s strategic depth.

Q3. The treaty required Bulgaria to cede the region of Western Thrace to which state?

🔹 A. Greece
🔹 B. Romania
🔹 C. Serbia
🔹 D. Italy

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Western Thrace was transferred to Greece, depriving Bulgaria of direct access to the Aegean Sea and limiting its maritime trade options in the interwar period.

Q4. According to the treaty, Bulgaria had to reduce its army to how many men?

🔹 A. \(20,000\)
🔹 B. \(30,000\)
🔹 C. \(40,000\)
🔹 D. \(50,000\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The treaty capped the Bulgarian armed forces at \(20,000\) personnel, a severe limitation intended to prevent any future large‑scale military aggression by the former Central Power.

Q5. What was the total reparations amount Bulgaria was required to pay?

🔹 A. \(100\) million marks
🔹 B. \(150\) million marks
🔹 C. \(200\) million marks
🔹 D. \(250\) million marks

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Allied negotiators set the reparations figure at \(150\) million marks, a sum that strained Bulgaria’s post‑war economy and contributed to widespread fiscal hardship.

Q6. Which of the following was NOT a stipulation of the Treaty of Neuilly?

🔹 A. Territorial loss
🔹 B. Military limitation
🔹 C. Colonies acquisition
🔹 D. Reparations payment

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The treaty did not grant Bulgaria any colonial possessions; instead, it focused on territorial concessions, army size limits, and reparations, reflecting the Allies’ aim to curtail Bulgarian power.

Q7. The treaty was signed at which French town?

🔹 A. Versailles
🔹 B. Neuilly‑sur‑Seine
🔹 C. Paris
🔹 D. Lyon

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Negotiations concluded in the Parisian suburb of Neuilly‑sur‑Seine, giving the agreement its name and marking the formal end of Bulgaria’s wartime status.

Q8. Scenario: If Bulgaria had retained Western Thrace, how might its access to the Aegean Sea have affected its post‑war economy?

🔹 A. Boost trade
🔹 B. No impact
🔹 C. Increase isolation
🔹 D. Reduce agricultural output

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Retaining Western Thrace would have provided Bulgaria with a coastline on the Aegean, facilitating maritime commerce, reducing transport costs, and potentially attracting foreign investment, thereby strengthening its struggling post‑war economy.

Q9. Compare the territorial losses imposed on Bulgaria by the Treaty of Neuilly with those imposed on Hungary by the Treaty of Trianon. Which statement is most accurate?

🔹 A. Bulgaria lost more land proportionally
🔹 B. Hungary lost more
🔹 C. Both lost similar proportion
🔹 D. Neither lost any land

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Trianon stripped Hungary of about two‑thirds of its pre‑war territory, a larger proportional loss than Bulgaria’s reductions, which, while significant, were less extensive in percentage terms.

Q10. The treaty created a new border between Bulgaria and which newly formed state?

🔹 A. Yugoslavia
🔹 B. Czechoslovakia
🔹 C. Poland
🔹 D. Finland

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The post‑war settlement established a fresh frontier between Bulgaria and the newly created Kingdom of Yugoslavia, redefining political boundaries in the Balkans.

Q11. According to the treaty, Bulgaria was required to cede the region of Pirin to which country?

🔹 A. Greece
🔹 B. Serbia
🔹 C. Romania
🔹 D. Turkey

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Pirin, a mountainous area in southwestern Bulgaria, was transferred to Greece, further diminishing Bulgarian territorial holdings and altering the ethnic composition of the region.

Q12. Under the treaty, Bulgaria's war guilt clause was similar to that of which other defeated Central Power?

🔹 A. Germany
🔹 B. Austria‑Hungary
🔹 C. Ottoman Empire
🔹 D. United Kingdom

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Like Austria‑Hungary, Bulgaria was forced to accept a war‑guilt clause, holding it responsible for the conflict and justifying the imposed reparations and territorial concessions.

Q13. Scenario: A Bulgarian diplomat argues that the reparations sum of \(150\) million marks is unsustainable. Which economic principle supports his claim?

🔹 A. Balance of payments
🔹 B. Purchasing power parity
🔹 C. Debt sustainability
🔹 D. Comparative advantage

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Debt sustainability theory examines a nation's capacity to meet long‑term obligations; a reparations burden of \(150\) million marks would likely exceed Bulgaria’s fiscal ability, validating the diplomat’s concern.

Q14. The treaty mandated that Bulgaria cede the district of Strumica to which country?

🔹 A. Serbia
🔹 B. Greece
🔹 C. Romania
🔹 D. Turkey

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Strumica, located in the eastern part of present‑day North Macedonia, was handed over to Serbia (later Yugoslavia), reducing Bulgaria’s influence in the Macedonian region.

📖 Explanation:

Limiting Bulgaria’s armed forces was intended to curtail any possibility of renewed militaristic expansion, ensuring long‑term stability in a region that had experienced repeated conflict.

📖 Explanation:

A refusal would likely have sparked renewed hostilities, prompting neighboring states to form defensive alliances, resulting in a more volatile Balkan landscape with shifting borders and possible territorial revisions.

Q18. Assess the impact of the territorial loss of Western Thrace on Bulgaria's military logistics in the interwar period. Which outcome is most directly linked?

Losing Western Thrace forced Bulgaria to depend on land corridors through neighboring states for supplies, complicating logistics and limiting rapid troop movements compared with a maritime route.

📖 Explanation:

The demilitarized strip pushed both sides to conduct clandestine rearmament activities, heightening suspicion and creating a covert arms race despite the formal prohibition.

Q21. Compare the treaty's punitive measures against Bulgaria with those imposed on the Ottoman Empire in the Treaty of Sèvres. Which statement is most accurate?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Sèvres imposed far‑more extensive territorial losses on the Ottoman Empire, carving up its Arab provinces, whereas Neuilly’s main emphasis lay in reparations and limited military capacity.

Q22. A historian argues that the Treaty of Neuilly sowed the seeds for Bulgaria's later alignment with the Axis powers. Which piece of evidence best supports this thesis?

📖 Explanation:

All three factors—territorial loss, heavy reparations, and army limits—generated national humiliation and economic strain, creating fertile ground for extremist politics that later gravitated toward the Axis camp.

Q23. If Bulgaria had complied fully with the treaty's terms, including reparations and territorial concessions, predict the likelihood of it regaining lost territories by the onset of World War II.

Given the strict enforcement of the treaty and the geopolitical realities of the 1930s, it would have been virtually impossible for Bulgaria to recover its forfeited lands before the outbreak of the second global conflict.
