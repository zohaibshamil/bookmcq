📝 All Treaty of Sevres 1920 Turkey MCQs

Q1. In which year was the Treaty of Sèvres signed?

🔹 A. 1919
🔹 B. 1920
🔹 C. 1921
🔹 D. 1922

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty was concluded on 10 August \(1920\) at the Paris Peace Conference. It marked the formal attempt by the Allied Powers to dismantle the Ottoman Empire after World War I, establishing new borders and mandates that would later be contested by Turkish nationalists.

Q2. Which empire was the primary subject of the Treaty of Sèvres?

🔹 A. Austro-Hungarian Empire
🔹 B. Ottoman Empire
🔹 C. Russian Empire
🔹 D. German Empire

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty targeted the Ottoman Empire, the last remnant of the centuries‑old Turkish state. By imposing territorial losses and political restrictions, the Allies aimed to curtail Ottoman sovereignty, paving the way for the emergence of a modern Turkish nation-state.

Q3. Which international body was tasked with overseeing the administration of the Straits under the treaty?

🔹 A. League of Nations
🔹 B. United Nations
🔹 C. International Court of Justice
🔹 D. World Bank

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Article 13 of the treaty created an International Commission of Control, to be supervised by the League of Nations, to manage the Dardanelles and Bosphorus. This arrangement was intended to guarantee free passage for all nations, reducing Ottoman control over these strategic waterways.

Q4. What capital city did the treaty designate for the Allied occupation zone?

🔹 A. Ankara
🔹 B. Istanbul
🔹 C. Izmir
🔹 D. Bursa

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty stipulated that the Allied forces would occupy parts of Istanbul (formerly Constantinople) while the rest of the country remained under Ottoman administration. This occupation was a key element of the Allies’ strategy to exert pressure on the Ottoman government.

Q5. Which conference produced the Treaty of Sèvres?

🔹 A. Yalta Conference
🔹 B. Paris Peace Conference
🔹 C. Versailles Conference
🔹 D. San Francisco Conference

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty emerged from the Paris Peace Conference, where the victorious Allied Powers negotiated the post‑war settlement. Delegates from Britain, France, Italy, and the United States shaped the terms that aimed to dismantle the Ottoman Empire and redistribute its territories.

Q6. What later treaty superseded the Treaty of Sèvres and recognized the modern borders of Turkey?

🔹 A. Treaty of Versailles
🔹 B. Treaty of Lausanne
🔹 C. Treaty of Trianon
🔹 D. Treaty of Saint-Germain

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Lausanne, signed in 1923, replaced Sèvres after the Turkish War of Independence. It acknowledged the sovereignty of the Republic of Turkey over Anatolia and Eastern Thrace, effectively nullifying the earlier punitive provisions of Sèvres.

Q7. Which of the following best describes the fate of the Ottoman navy under the treaty?

🔹 A. It was to be completely disbanded
🔹 B. It retained full operational capacity
🔹 C. It was limited to coastal defense only
🔹 D. It was transferred to Allied control

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The treaty imposed strict limits on the Ottoman navy, allowing only a small fleet for coastal defense. This restriction aimed to prevent Turkey from projecting naval power in the Mediterranean and the Black Sea, further weakening its strategic position.

Q8. The treaty granted independence to an Armenian state in which region?

🔹 A. Caucasus Mountains
🔹 B. Eastern Anatolia
🔹 C. Western Anatolia
🔹 D. Northern Syria

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Article 16 provided for an Armenian state in eastern Anatolia, extending to the Erzurum region. This arrangement was intended to reward Armenian aspirations for self‑determination but provoked strong opposition from Turkish nationalists who saw it as a loss of historic lands.

Q9. Which clause addressed the status of the Mosul Vilayet?

🔹 A. It was to be annexed by Turkey
🔹 B. It would become a League of Nations mandate
🔹 C. It was allocated to British‑mandated Iraq
🔹 D. It remained under Ottoman control

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The treaty stipulated that Mosul, rich in oil, would be transferred to the British‑mandated Iraq. This decision reflected Allied strategic interests in securing energy resources, while denying the Ottoman Empire access to one of its most valuable provinces.

Q10. How did the treaty propose to handle Kurdish aspirations?

🔹 A. Creation of an independent Kurdish state
🔹 B. Establishment of a Kurdish autonomous region within Turkey
🔹 C. No provisions for Kurdish autonomy
🔹 D. Inclusion of Kurdish representatives in the Ottoman parliament

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Article 64 suggested a Kurdish autonomous area in the east of the former Ottoman Empire, though the specifics were vague. This provision was intended to placate Kurdish demands but lacked clear boundaries, leading to later disputes over its implementation.

Q11. What economic obligation did the treaty impose on the Ottoman Empire?

🔹 A. Payment of war reparations to the Allies
🔹 B. Exclusive trade with the United States
🔹 C. Mandated free trade with all Allied nations
🔹 D. No economic obligations were imposed

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The treaty required the Ottoman Empire to pay substantial war reparations, similar to the demands placed on Germany by the Treaty of Versailles. These financial burdens were intended to weaken the Ottoman economy and compensate the victorious powers for wartime losses.

Q12. Which article established an International Commission of Control for the Straits, and what was its primary purpose?

🔹 A. Article 13; to ensure free navigation for all nations
🔹 B. Article 5; to supervise Turkish naval disarmament
🔹 C. Article 22; to manage oil resources
🔹 D. Article 30; to oversee refugee resettlement

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Article 13 created an International Commission of Control, overseen by the League of Nations, to guarantee the free passage of merchant vessels through the Dardanelles and Bosphorus. This arrangement aimed to prevent any single power from monopolizing these crucial maritime routes.

Q13. If Turkey had accepted the treaty in full, which of the following would have been true about its western border?

🔹 A. It would have included all of modern Greece
🔹 B. It would have been limited to the Bosphorus and Dardanelles only
🔹 C. It would have extended to include the city of Smyrna
🔹 D. It would have been identical to the current border with Greece

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Full acceptance would have ceded the western Anatolian region around Smyrna (İzmir) to Greece, dramatically reducing Turkish territory in the Aegean. This loss would have altered the demographic composition and economic resources of the nascent Turkish state.

Q14. How did the Turkish nationalist movement, led by Mustafa Kemal, react to the treaty’s terms?

🔹 A. They welcomed the provisions as a path to modernization
🔹 B. They ignored the treaty and continued cooperating with the Allies
🔹 C. They rejected the treaty and launched a war of independence
🔹 D. They sought to renegotiate the treaty through diplomatic channels only

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Mustafa Kemal and his followers denounced the treaty as an illegitimate imposition, mobilizing resistance that culminated in the Turkish War of Independence. Their rejection ultimately forced the Allies to replace Sèvres with the more favorable Treaty of Lausanne.

Q15. Compare the punitive nature of the Treaty of Sèvres with that of the Treaty of Versailles. Which statement is most accurate?

🔹 A. Both treaties imposed identical reparations on their respective defeated states
🔹 B. Sèvres was less punitive than Versailles because it offered more territorial gains
🔹 C. Sèvres was more punitive, demanding extensive territorial losses and minority protections
🔹 D. Both treaties were primarily focused on establishing new international organizations

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

While the Treaty of Versailles targeted Germany with severe reparations and territorial reductions, the Treaty of Sèvres went further by carving up Ottoman lands, imposing minority protections, and creating new states. Its punitive scope was broader, reflecting Allied ambitions to reshape the Near East.

Q16. Assuming the treaty’s economic reparations amounted to 2 billion gold francs, what would be the approximate percentage of the Ottoman Empire’s pre‑war annual revenue of 5 billion gold francs?

🔹 A. 0.2
🔹 B. 0.3
🔹 C. 0.4
🔹 D. 0.5

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The reparations represent \(\frac{2\,\text{billion}}{5\,\text{billion}} \times 100 = 40\%\) of the empire’s annual revenue. Such a heavy fiscal burden would have strained the Ottoman treasury, limiting its ability to fund public services and undermining economic stability.

Q17. Analyze how the provision for an independent Armenian state in eastern Anatolia would have affected regional power dynamics. Which outcome is most plausible?

🔹 A. It would have created a buffer against Russian expansion
🔹 B. It would have strengthened Turkish control over the region
🔹 C. It would have sparked a prolonged conflict between Turkey and Armenia
🔹 D. It would have led to immediate economic integration with the Ottoman Empire

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

An Armenian state in eastern Anatolia would have introduced a new nationalist entity directly bordering Turkish heartland, likely provoking hostilities. The presence of a Christian Armenian polity could have attracted support from Western powers, intensifying Turkish‑Armenian tensions and destabilizing the region.

Q18. If the Kurdish autonomous region outlined in the treaty had been implemented, what potential ethnic conflicts might have arisen?

🔹 A. Kurdish‑Armenian alliance against Turkey
🔹 B. Kurdish‑Turkish cooperation leading to shared governance
🔹 C. Kurdish aspirations for full independence causing friction with neighboring Turkish and Arab populations
🔹 D. No conflicts would have emerged due to clear boundaries

📖 Explanation:

Establishing a Kurdish autonomous zone could have ignited aspirations for full sovereignty, prompting resistance from both Turkish authorities and surrounding Arab communities. Competing claims over land and resources would likely have fueled inter‑ethnic tensions, complicating the post‑war settlement.

Q19. Assess the compatibility of the treaty’s clauses with the principle of self‑determination articulated in Wilson’s Fourteen Points. Which assessment best reflects the treaty’s alignment?

🔹 A. Fully compatible, as it grants independence to all subject peoples
🔹 B. Partially compatible, granting some autonomy but imposing extensive territorial losses on Turkey
🔹 C. Incompatible, because it disregards the wishes of the Turkish majority and imposes foreign control
🔹 D. Neutral, as the treaty does not address self‑determination at all

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

While the treaty offered limited autonomy to Armenians and Kurds, it largely ignored the principle of self‑determination for the Turkish majority, imposing foreign mandates and territorial partitions. This inconsistency undermined Wilson’s ideal and contributed to Turkish nationalist opposition.

Q20. What was the primary purpose of the Allied powers in establishing the International Commission of Control over the Straits?

🔹 A. To promote Ottoman naval resurgence
🔹 B. To ensure unrestricted commercial navigation for all nations

📖 Explanation:

The commission was created to guarantee free passage of merchant vessels through the Dardanelles and Bosphorus, preventing any single nation—particularly Turkey—from monopolizing this crucial maritime corridor, thereby serving Allied commercial and strategic interests.

Q21. Which of the following best describes the impact of the treaty on Turkey’s ability to conduct foreign trade?

🔹 C. It restricted access to the Black Sea and Mediterranean, limiting trade
🔹 D. It granted Turkey preferential trade agreements with the United States

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

By limiting Turkish control over the Straits and imposing territorial losses, the treaty curtailed the empire’s access to key maritime routes, thereby hampering its capacity to engage in international commerce and reducing economic growth prospects.

Q22. How did the treaty address the status of the city of Smyrna (İzmir) and its surrounding region?

🔹 A. It placed the city under direct Allied administration

🔹 C. It designated Smyrna as a neutral international zone

📖 Explanation:

The treaty allocated Smyrna and its environs to Greece, reflecting Allied support for Greek territorial ambitions. This decision heightened tensions between Greek and Turkish populations and later contributed to the Greco‑Turkish conflict of 1919‑1922.

Q23. Which of the following statements accurately reflects the role of the League of Nations in the enforcement of the treaty?

📖 Explanation:

The League’s involvement was limited to overseeing the International Commission of Control, ensuring that the Straits remained open to all nations. It did not administer territories directly, but its supervisory role was crucial for the implementation of certain treaty clauses.
