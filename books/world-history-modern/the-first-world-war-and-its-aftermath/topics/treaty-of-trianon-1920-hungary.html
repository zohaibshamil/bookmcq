📝 All Treaty of Trianon 1920 Hungary MCQs

Q1. In which year was the Treaty of Trianon signed, formally ending Hungary’s participation in World I?

🔹 A. 1919
🔹 B. 1920
🔹 C. 1921
🔹 D. 1922

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty was concluded on 4 June 1920 at the Paris Peace Conference, establishing the new borders of Hungary and ending its involvement in the war. This date is central to understanding the post‑war settlement and the geopolitical reshaping of Central Europe.

Q2. Which of the following territories was NOT ceded by Hungary under the Treaty of Trianon?

🔹 A. Transylvania
🔹 B. Slovakia
🔹 C. Croatia
🔹 D. Austria

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Austria remained an independent state and was not part of the territories taken from Hungary. The treaty stripped Hungary of lands such as Transylvania, Slovakia, and Croatia, but Austria was not among the regions transferred, making it the correct answer.

Q3. Approximately what percentage of Hungary’s pre‑war population became ethnic minorities after the treaty’s borders were redrawn?

🔹 A. 0.2
🔹 B. 0.3
🔹 C. 0.4
🔹 D. 0.5

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The post‑treaty configuration turned roughly three‑tenths of the pre‑war Hungarian population into minorities within the reduced state. This shift created significant demographic challenges and fueled nationalist grievances that shaped interwar politics.

Q4. A Hungarian family owned 150 hectares in a region that became part of Czechoslovakia. After the treaty they lost all of this land. How many hectares remained in their possession?

🔹 A. 0
🔹 B. 50
🔹 C. 75
🔹 D. 150

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because the entire area was transferred to Czechoslovakia, the family retained no land. The loss of 150 hectares illustrates the personal impact of border changes, where many landowners found themselves dispossessed overnight.

Q5. The treaty limited the Hungarian army to how many soldiers?

🔹 A. 10000
🔹 B. 20000
🔹 C. 30000
🔹 D. 40000

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Under the treaty’s military restrictions, Hungary could maintain only a modest force of 20,000 troops. This cap was intended to prevent future aggression but also left the nation vulnerable and contributed to internal tensions.

Q6. Which city remained the capital of Hungary after the Treaty of Trianon?

🔹 A. Budapest
🔹 B. Vienna
🔹 C. Bratislava
🔹 D. Prague

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Budapest continued as the political and cultural heart of the reduced Hungarian state. Despite losing vast territories, the capital’s continuity provided a focal point for national identity and governmental functions.

Q7. The name “Treaty of Trianon” derives from which location?

🔹 A. A suburb of Paris where the treaty was signed
🔹 B. The Hungarian Parliament building
🔹 C. A historic castle in Transylvania
🔹 D. The capital city of Vienna

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The treaty was signed at the Grand Trianon, a palace in the Versailles complex near Paris. Its name reflects the venue rather than any Hungarian geography, linking the agreement to the French royal setting.

Q8. How many new sovereign states emerged from the territories formerly belonging to Hungary?

🔹 A. 3
🔹 B. 4
🔹 C. 5
🔹 D. 6

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The redrawing of borders created five successor states that incorporated former Hungarian lands: Czechoslovakia, Romania, the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (later Yugoslavia), Poland, and a small portion allocated to Austria. This fragmentation reshaped Central Europe.

Q9. If pre‑war Hungary had a population of \(20\) million and \(60\%\) remained within its new borders, how many people stayed?

🔹 A. \(8\) million
🔹 B. \(10\) million
🔹 C. \(12\) million
🔹 D. \(14\) million

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Multiplying \(20\) million by \(0.60\) yields \(12\) million inhabitants who continued to reside inside the reduced Hungary. This demographic reduction underscores the scale of the population that became minorities abroad.

Q10. Which economic sector suffered the greatest loss of capacity due to the treaty’s territorial changes?

🔹 A. Mining
🔹 B. Textiles
🔹 C. Machinery
🔹 D. Agriculture

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Machinery and heavy industry were disproportionately affected because many factories were situated in ceded regions such as Transylvania. The loss of these industrial hubs crippled Hungary’s ability to produce complex goods and weakened its export potential.

Q11. Which principle primarily guided the border decisions in the Treaty of Trianon?

🔹 A. Self‑determination
🔹 B. Balance of Power
🔹 C. Wilsonian Idealism
🔹 D. All of the above

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The treaty’s architects invoked self‑determination, sought to maintain a balance of power, and incorporated Wilsonian ideals of national self‑rule. In practice, the combination of these concepts produced borders that often conflicted with ethnic realities, leading to lasting disputes.

Q12. Hungary was required to pay reparations of \(300\) million gold marks. If the payment was to be made over \(10\) years, what was the annual amount?

🔹 A. \(20\) million
🔹 B. \(30\) million
🔹 C. \(40\) million
🔹 D. \(50\) million

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Dividing \(300\) million by \(10\) yields an annual obligation of \(30\) million gold marks. This financial burden strained the already weakened Hungarian economy and contributed to post‑war inflation and social unrest.

Q13. Under the treaty, which ethnic minority became the largest within the new, smaller Hungarian borders?

🔹 A. Slovak
🔹 B. Romanian
🔹 C. German
🔹 D. Serbian

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Slovak minority, concentrated in the northern territories that remained, grew to become the most numerous non‑Hungarian group. Their presence influenced language policies and minority rights debates throughout the interwar period.

Q14. A Hungarian railway line of \(200\) km was divided, with \(60\%\) now lying in Czechoslovakia. How many kilometres remained under Hungarian control?

🔹 A. \(40\) km
🔹 B. \(80\) km
🔹 C. \(120\) km
🔹 D. \(160\) km

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Sixty percent of \(200\) km equals \(120\) km, leaving \(80\) km within Hungary. The loss of critical transport infrastructure hampered domestic logistics and reduced the nation’s connectivity to its own markets.

Q15. After losing \(60\%\) of its arable land, Hungary’s grain production fell by approximately what percentage?

🔹 A. 0.3
🔹 B. 0.45
🔹 C. 0.55
🔹 D. 0.7

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The reduction of fertile farmland led to a sharp decline in agricultural output, estimated at about \(55\%\). This contraction forced Hungary to import foodstuffs, exacerbating economic vulnerability and prompting agrarian reforms.

Q16. Which of the following was NOT a direct consequence of the Treaty of Trianon?

🔹 A. Rise of revisionist politics in Hungary
🔹 B. Creation of new borders in Central Europe
🔹 C. Increase in Hungarian territorial size
🔹 D. Strengthening of minority rights in the successor states

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The treaty did not expand Hungary’s territory; rather, it dramatically reduced it. The other listed outcomes—political revisionism, new borders, and evolving minority protections—were direct results of the settlement’s terms.

Q17. How did the treaty contribute to the rise of which political movement in interwar Hungary?

🔹 A. Communism
🔹 B. Fascism (Arrow Cross)
🔹 C. Liberal democracy
🔹 D. Restoration of the monarchy

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The sense of national humiliation and loss fostered extremist sentiments, providing fertile ground for the Arrow Cross Party’s fascist ideology. This movement capitalized on revisionist aspirations, promising to overturn the treaty’s constraints and restore Hungary’s former greatness.

Q18. If Hungary’s pre‑treaty GDP was \(100\) billion krones and fell to \(45\) billion after territorial losses, what was the percentage decrease?

🔹 A. 0.45
🔹 B. 0.55
🔹 C. 0.65
🔹 D. 0.75

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The drop from \(100\) billion to \(45\) billion represents a decline of \(55\) billion, which is a \(55\%\) reduction ((\(100-45\))/\(100\) × 100). This sharp contraction illustrates the economic shock caused by the loss of industrial and agricultural regions.

Q19. Which border region remained a source of conflict between Hungary and Romania until World II?

🔹 A. Transylvania
🔹 B. Banat
🔹 C. Vojvodina
🔹 D. Carpathian Ruthenia

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Transylvania, with its mixed Hungarian‑Romanian population, became a flashpoint after the treaty ceded the region to Romania. Ongoing disputes over its status persisted, influencing diplomatic tensions and occasional armed clashes up to the outbreak of World II.

Q20. Article 27 of the treaty limited the Hungarian navy to what total tonnage?

🔹 A. 1,000 tons
🔹 B. 5,000 tons
🔹 C. 10,000 tons
🔹 D. 15,000 tons

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The naval restriction confined Hungary’s fleet to a modest 1,000 tonnage, effectively eliminating any capacity for blue‑water operations. This limitation reflected Allied concerns about future aggression and underscored Hungary’s diminished military stature.

Q21. In what way was the principle of self‑determination applied inconsistently in the treaty’s border decisions?

🔹 A. All ethnic groups received territories matching their majority status
🔹 B. Only Slavic peoples were granted self‑determination, while others were ignored
🔹 C. Borders were drawn primarily on strategic considerations, sometimes overriding ethnic majorities
🔹 D. Self‑determination was uniformly applied without exception

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

While the treaty cited self‑determination, many borders were drawn to satisfy strategic and political aims, leading to situations where ethnic majorities found themselves on the “wrong” side of the line. This inconsistency fueled resentment and revisionist demands.

Q22. If 500,000 Hungarians were expelled from Romania and 300,000 from Czechoslovakia, what was the total number displaced?

🔹 A. 600000
🔹 B. 700000
🔹 C. 800000
🔹 D. 900000

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Adding the two groups—\(500,000\) and \(300,000\)—yields a total of \(800,000\) displaced persons. This massive population movement highlighted the human cost of the territorial rearrangements and contributed to demographic tensions in the region.
