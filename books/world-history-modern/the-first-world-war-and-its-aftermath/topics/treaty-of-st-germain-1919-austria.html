What is Treaty of St Germain 1919 Austria?

The Treaty of St Germain in 1919 formally dissolved the Austro-Hungarian Empire and recognized Austrian independence as a small rump state, prohibited union with Germany without League approval, mandated military restrictions, and confirmed territorial losses to Czechoslovakia, Poland, Yugoslavia, and Italy.

📝 All Treaty of St Germain 1919 Austria MCQs

Q1. In which year was the Treaty of St Germain signed?

A. 1918
B. 1919 ✅
C. 1920
D. 1921

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty was concluded at the Paris Peace Conference and formally signed on 10 September 1919, marking the official end of Austria‑Hungary as a political entity and establishing the new borders for the Republic of Austria.

Q2. Which multi‑ethnic empire was officially dissolved by the Treaty of St Germain?

A. Ottoman Empire
B. Austro‑Hungarian Empire ✅
C. German Empire
D. Russian Empire

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty dismantled the Austro‑Hungarian Empire, which had been a major Central European power composed of numerous nationalities, and created separate successor states, thereby ending its centuries‑old imperial structure.

Q3. Which guiding principle, articulated by President Wilson, most influenced the territorial decisions of the treaty?

A. Balance of Power
B. Self‑determination ✅
C. Legitimacy
D. Colonialism

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Wilson’s Fourteen Points emphasized the right of peoples to determine their own political status, and the treaty applied this principle by redrawing borders to reflect the ethnic composition of regions such as South Tyrol and Carinthia.

Q4. Which territory was ceded by Austria to Italy under the treaty?

A. South Tyrol ✅
B. Carinthia
C. Galicia
D. Bukovina

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The treaty transferred the predominantly German‑speaking province of South Tyrol to Italy, a decision intended to reward Italy for its participation in the war and to create a defensible northern border for the new Italian state.

Q5. Which region was transferred to Austria from Hungary as a result of the treaty?

A. Burgenland ✅
B. Transylvania
C. Bohemia
D. Moravia

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Burgenland, a largely German‑speaking area that had been part of the Kingdom of Hungary, was reassigned to Austria, completing the re‑allocation of territories in line with ethnic majorities and the treaty’s broader aim of stabilising borders.

Q6. What city remained the capital of the new Republic of Austria after the treaty?

A. Salzburg
B. Graz
C. Vienna ✅
D. Linz

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Vienna continued as the political and cultural centre of the reduced Austrian state, hosting the provisional government and later becoming the site of the First Austrian Republic’s institutions.

Q7. The treaty prohibited Austria from uniting with which neighboring country?

A. Hungary
B. Germany ✅
C. Italy
D. Yugoslavia

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

One of the treaty’s security clauses explicitly forbade any political union—known as Anschluss—between Austria and Germany, reflecting the Allied powers’ desire to prevent a possible resurgence of German‑Austrian power.

Q8. How did the loss of industrial regions such as the Sudetenland affect Austria's post‑war economy?

A. Boosted agriculture
B. Caused severe industrial decline ✅
C. Increased tourism
D. Improved trade balance

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The removal of the Sudetenland, which contained a large portion of Austria’s heavy industry and skilled workforce, dramatically reduced output, leading to unemployment, lower export revenues, and a reliance on foreign capital to rebuild the diminished economic base.

Q9. The treaty limited the size of the Austrian army to 30,000 men. What was the primary reason for this restriction?

A. To prevent future aggression ✅
B. To reduce unemployment
C. To align with League of Nations standards
D. To free up resources for civilian use

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Allied negotiators feared that a sizable Austrian force could be used to resurrect a Central European power bloc, so they imposed a strict cap to ensure the new state remained a peaceful, non‑militarised entity within the post‑war order.

Q10. Compared to the Treaty of Versailles, Austria's reparations under St Germain were:

A. Higher
B. Similar
C. Lower ✅
D. Non‑existent

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

While Germany faced massive reparations, Austria was assessed a comparatively modest sum because its reduced territory and limited industrial capacity meant it could not bear the same financial burden, reflecting the Allies’ differentiated approach to the defeated powers.

Q11. Which ethnic group was most directly affected by the transfer of South Tyrol to Italy?

A. Slovaks
B. Croatians
C. German‑speaking South Tyroleans ✅
D. Hungarians

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The annexation placed a substantial German‑speaking population under Italian rule, sparking cultural and political tensions that persisted throughout the interwar period and influenced later minority‑rights disputes.

Q12. If Austria had retained access to the Adriatic Sea after the treaty, which of the following economic outcomes would have been most likely?

A. Expansion of shipbuilding industry ✅
B. Decline in agricultural exports
C. Increased reliance on imports
D. No significant change

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A coastline would have given Austria a direct outlet for maritime trade, encouraging the development of shipyards, ports, and related industries, thereby diversifying its economy beyond the limited hinterland resources it possessed after losing its former coastal territories.

Q13. Which of President Woodrow Wilson's Fourteen Points most directly influenced the territorial decisions in the Treaty of St Germain?

A. Point 5 (self‑determination) ✅
B. Point 9 (open covenants)
C. Point 12 (independent nations)
D. Point 14 (League of Nations)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Point 5 advocated that peoples should have the right to choose their own governments, a principle that guided the redrawing of borders to align with ethnic majorities, as seen in the cession of South Tyrol and the creation of new states from former Austro‑Hungarian lands.

Q14. The Carinthian plebiscite of 1920, resulting from the treaty, decided the fate of which area?

A. Northern Carinthia
B. Southern Carinthia ✅
C. Eastern Carinthia
D. Western Carinthia

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The plebiscite allowed the inhabitants of southern Carinthia to vote on whether to join Austria or the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes; the majority chose to remain with Austria, solidifying the new border in that region.

Q15. How did the treaty address the rights of the Slovene minority living in the newly defined Austrian borders?

A. Granted full autonomy
B. Provided limited cultural rights ✅
C. Forced relocation
D. Ignored their status

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The agreement recognised the presence of Slovene communities and permitted limited cultural and educational provisions, but it fell short of granting extensive political autonomy, reflecting the Allies’ compromise between minority protection and the desire for stable, manageable borders.

Q16. Which clause of the treaty placed the war guilt primarily on Austria?

A. Article 2 ✅
B. Article 5
C. Article 8
D. Article 12

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Article 2 explicitly assigned responsibility for the war to Austria, mirroring the language used in the Treaty of Versailles for Germany, and providing a legal basis for reparations and the Allied powers’ justification for the imposed territorial reductions.

Q17. The prohibition against Anschluss in the treaty contributed to which later political development?

A. Rise of Austrofascism ✅
B. Formation of the European Union
C. Immediate reunification with Germany
D. Creation of a monarchy

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The restriction fostered resentment among nationalist elements in Austria, who later supported authoritarian movements such as Austrofascism, which sought to assert a distinct Austrian identity while opposing both democratic liberalism and union with Germany.

Q18. Which Allied power advocated the most stringent territorial reductions for Austria?

A. France ✅
B. Britain
C. United States
D. Japan

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

French negotiators, concerned about a revived Central European threat, pressed for the greatest possible loss of Austrian territory, influencing the final map that stripped Austria of most of its former lands and limited its strategic depth.

Q19. Before the treaty, Austria controlled approximately 676,000 km² of the former Austro‑Hungarian lands; after the treaty it retained about 83,900 km². What percentage of its former territory did Austria keep?

A. \(12.4\%\) ✅
B. \(15.3\%\)
C. \(9.8\%\)
D. \(20.1\%\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the retained area (83,900 km²) by the original extent (676,000 km²) yields roughly 0.124, which corresponds to about 12.4 % of the former empire’s territory, illustrating the dramatic territorial contraction imposed on Austria.

Q20. In what way did the economic and political constraints of the Treaty of St Germain lay the groundwork for the emergence of Austrofascism in the 1930s?

A. By fostering nationalist resentment ✅
B. By encouraging democratic reforms
C. By providing economic prosperity
D. By aligning with Soviet ideology

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The treaty’s harsh borders, limited military, and reparations generated feelings of humiliation and economic hardship, which extremist groups exploited to promote a nationalist, authoritarian agenda that culminated in the Austrofascist regime seeking to restore Austrian prestige.

Q21. If the treaty had allowed Austria a standing army of 150,000 instead of 30,000, which diplomatic scenario would have been most plausible in the early 1920s?

A. Greater influence in the League of Nations
B. Increased tension with neighboring states ✅
C. Immediate annexation by Italy
D. Neutrality in regional conflicts

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A larger army would have been perceived as a threat by Italy, Yugoslavia, and Czechoslovakia, likely prompting diplomatic protests, arms races, and heightened regional insecurity, rather than fostering cooperation or neutral status.

Q22. Legal scholars argue that the treaty violated the principle of self‑determination for Slovene minorities because:

A. It forced them into a German‑dominated state
B. It ignored plebiscite results
C. It assigned them to Italian administration without consent
D. It split their communities across borders ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The new borders divided Slovene‑speaking populations between Austria, Italy, and the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, preventing them from exercising collective self‑determination and creating minority issues that persisted throughout the interwar years.

Q23. Assess the long‑term impact of the Treaty of St Germain on Central European stability, considering the subsequent events up to 1938.

A. It created lasting peace
B. It contributed to recurring border disputes ✅
C. It led to economic integration
D. It prevented future wars

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The treaty’s arbitrary borders and minority grievances fueled nationalist tensions, territorial claims, and diplomatic frictions that resurfaced in the 1930s, ultimately culminating in the Anschluss and the broader destabilisation of Central Europe preceding World War II.
