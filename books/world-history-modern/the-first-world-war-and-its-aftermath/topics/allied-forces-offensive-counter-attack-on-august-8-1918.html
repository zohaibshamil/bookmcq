What is Allied Forces offensive counter attack on August 8 1918?


The Allied Forces offensive on August 8, 1918, refers to the Battle of Amiens, a major military operation during World War I that marked the beginning of the Hundred Days Offensive.

Example:
On this date, British, Canadian, Australian, and French troops launched a surprise attack against German lines near Amiens, France, utilizing tanks, aircraft, and infantry in coordinated assaults that advanced up to \( 12 \) kilometers on the first day.

Reason:
This offensive was significant because it demonstrated the effectiveness of combined arms tactics and marked a turning point in the war, leading German General Ludendorff to describe August 8 as the black day of the German Army, ultimately contributing to the end of World War I.

Q1. Why was August 8, 1918, termed the 'Black Day of the German Army' by General Ludendorff, considering the psychological and tactical shifts observed during the Allied offensive?

A. It marked the first use of nuclear weapons in warfare, causing unprecedented destruction.
B. It represented a catastrophic collapse in German morale and discipline as troops surrendered en masse without orders. ✅
C. It was the day Germany officially signed an armistice, ending all hostilities immediately.
D. It signified the arrival of American troops who had not previously engaged in combat.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

General Ludendorff used this term because the German army suffered a massive blow to its spirit. Unlike previous battles, soldiers surrendered willingly and in large numbers, indicating a breakdown in discipline and will to fight rather than just territorial loss. This psychological collapse signaled that victory was no longer possible for Germany.

Q2. How did the element of surprise contribute significantly to the initial success of the Allied offensive on August 8, 1918, compared to previous major battles?

A. The Allies used new radio technology to jam German communications completely.
B. They abandoned the traditional prolonged artillery bombardment, allowing them to attack before defenders could react. ✅
C. German spies provided false intelligence about the attack location, misleading their own high command.
D. The weather was so foggy that neither side could see, leading to accidental engagements.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Previous offensives relied on days of shelling that warned defenders. On August 8, the Allies used a 'creeping barrage' and limited preliminary fire. This lack of warning meant German front-line troops were overwhelmed before they could man their defenses or call for reserves, creating immediate chaos and breakthroughs.

Q3. If you were analyzing the logistical challenges faced by the Allied forces during the August 8 offensive, which factor would most likely have hindered sustained momentum after the initial breakthrough?

A. The inability to move heavy artillery and supplies forward quickly over shell-cratered terrain. ✅
B. A lack of ammunition due to factory strikes in Britain and France.
C. The refusal of colonial troops to advance beyond their home regions.
D. The German navy blocking supply lines from the United States across the Atlantic.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

While the initial attack succeeded, the rapid advance outpaced the supply lines. The devastated landscape made moving guns and food difficult. Horses struggled in the mud, and trucks were limited. This logistical lag allowed the Germans time to regroup behind new defensive lines, slowing the Allied progress despite early gains.

Q4. Compare the role of tanks in the August 8 offensive with their earlier deployments in 1916. What key tactical evolution is evident?

A. Tanks were used individually as mobile pillboxes rather than in coordinated groups.
B. They operated independently of infantry, seeking to break through lines alone.
C. Tanks were integrated closely with infantry and aircraft, providing direct support and suppressing machine-gun nests. ✅
D. They were primarily used for reconnaissance due to their mechanical unreliability.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In 1916, tanks were experimental and often used in small numbers. By August 1918, hundreds of tanks were deployed in mass formations. They worked in tandem with infantry, crushing barbed wire and neutralizing strongpoints, while aircraft provided cover. This combined arms approach maximized their effectiveness and minimized vulnerabilities.

Q5. Analyze the strategic error in the German defense strategy prior to August 8, 1918. Why did their reliance on deep defensive zones fail against the new Allied tactics?

A. The zones were too shallow, allowing easy penetration by infantry.
B. The Allies bypassed strongpoints using infiltration tactics, leaving isolated German units unable to counterattack effectively. ✅
C. German reserves were positioned too far forward, making them easy targets for artillery.
D. The German army had run out of concrete to build bunkers in the rear zones.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Germany had developed elastic defense-in-depth, expecting attackers to get bogged down. However, the Allies used infiltration tactics, ignoring strongpoints and pushing deep into rear areas. This left German forward units isolated and cut off from supplies and command, rendering their complex defensive structure ineffective against the fluid Allied advance.

Q6. Which combination of factors best explains why the August 8 offensive marked a turning point rather than just another battle in the war?

A. Superior Allied numbers alone overwhelmed the exhausted German forces.
B. The integration of air power, tanks, and improved infantry tactics broke the stalemate of trench warfare permanently. ✅
C. The entry of Italy into the war on the Allied side diverted German resources.
D. A diplomatic agreement between Russia and Germany freed up Allied troops from the Eastern Front.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

This offensive demonstrated that trench stalemate could be broken. It wasn't just numbers; it was the sophisticated coordination of different military branches. The psychological impact on Germany was profound, as their leadership realized they could no longer win militarily. This shifted the war from a contest of endurance to one of maneuver and eventual collapse.

Q7. Consider the impact of air superiority during the August 8 offensive. How did Allied control of the skies directly influence ground operations?

A. Allied pilots dropped paratroopers behind German lines to seize key bridges.
B. Fighter planes prevented German reconnaissance aircraft from spotting Allied troop movements, preserving surprise. ✅
C. Bombers destroyed all German factories in the Ruhr valley, halting production.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Control of the air blinded the enemy. German observers couldn't spot the buildup of Allied forces or direct their artillery accurately. Meanwhile, Allied planes could spot for their own guns and strafe German trenches. This information asymmetry was crucial for maintaining the element of surprise and coordinating the complex combined arms attack.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While industry provided the tools, winning required skilled execution. Coordinating tanks, infantry, artillery, and air power in real-time was a massive intellectual and organizational challenge. The success lay in the tactical ingenuity of commanders like Currie and Monash, who developed new methods to use these tools effectively, proving skill was as vital as steel.

Q9. Evaluate the significance of the Canadian Corps' role in the August 8 offensive. What specific attribute contributed most to their success at Amiens?

C. Their refusal to take prisoners, which terrified German defenders into surrendering.
D. Their exclusive use of poison gas, which cleared trenches without infantry assault.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Canadian Corps, under Currie, emphasized preparation. They built full-scale replicas of the battlefield behind lines for practice. Every soldier knew their objective. This meticulous planning reduced confusion during the actual attack, allowing them to maintain momentum and achieve objectives with fewer casualties compared to less prepared units.

Q10. Why did the German High Command fail to anticipate the scale and timing of the August 8 offensive despite having intelligence networks?

A. Allied counter-intelligence successfully fed false information about attacks elsewhere, diluting German focus. ✅
B. German spies had been arrested months earlier, leaving them blind.
C. The offensive was planned in secret by a single general without informing Allied governments.
D. Weather conditions prevented any aerial reconnaissance for weeks, hiding troop movements.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Deception played a key role. The Allies conducted feints and noisy preparations in other sectors, suggesting attacks there. This drew German reserves away from Amiens. Additionally, strict radio silence and night movements hid the true buildup. German intelligence was misled by these deliberate misdirections, causing them to underestimate the threat at the actual point of attack.

A. It led to immediate revolution and the overthrow of the Kaiser within 24 hours.

C. It strengthened the Kaiser's resolve to fight on, believing victory was still possible.
D. It caused France and Britain to offer generous peace terms immediately.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The shock of August 8 shattered the myth of invincibility. Politicians began questioning the generals' assurances. Public morale plummeted as news of defeats spread. This internal pressure forced the German leadership to consider negotiations, as continuing the war seemed futile. It started the process that eventually led to the abdication of the Kaiser and the armistice.

📖 Explanation:

Earlier, tanks were scattered thinly, making them easy targets. On August 8, hundreds were massed at Amiens. This concentration created a shockwave that punched through the line. It also allowed for mutual support among tanks. This efficient use of limited resources maximized impact, demonstrating a learning curve in mechanized warfare tactics.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Speed was the initial advantage, but it faded. As Allies moved forward, their artillery moved slower. Supplies ran low. Meanwhile, Germans rushed reserves to plug gaps. The defense stiffened. Without the element of surprise and with stretched supply lines, the Allies couldn't maintain the same pace, allowing Germans to stabilize the front and prevent a total collapse.

This contrasted with 1916, where determination was higher. The willingness to quit indicated a fundamental shift in the army's cohesion and belief in ultimate victory.

Combined arms means different units working together. On August 8, artillery suppressed enemies, tanks crushed obstacles, infantry seized ground, and planes protected from above. Q18. If you were a German commander on August 8, facing unexpected tank assaults, what immediate tactical adjustment would be most effective given limited resources?

A. Order a immediate counter-charge with infantry against the tanks.
B. Retreat to pre-prepared secondary lines and use anti-tank rifles and artillery to channel attackers into kill zones. ✅
C. Stand firm in the front trenches and hope the tanks break down mechanically.
D. Request immediate air support from the Luftwaffe, which had superior numbers.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Frontal infantry charges against tanks were suicidal. The best option was elastic defense: fall back to avoid being overrun, then use specialized weapons and artillery to target tanks as they advanced into prepared fields of fire. This preserved manpower and exploited the tanks' vulnerability to well-placed artillery, slowing the advance without wasting lives in futile resistance.

Q19. Which economic factor indirectly supported the Allied success on August 8, 1918, by ensuring steady supply of materiel?

A. The Allied naval blockade severely restricted German access to raw materials, while Allied trade routes remained open. ✅
B. Germany had superior industrial output but poor distribution networks.
C. Both sides had equal access to global markets, making economics irrelevant.
D. The US economy was in recession, reducing aid to the Allies.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

War is logistics. The British naval blockade starved Germany of rubber, oil, and metals needed for tanks and trucks. Meanwhile, Allies accessed global resources, including from the US. This disparity meant Allies could produce and sustain the massive tank and artillery buildup for August 8, while Germany struggled with shortages, weakening their ability to respond effectively.

Without this human element of leadership and flexibility, technological advantages would have been wasted in confusion and stagnation.

Q21. Synthesize the military and political implications of the August 8 offensive. How did it accelerate the end of the war?

A. It proved Germany could still win, prolonging the conflict.

C. It caused the Allies to demand unconditional surrender immediately, rejecting any negotiation.

It transformed the war from a stalemate to a rout, accelerating diplomatic efforts and ultimately leading to the November 1918 ceasefire, saving lives by ending the conflict earlier.
