What is Iran nuclear program controversy?

The Iran nuclear program controversy centers on concerns that Iran’s uranium enrichment activities could be used to develop nuclear weapons despite Tehran’s claims of peaceful energy purposes, leading to international sanctions, diplomatic negotiations like the JCPOA, and ongoing tensions over verification, compliance, and regional security implications.

Q27. Using game theory, if Iran and a neighboring state can choose Nuclear (N) or Conventional (C) with payoffs (N,N):‑5 each, (N,C):+3 for N, ‑2 for C, (C,N):‑2 for C, +3 for N, (C,C):0 each, what is the Nash equilibrium?

A.Both choose C
B.Both choose N ✅
C.Iran chooses N, neighbor chooses C
D.Iran chooses C, neighbor chooses N
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Each player’s best response to the other’s choice is to select N, because N yields a higher payoff regardless of the opponent’s move. Consequently, (N,N) is the Nash equilibrium despite the negative payoff, reflecting a classic security dilemma.

Q28. Evaluate the legal argument that Iran’s NPT signatory status forbids a covert weapons program.

D.The NPT does not address clandestine programs, so no legal breach occurs
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Article III of the NPT obliges non‑nuclear‑weapon states to accept IAEA safeguards on all nuclear material. A hidden weapons program would evade these safeguards, constituting a breach of treaty obligations and exposing Iran to potential sanctions.

Q29. If the IAEA’s annual detection probability for a hidden enrichment facility is \(0.2\), what is the chance it remains undetected after three years?

B.\(0.4\)
C.\(0.512\) ✅
D.\(0.8\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The probability of non‑detection each year is \(1‑0.2 = 0.8\). Over three independent years the chance of never being detected is \(0.8^3 = 0.512\), or roughly a \(51.2\%\) likelihood of evading discovery.
