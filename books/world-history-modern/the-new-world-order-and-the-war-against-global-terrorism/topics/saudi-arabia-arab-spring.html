What is Saudi Arabia Arab Spring?

Saudi Arabia experienced limited Arab Spring protests in 2011 driven by demands for political reform, women’s rights, and economic opportunity, but the monarchy suppressed dissent through arrests, financial concessions, and increased social spending while maintaining absolute rule, avoiding regime change but facing ongoing pressure for modernization and human rights improvements.

Q1. In which year did King Abdullah announce that women would be allowed to vote in municipal elections?

A. 2010
B. 2011 ✅
C. 2012
D. 2013

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The announcement was made in September 2011, stating that women could vote and stand as candidates in municipal elections beginning in 2015. This marked a historic shift toward gender inclusion, although other restrictions such as the driving ban remained in place at the time.

Q2. What was the new monthly minimum wage raised to after King Abdullah's reform programme?

📖 Explanation:

The reform programme increased the monthly minimum wage to £500. Raising the floor aimed to improve living standards for low‑income workers and to address high unemployment, especially among young men, while also signaling the government's responsiveness to popular discontent.

Q11. Assuming 2 million people were unemployed before the reform, what percentage reduction does the creation of 60 000 security jobs represent?

A. 0.02
B. 0.03
C. 0.05 ✅
D. 0.06

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

If 60 000 jobs are added to a pool of 2 million unemployed, the reduction is \(\frac{60\,000}{2\,000\,000}\times100 = 3\%\). However, the question asks for the percentage of the original total, which is 3 %, not 6 %. The correct answer is 3 % (option C).}

Q12. What potential macro‑economic effect might raising the minimum wage to £500 have on inflation, assuming price levels are sticky?

B. No effect
C. Moderate inflation ✅
D. Hyperinflation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Increasing the minimum wage to £500 raises household purchasing power, which can stimulate demand for goods and services. If supply cannot keep pace, firms may raise prices, leading to moderate inflation. The effect is generally limited, as price stickiness prevents rapid changes, but some upward pressure is expected.
