What is Egypt Arab Spring 2011?

Egypt’s Arab Spring erupted in January 2011 with mass protests in Tahrir Square demanding the end of Hosni Mubarak’s thirty-year dictatorship, leading to his resignation in February 2011, but subsequent political turmoil, military coups, and repression under Abdel Fattah el-Sisi reversed many democratic gains and restored authoritarian rule.

📝 All Egypt Arab Spring 2011 MCQs

Q1. In which year did Hosni Mubarak first assume the presidency of Egypt after Anwar Sadat's assassination?

A. 1979
B. 1981 ✅
C. 1983
D. 1985

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Mubarak was appointed president following Sadat's death in October 1981. This marked the beginning of a three‑decade rule that lasted until the 2011 uprising. The year 1981 is consistently referenced in academic and journalistic sources as the start of his tenure.

Q2. Which Tunisian street vendor whose self‑immolation sparked the Tunisian revolution inspired similar acts in Egypt?

A. Mohamed Bouazizi ✅
B. Ali Ghaleb
C. Ahmed Ben Ali
D. Rachid Slaoui

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Mohamed Bouazizi set himself on fire in December 2010, an act that quickly became a symbol of resistance across the Arab world. Egyptian activists emulated his protest, with a self‑immolation occurring outside Cairo’s parliament on 17 January 2011, directly referencing Bouazizi’s martyrdom.

Q3. On what date did the large‑scale protest begin in Tahrir Square, marking the start of Egypt’s revolutionary wave?

A. 25January2011 ✅
B. 15January2011
C. 30January2011
D. 1February2011

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The mass demonstration was launched on 25 January 2011 in Cairo’s Tahrir Square. This coordinated day of anger drew hundreds of thousands of citizens demanding the resignation of President Mubarak and set the stage for the subsequent weeks of civil unrest.

Q25. In a parliament of 600 members where 70% currently support the president, how many members must change their stance to achieve a simple majority (over 50%) for political reform?

B. 110
C. 121 ✅
D. 130

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A simple majority requires at least 301 members. Currently, 70% (420) back the president, leaving 180 opposed. To secure a majority for reform, 301 members must oppose the president, meaning 121 of the 180 supporters must switch sides (301 − 180 = 121).

Q27. If a protester's demand vector is represented as (economic = 1, political = 2, security = 3), what is the magnitude of this vector?
