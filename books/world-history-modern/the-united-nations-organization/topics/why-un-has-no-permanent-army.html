📝 All Why UN has no permanent army MCQs

Q1. What does the term 'permanent UN army' refer to?

🔹 A. A standing force owned and financed by the UN
🔹 B. A temporary coalition of member states for each mission
🔹 C. A reserve of civilian personnel
🔹 D. An alliance of NGOs

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A permanent UN army would be a continuously maintained, centrally funded military unit under direct UN command. The UN currently lacks such a force, relying instead on ad‑hoc contributions from member states, which limits its ability to act swiftly and independently.

Q2. In which year did the USSR ignore the UN demand for withdrawal of its troops from Hungary?

🔹 A. \(1954\)
🔹 B. \(1955\)
🔹 C. \(1956\)
🔹 D. \(1957\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Hungarian Revolution was crushed in \(1956\) after the Soviet Union refused to comply with UN calls for withdrawal. This episode illustrates how powerful states can disregard UN resolutions when they prioritize their own strategic interests over collective security mandates.

Q3. Which conflict demonstrated the UN's inability to stop a war when the warring parties were not ready to cease fighting?

🔹 A. Rwanda (1994)
🔹 B. Bosnia (1992‑1995)
🔹 C. Cambodia (1991)
🔹 D. Sierra Leone (2000)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Bosnian War highlighted the limits of UN peacekeeping when parties continue hostilities. Despite UNPROFOR's presence, the lack of a standing UN army meant the mission could not enforce cease‑fires, underscoring the reliance on member‑state troops and the challenges of compelling combatants to stop fighting.

Q4. Which two major powers initiated the 2003 invasion of Iraq without explicit UN authorization?

🔹 A. France and Russia
🔹 B. USA and Britain
🔹 C. China and India
🔹 D. Germany and Japan

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The United States and the United Kingdom led the coalition that invaded Iraq in 2003, bypassing the UN Security Council's lack of a new resolution. Their unilateral action exemplified how the absence of a permanent UN force leaves the organization powerless to prevent or halt such breaches of international law.

Q5. From where does the UN primarily obtain troops for its peacekeeping operations?

🔹 A. Its own standing army
🔹 B. Member nations volunteering forces
🔹 C. Private military contractors
🔹 D. Regional NGOs

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

UN peacekeeping relies on contributions from member states, which volunteer troops, police, and civilian staff. This dependency creates variability in capability and readiness, as the UN cannot compel contributions, making it vulnerable to geopolitical pressures and limiting its operational autonomy.

Q6. The lack of a permanent UN army most directly undermines which aspect of UN authority?

🔹 A. Legislative power
🔹 B. Enforcement capability
🔹 C. Diplomatic outreach
🔹 D. Humanitarian aid delivery

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Enforcement capability is the core function affected when the UN lacks its own standing force. Without a permanent army, the organization must negotiate troop contributions, reducing its capacity to implement resolutions, impose sanctions, or intervene decisively in conflicts.

Q7. During which decade did the UN intervene in Somalia, highlighting challenges of limited troop availability?

🔹 A. 1970s
🔹 B. 1980s
🔹 C. 1990s
🔹 D. 2000s

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN operation in Somalia, known as UNOSOM, took place primarily between \(1992\) and \(1995\). The mission struggled with insufficient troop numbers and unclear mandates, illustrating how the absence of a permanent UN force hampers rapid deployment and sustained peacekeeping efforts in crisis zones.

Q8. Which UN organ is chiefly responsible for authorizing peacekeeping missions?

🔹 A. General Assembly
🔹 B. Economic and Social Council
🔹 C. Security Council
🔹 D. International Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Security Council holds the primary authority to approve peacekeeping operations under Chapter VII of the UN Charter. Its decisions determine the mandate, size, and composition of missions, but the Council must also secure member‑state contributions, a process complicated by the lack of a permanent UN military structure.

Q9. Which article of the UN Charter outlines the principle that member states should provide armed forces for UN missions?

🔹 A. Article 2
🔹 B. Article 24
🔹 C. Article 43
🔹 D. Article 51

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Article 43 of the UN Charter obliges member states to make available to the UN such armed forces as may be required for the maintenance of international peace and security. However, the article is aspirational; the UN cannot compel contributions, leading to reliance on voluntary troop donations.

Q10. Which of the following is NOT a reason for the UN's limited enforcement capability?

🔹 A. Absence of a standing army
🔹 B. Veto power of permanent Security Council members
🔹 C. Limited budgetary resources
🔹 D. Lack of diplomatic recognition

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Lack of diplomatic recognition does not impede the UN's enforcement; the organization is universally recognized. The primary constraints are structural: no permanent army, the veto power that can block decisive action, and financial limitations that restrict the scale and speed of deployments.

Q11. A rogue state refuses to withdraw troops after a UN resolution. How might the UN most effectively respond given its lack of a permanent army?

🔹 D. Request NATO to intervene directly

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The UN typically resorts to diplomatic pressure combined with economic sanctions, while simultaneously appealing to member states for voluntary troop contributions. Direct military action without a standing force would be impractical, making coercive measures and coalition building the realistic pathways to compel compliance.

This reliance may compromise perceived impartiality, as troops may be reluctant to act against the interests of their own governments, undermining the UN's credibility.

Article 43 envisions a systematic pool of forces ready for UN deployment, yet in practice, the UN has never established such a standing cadre. The reliance on voluntary contributions means the article functions more as a principle than an enforceable rule, limiting the UN's rapid response capabilities.

The Responsibility to Protect (R2P) framework emphasizes the international community's duty to prevent mass atrocities. By invoking R2P, the UN can morally pressure reluctant states to contribute forces, highlighting the ethical imperative rather than coercive measures, which aligns with the Charter's principles.

Q20. Compare the effectiveness of ad‑hoc troop contributions versus a standing UN force in rapidly responding to emerging crises.

🔹 A. Ad‑hoc contributions are faster and more coordinated
🔹 B. Standing forces provide immediate deployment and consistent training
🔹 C. Both approaches are equally effective
🔹 D. Standing forces are less adaptable than ad‑hoc troops

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A standing UN force would be pre‑trained, equipped, and ready for rapid deployment, eliminating the delays inherent in negotiating voluntary contributions. While ad‑hoc troops bring diverse capabilities, the time required to assemble, transport, and integrate them often hampers swift crisis response, reducing overall effectiveness.

Q21. Assess the legal implications of a UN mission deploying troops without Security Council approval, given the Charter's provisions.

🔹 A. It is fully legal under Chapter VII
🔹 B. It violates the UN Charter and may be deemed unlawful
🔹 C. It is permissible if the General Assembly approves
🔹 D. It depends on the consent of the host state

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Deploying forces without Security Council authorization contravenes Article 2(4) and the Charter's requirement that the Council approve military actions. Such a deployment would lack legal legitimacy, risking accusations of illegality and potentially undermining the UN's credibility and the principle of collective security.

Creating a 100,000‑strong standing force would require substantial, recurring expenditures for training, equipment, salaries, and logistics.
