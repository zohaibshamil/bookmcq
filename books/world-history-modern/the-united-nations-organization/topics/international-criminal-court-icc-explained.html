What is International Criminal Court ICC explained?

The International Criminal Court is an independent tribunal not part of the UN system that prosecutes individuals for genocide, war crimes, crimes against humanity, and aggression, operating under the Rome Statute rather than the UN Charter.

📝 All International Criminal Court ICC explained MCQs

Q1. In which year was the Rome Statute, establishing the ICC, signed by 120 UN member states?

A. 1995
B. 1998 ✅
C. 2000
D. 2003

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Rome Statute was adopted and opened for signature on 17 July 1998. It marked a pivotal moment in international criminal law, creating a permanent court to prosecute genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity, and was later ratified by many states, though not all, including the United States.

Q2. Which city hosts the headquarters of the International Criminal Court?

A. Geneva
B. Vienna
C. The Hague ✅
D. Paris

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ICC is based in The Hague, Netherlands. This location was chosen because The Hague already hosts several major international legal institutions, such as the International Court of Justice, providing a supportive environment for the ICC’s operations and facilitating cooperation with other bodies.

Q3. Who was the first former head of state to appear before the ICC?

A. Charles Taylor
B. Slobodan Milošević ✅
C. Radovan Karadžić
D. Alberto Fujimori

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Slobodan Milošević, the former President of Yugoslavia, was the first former head of state to be tried by the ICC. His trial began in 2001, addressing alleged crimes against humanity in Bosnia, Croatia, and Kosovo, and set a historic precedent for holding leaders accountable under international law.

Q4. Which two special tribunals were established by the UN Security Council before the ICC’s creation?

A. Nuremberg and Tokyo
B. International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda and for the former Yugoslavia ✅
C. International Court of Justice and the World Trade Organization Dispute Panel
D. International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea and the International Labour Court

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The UN Security Council created the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda (ICTR) in 1994 and the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY) in 1995. These ad‑hoc tribunals paved the way for the permanent ICC by demonstrating the feasibility of prosecuting individuals for war crimes and genocide.

Q5. Which US President withdrew the United States’ signature from the Rome Statute?

A. Bill Clinton
B. George W. Bush ✅
C. Barack Obama
D. Donald Trump

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

President George W. Bush ordered the withdrawal of the United States’ signature from the Rome Statute in May 2002. The administration was concerned about the potential for US citizens, especially military personnel, to face politically motivated prosecutions before the ICC.

Q6. What is the minimum number of judges that must sit on an ICC trial chamber?

A. Three ✅
B. Five
C. Seven
D. Nine

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each trial chamber of the ICC is composed of three judges. This three‑judge panel ensures a balanced decision‑making process while allowing the court to handle multiple cases efficiently, reflecting the need for both expertise and procedural fairness in complex international trials.

Q7. If a state ratifies the Rome Statute but later withdraws, which principle of international law governs the consequences?

A. Lex posterior derogat legi priori
B. Pacta sunt servanda ✅
C. Res judicata
D. Ultra vires

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The principle of *pacta sunt servanda*—agreements must be kept—underlies treaty obligations. However, a state may withdraw in accordance with the treaty’s provisions, and the withdrawal takes effect after the prescribed notice period, affecting the state’s obligations from that point forward.

Q8. A UN peacekeeper is accused of committing war crimes in a conflict zone. Under the Rome Statute, which principle determines the court’s jurisdiction over this individual?

A. Territorial jurisdiction only
B. Personal jurisdiction based on nationality
C. Both territorial and personal jurisdiction ✅
D. No jurisdiction because of UN immunity

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Rome Statute provides for both territorial jurisdiction (crimes committed on the territory of a State Party) and personal jurisdiction (crimes committed by nationals of a State Party). Therefore, the ICC can assert jurisdiction if either condition is satisfied, even for UN peacekeepers.

Q9. Which of the following crimes is NOT within the ICC’s jurisdiction?

A. Genocide
B. Piracy on the high seas ✅
C. War crimes
D. Crimes against humanity

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity fall under the ICC’s jurisdiction, piracy on the high seas is traditionally dealt with under maritime law and is not listed as a core crime in the Rome Statute.

Q10. What is the term length for an ICC judge once elected?

A. Four years, renewable once
B. Six years, non‑renewable
C. Nine years, renewable once ✅
D. Ten years, renewable twice

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

ICC judges are elected for a term of nine years and may be re‑elected for a second term, providing continuity and expertise while ensuring periodic renewal of the bench to reflect evolving international legal standards.

Q11. A state party to the Rome Statute refuses to surrender a suspect to the ICC. Which mechanism can the ICC employ to address this non‑cooperation?

A. Issue a diplomatic note of protest
B. Invoke the principle of universal jurisdiction
C. Request a referral from the UN Security Council ✅
D. Impose sanctions through the World Bank

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

If a State Party fails to cooperate with a surrender request, the ICC may refer the matter to the UN Security Council, which can adopt measures—such as sanctions—to compel compliance, reflecting the court’s reliance on international political support for enforcement.

Q12. Which year did the ICC officially commence its operations?

A. 1998
B. 2002
C. 2003 ✅
D. 2005

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ICC was formally inaugurated on 1 July 2003, after the Rome Statute entered into force on 1 July 2002. This inauguration marked the beginning of its judicial activities, including the acceptance of cases and the establishment of its permanent structures.

Q13. How many judges compose the ICC’s Appeals Chamber?

A. Three
B. Five ✅
C. Seven
D. Nine

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The ICC’s Appeals Chamber consists of five judges. This larger panel allows for a more comprehensive review of trial decisions, ensuring that the appellate process maintains a high standard of legal scrutiny and consistency across the court’s jurisprudence.

Q14. A multinational corporation is accused of facilitating genocide. Under the ICC’s complementarity principle, when can the court intervene?

A. When the corporation is based in a non‑State Party
B. When national courts are unwilling or unable to prosecute ✅
C. Only if the corporation’s executives are nationals of a State Party
D. Never, because corporations are not natural persons

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The complementarity principle states that the ICC acts only when national jurisdictions are unwilling or genuinely unable to prosecute. If a corporation’s home state cannot or will not investigate the alleged facilitation of genocide, the ICC may step in to ensure accountability.

Q15. Which article of the Rome Statute defines the crime of genocide?

A. Article 5
B. Article 6 ✅
C. Article 7
D. Article 8

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Article 6 of the Rome Statute enumerates the core crimes, including genocide. It provides a detailed definition that aligns with the Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide, covering acts committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a protected group.

Q16. What is the primary function of the ICC’s Office of the Prosecutor?

A. Adjudicating cases
B. Drafting treaties
C. Investigating and prosecuting crimes within the court’s jurisdiction ✅
D. Providing legal aid to victims

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) is responsible for initiating investigations, gathering evidence, and prosecuting individuals accused of crimes under the ICC’s jurisdiction. It operates independently of the court’s judges to ensure impartiality and to uphold the principle of fair trial.

Q17. A former rebel leader is charged with crimes against humanity. Which procedural right guarantees his ability to challenge evidence?

A. Right to a fair trial under Article 67 ✅
B. Right to diplomatic immunity
C. Right to be tried in absentia
D. Right to self‑representation without counsel

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Article 67 of the Rome Statute guarantees the accused the right to a fair and public hearing, which includes the ability to challenge evidence, cross‑examine witnesses, and present a defense, ensuring procedural fairness in ICC proceedings.

Q18. Which of the following statements accurately reflects the United States’ relationship with the ICC?

A. The US is a full State Party
B. The US signed but never ratified the Rome Statute
C. The US withdrew its signature and does not recognize the ICC ✅
D. The US recognizes the ICC but does not contribute financially

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United States signed the Rome Statute in 2000 but later withdrew its signature in 2002 under President Bush. Consequently, the US does not recognize the ICC’s jurisdiction over its nationals, though it may cooperate on a case‑by‑case basis.

Q19. What is the minimum number of State Parties required to trigger the ICC’s jurisdiction over a crime?

A. One ✅
B. Two
C. Three
D. Five

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The ICC can exercise jurisdiction if the alleged crime occurred on the territory of a State Party or was committed by a national of a State Party. Therefore, even a single State Party’s participation is sufficient to trigger jurisdiction, provided the other criteria are met.

Q20. A victim’s family seeks reparations from the ICC. Which mechanism allows victims to receive compensation?

A. The Victims’ Trust Fund
B. The Court’s Restitution Order
C. The United Nations Compensation Committee
D. The International Criminal Court’s Victims’ Participation Programme ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The ICC’s Victims’ Participation Programme enables victims to be heard and, where appropriate, to receive reparations through restitution, compensation, or rehabilitation orders issued by the court as part of its sentencing authority.

Q21. Which of the following is a core principle guiding the ICC’s operations?

A. Doctrine of sovereignty
B. Doctrine of double jeopardy
C. Principle of complementarity ✅
D. Principle of non‑intervention

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The principle of complementarity is central to the ICC, meaning the court acts only when national jurisdictions are unable or unwilling to prosecute. This respects state sovereignty while ensuring that serious international crimes do not go unpunished.

Q22. During the Milošević trial, which legal concept was prominently debated regarding the applicability of the ICC’s jurisdiction?

A. Ex post facto law ✅
B. Retroactive criminality
C. Universal jurisdiction
D. Nullum crimen sine lege

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Milošević proceedings raised questions about the principle of *nullum crimen sine lege* (no crime without law) and whether the ICC could apply its jurisdiction retroactively to acts committed before the Rome Statute entered into force, highlighting challenges of temporal jurisdiction.

Q23. How many total judges are elected to serve on the ICC?

A. 12
B. 15
C. 18 ✅
D. 21

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ICC is composed of 18 judges elected by the Assembly of States Parties. This number is designed to ensure a diverse representation of legal traditions and geographic regions, facilitating balanced decision‑making across the court’s various chambers.

Q24. An alleged war crime occurred on the territory of a non‑State Party, but the perpetrator is a national of a State Party. Does the ICC have jurisdiction?

A. Yes, under personal jurisdiction ✅
B. No, because the territory is not a State Party
C. Yes, but only if the UN Security Council refers the case
D. Only if the crime is also classified as genocide

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The ICC’s jurisdiction can be established through personal jurisdiction if the alleged perpetrator is a national of a State Party, regardless of where the crime occurred, allowing the court to proceed even when the territorial element involves a non‑State Party.

Q25. Which organ of the United Nations can refer cases to the ICC?

A. UN General Assembly
B. UN Security Council ✅
C. UN Economic and Social Council
D. UN International Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The UN Security Council has the authority to refer situations to the ICC, as it did for the Darfur conflict. Such referrals enable the ICC to investigate and prosecute crimes that might otherwise fall outside its jurisdiction due to the lack of a State Party connection.

Q26. What is the primary source of funding for the ICC’s operations?

A. Member state contributions ✅
B. UN regular budget allocations
C. International donations from NGOs
D. Revenue from fines and penalties

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The ICC is financed primarily through assessed contributions from its State Parties, based on a scale that reflects each country’s ability to pay. This funding model ensures the court’s financial independence while allowing for supplemental voluntary contributions for specific projects.

Q27. A suspect is tried in absentia because he fled the country. Which ICC principle does this scenario potentially violate?

A. Right to be present at trial ✅
B. Right to a speedy trial
C. Right to legal representation
D. Right to appeal

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The ICC upholds the right of the accused to be present at trial, as enshrined in Article 67 of the Rome Statute. Trying a suspect in absentia would breach this fundamental principle unless the accused voluntarily waives the right, which is rarely permitted.

Q28. Which amendment to the Rome Statute allowed the ICC to exercise jurisdiction over crimes committed after its entry into force?

A. Article 12 amendment
B. Article 13 amendment ✅
C. Article 15 amendment
D. Article 17 amendment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The amendment to Article 13 extended the ICC’s temporal jurisdiction to cover crimes committed after the Statute entered into force on 1 July 2002, ensuring that the court could address offenses occurring subsequently, even if they pre‑dated the court’s formal inauguration.

Q29. What is the minimum age for criminal responsibility under the ICC’s jurisdiction?

A. 6 years old
B. 12 years old
C. 16 years old
D. There is no minimum age; individuals of any age can be prosecuted ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The ICC does not set a minimum age for criminal responsibility; its jurisdiction applies to any natural person regardless of age. However, in practice, prosecutions of children are extremely rare and would raise significant humanitarian and legal concerns.

Q30. A peacekeeping mission is accused of contributing to genocide. Which entity within the ICC would lead the investigation?

A. The Office of the Prosecutor ✅
B. The Registry
C. The Appeals Chamber
D. The Presidency

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Office of the Prosecutor (OTP) is tasked with initiating investigations into alleged crimes, including those involving peacekeeping forces. It gathers evidence, conducts legal analysis, and decides whether to bring charges before the judges of the ICC.

Q31. Which of the following best describes the ICC’s relationship with the International Labour Organization (ILO)?

A. The ICC reports to the ILO
B. The ILO provides legal advice to the ICC
C. They are separate UN agencies with distinct mandates ✅
D. The ILO funds the ICC’s operations

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ICC and ILO are independent entities within the UN system, each with its own mandate: the ICC prosecutes grave international crimes, while the ILO focuses on labor standards and workers’ rights. Their functions do not overlap, and they operate autonomously.

Q32. What is the role of the ICC’s Registry?

A. To draft statutes
B. To manage administrative and logistical support ✅
C. To adjudicate appeals
D. To conduct investigations

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Registry provides essential administrative, legal, and logistical support to the court, including witness protection, detention facilities, and court archives. Its role ensures that the ICC’s judicial processes function smoothly and that participants receive necessary services.

Q33. A State Party argues that the ICC lacks jurisdiction because the alleged crime occurred before the Rome Statute entered into force. Which principle counters this argument?

A. Lex specialis derogat legi generali
B. Non‑retroactivity of criminal law
C. Temporal jurisdiction under Article 12(2) ✅
D. Doctrine of sovereign immunity

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Article 12(2) of the Rome Statute provides that the ICC may exercise jurisdiction over crimes committed after the Statute’s entry into force, ensuring that the court can address offenses that occur subsequently, even if the crime began earlier.

Q34. Which article of the Rome Statute outlines the rights of victims to participate in proceedings?

A. Article 46
B. Article 49 ✅
C. Article 55
D. Article 68

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Article 49 guarantees victims the right to participate in ICC proceedings, including the ability to present their views and concerns, submit evidence, and seek reparations, thereby enhancing the court’s commitment to victim‑centered justice.
