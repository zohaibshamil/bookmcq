Q1. In which year did the Hungarian Rising occur?

🔹 A. \(1948\)
🔹 B. \(1956\)
🔹 C. \(1962\)
🔹 D. \(1970\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The uprising took place in the summer of \(1956\), when Hungarians attempted to break free from Soviet domination, leading to a brief but intense revolt that was ultimately suppressed by Soviet forces.

Q2. Which superpower vetoed the UN Security Council resolution demanding Soviet withdrawal from Hungary?

🔹 A. United States
🔹 B. United Kingdom
🔹 C. Soviet Union
🔹 D. France

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet Union used its permanent member veto power to block the resolution calling for the withdrawal of its troops, demonstrating how Cold War politics could paralyze UN action in crises such as the Hungarian revolt.

Q3. What was the primary reason the United Nations could not enforce its resolution on Hungary?

🔹 A. Lack of funding
🔹 B. Soviet non‑cooperation
🔹 C. Insufficient troops
🔹 D. Geographical distance

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet Union simply refused to cooperate with the UN‑appointed investigative committee, and without Soviet compliance the UN lacked the leverage to compel withdrawal, showcasing its limited capacity when a major power opposes its actions.

Q4. Which body of the UN passed a resolution supporting Hungarian independence after the Security Council was vetoed?

🔹 A. Economic and Social Council
🔹 B. General Assembly
🔹 C. International Court of Justice
🔹 D. Security Council

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The General Assembly, representing all member states, adopted a resolution condemning the Soviet intervention and called for Hungarian self‑determination, although it lacked enforcement mechanisms compared to the Security Council.

Q5. Which two Western powers chose to bow to international pressure during the Suez Crisis, contrasting with Soviet behavior in Hungary?

🔹 A. United States and Canada
🔹 B. United Kingdom and France
🔹 C. Germany and Italy
🔹 D. Netherlands and Belgium

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

During the Suez Crisis, Britain and France capitulated to global diplomatic pressure, whereas the Soviet Union ignored UN decisions regarding Hungary, highlighting differing approaches to international norms among the Cold War powers.

Q6. What was the immediate outcome of the Soviet troops' intervention in Hungary?

🔹 A. Establishment of a democratic government
🔹 B. Full independence from the USSR
🔹 C. Crushing of the revolt and reinstatement of a pro‑Soviet regime
🔹 D. Division of Hungary into two states

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Soviet forces swiftly moved in, suppressing the uprising, arresting leaders, and re‑installing a communist government aligned with Moscow, effectively ending the brief period of attempted autonomy.

Q7. Which Hungarian leader became a symbol of resistance after the 1956 uprising?

📖 Explanation:

Imre Nagy, the reformist prime minister, attempted to withdraw Hungary from the Warsaw Pact and was later executed after the Soviet crackdown, becoming a martyr for Hungarian freedom.

Q8. How did the UN's inability to intervene in Hungary affect its credibility during the 1950s?

📖 Explanation:

The failure to act against Soviet aggression illustrated the UN's dependence on consensus among great powers, reducing its perceived effectiveness in confronting Cold War confrontations.

Q9. What was the approximate number of Soviet troops deployed to suppress the Hungarian uprising?

🔹 B. \(15,000\)
🔹 C. \(30,000\)
🔹 D. \(50,000\)

📖 Explanation:

Historical records estimate that around \(30,000\) Soviet soldiers entered Hungary, overwhelming the relatively small Hungarian resistance and ensuring rapid suppression of the revolt.

Q10. If you were a UN diplomat in 1956 tasked with drafting a resolution, which phrase would best reflect the UN's principle of self‑determination?

🔹 A. \Support the Soviet Union's security interests\
🔹 B. \Affirm the right of peoples to freely determine their political status\
🔹 C. \Encourage economic sanctions against Hungary\
🔹 D. \Mandate a joint NATO‑UN intervention\

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A resolution emphasizing the right of peoples to freely determine their political status aligns with the UN Charter's Article 1(2). Including such language would reinforce the moral basis for supporting Hungarian aspirations while respecting sovereign equality.

Q11. Analyze why the Soviet veto in the Security Council was more decisive than the earlier British‑French bow in the Suez Crisis. Which factor was most critical?

🔹 A. Economic sanctions capability
🔹 B. Military superiority of the USSR
🔹 C. UN procedural rules granting veto power
🔹 D. Public opinion in Europe

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN Charter gives permanent members a veto, enabling any one of them to block resolutions regardless of broader consensus. This procedural authority made the Soviet veto decisive, whereas Britain and France could be pressured into compliance through diplomatic and economic channels.

Q12. Consider the following A UN committee is denied access by a state. Which diplomatic tool could the UN most effectively use to compel cooperation?

🔹 A. Direct military intervention
🔹 B. Economic sanctions approved by the General Assembly
🔹 C. Public condemnation through the media
🔹 D. Invitation to a peace conference

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While the UN cannot enforce military action without Security Council approval, it can impose sanctions if the General Assembly passes a resolution, applying economic pressure that may persuade the non‑cooperative state to engage with the committee.

Q13. Which of the following best explains the contrast between the UN's response to the Suez Crisis and the Hungarian Rising?

🔹 A. Both crises were resolved by UN peacekeepers
🔹 B. The UN had more member state support for Suez than for Hungary
🔹 C. The Soviet Union was a permanent Security Council member, giving it veto power
🔹 D. The UN lacked any authority in both cases

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Soviet membership and veto power meant that any resolution against its actions could be blocked, unlike the Suez situation where Britain and France were not permanent members and thus could be pressured without such a veto.

📖 Explanation:

Q15. If the UN had successfully deployed peacekeepers to Hungary, which of the following outcomes would have been most likely?

A credible UN peacekeeping presence could have created a buffer, encouraging negotiations that might have allowed Hungary to retain some degree of autonomy while avoiding outright Soviet re‑occupation.

Q16. Which legal principle does the UN Charter invoke when a member state uses force against another member's internal affairs?

🔹 A. Principle of non‑intervention
🔹 B. Doctrine of collective security
🔹 C. Right of self‑defense
🔹 D. Responsibility to protect

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Article 2(4) of the UN Charter prohibits the threat or use of force against the territorial integrity or political independence of any state, establishing a legal basis for condemning unilateral interventions like the Soviet action in Hungary.

Q17. Evaluate the impact of the Hungarian uprising on subsequent Cold War diplomacy. Which development was directly influenced?

🔹 C. Increased Soviet willingness to use force in satellite states
🔹 D. Détente policies of the 1970s

📖 Explanation:

The brutal suppression reinforced the Soviet Union's resolve to maintain tight control over Eastern Bloc countries, leading to a more hard‑line stance that persisted until later periods of détente, rather than prompting immediate diplomatic liberalization.

📖 Explanation:

Without a veto, the Security Council could have passed a resolution authorizing collective action, potentially allowing a multinational force to intervene and pressure Soviet troops to withdraw, altering the outcome significantly.
