📝 All UN peacekeeping Somalia MCQs

Q1. In which year did 37,000 UN troops arrive in Somalia to safeguard aid?

🔹 A. 1992
🔹 B. 1993
🔹 C. 1994
🔹 D. 1995

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations deployed a substantial force of 37,000 troops in December 1992 to protect humanitarian operations amid the chaos of civil war, marking a significant international intervention aimed at stabilizing the region and ensuring the delivery of crucial aid supplies.

Q2. Who was overthrown in 1991, leading to the Somali civil war?

🔹 A. Mohamed Siad
🔹 B. Siad Barre
🔹 C. Ali Mohammed
🔹 D. Aidid

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The long‑standing dictator Siad Barre was ousted in 1991, an event that precipitated the fragmentation of central authority and ignited a protracted civil conflict among competing warlords, fundamentally reshaping Somalia’s political landscape and triggering massive humanitarian crises.

Q3. Which two generals were involved in the power struggle after Siad Barre’s overthrow?

🔹 A. Siad and Aidid
🔹 B. Ali and Siad
🔹 C. Aidid and Ali Mohammed
🔹 D. None of the above

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Following the collapse of Siad Barre’s regime, General Mohamed Farah Aidid and General Ali Mohamed Osoble (Ali Mohammed) emerged as rival leaders, each commanding distinct militia factions, whose competition for control intensified the violence and complicated efforts to restore peace and order.

Q4. How many UN troops were initially deployed to Somalia in December 1992?

🔹 A. 30000
🔹 B. 35000
🔹 C. 40000
🔹 D. 37000

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations launched a large‑scale operation in December 1992, committing exactly 37,000 troops—primarily from the United States—to the Somali theater, with the objective of protecting humanitarian convoys and attempting to disarm entrenched warlords.

Q5. When did American troops withdraw from Somalia?

🔹 A. Mar-95
🔹 B. Mar-94
🔹 C. Dec-92
🔹 D. Jan-93

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

U.S. forces, which formed the core of the initial UN contingent, began pulling out of Somalia in March 1994 after sustaining casualties and facing heightened hostility, signaling a shift in the international commitment to the mission.

Q6. Which organization requested UN assistance for Somalia?

🔹 A. NATO
🔹 B. AU
🔹 C. OAU
🔹 D. ECOWAS

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Organization of African Unity (OAU) formally appealed to the United Nations for intervention, prompting the deployment of a multinational peacekeeping force to address the escalating humanitarian emergency and the breakdown of law and order in Somalia.

Q7. If 15% of the 37,000 UN troops suffered casualties, how many troops were affected?

🔹 A. 5550
🔹 B. 5600
🔹 C. 5700
🔹 D. 5800

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Calculating 15 % of 37,000 yields \(0.15 \times 37{,}000 = 5{,}550\) soldiers, indicating the scale of losses endured by the peacekeepers and underscoring the severe risks associated with operating in a hostile environment without clear consent from local factions.

Q8. Why was the UN mission in Somalia considered an impossible task?

🔹 A. Because the warlords were too powerful
🔹 B. Because the terrain was unsuitable
🔹 C. Because there was no humanitarian need
🔹 D. Because the UN lacked funding

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The mission's difficulty stemmed from the entrenched power of the warlords, whose armed factions were both well‑organized and deeply motivated to retain weapons, rendering any attempt at comprehensive disarmament highly unrealistic without a decisive political framework.

Q9. Which earlier UN interventions are cited as successful when troops supported one side?

🔹 A. Korea 1950‑3
🔹 B. Gulf War 1991
🔹 C. Rwanda 1994
🔹 D. Bosnia 1995

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The text highlights the United Nations’ effectiveness in the Korean War (1950‑1953) and the 1991 Gulf War, where forces explicitly backed one belligerent, illustrating that clear alignment with a legitimate party can enhance the prospects of mission success.

Q10. UN troops had the most chance of success when they:

🔹 A. Remained neutral
🔹 B. Focused on humanitarian aid only
🔹 C. Supported one side against the other
🔹 D. Engaged in nation‑building

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Historical analysis shows that UN operations achieve higher effectiveness when they actively support a recognized side, as this creates a clear mandate, reduces ambiguity, and allows forces to concentrate resources toward a decisive strategic objective.

Q11. How many years elapsed between the UN arrival in December 1992 and withdrawal in March 1995?

🔹 A. 2 years
🔹 B. 2.25 years
🔹 C. 3 years
🔹 D. 3.5 years

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

From December 1992 to March 1995 spans 27 months, which converts to \(27 \div 12 = 2.25\) years, reflecting the relatively brief duration of the UN’s direct engagement despite the protracted nature of the Somali conflict.

Q12. What were the two main tasks the UN tried to combine in Somalia?

🔹 A. Medical aid and infrastructure building
🔹 B. Disarmament of warlords and humanitarian relief
🔹 C. Political elections and education reform
🔹 D. Security patrols and trade negotiations

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations attempted to simultaneously disarm the powerful warlord factions and deliver essential humanitarian assistance, a dual‑objective strategy that proved exceptionally challenging given the volatile security context and divergent interests of local actors.

Q13. If each UN soldier required 3 L of water per day, how much water did 37,000 troops need daily?

🔹 A. 100,000 L
🔹 B. 111,000 L
🔹 C. 120,000 L
🔹 D. 130,000 L

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Multiplying 37,000 soldiers by 3 L each yields \(37{,}000 \times 3 = 111{,}000\) L of water per day, illustrating the substantial logistical burden of sustaining a large peacekeeping contingent in a resource‑scarce environment.

Q14. Which warlord was most resistant to disarmament?

🔹 A. Aidid

🔹 C. Siad Barre
🔹 D. Hussein Mohamed

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

General Mohamed Farah Aidid, commanding a substantial militia, vocally opposed UN attempts to confiscate weapons, making him the primary obstacle to the disarmament component of the mission and a focal point of subsequent confrontations with peacekeepers.

Q15. In what year did the Rwandan genocide occur, which the UN did not address while in Somalia?

🔹 A. 1994
🔹 B. 1995
🔹 C. 1993
🔹 D. 1992

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The tragic genocide in Rwanda unfolded in 1994, a period during which the United Nations was concurrently engaged in the Somali operation, leading to criticism that resources and attention were diverted away from the urgent crisis in Rwanda.

Q16. Which factor most contributed to the UN’s mission being deemed impossible?

🔹 A. Lack of clear mandate
🔹 B. Insufficient troops
🔹 C. Hostile climate
🔹 D. Poor media coverage

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The absence of a definitive, enforceable mandate to compel warlord disarmament meant that UN forces operated without the legal authority needed to compel compliance, rendering the core objective unattainable regardless of troop numbers or logistical support.

Q17. If each unit of 1,000 soldiers could secure 10 km of supply route, how many kilometers could 37,000 troops secure?

🔹 A. 300 km
🔹 B. 350 km
🔹 C. 370 km
🔹 D. 400 km

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Dividing 37,000 by 1,000 gives 37 units; multiplying 37 units by 10 km per unit results in \(37 \times 10 = 370\) km of secured supply lines, highlighting the limited reach of the force relative to the extensive terrain.

Q18. Compared with the Gulf War, what key difference is noted about UN involvement in Somalia?

🔹 A. UN supported both sides equally
🔹 B. UN supported one side decisively
🔹 C. UN remained neutral
🔹 D. UN withdrew before fighting began

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

In the Gulf War, UN forces backed a clear coalition, whereas in Somalia the mission attempted neutrality and simultaneous disarmament, a strategy that lacked the decisive alignment that historically contributed to UN success in other conflicts.

Q19. Assuming casualties were uniformly distributed, how many American casualties occurred if 5% of total troops were killed?

🔹 A. 1000
🔹 B. 1110
🔹 C. 1200
🔹 D. 1300

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Total casualties equal \(0.05 \times 37{,}000 = 1{,}850\). With American troops comprising 60 % of the force (\(0.60 \times 37{,}000 = 22{,}200\)), their share of casualties is \(0.05 \times 22{,}200 = 1{,}110\), illustrating the disproportionate impact on U.S. personnel.

Q20. Why did the UN not intervene in Rwanda while engaged in Somalia, according to resource allocation theory?

🔹 D. Because of political pressure from the US

📖 Explanation:

Resource allocation theory suggests that the UN prioritized the ongoing Somali operation, allocating most logistical, financial, and personnel assets there, which left insufficient capacity to launch a parallel large‑scale intervention in Rwanda despite the grave humanitarian emergency.
