Q1. In which year was the United Nations officially founded?

🔹 A. 1945
🔹 B. 1946
🔹 C. 1947
🔹 D. 1948

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations was established after the end of World War II, with the Charter coming into force on 24 October 1945. This date marks the formal beginning of the organization, which was created to replace the ineffective League of Nations and to promote lasting international peace.

Q2. Which city hosted the conference where the UN Charter was drafted?

🔹 A. London
🔹 B. San Francisco
🔹 C. New York
🔹 D. Paris

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations Charter was drafted at the United Nations Conference on International Organization held in San Francisco from April to June 1945. Delegates from the major Allied powers and other nations convened there to negotiate the text that would become the foundational document of the UN.

Q3. How many principal organs does the United Nations have according to the Charter?

🔹 A. Five
🔹 B. Six
🔹 C. Seven
🔹 D. Eight

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN Charter defines seven principal organs: the General Assembly, the Security Council, the Secretariat, the International Court of Justice, the Economic and Social Council, the Trusteeship Council, and the International Criminal Court (added later). This structure reflects the organization’s broad mandate across political, legal, and socioeconomic domains.

Q4. Which organ is primarily responsible for maintaining international peace and security?

🔹 A. General Assembly
🔹 B. Economic and Social Council
🔹 C. Security Council
🔹 D. International Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Security Council holds the principal responsibility for maintaining international peace and security. It can adopt binding resolutions, impose sanctions, and authorize the use of force. Its unique composition, including five permanent members with veto power, gives it a distinctive role among the UN organs.

Q5. What is the minimum voting requirement for the General Assembly to admit a new member state?

🔹 A. Simple majority
🔹 B. Two‑thirds majority
🔹 C. Unanimous vote
🔹 D. Three‑quarter majority

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

According to the UN Charter, a new member may be admitted by a two‑thirds majority of the General Assembly, provided the recommendation comes from the Security Council. However, the voting threshold for the recommendation itself is a two‑thirds majority, making the overall process require broad support.

Q6. Which language is NOT one of the six official UN languages?

🔹 A. German
🔹 B. English
🔹 C. Russian
🔹 D. Spanish

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The six official languages of the United Nations are English, French, Russian, Chinese, Spanish, and Arabic. German, while widely spoken, is not among the official languages, and therefore UN documents are not routinely translated into German.

Q7. Which organ was established most recently, in 2003?

🔹 A. Security Council
🔹 B. International Criminal Court
🔹 C. Trusteeship Council
🔹 D. Economic and Social Council

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The International Criminal Court (ICC) was inaugurated in July 2002, but the UN formally recognized it as a subsidiary organ in March 2003. It differs from the International Court of Justice, which is the principal judicial organ established by the original Charter.

Q8. How many permanent members sit on the UN Security Council?

🔹 A. Four
🔹 B. Five
🔹 C. Six
🔹 D. Seven

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The UN Security Council has five permanent members: the United States, the United Kingdom, France, Russia, and China. These powers hold veto authority, which allows any one of them to block substantive resolutions, shaping the Council’s decision‑making dynamics.

Q9. What is the primary function of the UN Trusteeship Council today?

🔹 A. Overseeing peacekeeping missions
🔹 B. Managing decolonization processes
🔹 C. Supervising trust territories
🔹 D. It has suspended operations

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Originally created to supervise trust territories and guide them toward self‑government, the Trusteeship Council completed its work as the last trust territory attained independence. Consequently, the Council has formally suspended its operations, reflecting the achievement of its original mandate.

Q10. Which organ can issue advisory opinions on legal questions?

🔹 A. Security Council
🔹 B. International Court of Justice
🔹 C. General Assembly
🔹 D. Economic and Social Council

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The International Court of Justice (ICJ) may issue advisory opinions when requested by the General Assembly or the Security Council. These opinions, while not binding, provide authoritative legal guidance on international law issues presented by UN bodies.

Q11. What proportion of UN member states are classified as developing countries?

🔹 A. Approximately \( \frac{3}{4} \)
🔹 B. Approximately \( \frac{1}{2} \)
🔹 C. Approximately \( \frac{2}{3} \)
🔹 D. Approximately \( \frac{5}{6} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

As of the latest data, roughly two‑thirds of UN member states are considered developing nations. This reflects the organization’s focus on supporting economic and social progress in less‑industrialized regions, aligning with its broader goals of reducing global inequality.

Q12. If a resolution in the General Assembly receives 120 votes in favor, 30 against, and 10 abstentions, does it meet the two‑thirds majority requirement?

🔹 A. Yes, because \( \frac{120}{160}=0.75 \)
🔹 B. No, because abstentions count as negatives
🔹 C. Yes, because any majority suffices
🔹 D. No, because a simple majority is required

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To determine a two‑thirds majority, abstentions are excluded from the total count. The effective voting total is 150 (120 + 30). The proportion in favor is \( \frac{120}{150}=0.80 \), exceeding the required \( \frac{2}{3}=0.667 \). Hence the resolution meets the two‑thirds threshold.

Q13. Which of the following best describes the principle of ‘collective security’ as envisioned by the UN Charter?

🔹 A. All nations act independently to protect themselves
🔹 B. A coalition of states intervenes only after a UN vote
🔹 C. Individual states are obligated to defend any member attacked
🔹 D. Security Council members must provide troops to every mission

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Collective security under the UN Charter implies that an attack against one member is considered an attack against all, obligating other members to assist, typically through the Security Council’s coordinated actions. This principle aims to deter aggression by ensuring a unified response.

Q14. A regional conflict escalates, and the Security Council is deadlocked due to a veto. Which UN organ could still take action to address humanitarian needs?

🔹 A. Security Council
🔹 B. General Assembly
🔹 C. International Court of Justice
🔹 D. Secretariat

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When the Security Council cannot act because of a veto, the General Assembly may invoke the ‘Uniting for Peace’ resolution, allowing it to recommend collective measures, including humanitarian assistance. Although its resolutions are not binding, the Assembly can mobilize resources and political pressure to alleviate suffering.

Q15. Which organ is primarily responsible for promoting sustainable development goals (SDGs)?

🔹 A. International Court of Justice
🔹 B. Economic and Social Council
🔹 C. Security Council
🔹 D. Trusteeship Council

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) coordinates the UN’s work on economic, social, and environmental issues, making it the lead organ for advancing the Sustainable Development Goals. It facilitates policy dialogue, monitors progress, and partners with specialized agencies to implement the 2030 agenda.

Q16. If the UN budget is allocated based on a formula where a country’s contribution equals \( 0.5\% \) of its Gross National Income (GNI) plus \( 0.1\% \) of its population, which factor has a larger impact for a nation with a small GNI but large population?

🔹 A. The GNI component
🔹 B. The population component
🔹 C. Both equally
🔹 D. Neither, the formula is fixed

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When a country's GNI is low, the \( 0.5\% \) of GNI contributes minimally to the overall contribution. Conversely, a large population amplifies the \( 0.1\% \) term, making the population component the dominant factor in determining the total assessed contribution.

Q17. Which of the following is a limitation of the UN’s peacekeeping operations?

🔹 A. They can enforce domestic laws
🔹 B. They rely on voluntary troop contributions
🔹 C. They have unlimited funding
🔹 D. They operate without host‑nation consent

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

UN peacekeeping missions depend on member states to provide troops, police, and equipment on a voluntary basis. This reliance can lead to delays, uneven capabilities, and political constraints, limiting the effectiveness and rapid deployment of peacekeeping forces.

Q18. A member state alleges that another state violated a treaty on marine resources. Which UN body is best suited to adjudicate this dispute?

🔹 A. Security Council
🔹 B. International Court of Justice
🔹 C. General Assembly
🔹 D. Economic and Social Council

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The International Court of Justice has jurisdiction over legal disputes between states, including treaty violations. If both parties consent to its jurisdiction, the ICJ can issue a binding judgment on the marine resource treaty breach.

Q19. What is the primary purpose of the UN’s ‘Human Rights Council’?

🔹 A. To enforce sanctions
🔹 B. To draft economic policies
🔹 C. To promote and protect human rights globally
🔹 D. To manage UN finances

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Human Rights Council, a subsidiary organ of the General Assembly, focuses on strengthening the promotion and protection of human rights worldwide. It conducts universal periodic reviews, addresses violations, and makes recommendations to member states and UN bodies.

Q20. Climate‑induced migration increases sharply. Which UN organ should lead a coordinated response involving refugees, development, and environmental policy?

🔹 A. Security Council
🔹 B. General Assembly
🔹 C. Economic and Social Council
🔹 D. International Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) integrates efforts across development, humanitarian, and environmental domains. It can convene relevant agencies, such as UNHCR and UNEP, to formulate comprehensive strategies addressing climate‑driven migration.

Q21. Which organ has the authority to appoint the UN Secretary‑General?

🔹 A. Security Council alone
🔹 B. General Assembly alone
🔹 C. Both Security Council and General Assembly
🔹 D. Economic and Social Council

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Secretary‑General is appointed by the General Assembly upon recommendation of the Security Council. This dual‑step process ensures that both the principal political organ and the plenary body have a role in selecting the organization’s chief administrative officer.

Q22. A non‑governmental organization (NGO) wants to influence UN policy on cyber‑security. Through which mechanism can it most effectively participate?

🔹 A. Apply for Security Council membership
🔹 B. Submit a petition to the International Court of Justice
🔹 C. Obtain consultative status with the Economic and Social Council
🔹 D. Request a seat in the General Assembly

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

NGOs can acquire consultative status with ECOSOC, granting them the ability to attend meetings, submit statements, and contribute expertise. This pathway enables NGOs to influence UN deliberations on specialized topics such as cyber‑security.

Q23. Which of the following best explains why the UN often struggles to enforce its resolutions?

🔹 A. Lack of a standing army
🔹 B. Excessive funding
🔹 C. Overreliance on judicial decisions
🔹 D. Frequent changes in leadership

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The UN does not possess its own armed forces; it relies on member states to provide troops for peacekeeping or enforcement actions. This dependence can hinder swift implementation of resolutions, especially when political will or resources are lacking among contributors.

Q24. A member state refuses to pay its assessed contribution, citing economic hardship. Which principle does the UN invoke to encourage compliance?

🔹 A. Sovereign immunity
🔹 B. Collective responsibility
🔹 C. Non‑intervention
🔹 D. Universal jurisdiction

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The principle of collective responsibility underpins the UN budgetary system: all members share the financial burden proportionally. When a state defaults, the organization may appeal to solidarity and, if necessary, impose penalties or restrict participation to encourage prompt payment.

Q25. Which organ was effectively rendered obsolete with the decolonization process?

🔹 A. Security Council
🔹 B. Trusteeship Council
🔹 C. Economic and Social Council
🔹 D. International Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Trusteeship Council was created to oversee trust territories and guide them toward self‑governance. With the completion of decolonization and the last trust territory achieving independence, the Council suspended its activities, making it largely obsolete.

Q26. A cyber‑attack targets critical UN infrastructure, disrupting communications. Which organ should coordinate the immediate response?

🔹 A. Security Council
🔹 B. Secretariat
🔹 C. General Assembly
🔹 D. International Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Secretariat, led by the Secretary‑General, manages the UN’s day‑to‑day operations and would coordinate technical and security responses to protect its infrastructure, ensuring continuity of essential services.

Q27. Imagine a future where AI‑driven autonomous weapons are widely deployed. Which UN body should be tasked with drafting binding regulations, and what challenges might it face?

🔹 A. Security Council; political vetoes
🔹 B. General Assembly; lack of enforcement power
🔹 C. Economic and Social Council; limited technical expertise
🔹 D. International Court of Justice; jurisdictional limits

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Security Council would likely spearhead negotiations for binding AI‑weapon regulations due to its mandate over international peace and security. However, the permanent members’ veto power could stall consensus, and divergent national interests on AI development may impede the formulation of universally acceptable rules.

📖 Explanation:

The Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) is best positioned to orchestrate a multi‑sectoral response, linking WHO’s health interventions with economic relief measures and educational continuity plans. Its mandate to foster cooperation across social and economic domains enables a comprehensive strategy that aligns health security with socioeconomic stability.

📖 Explanation:

ECOSOC can establish a dedicated climate fund and convene expert panels to prioritize the island’s vulnerabilities, demonstrating tangible support. By addressing the nation’s specific concerns through targeted resources, the UN reinforces its relevance and encourages continued participation.

Q30. Two rival blocs within the UN each propose different reforms to the veto power. Design an analytical framework the General Assembly could use to evaluate the proposals’ impact on global governance. Which factors should be included?

🔹 A. Military capability, economic size, historical alliances
🔹 B. Number of member states, voting thresholds, decision‑making speed, legitimacy perception

An analytical framework should assess how reforms affect the number of member states influencing decisions, the required voting thresholds for adoption, the speed at which the Council can act, and the perceived legitimacy of outcomes. By quantifying these dimensions, the General Assembly can compare proposals objectively and gauge their implications for equitable global governance.

Q31. A cyber‑war between two major powers threatens UN communications. Recommend a protocol for the UN to maintain operational continuity, citing relevant organ responsibilities. Which organ should lead implementation?

🔹 C. Economic and Social Council; coordinate economic impacts
🔹 D. International Court of Justice; adjudicate damages

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Secretariat, responsible for the UN’s administrative and logistical functions, should develop and enforce a robust cyber‑resilience protocol, including redundant communication systems, rapid incident response teams, and coordination with member states’ cyber agencies to ensure uninterrupted operations during conflicts.

Q32. In 2035, a coalition of emerging economies seeks to reform the UN’s funding model to reflect shifting economic power. Outline a step‑by‑step plan the General Assembly could adopt, and discuss potential obstacles.

🔹 A. Propose a new charter amendment; hold a Security Council vote

🔹 C. Request the International Court of Justice to rule on fairness
🔹 D. Ask the Secretariat to reallocate existing funds

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The General Assembly could first commission an expert study to evaluate current contributions versus economic realities, then draft a revised assessment formula that more accurately reflects GDP and capacity. Subsequent negotiations would aim for consensus among member states, followed by Security Council endorsement to ensure legitimacy, though political resistance and concerns over reduced influence may impede progress.

📖 Explanation:

ECOSOC, in partnership with the World Health Organization and the COVAX facility, can orchestrate a coordinated response that aligns health objectives with economic and social considerations, facilitating equitable vaccine allocation based on need rather than wealth, while leveraging its convening power to mobilize resources from diverse member states.
