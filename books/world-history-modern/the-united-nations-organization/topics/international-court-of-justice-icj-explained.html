What is International Court of Justice ICJ explained?

The International Court of Justice is the principal judicial organ of the UN that settles legal disputes between states and provides advisory opinions on international law, but it cannot prosecute individuals or enforce its rulings without state consent.

📝 All International Court of Justice ICJ explained MCQs

Q1. How many judges serve on the International Court of Justice?

A. 9
B. 12
C. 15 ✅
D. 18

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Statute of the International Court of Justice specifies that the Court shall be composed of fifteen judges. This number was chosen to ensure a broad representation of the world's legal systems while keeping the bench manageable for deliberations and decision‑making.

Q2. What is the length of a single term for an ICJ judge?

A. 666 years
B. \)9 years ✅
C. 121212 years
D. 151515 years

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each judge is elected for a term of nine years, as set out in the Court's founding statute. The long term is intended to safeguard judicial independence and allow judges to develop expertise on complex international legal issues.

Q3. How many judges retire every third year?

A. Three
B. Four
C. Five ✅
D. Six

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Court's design includes a staggered retirement system where five judges step down every three years. This mechanism provides continuity while allowing periodic renewal of the bench, preventing a complete turnover at any single point in time.

Q4. In which city is the International Court of Justice located?

A. Geneva
B. Vienna
C. The Hague ✅
D. Paris

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The International Court of Justice is seated in the Peace Palace at The Hague, Netherlands. The location was chosen after World War II to symbolize a commitment to peaceful settlement of disputes and to provide a neutral ground for all member states.

Q5. Which two UN bodies elect ICJ judges jointly?

A. General Assembly and Economic and Social Council
B. Security Council and General Assembly ✅
C. Security Council and International Monetary Fund
D. General Assembly and World Health Organization

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Judges are elected by both the United Nations General Assembly and the Security Council, each voting independently. A candidate must receive an absolute majority in both organs to be appointed, ensuring broad support across the UN system.

Q6. The ICJ primarily settles disputes between which entities?

A. Individuals
B. Non‑governmental organizations
C. States ✅
D. Corporations

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Court's jurisdiction is limited to legal disputes between sovereign states. It does not entertain cases brought by private individuals, NGOs, or corporations, reflecting its role as the principal judicial organ for inter‑state matters under international law.

Q7. Which UN organ theoretically has power to enforce ICJ decisions?

A. General Assembly
B. Security Council ✅
C. Economic and Social Council
D. UN Secretariat

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Under the UN Charter, the Security Council can take ‘appropriate measures’ to enforce judgments of the International Court of Justice, particularly when a decision threatens international peace and security. In practice, this power has rarely been exercised.

Q8. Which of the following pairs had a successful ICJ frontier dispute?

A. Holland and Belgium ✅
B. Britain and Norway
C. USA and Nicaragua
D. France and Germany

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

One of the early notable cases involved a boundary dispute between the Netherlands (Holland) and Belgium, which the Court resolved successfully, establishing a precedent for peaceful territorial adjudication.

Q9. In the 1984 Nicaragua‑USA case, what did the ICJ order the USA to do?

A. Withdraw troops
B. Pay compensation ✅
C. Apologize publicly
D. Close its embassies

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Court found that the United States had violated international law by mining Nicaraguan harbours and ordered the United States to pay reparations to Nicaragua. The decision highlighted the Court's authority to award monetary damages in cases of unlawful acts.

Q10. The ICJ can render a binding judgment only when:

A. Both parties consent to jurisdiction ✅
B. The Security Council votes
C. The General Assembly approves
D. A majority of judges concur

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Court's jurisdiction is based on the consent of the states involved. Without mutual agreement to submit the dispute to the Court, any judgment it issues would lack the necessary legal foundation to be considered binding.

Q11. If State X and State Y submit a maritime boundary dispute to the ICJ, which step must occur before the Court can hear the case?

A. Both states must sign a treaty
B. Both states must accept the Court’s jurisdiction ✅
C. The Security Council must refer the case
D. The General Assembly must approve the docket

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Jurisdiction over a dispute requires that each state expressly accepts the Court's authority, either through a prior treaty provision or a declaration of consent. Only after both parties have consented can the Court proceed to examine the merits of the case.

Q12. Which of the following best explains why the United States refused to comply with the ICJ’s 1984 judgment against it?

A. The Court lacked jurisdiction
B. The decision conflicted with domestic law
C. The Security Council did not enforce the ruling
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The United States argued that the Court had exceeded its jurisdiction, that the judgment conflicted with its internal legal framework, and pointed to the absence of any Security Council enforcement action. These combined reasons contributed to its decision to disregard the verdict.

Q13. In a year when five judges' terms expire, how many new judges are elected to maintain the total of fifteen?

A. Five ✅
B. Ten
C. Fifteen
D. Zero

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because the Court is designed to have a constant composition of fifteen judges, the five seats that become vacant are filled through new elections, ensuring that the total number of sitting judges remains unchanged.

Q14. Given that the Security Council has never taken ‘appropriate measures’ to enforce ICJ decisions, which principle of international law does this illustrate?

A. Principle of sovereign equality
B. Principle of consent ✅
C. Principle of non‑intervention
D. Principle of pacta sunt servanda

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The lack of enforcement underscores the principle that the Court’s jurisdiction is fundamentally based on the consent of the parties. Without that consent, the UN system lacks a compulsory mechanism to compel compliance, even when a judgment exists.

Q15. State Z wishes to bring a case against a more powerful State W, but W has not consented to ICJ jurisdiction. What is the most viable legal avenue for Z?

A. Unilaterally file the case
B. Request the UN General Assembly to refer the dispute ✅
C. Ask the Security Council to compel jurisdiction
D. Withdraw and seek arbitration elsewhere

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When a state refuses consent, the General Assembly may, by a resolution, refer the dispute to the Court. This provides a pathway for the aggrieved state to obtain a judicial forum, albeit one that still depends on political support.

Q16. What percentage of ICJ judges are required to represent different nationalities?

A. \(\)100\% ✅
B. 80%80\%80%
C. \(\)60\%
D. 50%50\%50%

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Statute mandates that the fifteen judges be nationals of different states, effectively requiring a 100 % representation of distinct nationalities. This rule prevents any single country from having more than one judge, promoting impartiality and global legitimacy.

Q17. If the Security Council were to adopt ‘appropriate measures’ under Chapter VII to enforce an ICJ judgment, which of the following could be a possible measure?

A. Economic sanctions
B. Military intervention
C. Diplomatic isolation
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Chapter VII grants the Council broad powers to maintain or restore international peace, including sanctions, the use of force, or diplomatic restrictions. Any of these tools could theoretically be employed to compel a state to comply with a Court order.

Q18. Considering the cases mentioned (Holland‑Belgium, Britain‑Norway, Nicaragua‑USA), which statement best reflects the ICJ’s overall effectiveness?

A. It resolves all disputes conclusively
B. It is effective only when parties cooperate ✅
C. It rarely issues judgments
D. Its judgments are always enforced by the UN

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Court’s success depends heavily on the willingness of the disputing states to accept its jurisdiction and comply with its rulings. When both parties cooperate, the Court can resolve issues; otherwise, its decisions may remain unimplemented.

Q19. A judge elected in 2022 will serve until which year, assuming a full term and no early resignation?

A. 2028
B. 2029
C. 2031 ✅
D. 2032

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A full term spans nine years. Adding nine to the election year of 2022 yields 2031, which marks the end of the judge’s tenure unless the judge resigns or is otherwise removed before that date.

Q20. What is a likely long‑term consequence when a major power refuses to comply with an ICJ judgment?

A. Erosion of the Court’s credibility ✅
B. Strengthening of the Court’s authority
C. Immediate sanctions by the UN
D. Automatic dismissal of the case

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Non‑compliance by a powerful state undermines the perceived authority of the Court, potentially discouraging other states from seeking its adjudication and weakening the overall system of peaceful dispute settlement.

Q21. In the Britain‑Norway fishing limits dispute, what type of legal principle did the ICJ apply to resolve the case?

A. Customary international law
B. Treaty interpretation
C. Equitable principles of maritime delimitation ✅
D. Human rights law

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Court employed equitable principles, which consider factors such as historic usage, proportionality, and the geography of the sea, to draw a fair maritime boundary. This approach is common in cases where precise treaty terms are absent.

Q22. Why does the ICJ require that all fifteen judges be of different nationalities?

A. To ensure linguistic diversity
B. To prevent dominance by any single state ✅
C. To reflect the UN’s five regional groups
D. To meet a requirement of the International Law Commission

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The rule that each judge hail from a distinct country is intended to avoid any one state exerting undue influence over the Court’s decisions, thereby preserving the perception of impartiality and equal representation.

Q23. State A and State B have a boundary dispute. State A accepts the ICJ’s jurisdiction, but State B does not. Which outcome is most likely?

A. The Court will hear the case anyway
B. The case will be dismissed ✅
C. The UN General Assembly will decide
D. The Security Council will intervene

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Without the consent of both parties, the Court lacks jurisdiction, and the dispute cannot proceed before it. Consequently, the case would be dismissed unless an alternative mechanism, such as a UN referral, is pursued.

Q24. The staggered nine‑year terms with five judges retiring every third year primarily serve to:

A. Increase judicial independence
B. Reduce political pressure
C. Maintain continuity while allowing periodic renewal ✅
D. Align with UN election cycles

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

This arrangement balances stability—by retaining a majority of experienced judges—with the opportunity for regular infusion of fresh perspectives, helping the Court adapt to evolving international legal challenges.

Q25. If the ICJ orders compensation but the losing party refuses to pay, what mechanism exists to compel payment?

A. Automatic seizure of assets by the UN
B. Referral to the International Monetary Fund
C. No direct enforcement mechanism ✅
D. Mandatory arbitration by a third state

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Court itself lacks coercive powers; it relies on the political will of the UN system, particularly the Security Council, to enforce judgments. In the absence of such action, there is no automatic legal process to collect damages.

Q26. State M alleges that State N violated its sovereign rights by constructing a dam on a river that forms their border. Which jurisdictional ground would most strongly support M’s ability to bring the case before the ICJ?

A. Treaty of mutual watercourse management
B. General principle of equitable utilization
C. Customary international law on transboundary watercourses
D. Consent of both states to the Court’s jurisdiction ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The essential prerequisite for the Court to hear the case is that both states have expressly consented to its jurisdiction, either through a treaty clause or a separate declaration, regardless of the substantive legal arguments involved.

Q27. Given the UNSC’s veto power, how could a permanent member impede the enforcement of an ICJ judgment against it?

A. By vetoing a Security Council resolution invoking Chapter VII ✅
B. By refusing to vote in the General Assembly
C. By withdrawing from the UN charter
D. By appointing additional judges

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A permanent member can block any Security Council resolution that seeks to implement or enforce a Court decision by exercising its veto, effectively preventing the Council from taking the ‘appropriate measures’ authorized under Chapter VII.

Q28. After the Nicaragua‑USA case, several small states hesitate to approach the ICJ. Which legal doctrine best explains this chilling effect?

A. Doctrine of jus cogens
B. Doctrine of estoppel
C. Doctrine of sovereign immunity
D. Doctrine of deterrence ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The observed reluctance reflects a deterrence effect: states may avoid bringing cases if they fear that powerful opponents will ignore judgments, thereby discouraging the use of the Court as a dispute‑resolution forum.

Q29. During the election of ICJ judges, a candidate must obtain an absolute majority in both the General Assembly and the Security Council. If there are 193 member states in the General Assembly, what is the minimum number of votes required for an absolute majority?

A. 979797 ✅
B. \)97
C. \)96
D. 989898

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

An absolute majority means more than half of the total members. Half of 193 is 96.5, so the smallest integer greater than this is 97. Therefore, a candidate needs at least 97 votes in the General Assembly to achieve an absolute majority.

Q30. Assume the fifteen judges are allocated to reflect the UN’s regional groups (5 Africa, 4 Asia‑Pacific, 3 Latin America, 2 Eastern Europe, 1 Western Europe). If a new judge from Western Europe is elected, which region will be over‑represented?

A. Africa
B. Asia‑Pacific
C. Western Europe ✅
D. None

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Adding another judge from Western Europe would raise that region’s representation from one to two seats, creating an over‑representation relative to the agreed regional balance, while the other regions would remain at their designated quotas.

Q31. Which of the following best characterizes the legal status of an ICJ judgment that a state has openly rejected?

A. It becomes void under international law
B. It remains binding but unenforced ✅
C. It transforms into a customary rule
D. It is subject to revision by the UN General Assembly

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Even if a state refuses to comply, the judgment retains its legal binding force under the Court’s statute. However, without enforcement mechanisms, the decision may remain ineffective, illustrating the gap between legal obligation and practical execution.

Q32. If the ICJ started with fifteen judges in 1946 and has maintained the five‑judge retirement cycle every three years, how many total judges have been elected up to and including 2023?

A. Approximately 120
B. Approximately 130 ✅
C. Approximately 140
D. Approximately 150

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

From 1946 to 2023 spans 77 years. Dividing by the three‑year cycle gives about 25.7, rounded up to 26 election periods. At five judges per period, roughly 130 judges have been elected, accounting for re‑elections and replacements over the decades.

Q33. How might the requirement that all judges be of different nationalities affect the Court’s deliberations?

A. It leads to linguistic barriers that delay rulings
B. It ensures a multiplicity of legal traditions, enriching reasoning ✅
C. It creates voting blocs based on regional alliances
D. It has no impact on deliberations

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Diverse national backgrounds bring varied legal philosophies, civil law and common law perspectives, and cultural understandings to the bench. This richness enhances the depth of deliberations and contributes to more balanced, well‑reasoned opinions.

Q34. A proposal suggests increasing the number of ICJ judges to twenty to improve case handling. Which of the following arguments most strongly opposes this change?

A. It would dilute the principle of equal representation
B. It would increase the Court’s budget unnecessarily
C. It would complicate the staggered term system
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

All three concerns are valid: expanding the bench could upset the current balance of national representation, raise operational costs, and disturb the carefully designed staggered election cycle, making the proposal problematic from multiple angles.
