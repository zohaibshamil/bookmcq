What is UN peacekeeping Suez Crisis 1956?

The UN Emergency Force I was created during the 1956 Suez Crisis as the first official armed peacekeeping mission, deploying neutral troops to supervise ceasefire and withdrawal of British, French, and Israeli forces from Egyptian territory.

📝 All UN peacekeeping Suez Crisis 1956 MCQs

Q1. In what year did President Nasser nationalize the Suez Canal?

A. 1955
B. 1956 ✅
C. 1957
D. 1958

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The nationalization occurred in \(1956\), a decisive move by Nasser that sparked international tension. The timing is crucial because it coincided with Cold War rivalries and the post‑World War II reshaping of colonial assets, leading directly to the crisis.

Q2. Which two European powers owned most shares of the Suez Canal before nationalization?

A. Britain and France ✅
B. Germany and Italy
C. Spain and Portugal
D. Netherlands and Belgium

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Before Nasser's decree, the canal was predominantly held by Britain and France, reflecting their historic imperial interests. Their financial stakes gave them a strong motive to intervene militarily, which ultimately exposed the limits of colonial economic control in the mid‑20th century.

Q3. Which country invaded Egypt from the east during the crisis?

A. Jordan
B. Israel ✅
C. Syria
D. Lebanon

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Israel launched its offensive from the Sinai Peninsula, aiming to neutralize Egyptian forces and open a corridor to the Gaza Strip. This action, coordinated with Britain and France, was framed as a response to Egyptian aggression but was largely motivated by strategic goals against Nasser.

Q4. How many countries contributed troops to the UN force that entered the canal zone?

📖 Explanation:

Ten different nations supplied soldiers for the UN Emergency Force, showcasing a broad coalition that lent legitimacy to the operation. This multinational composition was intended to demonstrate impartiality and to prevent any single power from dominating the peacekeeping mission.

Q5. Approximately how many UN troops were deployed?

The General Assembly recorded a decisive \(64\)‑to‑\(5\) vote condemning the military actions of Britain, France, and Israel. This overwhelming majority underscored global disapproval and reinforced the Assembly’s role as a moral counterweight when the Security Council was deadlocked.

Q8. Which two Western powers used their veto power in the Security Council?

📖 Explanation:

Britain and France each exercised their vetoes to block a resolution that would have condemned their own use of force. Their actions highlighted how the veto could be employed to protect national interests, prompting the Assembly to intervene through its own authority.

Both governments publicly claimed they were safeguarding their nationals, but the underlying motive was to defend their economic stakes in the canal. The strategic importance of the waterway for oil shipments made its control a critical national interest for Britain and France.
