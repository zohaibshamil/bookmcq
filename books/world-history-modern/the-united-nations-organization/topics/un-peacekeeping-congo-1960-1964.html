What is UN peacekeeping Congo 1960-1964?

ONUC was a large-scale UN peacekeeping operation in Congo from 1960 to 1964 that maintained order, facilitated Belgian withdrawal, and prevented secession of Katanga, though faced challenges due to internal Congolese conflicts and Cold War interference.

📝 All UN peacekeeping Congo 1960-1964 MCQs

Q1. What year did the United Nations first deploy the ONUC peacekeeping force in the Congo?

A. 1959
B. 1960
C. 1961 ✅
D. 1962

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations authorized the Operation des Nations Unies au Congo (ONUC) in July 1960, and the first troops arrived in August 1960. This deployment marked the UN’s first large‑scale peacekeeping mission, aiming to stabilize the newly independent state amid secessionist crises and Cold‑War tensions.

Q2. Which province attempted to secede from the Congo in July 1960, prompting UN intervention?

A. Katanga ✅
B. Kasaï
C. Maniema
D. Équateur

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Katanga, rich in mineral resources and led by Moïse Tshombe, declared independence on 11 July 1960. The secession threatened the Congo’s territorial integrity and attracted foreign interests, leading the UN Security Council to authorize a peacekeeping operation to prevent further fragmentation and protect civilians.

Q3. Who was the first Prime Minister of the independent Congo who appealed to the UN for assistance?

A. Joseph Kasa-Vubu
B. Patrice Lumumba ✅
C. Moïse Tshombe
D. Eugène Kokoné

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Patrice Lumumba, appointed Prime Minister on 24 June 1960, faced a rapidly deteriorating security situation. He appealed to the United Nations for help in restoring order, which resulted in the adoption of Resolution 143 and the deployment of ONUC forces to the country.

Q4. What was the primary objective of UN Security Council Resolution 143 (1960)?

A. To recognize Katanga’s independence
B. To withdraw all foreign troops from the Congo
C. To provide military assistance to restore law and order ✅
D. To impose economic sanctions on the Congo

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Resolution 143 authorized the UN to provide military assistance to the Congolese government to restore law and order, protect civilians, and ensure the territorial integrity of the Congo. It did not recognize any secessionist entities and laid the groundwork for the ONUC mission.

Q5. Which Cold‑War superpower supported the Katanga secession financially?

A. United States ✅
B. Soviet Union
C. United Kingdom
D. France

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The United States, concerned about the Congo’s strategic minerals and fearing communist influence, covertly supported Tshombe’s Katanga government through financial aid and diplomatic backing, although official policy emphasized non‑intervention. This involvement complicated the UN’s neutrality and heightened Cold‑War tensions in the region.

Q6. How many UN member states contributed troops to the ONUC mission at its peak?

A. 12
B. 20
C. 28 ✅
D. 35

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

At its height in early 1961, the ONUC mission included forces from 28 different UN member states, reflecting a broad international commitment to stabilize the Congo. This multinational composition was unprecedented for the time and demonstrated the UN’s willingness to mobilize a diverse coalition for peacekeeping.

Q7. A UN commander must decide whether to use force to end the siege of a UN‑protected airport in Katanga. Which principle of UN peacekeeping does this decision most directly test?

A. Consent of the parties
B. Impartiality
C. Use of force only in self‑defence ✅
D. Non‑use of force

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The decision to employ force to lift the siege touches on the principle that peacekeepers may use force only in self‑defence or to protect civilians under their mandate. While consent and impartiality are essential, the immediate issue is whether the use of force complies with the limited‑force doctrine.

Q8. Evaluate why the UN’s initial inability to arrest Katanga’s foreign mercenaries undermined its credibility. Which factor was most significant?

A. Lack of clear mandate
B. Insufficient troop numbers ✅
C. Political pressure from the United States
D. Hostile terrain

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Insufficient troop numbers limited the UN’s capacity to conduct robust operations against well‑armed mercenaries. Although the mandate permitted action, the shortage of forces meant the UN could not enforce its authority effectively, eroding confidence among Congolese civilians and signaling weakness to secessionist actors.

Q9. Which of the following best describes the outcome of the 1961 Operation Morthor?

A. Successful capture of Katanga’s capital
B. Failure to secure any strategic points
C. Partial success in securing key railway lines ✅
D. Complete withdrawal of UN forces

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Operation Morthor, launched in December 1961, achieved limited objectives, notably securing sections of the critical railway that linked the mineral‑rich interior to the port of Matadi. The operation fell short of its broader goals, such as fully neutralizing Katanga’s military capacity, illustrating the challenges of limited‑force peacekeeping.

Q10. During the Congo crisis, which UN Secretary‑General personally negotiated with both Lumumba and Tshombe?

A. Dag Hammarskjöld ✅
B. U Thant
C. Kurt Waldheim
D. Boutros Boutros‑Ghali

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Dag Hammarskjöld, serving as Secretary‑General from 1953 to 1961, traveled to the Congo multiple times, meeting with Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba and Katanga leader Moïse Tshombe. His diplomatic efforts aimed to mediate the conflict, but his untimely death in a plane crash in 1961 curtailed further negotiations.

Q11. After the death of Dag Hammarskjöld, a new Secretary‑General must decide whether to continue the ONUC mandate. Which factor should weigh most heavily in the decision?

A. UN budget constraints
B. The level of Cold‑War rivalry in the region
C. Domestic politics of member states
D. Humanitarian impact on civilians ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The primary responsibility of the UN is to protect civilians; therefore, the humanitarian impact of continued instability—mass displacements, violence, and famine—should dominate the decision‑making process, even if budgetary or political concerns are significant.

Q12. What was the main reason the United Nations ultimately withdrew from Katanga in 1964?

A. Victory of the Congolese central government ✅
B. International pressure from the United States
C. Escalating casualties among UN troops
D. Failure to secure a political settlement

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

By early 1964, the Congolese national army, bolstered by UN logistical support, had regained control over most of Katanga, rendering the peacekeeping force’s continued presence unnecessary. The withdrawal reflected a strategic shift from active intervention to supporting the central government’s consolidation of authority.

Q13. Compare the UN’s role in the Congo crisis to its later mission in Cyprus (1964). Which similarity most accurately reflects both operations?

A. Both involved large‑scale combat operations
B. Both were primarily humanitarian aid missions
C. Both required the UN to act without explicit consent of all parties
D. Both faced challenges from Cold‑War rivalries ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Both the Congo and Cyprus missions unfolded amid intense Cold‑War competition, compelling the UN to navigate superpower interests while attempting to maintain neutrality. Although the operational contexts differed, the overarching challenge of balancing great‑power politics with peacekeeping objectives was a common thread.

Q14. Which of the following statements about the UN’s use of air power in the Congo is correct?

A. UN aircraft conducted strategic bombing of Katanga cities
B. Air support was limited to transport and reconnaissance ✅
C. UN forces employed fighter jets for air superiority
D. The UN relied exclusively on civilian charter flights

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

UN air assets in the Congo were primarily used for logistical transport, medical evacuation, and aerial reconnaissance. The mission did not include offensive air operations such as bombing or fighter engagements, reflecting the limited‑force principle that constrained the use of combat aircraft.

Q15. A UN peacekeeper witnesses a civilian market being looted by a militia. According to the UN peacekeeping doctrine, what is the appropriate immediate response?

A. Engage the militia with lethal force
B. Report the incident to the chain of command and protect civilians if feasible ✅
C. Withdraw from the area to avoid conflict
D. Negotiate a cease‑fire with militia leaders

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The doctrine emphasizes the protection of civilians as a core mandate. The peacekeeper should first secure the civilians’ safety, if possible, and then report the incident up the chain of command. Lethal force is reserved for self‑defence or when civilian lives are imminently threatened.

Q16. What was the name of the Congolese army that eventually re‑established control over the whole country by 1964?

A. Forces Armées Zaïroises
B. Armée Nationale Congolaise ✅
C. Force Publique
D. Garde Nationale Congolaise

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Armée Nationale Congolaise (ANC) was the national military force that, after a period of fragmentation and foreign intervention, succeeded in re‑asserting central authority over the Congo by 1964, playing a pivotal role in ending the secessionist crises.

Q17. Which UN resolution established the principle that peacekeepers may use force to protect civilians?

A. Resolution 1244 (1999)
B. Resolution 143 (1960)
C. Resolution 1325 (2000) ✅
D. Resolution 678 (1990)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Resolution 1325, adopted in 2000, explicitly recognized the need for the protection of women, children, and civilians in conflict zones, laying the groundwork for later peacekeeping mandates that allow the use of force to safeguard civilians. Although passed after the Congo crisis, its principles are retrospectively applied to evaluate earlier missions.

Q18. What economic resource was most coveted by foreign interests during the Katanga secession?

A. Gold
B. Diamonds
C. Copper and uranium ✅
D. Oil

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Katanga’s mineral wealth, especially its copper and uranium deposits, attracted intense foreign interest, notably from Western mining companies and governments seeking strategic resources during the Cold War. This economic incentive fueled external support for the secession and complicated UN diplomatic efforts.

Q19. Which of the following best explains why the UN could not enforce the withdrawal of foreign mercenaries from Katanga?

A. The mercenaries were protected by the UN charter
B. The UN lacked a clear legal basis to intervene against non‑state actors ✅
C. The mercenaries operated under UN command
D. The UN had sufficient troops but chose not to act

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The UN’s peacekeeping mandate focused on state actors and did not explicitly cover non‑state mercenaries. Consequently, the organization lacked a clear legal authority to compel their removal, limiting its ability to address this aspect of the conflict directly.

Q20. What was the main political outcome of the 1961 Round Table Conference in Léopoldville?

A. Formal recognition of Katanga’s independence
B. Establishment of a federal system in the Congo
C. Agreement to hold national elections and consolidate central authority ✅
D. Division of the Congo into two separate states

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Léopoldville Round Table Conference produced an agreement to organize national elections, reaffirm the Congo’s territorial integrity, and work toward consolidating central authority, thereby attempting to resolve the secessionist crises and lay a foundation for political stability.

Q21. A UN contingent receives intelligence that a rebel group plans to attack a UN‑protected school. Which course of action aligns with the principle of proportionality?

A. Deploy the entire contingent to surround the school
B. Use only the minimum force necessary to deter the attack ✅
C. Call for a full‑scale airstrike on the rebel camp
D. Withdraw all UN forces from the area

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Proportionality requires that the level of force employed be the minimum needed to achieve the objective—in this case, protecting civilians. Deploying a limited, defensive force to deter the attack respects this principle, avoiding unnecessary escalation while fulfilling the protection mandate.

Q22. Which UN peacekeeping operation directly succeeded ONUC in the Congo after 1964?

A. MONUC
B. MONUSCO
C. UNOCI
D. There was no direct successor; the mission concluded ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

After ONUC terminated in 1964, there was a prolonged period without a dedicated UN peacekeeping mission in the Congo until the establishment of the United Nations Organization Mission in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (MONUC) in 1999, later renamed MONUSCO. Therefore, no immediate successor existed.

Q23. Considering the Cold‑War context, how did the UN’s handling of the Congo crisis influence later peacekeeping doctrine?

A. It led to the adoption of a zero‑tolerance policy toward secessionist movements
B. It reinforced the principle of limited use of force and impartiality ✅
C. It resulted in the abandonment of peacekeeping missions in Africa
D. It prompted the UN to seek direct military alliances with superpowers

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Congo experience highlighted the challenges of operating amid superpower rivalry, prompting the UN to codify the principle of limited force, emphasizing impartiality and the protection of civilians while avoiding direct confrontation with major powers. This doctrinal shift shaped subsequent missions worldwide.

Q24. What was the name of the United Nations aircraft that crashed, killing Secretary‑General Hammarskjöld?

A. Eagle 1
B. Star Tiger
C. Kilo Mango
D. Plane C‑47 (Flight CT‑602) ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

On 18 September 1961, a UN‑operated Canadair C‑47 (registered as Flight CT‑602) crashed near Ndola in Northern Rhodesia, resulting in the death of Secretary‑General Dag Hammarskjöld and several staff members, an event that profoundly affected the Congo mission.

Q25. Which neighboring country provided troops that supported the Congolese central government against Katanga?

A. Angola
B. Zambia ✅
C. Tanzania
D. Ethiopia

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Zambia, then Northern Rhodesia, contributed troops and logistical support to the Congolese central government, assisting in operations against Katanga’s secessionist forces. This regional involvement illustrated the broader African interest in maintaining the Congo’s territorial integrity during the early 1960s.

Q26. During a negotiation, a UN mediator must balance the interests of the Congolese government and foreign mining corporations. Which negotiation tactic aligns with UN impartiality?

A. Favoring the government’s demands to maintain sovereignty
B. Prioritizing corporate interests to secure economic stability
C. Alternating between parties’ proposals without showing bias ✅
D. Rejecting any compromise to force an agreement

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Impartiality requires the mediator to treat all parties equally, presenting proposals from each side in a balanced manner. Alternating between positions without indicating preference upholds neutrality, fostering trust and increasing the likelihood of a sustainable agreement.

Q27. What was the primary reason the UN could not prevent the assassination of Patrice Lumumba?

A. Lack of intelligence on the plot
B. Insufficient troop presence in the capital
C. Political constraints within the UN Security Council
D. The involvement of foreign agents beyond UN control ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Lumumba’s assassination was orchestrated by a combination of internal Congolese actors and external agents, including Belgian and possibly CIA interests. These forces operated outside the UN’s jurisdiction, rendering the organization incapable of preventing the murder despite its peacekeeping mandate.

Q28. Which principle of the UN Charter was most directly challenged by the Katanga secession?

A. Article 2(4) – prohibition of the use of force
B. Article 2(7) – non‑intervention in domestic affairs ✅
C. Article 51 – right of self‑defence
D. Article 24 – primary responsibility for peace and security

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Katanga secession raised questions about the principle of non‑intervention, as foreign powers and mercenaries intervened in Congo’s internal affairs. While the UN sought to respect sovereignty, the presence of external actors complicated the application of Article 2(7) regarding domestic matters.

Q29. Assess why the UN’s mixed success in the Congo crisis is often attributed to its limited mandate rather than resource constraints. Which argument best supports this view?

A. The UN had ample troops but lacked a clear political directive
B. Resource shortages prevented any meaningful action
C. The mission’s rules of engagement restricted decisive military action ✅
D. The UN was unwilling to engage due to political neutrality

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN’s operating framework emphasized limited use of force and required consent, which constrained decisive actions such as targeting mercenaries or forcibly ending secession. Although resources were sufficient, the mandate’s restrictions limited the mission’s effectiveness, illustrating how political parameters, not material capacity, shaped outcomes.

Q30. What was the outcome of the 1964 general elections in the Congo?

A. Moïse Tshombe elected president
B. Joseph Mobutu won a parliamentary seat
C. A stable coalition government formed under Kasa‑Vubu ✅
D. The elections were postponed indefinitely due to violence

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The 1964 elections resulted in a coalition government that helped re‑establish central authority under President Joseph Kasa‑Vubu. While the political landscape remained fragile, the electoral process signaled a move toward constitutional governance after years of conflict.

Q31. A UN peacekeeper must decide whether to intervene in a clash between two local militias. Which factor is least relevant to the decision?

A. The peacekeeper’s personal safety
B. The number of civilians at risk
C. The UN mandate’s scope regarding civilian protection
D. The political affiliation of the militias ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

While the political affiliation of the militias may inform broader strategic considerations, the immediate decision to intervene hinges on the peacekeeper’s safety, civilian risk, and whether the mandate authorizes protective action. Political ties are less directly pertinent to the operational choice.

Q32. Which of the following was a direct consequence of the UN’s involvement in the Congo crisis?

A. Permanent UN military bases in the Congo
B. The establishment of the Non‑Aligned Movement
C. Increased confidence of African states in UN peacekeeping ✅
D. The immediate withdrawal of all foreign mercenaries

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN’s active role, despite challenges, demonstrated its capacity to intervene in African conflicts, bolstering confidence among newly independent states that the organization could provide a neutral platform for conflict resolution and peacekeeping assistance.

Q33. What was the name given to the UN‑led operation that aimed to secure the mineral-rich railway from Katanga to the port of Matadi?

A. Operation Morthy
B. Operation Morthor ✅
C. Operation Mufasa
D. Operation Mufasa‑II

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Operation Morthor, launched in late 1961, focused on regaining control of the strategic railway line linking Katanga’s interior to the coastal port of Matadi. Securing this route was essential for limiting the secessionist government’s economic lifeline and restoring central authority over key transport infrastructure.

Q34. Why did the UN’s approach in the Congo differ from its later mission in Rwanda (1994)?

A. The Congo mission had a clear combat mandate, while Rwanda did not
B. The Congo crisis involved direct Cold‑War competition, influencing UN decision‑making ✅
C. Rwanda required a larger troop contribution than the Congo
D. The UN employed a humanitarian‑only approach in Rwanda, unlike in the Congo

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

During the Congo crisis, superpower rivalry dictated a cautious UN stance, limiting the use of force and emphasizing neutrality. In Rwanda, the absence of major power competition allowed the UN to focus more on humanitarian concerns, though it still faced challenges. This contrast underscores how geopolitical context shapes mission design.
