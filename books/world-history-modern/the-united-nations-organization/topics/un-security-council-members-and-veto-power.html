What is UN Security Council members and veto power?

The UN Security Council consists of five permanent members with veto power and ten elected members, responsible for maintaining international peace and security, where any permanent member can block substantive resolutions regardless of majority support.

📝 All UN Security Council members and veto power MCQs

Q1. How many permanent members were there when the Security Council was first established?

A. Four
B. Five ✅
C. Six
D. Seven

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The original Charter created five permanent members: China, France, the United States, the USSR, and Britain. This number has remained constant despite changes in the total council size, and it forms the core of the veto power that shapes Council decisions.

Q2. In which year was the number of non‑permanent members increased from six to ten?

A. 1955
B. 1960
C. 1963
D. 1965 ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The amendment to increase non‑permanent seats took effect in 1965, reflecting a desire for broader geographic representation. This change expanded the Council from eleven to fifteen members, allowing more states to participate without altering the permanent‑member structure.

Q3. What is the minimum number of affirmative votes required for a Security Council decision to pass?

A. Nine ✅
B. Ten
C. Eleven
D. Twelve

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

According to the Charter, substantive decisions need at least nine votes in favour, provided none of the five permanent members cast a veto. This rule ensures that a super‑majority of the Council supports the action while preserving the veto privilege of the permanent members.

Q4. Which permanent member vetoed the People's Republic of China’s application for UN membership in 1950?

A. USSR
B. France
C. USA ✅
D. Britain

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United States exercised its veto power to block the PRC’s admission, allowing the Republic of China (Taiwan) to retain its seat. This action exemplified Cold‑War politics and demonstrated how a single permanent member could shape the composition of the United Nations.

Q5. According to the UN Charter, what majority is needed in the General Assembly to admit a new member state?

A. Simple majority
B. Two‑thirds majority ✅
C. Three‑quarters majority
D. Unanimous

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Article 4 of the Charter requires a two‑thirds majority of members present and voting for admission of a new state. This high threshold reflects the importance of broad consensus among UN members before expanding the organization’s membership.

Q6. As of today, how many total members compose the Security Council?

A. 10
B. 12
C. 14
D. 15 ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Council currently consists of fifteen members: five permanent and ten non‑permanent seats. This composition was established after the 1965 amendment and has remained unchanged, providing a balance between the core powers and rotating representation from various regions.

Q7. The “Uniting for Peace” resolution allows the General Assembly to act when the Security Council is deadlocked by a veto. Which of the following actions can the Assembly decide under this resolution?

A. Only diplomatic statements
B. Economic sanctions
C. Military intervention ✅
D. None of the above

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Adopted during the Korean War, the resolution permits the General Assembly to recommend collective measures, including the use of force, when the Council cannot act because of a veto. Although not a formal amendment, it has been invoked in several crises.

Q8. Which permanent member historically used its veto power more frequently than any other?

A. USA
B. Britain
C. USSR ✅
D. France

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet Union (later Russia) employed the veto far more often than the other four permanent members, especially during the Cold War era. Its frequent use underscored the strategic importance of the veto and contributed to many deadlocks in Council deliberations.

Q9. For how many years are non‑permanent members elected to the Security Council?

A. One year
B. Two years ✅
C. Three years
D. Four years

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Non‑permanent members serve staggered two‑year terms, with half the seats up for election each year. This system ensures continuity while allowing a regular infusion of new members, promoting diversity and broader participation in Council affairs.

Q10. In 1971, which country's representation on the Security Council replaced that of the Republic of China (Taiwan)?

A. Japan
B. People's Republic of China ✅
C. North Korea
D. South Korea

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

After the United States ceased vetoing the PRC's application, the General Assembly voted to transfer the seat from the Republic of China (Taiwan) to the People's Republic of China. This shift recognized the PRC as the legitimate government of China and altered the Council’s permanent composition.

Q11. A permanent member abstains from voting on a resolution that receives nine affirmative votes including the abstention. Does the resolution pass?

A. Yes, because abstention is not a veto ✅
B. No, because all permanent members must vote in favour
C. Yes, but only if a two‑thirds majority in GA is also achieved
D. No, because abstention counts as a negative vote

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Customary practice treats abstention by a permanent member as a neutral stance, not a veto. Therefore, if the required nine votes are obtained and no permanent member casts a negative vote, the resolution is considered adopted despite the abstention.

Q12. If a resolution receives ten votes in favour, nine against, and one abstention from a permanent member, what is the outcome?

A. Resolution passes ✅
B. Resolution fails
C. Requires re‑vote
D. Depends on the content of the resolution

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The key condition is that no permanent member votes against; an abstention does not block the measure. With ten affirmative votes and no veto, the resolution meets the nine‑vote threshold and is adopted, regardless of the number of negative votes from non‑permanent members.

Q13. Under “Uniting for Peace,” what is the minimum voting threshold in the General Assembly to adopt a replacement measure after a Security Council veto?

A. Simple majority
B. Two‑thirds majority ✅
C. Three‑quarters majority
D. Unanimous

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The resolution expressly requires a two‑thirds majority of members present and voting in the General Assembly to endorse any alternative action. This high bar ensures that the Assembly’s decision reflects a substantial consensus before overriding a Council deadlock.

Q14. Considering the original eleven members and the current fifteen, by what percentage did the total number of Council members increase?

A. \( \frac{4}{11}\times100\% \) ✅
B. \( \frac{5}{11}\times100\% \)
C. \( \frac{15}{11}\times100\% \)
D. \( \frac{4}{15}\times100\% \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Council grew from eleven to fifteen members, an addition of four seats. Expressed as a percentage of the original size, the increase is \( \frac{4}{11}\times100\% \), approximately 36.4 %. This calculation highlights the relative expansion of the body.

Q15. During the Korean War, the Security Council was vetoed by a permanent member. Which body subsequently authorized collective action, and what principle justified it?

A. International Court of Justice, principle of jurisdiction
B. General Assembly, principle of “Uniting for Peace” ✅
C. Secretariat, principle of neutrality
D. Economic and Social Council, principle of development

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When the Council could not act because of a veto, the General Assembly invoked the “Uniting for Peace” principle, convening within 24 hours to recommend collective measures, including military intervention. This mechanism allowed the UN to respond to the crisis despite the deadlock.

Q16. If a permanent member uses its veto, and the remaining fourteen members vote unanimously in favour, what is the effect on the resolution?

A. It passes because of majority
B. It fails because of veto ✅
C. It is postponed
D. It is sent to the GA for review

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A single veto by any permanent member nullifies the resolution regardless of how the other members vote. The Charter explicitly grants each permanent member the right to block substantive decisions, making the unanimous support of the remaining members insufficient.

Q17. Suppose the Security Council decides to impose economic sanctions requiring a vote of at least nine in favour. Four permanent members vote for, three vote against, and two abstain, while the remaining six non‑permanent members vote for. Does the resolution pass?

A. Yes, because nine votes are in favour
B. No, because one permanent member voted against
C. Yes, because abstentions do not count as vetoes ✅
D. No, because a veto is required for sanctions

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Abstentions by permanent members are not treated as vetoes. The tally shows ten affirmative votes (four permanent + six non‑permanent) and no veto, satisfying the nine‑vote requirement, so the sanctions resolution is adopted.

Q18. The veto power is not explicitly limited by the Charter. Which statement best reflects a legal interpretation of abstention versus veto?

A. Abstention is equivalent to a negative vote
B. Abstention is treated as a neutral position and does not block a resolution ✅
C. Abstention automatically triggers a re‑vote
D. Abstention is prohibited by the Charter

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

International practice interprets abstention as a neutral act that does not prevent adoption, provided the nine‑vote threshold is met and no permanent member casts a negative vote. This interpretation, though not codified, is widely accepted by member states.

Q19. In 1950, the USA vetoed the PRC application each year for 20 years. If the General Assembly required a two‑thirds majority of 180 members to admit the PRC, how many votes were needed at minimum?

A. \( \frac{2}{3}\times180 = 120 \) ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{2}\times180 = 90 \)
C. \( \frac{3}{4}\times180 = 135 \)
D. \( 180-20 = 160 \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A two‑thirds majority of 180 members equals \( \frac{2}{3}\times180 = 120 \) votes. This figure shows the substantial support needed in the General Assembly to overcome the Security Council’s repeated vetoes and admit a new member.

Q20. Considering the requirement that all five permanent members must vote in favour, what is the maximum number of permanent members that can oppose a resolution without causing a veto?

A. Zero ✅
B. One
C. Two
D. Four

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Any negative vote by a permanent member constitutes a veto. Therefore, the only scenario without a veto is when no permanent member opposes; even a single opposition triggers the veto power, making the maximum number of opposing permanent members zero.

Q21. If a permanent member abstains and the vote tally is eight in favour, six against, and one abstention, does the resolution meet the “nine‑vote” rule?

A. Yes, because abstention counts toward nine
B. No, because only eight votes are in favour ✅
C. Yes, because abstention substitutes a vote
D. No, because any abstention triggers a veto

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The nine‑vote requirement counts only affirmative votes. With eight members voting in favour, the resolution falls short of the required nine, regardless of abstentions, and therefore cannot be adopted.

Q22. The expansion from six to ten non‑permanent members increased the total number of non‑permanent seats by what fraction?

A. \( \frac{4}{6} \)
B. \( \frac{2}{3} \) ✅
C. \( \frac{5}{6} \)
D. \( \frac{1}{2} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The increase added four seats to the original six, yielding a fraction of \( \frac{4}{6} \) which simplifies to \( \frac{2}{3} \). This proportion highlights the significant enlargement of the rotating membership component of the Council.

Q23. A resolution concerning a cease‑fire receives nine votes in favour, zero against, and five abstentions, including two from permanent members. What is the status of the resolution?

A. Adopted ✅
B. Rejected
C. Deferred
D. Sent to the GA

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since there are nine affirmative votes and no permanent member cast a negative vote, the abstentions do not constitute a veto. The resolution therefore meets the required threshold and is adopted despite the abstentions.

Q24. If the Security Council wanted to adopt a resolution requiring unanimity among permanent members, what is the minimum number of votes needed, assuming all non‑permanent members vote in favour?

A. 5
B. 10
C. 15 ✅
D. 9

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Unanimity among the five permanent members means all five must vote for the measure. Adding the ten non‑permanent members who are assumed to support it yields a total of fifteen votes required for adoption.

Q25. During a crisis, three permanent members are absent, one votes against, and the remaining vote in favour, reaching nine affirmative votes. Does the resolution pass?

A. Yes, because nine votes are present and no veto
B. No, because a permanent member voted against ✅
C. Yes, because absence is treated as abstention
D. No, because quorum is not met

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Any negative vote by a permanent member functions as a veto, regardless of the number of affirmative votes. Hence, the single against vote by a permanent member blocks the resolution, and it does not pass.

Q26. Discuss the legal implications of the “Uniting for Peace” resolution not being incorporated into the Charter. Which of the following best describes its status?

A. It is a binding amendment to the Charter
B. It is a customary international law principle ✅
C. It is a non‑binding recommendation
D. It is a secret protocol

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Although not a formal amendment, the repeated invocation of “Uniting for Peace” has generated a practice recognized as customary international law. This status grants it normative force, allowing the General Assembly to act when the Council is deadlocked, albeit without the same legal certainty as a charter provision.

Q27. Assume the Security Council passes a resolution with nine affirmative votes, but one permanent member casts a negative vote that is later withdrawn due to diplomatic pressure. What is the effect on the resolution’s validity?

A. The resolution remains invalid because the veto was initially exercised
B. The resolution becomes valid once the veto is withdrawn
C. The resolution must be re‑voted
D. The withdrawal has no effect; the original veto stands ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Under the Charter, a veto is effective the moment it is exercised and cannot be rescinded retroactively. Even if the permanent member later changes its position, the resolution remains null, and a new vote would be required to revive the measure.

Q28. Evaluate how the veto power influences the balance between the Security Council and the General Assembly in international peace‑keeping. Which statement captures the core tension?

A. The veto ensures the GA cannot override SC decisions
B. The veto allows the GA to bypass SC decisions
C. The veto creates a deadlock that the GA can override only under “Uniting for Peace” ✅
D. The veto has no impact on GA authority

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The veto gives permanent members decisive control over substantive Council actions, often leading to stalemates. The General Assembly can only intervene through the “Uniting for Peace” mechanism, which provides a limited, albeit significant, avenue to circumvent a deadlocked Council, illustrating the inherent tension.

Q29. If the Security Council decides to expand the number of non‑permanent seats again, requiring a two‑thirds majority in the GA to approve the amendment, how many votes are needed out of a 193‑member GA?

A. \( \frac{2}{3}\times193 \approx 129 \) ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{2}\times193 = 96.5 \)
C. \( \frac{3}{4}\times193 \approx 145 \)
D. 193

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A two‑thirds majority of 193 members equals \( \frac{2}{3}\times193 \), which calculates to approximately 128.7. Since votes must be whole numbers, at least 129 votes in favour are required to adopt the amendment, illustrating the high threshold for charter changes.

Q30. The United Nations Charter requires that decisions on substantive matters need the concurring votes of the permanent members. How does this requirement affect the probability of a resolution passing if each permanent member independently has a 70% chance of voting in favour?

A. \(0.7^{5}=0.16807\) ✅
B. \(0.7^{9}=0.04035\)
C. \(0.7=0.7\)
D. \(1-0.3^{5}=0.99757\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The probability that all five permanent members vote in favour is the product of their individual probabilities: \(0.7^{5}=0.16807\) (about 16.8 %). This low figure demonstrates how the veto can substantially reduce the likelihood of adoption, even before considering the nine‑vote threshold.

Q31. During a Cold War crisis, the USSR vetoed a resolution, but the GA passed a “Uniting for Peace” resolution with a two‑thirds majority of 140 votes. How many votes did the GA cast in favour?

A. \( \frac{2}{3}\times140 = 93.33\) rounded up to 94 ✅
B. 140
C. 70
D. 120

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A two‑thirds majority of 140 members equals \( \frac{2}{3}\times140 = 93.\overline{3}\). Since a vote cannot be fractional, at least 94 votes in favour are needed to meet the required threshold, demonstrating the numerical strength needed for GA action.

Q32. Compare the voting requirement of nine affirmative votes including all permanent members with the requirement of a two‑thirds majority in the GA (193 members). Which system requires a higher absolute number of votes to adopt a measure?

A. Security Council
B. General Assembly ✅
C. Both require same number
D. It depends on the issue

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A two‑thirds majority of 193 members is roughly 129 votes, far exceeding the nine‑vote threshold of the Security Council. Therefore, the General Assembly demands a considerably larger absolute number of affirmative votes to adopt a measure.

Q33. If a permanent member threatens to veto a resolution unless a specific clause is removed, and the Council negotiates to delete the clause, resulting in a vote of fifteen in favour, what does this illustrate about the veto’s strategic use?

A. It shows the veto is a formal tool only
B. It demonstrates the veto’s power to shape content ✅
C. It indicates the veto is irrelevant
D. It proves the veto can be overridden by majority

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The scenario shows that the mere threat of a veto can compel the Council to modify the text of a resolution, even when the final vote is unanimous. This strategic leverage underscores the veto’s ability to influence substantive content, not just to block measures outright.

Q34. Considering the historical pattern where the USA blocked the PRC’s membership for 20 years, calculate the total number of vetoes exercised if each annual application was vetoed once.

A. \(20\) ✅
B. \(40\)
C. \(10\)
D. \(0\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

If the United States vetoed the People's Republic of China's membership application each year for 20 consecutive years, the total number of vetoes equals the number of years, i.e., \(20\). This simple multiplication reflects the repeated use of the veto to maintain the status quo.
