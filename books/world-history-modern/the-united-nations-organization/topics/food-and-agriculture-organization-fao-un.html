Q1. In which year was the Rome Statute, establishing the ICC, signed?

🔹 A. 1998
🔹 B. 1999
🔹 C. 2000
🔹 D. 2001

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Rome Statute was adopted and opened for signature on 17 July 1998, marking a pivotal step toward creating a permanent international criminal court to prosecute war crimes, genocide, and crimes against humanity.

Q2. Where was the International Criminal Court formally inaugurated in March 2003?

🔹 A. The Hague
🔹 B. Geneva
🔹 C. New York
🔹 D. Brussels

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The ICC was officially inaugurated in The Hague, Netherlands, in March 2003, providing a permanent judicial body to address the most serious violations of international humanitarian law.

Q3. Who was the first former head of state ever brought before the ICC?

📖 Explanation:

Q12. A peacekeeper from a state party to the ICC is accused of war crimes. Which principle determines ICC jurisdiction?

🔹 A. Complementarity
🔹 B. Territoriality
🔹 C. Universal jurisdiction
🔹 D. Nullum crimen

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The principle of complementarity holds that the ICC may intervene only when national jurisdictions are unwilling or unable to prosecute, ensuring that the court acts as a complement, not a replacement, to domestic legal systems.

Q16. A country signs the Rome Statute but later enacts legislation excluding its nationals from ICC jurisdiction. Which ICC principle may be violated?

📖 Explanation:

The ICC’s jurisdiction depends on the consent of states parties; a domestic law that withdraws consent for its nationals undermines the principle that states must agree to ICC jurisdiction.
