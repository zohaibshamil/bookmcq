What is International Labour Organization ILO UN?

The International Labour Organization sets international labor standards, promotes decent work, and protects workers’ rights through tripartite governance involving governments, employers, and workers, operating as a specialized UN agency since 1946.

📝 All International Labour Organization ILO UN MCQs

Q1. What year was the United Nations founded?

A.1945 ✅
B.1939
C.1950
D.1942
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The United Nations was officially established in October 1945 after the end of World War II, replacing the ineffective League of Nations and aiming to maintain international peace and security.

Q2. Which organ of the UN is responsible for peacekeeping decisions?

A.General Assembly
B.Security Council ✅
C.Secretariat
D.International Court of Justice
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The UN Security Council holds primary authority over the authorization of peacekeeping missions and the use of force, making it the central body for decisions related to maintaining international peace and security.

Q3. How many official UN languages are there?

A.4
B.5
C.6 ✅
D.7
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The United Nations recognizes six official languages—English, French, Russian, Chinese, Spanish, and Arabic—to ensure broad accessibility and accurate translation of all official documents and speeches.

Q4. Who can send up to five representatives to the General Assembly?

A.Each member nation ✅
B.Permanent members only
C.Non‑members
D.Observer states
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: All member states of the United Nations are entitled to send up to five delegates to the General Assembly, although each nation holds only a single vote regardless of the number of representatives.

Q5. Which organ supervises the work of other UN bodies?

A.General Assembly ✅
B.Economic and Social Council
C.Secretariat
D.International Criminal Court
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The General Assembly monitors and evaluates the activities of the UN’s various specialized agencies, ensuring they operate in line with the organization’s overall objectives and budgetary constraints.

Q6. The UN Charter was drawn up in which city?

A.New York
B.London
C.San Francisco ✅
D.Geneva
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Delegates from the Allied powers convened in San Francisco in 1945 to draft the United Nations Charter, establishing the foundational legal framework for the organization.

Q7. In which year was the ILO founded?

A.1919 ✅
B.1945
C.1955
D.1930
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The International Labour Organization was established in 1919 as part of the Treaty of Versailles, making it one of the oldest specialized agencies of the United Nations system.

Q8. What is the primary purpose of the ILO?

A.Promote trade
B.Set labour standards ✅
C.Facilitate military cooperation
D.Explore space
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The ILO’s core mission is to promote decent work and improve labour standards worldwide, ensuring that workers enjoy rights such as safe conditions, fair wages, and freedom of association.

Q9. Where are the ILO headquarters located?

A.Geneva ✅
B.New York
C.Nairobi
D.Vienna
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The International Labour Organization’s headquarters are situated in Geneva, Switzerland, where it coordinates its global programmes and convenes its regular conferences.

Q10. Which of the following is NOT an ILO convention?

A.Convention 87
B.Convention 98
C.Convention 132
D.Convention 200 ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Convention 200 does not exist in the ILO catalogue; the organization has numbered conventions up to the low‑hundreds, but there is no Convention 200.

Q11. A developing country wants to improve workplace safety. Which ILO convention should it prioritize?

A.Convention 155 ✅
B.Convention 182
C.Convention 111
D.Convention 29
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Convention 155 specifically addresses occupational safety and health, providing guidelines for establishing safe work environments, making it the most relevant instrument for a country seeking to enhance workplace safety.

Q12. If a state ratifies Convention 138 (minimum age) and later raises the legal working age, what is the impact on compliance?

A.Improves compliance
B.No impact
C.Violates the convention ✅
D.Leads to suspension
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Raising the legal working age beyond the minimum set by Convention 138 strengthens compliance, but the act of raising the age itself does not violate the convention; rather, it exceeds its requirements.

Q13. During a UN crisis, the General Assembly votes with a two‑thirds majority to admit a new member. What minimum fraction of votes is required?

A.0.5
B.0.667 ✅
C.0.75
D.0.8
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A two‑thirds majority corresponds to approximately 66.7 % of the votes, which is the threshold the General Assembly must meet for decisions of particular importance, such as admitting new members.

Q14. The UN Security Council has five permanent members with veto power. If three permanent members vote against a resolution, what is the outcome?

A.Resolution passes
B.Resolution fails ✅
C.Requires a tie‑break
D.Requires a supermajority
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Any negative vote by a permanent member constitutes a veto, so if three permanent members oppose a draft, the resolution cannot be adopted and therefore fails.

Q15. An ILO tripartite conference includes government, employers, and workers. Which principle does this reflect?

A.Collective bargaining
B.Tripartism ✅
C.Universal suffrage
D.Non‑intervention
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The inclusion of government, employers, and workers exemplifies the principle of tripartism, a cornerstone of ILO governance that ensures balanced representation of all key labour‑related stakeholders.

Q16. The ILO's Decent Work Agenda includes four pillars. Which of the following is NOT one of them?

A.Employment
B.Rights at work
C.Social protection
D.Military service ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Decent Work Agenda focuses on employment, rights at work, social protection, and social dialogue; military service is unrelated to the agenda’s objectives and therefore is not a pillar.

Q17. A country wants to withdraw from an ILO convention it ratified. According to ILO rules, what is required?

A.Simple notification
B.Two‑year notice ✅
C.Unanimous member vote
D.No withdrawal allowed
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The ILO permits a member state to denounce a convention after a two‑year notice period following the convention’s entry into force for that state, allowing orderly withdrawal.

Q18. How does ECOSOC’s role differ from the General Assembly?

A.ECOSOC has legislative power
B.GA focuses on budget
C.ECOSOC handles economic and social issues ✅
D.GA handles peacekeeping
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: ECOSOC is tasked with coordinating the UN’s economic, social, and related work, while the General Assembly deals with broader issues such as the budget, admission of members, and overall policy direction.

Q19. A UN peacekeeping mission is authorized under Chapter VII of the UN Charter. What does Chapter VII allow?

A.Peaceful settlement only
B.Use of force ✅
C.Economic sanctions
D.Cultural exchange
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Chapter VII empowers the Security Council to take enforcement actions, including the use of force, to maintain or restore international peace and security when non‑peaceful measures are insufficient.

Q20. If the International Court of Justice issues an advisory opinion, is it binding?

A.Yes
B.No ✅
C.Only on parties
D.Only on UN bodies
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Advisory opinions from the ICJ are not legally binding; they provide expert legal guidance to the requesting UN organ but do not create enforceable obligations.

Q21. A member state reports high unemployment. Which ILO program would most directly address this?

A.Technical Cooperation Programme
B.Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work
C.Global Wage Report
D.Decent Work Agenda ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Decent Work Agenda directly targets job creation and quality employment, making it the most relevant ILO initiative for a country seeking to reduce unemployment.

Q22. What is the current status of the UN Trusteeship Council?

A.Active with new members
B.Suspended ✅
C.Merged with ECOSOC
D.Expanded role
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Trusteeship Council has been formally suspended since 1994, having completed its mandate of overseeing the transition of trust territories to self‑government.

Q23. Which organ decides the UN assessment scale for member contributions?

A.General Assembly ✅
B.Security Council
C.Secretariat
D.International Court of Justice
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The General Assembly, through its Fifth Committee, determines the scale of assessments that each member state must pay to fund the United Nations’ regular budget.

Q24. Which element is part of the ILO’s “Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work”?

A.Freedom of association ✅
B.Right to free trade
C.Right to private property
D.Right to military service
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Freedom of association and the right to organize are core components of the ILO’s fundamental principles, ensuring that workers can collectively bargain and protect their interests.

Q25. In a UN vote on a peacekeeping mission, a permanent member abstains. Does the resolution pass if it has the required majority?

A.Yes ✅
B.No
C.Depends on veto
D.Requires unanimous consent
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An abstention by a permanent member does not count as a veto; therefore, if the resolution meets the required majority, it can be adopted despite the abstention.

Q26. Compare the voting procedures of the UN General Assembly and the UN Security Council. Which statement accurately reflects a key difference?

A.GA requires unanimity for all decisions
B.SC decisions can be vetoed by any permanent member ✅
C.GA has veto power
D.SC requires two‑thirds majority including all permanent members
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Security Council’s decisions can be blocked by a single veto from any of its five permanent members, whereas the General Assembly generally decides by simple or two‑thirds majority without any veto power.

Q27. A state has been expelled from the UN for violating Charter principles. According to UN practice, what is the process for readmission?

A.Simple majority vote in GA
B.Recommendation by SC and two‑thirds GA vote ✅
C.Automatic after five years
D.No readmission possible
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Readmission requires the Security Council to recommend the state’s return, followed by a two‑thirds majority approval in the General Assembly, ensuring both political and procedural legitimacy.

Q28. ILO Convention 111 addresses discrimination. If a nation adopts a law allowing gender‑based wage differentials, how does this interact with Convention 111?

A.It complies
B.It partially complies
C.It violates ✅
D.It is neutral
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Convention 111 obliges member states to eliminate discrimination in employment, including wage disparities based on gender; a law permitting such differentials directly contravenes the convention’s provisions.

Q29. During the Cold War, the UN failed to resolve a conflict due to vetoes. Which structural feature most contributed to this stalemate?

A.Lack of funding
B.Veto power of permanent members ✅
C.Absence of a standing army
D.Limited membership
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The permanent members’ veto right allowed any of them to block substantive resolutions, often leading to deadlock when geopolitical interests diverged, as vividly illustrated during Cold‑War crises.

Q30. The ILO’s principle of “freedom of association” is protected under which conventions, and what are its core obligations?

A.Convention 87, requiring right to form organisations
B.Convention 98, requiring right to collective bargaining
C.Both 87 and 98 ✅
D.Neither
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Both Convention 87 (freedom of association) and Convention 98 (right to collective bargaining) jointly safeguard the ability of workers to organize and negotiate, forming the legal backbone of the ILO’s association principle.

Q31. A multinational corporation violates an ILO standard in a host country with weak enforcement. What mechanisms can the ILO employ to address this?

A.Direct sanctions
B.Trade embargo
C.Supervisory complaint and reporting ✅
D.Military intervention
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The ILO can receive supervisory complaints, conduct investigations, and publish findings, thereby applying moral and reputational pressure on the corporation and encouraging corrective action, even when national enforcement is limited.

Q32. The UN Charter’s Article 2(4) prohibits the use of force, yet Chapter VII allows exceptions. Explain the legal tension between these provisions.

A.No tension
B.Chapter VII overrides Article 2(4)
C.Chapter VII provides a framework for collective security that can legitimize force ✅
D.Article 2(4) is optional
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Article 2(4) bans unilateral force, while Chapter VII authorizes collective action by the Security Council; this creates a nuanced balance where force is permissible only when sanctioned by the Council, reconciling the prohibition with collective security needs.

Q33. An ILO member state has ratified Convention 138 (minimum age) but allows child labour under 15 in agriculture. How does this affect its compliance status?

A.Fully compliant
B.Partially compliant
C.Non‑compliant ✅
D.No effect
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Convention 138 sets a universal minimum age for employment; permitting child labour below that age, even in agriculture, breaches the convention, rendering the state non‑compliant with its obligations.
