📝 All UN peacekeeping Cambodia MCQs

Q1. Who led the Khmer Rouge that seized power in Cambodia in 1975?

🔹 A. Pol Pot
🔹 B. Hun Sen
🔹 C. Norodom Sihanouk
🔹 D. Khieu Samphan

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Pol Pot was the charismatic leader of the Khmer Rouge, a communist guerrilla group that overthrew Prince Sihanouk’s government in April 1975, initiating a period of extreme brutality and mass killings.

Q2. In which year did the Vietnamese army invade Cambodia to oust the Khmer Rouge?

🔹 A. 1975
🔹 B. 1978
🔹 C. 1980
🔹 D. 1985

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Vietnamese invasion occurred in late 1978, culminating in the capture of Phnom Penh in early 1979, which forced the Khmer Rouge to flee and allowed a new Vietnamese‑backed government to be installed.

Q3. Approximately what fraction of Cambodia’s population was killed during the Khmer Rouge regime?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{4} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{1}{3} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{1}{2} \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{2}{3} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Scholars estimate that roughly one‑third (\( \approx 33\% \)) of the country’s population died from execution, forced labor, and famine under Pol Pot’s rule between 1975 and 1979.

Q4. Why did the United States initially condemn Vietnam’s 1978 intervention in Cambodia?

🔹 A. It supported the Khmer Rouge
🔹 B. Cold War rivalry
🔹 C. Human‑rights concerns
🔹 D. Economic sanctions

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

During the Cold War, the United States viewed Vietnam as a Soviet ally; therefore any Vietnamese action, even against the brutal Khmer Rouge, was framed as expansionist and condemned by the U.S.

Q5. Which political party won the 1993 Cambodian elections?

🔹 A. Cambodian People's Party
🔹 B. FUNCINPEC
🔹 C. Khmer National Party
🔹 D. Sam Rainsy Party

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The FUNCINPEC party, led by Prince Norodom Sihanouk’s son, won the June 1993 elections, marking the first relatively free vote after decades of conflict and establishing a coalition government.

Q6. How did Cold War dynamics influence the UN’s ability to mediate the Cambodian peace process?

🔹 A. They accelerated negotiations
🔹 B. They delayed UN involvement
🔹 C. They forced the UN to stay neutral
🔹 D. They had no effect

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Because the United States and the Soviet Union were competing for influence, the UN had to maintain strict neutrality, which slowed early mediation but ultimately helped create a balanced peace framework acceptable to all sides.

Q7. If Vietnam had not withdrawn its forces in September 1989, which outcome would have been most likely for Cambodia’s political stability?

🔹 A. Rapid democratization
🔹 B. Continued civil war
🔹 C. UN‑led peacekeeping takeover
🔹 D. Immediate Khmer Rouge resurgence

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A continued Vietnamese presence would likely have prolonged resistance from anti‑Vietnamese factions, sustaining armed conflict and preventing the political opening that led to the 1993 elections.

Q8. Evaluate why the 1993 elections were widely accepted despite the Khmer Rouge’s boycott. Which factor was most decisive?

🔹 A. International observer legitimacy
🔹 B. Economic incentives
🔹 C. Domestic desire for peace
🔹 D. Media coverage

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Cambodian populace, exhausted by decades of war, prioritized stability and reconstruction, making the promise of a peaceful civilian government more compelling than the ideological stance of the Khmer Rouge.

Q9. What proportion of the population survived the Khmer Rouge era, expressed as a percentage?

🔹 A. 0.66
🔹 B. 0.7
🔹 C. 0.75
🔹 D. 0.8

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

If roughly one‑third perished, then about two‑thirds survived, which translates to approximately 66 % of the original population remaining after the regime’s collapse.

Q10. Suppose the UN wanted to accelerate the peace process in 1990. Which diplomatic tool would likely have been most effective?

🔹 A. Economic sanctions on Vietnam
🔹 B. Direct military intervention
🔹 C. Facilitated multi‑track negotiations
🔹 D. Public condemnation of the Khmer Rouge

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Multi‑track negotiations involving all parties—Vietnam, the Khmer Rouge remnants, and Cambodian factions—would have built trust, reduced tensions, and paved the way for a political settlement without resorting to force.

Q11. How did the Khmer Rouge genocide affect Cambodia’s age‑structure in the decades following 1979?

🔹 A. Increased youth proportion
🔹 B. Balanced gender ratio
🔹 C. Reduced elderly population
🔹 D. Higher life expectancy

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The massive loss of adults, especially men, created a demographic bulge of children and young survivors, leading to a long‑term imbalance where the working‑age cohort was under‑represented for many years.

Q12. Analyze how the end of the Cold War altered the United Nations’ capacity to implement a peacekeeping mission in Cambodia. Which change was pivotal?

🔹 A. Reduced superpower vetoes
🔹 B. Increased funding from the USSR
🔹 C. Shift to regional UN bodies
🔹 D. Expansion of UN military capabilities

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The dissolution of the bipolar rivalry removed the strategic deadlock in the Security Council, allowing member states to approve and fund a UN‑led transitional authority without fear of triggering a superpower confrontation.

Q13. If a similar internal conflict erupted today, how would the principle of Responsibility to Protect (R2P) influence international response?

🔹 A. It would justify unilateral intervention
🔹 B. It would require UN Security Council authorization
🔹 C. It would prevent any external involvement
🔹 D. It would mandate economic aid only

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Under R2P, the international community may intervene militarily, but only after the UN Security Council authorizes action, ensuring legitimacy while balancing state sovereignty with the need to stop mass atrocities.

Q14. Which of the following best describes the primary ideological difference between the Khmer Rouge and the Vietnamese-backed government that replaced it?

🔹 A. Monarchist vs. republican
🔹 B. Nationalist vs. communist
🔹 C. Communist vs. socialist‑market
🔹 D. Secular vs. religious

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Khmer Rouge espoused a radical Maoist communism that sought to erase urban life, whereas the Vietnamese-backed regime adopted a more pragmatic socialist‑market approach, allowing limited private enterprise and reconstruction.

Q15. During the 1993 elections, which mechanism ensured the credibility of the voting process?

🔹 A. Electronic voting machines
🔹 B. International observer mission
🔹 C. Domestic media monitoring
🔹 D. Military oversight

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A large contingent of international observers from the UN and NGOs monitored polling stations, counted votes, and reported irregularities, thereby bolstering confidence in the election’s fairness.

Q16. What was the main reason the Khmer Rouge refused to participate in the 1993 elections?

🔹 A. They lacked sufficient funding
🔹 B. They opposed the monarchy
🔹 C. They believed the process favored their enemies
🔹 D. They were already dissolved

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The remnants of the Khmer Rouge viewed the elections as legitimizing a political order that excluded them, and they feared that participation would undermine their revolutionary narrative.

Q17. Consider a scenario where the UN could not secure Vietnamese withdrawal in 1989. Which alternative conflict‑resolution model might have been viable?

🔹 A. Permanent UN trusteeship
🔹 B. Joint Vietnamese‑Cambodian administration
🔹 C. Regional ASEAN mediation
🔹 D. Military annexation by Thailand

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

ASEAN’s regional framework could have offered a neutral platform for dialogue, allowing neighboring states to mediate without directly aligning with either Cold War bloc, thus providing a plausible alternative to a UN trusteeship.

Q18. How did the withdrawal of Vietnamese troops in September 1989 affect the timeline for democratic elections?

🔹 A. Accelerated it by two years
🔹 B. Delayed it by five years
🔹 C. Had no effect
🔹 D. Caused immediate elections in 1990

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The removal of foreign military presence reduced tensions, creating a political environment conducive to negotiations that culminated in the 1993 elections, effectively moving the democratic timetable forward.

Q19. Which of the following statements accurately reflects the UN’s role after the 1993 elections?

🔹 A. It established a permanent military occupation
🔹 B. It administered a transitional authority
🔹 C. It withdrew entirely from Cambodia
🔹 D. It imposed economic sanctions

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations Transitional Authority in Cambodia (UNTAC) oversaw the implementation of the election results, demobilized forces, and helped rebuild governmental institutions until civilian control was restored.

Q20. What was the primary economic challenge Cambodia faced immediately after the Khmer Rouge era?

🔹 A. Hyperinflation
🔹 B. Industrial overcapacity
🔹 C. Food shortages
🔹 D. Currency devaluation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The collapse of agricultural production and the destruction of infrastructure led to severe food shortages, prompting massive humanitarian crises and the need for international aid.

Q21. Which international law principle was invoked to justify the Vietnamese invasion of Cambodia in 1978?

🔹 A. Self‑determination
🔹 B. Collective security
🔹 C. Humanitarian intervention
🔹 D. Non‑intervention

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Vietnam argued that its intervention was a humanitarian response to stop the mass killings perpetrated by the Khmer Rouge, invoking the principle of humanitarian intervention despite the lack of UN authorization.

Q22. If the United Nations had delayed the 1993 elections until 1995, which likely outcome would have been most probable?

🔹 A. Renewed civil war
🔹 B. Successful peace consolidation
🔹 C. Khmer Rouge resurgence
🔹 D. Economic boom

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Postponing elections would have prolonged political uncertainty, likely reigniting armed conflict among factions and providing the Khmer Rouge an opportunity to regroup and reassert influence.

Q23. Which factor most contributed to the international community’s eventual acceptance of the 1993 election results?

🔹 A. Low voter turnout
🔹 B. Presence of a strong monarchy
🔹 C. UN‑backed verification process
🔹 D. Absence of foreign troops

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN’s verification and monitoring provided transparency and legitimacy, ensuring that the electoral process was perceived as fair despite lingering political tensions.

Q24. How did the Cold War rivalry affect the funding sources for the Khmer Rouge’s insurgency after 1979?

🔹 C. It relied on Chinese aid
🔹 D. It was self‑funded through taxes

📖 Explanation:

China, seeking to counter Vietnamese influence, supplied arms and logistical support to the Khmer Rouge, turning the conflict into a proxy battleground within the broader Cold War context.

Q25. What was the main reason Prince Sihanouk’s party was able to win the 1993 elections?

🔹 C. Foreign election interference

📖 Explanation:

After decades of instability, the electorate gravitated toward a familiar figure associated with national unity, giving Prince Sihanouk’s party a decisive advantage in the first free elections.

Q26. Which of the following best describes the status of the Khmer Rouge after the 1993 elections?

🔹 A. Fully integrated into government
🔹 B. Operating as a legal political party
🔹 C. Continuing armed resistance
🔹 D. Disbanded and demobilized

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Even after the elections, the Khmer Rouge retained a guerrilla wing that continued low‑intensity conflict, refusing to recognize the political process and maintaining a hostile stance toward the new government.

Q27. In a hypothetical scenario where the UN could enforce a peace treaty in 1990, which clause would be essential to prevent future Khmer Rouge resurgence?

🔹 D. Mandatory foreign aid

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A comprehensive disarmament clause would remove the Khmer Rouge’s capacity to resume armed insurgency, thereby solidifying the peace framework and reducing the risk of renewed violence.

Q28. What was the approximate duration of Pol Pot’s regime from its seizure of power to its ouster?

🔹 A. 2 years
🔹 B. 3 years

🔹 D. 5 years

📖 Explanation:

Pol Pot’s Khmer Rouge held power from April 1975 until Vietnamese forces captured Phnom Penh in January 1979, a period of roughly three years marked by extreme repression.

Q29. Which element of the UN’s peacekeeping mandate in Cambodia was most controversial among Western nations?

🔹 A. Mandated Vietnamese presence

🔹 D. Military training of local forces

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Western nations were wary of the UN appearing to favor any side, especially given the recent Vietnamese involvement; maintaining strict neutrality was essential to avoid accusations of bias.

Sanctions would have increased pressure on Vietnam, potentially hastening its troop withdrawal and creating a more favorable environment for negotiations, albeit at the cost of humanitarian concerns.

The systematic extermination of educated individuals led to a sharp decline in literacy and professional expertise, hampering post‑conflict reconstruction and delaying socioeconomic development.
