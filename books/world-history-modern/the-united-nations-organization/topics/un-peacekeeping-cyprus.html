What is UN peacekeeping Cyprus?

UNFICYP has operated in Cyprus since 1964 to maintain buffer zones between Greek and Turkish Cypriot communities following intercommunal violence, representing one of the longest-running peacekeeping missions aimed at preventing renewed conflict.

📝 All UN peacekeeping Cyprus MCQs

Q1. In what year did Cyprus gain independence from Britain?

A. 1960 ✅
B. 1964
C. 1974
D. 1980

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Cyprus became an independent republic on 16 February 1960 after negotiations between the United Kingdom, Greece, and Turkey, ending its status as a British colony that began in 1878. This milestone set the stage for later inter‑communal tensions, as the new constitution attempted to balance the interests of both Greek and Turkish Cypriots.

Q2. When did the United Nations first deploy a peacekeeping force to Cyprus?

A. 1962
B. 1963
C. 1964 ✅
D. 1965

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN established its first peacekeeping contingent on the island in March 1964 following the outbreak of inter‑communal violence in 1963. The deployment, known as UNFICYP, aimed to prevent further clashes between Greek and Turkish Cypriots and to maintain a buffer zone, marking the beginning of a long‑term UN presence.

Q3. Approximately what percentage of Cyprus’s population was Greek Cypriot before 1974?

A. 0.7
B. 0.8 ✅
C. 0.85
D. 0.9

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Before the events of 1974, Greek Cypriots comprised roughly \(80\%\) of the island’s total population, with Turkish Cypriots making up about \(18\%\). This demographic imbalance contributed to political tensions, as the larger community sought greater influence while the minority feared marginalisation, fueling the disputes that led to UN intervention.

Q4. Which year saw the Turkish military intervention that resulted in the division of the island?

A. 1967
B. 1970
C. 1974 ✅
D. 1978

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In July 1974, following a Greek‑backed coup aiming for Enosis, Turkey launched a military operation to protect Turkish Cypriots, occupying roughly one‑third of the island’s northern territory. The intervention created a de facto partition that persists today, with the UN maintaining a buffer zone and ongoing diplomatic efforts to resolve the division.

Q5. How many UN troops have been permanently stationed on Cyprus since 1964?

A. 1000
B. 2000
C. 3000 ✅
D. 4000

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Since the initial deployment in 1964, the United Nations has kept approximately \(3{,}000\) peacekeeping personnel on the island. Their continuous presence is intended to monitor the cease‑fire line, facilitate humanitarian assistance, and deter renewed hostilities between the Greek and Turkish communities, making it one of the longest‑running UN missions.

Q6. Which peace proposal was accepted by Turkish Cypriots but rejected by Greek Cypriots in 2004?

A. The Green Line Plan
B. The Annan Plan ✅
C. The Nicosia Accord
D. The Cyprus Unity Initiative

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Annan Plan, proposed by UN Secretary‑General Kofi Annan in 2004, offered a comprehensive solution including a bi‑zonal, bi‑communal federation. While Turkish Cypriots voted in favour, Greek Cypriots overwhelmingly rejected it in a simultaneous referendum, illustrating the deep‑seated mistrust and divergent aspirations that have hampered reconciliation efforts.

Q7. Which colonial power administered Cyprus from 1878 until its independence?

A. France
B. Italy
C. Britain ✅
D. Germany

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Britain assumed control of Cyprus in 1878 under the Cyprus Convention, governing the island as a crown colony for nearly a century. The British administration introduced infrastructure and legal reforms but also sowed seeds of ethnic discord, as the island’s Greek and Turkish populations reacted differently to colonial policies, eventually leading to independence movements.

Q8. What event directly triggered the Turkish invasion of Cyprus in 1974?

A. A UN resolution
B. A Greek‑Cypriot coup d'état ✅
C. A British withdrawal
D. An economic crisis

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The immediate catalyst for Turkey’s 1974 intervention was a coup d'état orchestrated by the Greek military junta, which aimed to achieve Enosis—union with Greece. The overthrow of President Makarios III alarmed the Turkish Cypriot community and prompted Turkey to invoke its guarantor rights under the 1960 Treaty of Guarantee, leading to the invasion.

Q9. Which side expelled Greek Cypriots from the northern part of the island after 1974?

A. Greek forces
B. Turkish forces ✅
C. UN forces
D. British forces

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Following the military actions of 1974, Turkish forces—assisted by Turkish Cypriot militia—secured the northern third of Cyprus and subsequently forced the displacement of Greek Cypriots residing there. This population transfer solidified the island’s division, creating the self‑declared Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus, recognised only by Turkey.

Q10. What is the primary role of UN forces in Cyprus today?

A. Conducting elections
B. Policing the frontier between communities ✅
C. Providing humanitarian aid
D. Training local police

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations Peacekeeping Force in Cyprus (UNFICYP) today chiefly monitors the cease‑fire line, known as the Green Line, separating the Greek‑south and Turkish‑north. Their tasks include patrolling the buffer zone, facilitating civilian movement across checkpoints, and supporting confidence‑building measures, thereby helping to maintain a fragile but enduring peace on the island.

Q11. Why has the UN struggled to draft an acceptable constitution for a reunified Cyprus?

D. Geographic obstacles

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The core obstacle to a mutually acceptable constitution lies in the deep‑rooted political disagreement between Greek and Turkish Cypriots over power‑sharing, security guarantees, and territorial adjustments. Each side demands safeguards that the other perceives as compromising sovereignty, making consensus elusive despite numerous diplomatic initiatives and UN‑mediated proposals over the decades.

Q30. Using basic game theory, which strategy best describes the Turkish Cypriot decision to accept the 2004 Annan Plan?
