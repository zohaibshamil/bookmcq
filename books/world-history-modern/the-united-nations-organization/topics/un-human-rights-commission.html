📝 All UN Human Rights Commission MCQs

Q1. In which year was the Rome Statute, establishing the International Criminal Court, signed?

🔹 A. \(1995\)
🔹 B. \(1998\)
🔹 C. \(2001\)
🔹 D. \(2003\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Rome Statute was adopted on 17 July \(1998\) by 120 UN member states. It created the legal foundation for a permanent International Criminal Court to prosecute war crimes, genocide, and crimes against humanity, marking a major step in global accountability mechanisms.

Q2. Which city hosts the permanent International Criminal Court inaugurated in March \(2003\)?

🔹 A. The Hague
🔹 B. Geneva
🔹 C. New York
🔹 D. Vienna

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The ICC is based in The Hague, Netherlands. The court officially opened its doors in March \(2003\) after the Rome Statute came into force, providing a dedicated venue for prosecuting the most serious international crimes.

Q3. Who was the first former head of state tried before an international court for crimes against humanity?

🔹 A. Slobodan Milošević
🔹 B. Charles Taylor
🔹 C. Laurent Gbagbo
🔹 D. Radovan Karadžić

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Slobodan Milošević, former president of Yugoslavia, was the first former head of state to be indicted and tried by an international tribunal. His case began in July \(2001\) at The Hague, focusing on alleged atrocities in Bosnia, Croatia, and Kosovo.

Q4. What was the primary reason the United States withdrew its signature from the Rome Statute in May \(2002\)?

🔹 A. Concern over jurisdiction over US peacekeepers
🔹 B. Financial costs of membership
🔹 C. Disagreement with the number of judges
🔹 D. Opposition to war crimes definition

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The US withdrew because of concerns that American citizens, especially peacekeepers, could be subjected to politically motivated prosecutions. This political calculation outweighed financial or procedural objections, leading to the US not ratifying the ICC treaty.

Q5. How many judges constitute the International Criminal Court as originally established?

🔹 A. 12
🔹 B. 15
🔹 C. 18
🔹 D. 21

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ICC was designed to have 18 elected judges, representing the main legal systems of the world. These judges are chosen by the Assembly of States Parties and serve nine‑year terms, ensuring a balanced and independent judicial panel for adjudicating complex international crimes.

Q6. Which two special war‑crimes tribunals were set up by the UN Security Council before the ICC was created?

🔹 A. Nuremberg and Tokyo
🔹 B. Rwanda and Bosnia
🔹 C. Sierra Leone and Kosovo
🔹 D. Cambodia and Yugoslavia

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The UN Security Council established the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda in \(1994\) and the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (covering Bosnia) in \(1995\). These ad‑hoc courts laid groundwork for the later permanent ICC.

Q7. Which of the following was NOT a crime listed in the Rome Statute?

🔹 A. Genocide
🔹 B. Piracy on the high seas
🔹 C. War crimes
🔹 D. Crimes against humanity

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity are core crimes under the Rome Statute, piracy on the high seas is not enumerated. Piracy is addressed under other international conventions, not within the ICC’s jurisdiction.

Q8. Which UN body originally discussed the idea of an international criminal court in \(1937\)?

🔹 A. League of Nations
🔹 B. UN General Assembly
🔹 C. UN Security Council
🔹 D. International Law Commission

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The concept of an international criminal court was first raised at a League of Nations convention in \(1937\). Although the League later dissolved, its early deliberations foreshadowed the eventual establishment of the ICC decades later.

Q9. What was the approximate duration of Slobodan Milošević’s trial before his death?

🔹 A. Two years
🔹 B. Three years
🔹 C. Four years
🔹 D. Almost five years

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Milošević’s trial began in July \(2001\) and continued until his death in March \(2006\), lasting nearly five years. The lengthy proceedings reflected the complexity of gathering evidence and ensuring due process in high‑profile international criminal cases.

Q10. How many UN member states initially signed the Rome Statute in July \(1998\)?

🔹 A. 85
🔹 B. 100
🔹 C. 120
🔹 D. 150

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A total of 120 UN member states signed the Rome Statute at its inception, demonstrating broad international support for a permanent court to address the gravest crimes. This substantial signatory base helped legitise the ICC’s creation and subsequent operations.

Q11. If a UN peacekeeper from a non‑signatory state is accused of a war crime, which legal principle determines jurisdiction?

🔹 A. Territorial jurisdiction
🔹 B. Universal jurisdiction
🔹 C. Extraterritorial jurisdiction
🔹 D. Protective jurisdiction

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Universal jurisdiction allows states or international bodies to prosecute certain grave offenses, such as war crimes, regardless of where they occurred or the nationality of the perpetrator. This principle underpins the ICC’s ability to consider cases involving peacekeepers from states that have not ratified the Rome Statute.

Q12. A country ratifies the Rome Statute but later withdraws. Which outcome best reflects ICC jurisdiction over crimes committed after withdrawal?

🔹 A. No jurisdiction whatsoever
🔹 B. Jurisdiction only for crimes committed before withdrawal
🔹 C. Jurisdiction only if the UN Security Council refers the case
🔹 D. Jurisdiction persists for all crimes until the ICC’s term ends

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Even after withdrawal, the ICC can still prosecute crimes if the UN Security Council issues a referral under Chapter VII of the UN Charter. This mechanism ensures that serious violations can be addressed regardless of a state's later decision to exit the treaty.

Q13. A UN‑appointed humanitarian worker from a signatory state is accused of facilitating genocide in a conflict zone. Which ICC element must be proven to secure a conviction?

🔹 A. Intent to destroy a protected group
🔹 B. Failure to report the crime
🔹 C. Presence in the territory
🔹 D. Nationality of the accused

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To convict for genocide, the prosecution must demonstrate the specific intent (dolus specialis) to destroy, in whole or in part, a protected group. Mere participation without this intent is insufficient; the mental element is a critical threshold for ICC genocide charges.

Q14. Why did the Cold War impede progress toward establishing a permanent international criminal court?

🔹 A. Lack of legal expertise
🔹 B. Competing superpower interests
🔹 C. Absence of UN Charter provisions
🔹 D. Insufficient funding

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

During the Cold War, the United States and the Soviet Union prioritized geopolitical rivalry over collective legal initiatives. Their opposing views on sovereignty and international justice stalled consensus, preventing the formation of a permanent court until the post‑Cold War era allowed broader cooperation.

Q15. Which legal document formally inaugurated the International Criminal Court in March \(2003\)?

🔹 A. The Geneva Conventions
🔹 B. The Rome Statute
🔹 C. The UN Charter
🔹 D. The Hague Convention

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Rome Statute serves as the ICC’s founding treaty. After reaching the required number of ratifications, the Statute entered into force on 1 July \(2002\) and the court was officially inaugurated in March \(2003\), establishing its jurisdiction and institutional framework.

Q16. If a non‑signatory state requests the ICC to investigate alleged crimes on its territory, what is the required condition?

🔹 A. UN Security Council referral
🔹 B. Consent of the state where the crimes occurred
🔹 C. Approval by the ICC Assembly of States Parties
🔹 D. A majority vote by ICC judges

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The ICC can only exercise jurisdiction over crimes on the territory of a state party or when the UN Security Council refers a situation under Chapter VII. A non‑signatory state's request alone does not automatically confer jurisdiction without a Security Council mandate.

Q17. A multinational corporation supplies weapons used in a genocide. Under the ICC’s legal framework, which principle could be invoked to hold corporate executives accountable?

🔹 A. Corporate liability doctrine
🔹 B. Command responsibility
🔹 C. Joint criminal enterprise
🔹 D. State responsibility

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Joint criminal enterprise allows the ICC to prosecute individuals who contribute to the planning or execution of a common criminal purpose, such as supplying weapons that facilitate genocide. Executives who knowingly aid the enterprise can be held criminally responsible under this doctrine.

Q18. Which of the following best describes the relationship between the ICC and the UN Security Council?

🔹 A. The ICC is subordinate to the Security Council
🔹 B. The Security Council can refer cases to the ICC, but not vice versa
🔹 C. The ICC can override Security Council resolutions
🔹 D. Both bodies share equal decision‑making power

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Security Council possesses the authority to refer situations to the ICC under Chapter VII, enabling the Court to act even when a state has not ratified the Rome Statute. However, the ICC cannot compel the Council to act or overturn its resolutions.

Q19. What is the minimum number of ratifications required for the Rome Statute to enter into force?

🔹 A. 30
🔹 B. 45
🔹 C. 60
🔹 D. 100

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Rome Statute required at least 60 ratifications to become binding. Once this threshold was reached, the treaty entered into force on 1 July \(2002\), activating the ICC’s jurisdiction over qualifying crimes.

Q20. A peacekeeper from a signatory state is accused of a crime in a non‑signatory country. Which ICC jurisdictional basis applies?

🔹 A. Territorial jurisdiction of the non‑signatory state
🔹 B. Personal jurisdiction over the peacekeeper
🔹 C. Both A and B
🔹 D. Neither A nor B

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The ICC can assert personal jurisdiction over nationals of states parties, regardless of where the alleged crime occurred. Thus, a peacekeeper from a signatory state can be prosecuted even if the alleged acts took place in a non‑signatory country.

Q21. Which factor most directly influenced the United States’ decision to sign separate agreements with 37 states in June \(2003\)?

🔹 A. Desire to expand ICC jurisdiction
🔹 B. Concern over diplomatic immunity
🔹 C. Protection of US nationals from ICC prosecution
🔹 D. Interest in financial contributions to the ICC

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United States pursued bilateral agreements to ensure that its citizens, particularly military and peacekeeping personnel, would not be subject to ICC jurisdiction. These agreements reflected a strategic effort to safeguard US nationals from potential prosecution.

Q22. An analyst argues that the ICC’s 18‑judge composition may hinder representation of diverse legal traditions. Which reform proposal directly addresses this concern?

🔹 A. Increasing the number of judges to 24
🔹 B. Creating regional sub‑courts
🔹 C. Rotating judges every two years
🔹 D. appointing ad‑hoc judges from under‑represented systems

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Expanding the bench to 24 judges would allow a broader inclusion of civil law, common law, and other legal traditions, enhancing the Court’s representativeness. This structural change directly tackles concerns about limited diversity within the existing 18‑member composition.

Q23. Why did the ICC’s trial of Slobodan Milošević last nearly five years before his death?

🔹 A. Lack of evidence
🔹 B. Complexity of gathering witness testimony across multiple countries
🔹 C. Repeated appeals by the defense
🔹 D. Political interference from the UN

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Milošević trial involved extensive evidence collection, including testimonies from victims and experts across Bosnia, Croatia, and Kosovo. Coordinating this multinational evidence, ensuring witness protection, and managing procedural safeguards contributed significantly to the prolonged duration.

Q24. Which legal doctrine allows the ICC to hold a commander liable for crimes committed by subordinates?

🔹 A. Force majeure
🔹 B. Respondeat superior
🔹 C. Command responsibility
🔹 D. Stare decisis

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Command responsibility establishes that military or political leaders can be criminally liable for crimes they ordered, knew about, or failed to prevent or punish. This doctrine is central to ICC prosecutions involving hierarchical structures where subordinates commit atrocities.

Q25. If a state withdraws from the Rome Statute after a crime has been committed, what happens to the ICC’s jurisdiction over that crime?

🔹 A. Jurisdiction is lost retroactively
🔹 B. Jurisdiction continues for crimes committed before withdrawal
🔹 C. Jurisdiction depends on UN Security Council referral
🔹 D. Jurisdiction is transferred to domestic courts

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The ICC retains jurisdiction over crimes that occurred while the state was still a party to the Rome Statute. Withdrawal does not affect ongoing cases related to past offenses, preserving the Court’s authority over those incidents.

Q26. A UN‑appointed judge from a non‑signatory country is nominated to the ICC. Which eligibility criterion must they satisfy?

🔹 A. Must be a citizen of a state party
🔹 B. Must have at least ten years of legal experience
🔹 C. Must be elected by the Assembly of States Parties
🔹 D. Must have served in the International Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Eligibility for ICC judges requires election by the Assembly of States Parties, regardless of nationality, provided the candidate possesses the requisite legal expertise and moral integrity. This process ensures a merit‑based selection beyond simple state‑party affiliation.

Q27. What was the primary purpose of the 1989 suggestion to revive the ICC concept, according to the text?

🔹 A. To address climate change
🔹 B. To combat drug‑traffickers and terrorists
🔹 C. To resolve trade disputes
🔹 D. To manage global pandemics

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

In \(1989\), the ICC concept resurfaced as a potential mechanism to prosecute individuals involved in drug trafficking and terrorism. This focus reflected emerging security concerns of the era, linking criminal accountability with broader international peace and stability objectives.

Q28. Which of the following best explains why the ICC can prosecute individuals from non‑signatory states under certain circumstances?

🔹 A. Universal jurisdiction over all crimes
🔹 B. Referral by the UN Security Council
🔹 C. Reciprocal agreements with non‑signatory states
🔹 D. Automatic jurisdiction once crimes are reported

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The ICC’s jurisdiction can be triggered when the UN Security Council refers a situation, even if the alleged perpetrators or the territory involved belong to non‑signatory states. This mechanism ensures that serious crimes do not escape accountability due to gaps in treaty participation.

Q29. A diplomat from a signatory state commits genocide while on assignment in a non‑signatory country. Which ICC jurisdictional basis is most applicable?

🔹 A. Personal jurisdiction over the diplomat
🔹 B. Territorial jurisdiction of the non‑signatory state
🔹 C. Both personal and territorial jurisdiction
🔹 D. None, diplomatic immunity prevails

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ICC can assert both personal jurisdiction (because the diplomat is a national of a state party) and territorial jurisdiction (if the non‑signatory state consents or the Security Council refers the case). Diplomatic immunity may be challenged under international criminal law, allowing prosecution.

Q30. Why did the ICC’s establishment require the election of 18 judges rather than a smaller panel?

🔹 A. To ensure representation from all continents
🔹 B. To meet the minimum number stipulated by the UN Charter
🔹 C. To handle an anticipated high caseload and diverse legal systems
🔹 D. To satisfy the demands of the United Nations Security Council

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A larger bench of 18 judges enables the ICC to manage a substantial docket of complex cases while incorporating a variety of legal traditions. This structure balances efficiency with the need for diverse expertise across different jurisdictions.

Q31. If a state party to the Rome Statute experiences a civil war and fails to investigate alleged war crimes, what ICC mechanism may be invoked?

🔹 A. Complementarity principle
🔹 B. Retroactive jurisdiction
🔹 C. Ad hoc tribunal creation
🔹 D. Security Council veto

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The complementarity principle holds that the ICC acts only when national jurisdictions are unwilling or unable to prosecute. In cases where a state party does not investigate alleged war crimes during a civil conflict, the ICC may step in to fulfill its mandate.

Q32. An NGO alleges that a multinational corporation aided in genocide by providing logistical support. Under ICC law, which element must the prosecution establish?

🔹 A. Direct participation in killing
🔹 B. Knowledge of the genocidal intent
🔹 C. Financial profit from the crime
🔹 D. Ownership of the corporation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

To secure a conviction, the prosecution must demonstrate that corporate executives had knowledge of the genocidal intent and knowingly contributed to the enterprise. Mere financial gain or peripheral involvement is insufficient without proven awareness of the crime’s purpose.

Q33. Which of the following statements accurately reflects the ICC’s relationship with the UN General Assembly?

🔹 A. The ICC reports directly to the General Assembly
🔹 B. The General Assembly appoints ICC judges
🔹 C. The ICC operates independently but can receive advisory opinions from the Assembly
🔹 D. The General Assembly can amend the Rome Statute

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ICC is an autonomous institution; however, the UN General Assembly may request advisory opinions on legal questions concerning the Court. This consultative relationship allows the Assembly to seek clarification without compromising the ICC’s independence.

Q34. Why was the trial of Slobodan Milošević considered a landmark in international criminal law?

🔹 A. It was the first trial of a sitting head of state
🔹 B. It introduced the concept of universal jurisdiction
🔹 C. It was the first case where the ICC exercised the death penalty
🔹 D. It was the first trial of a former head of state before an international court

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Milošević’s case marked the first occasion a former head of state was brought before an international tribunal, setting a precedent for accountability at the highest political levels. This milestone underscored the evolving reach of international criminal jurisprudence.
