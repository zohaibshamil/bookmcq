What is UN financial and economic agencies?

UN financial and economic agencies include IMF, World Bank Group, and regional development banks that provide loans, technical assistance, and policy advice to promote global financial stability, reduce poverty, and support sustainable economic development.

📝 All UN financial and economic agencies MCQs

Q1. In what year was the Rome Statute, which established the International Criminal Court, signed by 120 UN member states?

A.1995
B.1998 ✅
C.2001
D.2003
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Rome Statute was adopted and opened for signature on 17 July 1998, with 120 United Nations member states signing the agreement that year. This treaty later led to the creation of the permanent International Criminal Court headquartered in The Hague.

Q2. Where is the International Criminal Court headquartered?

A.Geneva
B.New York
C.The Hague ✅
D.Brussels
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The International Criminal Court is based in The Hague, Netherlands. Established by the Rome Statute, the court operates from this location, providing a neutral venue for the prosecution of war crimes, genocide, and crimes against humanity on a global scale.

Q3. What is the term length for an ICC judge?

A.4 years
B.6 years
C.9 years ✅
D.12 years
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: ICC judges are elected for a single term of nine years without the possibility of re‑election. This lengthy term is intended to ensure judicial independence, allowing judges to adjudicate complex international criminal cases without undue political pressure.

Q4. How many judges constitute the ICC?

A.12
B.15
C.18 ✅
D.21
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The International Criminal Court is composed of 18 judges elected by the Assembly of States Parties. These judges represent the principal legal systems of the world, ensuring a balanced and diverse bench to hear cases concerning the most serious international crimes.

Q5. If a UN peacekeeper from the United States is accused of a war crime, which principle is most likely cited by the US to oppose ICC jurisdiction?

A.Sovereignty ✅
B.Immunity of heads of state
C.Non‑refoulement
D.Double jeopardy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The United States often invokes the principle of sovereignty, arguing that domestic courts are best positioned to try its nationals. By emphasizing national jurisdiction, the US seeks to limit the ICC’s reach over its peacekeepers and avoid perceived external interference.

Q6. Why did the Cold War delay the creation of a permanent International Criminal Court?

A.Lack of funding
B.Ideological rivalry between blocs ✅
C.Technological constraints
D.Language barriers
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: During the Cold War, the East and West blocs competed for influence, making cooperation on a universal criminal tribunal difficult. Mutual suspicion hindered consensus on jurisdiction, legal standards, and enforcement mechanisms, postponing the ICC’s establishment until the geopolitical climate eased.

Q7. If a country withdraws from the Rome Statute after ratification, which consequence is most likely?

A.Immediate loss of ICC jurisdiction
B.No effect on ongoing cases ✅
C.Automatic reinstatement of domestic courts
D.Mandatory UN sanctions
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Withdrawal does not retroactively affect cases already initiated. The ICC retains jurisdiction over crimes committed while the state was a party, and ongoing investigations may continue, though the state’s future cooperation becomes uncertain and politically sensitive.

Q8. Which legal doctrine allows the ICC to assert jurisdiction over a US peacekeeper accused of war crimes?

A.Complementarity ✅
B.Universal jurisdiction
C.Territorial principle
D.Personal jurisdiction
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The principle of complementarity holds that the ICC may intervene only when national jurisdictions are unwilling or unable to prosecute. By invoking this doctrine, the Court can claim jurisdiction over a US peacekeeper if domestic proceedings are inadequate or absent.

Q9. In which year did Slobodan Milošević's trial open at the ICC?

A.1999
B.2000
C.2001 ✅
D.2002
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Milošević’s trial commenced on 12 July 2001 in The Hague, marking the first time a former head of state faced charges before an international criminal tribunal. The proceedings addressed alleged crimes against humanity across Bosnia, Croatia, and Kosovo.

Q10. Approximately how long did Milošević's trial last before his death?

A.2 years
B.3 years
C.5 years ✅
D.7 years
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Milošević’s trial spanned nearly five years, beginning in July 2001 and ending with his death in March 2006. The prolonged duration reflected the complexity of gathering evidence, witness testimony, and legal arguments in a high‑profile international case.

Q11. Milošević faced charges related to crimes in which three regions?

A.Bosnia, Croatia, Kosovo
B.Rwanda, Bosnia, Kosovo
C.Bosnia, Croatia, Kosovo ✅
D.Rwanda, Croatia, Kosovo
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The indictment alleged that Milošević bore responsibility for crimes against humanity committed in Bosnia, Croatia, and Kosovo. These allegations covered a range of atrocities, including persecution, murder, and forced displacement during the Yugoslav wars.

Q12. If the ICC were to try a US peacekeeper, which procedural safeguard ensures fairness?

A.Right to a public trial
B.Right to counsel ✅
C.Right to appeal to the UN Security Council
D.Right to be tried by a jury of peers
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The right to counsel guarantees that an accused individual receives legal representation, enabling a fair defense. This safeguard is essential in international criminal proceedings to balance the prosecutorial power of the ICC and protect the rights of the defendant.

Q13. By June 2003, the US had signed separate agreements with how many states promising that no US personnel would be surrendered to the ICC?

A.27
B.37 ✅
C.47
D.57
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In June 2003, the United States concluded bilateral agreements with 37 countries, assuring that its nationals, including military and peacekeeping personnel, would not be handed over to the International Criminal Court for prosecution.

Q14. Which UN organ established the special war crimes tribunals for Rwanda (1994) and Bosnia (1995)?

A.General Assembly
B.Security Council ✅
C.Economic and Social Council
D.International Court of Justice
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The United Nations Security Council created the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda and the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia. These ad‑hoc tribunals were designed to address grave violations of international humanitarian law in the respective conflicts.

Q15. Why did the US fear ‘politicized prosecutions’ of its peacekeepers by the ICC?

A.Concern over biased judges
B.Fear of double jeopardy
C.Perception of selective enforcement
D.Desire to strengthen UN ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: US officials worried that politically motivated prosecutions could target American peacekeepers, undermining national sovereignty and morale. They feared that the ICC might be used as a tool for selective justice, reflecting geopolitical interests rather than impartial legal standards.

Q16. If a state withdraws from the ICC, can it be re‑admitted?

A.Yes after 1 year
B.Yes after 5 years
C.No permanent ban
D.Yes anytime after ratification ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A state may re‑join the Rome Statute at any time by depositing a new instrument of ratification, provided it satisfies the procedural requirements. Withdrawal does not create an irreversible barrier; re‑admission depends on political will and compliance with the treaty’s obligations.

Q17. What percentage of UN member states signed the Rome Statute in 1998, given there were roughly 193 members?

A.0.62
B.0.625
C.0.622 ✅
D.0.621
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The calculation is \( \frac{120}{193}\times 100 \approx 62.2\% \). Thus, about sixty‑two percent of United Nations members endorsed the Rome Statute when it opened for signature, providing a broad foundation for the ICC’s legitimacy.

Q18. In which year was the ICC formally inaugurated?

A.2000
B.2001
C.2002
D.2003 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The International Criminal Court was officially inaugurated on 1 July 2003 in The Hague, following the entry into force of the Rome Statute two years earlier. This event marked the commencement of the Court’s permanent operations to prosecute the most serious international crimes.

Q19. Which country hosted the ICC trial of Milošević?

A.Netherlands ✅
B.Belgium
C.Germany
D.United Kingdom
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Milošević’s trial was held at the International Criminal Court’s facilities in The Hague, Netherlands. The Dutch venue provided a neutral location for the proceedings, reflecting the ICC’s commitment to impartial justice for individuals accused of war crimes and crimes against humanity.

Q20. A country argues its domestic courts can handle war crimes. Which ICC principle does this invoke?

A.Complementarity ✅
B.Universal jurisdiction
C.Jurisdictional supremacy
D.Nullum crimen
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The principle of complementarity asserts that the ICC acts only when national jurisdictions are unwilling or unable to prosecute. By claiming sufficient domestic capacity, a state seeks to preempt ICC intervention, emphasizing the primacy of internal legal mechanisms.

Q21. If the ICC issues an arrest warrant for a sitting head of state, which legal doctrine may limit enforcement?

A.Immunity of sitting heads of state under customary international law
B.Territorial sovereignty
C.Non‑intervention
D.All of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: All listed doctrines—customary immunity for sitting heads of state, respect for territorial sovereignty, and the principle of non‑intervention—can impede the practical enforcement of an ICC arrest warrant against a current leader, creating complex legal and diplomatic challenges.

Q22. How many special war crimes tribunals were established before the ICC?

A.One
B.Two ✅
C.Three
D.Four
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Before the permanent International Criminal Court, the United Nations created two ad‑hoc tribunals: the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda (1994) and the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (1995). These bodies addressed specific conflicts and laid groundwork for the ICC.

Q23. Which of the following was NOT a reason for US opposition to the ICC?

A.Fear of politicized prosecutions
B.Concern over jurisdiction over citizens
C.Desire to strengthen the UN ✅
D.Opposition to the definition of war crimes
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The United States did not oppose the ICC out of a desire to bolster the United Nations; rather, its concerns centered on potential prosecutions of its nationals, perceived politicization, and disagreements over legal definitions. Strengthening the UN was not a primary motive.

Q24. If a UN peacekeeper commits genocide in a non‑ICC member state, can the ICC claim jurisdiction?

A.Yes under universal jurisdiction
B.No because the state is not a party
C.Yes if the Security Council refers the case ✅
D.No unless the peacekeeper's home state is a party
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The ICC can obtain jurisdiction over crimes committed in non‑party states if the UN Security Council refers the situation to the Court. This referral overrides the usual territorial limitation, allowing the ICC to prosecute the peacekeeper despite the state’s non‑party status.

Q25. The ICC is an example of what type of international organization?

A.Specialized agency
B.Judicial organ ✅
C.Economic commission
D.Regional organization
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The International Criminal Court functions as a judicial organ of the international community, tasked with adjudicating the gravest crimes such as genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity, rather than serving as a specialized agency or economic body.

Q26. Which article of the Rome Statute defines the crime of genocide?

A.Article 5
B.Article 6 ✅
C.Article 7
D.Article 8
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Article 6 of the Rome Statute enumerates the crime of genocide, outlining the specific intent required to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnical, racial, or religious group. This provision forms a core element of the ICC’s jurisdiction over the most serious offenses.

Q27. How does the establishment of the ICC influence the development of international criminal law norms?

A.It creates binding precedents that shape state practice ✅
B.It merely records existing customs without effect
C.It weakens existing treaties by introducing redundancy
D.It replaces national criminal codes entirely
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The ICC’s jurisprudence generates authoritative case law, clarifying definitions and standards for crimes such as genocide and war crimes. These decisions inform state behavior, contribute to the progressive development of customary international law, and encourage harmonization of domestic legislation with global norms.

Q28. The ICC's jurisdiction is limited to crimes committed after which date?

A.1 July 1998
B.1 July 2000
C.1 July 2002 ✅
D.1 July 2004
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Rome Statute entered into force on 1 July 2002, establishing the ICC’s temporal jurisdiction. Consequently, the Court may only prosecute crimes that occurred on or after that date, ensuring that it does not apply retroactively to earlier acts.

Q29. Which of the following is NOT a crime under the ICC's jurisdiction?

A.War crimes
B.Genocide
C.Piracy ✅
D.Crimes against humanity
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Piracy, while a serious offense under maritime law, is not listed among the core crimes—genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity—that fall within the ICC’s mandate as defined by the Rome Statute.

Q30. A state party to the Rome Statute refuses to cooperate with an ICC investigation. What mechanism can the ICC use to compel cooperation?

A.Referral to the UN Security Council
B.Request to the International Court of Justice
C.Issuing a summons and imposing sanctions
D.Requesting assistance from Interpol ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: When a state fails to comply with ICC requests, the Court may issue a formal request for cooperation to Interpol, seeking international police assistance to locate suspects, gather evidence, or enforce arrest warrants, thereby enhancing its investigative capacity.

Q31. Which US president withdrew the US signature from the Rome Statute?

A.Clinton
B.Bush ✅
C.Obama
D.Trump
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: President George W. Bush, in May 2002, directed the United States to withdraw its signature from the Rome Statute, reflecting concerns that the ICC might assert jurisdiction over American personnel, particularly military and peacekeeping forces.

Q32. Which of the following is NOT a crime under the ICC's jurisdiction?

A.War crimes
B.Genocide
C.Piracy ✅
D.Crimes against humanity
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Piracy is not among the core crimes—genocide, war crimes, and crimes against humanity—enumerated in the Rome Statute. While piracy is a serious violation of international law, it falls outside the ICC’s mandated jurisdiction.

Q33. Suppose the ICC issues an arrest warrant for a corporate CEO for aiding war crimes. Which principle allows individual criminal responsibility for corporate actions?

A.Command responsibility
B.Joint criminal enterprise ✅
C.Corporate liability
D.Direct perpetration
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Joint criminal enterprise permits the prosecution of individuals who knowingly contribute to the planning or execution of a crime, even when the wrongdoing is carried out through a corporate entity. This doctrine bridges the gap between corporate conduct and personal accountability.
