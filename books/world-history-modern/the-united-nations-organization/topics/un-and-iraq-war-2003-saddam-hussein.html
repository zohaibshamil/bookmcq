What is UN and Iraq war 2003 Saddam Hussein?

The 2003 Iraq War proceeded without explicit UN Security Council authorization despite weapons inspections, causing deep divisions among members and undermining UN credibility when no WMDs were found, revealing tensions between unilateralism and multilateral legitimacy.

📝 All UN and Iraq war 2003 Saddam Hussein MCQs

Q1. In what year did the US‑led coalition begin the invasion that led to the overthrow of Saddam Hussein?

A.1999
B.2001
C.2003 ✅
D.2005
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The invasion, known as Operation Iraqi Freedom, commenced on 20 March 2003 when coalition forces launched a rapid assault across the Iraqi border. This date marks the formal start of the military campaign that ultimately resulted in the removal of Saddam Hussein from power after nearly a quarter‑century of authoritarian rule.

Q2. Which United Nations Security Council resolution authorized member states to use “all necessary means” to disarm Iraq?

A.Resolution 678 ✅
B.Resolution 687
C.Resolution 1441
D.Resolution 1483
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Resolution 678, adopted on 29 November 1990, gave member states the authority to use force if Iraq did not withdraw from Kuwait. It was the legal basis for the coalition’s military action in the Gulf War, establishing the precedent for later UN authorizations concerning Iraqi disarmament and enforcement measures.

Q3. What was the name of the Iraqi city that served as the capital after the fall of Baghdad in 2003?

A.Basra
B.Mosul
C.Fallujah
D.None of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: After the coalition seized Baghdad, no other Iraqi city was formally declared as a new capital. The provisional authorities continued to operate from Baghdad, while other cities such as Basra and Mosul served as regional administrative centres, but none replaced Baghdad as the national capital.

Q4. At the peak of UN peacekeeping in the mid‑1990s, over 80 000 troops from 77 countries were deployed. If each country contributed an equal number of troops, approximately how many troops did each country provide? Use rounding to the nearest whole number.

A.1030
B.1040 ✅
C.1050
D.1060
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Dividing the total force of 80 000 by the 77 contributing nations yields roughly \( \frac{80,000}{77}\approx1,039.0\). Rounding to the nearest whole number gives about 1,040 troops per country. This simplified calculation illustrates the average contribution, though actual national contributions varied widely based on capacity and political willingness.

Q5. Which Iraqi political party emerged as the largest bloc in the January 2005 parliamentary elections?

A.Islamic Dawa Party
B.Iraqi Accord Front
C.United Iraqi Alliance ✅
D.Kurdish Democratic Party
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The United Iraqi Alliance, a coalition of Shi’a parties led primarily by the Islamic Dawa Party, captured the most seats in the 2005 elections. Its dominance reflected the demographic weight of the Shi’a majority and the electorate’s desire for a government that could address post‑invasion reconstruction and security challenges.

Q6. During the initial phase of the 2003 invasion, coalition forces suffered approximately 200 casualties while Iraqi military casualties were estimated at 5 000. What was the ratio of Iraqi to coalition casualties?

A.5 : 1
B.10 : 1
C.25 : 1 ✅
D.50 : 1
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The ratio is calculated by dividing Iraqi casualties (5 000) by coalition casualties (200), yielding \(5,000 ÷ 200 = 25\). Hence the ratio is 25 : 1, indicating that for every coalition casualty there were roughly twenty‑five Iraqi military losses, a figure that underscores the asymmetry of combat power in the opening weeks.

Q7. If the UN had deployed a peacekeeping force equal to 2 % of the total troops it had at its peak (80 000), how many troops would that be?

A.1200
B.1500
C.1600 ✅
D.1800
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Two percent of 80 000 equals \(0.02 \times 80,000 = 1,600\). This modest contingent would represent a symbolic contribution, illustrating how a small fraction of the total force could be allocated to a specific mission, though operational effectiveness would depend on mission scope and rules of engagement.

Q8. Which of the following was a primary justification given by the US for invading Iraq in 2003?

A.Weapons of mass destruction ✅
B.Oil reserves
C.Human rights abuses
D.All of the above
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Bush administration emphasized the alleged existence of weapons of mass destruction (WMD) as the principal reason for the 2003 invasion. While oil interests and concerns about Saddam’s human‑rights record were discussed, the public narrative centered on the threat posed by WMDs, which later proved to be unsubstantiated.

Q9. Consider the timeline: UN Security Council Resolution 687 (1991) imposed disarmament obligations, while Resolution 1441 (2002) warned of “serious consequences”. If a state fails to comply with both, under UN Charter Article 42, what type of action may the Security Council authorize?

A.Economic sanctions
B.Military enforcement ✅
C.Diplomatic mediation
D.No action
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Article 42 allows the Security Council to take “enforcement measures”, including the use of armed force, when peaceful means have failed. Non‑compliance with disarmament resolutions can trigger such measures, enabling member states to act militarily to restore international peace and security, as was argued in the lead‑up to the 2003 Iraq war.

Q10. The overthrow led to a power vacuum that allowed insurgent groups to rise. If insurgent attacks increased by 150 % from 2004 to 2006, and there were 800 attacks in 2004, how many attacks occurred in 2006?

A.1200
B.1600
C.1800
D.2000 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A 150 % increase means the number of attacks grew to 250 % of the original figure: \(800 \times 2.5 = 2,000\). This sharp rise reflects the deteriorating security environment after the collapse of central authority, illustrating how insurgent activity can accelerate when governance structures are weak or absent.

His briefing outlined the objectives of the campaign and underscored the United States’ commitment to removing Saddam Hussein from power.

Q12. After Saddam's removal, the Coalition Provisional Authority (CPA) transferred sovereignty to the Iraqi Interim Government on 28 June 2004. How many months elapsed between the start of the invasion (20 March 2003) and this transfer?

A.12
B.13
C.14
D.15 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: From 20 March 2003 to 28 June 2004 spans roughly 15 months and a few days. Counting full months, March 2003 to March 2004 is 12 months, then April, May, and June add three more months, totaling 15 months. This period reflects the time needed to establish provisional governance structures before handing authority to Iraqi officials.

Q13. Assume the UN could have prevented the invasion by deploying a peacekeeping force of 10 000 troops to Iraq in early 2003. If each troop costs \$150 000 per year, what would be the annual cost?

A.\$0.5 billion
B.\$1.0 billion
C.\$1.5 billion ✅
D.\$2.0 billion
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Multiplying the number of troops (10 000) by the per‑troop annual cost (\$150 000) yields \(10,000 \times 150,000 = 1,500,000,000\). This results in an annual expense of \$1.5 billion, illustrating the substantial financial commitment required for a sizable UN peacekeeping presence in a high‑risk environment.

Q14. Which Iraqi city saw the longest and most intense battle during the 2003 invasion?

A.Baghdad
B.Fallujah ✅
C.Najaf
D.Mosul
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Fallujah became the focal point of prolonged and ferocious fighting, especially during the 2004–2005 confrontations that followed the initial invasion. The city’s resistance, coupled with its strategic location, led to multiple major operations, extensive urban combat, and significant civilian casualties, making it the most hard‑fought engagement of the campaign.

Q15. The UN peacekeeping operations peaked at 80 000 troops. If the number of contributing countries increased by 25 % while total troops remained the same, what would be the new average troops per country?

A.800
B.833 ✅
C.860
D.900
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An increase of 25 % raises the number of contributing nations from 77 to about 96 (77 × 1.25 ≈ 96). Dividing the unchanged total of 80 000 troops by 96 yields roughly \(80,000 ÷ 96 ≈ 833\) troops per country, demonstrating how broader participation lowers the average contribution per nation.

Q16. Which UN mission was specifically established to oversee the disarmament of Iraq after the 1991 Gulf War?

A.UNIKOM ✅
B.UNPROFOR
C.UNOSAT
D.UNMIBH
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: UNIKOM – the United Nations Iraq–Kuwait Observation Mission – was created by Security Council Resolution 689 in 1991 to monitor the demilitarized zone and ensure Iraqi compliance with disarmament and border‑security provisions following the ceasefire.

Q17. If 30 % of the original UN troops (80 000) were redeployed to other missions by 1998, how many troops remained in the original operation?

A.50 000
B.56 000 ✅
C.60 000
D.70 000
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Thirty percent of 80 000 equals \(0.30 \times 80,000 = 24,000\) troops redeployed elsewhere. Subtracting this from the original total leaves \(80,000 - 24,000 = 56,000\) troops still engaged in the initial mission, reflecting the fluid nature of UN deployments as global crises evolve.

This principle underpins the legitimacy of the Iraqi Governing Council and reinforces the notion that each state retains its inherent equality regardless of external interventions.

Q20. During the post‑invasion period, the US allocated approximately \$10 billion annually for reconstruction. If this amount represented 2 % of the US federal budget that year, what was the approximate total federal budget?

Hence the US federal budget for that fiscal year was roughly \$500 billion, highlighting the scale of national spending relative to the reconstruction effort in Iraq.

Q22. If the number of insurgent groups grew from 5 in 2003 to 20 in 2006, what was the average annual growth rate assuming exponential growth? Use formula \( N = N_0 e^{rt} \).

A.0.3
B.0.46 ✅
C.0.6
D.0.75
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Setting \(20 = 5 e^{3r}\) gives \(e^{3r}=4\). Taking natural logs, \(3r = \ln 4 \approx 1.386\), so \(r \approx 0.462\) or 46 % per year. This exponential increase illustrates how rapidly insurgent activity expanded in the early post‑invasion years.

Q23. The UN's peacekeeping budget is funded by member states based on a scale. If Iraq contributed 0.5 % of the total UN peacekeeping budget of \$6.5 billion in 2002, what was Iraq's contribution?

A.\$15 million
B.\$32.5 million ✅
C.\$45 million
D.\$60 million
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Multiplying the total budget by Iraq’s share yields \(0.005 \times 6.5 billion = 0.0325 billion\), which equals \$32.5 million. This modest contribution reflects Iraq’s limited financial capacity during a period of reconstruction and highlights the broader principle of equitable cost‑sharing among UN members.

This logistical calculation underscores the substantial daily supply requirements for large‑scale military operations in arid environments.

Q26. UN Security Council resolution 687 required Iraq to allow weapons inspections. If inspections reduced Iraq's declared weapons stockpile by 85 % over a 5‑year period, and the original stockpile was estimated at 1 200 warheads, how many warheads remained?

A.100
B.150
C.180 ✅
D.200
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: An 85 % reduction leaves 15 % of the original stockpile: \(1,200 \times 0.15 = 180\) warheads. This substantial decrease demonstrates the impact of sustained inspection regimes, though verification challenges and political disputes often complicated the disarmament process.
