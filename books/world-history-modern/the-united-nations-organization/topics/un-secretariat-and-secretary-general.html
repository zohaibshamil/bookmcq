What is UN Secretariat and Secretary General?

The UN Secretariat is the administrative arm led by the Secretary-General, who serves as the chief diplomat and spokesperson, managing daily operations, implementing decisions, and bringing urgent threats to world peace to the attention of the Security Council.

📝 All UN Secretariat and Secretary General MCQs

Q1. Who became the Secretary‑General of the United Nations in 2006?

A. Trygve Lie
B. Ban Ki‑moon ✅
C. Kofi Annan
D. Javier Pérez de Cuellar

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Ban Ki‑moon was appointed in 2006 after Kofi Annan's term ended. The appointment follows the UN Charter, where the General Assembly elects the Secretary‑General based on the Security Council’s recommendation. His tenure began in a period of expanding UN operations and reforms.

Q2. Who was the first Secretary‑General of the United Nations?

A. Dag Hammarskjöld
B. Kurt Waldheim
C. Trygve Lie ✅
D. U Thant

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Trygve Lie of Norway served as the first Secretary‑General from 1946 to 1952. His leadership helped establish the Secretariat’s administrative foundations. As the inaugural holder of the post, he set precedents for future secretaries‑general, including the principle of impartiality and the five‑year term length.

Q3. Which Secretary‑General served two full ten‑year terms, the longest continuous service recorded up to 2020?

A. Boutros Boutros‑Ghali
B. Kofi Annan ✅
C. Ban Ki‑moon
D. U Thant

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Kofi Annan of Ghana served from 1996 to 2006, completing two consecutive five‑year terms, totaling \(2 \times 5 = 10\) years. His decade in office was marked by major reforms, the creation of the Millennium Development Goals, and extensive diplomatic mediation, making his tenure the longest uninterrupted period at the time.

Q4. What mechanism helps ensure the Secretary‑General is not from a major power?

A. Appointment by the Security Council alone
B. Selection by the General Assembly without any recommendation
C. Recommendation by the Security Council followed by Assembly election, with a tradition of choosing a non‑major‑power national ✅
D. Random lottery among member states

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN practice is that the Security Council recommends a candidate, and the General Assembly elects the Secretary‑General. To preserve impartiality, the tradition is to select someone who does not belong to any of the major powers, reducing the risk of bias in diplomatic leadership.

Q5. If a crisis erupts in a region where the Secretary‑General’s home country has vested interests, what is the most appropriate UN response?

A. The Secretary‑General should recuse himself and delegate duties to the Deputy Secretary‑General ✅
B. He should personally mediate to show commitment
C. The UN should suspend all operations in that region
D. The Secretary‑General should ignore the conflict to avoid bias

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Recusal preserves the Secretariat’s credibility and avoids perceived partiality. Delegating to the Deputy Secretary‑General maintains continuity while upholding the principle of impartiality, essential for the UN’s legitimacy. This approach aligns with established diplomatic norms and minimizes accusations of favoritism.

Q6. Which of the following tasks is NOT a primary responsibility of the UN Secretariat?

A. Preparing minutes of meetings
B. Conducting peace‑keeping operations on the ground ✅
C. Providing translation services
D. Managing information dissemination

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Peace‑keeping operations are carried out by the UN Department of Peace Operations, not the Secretariat. The Secretariat’s core duties include administrative support such as drafting minutes, translating documents, and handling communications, enabling the broader UN system to function efficiently.

Q7. During which Secretary‑General’s tenure did the UN adopt the Millennium Development Goals (MDGs)?

A. Dag Hammarskjöld
B. Kofi Annan ✅
C. Ban Ki‑moon
D. Javier Pérez de Cuellar

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Kofi Annan’s term (1996‑2006) saw the adoption of the MDGs in 2000. These eight goals aimed to address poverty, education, health, and environmental sustainability by 2015. Annan’s leadership was instrumental in rallying member states around a common development agenda, marking a significant policy milestone.

Q8. If the General Assembly votes 120 in favor, 30 against, and 15 abstain on a Secretary‑General appointment, does the candidate meet the required majority?

A. Yes, because a simple majority of votes cast is sufficient ✅
B. No, because a two‑thirds majority of all members is required
C. Yes, because the candidate received more than half of the total 165 members
D. No, because abstentions count as negative votes

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

UN procedural rules require a simple majority of the votes cast, excluding abstentions. Here, 120 out of 150 votes cast (120 + 30) yields an 80 % approval, surpassing the required >50 % threshold, so the candidate is duly elected.

Q9. Which Secretary‑General was the first to be appointed from a non‑European country?

A. U Thant ✅
B. Kurt Waldheim
C. Dag Hammarskjöld
D. Boutros Boutros‑Ghali

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

U Thant of Burma (now Myanmar) became Secretary‑General in 1961, marking the first appointment of a leader from outside Europe. His tenure (1961‑1971) represented a shift toward greater geographic diversity within UN leadership, reflecting the growing influence of newly independent Asian nations.

Q10. What is the standard length of a Secretary‑General’s term according to UN practice?

A. Three years
B. Five years ✅
C. Seven years
D. Ten years

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The UN Charter and subsequent practice set the Secretary‑General’s term at five years, renewable once. This fixed term provides stability while allowing periodic renewal to reflect the evolving priorities of the international community.

Q11. A member state alleges that the Secretary‑General favors another nation during a trade dispute. Which UN principle should be invoked to address the complaint?

A. Collective security
B. Impartiality of the Secretariat ✅
C. Sovereign equality
D. Non‑intervention

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Impartiality is a core principle of the Secretariat, ensuring that the Secretary‑General acts without bias. Invoking impartiality prompts an internal review and reinforces the expectation that the Secretariat remains neutral, safeguarding the UN’s credibility in contentious matters.

Q12. During which Secretary‑General’s term did the UN adopt the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) in 2015?

A. Ban Ki‑moon ✅
B. Kofi Annan
C. Boutros Boutros‑Ghali
D. U Thant

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Ban Ki‑moon’s tenure (2006‑2016) oversaw the transition from the MDGs to the SDGs in 2015. The 17 goals expanded the development agenda to include climate action, reduced inequalities, and sustainable economic growth, reflecting a broader, more integrated approach to global challenges.

Q13. Which of the following statements about the Secretary‑General’s appointment process is FALSE?

A. The Security Council recommends a candidate
B. The General Assembly elects the Secretary‑General
C. The Secretary‑General serves a lifetime term ✅
D. The appointment requires a five‑year term

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Secretary‑General does not serve a lifetime term; the position is limited to a five‑year term, renewable once. The other statements accurately describe the recommendation by the Security Council and election by the General Assembly, which together shape the appointment.

Q14. If the Secretary‑General’s term is five years, how many terms would equal a total of 20 years of service?

A. Three terms
B. Four terms ✅
C. Five terms
D. Six terms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each term is \(5\) years, so \(4 \times 5 = 20\) years. Therefore, four full terms would be required to reach a 20‑year total, though traditionally the role is limited to two terms.

Q15. During a global pandemic, the Secretary‑General proposes a coordinated vaccination effort. Which UN body would most likely collaborate with the Secretariat to implement this plan?

A. International Court of Justice
B. World Health Organization ✅
C. UN Security Council
D. UNESCO

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The World Health Organization (WHO) is the UN’s specialized agency for public health. Collaboration between the Secretary‑General and WHO would be essential for coordinating vaccine distribution, policy guidance, and logistical support, leveraging both entities’ expertise to address a worldwide health emergency.

Q16. Which Secretary‑General was in office when the UN adopted the Convention on the Rights of the Child in 1989?

A. Kurt Waldheim
B. Dag Hammarskjöld
C. U Thant
D. Javier Pérez de Cuellar ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Javier Pérez de Cuellar served from 1981 to 1991, encompassing the adoption of the Convention on the Rights of the Child in 1989. His administration emphasized human rights, and the convention marked a landmark achievement in protecting children globally.

Q17. What is the primary role of the Deputy Secretary‑General?

A. To replace the Secretary‑General in case of resignation
B. To oversee the day‑to‑day management of the Secretariat’s operations ✅
C. To represent the UN at the International Monetary Fund
D. To draft all General Assembly resolutions

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Deputy Secretary‑General assists the Secretary‑General by managing daily operations, ensuring administrative efficiency, and stepping in when needed. This role supports the Secretariat’s continuity and effectiveness, allowing the Secretary‑General to focus on high‑level diplomatic engagement.

Q18. A new member state requests that the Secretary‑General prioritize its regional conflict in the UN agenda. Which principle should guide the Secretary‑General’s decision?

A. Universal jurisdiction
B. Equal consideration of all member states ✅
C. Veto power
D. Collective security

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The UN upholds the principle of equal consideration, meaning no single member state can unilaterally dictate agenda priorities. The Secretary‑General must balance diverse interests, ensuring that all member states receive fair attention while respecting the organization’s collective decision‑making processes.

Q19. Which Secretary‑General’s tenure overlapped with the end of the Cold War in 1991?

A. Kurt Waldheim ✅
B. Boutros Boutros‑Ghali
C. U Thant
D. Dag Hammarskjöld

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Kurt Waldheim served from 1971 to 1981, ending before the Cold War concluded. The correct answer is actually Boutros Boutros‑Ghali (1992‑1996) who began after the Cold War, so the statement is a trick; the correct answer is none of the above. However, according to the timeline, the Secretary‑General at the moment of 1991 was Javier Pérez de Cuellar (1981‑1991).

Q20. If the Secretariat employs over 50 000 staff members, what is the average number of employees per year over a 5‑year term?

A. 10 000
B. 25 000
C. 50 000 ✅
D. 100 000

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The staffing figure is a snapshot rather than a cumulative total. Over a five‑year term, the average remains \(50\,000\) employees, assuming staffing levels stay relatively constant, because the average does not change without significant hiring or reductions.

Q21. During a natural disaster, the Secretary‑General wants to mobilize UN resources quickly. Which existing UN mechanism facilitates rapid response?

A. The Security Council’s Chapter VII powers
B. The UN Peacekeeping Rapid Deployment Force
C. The Emergency Relief Coordinator under OCHA ✅
D. The International Court of Justice’s advisory jurisdiction

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Emergency Relief Coordinator, leading OCHA, is designed for swift humanitarian action. This role coordinates agencies, NGOs, and donor governments to deliver aid rapidly, making it the most appropriate mechanism for immediate disaster response.

Q22. Which Secretary‑General introduced the concept of “Responsibility to Protect” (R2P) in the early 2000s?

A. Ban Ki‑moon
B. Kofi Annan ✅
C. Boutros Boutros‑Ghali
D. Javier Pérez de Cuellar

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Kofi Annan championed the Responsibility to Protect doctrine, emphasizing that the international community must intervene when states fail to protect their populations from mass atrocities. The concept was formally endorsed at the 2005 World Summit, reflecting Annan’s commitment to strengthening humanitarian norms.

Q23. What is the primary function of the UN’s Translation Service within the Secretariat?

A. To draft new treaties
B. To provide multilingual interpretation for meetings and documents ✅
C. To enforce language‑based sanctions
D. To negotiate trade agreements

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Translation Service ensures that UN meetings, resolutions, and official documents are accurately rendered into the six official languages, facilitating clear communication among member states and preserving the organization’s multilingual heritage.

Q24. A member state claims the Secretary‑General is overstepping his diplomatic role by publicly criticizing its domestic policies. Which UN principle addresses this concern?

A. Non‑intervention in domestic affairs
B. Collective security
C. Impartiality of the Secretariat ✅
D. Sovereign equality

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Impartiality requires the Secretary‑General to act without bias, but it does not grant unlimited freedom to comment on internal matters. The principle of non‑intervention limits external commentary, balancing the need for impartial advocacy with respect for state sovereignty.

A. Kofi Annan ✅
B. Boutros Boutros‑Ghali
C. Javier Pérez de Cuellar
D. U Thant

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Kofi Annan’s term ended in December 2006, after which Ban Ki‑moon took office. Annan’s decade‑long leadership set the stage for subsequent reforms and initiatives that Ban Ki‑moon would continue to develop.

Q26. If a proposal to increase the Secretariat’s staff by 10 % is approved, how many additional employees will be hired?

A. 5 000 ✅
B. 4 500
C. 5 500
D. 6 000

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A 10 % increase on a baseline of 50 000 employees equals \(0.10 \times 50\,000 = 5\,000\) new staff members, expanding the workforce to 55 000.

Q27. During a peace negotiation, the Secretary‑General is asked to act as a mediator. Which skill is most essential for successful mediation?

A. Military strategy expertise
B. Impartiality and active listening ✅
C. Economic forecasting
D. Legal drafting

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Impartiality ensures the mediator is trusted by all parties, while active listening helps identify underlying interests and build consensus. These competencies are vital for navigating complex diplomatic discussions and achieving durable agreements.

The “good offices” refer to the Secretary‑General’s ability to use personal influence, neutrality, and diplomatic channels to facilitate dialogue and conflict resolution, without imposing decisions, thereby supporting peaceful settlement of disputes.

Q32. If the Secretariat’s budget is \( \$10 \) billion and administrative costs represent 15 %, how much is allocated to administration?

A. \( \$1.5 \) billion ✅
B. \( \$2 \) billion
C. \( \$1 \) billion
D. \( \$0.5 \) billion

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Fifteen percent of \( \$10 \) billion equals \(0.15 \times 10 = 1.5\) billion dollars, which is the portion earmarked for administrative expenses.

📖 Explanation:

Kofi Annan introduced “human security” to broaden the UN’s focus beyond state security, highlighting the protection of individuals from threats such as disease, famine, and repression. This paradigm shift influenced subsequent UN policies and development agendas.
