Q1. In which year were the majority of the UN's specialized agencies, except the International Labour Organization, established?

🔹 A. 1945
🔹 B. 1919
🔹 C. 1963
🔹 D. 2002

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The UN created most of its specialized agencies in the immediate post‑World War II period to address global economic and social issues; the founding year for these agencies, apart from the ILO, was \(1945\). This concentration of establishment reflects the UN's broader mandate compared with the League, showing a decisive shift toward comprehensive international cooperation.

Q2. Which organization, founded in 1919, is the sole specialized agency of the UN that predates the 1945 wave of agency creation?

🔹 A. World Health Organization
🔹 B. International Labour Organization
🔹 C. UNESCO
🔹 D. UNICEF

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The International Labour Organization (ILO) was founded in \(1919\) as a response to labor concerns after World War I, making it the only UN specialized agency that predates the post‑World War II establishment surge. Its early inception highlights the continuity of labor rights advocacy, distinguishing it from agencies formed later under the UN umbrella.

Q3. By 2002, how many member states did the United Nations have after East Timor's admission?

🔹 A. 191
🔹 B. 159
🔹 C. 183
🔹 D. 193

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

East Timor became the 191st member of the United Nations in 2002, expanding the organization’s membership to \(191\) states. This milestone underscores the UN’s ongoing role in recognizing newly independent nations and contrasts with the League of Nations, which never exceeded fifty members, illustrating the UN’s broader global reach.

Q4. Which major power never joined the League of Nations, though it was a founding member of the United Nations?

🔹 A. United Kingdom
🔹 B. Soviet Union
🔹 C. United States
🔹 D. France

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United States chose not to join the League of Nations, despite President Wilson’s advocacy for it, reflecting domestic political concerns and skepticism about collective security. However, the U.S. became a founding member of the United Nations in 1945, demonstrating a shift toward greater multilateral engagement after World War II.

Q5. What is the name of the General Assembly resolution that expanded its authority to act when the Security Council is deadlocked?

🔹 A. Resolution 242
🔹 B. Uniting for Peace
🔹 C. Resolution 331
🔹 D. Peaceful Settlement

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The “Uniting for Peace” resolution, adopted by the General Assembly in 1950, empowers the Assembly to recommend collective measures when the Security Council cannot act due to vetoes. This procedural innovation gave the UN a mechanism for decisive action, a capability the League of Nations lacked, thereby enhancing the UN’s effectiveness in crisis situations.

Q6. Which UN organ’s enhanced prestige and authority has allowed it to take more decisive actions than the League ever could?

🔹 A. International Court of Justice
🔹 B. Economic and Social Council
🔹 C. Security Council
🔹 D. General Assembly

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Security Council, endowed with enforcement powers and the ability to authorize sanctions or military action, has seen its prestige rise, especially after the “Uniting for Peace” resolution. This elevated status enables the Council to act decisively in international conflicts, a capacity the League of Nations never possessed due to its limited enforcement mechanisms.

Q11. A low‑income country seeks development assistance. Which UN specialized agency is most appropriate for coordinating economic and social programs?

🔹 A. UNESCO
🔹 B. UNDP
🔹 C. UNHCR
🔹 D. FAO

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) focuses on poverty reduction, sustainable development, and capacity building, making it the primary agency for coordinating economic and social assistance to low‑income nations. By contrast, UNESCO emphasizes education and culture, UNHCR protects refugees, and FAO deals with agriculture, illustrating the UN’s diversified yet targeted approach.

Q12. Compare the UN’s approach to human‑rights protection with that of the League of Nations. Which statement best captures the difference?

🔹 A. The UN has no dedicated human‑rights mechanisms, unlike the League.
🔹 B. Both organizations prioritize human rights equally.
🔹 C. The UN explicitly safeguards individual rights, whereas the League largely ignored them.
🔹 D. The League had a stronger human‑rights charter than the UN.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations explicitly incorporates the protection of individual human rights into its charter and through bodies such as the Human Rights Council, a focus absent in the League of Nations, which primarily dealt with state‑to‑state issues. This distinction underscores the UN’s broader moral mandate and its evolution toward a rights‑based international order.

Q13. A dispute between two member states is blocked by a veto in the Security Council. Which UN mechanism can the General Assembly invoke to address the deadlock?

🔹 A. Peacekeeping Forces
🔹 B. Uniting for Peace resolution
🔹 C. Economic Sanctions Committee
🔹 D. International Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When a Security Council veto prevents action, the General Assembly may invoke the “Uniting for Peace” resolution, allowing it to recommend collective measures, including the use of force, to maintain international peace and security. This procedural tool provides a fallback that the League of Nations lacked, enhancing the UN’s capacity to respond to crises.

Q14. Why did the UN’s membership surpass the League’s maximum of 50 members, despite both being international organizations?

🔹 A. Because the UN limited admissions to powerful states only.
🔹 B. Because the UN accepted former colonies as independent members after decolonization.
🔹 C. Because the League prohibited new admissions after World War I.
🔹 D. Because the UN merged with regional organizations to increase numbers.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations’ membership grew beyond the League’s ceiling because it embraced the wave of decolonization, admitting newly independent states from Africa, Asia, and later the former Soviet republics. This inclusive policy contrasted with the League’s more restrictive framework, enabling the UN to become a genuinely global body representing a broader spectrum of nations.

Q15. A humanitarian crisis emerges in a conflict zone. Which UN body is primarily responsible for coordinating relief efforts?

🔹 A. Security Council
🔹 B. Economic and Social Council
🔹 C. Human Rights Council
🔹 D. Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) leads the UN’s response to emergencies, orchestrating the delivery of aid, mobilizing resources, and ensuring coherent action among agencies. While the Security Council may authorize interventions and the Human Rights Council monitors violations, OCHA’s mandate centers on immediate humanitarian coordination.

Q17. A newly independent state wishes to join the UN but faces opposition from a permanent Security Council member. Which principle of the UN Charter could be invoked to argue for its admission?

🔹 A. Principle of Sovereign Equality
🔹 B. Principle of Non‑Intervention
🔹 C. Principle of Collective Security
🔹 D. Principle of Self‑Determination

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The principle of sovereign equality, enshrined in Article 1 of the UN Charter, asserts that all member states enjoy equal rights and duties. Advocates can cite this principle to counter a veto based on political bias, emphasizing that every sovereign nation—regardless of size—has a legitimate claim to UN membership.

🔹 A. Security Council
🔹 B. General Assembly
🔹 C. Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)
🔹 D. International Court of Justice

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

ECOSOC serves as the principal forum for economic, social, and environmental issues, facilitating negotiations on technical matters like climate policy. Its subsidiary bodies, such as the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), specialize in the scientific and policy dimensions, making ECOSOC the logical venue for detailed treaty work.
