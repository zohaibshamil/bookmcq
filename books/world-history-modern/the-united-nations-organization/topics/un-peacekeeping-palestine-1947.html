What is UN peacekeeping Palestine 1947?

Following the 1947 UN partition plan for Palestine, the UN deployed observers through UNTSO in 1948 to monitor ceasefires after the first Arab-Israeli war, marking one of the earliest peacekeeping efforts though unable to prevent conflict escalation.

📝 All UN peacekeeping Palestine 1947 MCQs

Q1. What year did the UN propose the partition plan for Palestine?

A. 1945
B. 1946
C. 1947 ✅
D. 1948

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United Nations General Assembly adopted the partition plan in November 1947, recommending the creation of separate Jewish and Arab states. This decision was formalized in Resolution 181, which became a pivotal moment in the region's history and set the stage for subsequent conflicts.

Q2. Which UN body was primarily responsible for passing the partition resolution?

A. Security Council
B. Economic and Social Council
C. General Assembly ✅
D. Secretariat

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The General Assembly, as the main deliberative organ of the UN, adopted the partition plan in Resolution 181. While the Security Council deals with peace and security issues, the General Assembly handles broader political decisions, including the contentious proposal to divide Palestine.

Q3. How many votes were needed for the partition plan to pass in the General Assembly?

A. Two‑thirds majority ✅
B. Simple majority
C. Three‑quarter majority
D. Unanimous

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The partition plan required a two‑thirds majority of the members present and voting. In the 1947 vote, 33 out of 58 members supported the plan, satisfying the two‑thirds threshold and allowing the resolution to be adopted despite significant opposition.

Q4. Which of the following was NOT a direct consequence of the 1947 UN partition plan?

A. Establishment of the State of Israel
B. Immediate recognition by all Arab states ✅
C. Outbreak of the 1948 Arab‑Israeli War
D. Creation of UNRWA

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While the plan led to the creation of Israel and subsequent conflict, it did not result in immediate recognition by Arab states. Instead, neighboring Arab nations rejected the plan and later engaged militarily, prompting the UN to establish the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestinian refugees.

Q5. In the UN vote on the partition plan, how many member states voted against it?

A. 15
B. 20 ✅
C. 30
D. 33

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

During the 1947 vote, 13 member states voted against the partition plan, while 33 supported it and 10 abstained. This opposition reflected the concerns of many Arab and Muslim-majority countries, who viewed the division as unfair and detrimental to the Arab population in Palestine.

Q6. A UN delegate argues that the partition plan should have allocated more land to the Arab state based on population distribution. Which principle does this argument primarily invoke?

A. Self‑determination
B. Territorial integrity
C. Equitable distribution ✅
D. Historical claims

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The delegate emphasizes equitable distribution, suggesting that land allocation should reflect demographic realities. This principle focuses on fairness in dividing resources, contrasting with self‑determination (people's right to choose) or historical claims (rights based on past ownership).

Q7. Considering the UN’s inability to prevent the 1948 war, what does this suggest about the limits of UN authority in post‑World War II conflicts?

A. UN resolutions are binding and enforceable
B. UN can only recommend actions, lacking enforcement mechanisms ✅
C. UN can deploy troops unilaterally
D. UN has absolute veto power over member states

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The failure to stop the 1948 war highlights that UN resolutions often depend on member states’ willingness to enforce them. Without a standing army or compulsory enforcement, the UN’s role is largely advisory, demonstrating the challenges of translating diplomatic decisions into effective peacekeeping.

Q8. What was the primary purpose of establishing the United Nations Relief and Works Agency (UNRWA) after the 1948 conflict?

A. To provide military assistance to Israel
B. To facilitate peace negotiations between Israel and Arab states
C. To care for Palestinian refugees ✅
D. To oversee the construction of new settlements

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

UNRWA was created to address the humanitarian crisis caused by the displacement of hundreds of thousands of Palestinians. Its mandate includes providing shelter, education, health services, and other essential aid to refugees, reflecting the UN’s commitment to humanitarian assistance in conflict zones.

Q9. Which Arab country first declared war on the newly proclaimed State of Israel in 1948?

A. Egypt ✅
B. Jordan
C. Syria
D. Lebanon

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Egypt was among the first Arab nations to engage militarily against Israel after its declaration of independence in May 1948. The ensuing conflict, known as the 1948 Arab‑Israeli War, involved multiple neighboring states, each aiming to support the Arab population and oppose the establishment of a Jewish state.

Q10. A historian claims that the UN’s partition plan was influenced by geopolitical interests of major powers. Which evidence would best support this claim?

A. The voting record showing unanimous support

C. The absence of any vetoes during the vote
D. The plan’s focus on religious sites

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The drafting process involved input from major powers like Britain and the United States, reflecting their strategic interests in the Middle East. This involvement suggests that the plan was not purely a neutral solution but was shaped by the geopolitical aims of influential UN members.

Q11. What percentage of the UN member states voted in favor of the partition plan?

A. 0.57 ✅
B. 0.65
C. 0.7
D. 0.8

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Out of 58 members present, 33 voted for the plan, which translates to roughly 57% support. This modest majority indicates significant division among the international community regarding the feasibility and fairness of partitioning Palestine into separate Jewish and Arab states.

Q12. Which resolution formally endorsed the UN partition plan?

A. Resolution 181 ✅
B. Resolution 194
C. Resolution 242
D. Resolution 338

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Resolution 181, adopted by the UN General Assembly on November 29, 1947, outlined the proposed division of Palestine into Jewish and Arab states, with an international regime for Jerusalem. This resolution became the legal basis for the partition plan, despite subsequent opposition and conflict.

Q13. How did the absence of a veto by the Soviet Union affect the adoption of the partition plan?

A. It allowed the plan to pass unopposed
B. It forced a compromise between East and West
C. It had no impact because the USSR supported the plan ✅
D. It led to a deadlock in the Security Council

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet Union, while generally supportive of the partition, did not exercise its veto power, enabling the General Assembly to adopt the plan. This lack of opposition from a major power facilitated the resolution’s passage, illustrating how veto dynamics can influence UN decision‑making.

Q14. Which city was designated as an international zone under the UN partition plan?

A. Tel Aviv
B. Haifa
C. Jerusalem ✅
D. Beersheba

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN plan called for Jerusalem to become a corpus separatum, an internationally administered city, due to its religious significance to Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. This special status was intended to ensure access and protection for all faiths, though it was never fully implemented.

Q15. What was the estimated total area (in square kilometers) allocated to the Jewish state under the partition plan?

B. \(6,800\) km² ✅
C. \(7,400\) km²
D. \(9,000\) km²

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The partition plan assigned approximately \(6,800\) square kilometers to the Jewish state, encompassing coastal regions and parts of the Galilee. This allocation was intended to provide a viable territory for the new state, though it left many demographic and security challenges unresolved.

Q16. A diplomat argues that the partition plan's failure was due to inadequate provision for economic sustainability. Which factor most supports this argument?

A. Lack of water resources for the Arab state
B. Absence of a shared currency
C. Insufficient allocation of industrial zones
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Economic viability was a critical concern; the plan did not adequately address water distribution, industrial development, or monetary coordination. These shortcomings would have made it difficult for both states to thrive independently, highlighting the plan’s limited consideration of long‑term economic needs.

Q17. Which of the following best describes the UN’s role during the 1948–1949 Arab‑Israeli war?

B. Neutral mediator providing cease‑fire proposals ✅
C. Primary victor in the conflict
D. Supplier of weapons to Israel

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The UN acted mainly as a mediator, issuing cease‑fire resolutions and establishing truce lines such as the Green Line. While it lacked enforcement capabilities, its diplomatic efforts aimed to limit hostilities and lay groundwork for future negotiations, rather than taking an active combat role.

Q18. How many Arab states participated in the 1948 war against Israel?

B. Four
C. Five ✅
D. Six

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Five Arab nations—Egypt, Transjordan (Jordan), Syria, Lebanon, and Iraq—joined the conflict against Israel. Their involvement reflected broader regional opposition to the partition plan and aimed to support the Palestinian Arab population, though the war's outcomes varied among the participants.

Q19. What was the primary reason the Soviet Union abstained from vetoing the partition resolution?

A. Support for a Jewish state as a Soviet ally ✅
B. Desire to maintain neutrality in the Middle East
C. Opposition to colonial powers' influence
D. Lack of interest in the region

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The USSR saw the establishment of Israel as potentially beneficial for expanding its influence, hoping the new state might align with Soviet interests. This strategic calculation led to abstention rather than a veto, allowing the plan to proceed while positioning the USSR favorably in the region.

Q20. Which UN agency was created specifically to address the refugee crisis resulting from the 1948 conflict?

A. UNICEF
B. UNHCR
C. UNRWA ✅
D. UNESCO

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

UNRWA—United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees—was established in 1949 to provide assistance, education, and health services to Palestinians displaced by the war. Its creation underscores the UN’s commitment to humanitarian relief in the aftermath of the conflict.

Q21. If the partition plan had allocated 60% of the land to the Jewish state instead of the actual proportion, which outcome would most likely have changed?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A larger territorial allocation could have offered the Jewish state more resources and strategic depth, potentially decreasing immediate security pressures and reducing the impetus for war. However, it might also have intensified Arab opposition, making diplomatic acceptance more challenging.

Q22. What was the main argument of Arab leaders against the UN partition plan?

A. It ignored Palestinian self‑determination ✅
B. It favored the Soviet Union
C. It allocated too much land to the Arab state
D. It threatened religious sites

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Arab leaders contended that the plan violated the principle of self‑determination by imposing a division without the consent of the majority Arab population. They argued that the plan unfairly favored the minority Jewish community, leading to widespread rejection across the Arab world.

Q23. Which of the following was a direct result of the UN’s cease‑fire proposals in 1948?

A. Establishment of the Green Line as a de‑facto border ✅
B. Full recognition of Israel by all Arab states
C. Creation of a joint Israeli‑Arab parliament
D. Immediate resettlement of refugees

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The cease‑fire agreements led to the emergence of the Green Line, which functioned as the de‑facto boundary between Israel and its neighbors until the 1967 war. This line became a reference point for future negotiations, despite not being an officially recognized border.

During the Korean War, the UN was able to act decisively because member states, especially the United States, provided forces, whereas in Palestine the UN lacked its own military capability. This contrast illustrates how the absence of a standing UN force limited its ability to enforce decisions like the partition.

The UN’s emphasis on cease‑fire agreements and diplomatic talks reveals a preference for mediation over force. By facilitating negotiations and establishing temporary boundaries, the organization seeks to manage hostilities while avoiding direct combat, aligning with its charter’s emphasis on peaceful dispute settlement.

Q28. What was the primary reason the United Nations could not prevent the series of wars after 1948?

A. Lack of funding
B. Member states' conflicting interests ✅
C. Absence of a UN Secretary‑General
D. Inadequate legal authority

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Divergent national interests among UN members, especially between Western and Arab nations, hindered unified action. This discord prevented the UN from forming a cohesive response, limiting its ability to stop subsequent conflicts such as those in 1967 and 1973.

Q29. Which UN resolution introduced the concept of 'Uniting for Peace' after the Korean War?

A. Resolution 68/1
B. Resolution 377(V) ✅
C. Resolution 181
D. Resolution 242

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Resolution 377(V), adopted in 1950, established the 'Uniting for Peace' mechanism, allowing the General Assembly to act when the Security Council is deadlocked by vetoes. This innovative provision emerged from the Korean War experience, aiming to preserve UN effectiveness despite veto-related impasses.

UNSCOP was tasked with examining the Palestine problem and recommending a viable solution to the General Assembly. Its findings and proposals formed the basis for the partition plan, reflecting the UN’s reliance on investigative committees to inform policy decisions.

Q32. How many member states abstained from voting on the partition plan?

Q33. Which of the following best explains why the UN could not enforce the partition plan immediately after its adoption?

📖 Explanation:

The UN lacked its own armed forces to implement the plan, relying on member states for enforcement. This structural limitation meant that without cooperation from the involved parties, the UN could not compel compliance, leading to delays and eventual conflict.
