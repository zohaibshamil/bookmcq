What is UN and Gulf War 1991?

The UN authorized coalition military action against Iraq in 1991 through Resolution 678 after Iraq’s invasion of Kuwait, demonstrating rare Security Council unity post-Cold War to enforce collective security and liberate occupied territory.

📝 All UN and Gulf War 1991 MCQs

Q1. What year did Iraq invade Kuwait, prompting the UN Security Council’s first resolution on the conflict?

A.1990 ✅
B.1991
C.1992
D.1993
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The invasion took place on 2 August 1990 when Iraqi forces crossed into Kuwait. This act triggered immediate diplomatic responses, leading the UN Security Council to adopt Resolution 660 in the same month, demanding Iraq’s withdrawal. The year is crucial for understanding the timeline of subsequent UN actions and the war’s onset.

Q2. Which UN Security Council resolution first authorized member states to use “all necessary means” to expel Iraqi forces from Kuwait?

A.Resolution 660
B.Resolution 661
C.Resolution 678 ✅
D.Resolution 687
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Resolution 678, adopted on 29 November 1990, explicitly authorized member states, acting in accordance with the UN Charter, to employ all necessary means to restore international peace and security in the region, effectively permitting military action to liberate Kuwait. This resolution marked the legal basis for the coalition’s offensive operations in early 1991.

Q3. What was the primary strategic objective of the coalition’s air campaign, Operation Desert Storm, launched in January 1991?

A.Destroy Iraqi ground forces
B.Neutralize Iraq’s naval capabilities
C.Degrade Iraq’s command, control, and air defenses ✅
D.Secure oil fields in Kuwait
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The initial phase focused on crippling Iraq’s command, control, communications, and air defense systems to achieve air superiority. By targeting radar installations, communication nodes, and airfields, the coalition aimed to isolate Iraqi forces, reduce their ability to coordinate defenses, and pave the way for a swift ground offensive.

Q4. Which country contributed the largest number of ground troops to the coalition forces after the United States?

A.United Kingdom
B.France
C.Saudi Arabia ✅
D.Egypt
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Saudi Arabia, as a neighboring Gulf state, provided a substantial ground contingent, second only to the United States in troop numbers. Their forces were crucial for securing the southern flank, protecting oil installations, and offering regional legitimacy. The Saudi contribution underscored the coalition’s broad Arab participation alongside Western powers.

Q5. What was the primary reason critics argued that UN intervention favored Kuwait over other nations like East Timor?

A.Kuwait’s oil wealth ✅
B.Kuwait’s strategic location
C.Kuwait’s alliance with the United Nations
D.Kuwait’s democratic government
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Critics highlighted that Kuwait’s vast oil reserves made it vital to Western economies, prompting swift UN action to protect those interests. In contrast, less economically significant territories such as East Timor received limited international response, suggesting a bias toward nations whose resources were crucial to global markets.

Q6. Which of the following was NOT a direct consequence of the coalition’s ground offensive that began on 24 February 1991?

A.Liberation of Kuwait
B.Destruction of Iraqi Scud missile sites in Iraq
C.Rapid collapse of Iraqi front‑line units
D.Heavy civilian casualties in Kuwait ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: While the ground offensive swiftly liberated Kuwait and shattered Iraqi front‑line formations, the coalition largely avoided extensive civilian casualties within Kuwait itself. The main civilian impact occurred in Iraq, where infrastructure damage from preceding air strikes caused indirect hardships, but Kuwait’s civilian population remained relatively unharmed during the ground campaign.

Q7. How many UN member states voted in favor of Resolution 678, authorizing force against Iraq?

A.13
B.15
C.16 ✅
D.20
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Resolution 678 received 13 votes in favor, with 2 abstentions (China and the Soviet Union) and no votes against. This narrow majority reflected Cold‑War tensions but nonetheless provided the legal framework for coalition forces to intervene militarily, illustrating the UN’s ability to act despite geopolitical divisions.

Q8. Which UN organ was primarily responsible for coordinating the humanitarian assistance to Kuwaiti refugees after the liberation?

A.UN Security Council
B.UN General Assembly
C.UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) ✅
D.UN Development Programme (UNDP)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The UNHCR took the lead in delivering emergency shelter, food, and medical aid to displaced Kuwaitis after the war. While the Security Council set the political mandate, the specialized agency’s expertise in refugee protection enabled a rapid and organized response to the humanitarian crisis that followed the conflict.

Q9. What was the main legal justification used by the United Nations to deem Iraq’s invasion of Kuwait a violation of international law?

A.Violation of the Treaty of Baghdad
B.Breach of the Gulf Cooperation Council charter
C.Aggression under the UN Charter’s Article 2(4) ✅
D.Failure to pay UN dues
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Article 2(4) of the UN Charter prohibits the use of force against the territorial integrity or political independence of any state. Iraq’s unprovoked incursion into Kuwait directly contravened this provision, providing a clear legal basis for the UN to condemn the action and authorize collective measures to restore Kuwait’s sovereignty.

Q10. Which of the following best describes the “scorched‑earth” policy employed by retreating Iraqi forces?

A.Systematic destruction of civilian infrastructure ✅
B.Deliberate release of chemical weapons
C.Massive withdrawal of troops without engagement
D.Construction of defensive fortifications
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Retreating Iraqi units deliberately damaged oil wells, set fire to power plants, and demolished bridges to impede coalition advances and deny resources. This scorched‑earth tactic aimed to cause economic and environmental damage, complicate reconstruction efforts, and create a humanitarian crisis, reflecting a strategy of total war beyond conventional battlefield engagements.

Q11. A coalition commander must decide whether to prioritize neutralizing Iraq’s air defenses or securing key oil fields first. Which UN‑mandated objective should guide his decision?

A.Securing oil fields to protect global markets
B.Neutralizing air defenses to protect coalition troops ✅
C.Both objectives are equally mandated
D.Prioritizing political negotiations over military actions
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: UN resolutions, particularly 678, emphasized restoring international peace and security, which includes eliminating threats to coalition forces. Neutralizing air defenses reduces the risk to troops and ensures freedom of movement, aligning with the UN’s primary security mandate. While protecting oil fields is economically important, the immediate military priority under the UN framework is to safeguard personnel.

Q12. Evaluate how the UN’s response to the Gulf War influenced subsequent UN peacekeeping doctrines. Which statement best reflects this impact?

A.It led to the creation of a permanent UN standing army
B.It reinforced the principle of collective security with multinational coalitions ✅
C.It caused the UN to abandon collective action in favor of unilateral interventions
D.It resulted in the UN focusing solely on humanitarian aid
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Gulf War demonstrated that the UN could effectively authorize and coordinate multinational coalitions under collective security principles, reinforcing the doctrine that member states could act together to enforce resolutions. This experience shaped later UN approaches, encouraging collaborative military responses while maintaining the central role of the Security Council in legitimizing force.

Q13. A diplomat argues that the UN’s intervention was primarily driven by oil interests rather than legal principles. Which piece of evidence most directly challenges this claim?

A.The inclusion of non‑oil‑producing Arab states in the coalition
B.The unanimous Security Council vote for Resolution 678 ✅
C.The timing of the intervention coinciding with global oil price spikes
D.The focus on protecting Kuwaiti civilians
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Resolution 678 was adopted after extensive diplomatic negotiations and reflected a consensus on the legal violation of Iraq’s aggression, not merely economic concerns. While oil interests were a factor, the Security Council’s formal legal endorsement of force indicates that the primary justification rested on upholding international law, countering the claim of purely economic motives.

Q14. Considering the UN’s limited ability to enforce sanctions, what lesson did the Gulf War teach regarding the effectiveness of economic measures against non‑compliant states?

A.Sanctions alone are sufficient to compel compliance
B.Sanctions require complementary military enforcement to be effective ✅
C.Sanctions are ineffective without UN approval
D.Sanctions work better when targeted at civilian populations
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Gulf War highlighted that sanctions, while signaling disapproval, often need credible military backing to compel a state to change behavior. Iraq’s continued defiance despite sanctions demonstrated the necessity of a credible threat of force, leading to the combined use of economic pressure and the authorized use of force in future UN strategies.

Q15. An analyst assesses the impact of the coalition’s air campaign on Iraqi civilian morale. Which outcome best aligns with post‑war reports?

A.Significant increase in civilian support for Saddam Hussein
B.Widespread civilian panic and loss of confidence in the regime ✅
C.Minimal impact on civilian morale
D.Enhanced loyalty to the Iraqi military
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Intense bombing of civilian infrastructure, including power plants and water supplies, caused widespread disruption and fear among Iraq’s population. Reports indicated that the air campaign eroded confidence in the regime’s ability to protect its citizens, leading to increased civilian panic and diminishing support for Saddam Hussein’s government.

Q16. Which UN‑established body was tasked with monitoring the cease‑fire and verifying Iraq’s compliance after the war?

A.UN Security Council
B.UN Special Commission (UNSCOM) ✅
C.UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA)
D.UN Development Programme (UNDP)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: UNSCOM, the United Nations Special Commission, was created to oversee the implementation of cease‑fire terms, including the destruction of Iraq’s weapons of mass destruction. Its mandate involved inspections, monitoring, and verification activities, playing a central role in ensuring Iraq’s compliance with the post‑war disarmament obligations imposed by the UN.

Q17. Critically examine why the UN’s intervention in the Gulf War is often cited as a rare example of successful collective security, yet also criticized for its selective application. Which dual‑aspect best captures this paradox?

A.It succeeded militarily but failed diplomatically
B.It was swift but lacked long‑term regional stability
C.It restored Kuwait’s sovereignty yet highlighted bias toward oil‑rich states ✅
D.It demonstrated UN authority but ignored humanitarian law
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The operation effectively liberated Kuwait, showcasing the UN’s capacity to mobilize a coalition under collective security. However, the selective nature—prioritizing a wealthy, oil‑dependent nation while ignoring other crises—exposes an inconsistency in applying the same principles universally, underscoring a paradox between operational success and equitable principle enforcement.

Q18. Analyze how the doctrine of “pre‑emptive self‑defence” was debated during the Gulf War. Which argument most accurately reflects the legal contention surrounding this concept?

A.Pre‑emptive action is always permissible under international law
B.Pre‑emptive self‑defence requires an imminent threat, which was not proven in Kuwait’s case ✅
C.The UN explicitly authorized pre‑emptive strikes against Iraq
D.Pre‑emptive defence was rejected by all Security Council members
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Legal scholars argued that pre‑emptive self‑defence must meet the criteria of immediacy, necessity, and proportionality. In the Gulf War, Iraq’s aggression was already underway, so the coalition’s actions were framed as a response to an ongoing violation rather than a pre‑emptive measure, making the justification of pre‑emptive self‑defence contentious.

Q19. Evaluate the role of media coverage in shaping international public opinion about the UN’s actions during the Gulf War. Which effect was most pronounced?

A.Media largely ignored UN involvement

C.Media emphasized coalition forces, marginalizing UN’s diplomatic role ✅
D.Media focused exclusively on humanitarian aspects
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Coverage predominantly highlighted the actions of the United States and its allies, often overshadowing the UN’s diplomatic and legal contributions. This emphasis shaped public perception, attributing the war’s success to coalition forces while the UN’s foundational role in authorizing and legitimizing the operation received comparatively less attention.

Q20. Considering the post‑war sanctions imposed on Iraq, what unintended consequence emerged that affected regional stability?

A.Strengthening Iraq’s economy
B.Improving Iraq‑Iran relations
C.Humanitarian crises leading to increased refugee flows ✅
D.Accelerating democratic reforms in Iraq
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The sanctions caused severe shortages of food, medicine, and essential goods, leading to widespread humanitarian suffering. This situation prompted mass displacement of Iraqis, creating refugee flows that strained neighboring countries and contributed to regional instability, illustrating how punitive economic measures can have far‑reaching, unintended social impacts.

Q21. Discuss why the UN’s inability to intervene in East Timor in 1975 is often contrasted with its decisive action in Kuwait. Which factor is most commonly cited as the differentiator?

A.Geopolitical alignment with Western powers ✅
B.The size of the invading force
C.The presence of oil resources in the contested territory
D.The UN’s lack of legal authority at the time
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: East Timor’s plight occurred during a Cold‑War era when Western powers were less inclined to confront Indonesia, a strategic ally. In contrast, Kuwait’s oil significance and the geopolitical interests of the United States and its allies galvanized swift UN action, highlighting how political alliances heavily influence the organization’s willingness to intervene.

Q22. Medium: Which of the following best describes the primary purpose of UN Resolution 687, adopted after the cease‑fire?

A.Establishing a new Kuwaiti government
B.Mandating Iraq’s disarmament of weapons of mass destruction ✅
C.Providing humanitarian aid to Kuwait
D.Setting up a UN peacekeeping force in the Gulf
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Resolution 687 outlined the terms for the cease‑fire, explicitly requiring Iraq to eliminate its weapons of mass destruction, including chemical, biological, and nuclear arms. It also established mechanisms for monitoring compliance, thereby forming the legal foundation for subsequent inspection regimes and ensuring long‑term regional security.

Q23. Medium: In what way did the coalition’s “left‑hook” maneuver contribute to the rapid defeat of Iraqi forces?

D.It allowed coalition forces to secure oil fields first
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The “left‑hook” was a massive flanking movement that swept around Iraqi defensive lines, avoiding direct confrontation with fortified positions. By advancing through the desert, coalition forces struck deep into Iraqi rear areas, disrupting command and supply lines, and compelling a swift collapse of Iraqi resistance, accelerating the overall victory.

This stance reinforced international norms against chemical warfare and underscored the Security Council’s commitment to upholding the Chemical Weapons Convention, even amid active hostilities.

This numerical advantage, combined with coordinated targeting and logistical support, enabled sustained sorties, rapid attrition of Iraqi air capabilities, and the establishment of uncontested air dominance early in the campaign.

The Kingdom’s willingness to host foreign troops also facilitated rapid deployment, underscoring strategic considerations over political neutrality or resource factors.

This group worked with regional authorities to develop strategies for extinguishing the fires, containing oil spills, and rehabilitating damaged ecosystems, demonstrating the UN’s commitment to addressing wartime environmental consequences.

Q29. Medium: How did the UN’s endorsement of the coalition’s use of force affect the principle of state sovereignty?

📖 Explanation: By authorizing collective action through Resolution 678, the UN affirmed that a state’s sovereignty may be lawfully limited when it violates the territorial integrity of another state. This established a precedent that the Security Council could intervene militarily to uphold international peace, balancing sovereignty with the need to deter aggression.

A.A complete withdrawal of U.S. forces from the region
B.Increased U.S. military presence and deeper political involvement in Gulf states ✅
C.A pivot toward isolationist policies in the Middle East
D.A transition to solely humanitarian engagement
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The war cemented a robust U.S. military footprint across the Gulf, leading to permanent bases, security agreements, and heightened political influence over regional allies. This expanded presence reshaped diplomatic dynamics, influencing subsequent conflicts and policy decisions, and underscored the United States’ enduring strategic commitment to Middle Eastern stability and energy security.

Q31. Examine the role of the UN’s “collective security” principle during the Gulf War. Which statement best captures its effectiveness and limitations?

A.Collective security succeeded without any need for member‑state cooperation
B.It proved effective when major powers supported the resolution, but limited when political interests diverged ✅
C.It was ineffective due to the UN’s inability to enforce any resolutions
D.It demonstrated that collective security is irrelevant in modern conflicts
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Gulf War illustrated that collective security can operate efficiently when leading powers back Security Council decisions, enabling swift coalition formation. However, the principle’s reliance on political consensus means that in situations lacking such alignment, the UN may struggle to mobilize action, revealing both its strengths and inherent constraints.

Q32. Considering the UN’s handling of post‑war sanctions, what lesson does the Gulf War provide about the design of effective sanctions regimes?

A.Sanctions should be unconditional and indefinite
B.Sanctions must be paired with clear, enforceable disarmament milestones and humanitarian exemptions ✅
C.Sanctions are most effective when they target civilian populations
D.Sanctions should be imposed without any diplomatic dialogue
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Gulf War experience showed that sanctions lacking precise objectives, enforcement mechanisms, and allowances for humanitarian needs can exacerbate civilian suffering and undermine legitimacy. Designing sanctions with specific, measurable targets, robust monitoring, and exemptions for essential goods helps maintain pressure on regimes while mitigating undue humanitarian harm.

Q33. Analyze why the UN’s response to Iraq’s invasion of Kuwait is often cited as a benchmark for future interventions, yet also criticized for its inconsistency. Which dual‑nature assessment best reflects this view?

A.It set a clear legal precedent but was applied selectively based on strategic interests ✅
B.It demonstrated military superiority but failed diplomatically
C.It succeeded in humanitarian aid but neglected economic reconstruction
D.It proved the UN’s neutrality but ignored regional politics
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The operation established a legal framework for collective security and UN‑authorized force, serving as a reference point for later actions. However, the decision to intervene was heavily influenced by Kuwait’s oil significance, leading to criticism that similar aggressions lacking strategic value have not received comparable UN attention, highlighting a tension between principle and practice.
