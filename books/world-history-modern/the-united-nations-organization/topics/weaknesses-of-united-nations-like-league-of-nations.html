📝 All Weaknesses of United Nations like League of Nations MCQs

Q1. What power can any permanent member of the Security Council use to stop a resolution?

🔹 A. Veto
🔹 B. Override
🔹 C. Amendment
🔹 D. Consultation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The veto power allows a permanent member to reject a substantive draft. When exercised, the proposal cannot be adopted regardless of how many other members support it. This mechanism mirrors the weakness of the League where a single member could block collective action, limiting the UN’s ability to act decisively.

Q2. Why does the UN lack its own standing army?

🔹 A. Treaty limits
🔹 B. Funding issues
🔹 C. Member state sovereignty
🔹 D. Charter prohibition

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The UN relies on contributions from member states because the Charter does not grant it authority to maintain a permanent force. Sovereign nations are reluctant to cede direct control of troops, fearing loss of autonomy. Consequently, the organization must negotiate deployments, which can delay response times and weaken collective security.

Q3. Which document first introduced the concept of a veto in an international organization?

🔹 A. Treaty of Versailles
🔹 B. Charter of the United Nations
🔹 C. League of Nations Covenant
🔹 D. Paris Peace Accords

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The concept of a veto was formalized in the UN Charter, granting each of the five permanent Security Council members the right to block substantive decisions. This provision was designed to prevent major power conflicts but also replicates the paralysis seen in the League when a single nation could halt progress, highlighting a persistent structural flaw.

Q5. What is the primary reason the UN cannot enforce its resolutions without member state troops?

🔹 B. Insufficient funding
🔹 C. Absence of a standing army
🔹 D. Political disagreement

📖 Explanation:

UN resolutions rely on the willingness of member states to provide forces. Without its own army, the organization lacks direct enforcement power. This dependency means that political considerations of contributing nations can affect deployment, creating a gap between decision and implementation that echoes the League’s reliance on voluntary cooperation.

Q6. Consider a crisis where two permanent members disagree on intervention. How does the veto affect the Security Council's ability to act?

🔹 A. It forces compromise
🔹 B. It blocks any action
🔹 C. It allows a thin majority
🔹 D. It triggers automatic deployment

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When two permanent members are opposed, each may threaten to veto any proposal. The necessity of avoiding a veto means the Council often seeks a compromise that satisfies both, or it may be unable to adopt any resolution. This deadlock demonstrates how the veto can paralyze decision‑making, a weakness shared with the League’s unanimity requirement.

Q7. Why might a permanent member choose to use its veto strategically rather than on principle?

🔹 A. To gain diplomatic leverage
🔹 B. To uphold international law
🔹 C. To protect national security
🔹 D. To support humanitarian causes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Strategic veto use allows a permanent member to negotiate concessions on unrelated issues, leveraging its decisive power for broader diplomatic goals. By threatening to block a resolution, the member can extract favorable terms elsewhere, turning the veto into a bargaining chip rather than a purely principled stance, which can undermine collective action.

Q8. Which organ of the UN does NOT have veto power?

🔹 A. General Assembly
🔹 B. Security Council
🔹 C. International Court of Justice
🔹 D. Economic and Social Council

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Only the Security Council possesses the veto right among UN bodies. The General Assembly, International Court of Justice, and Economic and Social Council operate without such a mechanism, meaning their decisions cannot be unilaterally blocked by a single member. This distinction highlights the unique authority and potential for paralysis within the Council.

📖 Explanation:

Without its own troops, the UN must request forces from member nations, a process that can be time‑consuming due to political approvals and logistical arrangements. This dependency slows the organization’s ability to respond swiftly to crises, a limitation comparable to the League’s reliance on voluntary contributions that often hampered timely action.

This nuance shows the strategic use of abstentions.
