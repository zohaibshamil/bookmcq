What is Political Disputes Resolved by League of Nations?

The League successfully mediated several political disputes in the 1920s, including the Åland Islands conflict between Finland and Sweden and the Greek-Bulgarian border incident, proving that arbitration and diplomatic pressure could peacefully settle conflicts when major powers were not directly involved.

📝 All Political Disputes Resolved by League of Nations MCQs

Q1. On which date did the League of Nations formally come into existence?

A.\(10\ January\ 1919\)
B.\(10\ January\ 1920\) ✅
C.\(11\ November\ 1918\)
D.\(28\ June\ 1919\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League was officially established on \(10\ January\ 1920\), the same day the Treaty of Versailles entered into force, marking the beginning of an international body tasked with preventing future wars by providing a forum for diplomatic negotiation and collective security measures.

Q2. Where were the headquarters of the League of Nations located?

A.Geneva ✅
B.Lausanne
C.Zurich
D.Vienna
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League of Nations set its headquarters in Geneva, Switzerland, a neutral location chosen to facilitate cooperation among member states and to symbolize the organization’s commitment to peace, diplomacy, and international collaboration throughout its existence.

Q3. Which South African statesman praised the League as “one of the great miracles of history”?

A.Nelson Mandela
B.Desmond Tutu
C.Jan Smuts ✅
D.Cecil Rhodes
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Jan Smuts, a prominent South African statesman, expressed optimism about the League in 1930, declaring that the world was witnessing “one of the great miracles of history,” reflecting his belief in the League’s potential to maintain peace and resolve disputes.

Q4. Which two aggressor nations challenged the League’s authority in the 1930s?

A.Germany and Soviet Union
B.Spain and Portugal
C.France and Belgium
D.Japan and Italy ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The League faced serious challenges when Japan invaded Manchuria in 1931 and Italy attacked Abyssinia in 1935, actions that directly defied the League’s calls for restraint and highlighted its inability to enforce collective security against determined aggressors.

Q5. In what year was the League of Nations formally dissolved?

A.\(1945\)
B.\(1946\) ✅
C.\(1947\)
D.\(1950\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: After World War II, the League of Nations ceased operations and was formally dissolved in \(1946\), making way for the establishment of the United Nations, which inherited many of its responsibilities but sought to address the structural weaknesses that had limited the League’s effectiveness.

Q6. Which British statesman helped draft the League’s Covenant?

A.Lord Robert Cecil ✅
B.Arthur Balfour
C.Winston Churchill
D.David Lloyd George
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Lord Robert Cecil of Britain was instrumental in drafting the League’s Covenant, collaborating with other world leaders to outline the organization’s principles, including collective security and dispute‑resolution mechanisms that were later incorporated into the peace treaties ending World War I.

Q7. According to the principle of collective security, what should member states do if one member is attacked?

A.Impose economic sanctions
B.Issue a formal statement of condemnation
C.Collectively use military force to restrain the aggressor ✅
D.Remain neutral and observe the conflict
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Collective security obliges all member nations to act together, typically by deploying military forces, to deter or stop aggression against any member; this unified response is intended to make the cost of aggression prohibitive and preserve peace among the League’s participants.

Q8. Why was the League unable to stop Japan’s invasion of Manchuria in 1931?

A.Japan withdrew voluntarily after diplomatic pressure
B.The League lacked a standing army to enforce its resolutions ✅
C.Member states imposed immediate economic sanctions that succeeded
D.The League’s decision‑making required unanimity, which was not achieved
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League’s inability to halt the invasion stemmed largely from its lack of an enforcement mechanism such as a standing army, leaving it dependent on moral persuasion and voluntary compliance, which Japan ignored, exposing a critical weakness in collective security.

Q9. If the League originally had 58 members and 12 withdrew, what percentage of the original membership remained?

A.\( \frac{46}{58}\times100 \approx 79.3\% \) ✅
B.\( \frac{50}{58}\times100 \approx 86.2\% \)
C.\( \frac{40}{58}\times100 \approx 69.0\% \)
D.\( \frac{30}{58}\times100 \approx 51.7\% \)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: After 12 of the original 58 members left, 46 remained. Calculating the proportion gives \( \frac{46}{58}\times100 \approx 79.3\% \), indicating that roughly four‑fifths of the founding membership continued to support the League despite early attrition.

Q10. Which clause ensured that the League Covenant would be included in each separate peace treaty?

A.Article 1
B.Article 10
C.Article 13 ✅
D.Article 17
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Article 13 of the Covenant stipulated that the League’s rules be incorporated into each individual peace treaty, guaranteeing that the organization would have a legal foundation across all participating nations rather than remaining a mere proposal without binding effect.

Q11. Why was the League unable to intervene in the Germany‑Czechoslovakia dispute leading up to World War II?

A.The League lacked authority to mediate territorial claims
B.Member states were preoccupied with domestic economic crises ✅
C.The League’s decision‑making required unanimous consent, which was not achieved
D.The League had already dissolved by the time the dispute arose
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: By the late 1930s, many League members were grappling with severe economic problems stemming from the Great Depression, limiting their willingness to allocate resources or political capital toward mediating the German‑Czechoslovakian crisis, thereby weakening the League’s capacity to act.

Q12. If the League imposed sanctions equal to 10 % of an aggressor’s GDP, which economic effect would most likely occur?

A.Hyperinflation due to excess money supply
B.A recession caused by reduced government spending and investment ✅
C.A trade surplus from increased exports
D.Currency appreciation due to higher demand for the nation’s goods
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Sanctions amounting to 10 % of a country’s Gross Domestic Product would sharply curtail government expenditures and foreign investment, likely triggering a recession as businesses cut back production and employment in response to the fiscal contraction.

Q13. Which of the following was NOT a primary aim of the League of Nations?

A.Maintain peace through collective security
B.Promote international trade agreements ✅
C.Protect minority rights in member states
D.Foster economic and social cooperation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: While the League pursued peace, minority protection, and social welfare, it did not have a specific mandate to negotiate or enforce international trade agreements, which were later addressed by separate economic institutions.

Q14. Critically assess the claim that the League’s failure was mainly due to the United States not joining. Which statement best reflects this view?

A.The U.S. absence denied the League crucial military power, making enforcement impossible
B.Without U.S. financial contributions, the League could not fund its operations ✅
C.American public opinion opposed any international organization, delegitimizing the League
D.The League’s structural flaws outweighed any single nation’s participation, including the U.S.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Although the United States never became a member, the League’s failure cannot be solely attributed to that omission; its structural weaknesses—such as the requirement for unanimity, lack of enforcement mechanisms, and dependence on voluntary cooperation—proved far more decisive in limiting its effectiveness.

Q15. Compare collective‑security mechanisms of the League and the United Nations; which factor most contributed to the League’s weakness?

A.The League required unanimous approval for sanctions, whereas the UN uses a Security Council majority
B.The League lacked a permanent secretariat to coordinate actions
C.The League’s decisions were non‑binding, while UN resolutions are legally enforceable ✅
D.The League allowed any member to veto actions, unlike the UN’s veto system
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The League’s decisions were largely advisory and lacked binding legal force, meaning member states could ignore resolutions without penalty; in contrast, the United Nations’ Charter grants the Security Council authority to impose binding sanctions, giving it a stronger enforcement capacity.

Q16. If the League had a binding military clause, how might the outcome of Italy’s invasion of Abyssinia have differed?

A.Italy would have withdrawn voluntarily after diplomatic pressure
B.The League would have imposed economic sanctions only
C.Member states would have launched a joint naval blockade but no ground troops
D.A coalition of League members would have deployed forces to force Italian withdrawal ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A binding military clause would have compelled member nations to contribute troops or other coercive measures, enabling a coordinated military response that could have compelled Italy to retreat from Abyssinia, demonstrating the potential power of collective enforcement.

Q17. The League prevented 15 out of 30 potential conflicts. What was its success rate?

A.\( \frac{15}{30}\times100 = 50\% \) ✅
B.\( \frac{15}{30}\times100 = 75\% \)
C.\( \frac{15}{30}\times100 = 30\% \)
D.\( \frac{15}{30}\times100 = 90\% \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Success rate is calculated by dividing the number of conflicts averted (15) by the total potential conflicts (30) and multiplying by 100, yielding \( \frac{15}{30}\times100 = 50\% \), indicating that the League resolved half of the disputes it faced.

Q18. Evaluate the impact of Jan Smuts’ quote on public perception; which effect was most likely?

A.It heightened skepticism about the League’s effectiveness
B.It increased public confidence and support for the League’s mission ✅
C.It led to immediate policy changes in member states
D.It caused a surge in membership applications from new countries
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Smuts’ optimistic statement reinforced the belief that the League could achieve unprecedented peace, bolstering public confidence and encouraging member states to view the organization as a viable mechanism for resolving international disputes.

Q19. If the League required unanimity for sanctions, how would this have affected its response to the Manchuria crisis?

A.Sanctions would have been imposed quickly because all members agreed
B.The requirement would have delayed or blocked sanctions, reducing effectiveness
C.Unanimity would have forced a military intervention instead of sanctions ✅
D.It would have led to economic cooperation with Japan rather than punishment
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Requiring unanimity would have made it difficult to achieve consensus, often resulting in delayed or absent sanctions; in the Manchuria case, this obstacle likely prevented decisive action, diminishing the League’s capacity to deter aggression.

Q20. Which social achievement is attributed to the League of Nations?

A.Assisting thousands of refugees and former prisoners of war ✅
B.Launching the first satellite into orbit
C.Negotiating the nuclear non‑proliferation treaty
D.Developing the internet protocol suite
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: One of the League’s most notable social contributions was its humanitarian work, which helped thousands of refugees and former prisoners of war return to their homes, demonstrating its commitment to humanitarian relief beyond political dispute resolution.

Q21. In 1930, supporters felt optimistic about the League. Which event contributed most to this optimism?

A.The League’s first successful resolution of a border dispute
B.Jan Smuts’ public endorsement of the League as a miracle ✅
C.The creation of the World Health Organization
D.The establishment of the United Nations Charter
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Jan Smuts publicly praised the League in 1930, declaring it a “miracle of history,” a statement that resonated worldwide and bolstered confidence in the organization’s capacity to maintain peace and resolve conflicts.

Q22. What primary reason did the League not meet after December 1939?

A.Lack of funding from member states
B.The outbreak of World II made continued meetings impossible ✅
C.Massive withdrawal of member countries
D.Expiration of the League’s founding charter

📖 Explanation: The onset of World II in September 1939 shattered the League’s purpose, as the major powers were engaged in full‑scale war, rendering the organization incapable of convening meetings or influencing events, leading to its final session in December 1939.
