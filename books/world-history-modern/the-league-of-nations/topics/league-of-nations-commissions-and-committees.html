What is League of Nations commissions and committees?

Commissions and committees were specialized bodies created by the League to address specific transnational issues like health, labor, refugees, and drug trafficking, allowing experts to develop international standards and coordinate humanitarian efforts beyond pure politics.

📝 All League of Nations commissions and committees MCQs

Q1. On which date did the League of Nations formally come into existence?

A.10-Jan-20 ✅
B.28-Jun-19
C.11-Nov-18
D.24-Oct-20
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League was established on \(10 January 1920\), the same day the Treaty of Versailles entered into force. This date marks the official start of the organization’s activities, as the Covenant was activated and member states began to participate in its mechanisms.

Q2. Where were the headquarters of the League of Nations located?

A.Paris, France
B.Geneva, Switzerland ✅
C.London, United Kingdom
D.The Hague, Netherlands
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League’s permanent secretariat was based in Geneva, Switzerland. Geneva provided a neutral location and was already a hub for international diplomacy, facilitating the League’s work on peace‑keeping, humanitarian aid, and economic cooperation among its member nations.

Q3. Which South African statesman praised the League as a ‘great miracle of history’?

A.Nelson Mandela
B.Cyril Ramaphosa
C.Jan Smuts ✅
D.Desmond Tutu
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Jan Smuts, a prominent South African leader and former Prime Minister, expressed optimism about the League in 1930, describing its achievements as one of the great miracles of history. His endorsement reflected the confidence many held in the League’s potential to maintain world peace.

Q4. The League of Nations was created as part of which treaty’s implementation?

A.Treaty of Trianon
B.Treaty of Saint-Germain
C.Treaty of Locarno
D.Treaty of Versailles ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The League’s Covenant was incorporated into the Treaty of Versailles, which officially ended World War I. When the treaty entered into operation on \(10 January 1920\), the League simultaneously began its work, linking its legal foundation to the peace settlement.

Q5. Which two aggressor nations challenged the League’s authority in the 1930s?

A.Germany and Spain
B.Japan and Italy ✅
C.France and Soviet Union
D.United Kingdom and United States
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Japan’s invasion of Manchuria in 1931 and Italy’s attack on Abyssinia in 1935 directly tested the League’s ability to enforce collective security. Both countries ignored League resolutions, exposing the organization’s limited enforcement power and contributing to its decline.

Q6. What was the primary purpose of collective security under the League?

A.To promote economic trade between members
B.To establish a common currency
C.To have member states act together against aggressors ✅
D.To create a shared military alliance
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Collective security meant that if any member state was attacked, all other members would jointly respond, using diplomatic, economic, or military measures to deter aggression. This principle aimed to make the cost of war prohibitive for potential aggressors, thereby preserving peace.

Q7. Which individual was NOT involved in drafting the League Covenant?

A.Winston Churchill
B.Lord Robert Cecil
C.Jan Smuts
D.George Bernard Shaw ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Winston Churchill, though a prominent British politician, did not take part in the drafting committee for the League Covenant. The Covenant was prepared by an international group that included Lord Robert Cecil, Jan Smuts, Léon Bourgeois, Paul Hymans, and U.S. President Woodrow Wilson.

Q8. In Woodrow Wilson’s 14 Points, which point specifically called for the creation of the League?

A.Point Fourteen ✅
B.Point One
C.Point Seven
D.Point Ten
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Point Fourteen of Wilson’s Fourteen Points called for the establishment of a general association of nations to provide mutual guarantees of political independence and territorial integrity, which became the League of Nations. This point encapsulated Wilson’s vision for a permanent international organization to prevent future wars.

Q9. Why did the League fail to prevent the outbreak of World II after 1935?

A.Member states lacked willingness to enforce sanctions
B.The League had no military forces
C.Economic depression weakened cooperation
D.All of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The League’s failure stemmed from multiple factors: major powers were reluctant to impose or enforce economic sanctions, the organization possessed no standing army, and the global economic depression reduced cooperation. These weaknesses combined to render the League ineffective in curbing aggression that led to World War II.

Q10. If a member state breached a League covenant, which action would the League most likely take first?

A.Issue a formal reprimand
B.Deploy troops immediately ✅
C.Impose a trade embargo
D.Expel the member
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League typically began with diplomatic pressure, issuing a formal reprimand or condemnation to signal disapproval. Direct military intervention was rare due to the lack of an enforcement army, and economic sanctions were considered only after diplomatic efforts failed, making a reprimand the initial response.

Q11. Which mechanism would have been most effective for the League to enforce collective security against Japan’s 1931 invasion of Manchuria?

A.A coordinated economic embargo by all members
B.Dispatching a multinational peacekeeping force
C.Holding a special emergency session to issue a unanimous resolution ✅
D.Providing humanitarian aid to Chinese civilians
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: An economic embargo, agreed upon and applied uniformly by all League members, would have exerted significant pressure on Japan by restricting access to essential resources and markets, creating a costly incentive for withdrawal. Military deployment was impractical, and resolutions alone lacked enforcement power.

Q12. How did the League’s commissions contribute to refugee assistance after World I?

A.They organized transport and repatriation of thousands of displaced persons ✅
B.They provided military protection for refugees
C.They created a permanent settlement for all refugees in Geneva
D.They limited refugee movement to prevent disease spread
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League’s specialized commissions coordinated the identification, transport, and repatriation of refugees and former prisoners of war, helping thousands return to their homes. Their work laid groundwork for modern humanitarian agencies, demonstrating the League’s capacity for non‑military, humanitarian interventions.

Q13. In which year was the League of Nations officially dissolved?

A.1945
B.1946 ✅
C.1939
D.1920
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League of Nations formally ceased to exist in 1946, following the establishment of the United Nations. Its assets and responsibilities were transferred to the new organization, marking the end of its existence after having failed to prevent the outbreak of World War II.

Q14. Which country launched the attack on Abyssinia in 1935?

A.Germany
B.Japan
C.Italy ✅
D.Spain
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Italy, under Benito Mussolini’s fascist regime, invaded Abyssinia (modern‑day Ethiopia) in 1935. The aggression challenged the League’s authority, as the organization’s sanctions were ineffective, highlighting its inability to curb the expansionist policies of powerful member states.

Q15. Which committee was responsible for drafting the League Covenant?

A.International Committee of Jurists
B.League’s Executive Committee
C.League’s Drafting Committee ✅
D.Peace and Security Committee
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The League’s Covenant was drafted by a dedicated International Drafting Committee composed of representatives such as Lord Cecil, Jan Smuts, Léon Bourgeois, Paul Hymans, and Woodrow Wilson. This body crafted the legal framework that defined the League’s functions and obligations for member nations.

Q16. If a modern crisis required international cooperation, which League principle could be applied today?

A.Collective security through joint sanctions ✅
B.Isolation of the offending state
C.Unilateral military action by a single power
D.Non‑intervention in all disputes
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The principle of collective security—where states act together to impose joint sanctions or other measures against an aggressor—remains relevant. It encourages multilateral action, distributes responsibility, and seeks to deter aggression without resorting to unilateral force, mirroring the League’s original intent.

Q17. What role did Lloyd George play concerning the League of Nations?

A.He identified the League as a British war aim
B.He championed the League as part of Britain’s post‑war goals ✅
C.He served as the League’s first Secretary‑General
D.He drafted the League’s economic policies
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: British Prime Minister David Lloyd George championed the League as a key component of Britain’s war aims, promoting its inclusion in the post‑war settlement. His advocacy helped secure British support for the League, though he did not hold an official position within the organization itself.

Q18. Which similarity exists between the League’s collective security concept and the modern UN Security Council’s enforcement powers?

A.Both rely on member consensus to authorize collective action ✅
B.Both have standing armies to enforce decisions
C.Both can unilaterally impose sanctions without vote
D.Both lack any mechanism for peacekeeping
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Both the League’s collective security and the UN Security Council depend on consensus among member states to legitimize collective measures, such as sanctions or authorized force. While the UN has peacekeeping troops, the principle of joint decision‑making remains a common thread.

Q19. One primary function of the League’s commissions was to:

A.Address specific international problems like health and labor ✅
B.Command military operations across borders
C.Negotiate trade tariffs exclusively
D.Elect the League’s President
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League established specialized commissions—such as the Health, Labor, and Economic committees—to tackle particular global issues. These bodies researched, reported, and recommended policies, providing expertise and coordination that complemented the League’s broader diplomatic efforts.

Q20. What was the outcome of the League’s response to the Manchurian crisis?

A.It issued a condemnation but could not enforce withdrawal ✅
B.It successfully forced Japan to retreat
C.It led to immediate military intervention
D.It resulted in Japan’s expulsion from the League
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League’s reaction to Japan’s invasion of Manchuria involved issuing a formal condemnation and calling for withdrawal, yet it lacked enforcement mechanisms. Consequently, Japan ignored the resolution and continued its occupation, illustrating the League’s limited capacity to compel compliance.

Q21. If a League refugee commission could relocate 10,000 refugees each month, how many could it move in a full year?

A.\(10{,}000 \times 12 = 120{,}000\) ✅
B.\(10{,}000 + 12 = 10{,}012\)
C.\(10{,}000 \div 12 \approx 833\)
D.\(12^{10{,}000}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Multiplying the monthly capacity by twelve months yields \(10{,}000 \times 12 = 120{,}000\) refugees per year. This simple calculation demonstrates the potential scale of the League’s humanitarian impact when its commissions operated at full efficiency over an extended period.

Q22. Which statement best explains the League’s overall failure to prevent World War II?

A.Its lack of enforcement power and member commitment ✅
B.Its excessive military capabilities
C.Its focus on economic integration over peace
D.Its over‑reliance on secret diplomacy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League’s inability to stop the march toward World War II stemmed mainly from insufficient enforcement tools and the unwillingness of major powers to commit to collective action. Without credible sanctions or military support, the League could not deter aggressive states, leading to its eventual collapse.

Q23. Assuming the probability of war without collective security is 0.8 and with it drops to 0.5, what is the percent reduction in war probability?

A.\( (0.8-0.5)/0.8 \times 100 = 37.5\% \) ✅
B.\(0.8-0.5 = 0.3\)
C.\(0.5/0.8 = 62.5\% \)
D.\(0.8 \times 0.5 = 0.4\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The reduction is calculated by \((0.8-0.5)/0.8 \times 100 = 37.5\%\). This shows that implementing collective security could lower the chance of war by roughly a third, illustrating the potential effectiveness of such mechanisms even in simplified probabilistic models.
