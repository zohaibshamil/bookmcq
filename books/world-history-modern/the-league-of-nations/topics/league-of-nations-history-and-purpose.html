What is League of Nations history and purpose?

Established in 1920 and dissolved in 1946, the League’s fundamental purpose was to maintain world peace through collective security, disarmament, and arbitration, representing the first global attempt to replace balance-of-power diplomacy with an institutionalized system of international law and cooperation.

Q1. What year did the League of Nations formally come into existence?

A. 1919
B. 1920 ✅
C. 1921
D. 1922

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League was officially established on 10 January 1920, the same day the Treaty of Versailles entered into force. This historic moment marked the creation of an international body intended to prevent future wars by providing a forum for diplomatic resolution of disputes.

Q2. Which city hosted the League's headquarters?

A. Paris
B. London
C. Geneva ✅
D. New York

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Geneva, Switzerland, was selected as the seat of the League of Nations because of its neutral status and central location in Europe. The city’s diplomatic tradition and existing infrastructure made it a practical choice for the League’s administrative and meeting activities.

Q3. Who was the South African statesman who praised the League as a \great miracle\?

A. Nelson Mandela
B. Jan Smuts ✅
C. Desmond Tutu
D. F.W. de Klerk

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Jan Smuts, a prominent South African leader and former British Imperial War Cabinet member, expressed admiration for the League in 1930, calling its achievements a \great miracle of history.\ His endorsement reflected the optimism of many supporters during the League’s early years.

Q4. If a member state attacks another, what mechanism was the League supposed to employ?

A. Economic sanctions
B. Collective security ✅
C. Diplomatic isolation
D. Military alliance

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League’s founding covenant articulated a principle of collective security, meaning that an attack on any member would trigger a coordinated response by all other members. This was intended to deter aggression by presenting a united front, though in practice the League often lacked the means to enforce it.

Q5. Considering the League's failure after 1935, which factor most contributed to its inability to enforce decisions?

A. Lack of US membership
B. Absence of armed forces
C. Economic depression
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The League suffered from multiple structural weaknesses: the United States never joined, depriving it of a major power; it possessed no standing army to enforce resolutions; and the worldwide economic depression reduced willingness to fund collective actions. Together these issues crippled its effectiveness.

Q6. Suppose the League had an enforcement army of 100,000 troops divided equally among 5 major powers. How many troops per power?

A. 10000
B. 15000
C. 20000 ✅
D. 25000

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Dividing \(100,000\) troops by 5 powers yields \( \frac{100,000}{5}=20,000\) troops each. This simple arithmetic illustrates the scale of force that would have been required for the League to act decisively, a capability it never possessed in reality.

Q7. Which treaty came into operation the same day the League was founded?

A. Treaty of Versailles ✅
B. Treaty of Trianon
C. Treaty of Saint-Germain
D. Treaty of Brest-Litovsk

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Versailles, which formally ended World War I, entered into force on 10 January 1920—the exact date the League of Nations began its work. This synchronization symbolized the transition from wartime peace settlements to a new system of collective international governance.

Q8. Why was including the League covenant in each peace treaty crucial for its existence?

A. It provided legal legitimacy ✅
B. It ensured funding
C. It guaranteed US participation
D. It allowed automatic membership

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Embedding the covenant within every major peace treaty gave the League a binding legal foundation, turning a theoretical concept into a concrete institution. Without this inclusion, the League might have remained a mere proposal lacking enforceable authority among the signatory nations.

Q9. Who were the four main architects of the League's Covenant?

A. Wilson, Cecil, Smuts, Bourgeois ✅
B. Wilson, Churchill, Mussolini, Bismarck
C. Wilson, Roosevelt, Lenin, Stalin
D. Wilson, de Gaulle, Gandhi, Mandela

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Covenant was drafted by an international committee that included U.S. President Woodrow Wilson, British statesman Lord Robert Cecil, South African leader Jan Smuts, and French diplomat Leon Bourgeois, among others. Their combined efforts produced the framework that guided the League’s operations.

Q10. During the Manchuria crisis (1931), the League imposed sanctions but Japan ignored them. What principle did this illustrate?

A. Collective security
B. Sovereign immunity
C. Power politics ✅
D. Deterrence theory

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Japan’s disregard for League sanctions highlighted the reality of power politics, where a nation’s strategic interests can outweigh international norms. The incident exposed the League’s limited capacity to compel compliance when powerful states chose to act unilaterally.

Q11. If the League had 58 member states in 1935, and 10 withdrew by 1939, what percentage remained?

A. 0.7
B. 0.8
C. 0.828 ✅
D. 0.9

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

After losing 10 members, the League retained \(58-10=48\) members. The percentage remaining is \( \frac{48}{58}\times100\approx82.76\%\), which rounds to 82.8 %. This decline reflected growing disillusionment with the organization’s effectiveness.

Q12. Which country never joined the League despite Wilson's advocacy?

A. United States ✅
B. Canada
C. Australia
D. New Zealand

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Although President Wilson championed the League, the United States Senate refused to ratify the Treaty of Versailles, preventing U.S. membership. This absence deprived the League of one of the world’s most influential powers and contributed to its later weaknesses.

Q13. The League's social work included helping refugees. Estimate the number of refugees assisted if 5% of the world’s displaced persons (approx. 20 million) were helped.

A. 500000
B. 1000000 ✅
C. 2000000
D. 5000000

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Calculating 5 % of 20 million yields \(0.05\times20,000,000=1,000,000\). This figure demonstrates the substantial humanitarian impact the League achieved, even as its political influence waned during the interwar period.

Q14. Compare the League's collective security model to NATO’s Article 5. Which statement is accurate?

A. Both guarantee automatic military response
B. Both rely on UN approval
C. Both are voluntary alliances ✅
D. Both were founded in the same decade

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Both the League and NATO operate on the principle that members agree to assist one another if any member is attacked, but participation remains voluntary. Neither organization can compel a sovereign state to join, and both rely on the political will of their members to act.

Q15. Which of the following was NOT a reason for the League's decline in the 1930s?

A. Japanese aggression
B. Italian invasion of Abyssinia
C. US participation ✅
D. Economic depression

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United States never joined the League, but its non‑participation pre‑dated the 1930s decline. The primary causes of the League’s weakening were the aggressive actions of Japan and Italy, combined with the global economic hardships that limited collective action.

Q16. Who was the British Prime Minister that referred to the League as one of Britain’s war aims?

A. Winston Churchill
B. David Lloyd George ✅
C. Neville Chamberlain
D. Clement Attlee

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

David Lloyd George, serving as British Prime Minister during the final year of World War I, highlighted the League of Nations as a central component of Britain’s post‑war objectives, emphasizing the desire for a new international framework for peace.

Q17. If the League required a two‑thirds majority of 58 members to pass a resolution, how many votes were needed?

A. 36
B. 38
C. 39 ✅
D. 40

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Two‑thirds of 58 equals \( \frac{2}{3}\times58=38.\overline{6}\). Since partial votes are impossible, the resolution would need at least 39 affirmative votes to meet the required super‑majority threshold.

Q18. The League was dissolved in which year?

A. 1945
B. 1946 ✅
C. 1947
D. 1948

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

After World War II, the League of Nations formally ceased operations in 1946, making way for the newly created United Nations, which incorporated many of the League’s ideas while aiming to address its previous shortcomings.

Q19. Assess why the League’s inability to consult during Germany’s disputes with Czechoslovakia and Poland contributed to WWII.

A. It showed irrelevance
B. It emboldened aggressors ✅
C. It caused economic collapse
D. It led to diplomatic isolation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By failing to intervene or mediate during Germany’s expansionist moves, the League signaled its impotence, encouraging further aggression. This lack of response helped pave the road to full‑scale war, as other nations perceived the League as ineffective.

Q20. Which of the following individuals was NOT part of the committee that drafted the League Covenant?

A. Paul Hymans
B. Jan Smuts
C. Leon Bourgeois
D. Otto von Bismarck ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Otto von Bismarck, the 19th‑century German chancellor, died long before the League’s formation and had no role in its covenant. The drafting committee comprised contemporary diplomats such as Hymans, Smuts, and Bourgeois.

Q21. The League aimed to settle disputes before they escalated into what?

A. Economic crises
B. Military conflicts ✅
C. Political elections
D. Cultural wars

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A core purpose of the League of Nations was to prevent the outbreak of war by providing a platform where nations could resolve disagreements peacefully before they turned into armed conflicts.

Q22. If the League’s budget was 10 million Swiss francs, and each member contributed equally, how much would each of the 58 members pay?

A. 150000
B. 172414 ✅
C. 200000
D. 250000

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the total budget by 58 members gives \( \frac{10,000,000}{58}\approx172,413.79\). Rounded to the nearest franc, each nation would be responsible for about 172,414 Swiss francs, illustrating the financial commitment required for collective action.

Q23. Which principle was central to the League’s purpose, as reflected in its covenant?

A. Self‑determination
B. Collective security ✅
C. Free trade
D. Non‑intervention

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The covenant’s cornerstone was collective security: an agreement that an attack on any member would be treated as an attack on all, prompting a coordinated response. This principle sought to deter aggression by ensuring that no nation could act alone without consequences.
