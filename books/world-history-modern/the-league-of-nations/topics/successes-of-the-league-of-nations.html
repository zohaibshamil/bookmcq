📝 All Successes of The League of Nations MCQs

Q1. On which date did the League of Nations formally come into existence?

🔹 A. \(9\) January \(1920\)
🔹 B. \(10\) January \(1920\)
🔹 C. \(11\) January \(1920\)
🔹 D. \(12\) January \(1920\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League was officially established on \(10\) January \(1920\), coinciding with the activation of the Treaty of Versailles, marking the beginning of its diplomatic efforts to maintain international peace and security.

Q2. Which South African statesman praised the League as one of the great miracles of history?

🔹 A. Winston Churchill
🔹 B. Jan Smuts
🔹 C. David Lloyd George
🔹 D. Lord Robert Cecil

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Jan Smuts, a prominent South African leader and former British Empire prime minister, lauded the League’s achievements in the early 1930s, describing its work as a historic miracle that demonstrated the potential of collective international cooperation.

Q3. Why was the League unable to stop the Japanese invasion of Manchuria in \(1931\)?

🔹 A. League had strong military forces
🔹 B. Member states refused to impose sanctions
🔹 C. League successfully mediated
🔹 D. League lacked enforcement mechanisms and members were reluctant

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The League’s charter did not include a standing army, and key members were hesitant to impose economic or military sanctions on Japan, leaving the organization powerless to compel withdrawal, which highlighted its structural weakness in enforcing collective security.

Q4. If the League had applied collective security during Italy’s attack on Abyssinia in \(1935\), what would most likely have occurred?

🔹 A. League imposed economic sanctions
🔹 B. League ignored the conflict
🔹 C. League provided direct military support to Italy
🔹 D. League dissolved

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Collective security meant that member states would coordinate sanctions and possibly provide material aid to the victim nation; in the case of Abyssinia, coordinated economic pressure could have forced Italy to halt its aggression, demonstrating the intended deterrent effect of the League’s system.

Q5. Which of the following was NOT a primary aim of the League?

🔹 A. Maintain peace through collective security
🔹 B. Settle international disputes
🔹 C. Promote trade
🔹 D. Assist refugees

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

While the League focused on peacekeeping, dispute resolution, and humanitarian assistance, promoting trade was not listed among its core objectives, which were primarily political and social rather than economic in nature.

Q6. What was the impact of including the League Covenant in each separate peace treaty?

🔹 A. Made League optional
🔹 B. Ensured legal existence
🔹 C. Had no effect
🔹 D. Delayed formation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Embedding the Covenant in the post‑war treaties gave the League a binding legal foundation, turning it from a theoretical concept into an operative international body, thereby guaranteeing its establishment and providing a framework for future cooperation among signatory nations.

Q7. In which city was the League of Nations headquartered?

🔹 A. Paris
🔹 B. Geneva
🔹 C. London
🔹 D. New York

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League’s permanent secretariat was located in Geneva, Switzerland, a neutral hub that facilitated diplomatic meetings and allowed the organization to operate from a central European location throughout its existence.

Q8. According to League procedures, what happens when a member state violates the covenant in \(1932\)?

🔹 A. Immediate military intervention
🔹 B. Complaint to Council, investigation, possible sanctions
🔹 C. Automatic expulsion
🔹 D. No action

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The breach would be reported to the League Council, which would launch an investigation; depending on findings, the Council could recommend sanctions, diplomatic pressure, or other measures, illustrating the procedural approach rather than immediate punitive action.

Q9. What contributed most to the League’s decline after \(1935\)?

🔹 A. Successful sanctions
🔹 B. Aggressors ignored orders
🔹 C. League expanded membership
🔹 D. Economic boom

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The blatant disregard of League directives by Japan and Italy, coupled with the lack of effective enforcement, eroded confidence in the organization and demonstrated its inability to curb major acts of aggression, accelerating its loss of credibility.

Q10. What proportion of the League’s existence (1920‑1946) did it hold meetings (1920‑1939)?

🔹 A. \(10/13\)
🔹 B. \(5/13\)
🔹 C. \(15/26\)
🔹 D. \(20/26\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League operated for \(26\) years in total, holding meetings for \(20\) of those years. The ratio \(20/26\) simplifies to \(10/13\), indicating that roughly \(77\%\) of its lifespan involved active diplomatic sessions before its suspension.

Q11. Which British statesman helped draft the League Covenant?

🔹 A. Lord Robert Cecil
🔹 B. Neville Chamberlain
🔹 C. Harold Macmillan
🔹 D. Anthony Eden

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Lord Robert Cecil, a senior British diplomat, collaborated with other world leaders to shape the Covenant’s provisions, ensuring that the League’s principles were embedded in the post‑war settlement and providing a blueprint for collective security and dispute resolution.

Q12. What type of humanitarian work did the League perform for refugees and former prisoners of war?

🔹 A. Providing financial loans
🔹 B. Repatriation assistance
🔹 C. Military training
🔹 D. Colonial administration

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League organized programs to locate displaced persons, facilitated their return to home countries, and coordinated with national authorities to ensure safe repatriation, thereby alleviating the suffering of millions affected by the aftermath of World War I.

Q13. Why was the League not consulted during Germany’s disputes with Czechoslovakia and Poland?

🔹 A. League was dissolved
🔹 B. Member states ignored it
🔹 C. League lacked authority and member support
🔹 D. Germany was a League member

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

By the late‑1930s, the League’s enforcement mechanisms were weak, and major powers were unwilling to commit resources; consequently, when Germany pursued expansionist policies, the organization lacked both the authority and the backing to intervene effectively.

Q14. Which treaty came into operation on the same day as the League’s formation?

🔹 A. Treaty of Versailles
🔹 B. Treaty of Trianon
🔹 C. Treaty of Saint‑Germain
🔹 D. Treaty of Locarno

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Versailles, which formally ended World War I, entered into force on \(10\) January \(1920\), the exact date the League of Nations was inaugurated, linking the two landmark post‑war initiatives.

Q15. If collective security had been applied during the Manchuria crisis, what likely outcome would have occurred?

🔹 A. Japan would have been expelled
🔹 B. Sanctions would have pressured Japan to withdraw
🔹 C. League would have sent troops
🔹 D. No change

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A coordinated sanctions regime, backed by multiple member states, could have created economic and diplomatic pressure on Japan, potentially compelling it to retreat from Manchuria and demonstrating the deterrent power of collective security.

Q16. How do the League’s achievements in the 1920s compare to those in the 1930s?

🔹 A. 1920s: many successes; 1930s: many failures
🔹 B. Both decades equal
🔹 C. 1920s: limited activity; 1930s: high activity
🔹 D. 1920s: economic work only; 1930s: diplomatic only

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The 1920s saw the League resolve minor disputes and launch humanitarian programs, whereas the 1930s were marked by high‑profile aggressions that the League could not curb, highlighting a stark contrast between early optimism and later impotence.

Q17. In which year was the League of Nations dissolved?

🔹 A. 1945
🔹 B. 1946
🔹 C. 1947
🔹 D. 1948

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

After the conclusion of World War II, the League formally ceased operations in \(1946\), handing over its responsibilities to the newly created United Nations, which was intended to address the shortcomings of its predecessor.

Q18. What does Jan Smuts’s quote about the League imply?

🔹 A. League was a minor experiment
🔹 B. League represented a historic miracle
🔹 C. League was a threat
🔹 D. League was irrelevant

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Smuts’s statement that the League was “one of the great miracles of history” underscores his belief that the organization embodied an unprecedented achievement in collective peace‑building and international cooperation.

Q19. If the League solved an average of five minor disputes per year from \(1920\) to \(1930\), how many disputes were resolved in total?

🔹 A. 50
🔹 B. 55
🔹 C. 60
🔹 D. 65

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The period from \(1920\) through \(1930\) spans \(11\) years. Multiplying the average of five disputes per year by \(11\) yields \(55\) total disputes, illustrating the League’s modest but measurable role in conflict resolution during its early years.

Q20. Which leader was NOT involved in drafting the League Covenant?

🔹 A. Woodrow Wilson
🔹 B. Leon Bourgeois
🔹 C. Paul Hymans
🔹 D. Winston Churchill

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Winston Churchill, while a prominent British politician, did not take part in the drafting of the League Covenant; the document was prepared by a committee that included Wilson, Bourgeois, Hymans, and Cecil among others.

Q21. What principle should a new international organization prioritize, learning from the League’s failures?

🔹 A. Strong enforcement mechanisms
🔹 B. Limited membership
🔹 C. Economic sanctions only
🔹 D. No collective security

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

One of the League’s chief shortcomings was its inability to enforce decisions; a modern body must therefore embed robust enforcement tools—such as credible sanctions or collective military response—to ensure compliance and maintain credibility among members.

Q22. The League’s inability to prevent war is most often cited as an example of which International Relations theory?

🔹 A. Realism
🔹 B. Liberalism
🔹 C. Constructivism
🔹 D. Marxism

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Realist scholars argue that the League’s failure illustrates the dominance of state power and self‑interest over collective institutions, emphasizing that without a balance of power or enforceable authority, international organizations cannot reliably prevent conflict.

Q23. How did the League assist former prisoners of war?

🔹 A. Providing weapons
🔹 B. Facilitating repatriation
🔹 C. Training camps
🔹 D. Negotiating trade deals

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League organized efforts to locate and return former prisoners of war to their home countries, coordinating with national governments and relief agencies to ensure that these individuals could be reunited with their families after the chaos of the war.
