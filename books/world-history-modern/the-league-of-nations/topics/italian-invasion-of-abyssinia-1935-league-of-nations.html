Q1. In which month and year did Italy launch its invasion of Abyssinia?

🔹 A. October \(1935\)
🔹 B. September \(1935\)
🔹 C. June \(1936\)
🔹 D. January \(1935\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Italian offensive began on the 3rd of October \(1935\), marking the start of a prolonged campaign that would eventually lead to the occupation of Abyssinia. This date is widely recorded in contemporary diplomatic correspondence and is a basic factual point that students of interwar history should know.

Q2. What was the League of Nations' first major action in response to Italy's aggression?

🔹 A. Imposed economic sanctions on Italy
🔹 B. Declared war on Italy
🔹 C. Sent a peacekeeping force to Abyssinia
🔹 D. Did nothing and waited for diplomatic talks

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League quickly reacted by adopting a sanctions regime, aiming to pressure Italy economically without resorting to military conflict. This decision reflected the League's collective‑security principle, though the sanctions were limited and ultimately failed to deter Mussolini's expansionist ambitions.

Q3. As a League delegate in 1935, which principle would you cite to justify sanctions against Italy?

🔹 A. Collective security and the Covenant's Article 10
🔹 B. National self‑determination of Italy
🔹 C. Non‑intervention in internal affairs
🔹 D. Economic competition among member states

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Delegates would invoke the collective‑security clause, which obliges members to act together when any state breaches peace. Article 10 of the Covenant specifically called for coordinated measures, such as sanctions, to maintain international order and discourage unilateral aggression like Italy's invasion.

Q4. Why were the economic sanctions imposed on Italy largely ineffective?

🔹 A. Key resources like oil were exempted
🔹 B. Sanctions were not enforced by major powers
🔹 C. Italy had abundant domestic production
🔹 D. Sanctions were accompanied by diplomatic support

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The sanctions lacked full enforcement because Britain and France, wary of antagonising Italy, allowed critical commodities to continue flowing. This half‑hearted implementation meant that Italy could sustain its war effort, demonstrating the League's limited capacity to impose truly coercive measures without unanimous member commitment.

Q5. If Italy had remained a member of the League after the invasion, how might diplomatic dynamics have changed?

🔹 A. Italy could have vetoed future sanctions
🔹 B. Italy would have been expelled later
🔹 C. Italy would have lost its colonial holdings sooner
🔹 D. Italy would have been forced to negotiate a cease‑fire

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Retaining membership would have granted Italy a formal voice in Council deliberations, potentially allowing it to block or dilute punitive measures against itself. This scenario underscores the paradox of a peace‑keeping body that includes aggressor states, complicating collective‑action decisions.

Q6. Which key difference distinguished the League's handling of Manchuria from Abyssinia?

🔹 A. Manchuria saw a full embargo, Abyssinia only limited sanctions
🔹 B. Abyssinia prompted a military coalition, Manchuria did not
🔹 C. Manchuria involved non‑member powers, Abyssinia involved only League members
🔹 D. Sanctions were stricter and more universally applied in Abyssinia

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Manchuria crisis saw Japan withdraw from the League, and the League's response was limited to moral condemnation. In Abyssinia, the League imposed sanctions, albeit weak, and engaged in diplomatic negotiations, reflecting a more active but still insufficient approach to collective security.

Q7. Who was the head of the Italian government that ordered the invasion?

🔹 A. Benito Mussolini
🔹 B. Giovanni Giolitti
🔹 C. Victor Emmanuel III
🔹 D. Alfredo Ottaviani

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Benito Mussolini, the Fascist Prime Minister, orchestrated the campaign to expand Italy's imperial ambitions. His rhetoric framed the war as a civilising mission, and his authoritarian regime directed both the political justification and the military logistics of the invasion.

Q8. What primary justification did Italy give for invading Abyssinia?

🔹 A. A civilising mission to bring progress
🔹 B. Defending its borders from Abyssinian attacks
🔹 C. Securing oil resources
🔹 D. Protecting Italian nationals in the region

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Mussolini's regime presented the war as a mission to modernise Abyssinia, claiming it would bring infrastructure, education, and order. This narrative aimed to garner domestic support and legitimize expansion by portraying Italy as a benevolent coloniser rather than an aggressor.

Historical records indicate that Italy mobilised roughly one hundred and fifty thousand troops for the first major offensives. This sizable force, equipped with modern artillery and air support, underscored the scale of the operation and highlighted the League's inability to counter such a well‑planned military deployment.

This ambivalence weakened the enforcement of the sanctions and illustrated the competing strategic interests that hampered unified League action.

The failure to halt Italy’s aggression exposed the League’s structural weaknesses, especially the reliance on unanimous support for sanctions. This erosion of credibility contributed to a broader disillusionment, encouraging nations to pursue bilateral security arrangements and undermining the League’s authority.

Q14. Which treaty came into effect on the same day the League was formally established?

🔹 C. Treaty of Saint‑Germain‑en‑Laye
🔹 D. Treaty of Locarno

The Treaty of Versailles, signed in 1919, entered into force on \(10\ January\ 1920\), coinciding with the League’s formal inception. This alignment symbolised the post‑World War I attempt to institutionalise peace through both legal settlement of territorial issues and an international body for dispute resolution.

Q17. Which fundamental League principle did Italy violate by invading Abyssinia?

🔹 A. Collective security
🔹 B. Self‑determination of peoples
🔹 C. Freedom of navigation
🔹 D. Disarmament

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League’s Covenant stipulated that member states would collectively respond to aggression, embodying the collective‑security doctrine. Italy’s unilateral military action directly contravened this principle, undermining the very foundation upon which the League was intended to preserve international peace.

Q18. Which of the following nations was NOT a founding member of the League of Nations?

🔹 B. United Kingdom
🔹 C. France
🔹 D. Italy

📖 Explanation:

The inability to restrain Italy signalled to other revisionist states that the League lacked teeth, encouraging further acts of aggression. This perception contributed to the erosion of the collective‑security system and paved the way for the eventual collapse of the League.
