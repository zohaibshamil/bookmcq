📝 All The General Assembly of the League of Nations MCQs

Q1. On which date did the League of Nations formally come into existence?

🔹 A. \(1918\)
🔹 B. \(1920\)
🔹 C. \(1922\)
🔹 D. \(1930\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League officially began on \(10\) January \(1920\), the same day the Treaty of Versailles entered into force, marking the first concerted international effort to prevent another large‑scale war after World War I.

Q2. In which city was the League of Nations headquartered?

🔹 A. Paris
🔹 B. London
🔹 C. New York
🔹 D. Geneva

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The League established its permanent headquarters in Geneva, Switzerland, a neutral location that facilitated diplomatic dialogue among member states and allowed the organization to operate without the political pressures of a capital tied to any major power.

Q3. What was one of the League’s principal aims?

🔹 A. Maintain peace through collective security
🔹 B. Promote colonial expansion
🔹 C. Regulate global trade
🔹 D. Standardize currencies

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League was created to preserve international peace by employing collective security, meaning that an attack on one member would elicit a coordinated response from all other members, thereby deterring aggression through shared responsibility.

Q4. Which South African statesman praised the League in 1930, calling its progress a 'great miracle of history'?

🔹 A. Nelson Mandela
🔹 B. Desmond Tutu
🔹 C. Jan Smuts
🔹 D. Jacob Zuma

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Jan Smuts, a prominent South African leader and former British Empire minister, expressed optimism about the League’s achievements in 1930, reflecting the contemporary belief that the organization could become a lasting engine for global stability.

Q5. Which treaty came into operation on the same day the League was founded?

🔹 A. Treaty of Versailles
🔹 B. Treaty of Trianon
🔹 C. Treaty of Saint‑Germain
🔹 D. Treaty of Locarno

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Versailles, which formally ended World War I, entered into force on \(10\) January \(1920\), the very day the League of Nations was inaugurated, linking the two historic peace‑building initiatives.

Q6. Which British statesman helped draft the League Covenant?

🔹 A. Winston Churchill
🔹 B. Arthur Balfour
🔹 C. David Lloyd George
🔹 D. Lord Robert Cecil

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Lord Robert Cecil, a senior British diplomat, contributed significantly to the drafting of the League Covenant, collaborating with other world leaders to outline the legal framework that would govern the new international organization.

Q7. In which year was the League of Nations dissolved?

🔹 A. \(1944\)
🔹 B. \(1946\)
🔹 C. \(1950\)
🔹 D. \(1939\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

After its functions became obsolete following World War II, the League formally ceased to exist in \(1946\), making way for the United Nations to assume the role of the principal intergovernmental body for maintaining peace.

Q8. If a member state breaches collective security, what action was the League designed to take?

🔹 A. Issue a formal apology
🔹 B. Provide humanitarian aid
🔹 C. Impose economic sanctions and a collective military response
🔹 D. Ignore the violation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The League’s collective‑security principle stipulated that any aggression against a member would trigger coordinated economic sanctions and, if necessary, a joint military response by other members, thereby discouraging unilateral acts of force.

Q9. Which event first challenged the League’s authority in the 1930s?

🔹 A. Japanese invasion of Manchuria (1931)
🔹 B. Italian invasion of Abyssinia (1935)
🔹 C. German annexation of Austria (1938)
🔹 D. Spanish Civil War (1936)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League’s first serious test came with Japan’s seizure of Manchuria in \(1931\); the organization’s inability to compel Japan to withdraw demonstrated its limited enforcement power and foreshadowed later failures.

Q10. What was Woodrow Wilson’s key contribution to the League’s formation?

🔹 A. He opposed the League
🔹 B. He drafted the Covenant alone
🔹 C. He ignored the League in his policies
🔹 D. He ensured the Covenant was included in each peace treaty

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

President Wilson insisted that the League Covenant be embedded in the post‑war treaties, guaranteeing that the organization would be legally binding on signatory nations rather than remaining a mere proposal, thus giving the League a solid foundation.

Q11. Why did the League fail to stop Italy’s aggression against Abyssinia?

🔹 A. It imposed strong sanctions that succeeded
🔹 B. It lacked enforcement mechanisms and major powers were unwilling to act
🔹 C. Italy withdrew voluntarily
🔹 D. The League expelled Italy immediately

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Italy’s invasion exposed the League’s structural weakness: without a standing army or mandatory sanctions, and with key powers reluctant to enforce measures, the League could only issue condemnations, which were insufficient to halt the aggression.

Q12. Which principle was NOT included in the League’s Covenant?

🔹 A. Collective security
🔹 B. Disarmament
🔹 C. Free trade
🔹 D. Arbitration of disputes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

While the Covenant emphasized collective security, disarmament, and dispute arbitration, it did not contain provisions for free trade, reflecting the organization’s primary focus on peace rather than economic integration.

Q13. How did the League assist refugees and former prisoners of war after World War I?

🔹 A. Established the High Commission for Refugees to repatriate displaced persons
🔹 B. Created a military force to protect them
🔹 C. Ignored refugee issues
🔹 D. Provided financial loans to host countries

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League set up a High Commission for Refugees, coordinating efforts to locate, transport, and reunite millions of displaced civilians and ex‑POWs with their families, thereby addressing one of the most pressing humanitarian crises of the era.

Q14. In what way did the League’s decision‑making structure limit its effectiveness?

🔹 A. It had a powerful executive council
🔹 B. It required unanimity for decisions
🔹 C. It could enforce sanctions without member consent
🔹 D. It had a secret police force

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Requiring unanimous agreement among member states for many resolutions made it extremely difficult to adopt decisive actions, as any single nation could veto measures, undermining the League’s ability to respond swiftly to crises.

Q15. Suppose a new international conflict broke out in 1934. How could the League have responded according to its charter?

🔹 A. Deploy troops directly
🔹 B. Convene an emergency session and recommend arbitration
🔹 C. Impose immediate military occupation
🔹 D. Remain neutral and do nothing

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League’s charter called for the rapid convening of an emergency assembly to discuss the dispute and to recommend arbitration or mediation, reflecting its emphasis on diplomatic resolution rather than direct military intervention.

Q16. Which country was NOT a founding member of the League of Nations?

🔹 A. United States
🔹 B. France
🔹 C. Belgium
🔹 D. Japan

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Although President Wilson championed the League, the United States never ratified the Covenant and therefore did not become a founding member, leaving European powers such as France, Belgium, and Japan to dominate the early membership.

Q17. What was the primary function of the League’s General Assembly?

🔹 A. To serve as a deliberative body where all member states could discuss and vote on issues
🔹 B. To command military operations
🔹 C. To enforce economic sanctions
🔹 D. To appoint the Secretary‑General

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The General Assembly acted as the League’s main forum, granting each member state an equal voice to debate, propose resolutions, and vote on matters ranging from peace‑keeping to social welfare, thereby embodying the principle of collective decision‑making.

Q18. How did the League’s failure influence the design of the United Nations?

🔹 A. It had no impact
🔹 B. It inspired the UN to avoid collective security
🔹 C. It led the UN to focus solely on economic development
🔹 D. It motivated architects to create a more robust organization with stronger enforcement mechanisms

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The League’s shortcomings highlighted the need for a stronger, more enforceable body; consequently, the United Nations incorporated a Security Council with permanent members and veto power, as well as mechanisms for peace‑keeping forces, to address the League’s previous weaknesses.

Q19. Compare the collective‑security mechanisms of the League and the UN Security Council.

🔹 A. Both have identical veto powers
🔹 B. League required unanimous action, UN Security Council allows veto by permanent members
🔹 C. UN does not use collective security
🔹 D. League’s mechanism was more enforceable

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League demanded unanimity, making decisive action rare, whereas the UN Security Council permits decisions by majority vote but grants veto rights to its five permanent members, balancing the need for swift action with the political realities of major powers.

Q20. If the League possessed full economic‑sanctions authority, could it have prevented World War II?

🔹 A. Yes, sanctions would have stopped aggression
🔹 B. No, sanctions are ineffective
🔹 C. Possibly, but only if major powers cooperated
🔹 D. It would have caused immediate war

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Economic sanctions can deter aggression only when all major powers enforce them uniformly; without such consensus, even a powerful sanctions regime would have been insufficient to stop the aggressive policies of Germany, Italy, and Japan that led to World War II.

Q21. Calculate the proportion of original members that left the League by 1939 if 58 founded and 20 withdrew.

🔹 A. \( \frac{20}{58} \approx 0.34\)
🔹 B. \( \frac{20}{58} \approx 0.28\)
🔹 C. \( \frac{20}{58} \approx 0.45\)
🔹 D. \( \frac{20}{58} \approx 0.20\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the 20 departing states by the 58 original members yields approximately \(0.34\) (or 34 %). This sizable fraction illustrates the League’s diminishing credibility as key nations exited in the lead‑up to World War II.

Q22. Why was embedding the League Covenant in peace treaties crucial for its legitimacy?

🔹 A. It made the League optional for states
🔹 B. It ensured the League existed only on paper
🔹 C. It bound signatory nations legally, giving it authority
🔹 D. It prevented any enforcement

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Including the Covenant in each peace treaty created a legally binding commitment for signatory states, transforming the League from a theoretical concept into an operative institution with recognized obligations and a mandate to act on the international stage.

Q23. From its first meeting in January 1920 to its final session in December 1939, how many years did the League of Nations operate?

🔹 A. 15 years
🔹 B. 19 years
🔹 C. 23 years
🔹 D. 30 years

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League functioned from January 1920 until December 1939, a span of just under twenty years—specifically nineteen full years—before its activities ceased and it was formally dissolved in the post‑war restructuring.
