What is Permanent Court of International Justice?

The Permanent Court of International Justice was the League’s judicial organ based in The Hague that settled legal disputes between nations and provided advisory opinions on international law, operating independently but funded by the League to promote peaceful resolution through legal means.

📝 All Permanent Court of International Justice MCQs

Q1. When was the League of Nations formally established?

A.10-Jan-20 ✅
B.10-Feb-20
C.10-Mar-20
D.10-Apr-20
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League was officially created on 10 January 1920, coinciding with the entry into force of the Treaty of Versailles. This date marks the moment the League began operating as an international body intended to promote peace and resolve disputes among nations.

Q2. In which city were the League of Nations headquarters located?

A.Paris
B.London
C.Geneva ✅
D.New York
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The League’s secretariat was based in Geneva, Switzerland. Geneva was chosen for its neutral status and central location in Europe, allowing the League to coordinate diplomatic activities and host meetings of its Assembly and Council throughout the interwar period.

Q3. Which South African statesman praised the League as a ‘great miracle’ in 1930?

A.Nelson Mandela
B.Desmond Tutu
C.Jan Smuts ✅
D.Oliver Tambo
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Jan Smuts, a prominent South African leader and former Prime Minister, expressed optimism about the League’s future in 1930, describing its achievements as one of history’s great miracles. His endorsement reflected the belief that the League could sustain international cooperation and peace.

Q4. Which of the following individuals was NOT involved in drafting the League’s Covenant?

A.Woodrow Wilson
B.Lord Robert Cecil
C.Leon Bourgeois
D.Otto von Bismarck ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Otto von Bismarck, the 19th‑century German chancellor, had died long before the League was conceived. The Covenant was drafted by a committee that included Woodrow Wilson, Lord Robert Cecil, Leon Bourgeois, Paul Hymans and Jan Smuts, among others.

Q5. The League’s Covenant was incorporated into the peace treaties after which major conflict?

A.World War I ✅
B.World War II
C.Crimean War
D.Franco‑Prussian War
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Covenant was embedded in the treaties that concluded World I, especially the Treaty of Versailles. By inserting the Covenant into these agreements, the League moved from a theoretical concept to an operational organization with legal standing among the victorious powers.

Q6. What was the first major test of the League’s authority?

A.Italian attack on Abyssinia
B.German annexation of Austria
C.Japanese invasion of Manchuria ✅
D.Spanish Civil War
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Japanese invasion of Manchuria in 1931 represented the League’s first serious challenge. Despite issuing a condemnation and urging withdrawal, the League could not compel Japan to reverse its actions, exposing weaknesses in collective security and enforcement mechanisms.

Q7. In which year was the League of Nations formally dissolved?

A.1945
B.1946 ✅
C.1950
D.1939
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: After the outbreak of World II, the League ceased to function and held its final meeting in 1939. Its formal dissolution occurred in April 1946, when its assets were transferred to the newly created United Nations, marking the end of the interwar organization.

Q8. Scenario: A member state is attacked by a non‑member aggressor. According to the League’s principle of collective security, what should the League members do?

A.Impose economic sanctions
B.Issue a diplomatic protest
C.Do nothing
D.Provide military assistance to the victim state ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Collective security required that all members act together to deter aggression. When a non‑member aggressor attacks a member, the League was expected to coordinate a collective response, which could include diplomatic pressure, economic sanctions, or, as a last resort, coordinated military assistance to restore peace.

Q9. Analytical: Why did the League fail to enforce its order against Italy in 1935 despite majority support for sanctions?

A.Key powers were unwilling to commit troops
B.The League lacked a clear voting procedure ✅
C.Italy withdrew voluntarily
D.Sanctions were never proposed
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Although many members voted for sanctions, the League’s decision‑making required unanimity for decisive action. Britain and France, fearing a wider conflict, were reluctant to enforce harsh measures, leading to a weak response that Italy ignored, illustrating the League’s dependence on political will rather than legal authority.

Q10. Which committee drafted the League’s Covenant?

A.International Committee including Cecil, Smuts, Bourgeois, Hymans and Wilson ✅
B.League Assembly
C.Council of the League
D.Permanent Court of International Justice
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Covenant was drafted by an international committee composed of Lord Robert Cecil, Jan Smuts, Léon Bourgeois, Paul Hymans and President Woodrow Wilson. Their collaborative work produced the legal framework that defined the League’s structure, functions and principles.

Q11. Scenario: If the League could impose compulsory economic sanctions during the Manchurian crisis, which outcome would be most likely?

A.Japan would withdraw from Manchuria
B.A prolonged war would ensue
C.The League would dissolve ✅
D.All members would leave the League
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: If the League possessed the authority to enforce mandatory sanctions, the diplomatic pressure on Japan would have intensified, potentially compelling a withdrawal. However, the likelihood of a prolonged conflict or League dissolution would increase, as member states might resist compulsory measures that threatened their own economic interests.

Q12. Analytical: How does the League’s refugee assistance compare with the modern UNHCR’s work?

A.League focused on repatriation, UNHCR on protection ✅
B.Both provide identical services
C.League offered no assistance, UNHCR created the concept
D.League dealt only with prisoners of war, UNHCR with all refugees
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League primarily facilitated the return of displaced persons and former prisoners of war to their homes, emphasizing repatriation. In contrast, the UNHCR adopts a broader mandate that includes protection, shelter, and legal assistance for refugees worldwide, reflecting an expanded humanitarian scope.

Q13. Which statement best describes the League’s role during the 1920s?

A.It resolved major wars
B.It solved minor disputes and provided humanitarian aid ✅
C.It enforced strict military alliances
D.It acted as a global trade organization
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: During the 1920s the League successfully mediated several minor international disputes and undertook substantial humanitarian work, such as assisting refugees and former prisoners of war. Its achievements were modest but demonstrated the potential of multilateral cooperation in maintaining peace and addressing social issues.

Q14. Scenario: Two member states claim overlapping sovereignty over a river. According to League procedures, what is the first step?

A.Immediate military action
B.Referral to the Permanent Court of International Justice
C.Mediation by the League’s Council ✅
D.Economic sanctions
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The League’s standard process began with mediation, wherein the Council would appoint a neutral party to facilitate dialogue between the disputing states. Only if mediation failed would the dispute be escalated to arbitration or judicial settlement, ensuring a peaceful, step‑by‑step approach.

Q15. Which principle was central to the League’s aim of maintaining peace?

A.Collective security ✅
B.Balance of power
C.Deterrence through nuclear weapons
D.Unilateral disarmament
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Collective security was the cornerstone of the League’s strategy: an attack on one member was considered an attack on all, obligating the remaining members to act together—politically, economically, or militarily—to restore peace and deter aggression.

Q16. Analytical: Assess the impact of the United States’ absence from the League on its effectiveness.

A.It had no impact
B.It weakened legitimacy and limited resources ✅
C.It improved decision‑making speed
D.It caused immediate dissolution
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The United States, despite President Wilson’s advocacy, never ratified the Covenant, depriving the League of a major power, financial contributions, and political influence. This absence undermined the League’s legitimacy, constrained its capacity to enforce decisions, and contributed to its eventual failure.

Q17. In 1930 Jan Smuts described the League as a ‘great miracle.’ To what did he most likely refer?

A.Its ability to prevent wars
B.Its humanitarian work for refugees and POWs ✅
C.Its economic achievements
D.Its diplomatic successes in trade
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Smuts highlighted the League’s remarkable humanitarian achievements, especially its assistance to thousands of refugees and former prisoners of war. By emphasizing the organization’s capacity to alleviate human suffering, he underscored a tangible benefit that seemed miraculous in the post‑war context.

Q18. Scenario: If a permanent judicial body like the PCIJ had compelled Italy to appear before it before the Abyssinia invasion, how might the crisis have differed?

A.Italy would have been forced to withdraw peacefully ✅
B.The League would have dissolved
C.Italy would have proceeded unchanged
D.The crisis would have escalated into a world war
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A judicial hearing could have provided a legal avenue for dispute resolution, increasing international pressure on Italy. By obliging Italy to justify its actions before a respected court, the League might have deterred the invasion or at least delayed it, potentially preserving peace in the short term.

Q19. Analytical: What is the legal significance of the League Covenant’s Article X (collective security) for later UN Charter provisions?

A.It was identical to UN Article 51
B.It influenced the development of UN Chapter VII on collective action ✅
C.It was discarded entirely
D.It became the basis for UN peacekeeping only
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Article X introduced the concept that an attack on one member constitutes a threat to all, laying groundwork for the United Nations’ Chapter VII, which empowers the Security Council to take collective measures—including sanctions and military action—to maintain or restore international peace and security.

Q20. Which limitation of the League was the PCIJ designed to address?

A.Lack of a permanent enforcement army
B.Absence of a judicial mechanism to resolve treaty disputes ✅
C.Inability to hold elections
D.No provision for economic cooperation
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League suffered from an inability to adjudicate legal disputes between members. The establishment of the Permanent Court of International Justice aimed to fill this gap by providing a permanent judicial forum where states could bring cases concerning treaty violations, thereby strengthening the rule of law in international relations.

Q21. Scenario: A member state brings a breach case before the PCIJ; the offending state refuses to comply with the judgment. According to League principles, what recourse exists?

A.Immediate military intervention
B.Expulsion from the League
C.Referral to the League Assembly for possible sanctions ✅
D.No further action possible
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: If a state ignored a PCIJ judgment, the League could refer the matter to its Assembly, which might adopt sanctions or other measures against the non‑compliant state. This process reflected the League’s reliance on collective political pressure rather than direct enforcement mechanisms.

Q22. Evaluate the statement: ‘The League’s failure was primarily due to its inability to enforce decisions, not because of its structural design.’

A.Agree
B.Disagree
C.Partially agree ✅
D.Not sure
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: While enforcement shortcomings were central, the League’s structural design—such as unanimity requirements and the absence of major powers—also hampered decisive action. Therefore, the statement is only partially accurate: both enforcement limitations and institutional flaws contributed to the League’s overall ineffectiveness.

Q23. The League’s dissolution in 1946 led to the creation of which successor organization that incorporated many of its principles?

A.United Nations ✅
B.NATO
C.OECD
D.European Union
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Following the League’s formal termination in 1946, its functions, assets, and many of its ideals were transferred to the United Nations. The UN adopted a similar commitment to collective security, dispute resolution, and humanitarian assistance, building on the League’s legacy while addressing its previous shortcomings.
