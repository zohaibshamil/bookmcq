📝 Weaknesses of League of Nations Covenant

📖 From World History: Modern • 3. The League of Nations • 23 questions available

What is Weaknesses of League of Nations Covenant?

Key weaknesses in the Covenant included the requirement for unanimous voting which allowed any single nation to veto action, ambiguous language regarding mandatory sanctions, absence of provisions for military enforcement, and loopholes that permitted nations to pursue war if they waited three months after arbitration.

📝 All Weaknesses of League of Nations Covenant MCQs

Q1. When did the League of Nations formally come into existence?

A. \(10\) January \(1920\) ✅
B. \(28\) June \(1919\)
C. \(1\) September \(1919\)
D. \(15\) March \(1921\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League was officially inaugurated on \(10\) January \(1920\), the same day the Treaty of Versailles entered into force, marking the start of an international body intended to prevent future wars through collective security and diplomatic dialogue.

Q2. Where were the League of Nations headquarters located?

A. Geneva, Switzerland ✅
B. Paris, France
C. London, United Kingdom
D. New York, USA

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League chose Geneva, Switzerland, as its seat because the neutral city offered a central location in Europe, facilitating access for member nations and reinforcing the League’s commitment to impartiality and international cooperation.

Q3. One of the League’s main aims was to maintain peace through what mechanism?

A. Collective security ✅
B. Free trade agreements
C. Cultural exchange programs
D. Scientific collaboration

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Collective security meant that an attack on any member would be treated as an attack on all, prompting a coordinated response designed to deter aggression and preserve global stability, a core principle embedded in the Covenant.

Q4. Which South African statesman remarked that the League was a \great miracle of history\?

A. Jan Smuts ✅
B. Woodrow Wilson
C. Lloyd George
D. Robert Cecil

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Jan Smuts, a prominent South African leader, expressed optimism about the League in the early 1930s, highlighting the hope that the organization could become a lasting instrument for peace despite emerging geopolitical tensions.

Q5. Which treaty came into operation on the same day as the League’s founding?

A. Versailles Treaty ✅
B. Treaty of Trianon
C. Treaty of Saint-Germain
D. Locarno Pact

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of Versailles, which formally ended World War I, entered into force on \(10\) January \(1920\), coinciding with the League’s establishment and providing the legal framework for the Covenant’s inclusion in the peace settlement.

Q6. Which two aggressions in the 1930s most directly challenged the League’s authority?

A. Japanese invasion of Manchuria and Italian attack on Abyssinia ✅
B. German remilitarisation of the Rhineland and Soviet annexation of Finland
C. Spanish Civil War and Soviet invasion of Poland
D. British rearmament and French colonial expansion

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League faced its toughest tests when Japan seized Manchuria in \(1931\) and Italy invaded Abyssinia in \(1935\); both actions defied League resolutions and exposed the organization’s inability to enforce collective security.

Q7. In what year was the League of Nations officially dissolved?

A. 1946 ✅
B. 1939
C. 1950
D. 1945

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

After World War II rendered the League obsolete, its final meeting was held in April \(1946\) and the organization was formally terminated, paving the way for the United Nations to assume its responsibilities.

Q8. Which individuals were part of the committee that drafted the League Covenant?

A. Robert Cecil, Jan Smuts, Leon Bourgeois, Paul Hymans
B. Winston Churchill, Georges Clemenceau, David Lloyd George, Woodrow Wilson ✅
C. Franklin D. Roosevelt, Benito Mussolini, Hideki Tojo, Joseph Stalin
D. Charles de Gaulle, Jawaharlal Nehru, Mao Zedong, Ho Chi Minh

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Covenant was crafted by a multinational committee that included Britain’s Robert Cecil, South Africa’s Jan Smuts, France’s Léon Bourgeois, and Belgium’s Paul Hymans, reflecting a broad consensus on the structure of an international peace‑keeping body.

Q9. Which British statesman referred to the League as one of Britain’s war aims?

A. Lloyd George ✅
B. Neville Chamberlain
C. Winston Churchill
D. Anthony Eden

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Prime Minister David Lloyd George championed the League as a key objective of Britain’s wartime diplomacy, believing that a permanent international organization could transform the brutal conflict into a foundation for lasting peace.

Q10. What does the principle of collective security entail?

A. All members agree to use force only after a UN vote
B. If one member is attacked, all other members act together to stop the aggressor
C. Each nation maintains its own independent defense policy
D. Members must contribute equal financial resources to a common fund ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Collective security obliges every League member to treat an aggression against one as an aggression against all, thereby mobilising diplomatic, economic, or military measures collectively to deter or repel the offending state.

Q11. Why was the League unable to stop the Japanese invasion of Manchuria?

A. Japan withdrew voluntarily after sanctions
B. The League lacked a standing army and unanimous voting blocked decisive action
C. Japan was a permanent member with veto power ✅
D. The League imposed effective economic sanctions that Japan ignored

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The League’s decision‑making required unanimity, and because Japan’s aggression was not universally condemned, the organization could not muster the coordinated pressure needed to compel withdrawal, demonstrating a structural weakness.

Q12. How did the League assist refugees and former prisoners of war after World War I?

A. By repatriating them to their home countries ✅
B. By providing them with military training
C. By offering them scholarships in Geneva
D. By relocating them to colonies in Africa

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The League’s humanitarian agencies organized large‑scale repatriation efforts, tracking displaced persons and arranging transport back to their nations, which helped alleviate the massive post‑war humanitarian crisis.

Q13. If a member state attacked another in \(1932\), which response could the League legally undertake under its Covenant?

A. Deploy a multinational peace‑keeping army
B. Impose economic sanctions on the aggressor ✅
C. Offer a joint scientific research grant
D. Do nothing and wait for a UN decision

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Covenant authorized economic sanctions as a non‑military tool to coerce compliance, allowing the League to pressure the offending nation without resorting to armed intervention, though political will often limited its effectiveness.

Q14. How did the League’s performance in the 1920s compare with its effectiveness in the 1930s?

A. It was equally effective in both decades

C. Its influence grew steadily throughout the 1930s

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

During the 1920s the League resolved several minor disputes and provided humanitarian aid, but the 1930s brought major challenges—Manchuria, Abyssinia, and the Czechoslovak crisis—where the organization’s lack of enforcement mechanisms led to a marked decline in credibility.

Q15. Which article of the Covenant required its inclusion in every separate peace treaty?

A. Article X
B. Article V
C. Article XIII ✅
D. Article II

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Article XIII stipulated that the Covenant be incorporated into each post‑war treaty, ensuring that the League’s legal framework became binding on all signatory states rather than remaining a mere proposal.

Q16. What was the impact of the League’s failure to intervene in the Czechoslovakia crisis?

A. It strengthened the League’s authority
B. It led to immediate disarmament of Germany
C. It demonstrated the League’s inability to curb expansionist policies, encouraging further aggression
D. It caused the League to dissolve in 1935 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The League’s inaction signalled to revisionist powers that territorial expansion could proceed unchecked, emboldening Germany and undermining confidence in collective security, which contributed directly to the outbreak of World War II.

Q17. What role did Woodrow Wilson play in the adoption of the League Covenant?

A. He opposed its inclusion in the peace treaties ✅
B. He insisted the Covenant be embedded in all peace settlements
C. He drafted the entire Covenant alone
D. He withdrew the United States from the League

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Wilson championed the Covenant as the final point of his Fourteen Points, pushing for its incorporation into each treaty to guarantee the League’s existence, though his own country never joined, limiting its ultimate influence.

Q18. If five of ten League members support an action, what fraction of total support does that represent?

A. \(0.2\)
B. \(0.5\) ✅
C. \(0.7\)
D. \(0.9\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Five out of ten members equate to a support level of \( \frac{5}{10}=0.5\) or fifty percent, which falls short of the two‑thirds majority often required for decisive League measures, illustrating the difficulty of achieving consensus.

Q19. Why did collective security fail when Italy attacked Abyssinia in \(1935\)?

A. Italy was a permanent League member with veto rights ✅
B. The Covenant lacked clauses allowing economic sanctions
C. Member states were unwilling to risk war, and unanimity blocked enforcement
D. The League successfully imposed a naval blockade, which Italy ignored

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Italy’s aggression exposed the Covenant’s reliance on unanimous consent; because several members were reluctant to confront Italy militarily, the League could not marshal a collective response, revealing a fundamental flaw in the collective‑security model.

Q20. If the League had 58 members and required a two‑thirds majority to pass a resolution, how many votes were needed?

A. 36
B. 38
C. 39 ✅
D. 40

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Two‑thirds of 58 equals \( \frac{2}{3}\times58 = 38.\overline{6}\); rounding up to the next whole number gives 39 votes, the minimum required to achieve the super‑majority necessary for binding action.

Q21. How did the United States’ non‑membership affect the League’s authority?

A. It had no impact because the League was purely European
B. It weakened the League’s legitimacy and limited its capacity to enforce decisions
C. It strengthened the League by avoiding US dominance
D. It caused the League to focus exclusively on economic matters ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The absence of the United States, the world’s foremost economic and military power, deprived the League of crucial resources and credibility, making it harder to compel major powers to obey its resolutions and diminishing its global standing.

Q22. Which principle was violated when Japan and Italy ignored League orders?

A. Sovereignty
B. Self‑determination
C. Collective security ✅
D. Non‑intervention

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

By flouting the League’s directives, both aggressors breached the collective‑security principle embedded in the Covenant, which required all members to respect the decisions made by the organization to preserve international peace.

Q23. Which revised Covenant clause could have potentially prevented the outbreak of World War II?

A. A mandatory arbitration clause with automatic sanctions for non‑compliance ✅
B. A provision allowing any member to unilaterally declare war on aggressors
C. A clause permitting the League to bypass unanimity and act by simple majority
D. A requirement that all treaties be ratified by the League before taking effect

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A mandatory arbitration clause paired with automatic sanctions would have created a clear, enforceable pathway for resolving disputes, reducing the chance that aggressive states could act with impunity and thereby strengthening the League’s preventive capacity.
