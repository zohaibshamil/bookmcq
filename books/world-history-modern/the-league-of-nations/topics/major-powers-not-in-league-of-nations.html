What is Major powers not in League of Nations?

📝 All Major powers not in League of Nations MCQs

Q1. When did the League of Nations formally come into existence?

A.10-Jan-20 ✅
B.28-Jun-19
C.11-Nov-18
D.04-Jul-22
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League was officially established on 10 January 1920, the same day the Treaty of Versailles entered into force. This date marks the formal beginning of the organization’s attempts to promote collective security and prevent future conflicts, even though its effectiveness would later be questioned.

Q2. Which city hosted the League of Nations headquarters?

A.London
B.Geneva ✅
C.Paris
D.New York
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Geneva, Switzerland, was chosen as the seat of the League of Nations because of its neutral status and central location in Europe. The city provided a diplomatic environment conducive to international cooperation, and the League’s offices there became a symbol of the post‑World War I peace‑building effort.

Q3. Who was the South African statesman that praised the League in 1930?

A.Nelson Mandela
B.Desmond Tutu
C.Jan Smuts ✅
D.Oliver Tambo
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Jan Smuts, a prominent South African leader, expressed optimism about the League’s future in 1930, calling its work a “great miracle of history.” His endorsement reflected the broader international hope that the League could maintain peace, despite growing challenges from aggressive powers.

Q4. Which treaty came into operation on the same day as the League's founding?

A.Treaty of Westphalia
B.Treaty of Paris
C.Versailles Treaty ✅
D.Treaty of Brest-Litovsk
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Treaty of Versailles, which officially ended World War I, entered into force on 10 January 1920—the same day the League of Nations was created. This simultaneity linked the peace settlement with the new international body designed to prevent future wars, illustrating the intertwined nature of diplomatic and institutional reforms.

Q5. Which of the following was NOT a primary aim of the League?

A.Maintain peace
B.Promote trade
C.Colonize territories ✅
D.Refugee assistance
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The League of Nations was established to maintain peace, promote economic and social welfare, and assist refugees and former prisoners of war. Colonization was never part of its mandate; instead, the organization sought to resolve disputes through diplomacy and collective security, distinguishing it from imperial ambitions.

Q6. Which country invaded Manchuria in 1931?

A.Italy
B.Soviet Union
C.Japan ✅
D.Germany
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: In 1931, the Imperial Japanese Army staged the Mukden Incident as a pretext to occupy Manchuria, a region in northeastern China. This aggression challenged the League’s authority early in its existence and exposed the organization’s limited capacity to enforce its own resolutions against a major power.

Q7. In which year was the League dissolved?

A.1945
B.1946 ✅
C.1939
D.1950
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League of Nations formally ceased to exist in April 1946, after having held its final meeting in 1939. Its dissolution paved the way for the creation of the United Nations, which sought to address many of the League’s shortcomings while continuing the goal of preventing another world war.

Q8. If a member state attacked another in 1925, what collective action was the League expected to take?

A.Diplomatic mediation
B.No action
C.Economic sanctions
D.Military alliance ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The League’s principle of collective security mandated that member nations respond jointly to aggression, often through coordinated military measures or allied forces. While diplomatic efforts were also used, the expectation was that a unified military alliance would deter further hostilities and uphold the organization’s peace‑keeping purpose.

Q9. Given that the League's Covenant was included in each separate peace treaty, what legal effect did this have?

A.It made League obligations binding ✅
B.It made League optional
C.It limited League to economic matters
D.It excluded non-European states
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Embedding the Covenant within each peace treaty gave the League a solid legal foundation, turning its principles from mere proposals into enforceable commitments for signatory nations. This integration ensured that the League’s rules were recognized as part of the formal post‑war settlement, increasing its legitimacy.

Q10. Imagine the League had successfully forced Italy to withdraw from Abyssinia in 1935. Which outcome would most likely have occurred?

A.Strengthened League credibility ✅
B.Accelerated WWII
C.Collapse of League
D.Increased Japanese expansion
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A successful Italian withdrawal would have demonstrated the League’s capacity to enforce collective security, bolstering its authority and encouraging compliance by other states. Such a precedent could have deterred future aggression, thereby reinforcing the organization’s role as a credible arbiter of international disputes.

Q11. Which statement best reflects why the League failed to prevent WWII?

A.Lack of enforcement mechanisms ✅
B.Overpowered by US
C.No member states
D.Economic depression
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League’s inability to stop the rise of militaristic regimes stemmed largely from its lack of concrete enforcement tools, such as a standing army or mandatory sanctions. Without these mechanisms, the organization could issue condemnations but could not compel aggressive powers to reverse their actions, leading to its eventual impotence.

Q12. During the 1930s, the League's authority was challenged by Japan and Italy. What common factor contributed to the League's inability to act?

A.Both aggressors were major powers ✅
B.Both had veto power
C.Both were members of the League
D.Both withdrew from the League
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Japan and Italy were both significant military powers whose strategic importance made other members reluctant to impose harsh penalties. Their status as influential nations limited the League’s willingness to enforce its resolutions, revealing how the balance of power could undermine collective security.

Q13. If the League had included the United States as a member, how might its capacity to enforce collective security have changed?

A.Increased resources and legitimacy ✅
B.Decreased due to isolationism
C.No change
D.Made it weaker
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: U.S. membership would have brought considerable economic, military, and diplomatic resources, enhancing the League’s ability to impose sanctions or deploy forces. Moreover, American participation would have added global legitimacy, potentially encouraging broader cooperation and deterring aggressors who might otherwise ignore the League’s directives.

Q14. Which principle underpinned the League's collective security approach?

A.Mutual defense ✅
B.Territorial expansion
C.Economic competition
D.Cultural exchange
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Mutual defense, or collective security, was the cornerstone of the League’s strategy: an attack on one member was considered an attack on all, obligating members to respond together. This principle aimed to create a deterrent effect, discouraging potential aggressors through the promise of unified opposition.

Q15. A dispute arises between Czechoslovakia and Germany in 1938. If the League had been consulted, which action would align with its charter?

A.Direct military intervention
B.Supporting Germany
C.Ignoring the dispute
D.Mediation and possible sanctions ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The League’s charter emphasized peaceful dispute resolution, so the appropriate response would have been mediation coupled with the threat of sanctions if diplomatic efforts failed. Direct military action or favoritism contradicted its principles, whereas a mediated settlement represented the organization’s intended method of conflict management.

Q16. Which of the following individuals was NOT among the original architects of the League?

A.Woodrow Wilson
B.Lord Robert Cecil
C.Jan Smuts
D.Adolf Hitler ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Adolf Hitler, who rose to power in Germany in the early 1930s, had no involvement in drafting the League’s founding documents. The organization’s conceptual design was shaped by leaders such as Wilson, Cecil, and Smuts, who each contributed proposals for an international body to maintain peace.

Q17. The League's Covenant emphasized collective security. Which later international organization adopted a similar principle?

A.NATO
B.European Union
C.WTO
D.United Nations ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The United Nations, established after World War II, incorporated collective security into its Charter, mirroring the League’s core idea that an attack on one member constitutes an attack on all. This principle remains central to the UN’s peace‑keeping and security operations.

Q18. Analyze why the League's response to the Japanese invasion of Manchuria failed despite issuing the Lytton Report. Which factor was most decisive?

A.Lack of unanimity among members
B.Economic dependence on Japan
C.Absence of armed forces
D.All of the above ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: All three factors intertwined to weaken the League’s response: member states could not reach a unanimous decision, many relied economically on Japanese trade, and the League lacked its own military force to enforce recommendations. This combination rendered the Lytton Report ineffective and exposed systemic vulnerabilities.

Q19. Considering the League's structure, evaluate how the requirement for unanimous decisions in the Council affected its ability to act during crises.

A.It caused delays ✅
B.It ensured consensus
C.It prevented bias
D.It made decisions easier
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Unanimity meant that any single member could veto action, leading to protracted negotiations and inaction during urgent crises. While intended to protect minority interests, this rule often stalled decisive measures, undermining the League’s capacity to respond swiftly to aggression and weakening its overall credibility.

Q20. Compare the League's handling of the refugee crisis after WWI with its later inability to prevent war. What does this contrast reveal about its strengths and weaknesses?

A.Strength in humanitarian aid but weak in security ✅
B.Strength in military, weak in diplomacy
C.No contrast
D.It excelled in both areas
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League succeeded in coordinating relief for refugees and displaced persons, demonstrating effective humanitarian collaboration. However, its lack of enforcement mechanisms and reliance on diplomatic persuasion limited its ability to deter military aggression, highlighting a disparity between its social achievements and security shortcomings.

Q21. If the League had possessed its own standing army, how might the outcome of the Abyssinia crisis differ? Assess the plausibility of this scenario.

A.It could have deterred Italy ✅
B.It would have escalated conflict
C.It would have had no effect
D.It would have caused internal dissent
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A permanent League force could have signaled a credible threat of force, possibly persuading Italy to withdraw from Abyssinia to avoid confrontation. While not guaranteeing peace, the presence of an armed body would have altered the cost‑benefit analysis for aggressors, making unilateral expansion riskier.

Q22. Critically assess the statement that the League was a 'complete failure' with respect to preventing war, considering its achievements in other domains.

A.Partially true ✅
B.Entirely false
C.Neither true nor false
D.Not applicable
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Labeling the League a total failure ignores its notable humanitarian successes, such as refugee assistance and health initiatives. However, its primary mission of preventing war was largely unmet, especially in the 1930s. Therefore, the assessment is partially true: it fell short in security but succeeded in other areas.

Q23. Given the timeline of events, calculate the number of years the League operated before its dissolution.

A.\(26\) years ✅
B.\(25\) years
C.\(27\) years
D.\(28\) years
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League was founded on 10 January 1920 and formally dissolved in April 1946. Subtracting 1920 from 1946 yields \(26\) years of operation, during which the organization attempted to maintain peace, manage humanitarian crises, and lay groundwork for later international institutions.
