What is Was League of Nations a failure or success?

The League is generally considered a failure in its primary goal of preventing World War II due to structural weaknesses and lack of enforcement power, yet it succeeded as a pioneering experiment in international governance that laid the essential groundwork for the modern United Nations.

Q1. When was the League of Nations formally created?

A.\(9\) January \(1920\)
B.\(10\) January \(1920\) ✅
C.\(11\) January \(1920\)
D.\(12\) January \(1920\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League was officially established on \(10\) January \(1920\), the same day the Versailles Treaty entered into force, providing a legal basis for its operations and signaling a new era of collective diplomacy.

Q2. Where were the League’s headquarters located?

A.Paris, France ✅
B.Geneva, Switzerland
C.London, United Kingdom
D.New York, USA
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The League’s permanent secretariat was based in Geneva, Switzerland, a neutral location that facilitated international cooperation and allowed the organization to function without the political pressures associated with a capital city of a major power.

Q3. Which statesman praised the League as a “great miracle of history”?

A.Winston Woods
B.Jan Smuts ✅
C.David Lloyd George
D.Woodrow Wilson
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: South African leader Jan Smuts expressed optimism in \(1930\), describing the League’s achievements as one of history’s greatest miracles, reflecting contemporary confidence in the organization’s potential to maintain peace.

Q4. Which of the following individuals was NOT directly involved in drafting the League Covenant?

A.Lord Robert Cecil
B.Leon Bourgeois
C.Paul Hymans
D.Franz Fischer ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Cecil, Bourgeois, and Hymans all participated in the inter‑Allied committee that produced the Covenant; Franz Fischer, a German diplomat, was not part of that drafting process, highlighting the limited German involvement in the League’s foundation.

Q5. If a member state attacked another, what primary mechanism did the League intend to employ?

A.Economic sanctions only
B.Collective military action ✅
C.Diplomatic mediation
D.Withdrawal from the League
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The League’s core principle of collective security called for member nations to jointly respond—often through a combination of diplomatic pressure and, if necessary, coordinated military measures—to deter aggression and preserve peace.

Q6. Why did the League fail to stop Japan’s invasion of Manchuria in \(1931\)?

A.Japan withdrew voluntarily after sanctions
B.The League lacked enforcement troops
C.Member states imposed effective economic embargoes ✅
D.The League’s decision was vetoed by the United Nations
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Although the League condemned the aggression, it could only issue moral censure; without an enforcement arm or unified economic pressure, Japan ignored the resolutions, exposing a critical weakness in the League’s authority.

Q7. How did the League’s role differ between the 1920s and the 1930s?

A.It became more active in the 1930s
B.It shifted from economic aid to military enforcement
C.It moved from solving minor disputes to being largely ineffective
D.It dissolved after the 1920s ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: During the 1920s the League successfully mediated several minor conflicts and provided humanitarian assistance; however, the 1930s saw rising aggression, and the League’s inability to enforce its decisions led to a perception of ineffectiveness.

Q8. Suppose the League possessed a binding economic‑sanction clause; which outcome is most plausible?

A.Japan would have withdrawn from Manchuria ✅
B.Italy would have postponed the Abyssinia campaign
C.Both aggressors would have faced immediate trade embargoes
D.Sanctions would have had no effect on either aggressor
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A binding sanction mechanism could have compelled Japan to reconsider its costs, as a coordinated trade embargo among major powers would have strained its economy, potentially forcing a withdrawal and demonstrating the deterrent power of collective economic action.

Q9. What impact did including the League Covenant in each peace treaty have?

A.It delayed the League’s formation

D.It caused immediate disbandment after World War I
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: By embedding the Covenant within the individual peace treaties, the League gained a concrete legal foundation, turning a theoretical concept into an operative body and preventing it from remaining merely a proposal on paper.

Q10. If collective security had been invoked during the 1938 Sudeten crisis, which scenario might have unfolded?

Q21. If the League possessed an enforcement army of \(n\) troops, and the probability of war drops according to \(P = 1 - \frac{n}{10}\), what would be the war probability with \(n = 4\)?

Q22. What direct consequence resulted from the League’s inaction during Germany’s disputes with Czechoslovakia and Poland?

B.Rise of the United Nations

D.Economic boom in Europe
