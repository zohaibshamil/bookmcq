What is Problems facing Latin American countries?

Latin American nations have historically struggled with severe economic inequality, political instability caused by military dictatorships and corruption, and a heavy dependence on exporting raw materials which makes their economies vulnerable to global price fluctuations.

Q1. Which commodity accounted for about 80% of Bolivia's export revenue in the 1950s?

A. Tin ✅
B. Copper
C. Coffee
D. Soybeans

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Tin was the dominant export for Bolivia during the 1950s, providing roughly four‑fifths of the nation's earnings. The other options were important for other countries but not for Bolivia at that time. Recognizing the reliance on a single commodity helps explain why a price drop would have caused a fiscal crisis, illustrating the vulnerability of export‑dependent economies.

Q2. In which country are the informal settlements called \favelas\ located?

A. Brazil ✅
B. Argentina
C. Peru
D. Chile

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The term favelas refers specifically to the improvised shanty towns surrounding Brazil's major cities. While Argentina, Peru, and Chile also have informal settlements, they are not called favelas. Knowing the geographic label clarifies regional urban challenges and shows how cultural terminology can identify particular housing problems in Latin America.

Q3. Why did rapid population growth after World War II worsen urban poverty in Latin America?

A. It increased agricultural output, reducing food prices.
B. It caused a surge of migrants to cities where jobs were scarce, expanding slums. ✅
C. It led to higher tax revenues that funded social programs.
D. It encouraged emigration to Europe, relieving domestic pressures.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The population boom, driven by medical advances and limited birth‑control, produced many people seeking work in cities. Urban labor markets could not absorb them, so informal settlements grew, lacking basic services. This scenario explains why poverty deepened despite more people overall, linking demographic trends to the expansion of shanty towns and the widening wealth gap.

Q4. If a Latin American nation depends on a single export commodity and the world price falls 30%, which impact is most likely?

A. Higher inflation
B. Increased unemployment
C. Widened trade‑deficit ✅
D. Boosted foreign investment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A sharp price decline reduces export earnings, shrinking foreign exchange earnings and worsening the trade balance. Inflation and unemployment may follow later, but the immediate macro‑economic effect is a larger deficit because the country still imports similar amounts while earning less. This illustrates the risk of over‑reliance on one commodity and why diversification is crucial.

Q5. What was a primary reason foreign investment often limited economic benefits for host Latin American nations?

A. Profits were repatriated to investors' home countries ✅
B. It introduced advanced technology
C. It created many local jobs
D. It financed public infrastructure

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When foreign firms earned profits, they typically transferred them abroad rather than reinvesting locally, limiting capital accumulation within the host country. Although technology and jobs sometimes followed, the dominant effect was capital outflow, reducing the net benefit of foreign presence. Understanding this dynamic helps explain why external investment did not always spur domestic development.

Q6. A government proposes land reform to break up large estates. Which group is most likely to use the military to block the reform?

A. Urban workers
B. Foreign investors
C. Landowners ✅
D. Peasants

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Large estate owners possess significant economic and political power and have historically allied with the military to protect their interests. They view land redistribution as a direct threat to their wealth, so they are prone to enlist armed forces to suppress reformist policies. This pattern reflects how elite groups can leverage the army to maintain privileged positions.

Q7. How did the shortage of industrial capital affect Latin American economies compared with developed nations?

A. It led to higher productivity
B. It caused lower export diversification ✅
C. It spurred greater technological innovation
D. It increased domestic consumption

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Insufficient capital limited the ability to develop varied industries, so Latin American economies relied heavily on a few primary commodities for export. In contrast, developed nations possessed diversified manufacturing bases. This lack of diversification made them vulnerable to commodity price swings, demonstrating how capital constraints shape export structures and economic resilience.

Q8. Assume Country X reduces its reliance on copper exports by 40% and invests that capital in textile manufacturing. Which short‑term macroeconomic effect is most plausible?

A. Rise in unemployment
B. Increase in trade‑balance deficit
C. Rise in GDP ✅
D. Depreciation of the currency

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Redirecting resources into a new manufacturing sector typically raises overall output, boosting gross domestic product even if the transition is still early. While employment may lag and trade balances could shift, the immediate measurable effect is higher GDP due to added value from textiles. This scenario shows how diversification can stimulate economic growth despite short‑term adjustments.

Q9. Explain why the Roman Catholic Church's opposition to birth control contributed to persistent poverty in rural Latin America.

A. It increased labor supply, lowering wages
B. It reduced agricultural output
C. It encouraged migration to cities ✅
D. It promoted education

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

By discouraging birth control, the Church helped maintain large families, creating a surplus of labor in rural areas where jobs were scarce. This oversupply kept wages low and forced many to move to urban centers, where they encountered inadequate housing and services, perpetuating poverty cycles. The link highlights how demographic policies influence economic conditions.

Q10. If a military coup overturns a reformist government, what is the most likely immediate impact on foreign direct investment?

A. Increase in investment
B. No change in investment levels
C. Decrease in investment ✅
D. Shift toward domestic investors

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A coup creates political instability, raising perceived risk for foreign investors. They often pause or withdraw capital to avoid potential losses, resulting in an immediate decline in foreign direct investment. While long‑term effects depend on subsequent policies, the short‑term reaction typically reflects caution, illustrating how governance disruptions can deter external economic participation.

Q11. Country Y experiences a 25% rise in urban population over a decade. Which policy would most effectively reduce growth of informal settlements?

A. Building high‑rise apartments ✅
B. Expanding agricultural subsidies
C. Increasing import tariffs
D. Reducing education spending

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Providing affordable, high‑density housing directly addresses the shortage of legal dwellings that drives the expansion of shanty towns. Agricultural subsidies and tariffs influence rural economies but do not solve urban housing deficits, and cutting education spending would exacerbate poverty. Hence, constructing high‑rise apartments offers the most targeted solution to curb informal settlement growth.

Q12. Which Latin American country experienced a military coup in 1973 that ended a democratically elected government?

A. Chile ✅
B. Brazil
C. Argentina
D. Peru

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In 1973, Chile's elected president was overthrown by a military junta, marking a pivotal moment in the region's political history. Brazil, Argentina, and Peru also faced coups, but those occurred in different years. Recognizing the specific year and country clarifies the timeline of authoritarian interventions in Latin America.

Q13. A development bank offers low‑interest loans to modernize agriculture, but large estate owners resist. Which economic theory best explains their behavior?

A. Rent‑seeking ✅
B. Comparative advantage
C. Tragedy of the commons
D. Keynesian multiplier

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Rent‑seeking describes how powerful groups use political influence to protect existing profits rather than pursue efficiency‑enhancing investments. Large estate owners may oppose reforms that threaten their rent extraction, even when modernization could benefit the broader economy. This theory captures why privileged actors block changes that could reduce their monopoly advantages.

Q14. Why did plentiful, cheap peasant labor discourage modernization of agriculture?

A. Because wages were high
B. Because profit margins were low ✅
C. Because mechanization costs were high
D. Because there was a lack of land

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When labor is abundant and inexpensive, farm owners can maintain production with minimal mechanization, leading to thin profit margins that do not justify capital‑intensive upgrades. High wages or lack of land are not the primary issues; the key factor is that cheap labor reduces incentives to invest in modern equipment, slowing agricultural advancement.

Q15. Suppose Country Z diversifies its exports from 90% tin to a mix of tin (30%), coffee (35%), and manufactured goods (35%). Using the export diversification index \(E = 1 - \sum p_i^2\), where \(p_i\) is the share of each export, what is the new index value?

A. 0.41
B. 0.66 ✅
C. 0.62
D. 0.7

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

First calculate each share squared: \(0.30^2 = 0.09\), \(0.35^2 = 0.1225\) (twice). Sum = 0.09 + 0.1225 + 0.1225 = 0.335. Then \(E = 1 - 0.335 = 0.665\), which rounds to 0.66. This higher index indicates greater diversification compared with the previous near‑monoculture, showing how spreading export shares improves economic resilience.
