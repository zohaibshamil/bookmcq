What is Mexico history and problems 20th century?

Twentieth-century Mexico was dominated by the Institutional Revolutionary Party which provided political stability and enacted land reforms but also fostered corruption and suppressed dissent, while the economy struggled with unequal development and dependence on the United States.

📝 All Mexico history and problems 20th century MCQs

Q1. In what year did Mexico gain independence from Spain?

A.1821 ✅
B.1810
C.1825
D.1830
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is 1821 because the Mexican War of Independence ended that year with the signing of the Treaty of Córdoba. 1810 marks the start of the revolt, not its conclusion, and the other dates are unrelated to independence. Understanding the timeline helps place later events, such as the loss of Texas, in context. This explanation clarifies the historical sequence for students.

Q2. Who was the long‑time dictator ruling Mexico from 1876 to 1910?

A.Porfirio Díaz ✅
B.Benito Juárez
C.Lázaro Cárdenas
D.Francisco I. Madero
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Porfirio Díaz held power for more than three decades, from 1876 until his resignation in 1911, making him the dominant figure of that era. Benito Juárez served earlier, Cárdenas later, and Madero briefly after Díaz. Recognizing Díaz’s rule is essential for grasping the causes of the Mexican Revolution and the political climate that preceded it.

Q3. Which Mexican president nationalized the oil industry in 1938?

A.Lázaro Cárdenas ✅
B.Victoriano Huerta
C.Alfonso Reyes
D.Adolfo López Mateos
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Lázaro Cárdenas issued the decree that placed petroleum reserves under state control in 1938, a move celebrated by many Mexicans. Huerta was a short‑lived ruler, Reyes never became president, and López Mateos served later. Knowing the president responsible for nationalization links directly to later diplomatic tensions with the United States and the United Kingdom.

Q4. In which year did Texas become a state of the United States?

A.1845 ✅
B.1836
C.1861
D.1870
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Texas was admitted to the Union in 1845 after being an independent republic following its 1836 revolt against Mexico. The other years correspond to different events: 1836 marks Texas’s independence, 1861 to the Civil War, and 1870 to Reconstruction. This date is pivotal for understanding the territorial losses Mexico suffered in the mid‑19th century.

Q5. Why did Mexico lose about one‑third of its territory after the war with the United States (1846‑1848)?

A.Because of the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo ✅
B.Because Mexico voluntarily ceded the land
C.Because of a internal civil war
D.Because the land was uninhabited
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, signed in 1848, required Mexico to surrender roughly one‑third of its land, including present‑day California and the Southwest, to the United States. The loss was not a voluntary giveaway or due to internal conflict, and the ceded areas were inhabited by diverse populations. This treaty reshaped North American borders and set the stage for future U.S.–Mexico relations.

Q6. What was the main economic effect of foreign ownership during the Porfiriato?

A.Profits largely left Mexico, limiting domestic benefit ✅
B.It created widespread wealth for Mexican peasants
C.It eliminated all foreign debt
D.It led to immediate industrialization for locals
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: During Porfirio Díaz’s rule, foreign investors dominated mining, oil, and railways, so most profits were repatriated abroad, leaving Mexican workers with little improvement in living standards. The era did not generate mass wealth for peasants, nor did it erase Mexico’s foreign debt, and while infrastructure grew, the benefits were unevenly distributed. This imbalance contributed to revolutionary discontent.

Q7. How did the fraudulent 1910 election lead to the Mexican Revolution?

A.It sparked outrage and mobilized opposition groups ✅
B.It resulted in immediate economic growth
C.It caused Mexico to join World War I
D.It led to the annexation of more territory
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The blatant fraud in the 1910 presidential election delegitimized Díaz’s regime, provoking widespread anger among reformers, peasants, and emerging middle‑class leaders. This anger ignited organized resistance that evolved into the Mexican Revolution. The election did not produce economic growth, involve World War I, nor expand Mexico’s borders; instead, it cataly ​the armed struggle for political change.

Q8. Why did Mexico join the Allied side in 1942?

A.German submarines sank Mexican oil tankers, prompting a declaration of war ✅
B.Mexico wanted to acquire more territory from the United States
C.Mexico sought to support the Axis powers economically
D.Mexico was forced by a UN resolution
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: German U‑boats attacked Mexican oil shipments, leading Mexico to declare war on the Axis in 1942 and align with the Allies. The decision was driven by direct aggression, not territorial ambitions, Axis support, or an external UN mandate. This alignment allowed Mexico to supply vital oil to the Allied war effort and enhanced its international standing.

Q9. What were the main factors that allowed Porfirio Díaz to maintain stability during his rule?

A.Foreign investment, infrastructure development, and political repression ✅
B.Universal suffrage, land redistribution, and free press
C.Complete isolation from foreign powers and agrarian reform
D.Abolition of the military and establishment of a welfare state
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Díaz’s regime relied on foreign capital to fund railroads, mines, and oil fields, while also investing in roads and education, which created a veneer of progress. At the same time, political dissent was suppressed through censorship and coercion, preventing organized opposition. The other options describe policies Díaz did not pursue, such as broad democratic reforms or welfare programs, which would have threatened his authority.

Q10. How did the loss of Texas in 1836 affect Mexico’s northern frontier?

A.It weakened Mexican control and encouraged further U.S. settlement ✅
B.It strengthened Mexico’s military presence in the region
C.It led to immediate economic prosperity for Mexico
D.It caused Mexico to annex Canada
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The independence of Texas removed a buffer zone, leaving Mexico’s northern border exposed and inviting American settlers to push further south. This loss did not bolster Mexico’s military, nor did it generate wealth or result in annexing Canada. The weakened frontier set the stage for later conflicts, including the Mexican‑American War.

Q11. Explain how the nationalization of oil in 1938 affected Mexico’s relationship with the United States and the United Kingdom.

A.It caused a boycott of Mexican goods, forcing Mexico to trade with Axis powers before a later agreement ✅
B.It strengthened diplomatic ties immediately
C.It had no impact on trade relations
D.It led to immediate military alliances with the United States
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When President Cárdenas expropriated foreign oil assets, the United States and United Kingdom responded with a boycott, cutting off Mexican oil exports. Consequently, Mexico turned to Germany and Italy for buyers until a compensation deal in 1941 restored U.S. trade. The other choices misrepresent the sequence: ties were strained, not strengthened, and no military alliance formed until after the compensation agreement.

Q12. What role did trade unions play in early 20th‑century Mexico, and why were they suppressed?

A.They sought better wages and conditions, threatening elite interests, leading to repression ✅
B.They supported the government and were rewarded
C.They were irrelevant to the economy
D.They were purely social clubs with no political impact
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Workers organized unions to demand higher pay, safer conditions, and legal protections, posing a challenge to landowners and industrialists who benefited from low labor costs. The government, fearing instability, responded with arrests and anti‑union laws. The unions did not align with the regime, nor were they insignificant; their activism directly confronted the power structure, prompting suppression.

Q13. How did the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) manage to stay in power for 70 years?

A.Through co‑optation of revolutionary ideals, patronage networks, and limited opposition ✅
B.By maintaining a strict one‑party dictatorship with no elections
C.By abolishing all forms of political participation
D.By focusing exclusively on foreign policy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The PRI blended the rhetoric of the Revolution with practical patronage, offering jobs and resources to supporters while controlling candidate selection and limiting genuine competition. This strategy differed from overt dictatorship because it preserved an electoral façade, unlike eliminating participation entirely or focusing solely on foreign affairs.

Q14. Why was the U.S. payment of $18 million after the Mexican‑American war significant?

A.It compensated Mexico for land loss and helped ease its national debt ✅
B.It funded the construction of the Panama Canal
C.It was a loan for Mexican industrialization
D.It was a donation for cultural exchange
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The United States paid $18 million as part of the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, providing monetary compensation for the territories it acquired. This sum helped Mexico settle some debts, though it did not fund the Panama Canal, industrial projects, or cultural programs. The payment illustrates how monetary settlements can mitigate the economic fallout of territorial loss.

Q15. What economic changes occurred during the Porfiriato that set the stage for the Mexican Revolution?

A.Industrial growth, foreign investment, and rising inequality ✅
B.Complete agrarian reform and land redistribution
C.Nationalization of all natural resources
D.Universal healthcare and education for all citizens
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Under Díaz, railways, mining, and oil expanded rapidly thanks to foreign capital, creating wealth for investors but widening the gap between elites and peasants. The absence of land reform, resource nationalization, or universal social services meant many Mexicans felt excluded, fueling revolutionary sentiment. The other options describe policies not implemented during the Porfiriato.

Q16. How did Mexico’s decision to sell oil to Nazi Germany affect its wartime diplomacy?

A.It strained relations with the United States until a compensation agreement was reached ✅
B.It strengthened its alliance with the United States
C.It led to immediate entry into the war on the Allied side
D.It caused the United Kingdom to invade Mexico
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Selling oil to Germany after the 1938 boycott antagonized the United States, prompting diplomatic tension that lasted until a 1941 compensation deal restored U.S. purchases. The trade did not improve U.S. ties, nor did it trigger immediate Allied entry or a British invasion. Understanding this episode shows how economic necessity can clash with geopolitical alliances.

Q17. Suppose a modern Mexican government wants to attract foreign investment without repeating Porfiriato’s inequality. Which policy would best balance foreign capital and domestic benefit?

A.Mandating joint‑venture agreements that require profit‑sharing with Mexican partners ✅
B.Offering tax‑free status to all foreign firms without regulation
C.Nationalizing all foreign‑owned enterprises immediately
D.Prohibiting any foreign investment in strategic sectors
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Requiring joint ventures forces foreign investors to partner with Mexican entities, ensuring that a portion of profits stays within the country and benefits local workers, unlike unrestricted tax breaks or outright nationalization, which either risk capital flight or provoke retaliation. A complete ban would deter needed capital, making joint‑venture mandates the most balanced approach.

Q18. If Texas had never become a U.S. state, how might Mexico’s economy differ today?

A.It would likely retain a larger tax base and have more diversified industrial development ✅
B.It would be poorer because Texas would have remained underdeveloped
C.Mexico would have become a federal republic like the United States
D.Mexico’s GDP would be unchanged because Texas’s economy is independent
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Had Texas remained Mexican, Mexico would have kept the oil‑rich Gulf Coast, the cattle industry, and later technology hubs, providing a broader tax base and encouraging diversified growth. The state’s development would not have been stagnant, and Mexico would not have adopted a U.S.‑style federal system. The other statements misinterpret the economic influence of Texas.

Q19. A policy analyst proposes a modern “oil nationalization 2.0” where profits are shared with local communities. Based on historical outcomes, which constitutional amendment would be most crucial to prevent foreign retaliation?

A.An amendment guaranteeing state ownership of natural resources while protecting foreign investors’ contractual rights ✅
B.An amendment abolishing all taxes on oil companies
C.An amendment allowing unlimited foreign extraction without regulation
D.An amendment that removes all environmental protections
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A constitutional provision that affirms state ownership yet respects pre‑existing contracts would signal Mexico’s commitment to sovereignty while reassuring investors, reducing the chance of boycotts similar to those after 1938. Eliminating taxes or regulation would either discourage investment or provoke environmental backlash, and removing protections would not address the core issue of ownership rights.

Q20. Imagine a scenario where the U.S. never intervened in 1916‑17. Predict how the Mexican Revolution’s timeline and outcomes might differ, focusing on revolutionary factions.

A.The revolutionary movement would likely have continued longer, allowing moderate factions more influence before a possible settlement ✅
B.The revolution would have ended instantly with a victory for the federal government
C.The United States would have annexed northern Mexico
D.The revolution would have turned into a civil war with no foreign involvement
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Without U.S. troops pressuring border security, revolutionary groups such as Villa’s forces could have sustained operations in the north, extending the conflict and giving moderate leaders more leverage in negotiations. The other options either unrealistically end the revolution, imply annexation, or ignore the impact of foreign pressure on internal dynamics.

Q21. Given that foreign investors owned 80% of Mexico’s mining sector in 1900, calculate the share of total mining revenue that remained with Mexican owners if the total revenue was \( \$100 \) million.

A.\( \$20 \) million ✅
B.\( \$80 \) million
C.\( \$50 \) million
D.\( \$0 \) million
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: If foreigners owned 80%, they received 80% of revenue, amounting to \( 0.80 \times 100 = 20 \) million dollars. Consequently, Mexican owners retained the remaining 20% or \( \$20 \) million. The other options miscalculate the proportion, either reversing the share or assuming an even split.

Q22. A diplomat in 1941 must negotiate oil sales with both the UK and the US after the boycott. Which negotiation strategy would likely secure the best price while maintaining sovereignty?

A.Propose a joint‑venture framework that splits profits equally among Mexico, the UK, and the US ✅
B.Offer a flat‑rate discount to the United States only
C.Demand exclusive rights for the United Kingdom
D.Refuse all negotiations and halt production
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A joint‑venture arrangement demonstrates Mexico’s control over its resource while giving both allies a stake, encouraging higher prices and shared risk. A unilateral discount or exclusive rights would undermine sovereignty, and stopping production would forfeit revenue, making the joint‑venture approach the most advantageous.

Q23. Suppose Mexico had implemented land reform earlier, in 1880, distributing 30% of hacienda land to peasants. How might this have altered the causes of the 1910 revolution?

A.It would have reduced agrarian discontent, possibly delaying or moderating revolutionary fervor ✅
B.It would have caused immediate economic collapse
C.It would have increased foreign investment dramatically
D.It would have led to the annexation of Mexico by the United States
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Early redistribution would have alleviated some peasant grievances, removing a major catalyst for rebellion and potentially postponing the 1910 uprising or shifting its focus toward political rather than agrarian demands. The other outcomes are historically unlikely; land reform would not instantly crash the economy, nor would it boost foreign investment or provoke U.S. annexation.

Q24. A historian compares the Mexican Revolution to the Russian Revolution. Which factor most distinguishes the Mexican case in terms of foreign influence?

A.The dependence on foreign capital and subsequent nationalist backlash over resources ✅
B.The absence of any foreign involvement in Mexico’s economy
C.The immediate establishment of a communist government
D.The lack of any land reform issues
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Mexico’s revolution was deeply shaped by foreign investment in oil, mining, and railways, prompting nationalist responses like the 1938 oil expropriation. In contrast, Russia’s revolution centered on internal class struggle and resulted in a communist regime. Foreign capital was not a primary driver in Russia, and land reform was central to both, making the foreign‑capital element the key differentiator.

Q25. If the PRI had adopted a proportional representation electoral system in 1970, how would that have changed party competition and policy outcomes?

A.It would have allowed smaller parties to gain seats, reducing PRI dominance and encouraging coalition politics ✅
B.It would have eliminated all opposition parties
C.It would have guaranteed the PRI 100% of the seats
D.It would have caused immediate dissolution of the PRI
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Proportional representation allocates legislative seats based on vote share, enabling minor parties to enter Congress, thereby curbing the PRI’s monopoly and fostering coalition‑building. This system would not ban opposition, nor would it give the PRI total control or dissolve it, making the first option the most plausible outcome.

Q26. If Mexico had retained the territories that became California, New Mexico, Nevada, Utah, and Arizona, estimate the impact on its GDP by 2020 using a simple per‑capita GDP multiplier of \( \$20{,}000 \). Which range best approximates the added GDP?

A.\( \$1.2 \) trillion to \( \$1.5 \) trillion ✅
B.\( \$300 \) billion to \( \$400 \) billion
C.\( \$50 \) billion to \( \$70 \) billion
D.Less than \( \$10 \) billion
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The combined population of those states is roughly 70 million. Multiplying by \( \$20{,}000 \) per person yields \( 70{,}000{,}000 \times 20{,}000 = 1.4 \times 10^{12} \) dollars, or about \( \$1.4 \) trillion, which falls within the \( \$1.2 \)–\( \$1.5 \) trillion range. The other options underestimate the contribution by orders of magnitude.

Q27. A student analyzes the impact of the 1910 election fraud. Which immediate action would most likely have prevented the ensuing revolution?

A.Implementing free and fair elections with transparent vote counting ✅
B.Increasing the tax burden on the wealthy
C.Suppressing all political parties
D.Inviting foreign observers to monitor the election without changing the process
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A genuinely democratic election would have removed the catalyst of perceived illegitimacy, reducing popular anger and the impulse for armed revolt. Raising taxes or suppressing parties would have heightened tensions, and merely inviting observers without reform would not address fraud, making the first option the most effective preventative measure.
