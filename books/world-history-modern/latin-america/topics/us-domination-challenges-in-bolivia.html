What is US domination challenges in Bolivia?

Bolivia challenged U.S. domination through indigenous-led movements that reclaimed control over natural gas and water resources, expelled U.S. drug enforcement agencies, and elected leaders who prioritized decolonization and regional integration over American interests.

Q1. In what year did Bolivia return to democracy after military rule?

A.1978 ✅
B.1982
C.1990
D.1995
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is 1982 because the military government ended that year and civilian rule was restored. Option A (1978) predates the transition, Option C (1990) is after democracy was already in place, and Option D (1995) is far later. Recognizing the 1982 date shows how political change aligns with regional democratic trends. It also illustrates the end of a long period of instability that began with the 1964 coup, paving the way for subsequent leaders to engage with international partners.

Q2. Why was the near‑eradication of coca farming in 2001 considered controversial?

A.It threatened cultural traditions ✅
B.It increased US influence
C.It caused unemployment among farmers
D.It boosted export revenue
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Because the eradication program eliminated most coca farms, many traditional growers lost their primary source of income, leading to widespread poverty and social unrest. Option A (cultural traditions) is partly true but not the main controversy; Option B (increased US influence) is indirect; Option D (boosted export revenue) is opposite of reality. Understanding the economic impact clarifies why the policy sparked protests and shaped later political movements. The loss of livelihoods forced thousands to join activist groups, contributing to the rise of movements demanding coca decriminalization and resource nationalization, which later influenced national elections.
