What is US domination challenges in Ecuador?

Q1. In what year did Ecuador achieve full independence from Spain?

A. 1810
B. 1825
C. 1830 ✅
D. 1845

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is C because Ecuador formally declared its independence in 1830 after a series of battles against Spanish forces. Option A (1810) refers to the start of independence movements in other parts of Latin America, while B (1825) is a date related to the dissolution of the Gran Colombia federation, not Ecuador's own independence. Option D (1845) is unrelated to any major political event in Ecuador. Understanding the exact year helps place Ecuador’s early nation‑building in its regional context.

Q2. Why did the 1998 drop in world oil prices cause Ecuador's poverty rate to rise sharply?

A. Because oil accounted for a small portion of exports
B. Because the government cut social programs
C. Because oil was the main export and revenue fell ✅
D. Because inflation decreased

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Option C is correct because oil was Ecuador’s principal export, so a sharp decline in oil prices dramatically reduced national income, limiting funds for social programs and increasing poverty. Option A is wrong since oil was not a minor export; Option B misstates the cause—social programs were already underfunded, not cut because of the price drop. Option D is incorrect because inflation actually rose, worsening economic hardship. This link shows how reliance on a single commodity can amplify vulnerability.

Q3. If the government renegotiates contracts with foreign oil firms to increase its share of profits, which immediate fiscal effect is most likely?

A. Decrease in government revenue
B. Increase in foreign investment
C. Higher tax receipts from oil ✅
D. Reduction in oil production

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is C because renegotiated contracts typically raise the government's share of oil profits, boosting tax receipts and other royalties. Option A is the opposite of what renegotiation aims to achieve. Option B may happen later but is not an immediate fiscal effect; investors often react cautiously to contract changes. Option D could occur if terms become too restrictive, but the primary short‑term fiscal impact is the increase in revenue from existing production.

Q10. Assume oil exports represent 30% of Ecuador’s GDP. If oil prices return to pre‑1998 levels, raising oil revenue by 20%, what approximate change in total GDP would you expect, all else equal?

A. 6% increase ✅
B. 12% increase
C. 20% increase
D. No change

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Option A is correct because a 20% rise in the oil sector (30% of GDP) translates to 0.30 × 0.20 = 0.06, or a 6% increase in overall GDP. Option B (12%) would require a 40% boost in the oil sector. Option C (20%) assumes the entire economy grows proportionally with oil, which is unrealistic. Option D ignores the direct contribution of oil to GDP. This illustrates how sectoral changes affect aggregate economic performance.
