Q1. Who was the first woman to serve as president of Chile?

🔹 A. Michelle Bachelet
🔹 B. Isabel Allende
🔹 C. Gabriela Mistral
🔹 D. Patricio Aylwin

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is A because Michelle Bachelet won the 2006 election, becoming Chile’s first female president. Isabel Allende and Gabriela Mistral are prominent Chilean figures but never held the presidency, and Patricio Aylwin served earlier as a male president. Recognizing Bachelet’s historic role helps students connect Chile’s recent democratic era with broader social progress, illustrating how leadership diversity can shape policy priorities such as education and health reforms.

Q2. Which external factor most directly contributed to the 1973 military coup that removed President Salvador Allende from office?

🔹 A. Economic sanctions
🔹 B. CIA-backed support for the military
🔹 C. Regional trade embargo
🔹 D. Natural disaster

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B because declassified documents reveal the CIA provided covert assistance to Chilean armed forces, accelerating the coup that ousted Allende. Economic sanctions (A) existed but were not the decisive trigger, a regional trade embargo (C) never occurred, and a natural disaster (D) is unrelated. Understanding foreign intervention clarifies how external actors can reshape a nation’s political trajectory, a key concept in modern Latin American history.

Q3. How can Pinochet’s legacy be described in terms of economic policy and human rights?

🔹 B. He liberalized markets but oversaw widespread human‑rights abuses
🔹 C. He maintained a socialist economy with minimal repression
🔹 D. He isolated Chile economically and politically

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B because Pinochet implemented free‑market reforms that boosted growth, yet his regime was marked by severe repression, disappearances, and torture. Option A incorrectly suggests protection of liberties, while C misstates his economic orientation, and D exaggerates isolation. Recognizing this dual legacy helps students see how rapid economic change can coexist with authoritarian practices, informing debates on the trade‑offs between growth and rights.

Q6. After a devastating earthquake, the new president must raise \(20\) billion dollars for reconstruction. Which financing mix reflects Chile’s move toward state involvement while still using private capital?

🔹 A. 100 % public bonds
🔹 B. 50 % public bonds and 50 % private loans
🔹 C. Entirely private insurance payouts
🔹 D. No financing needed

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is B because Chile combined government‑issued bonds with private‑sector loans, balancing state control of resources with market financing. Funding solely through public bonds (A) would exclude private participation, while relying only on insurance payouts (C) would ignore the state’s role, and claiming no financing (D) is unrealistic. This mixed approach demonstrates how governments can leverage both public and private mechanisms to rebuild after disasters, aligning with Chile’s broader trend of selective state intervention.

Q7. When Codelco was placed under state control, which immediate fiscal effect was most likely?

🔹 A. A decrease in tax revenue
🔹 B. An increase in government revenue from profits
🔹 C. No change in public finances
🔹 D. A reduction in foreign investment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is C because state ownership of Codelco allows the government to capture a larger share of copper profits, boosting public revenue. A decrease in tax revenue (A) would occur if the firm were privatized, not nationalized. No change (B) is inaccurate because profit flows shift to the state, and a reduction in foreign investment (D) may happen later but is not the immediate fiscal outcome. Understanding this effect clarifies why Chile used the copper sector to fund expanded social programs.
