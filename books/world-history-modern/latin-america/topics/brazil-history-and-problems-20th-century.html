What is Brazil history and problems 20th century?

Throughout the twentieth century, Brazil experienced cycles of military dictatorship and democratic restoration while grappling with extreme income inequality, rapid urbanization that created vast favelas, and chronic inflation despite periods of significant industrial growth.

Q1. In which year did Brazil gain independence from Portugal?

A. \(1825\) ✅
B. 1800
C. 1889
D. 1900

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is \(1825\) because Brazil formally ended Portuguese rule that year. The other dates refer to unrelated events: 1800 predates independence, 1889 marks the transition to a republic, and 1900 is after independence. Knowing the exact year allows students to locate Brazil’s early nation‑building within the broader timeline of Latin American independence movements.

Q2. Which president is credited with constructing the new capital, Brasília?

A. Getulio Vargas
B. Juscelino Kubitschek ✅
C. Janio Quadros
D. Julio Goulart

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Juscelino Kubitschek launched the project to build Brasília, aiming to promote interior development and national integration. Vargas, Quadros, and Goulart were involved in other reforms but never initiated the capital’s construction. Recognizing Kubitschek’s role helps learners see how ambitious infrastructure can shape a country’s identity, even when it strains finances.

Q3. In what year did Getúlio Vargas first assume power as Brazil’s leader?

A. 1945
B. 1937
C. 1930 ✅
D. 1950

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Vargas rose to power in 1930 after a revolution that ended the old oligarchic order. The other years correspond to later events: 1945 marks his temporary removal, 1937 is when he established an authoritarian constitution, and 1950 is when he returned for a second term. Pinpointing 1930 clarifies the start of Brazil’s modern industrialization drive.

Q4. Why did the Brazilian military initially support Getúlio Vargas in the 1930s?

A. Because he promised land reform
B. Because he opposed communism
C. Because he promoted industrialization ✅
D. Because he was a military officer

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The military backed Vargas chiefly because his policies emphasized rapid industrial growth, which aligned with their interest in modernizing the armed forces and the nation’s economy. Land reform and anti‑communist stances were not his primary focus, and Vargas was a civilian, not a military officer. This shows how economic agendas can secure military backing.

Q5. What was the primary motive behind Getúlio Vargas’s decision to commit suicide in 1954?

A. To avoid impeachment
B. To protest corruption
C. As a sacrifice for Brazilian workers ✅
D. Due to personal health issues

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Vargas framed his death as a “sacrifice” for the working class, aiming to preserve his legacy and shield his supporters from political retaliation. The other options misrepresent his intent: he was not fleeing impeachment, nor was he protesting corruption, and there was no documented health crisis. Understanding this motive illustrates how leaders may use personal actions to influence public perception.

Q6. How did the construction of Brasília impact Brazil’s fiscal situation during Juscelino Kubitschek’s presidency?

C. It attracted foreign investment
D. It improved education funding

📖 Explanation:

Building Brasília required massive public spending, which strained the national budget and diverted funds from other needs, leading to fiscal deficits. Tourism and foreign investment benefits emerged later, but they did not offset the immediate cost. Recognizing this fiscal strain helps students evaluate the trade‑offs of large‑scale capital projects.

Q8. Which of the following actions by President Goulart most directly threatened United States interests during the Cold War?

A. Nationalizing Brazil’s oil refineries
B. Aligning Brazil with the Soviet Union
C. Opposing economic sanctions on Cuba
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Goulart’s policies—nationalizing oil, seeking closer ties with the USSR, and rejecting sanctions on Cuba—collectively alarmed the United States, which feared a shift toward the Soviet bloc in Latin America. Each individual action posed a challenge, but together they represented a broader ideological threat. This highlights how foreign policy choices can affect international alliances.

Q9. If a modern Brazilian president wanted to replicate Vargas’s industrial growth, which policy would be most effective?

C. Deregulating the manufacturing sector
D. Reducing taxes on imported machinery

📖 Explanation:

Vargas succeeded by creating state‑run companies that drove steel and electrical production, directly spurring industrial capacity. Q10. Based on historical patterns, what is the most likely outcome when the Brazilian military opposes a popular civilian leader?

A. A successful coup d’état ✅
B. A peaceful transition of power
C. The military assumes an advisory role
D. The leader voluntarily steps down

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

History shows that when the armed forces view a popular leader as a threat to their influence, they often intervene directly, leading to coups. Peaceful transitions or advisory roles have been rare, and voluntary resignation seldom occurs under strong military pressure. This underscores the risk of civilian‑military conflict in unstable political environments.
