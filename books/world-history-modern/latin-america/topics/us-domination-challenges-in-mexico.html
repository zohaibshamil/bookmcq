What is US domination challenges in Mexico?

Despite close economic ties, Mexico challenged U.S. domination through the nationalist policies of the Mexican Revolution, the assertion of control over natural resources like oil, and diplomatic efforts to maintain sovereignty even while integrating into North American trade agreements.

Q1. In what year did Vicente Fox become President of Mexico?

A. 1994
B. 2000 ✅
C. 2006
D. 2012

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Vicente Fox won the presidential election in the year 2000, ending 71 years of PRI rule. The correct answer is 2000 (option B). The other years listed either precede his term (1994) or refer to later elections (2006 for Calderón and 2012 for Peña Nieto). Knowing the election year helps place Fox’s reforms in the correct historical context of Mexico’s political transition.

Q2. A Mexican farmer is facing a surge in cheap American meat imports after NAFTA. Which strategy would most likely help the farmer remain competitive?

A. Request higher government subsidies
B. Renegotiate NAFTA terms
C. Diversify crops and add value‑added processing ✅
D. Lobby for higher import tariffs

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The surge of cheap American meat makes it difficult for Mexican producers to compete on price alone. Diversifying crops and adding value‑added processing (option C) allows farmers to target niche markets and increase profit margins, reducing reliance on direct competition with imports. Higher subsidies (A) are unlikely given fiscal constraints, renegotiating NAFTA (B) is a long‑term diplomatic process, and higher import tariffs (D) could violate trade agreements and provoke retaliation.

Q4. If a severe recession in the United States reduces remittance flows by 20 % to Mexico, what is the most direct effect on Mexico’s GDP?

A. GDP will increase
B. GDP will decrease ✅
C. GDP will stay unchanged
D. GDP will cause inflation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Remittances are a major source of income for many Mexican households, and they directly affect consumption and investment. A 20 % reduction lowers household spending, leading to a contraction in GDP (option B). An increase in GDP (A) would contradict the expected effect, while unchanged GDP (C) ignores the impact of income flows. Inflation (D) might result indirectly, but the most immediate effect is a decline in overall economic output.

Q5. Since December 2006, an estimated 35 000 people have been killed in drug‑related violence. Assuming the data covers up to the end of 2020, what is the average annual death toll?

A. ≈ 2 500 ✅
B. ≈ 1 000
C. ≈ 3 500
D. ≈ 5 000

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

From December 2006 to the end of 2020 spans 14 full years. Dividing the total estimated deaths (35 000) by 14 yields an average of about 2 500 deaths per year, corresponding to option A. The other choices either overestimate (≈ 3 500, ≈ 5 000) or underestimate (≈ 1 000) the yearly toll. This average helps illustrate the persistent scale of violence linked to drug trafficking in Mexico.

Q7. Why did Mexican farmers protest after NAFTA’s implementation?

A. Because NAFTA increased imports of American produce ✅
B. Because NAFTA lowered Mexican export taxes
C. Because NAFTA required farmers to adopt organic methods
D. Because NAFTA eliminated all agricultural subsidies

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Mexican farmers protested because NAFTA led to a large influx of inexpensive American agricultural products, especially meat, which undercut domestic prices. This direct competition (option A) sparked widespread unrest. Higher export taxes (B) were not introduced, organic methods (C) were not mandated, and the removal of all subsidies (D) did not occur, making those options inaccurate. Understanding trade‑agreement impacts explains the root of agricultural discontent.

Q8. How did Vicente Fox’s proposal to open the Mexico‑USA border affect his political standing?

A. It strengthened support among left‑wing parties
B. It alienated right‑wing supporters who favored stricter immigration
C. It drew criticism from both his own party and opposition for aligning too closely with the United States ✅
D. It had no noticeable impact on his popularity

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Fox’s call to open the border for Mexican workers was seen as aligning Mexico closely with the United States, which drew criticism from his own party’s right‑wing base and from left‑wing opponents who feared loss of sovereignty. This mixed reaction is captured by option C. Strengthening left‑wing support (A) or alienating right‑wing supporters (B) alone does not reflect the dual criticism, and the claim of no impact (D) ignores the political fallout.

Q9. What was the primary reason the 2008 global credit crisis hit Mexico’s economy hard?

A. Mexico’s heavy reliance on U.S. demand for exports ✅
B. Mexico’s dependence on oil revenues
C. Low domestic consumption rates
D. High inflation rates

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Mexico’s economy is heavily tied to U.S. demand for its exports, so the 2008 credit crisis that depressed American consumption directly reduced Mexican export earnings, causing a sharp downturn (option A). While oil revenue (B) is significant, the immediate shock stemmed from reduced U.S. trade. Low domestic consumption (C) and high inflation (D) were not primary drivers of the 2008 slump, making those choices incorrect.

Q10. Deploying the army against drug cartels can influence civil‑military relations in Mexico. Which outcome is most likely?

A. Increased public trust in the military
B. Erosion of civilian control over security forces ✅
C. Strengthening of democratic institutions
D. Reduction in cartel violence without any political side‑effects

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Deploying the military against cartels shifts security responsibilities from civilian police to the armed forces, which can erode civilian oversight and weaken democratic control (option B). Public trust may rise temporarily (A) but often declines as abuses emerge. Strengthening democratic institutions (C) is unlikely without reforms, and a reduction in violence without side‑effects (D) is unrealistic given the complexities of organized crime. This highlights the delicate balance of security and governance.

Q11. Which factor most directly contributed to Mexico becoming the second‑largest economy in Latin America by 2012?

A. Rapid growth in the tourism sector
B. Significant foreign investment and diversification beyond oil ✅
C. Large-scale land reforms
D. Adoption of a universal basic income

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Mexico’s rise to the second‑largest Latin American economy was driven by a surge in foreign direct investment and efforts to diversify its export base beyond oil, as reflected in option B. While tourism (A) contributed, it was not the principal factor. Large‑scale land reforms (C) and universal basic income (D) were not implemented, making those options inaccurate. Recognizing the role of investment and diversification clarifies how Mexico expanded its economic stature.
