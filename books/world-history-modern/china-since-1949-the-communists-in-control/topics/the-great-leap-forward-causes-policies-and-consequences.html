📝 All The Great Leap Forward: Causes, Policies, and Consequences MCQs

Q1. What was the primary purpose of establishing communes during the Great Leap Forward?

🔹 A. To increase individual land ownership
🔹 B. To centralize agricultural and industrial production under larger units
🔹 C. To reduce the role of the Communist Party
🔹 D. To promote private entrepreneurship

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Communes were created to consolidate smaller collective farms into larger units, allowing the state to coordinate both agricultural and industrial activities more efficiently. This centralization aimed to mobilize resources, standardize production, and integrate communal governance, rather than encouraging private ownership or reducing party influence.

Q2. Approximately how many people could a typical commune contain according to the description?

🔹 A. \(10{,}000\)
🔹 B. \(30{,}000\)
🔹 C. \(75{,}000\)
🔹 D. \(100{,}000\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The text states that communes could hold up to \(75{,}000\) individuals, making them significantly larger than earlier collective farms. This scale was intended to facilitate coordinated economic planning and social services within a single administrative unit, reflecting Mao's vision of mass mobilization.

Q3. Which of the following best describes the change in industrial emphasis during the Great Leap Forward?

🔹 A. Building massive steel plants similar to the USSR
🔹 B. Focusing on small, rural factories to support agriculture
🔹 C. Investing heavily in high‑tech electronics
🔹 D. Privatizing existing factories

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Mao shifted policy from large‑scale industrial complexes to numerous small factories located in the countryside. These facilities were meant to produce agricultural machinery and other tools needed locally, aligning industrial output with the needs of rural production rather than pursuing heavy industry.

Q4. A commune reports that its backyard steel furnace produced 150 kg of steel in a week, but the target was \(200\) kg. Which action would most likely align with the policy directives of the time?

🔹 A. Reduce furnace output to match the target
🔹 B. Increase labor hours and add more furnaces
🔹 C. Report the shortfall as a success to the central committee
🔹 D. Switch to producing agricultural tools instead of steel

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Great Leap emphasized rapid steel output; a shortfall would push cadres to intensify labor and expand furnace numbers, even at the cost of efficiency. Reporting a shortfall as success would contradict the propaganda of progress, while shifting focus away from steel would defeat the policy’s industrial goals.

Q5. How did the withdrawal of Russian aid affect the Great Leap Forward?

🔹 A. It accelerated industrial growth
🔹 B. It caused a shortage of technical expertise and equipment
🔹 C. It led to immediate abandonment of communes
🔹 D. It improved food production

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When Soviet assistance ended, China lost valuable technical support, machinery, and expertise crucial for large‑scale projects. This gap exacerbated existing inefficiencies, contributing to the hardships and failures observed during the famine and industrial missteps of the Great Leap.

Q6. In 1960, a village experiences a harvest shortfall of \(15\%\). According to the policies described, what would be the expected response from local commune leaders?

🔹 A. Distribute existing grain reserves equally among households
🔹 B. Increase labor on collective farms to boost next year’s output
🔹 C. Request additional grain from neighboring communes
🔹 D. Encourage private farming to compensate

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The commune system emphasized collective responsibility and inter‑communal support. Faced with a deficit, leaders would likely appeal to neighboring communes for grain aid, maintaining the collective framework rather than reverting to private farming or solely increasing labor, which might not quickly resolve the shortage.
