What is The Cultural Revolution in China (1966–1969): Causes and Effects?

The Cultural Revolution began in 1966 when Mao Zedong launched a campaign to remove people he believed were opposing his revolutionary ideas and to restore his political influence. Young people known as the Red Guards attacked traditional beliefs, intellectuals, officials, and suspected political opponents. Schools and universities were disrupted, while violence and political struggle spread across the country. The movement caused social disorder, economic disruption, persecution, and the removal or death of many people before the Red Guards were eventually brought under control.

Q1. What year did the Cultural Revolution officially begin?

A. 1965
B. 1966 ✅
C. 1967
D. 1968

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Cultural Revolution was launched by Mao Zedong in May 1966, marking the start of a decade-long political campaign that aimed to purge capitalist and traditional elements from Chinese society and reassert Maoist ideology.

Q2. Which organization was primarily responsible for directing the Cultural Revolution’s policies?

A. The State Council
B. The Central Military Commission
C. The Cultural Revolution Group ✅
D. The National People’s Congress

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Cultural Revolution Group, a small body of senior Party officials chaired by Mao, formulated and oversaw the implementation of the campaign’s policies, making it the central engine behind the political upheavals of the period.

Q3. According to the 1950 Constitution, how often were members of the National People’s Congress elected?

A. Every 2 years
B. Every 3 years
C. Every 4 years ✅
D. Every 5 years

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Constitution stipulated that NPC members served four‑year terms, reflecting the new centralised governance structure that sought to legitimize the Communist Party’s rule through periodic, though tightly controlled, elections.

📖 Explanation:

The movement forced millions of educated urban youth to relocate to the countryside, where they lived and worked alongside peasants, a policy intended to break class distinctions but which also disrupted education and career trajectories.
