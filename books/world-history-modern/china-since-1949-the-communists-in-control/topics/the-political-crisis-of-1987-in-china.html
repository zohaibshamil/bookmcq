📝 All The Political Crisis of 1987 in China MCQs

Q1. Who resigned as party general secretary in 1986 after hardliners accused him of being too liberal?

🔹 A. Hu Yaobang
🔹 B. Zhao Ziyang
🔹 C. Li Peng
🔹 D. Deng Xiaoping

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Hu Yaobang stepped down because hardliners claimed he encouraged intellectuals to demand greater democracy. His removal showed the tension between reformers and conservatives, and it paved the way for Zhao Ziyang to assume the role while maintaining a tighter political line.

Q2. Which reformer succeeded Hu Yaobang as party general secretary?

🔹 A. Zhao Ziyang
🔹 B. Li Peng
🔹 C. Deng Xiaoping
🔹 D. Wang Zhen

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Zhao Ziyang took over after Hu's resignation. Unlike his predecessor, Zhao avoided overt political liberalization, focusing instead on economic reforms, which helped Deng retain control while appeasing the more conservative elements of the Politburo.

Q3. Who became prime minister after Zhao Ziyang, representing hard‑line interests?

🔹 A. Li Peng
🔹 B. Zhou Enlai
🔹 C. Wen Jiabao
🔹 D. Hu Jintao

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Li Peng, known for his emphasis on order and authority, replaced Zhao Ziyang as premier. His appointment reflected the hardliners' desire to clamp down on protests and maintain strict party discipline during a period of growing unrest.

Q4. What were the Four Modernizations championed by Deng?

🔹 A. Agriculture, industry, science, defence
🔹 B. Education, health, culture, tourism
🔹 C. Finance, trade, transport, communications
🔹 D. Military, diplomacy, law, technology

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Four Modernizations—agriculture, industry, science, and defence—were Deng's blueprint for modernizing China. These sectors were seen as essential for raising productivity and improving living standards, and they became a rallying point for student demonstrations demanding faster implementation.

Q5. Which year did student demonstrations in December that pressured the hardliners occur?

🔹 A. 1986
🔹 B. 1987
🔹 C. 1988
🔹 D. 1989

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In December 1986, students staged protests supporting Deng's reforms and demanding quicker progress and more democracy. The demonstrations alarmed hardliners, leading to the resignation of Hu Yaobang and setting the stage for the political crises that unfolded in the following years.

Q6. What was the approximate inflation rate reported for 1988‑89?

🔹 A. \(30\%\)
🔹 B. \(15\%\)
🔹 C. \(45\%\)
🔹 D. \(5\%\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Inflation surged to about \(30\%\) during 1988‑89, eroding the real wages of state employees and creating widespread discontent. This economic pressure intensified calls for political reform and contributed to the volatile climate that culminated in the Tiananmen Square protests.

Q7. Which group did Zhao Ziyang promise to clamp down on while continuing economic reforms?

🔹 A. Bourgeois intellectuals
🔹 B. Rural peasants
🔹 C. Military officers
🔹 D. Foreign investors

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Zhao announced that while China would press ahead with market‑oriented reforms, the government would tighten control over 'bourgeois intellectuals' who were perceived as challenging party authority. This dual strategy illustrated the regime's attempt to balance economic liberalization with political stability.

Q8. A student leader argues that economic reforms must be accompanied by political liberalization. Which of the following best reflects Deng’s dilemma as described in the text?

🔹 A. Balancing market choice with restricted political choice
🔹 B. Eliminating all reforms to preserve party control
🔹 C. Prioritizing military strength over education
🔹 D. Focusing solely on agricultural output

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Deng faced a paradox: he wanted to allow citizens to buy and sell freely, yet he feared that granting political choices would undermine party dominance. This tension is evident in the reforms that expanded economic freedoms while simultaneously tightening political controls, a core issue in the 1987 crisis.

Q9. Why did the hardliners push for Hu Yaobang’s resignation despite his support for economic modernization?

🔹 A. They feared his liberal political stance could erode party authority
🔹 B. They wanted to accelerate economic reforms
🔹 C. They opposed any change to agriculture policies
🔹 D. They sought to increase military spending

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Hardliners viewed Hu’s encouragement of open debate and democratic ideas as a threat to the Communist Party’s monopoly on power. Even though he backed economic modernization, his political liberalism risked encouraging opposition, prompting hardliners to remove him to safeguard ideological control.

Q10. After the 1986 student protests, the Politburo imposes a five‑day notice rule for demonstrations. Which outcome best illustrates the rule’s impact?

🔹 A. Students become more cautious, reducing spontaneous protests
🔹 B. Students ignore the rule and increase street actions
🔹 C. The rule leads to immediate cessation of all dissent
🔹 D. The rule causes a split in the Politburo

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The new notice requirement was intended to give the party time to prepare, and it succeeded in making students more hesitant to organize spontaneous demonstrations. While it did not eliminate dissent, it limited the immediacy and scale of protests, reflecting the regime’s attempt to control public expression.

Q11. Which reformer was known for keeping clear of controversial political ideas while advancing economic change?

🔹 A. Zhao Ziyang
🔹 B. Hu Yaobang
🔹 C. Li Peng
🔹 D. Deng Xiaoping

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Zhao Ziyang, after succeeding Hu Yaobang, focused on economic restructuring without publicly endorsing political liberalization. This cautious approach allowed him to continue reforms while avoiding direct confrontation with hardliners, illustrating the delicate balance the leadership sought during the crisis.

Q12. How did the 1986 student demonstrations influence the subsequent appointment of Li Peng as premier?

🔹 A. The protests alarmed conservatives, prompting them to select a hard‑line leader
🔹 B. The protests demonstrated the need for a reformist premier
🔹 C. Li Peng was chosen to appease foreign investors
🔹 D. The protests had no impact on Li Peng’s appointment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The student activism heightened hardliner fears of losing control, leading them to push for a premier who would enforce order. Li Peng’s reputation for strict discipline made him an ideal candidate to suppress future unrest while preserving the party’s authority.

Q13. What policy did Zhao Ziyang announce to accelerate financial reform?

🔹 A. New measures to speed up financial reform
🔹 B. Nationalization of all banks
🔹 C. Fixed exchange rate with the US dollar
🔹 D. Withdrawal from international trade

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Zhao pledged to introduce measures that would modernize China’s financial system, such as allowing more market‑based interest rates and expanding credit availability. These steps were intended to support rapid economic growth, even as political controls were being tightened.

Q14. If inflation reaches \(30\%\) while wages lag, what macro‑economic effect is most likely to occur among state employees?

🔹 A. Decreased real purchasing power
🔹 B. Increased savings rates
🔹 C. Higher investment in private enterprises
🔹 D. Improved living standards

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When prices rise faster than wages, the real income of workers falls, reducing their ability to purchase goods and services. This erosion of purchasing power can fuel dissatisfaction and pressure the government to address both price stability and wage growth.

Q15. Which foreign leader’s view mirrored the Chinese debate that economic freedom requires political freedom?

🔹 A. Mikhail Gorbachev
🔹 B. Ronald Reagan
🔹 C. Margaret Thatcher
🔹 D. Fidel Castro

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Gorbachev, the Soviet leader, argued that market reforms could not be sustained without political openness, a stance that echoed the Chinese dilemma of balancing economic liberalization with strict party control during the 1987 crisis.

Q16. Considering the events of 1986‑87, which factor most directly contributed to the hardliners’ confidence in overturning reforms?

🔹 A. The perceived loss of party discipline after student protests
🔹 B. International pressure from the United Nations
🔹 C. A sudden decline in agricultural output
🔹 D. A major natural disaster in the south

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Student demonstrations, especially those ignoring new restrictions, signaled a weakening of party discipline. Hardliners interpreted this as a direct challenge to authority, giving them justification to reassert control by removing reform‑friendly officials and tightening political measures.

Q17. A reform-minded official argues that faster implementation of the Four Modernizations could reduce social unrest. Which counter‑argument would a hardliner most likely present?

🔹 A. Rapid change could destabilize the socialist system
🔹 B. Faster reforms will increase foreign debt
🔹 C. Modernization will lead to environmental degradation
🔹 D. Economic growth will lower party prestige

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Hardliners feared that accelerating reforms without adequate political control could undermine socialist ideology and the party’s monopoly. They argued that too‑quick modernization might create social upheaval, eroding the foundations of the political system they sought to protect.

Q18. Which event directly led to the resignation of Hu Yaobang?

🔹 A. Hardliners’ accusation of excessive liberalism
🔹 B. Economic recession in 1985
🔹 C. Foreign invasion threat
🔹 D. Natural disaster in Sichuan

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The hardliners accused Hu Yaobang of being overly liberal, especially after student protests demanded more democracy. This political charge forced his resignation, illustrating how ideological concerns could outweigh economic performance in determining leadership changes during the crisis.

Q19. How does the tension between offering market choice and denying political choice reflect a classic ‘dual‑track’ reform model?

🔹 A. It separates economic liberalization from political control, creating parallel tracks
🔹 B. It merges economic and political reforms into a single package
🔹 C. It eliminates the need for any political reforms
🔹 D. It prioritizes political reform over economic change

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A dual‑track model allows market mechanisms to operate while the political system remains authoritarian. This separation can generate growth but also creates contradictions, as citizens experience greater economic freedom yet remain constrained politically, a core paradox evident in China’s 1987 crisis.

Q20. Suppose the inflation rate stays at \(30\%\) for two consecutive years while the government continues to suppress political dissent. What long‑term risk does this pose to regime stability?

🔹 A. Growing public discontent that may culminate in mass protests
🔹 B. Immediate collapse of the banking sector
🔹 C. Rapid industrialization without social costs
🔹 D. Improved international trade balances

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Sustained high inflation erodes living standards, especially when wages do not keep pace. Coupled with political repression, this combination can intensify public frustration, making the population more likely to organize large‑scale protests that threaten the regime’s legitimacy.

Q21. Which of the following best describes the “balancing act” Deng performed between reformers and hardliners?

🔹 A. Managing competing pressures to keep both sides partially satisfied
🔹 B. Choosing exclusively one faction and eliminating the other
🔹 C. Implementing only economic reforms while ignoring politics
🔹 D. Delegating all decisions to foreign advisors

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Deng’s strategy involved allowing limited economic liberalization while restricting political expression, thereby placating reformers enough to maintain momentum, yet keeping hardliners reassured that the party’s authority remained intact. This precarious equilibrium defined the political climate of the late 1980s.

Q22. If Zhao Ziyang had openly supported the formation of an opposition party, what likely immediate consequence would have occurred?

🔹 A. Hardliners would have accelerated the removal of reformist officials
🔹 B. International allies would have increased aid
🔹 C. The economy would have instantly doubled its growth rate
🔹 D. Student protests would have ceased entirely

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Open support for an opposition party would have crossed a red line for the hardliners, prompting a swift purge of reformist elements, including Zhao himself. The party’s priority of maintaining a single‑party system would have outweighed any potential economic benefits from political pluralism.

Q23. A historian argues that the 1986 student demands for “more democracy” were a catalyst for later events in 1989. Which piece of evidence best supports this claim?

🔹 A. The same slogans reappeared during the Tiananmen Square protests
🔹 B. Economic growth peaked in 1986
🔹 C. Foreign media praised China’s reforms in 1986
🔹 D. The military withdrew from Beijing in 1986

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The continuity of slogans and demands between the 1986 demonstrations and the 1989 Tiananmen Square protests indicates a direct ideological lineage. This link demonstrates how early calls for political openness laid the groundwork for the larger, more confrontational movement that emerged three years later.
