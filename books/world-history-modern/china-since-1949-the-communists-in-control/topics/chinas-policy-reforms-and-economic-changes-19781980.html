What is China's Policy Reforms and Economic Changes (1978–1980)?

From 1978, China's leaders began changing the economic policies that had been used under Mao. Under Deng Xiaoping's growing influence, the government reduced the importance of strict central planning and introduced greater freedom for farmers and businesses. Agricultural communes were gradually replaced by a household responsibility system, allowing families to make more decisions about production. China also began encouraging foreign investment, trade, and modern technology, creating the foundations for rapid economic growth.

Q3. Which organizational structure, created during the Cultural Revolution, was abolished after Deng's reforms?

A. Revolutionary committees ✅
B. People's communes
C. The Great Leap Forward
D. The Cultural Revolution itself

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Revolutionary committees, which had been set up to manage local governance during the Cultural Revolution, were dismantled and replaced by more democratic bodies, reflecting Deng’s intent to restore conventional administrative mechanisms and reduce ideological turbulence.

Q4. Which policy change directly allowed the establishment of private industrial enterprises in China during the early reform period?

B. Household responsibility system
C. Permission for private industrial companies ✅
D. Collectivization of all industry

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The reform package explicitly granted permission for the creation of privately owned industrial firms, breaking the previous monopoly of state ownership and encouraging entrepreneurial activity, which was a key step toward modernising China’s industrial sector.

Membership in these institutions opened channels for Chinese officials to secure external financing, receive technical expertise, and engage in global development projects, all of which were essential for the country’s rapid industrial and agricultural modernization during the early reform era.
