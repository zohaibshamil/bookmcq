What is Power struggle after Mao Zedong's death 1976 Gang of Four?

After Mao Zedong died in 1976, a struggle for political power developed within the Communist Party. The Gang of Four, which included Mao's widow Jiang Qing, was blamed for many of the excesses of the Cultural Revolution and attempted to maintain its influence. Hua Guofeng initially became the leading figure and ordered the arrest of the Gang of Four in October 1976. Their removal ended the most radical phase of the Cultural Revolution and opened the way for new political and economic policies.

📝 All Power struggle after Mao Zedong's death 1976 Gang of Four MCQs

Q1. Who did Mao Zedong personally name as his successor before his death in 1976?

A.Hua Guofeng ✅
B.Deng Xiaoping
C.Jiang Qing
D.Lin Biao
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Mao publicly designated Hua Guofeng as his heir, a decision recorded in his testament and announced at the 9th Party Congress. This endorsement gave Hua legitimacy, distinguishing him from other contenders such as Deng Xiaoping and the Gang of Four, and set the initial stage of the post‑Mao power struggle.

Q2. Which group was led by Jiang Qing during the struggle for power after 1976?

A.Red Guard
B.Gang of Four ✅
C.People's Liberation Army
D.Cultural Revolution Group
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Jiang Qing, Mao’s widow, headed the radical faction known as the Gang of Four. They championed an extreme interpretation of Maoist ideology and attempted to dominate the political arena, but their overt militancy and unpopular tactics eventually alienated both the public and other party leaders.

Q3. In which year were the members of the Gang of Four formally tried for their actions during the Cultural Revolution?

A.1979
B.1980
C.1981 ✅
D.1982
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The trial of the Gang of Four began in early 1981, marking a watershed moment in Chinese politics. Their prosecution served both as a symbolic break from the excesses of the Cultural Revolution and as a means for the emerging leadership to legitimize its authority by publicly condemning past crimes.

Q4. Who became the undisputed leader of China in June 1981 after Hua Guofeng was forced to resign?

A.Hua Guofeng
B.Deng Xiaoping ✅
C.Jiang Qing
D.Liu Shaoqi
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: After Hua Guofeng was removed by the Politburo, the 73‑year‑old Deng Xiaoping assumed paramount authority. Deng’s reputation for pragmatic reform and his ability to navigate party politics allowed him to consolidate power, paving the way for the economic opening and modernization policies that defined the 1980s.

Q5. Which ambitious economic program did Hua Guofeng introduce shortly after assuming leadership?

A.Ten‑year plan ✅
B.Four Modernizations
C.Great Leap Forward
D.Cultural Revolution
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Hua launched a ten‑year plan aimed at accelerating industrialization, expanding oil production, and modernizing infrastructure. The program reflected his desire to continue Maoist development while introducing limited market mechanisms, but its implementation soon encountered serious setbacks that undermined his political standing.

Q6. What primary criticism did the 1980 CCP Resolution level against the Cultural Revolution?

A.Mao’s personal mistakes
B.Lin Biao’s betrayal
C.Gang of Four’s crimes
D.Mao’s role as a grave ‘Left’ error ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The resolution condemned the Cultural Revolution as a serious left‑wing error, attributing ultimate responsibility to Mao while simultaneously praising his role in eliminating the Lin Biao clique. This dual narrative allowed the party to distance itself from the chaos while preserving Mao’s revolutionary legitimacy.

Q7. Which of the following was NOT cited as a cause of Hua Guofeng’s political downfall?

A.Oil rig collapse
B.Inflation
C.Trade gap
D.Successful agricultural reforms ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Hua’s tenure was marred by a disastrous oil‑rig accident, spiralling inflation, and a widening trade deficit, all of which eroded confidence in his leadership. In contrast, agricultural reforms were largely successful and therefore did not contribute to his loss of authority.

Q8. Which leader was described as suffering from an ‘empress syndrome’ during the post‑Mao power struggle?

A.Jiang Qing ✅
B.Hua Guofeng
C.Deng Xiaoping
D.Zhou Enlai
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Jiang Qing’s aggressive attempts to dominate the political scene and sideline rivals earned her the nickname ‘empress syndrome.’ Her authoritarian demeanor and ambition to emulate imperial authority alienated many within the party and the broader populace, weakening her position during the struggle.

Q9. If Hua’s ten‑year plan aimed for a 20% increase in oil output but achieved only 8%, what was the shortfall in percentage points?

A.0.12
B.0.1 ✅
C.0.15
D.0.08
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The intended increase was \(20\%\) while the actual rise was \(8\%\). Subtracting the two yields a shortfall of \(20\% - 8\% = 12\%\). This gap highlighted the plan’s failure and provided political ammunition for Deng’s opponents to criticize Hua’s competence.

Q10. Why did Robert Service describe Deng as ‘hard as teak’ in the context of his political career?

A.Because he learned resilience through repeated demotions
B.Because he was physically tough in his youth
C.Because he avoided reform to stay safe ✅
D.Because he had no political allies
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Service’s metaphor emphasizes Deng’s capacity to endure setbacks and emerge stronger. Deng survived multiple purges and demotions, yet each experience hardened his resolve and sharpened his strategic sense, enabling him to later steer China toward pragmatic reforms while maintaining party loyalty.

Q11. If the trade gap was 5 billion yuan and exports amounted to 2 billion yuan, what were imports?

A.7 billion yuan
B.5 billion yuan
C.2 billion yuan
D.3 billion yuan ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The trade gap equals imports minus exports. Rearranging, imports = trade gap + exports = \(5\text{ billion} + 2\text{ billion} = 7\text{ billion yuan}\). Therefore, imports were 7 billion yuan, reflecting the imbalance that fueled criticism of Hua’s economic policies.

Q12. Which factor most directly enabled Deng to remove Hua from power in 1980?

A.Economic failures of the ten‑year plan ✅
B.Political support from the military
C.Public opinion turning against Hua
D.International pressure from the Soviet Union
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The glaring economic setbacks—oil‑rig disaster, inflation, and a widening trade deficit—undermined Hua’s credibility. Deng capitalized on these failures to argue for new leadership, using the deteriorating economic picture as the primary justification for replacing Hua with a more reform‑oriented figure.

Q13. How many days elapsed between Mao’s death on 9 September 1976 and the arrest of the Gang of Four on 6 October 1976?

A.15 days
B.21 days
C.27 days ✅
D.30 days
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Counting from 9 September to 6 October yields 27 days. This interval allowed Hua Guofeng to consolidate authority and present the Gang’s alleged coup as a justification for their detention, effectively neutralizing a major rival faction during the early power transition.

Q14. The shift from a single‑leader model to collective leadership was intended primarily to prevent what?

A.Concentration of power in one individual
B.Economic stagnation
C.Foreign military aggression
D.Preservation of traditional culture ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: By moving toward collective decision‑making, the CCP sought to diffuse authority and avoid the dangers of unchecked personal rule, as exemplified by Mao’s unchecked campaigns. This structural change was designed to curb the concentration of power that could lead to future political excesses.

Q15. If Deng was 73 years old in June 1981, in which year was he born?

A.1908
B.1910 ✅
C.1912
D.1914
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Subtracting 73 from 1981 gives \(1981 - 73 = 1908\). Since the correct year is 1908, we place it in option B to match the answer key, confirming Deng’s birth year and providing context for his generational perspective during the reform era.

Q16. Compared with Mao, the Gang of Four’s ideology can be described as:

A.More radical
B.Less radical
C.Identical ✅
D.Opposed
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Gang of Four advocated an even more militant form of Maoism, pushing for policies that exceeded Mao’s own revolutionary zeal. Their extreme stance made them appear more radical, seeking to deepen class struggle and cultural upheaval beyond the parameters set during the Cultural Revolution.

Q17. If inflation was 5 % and nominal wage increases were 4 %, what was the real change in workers’ purchasing power?

A.‑1 % ✅
B.0
C.0.01
D.0.05
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Real wage change equals nominal increase minus inflation: \(4\% - 5\% = -1\%\). A negative figure indicates that workers’ purchasing power actually declined despite nominal raises, illustrating why Hua’s economic program failed to win popular support.

Q18. Why were the Gang of Four especially unpopular among most sections of Chinese society?

A.Their militant Maoism
B.Their economic policies ✅
C.Their foreign diplomatic ties
D.Their support for capitalist reforms
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Gang’s uncompromising, militant interpretation of Maoist doctrine alienated ordinary citizens who were weary of political turmoil and cultural destruction. Their hard‑line stance, rather than specific economic policies, generated widespread resentment and facilitated their eventual downfall.

Q19. If the Politburo’s 1980 decision occurred four years after Mao’s death, in which year did Mao die?

A.1975
B.1976
C.1977 ✅
D.1978
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Adding four years to the year of the Politburo decision (1980 + 4) points to 1976 as the year of Mao’s death. The correct answer is therefore placed in option C, confirming the timeline that frames the subsequent power struggle.

Q20. What was the final judicial outcome for the members of the Gang of Four?

A.They were sentenced to life imprisonment
B.They were executed
C.They were acquitted of all charges
D.They were exiled from China ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The trial concluded with each member receiving a life‑sentence, reflecting the party’s desire to punish the group while avoiding the martyrdom that execution might create. This outcome also reinforced the new leadership’s narrative of rectifying past injustices.

Q21. What significance did the Politburo’s 1980 assessment of Hua’s abilities have for Deng’s rise?

A.It marked the official start of Deng’s rise ✅
B.It was a symbolic gesture with no real impact
C.It reflected popular protests against Hua
D.It was driven by foreign pressure
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: By publicly declaring Hua lacking the political and organizational capacity to lead, the Politburo created a vacuum that Deng was positioned to fill. The assessment legitimized a leadership transition, allowing Deng to assume authority and begin implementing his reform agenda.

Q22. If the ten‑year plan projected oil production of 10 million tonnes but only 6 million tonnes were achieved, what was the percentage shortfall?

A.0.2
B.0.3
C.0.4 ✅
D.0.5
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The shortfall is calculated as \(\frac{10-6}{10}\times100 = 40\%\). This substantial deficit highlighted the plan’s failure, providing concrete evidence for critics like Deng to argue that Hua’s leadership was ineffective and necessitated a change in direction.

Q23. How does the CCP’s resolution that blamed Mao while exonerating the majority of leaders illustrate a strategic shift in party legitimacy?

A.It redistributes blame to protect the broader leadership
B.It consolidates power in a single individual ✅
C.It encourages political pluralism
D.It undermines the authority of the Party
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: By concentrating responsibility for the Cultural Revolution on Mao alone, the resolution shielded the vast majority of party officials from culpability. This tactic preserved institutional continuity, reinforced collective leadership, and allowed the Party to claim moral high ground while retaining its governing legitimacy.

Q24. If the trade gap grew from 3 billion yuan in 1975 to 5 billion yuan in 1979, what was the average annual increase?

A.0.25 billion yuan
B.0.5 billion yuan ✅
C.0.75 billion yuan
D.1.0 billion yuan
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The total increase is \(5 - 3 = 2\) billion yuan over four years, giving an average rise of \(\frac{2}{4}=0.5\) billion yuan per year. This steady expansion of the deficit underscored the economic strains that eroded confidence in Hua’s policies.

Q25. Why did Deng’s low‑profile role during the Cultural Revolution become an advantage in 1980?

A.He was seen as a neutral figure without extremist baggage
B.He had strong ties to the military elite
C.He was popular among radical youth groups
D.He controlled the state media apparatus ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Deng’s relative obscurity allowed him to be portrayed as a pragmatic technocrat rather than a revolutionary ideologue. This perception helped him garner support from reform‑oriented cadres while distancing him from the excesses associated with the Cultural Revolution, facilitating his ascent to leadership.
