What is China's Modernization and its problems after Mao?

After Mao's death, China pursued modernization in agriculture, industry, science and technology, and defense. Economic reforms increased production, encouraged private enterprise, expanded foreign trade, and improved living standards for many people. However, modernization also created problems such as growing inequality between regions and social groups, corruption, unemployment in some sectors, pollution, rapid urbanization, and tension between economic freedom and continued one-party political control.

Q1. Who were the two protégés of Deng Xiaoping mentioned as pushing ahead with modernization?

A. Hu Yaobang
B. Zhao Ziyang
C. Both Hu Yaobang and Zhao Ziyang ✅
D. Neither

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Both Hu Yaobang and Zhao Ziyang were identified as Deng's close collaborators who championed rapid modernization. Their combined political influence helped steer reforms after the Democracy Wall episode, emphasizing the importance of continuity in leadership while introducing new economic policies.

Q2. By what percentage did industrial production increase in Sichuan province under Zhao Ziyang in 1979?

A. 0.5
B. 0.6
C. 0.7
D. 0.8 ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Zhao Ziyang's administration in Sichuan is credited with a remarkable surge in industrial output, achieving an increase of 80% in 1979. This dramatic growth illustrated the potential of pragmatic management and set a benchmark for other provinces, reinforcing the government's confidence in market‑oriented reforms.

Q3. In which year did Zhao Ziyang announce the abandonment of compulsory state purchase of crops?

A. 1982
B. 1983
C. 1984 ✅
D. 1985

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The policy shift occurred in December 1984 when Zhao Ziyang declared that the state would no longer compel farmers to sell all their produce. This change allowed peasants to retain a larger share of market profits, signaling a move toward greater economic autonomy and the early stages of market socialism.

Q4. If imports rose by 38% while exports rose by 10% in 1984, which policy would most directly curb the import surge?

A. Reduce export taxes
B. Impose heavy duties on non‑essential imports ✅
C. Increase domestic production of staples
D. Lower the price of imported raw materials

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Imposing steep duties on non‑essential imports directly raises their cost, discouraging excessive foreign purchases. By targeting items like cars and televisions, the government could protect scarce foreign exchange reserves while still allowing vital raw materials and microchip equipment to enter the market.

Q5. A consumer bought a basket of goods for ¥100 in 1985. With the annual inflation rate of 22% in 1986, what would be the approximate cost of the same basket in 1986?

A. ¥122 ✅
B. ¥120
C. ¥132
D. ¥140

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Inflation erodes purchasing power, so a 22% rise means the original ¥100 price would increase by 0.22 × 100 = ¥22. Adding this to the base amount yields ¥122. This calculation demonstrates how rapidly household expenses could climb during the mid‑1980s, contributing to broader economic concerns.

Q6. If a peasant household earned a profit of ¥100 from surplus grain and was allowed to keep 70% of it, how much profit does the household retain?

A. ¥50
B. ¥60
C. ¥70 ✅
D. ¥80

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Keeping 70% of a ¥100 profit translates to 0.70 × 100 = ¥70. This retention policy was designed to incentivize higher agricultural output by allowing farmers to benefit directly from market sales, thereby linking personal gain to national production targets.

Q7. What duty rate was applied to imported cars to discourage excessive imports?

A. 80% ✅
B. 70%
C. 60%
D. 50%

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The government levied an 80% duty on imported automobiles, dramatically raising their price for Chinese consumers. This high tariff aimed to limit the outflow of foreign exchange, protect nascent domestic manufacturers, and curb the surge in imports that threatened the nation’s balance of payments.

Q8. Which of the following best explains why the shift to market socialism contributed to rising inflation in the mid‑1980s?

A. Reduced state control lowered production costs, causing price drops
B. Higher export earnings increased domestic purchasing power
C. Rapid import growth and a widening trade deficit increased demand for foreign currency, pressuring prices upward ✅
D. Strict price controls on staple foods eliminated inflationary pressures

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The rapid expansion of imports, combined with a substantial trade deficit, strained foreign‑exchange reserves. To obtain needed foreign currency, the government often printed more money, which, together with higher demand for goods, pushed overall price levels upward, resulting in the observed inflation.

Q10. Which categories of imported goods were exempt from the heavy duties imposed by the Chinese government in the 1980s?

A. Consumer electronics
B. Luxury clothing
C. Agricultural machinery
D. Vital raw materials and microchip equipment ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The policy spared essential inputs such as raw materials and microchip equipment from the punitive tariffs. By allowing these strategic imports at lower cost, the government ensured that key industrial sectors could continue to develop while still restricting discretionary consumer goods that drained foreign exchange.

Q11. What was the annual inflation rate recorded in China in 1986?

A. 22% ✅
B. 15%
C. 10%
D. 30%

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Official statistics indicate that inflation surged to 22% in 1986, reflecting the combined effects of rapid import growth, trade deficits, and the loosening of price controls. This high rate strained household budgets and signaled the need for tighter macro‑economic management amid ongoing reforms.

Q12. Allowing price fluctuations for surplus grain while keeping staple product prices stable primarily aimed to achieve which economic outcome?

A. Eliminate all market signals
B. Encourage efficient allocation of resources and incentivize higher production ✅
C. Reduce government revenue
D. Increase state control over agriculture

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By permitting market‑driven price changes for surplus grain, the government sought to let supply and demand determine returns, motivating farmers to increase output. Simultaneously, stabilizing staple prices protected consumers from volatility, balancing incentives with social welfare considerations.

Q13. If the market price of pork rose by 30% and a family previously spent ¥200 on pork, what is the new expenditure on pork?

A. ¥210
B. ¥230
C. ¥250
D. ¥260 ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A 30% increase means the cost rises by 0.30 × 200 = ¥60. Adding this to the original ¥200 results in a new total of ¥260. This example illustrates how commodity price spikes can quickly amplify household expenses, especially when essential foods become more expensive.

Q14. According to the reforms, who retained legal ownership of land after it was divided among peasant households?

A. The state ✅
B. Collective farms
C. Individual households
D. Local governments

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Although land parcels were allocated to individual households for cultivation, the underlying title remained with the state. This arrangement preserved central authority over land while granting peasants practical control, reflecting the hybrid nature of the reforms that blended socialist ownership with market‑oriented incentives.

Q15. Considering rising domestic demand, what is a likely long‑term effect of maintaining an 80% duty on imported cars?

A. Domestic car manufacturers will become globally competitive
B. Consumers will switch to cheaper foreign models
C. Black‑market imports may increase, undermining the duty’s effectiveness ✅
D. The duty will have no impact on the auto market

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Sustained high tariffs can encourage smuggling and the growth of informal markets, as consumers seek affordable alternatives. While the duty protects domestic producers in the short term, persistent demand may erode its effectiveness, leading to illegal channels that bypass official controls and reduce fiscal revenues.
