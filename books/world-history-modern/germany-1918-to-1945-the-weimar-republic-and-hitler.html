About 14. Germany (1918 to 1945): The Weimar Republic and Hitler

The Weimar Republic was Germany’s democratic experiment born from defeat in WWI, plagued from the start by political extremism, economic crises like hyperinflation and depression, and widespread resentment toward the Versailles Treaty. Despite cultural flourishing and constitutional innovations, the republic never gained full legitimacy and collapsed under pressure as voters turned to radical parties promising solutions.

Adolf Hitler and the Nazis exploited these weaknesses through propaganda, intimidation, and legal manipulation to seize power in 1933, swiftly establishing a totalitarian dictatorship. Their racist ideology, rearmament, and expansionist ambitions led directly to the Holocaust and World War II, representing the catastrophic failure of democracy and the ultimate warning against unchecked authoritarianism.
