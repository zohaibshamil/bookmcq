What is Nuclear arms race Cold War?

Both superpowers engaged in a dangerous competition to develop more powerful nuclear weapons and delivery systems, creating a balance of terror where neither side could attack without facing devastating retaliation.

📝 All Nuclear arms race Cold War MCQs

Q1. In which year was the Strategic Arms Limitation Treaty (SALT I) signed?

A. 1970
B. 1972 ✅
C. 1974
D. 1976

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The SALT I agreement was concluded in 1972, marking a milestone in superpower negotiations that sought to cap strategic offensive arms and reduce the risk of nuclear confrontation during a period of détente.

Q2. Which country did the United States assist in rebuilding its economy after World War II, eventually becoming a key anti‑communist ally?

A. Japan ✅
B. Germany
C. Italy
D. France

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

After World War II, the United States provided extensive economic aid and technical assistance to Japan, helping it recover rapidly and become a steadfast partner in the Western anti‑communist bloc throughout the Cold War.

Q3. The term 'détente' in the Cold War context primarily refers to:

A. Escalation of nuclear arms
B. A period of reduced tensions ✅
C. The division of Germany
D. The Korean War

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Détente describes the easing of geopolitical tensions between the United States and the Soviet Union, especially during the 1970s, when both sides pursued arms control agreements and diplomatic dialogue to avoid direct confrontation.

Q4. Which Soviet leader died in 1953, leading to a shift toward 'peaceful coexistence'?

A. Khrushchev
B. Stalin ✅
C. Brezhnev
D. Gorbachev

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Joseph Stalin's death in 1953 opened the way for new Soviet leadership that promoted a less confrontational stance, famously termed “peaceful coexistence,” to alleviate economic and military pressures on the USSR.

Q5. Which event in 1962 brought the world closest to nuclear war?

📖 Explanation:

The Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962 placed the United States and the Soviet Union on the brink of nuclear exchange after American reconnaissance discovered Soviet ballistic missiles stationed in Cuba.

Q7. The first nuclear weapons used in combat were dropped on which country?

Q8. What was the primary purpose of the Marshall Plan?

The Marshall Plan supplied extensive financial assistance to European nations devastated by World War II, fostering economic recovery and strengthening democratic governments against Soviet influence.

Q9. Scenario: After the 1955 Austrian State Treaty, both superpowers agreed to withdraw troops. If each side had deployed 30,000 soldiers, how many soldiers in total were removed from Austria?

A. \(30,000\)
B. \(45,000\)
C. \(60,000\) ✅
D. \(90,000\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Each side withdrew 30,000 troops, so the combined total of soldiers removed from Austria was 30,000 + 30,000 = \(60,000\). This joint withdrawal symbolized a rare moment of cooperation amid Cold War rivalry.

Q10. Scenario: In 1950 the United States deployed 300 strategic bombers, each armed with a single nuclear weapon, while the Soviet Union had only 120 bombers. Given this disparity, which factor most directly motivated the United States to further expand its nuclear arsenal?

A. Desire for scientific prestige
B. Fear of Soviet conventional superiority ✅
C. Need to protect European colonies
D. Competition for space exploration

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

American policymakers feared that Soviet conventional forces, though numerically smaller, could threaten Western Europe, prompting a strategic push to enlarge the nuclear stockpile as a deterrent against any potential Soviet aggression.

Q11. Which doctrine asserted that the United States would intervene to contain communism worldwide?

A. Truman Doctrine ✅
B. Eisenhower Doctrine
C. Reagan Doctrine
D. Nixon Doctrine

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Truman Doctrine, announced in 1947, pledged U.S. support for nations resisting communist subversion, establishing a policy of global containment that shaped American foreign strategy throughout the Cold War.

Q12. Scenario: In 1962, the United States discovered 10 Soviet missile sites in Cuba, each capable of launching a warhead of \(1\) megaton. If the U.S. could neutralize each site with a single strike, how many megatons could be eliminated?

A. 5
B. 10 ✅
C. 15
D. 20

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each of the ten sites could deliver a \(1\)‑megaton warhead, so a successful strike on all sites would remove a total of 10 × \(1\) = 10 megaton of potential firepower, dramatically reducing the immediate nuclear threat to the United States.

A. Collectivization
B. Peaceful coexistence ✅
C. Perestroika

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Soviet leadership promoted “peaceful coexistence” to ease the financial and industrial burdens of an endless arms race, hoping to shift resources toward domestic development while maintaining a credible deterrent.

Q16. Which treaty limited the number of strategic ballistic missile launchers for the United States and USSR?

A. SALT I ✅
B. START I
C. INF Treaty
D. New START

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The 1972 SALT I agreement imposed limits on the deployment of strategic ballistic missile launchers, representing the first major bilateral effort to control the most destabilizing elements of the nuclear arsenals of the two superpowers.

Q17. Scenario: By 1972, the United States possessed 1,000 strategic bombers each carrying \(2\) nuclear warheads. If the SALT I treaty limited the total warheads to 1,500, how many bombers would need to be de‑armed?

A. 250 ✅
B. 500
C. 750
D. 1000

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each bomber carried two warheads, so 1,000 bombers equaled 2,000 warheads. To meet the 1,500‑warhead limit, 500 warheads must be removed, which corresponds to 250 bombers (500 ÷ 2). Thus, 250 bombers would be taken out of service.

Q18. How did the presence of nuclear weapons influence the United Nations' ability to enforce peace during the Cold War?

A. It increased UN authority
B. It caused paralysis due to veto power ✅
C. It had no effect
D. It led to UN disbanding

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Because both superpowers possessed nuclear arsenals, each could veto Security Council resolutions that might undermine its strategic interests, often leading to stalemate and limiting the UN’s capacity to intervene decisively in conflicts.

Q19. Which of the following was a direct result of the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in 1979?

A. End of détente ✅
B. Creation of NATO
C. Collapse of the USSR
D. Start of the Korean War

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The 1979 Soviet incursion into Afghanistan ended the period of détente, provoking a renewed Cold War confrontation, heightened U.S. support for anti‑Soviet forces, and a series of retaliatory measures such as the boycott of the 1980 Moscow Olympics.

Q21. Which policy aimed to limit the deployment of intermediate‑range nuclear missiles in Europe?

A. INF Treaty ✅
B. SALT II
C. ABM Treaty
D. START II

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The 1987 Intermediate‑Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty eliminated an entire class of ground‑launched missiles with ranges between 500 and 5,500 km, thereby reducing the nuclear threat on both sides of the European theater.

Q22. Scenario: During the Cuban Missile Crisis, the U.S. naval blockade covered a radius of \(150\) nautical miles around Cuba. If a Soviet ship traveling at \(20\) knots entered the zone, how many hours would it take to cross the blocked area?

A. 5
B. 7.5
C. 10
D. 15 ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The blocked zone spans a diameter of 300 nautical miles (2 × 150). At 20 knots, the ship would travel 300 nm ÷ 20 kn = 15 hours to traverse the entire zone.

Q23. Assess why the development of MIRVs (Multiple Independently targetable Reentry Vehicles) in the 1960s escalated the arms race, despite existing treaty limits.

A. They reduced cost
B. They increased target flexibility
C. They allowed more warheads per missile circumventing caps ✅
D. They were purely defensive

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

MIRVs enabled a single delivery system to carry several warheads, each capable of striking different targets, effectively bypassing quantitative limits set by treaties and prompting both sides to expand their arsenals to maintain parity.

Q24. Scenario: If the USSR allocated 12% of its GDP to defense in 1965, and its GDP was \(500\) billion rubles, how much was spent on defense?

A. \(30\) billion
B. \(50\) billion
C. \(60\) billion ✅
D. \(120\) billion

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Twelve percent of \(500\) billion rubles equals 0.12 × \(500\) = \(60\) billion rubles, representing a substantial portion of the Soviet economy devoted to sustaining its military and nuclear programs.

Q25. Which strategic concept justified the United States' policy of 'massive retaliation' after 1950?

B. Credible threat of overwhelming force ✅
C. Mutual assured destruction
D. Flexible response

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Massive retaliation rested on the premise that the United States would respond to any Soviet aggression with an overwhelming nuclear strike, thereby deterring adversaries by projecting an unbeatable level of force.

Q27. Scenario: By 1985, the United States possessed \(10{,}000\) nuclear warheads, while the USSR had \(12{,}000\). If each side reduced its arsenal by 15%, how many warheads would each retain?

A. 8,500 and 10,200 ✅
B. 9,000 and 11,000
C. 7,500 and 9,500
D. 8,500 and 11,000

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A 15 % reduction of 10,000 yields 10,000 × 0.85 = 8,500 warheads for the United States; a 15 % reduction of 12,000 yields 12,000 × 0.85 = 10,200 warheads for the USSR, matching option A.
