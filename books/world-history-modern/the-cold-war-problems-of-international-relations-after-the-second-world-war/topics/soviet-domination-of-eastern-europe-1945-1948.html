Q1. When did the USSR remove occupying troops from Austria?

🔹 A. 1949
🔹 B. 1950
🔹 C. \(1955\)
🔹 D. 1960

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Austrian State Treaty of \(1955\) stipulated that all Allied occupation forces withdraw, restoring Austria’s full sovereignty and neutrality. This withdrawal marked a rare moment of cooperation between East and West during the early Cold War, signaling a modest easing of tensions in Central Europe.

Q2. Which of the following was NOT a communist state in Eastern Europe after WWII?

🔹 A. Poland
🔹 B. Hungary
🔹 C. Italy
🔹 D. Czechoslovakia

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Italy retained a democratic government and a market economy after the war, unlike its neighbors Poland, Hungary, and Czechoslovakia, which fell under Soviet‑installed communist regimes. Italy’s Western alignment meant it was excluded from the Eastern Bloc, illustrating the geographic limits of Soviet expansion.

Q3. In which year did the Soviet Union collapse?

🔹 A. \(1985\)
🔹 B. \(1987\)
🔹 C. \(1991\)
🔹 D. \(1993\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The formal dissolution of the USSR occurred in December \(1991\), ending the Cold War era. Economic stagnation, nationalist movements, and political reforms under Gorbachev accelerated the breakup, leading to the emergence of fifteen independent republics and a profound shift in global power structures.

Q4. What was the name of the policy aimed at reducing Cold War tensions in the 1970s?

🔹 A. Containment
🔹 B. Détente
🔹 C. Brinkmanship
🔹 D. Domino Theory

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Détente described the series of diplomatic initiatives, arms control agreements, and mutual concessions between the United States and the Soviet Union during the 1970s. It sought to curb the risk of nuclear confrontation and foster limited cooperation, though underlying rivalry persisted throughout the decade.

Q5. Which treaty, signed in \(1972\), limited the number of strategic nuclear weapons?

🔹 A. INF Treaty
🔹 B. START I
🔹 C. SALT I
🔹 D. NPT

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Strategic Arms Limitation Talks (SALT I) culminated in the 1972 agreement, capping the number of intercontinental ballistic missiles and submarine‑launched ballistic missiles each superpower could possess. This landmark accord demonstrated a mutual willingness to restrain the arms race while maintaining deterrence.

Q6. The Soviet strategy of “peaceful coexistence” after Stalin’s death was primarily intended to address what domestic issue?

🔹 A. Economic stagnation
🔹 B. Military overextension
🔹 C. Agricultural crisis
🔹 D. Political dissent

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

After Stalin’s death in \(1953\), the USSR faced severe economic strain from rapid industrialization and military expenditures. By promoting peaceful coexistence, Soviet leaders hoped to reduce external pressures, allowing resources to be redirected toward domestic recovery and modernization.

Q7. The frontier dispute between Poland and Germany after WWII centered on which river?

🔹 A. Vistula
🔹 B. Oder
🔹 C. Danube
🔹 D. Elbe

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Oder River, together with the Neisse tributary, formed the Oder‑Neisse line that defined the post‑war border. This demarcation resulted in significant population transfers and remains a symbolic point of contention, reflecting the broader territorial adjustments imposed by the Allies.

Q8. US aid to Turkey in the late 1940s was mainly intended to:

🔹 A. Promote democracy
🔹 B. Counter Soviet influence
🔹 C. Support economic development
🔹 D. Facilitate NATO membership

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Providing military and economic assistance to Turkey helped secure the southeastern flank of the emerging Western alliance. By bolstering Turkey’s capacity to resist Soviet pressure, the United States reinforced its containment strategy and laid groundwork for Turkey’s eventual NATO entry.

Q9. Which event marked the first major escalation of Cold War tensions in the 1960s?

🔹 A. Berlin Blockade
🔹 B. Korean War
🔹 C. Cuban Missile Crisis
🔹 D. Vietnam War

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

The Cuban Missile Crisis of \(1962\) brought the United States and the Soviet Union to the brink of nuclear war when Soviet missiles were discovered in Cuba. The intense diplomatic standoff and subsequent agreement to remove the missiles exemplify the heightened peril of the early 1960s.

🔹 A. Containment
🔹 B. Isolationism
🔹 C. Detente
🔹 D. Appeasement

📖 Explanation:

The triumph of Chinese communists heightened American fears of a worldwide domino effect, prompting the U.S. to intensify its containment policy. This strategy aimed to prevent further communist expansion through diplomatic, economic, and military measures across Asia and beyond.
