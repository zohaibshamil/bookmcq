📖 Conflict in the Middle East

Middle East Conflict: Arab-Israeli Wars and Regional Rivalries

Since the establishment of Israel in 1948, the Middle East has experienced repeated Arab-Israeli wars, regional rivalries, foreign intervention, and competing nationalist movements. Pan-Arabism, disputes over Palestine, territorial conflicts, and strategic control of resources shaped regional politics. The Suez Crisis, wars of 1967 and 1973, and subsequent peace negotiations transformed diplomatic relationships. Meanwhile, civil war in Lebanon, the Iran-Iraq War, and the Gulf War expanded regional instability. The Oslo process offered prospects for Israeli-Palestinian reconciliation but left fundamental political disputes unresolved.

Background: Ottoman Collapse, Balfour Declaration and Palestine

Students should understand the collapse of the Ottoman Empire, the establishment of the British Mandate in Palestine, the Balfour Declaration of 1917, and the growth of Zionism and Arab nationalism. Knowledge of the United Nations Partition Plan of 1947, the establishment of Israel in May 1948, and the Palestinian displacement known as the Nakba is essential. Familiarity with postwar British and French withdrawal, Cold War competition between the United States and Soviet Union, the rise of Egyptian nationalism under Gamal Abdel Nasser, and the strategic importance of Middle Eastern oil provides important historical context.

Learning Outcomes: Pan-Arabism, Wars and Regional Interventions

Students should be able to explain the development of Pan-Arabism, analyse the causes and consequences of the major Arab-Israeli wars, and compare the political and territorial outcomes of 1948, 1956, 1967, and 1973. They should assess foreign intervention, explain the origins and consequences of the Lebanese Civil War and Iran-Iraq War, and analyse Iraq's invasion of Kuwait. They should also evaluate the Camp David Accords, Oslo agreements, Palestinian self-government, the unresolved Jerusalem question, and the difficulties of achieving lasting peace.

Topics: Suez, the 1967 and 1973 Wars, Lebanon and Gulf Conflicts

  • Arab Nationalism and the 1948 War - Pan-Arabism, foreign intervention, Israel's establishment, and Palestinian displacement.
  • Suez Crisis and Arab-Israeli Wars, 1956–1973 - Nasser, Suez invasion, Six-Day War, and Yom Kippur War.
  • Regional Rivalries and Lebanese Conflicts, 1975–2006 - Lebanese Civil War, Taif Agreement, Israeli interventions, and Hezbollah.
  • Iran-Iraq War and Gulf Conflict, 1980–1991 - Territorial disputes, regional ambitions, Kuwait's invasion, and international coalition.
  • Egyptian-Israeli Peace Process, 1977–1982 - Sadat's diplomacy, Camp David negotiations, peace treaty, and Sinai withdrawal.
  • Oslo Agreements and Palestinian Self-Government, 1993–1995 - PLO-Israeli negotiations, Palestinian Authority, and unresolved final-status questions.
  • Failed Peace Initiatives and Renewed Tensions - Jerusalem disputes, Second Intifada, post-Gulf War diplomacy, and Road Map proposals.

Study Sequence: From Israel's Establishment to Regional Peace Agreements

Begin with Arab nationalism, Pan-Arabism, foreign intervention, and the political circumstances surrounding the establishment of Israel in 1948. Examine the first Arab-Israeli War and its territorial and demographic consequences. Continue with the Suez Crisis, Six-Day War, and Yom Kippur War to understand changing regional power relationships. Study the rise of Egyptian-Israeli diplomacy and the Camp David agreements before examining conflicts in Lebanon and the Iran-Iraq War. Continue with Iraq's invasion of Kuwait and the 1991 Gulf War. Finish with the Oslo negotiations, Palestinian self-government, Jerusalem disputes, the Second Intifada, and subsequent peace proposals.

How Arab-Israeli Disputes Intersected with Regional Wars

The establishment of Israel and the unresolved Palestinian question contributed to repeated Arab-Israeli confrontations. The 1967 war transformed territorial disputes, while the 1973 war encouraged diplomatic negotiations and produced the Camp David agreements. Regional rivalries also fuelled conflicts in Lebanon and between Iran and Iraq. The Gulf War changed international diplomacy, creating conditions for the Madrid Conference and Oslo process, but unresolved territorial and political questions prevented a comprehensive settlement.

Timeline: Yom Kippur War, Camp David and Later Conflicts

On 6 October 1973, Egyptian and Syrian forces launched coordinated attacks against Israeli positions in Sinai and the Golan Heights. The fighting ended following United Nations ceasefire initiatives and Resolution 338. Egyptian President Anwar Sadat subsequently visited Jerusalem in November 1977 to pursue negotiations. On 17 September 1978, Sadat and Israeli Prime Minister Menachem Begin signed the Camp David Accords with Jimmy Carter's mediation. The Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty followed on 26 March 1979, leading to Israel's completed withdrawal from Sinai in April 1982.

Why Middle Eastern Conflicts Shaped International Relations

Middle Eastern conflicts shaped international diplomacy, petroleum supplies, military alliances, and relations between major powers. Understanding the Arab-Israeli wars, regional interventions, and peace negotiations explains continuing disputes over territory, sovereignty, and Palestinian self-determination. These developments also provide essential background for understanding the Gulf conflicts, regional security arrangements, and subsequent international peace initiatives.

Common Misconceptions: Camp David, Oslo and Palestinian Statehood

Students sometimes claim that 'the 1978 Camp David Accords and 1979 peace treaty were the same document'; the former established a negotiating framework, while the latter formally ended the state of war between Egypt and Israel. 'The Oslo Accords created an independent Palestinian state' is incorrect because they established interim self-government arrangements. Finally, 'Ariel Sharon's 2000 visit to Jerusalem caused the 1991 Gulf War' reverses the chronology; his visit preceded the Second Intifada, while the Gulf War occurred nine years earlier.

Exam Focus: Arab-Israeli Wars, Agreements and the Gulf War

Frequently examined topics include arranging the Arab-Israeli wars chronologically, identifying the territorial consequences of 1967, and explaining the causes of the Suez Crisis. Students should compare the Camp David Accords with the Oslo agreements, identify the purposes of the Taif Agreement, and explain Iraq's invasion of Kuwait. Analytical questions examine Pan-Arabism, foreign intervention, the failure of Palestinian negotiations, the Jerusalem dispute, and the difficulties of achieving regional peace.

Summary: Nationalism, Territorial Conflict and Diplomacy

Arab nationalism, the establishment of Israel, and competing territorial claims produced repeated regional confrontations. Military conflicts altered borders and encouraged changing diplomatic alliances, while foreign intervention expanded the international importance of regional disputes. Egyptian-Israeli peace demonstrated the possibility of negotiated settlements, but Lebanon, Iran-Iraq, and Gulf conflicts sustained instability. The Oslo process introduced Palestinian self-government without resolving fundamental political disagreements, leaving the wider peace process incomplete.

Key Terms, Political Leaders and Regional Movements

  • Pan-Arabism - A political movement promoting Arab unity, cooperation, and common cultural and political identity.
  • Nakba - The Palestinian catastrophe associated with mass displacement and dispossession during the 1948 Arab-Israeli War.
  • Gamal Abdel Nasser - Egyptian president who promoted Pan-Arabism, nationalised the Suez Canal, and became a leading regional figure.
  • Six-Day War - The June 1967 conflict in which Israel captured Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and the Golan Heights.
  • Yom Kippur War - The October 1973 war launched by Egypt and Syria to recover territories occupied by Israel in 1967.
  • Camp David Accords - The September 1978 agreements that established a framework for peace between Egypt and Israel.
  • Taif Agreement - The 1989 Lebanese political settlement that reorganised power-sharing arrangements and provided a framework for ending the civil war.
  • Oslo Accords - The 1993 and subsequent agreements establishing an interim framework for Israeli-Palestinian negotiations and Palestinian self-government.

UN Palestine Records and Middle East Peace Agreements

  • United Nations Question of Palestine Archives - Examine Resolution 181, Resolution 194, Resolution 242, Resolution 338, Palestinian displacement records, and original diplomatic documents concerning the Arab-Israeli conflict.
  • Camp David Accords and Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty - Study the original agreements of 17 September 1978 and 26 March 1979, together with correspondence and diplomatic records available through the Jimmy Carter Presidential Library.
  • Oslo Accords Primary Documents - Compare the 1993 Declaration of Principles, 1995 Interim Agreement, and subsequent negotiations concerning Jerusalem, borders, settlements, refugees, and Palestinian self-government.
  • United Nations Security Council Records - Investigate Resolution 242, Resolution 338, Resolution 598, and Resolution 1701 concerning territorial disputes, the Iran-Iraq War, and the 2006 Lebanon conflict.
  • Iran-Iraq War Historical Archives - Explore Iraqi and Iranian diplomatic correspondence, United Nations investigations, battlefield records, and the implementation of Security Council Resolution 598 through the United Nations and Encyclopaedia Iranica.
  • Gulf War Diplomatic Records - Examine Iraq's invasion of Kuwait, Security Council Resolution 678, Operation Desert Storm, and the international coalition through the US Department of State Office of the Historian and National Archives.
  • Lebanese Civil War and Taif Agreement Archives - Research the original 1989 agreement, Lebanese political power-sharing arrangements, Syrian and Israeli interventions, and the 2006 conflict through UN records and the International Center for Transitional Justice.
  • Middle East Peace Process and Road Map - Investigate the 1991 Madrid Conference, Second Intifada, Quartet Road Map of 2003, and subsequent diplomatic initiatives through the UN and official diplomatic archives.
