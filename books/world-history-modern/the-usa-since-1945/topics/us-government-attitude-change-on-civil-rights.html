What is US government attitude change on civil rights?

The US government shifted from passive tolerance of segregation to active enforcement of racial equality through Supreme Court rulings like Brown v. Board of Education, federal deployment to protect protesters, and landmark legislation such as the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and Voting Rights Act of 1965.

Q1. Who appointed the committee to investigate civil rights in 1946?

A. President Harry S. Truman ✅
B. President Franklin D. Roosevelt
C. President Dwight D. Eisenhower
D. President John F. Kennedy

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is President Harry S. Truman because he created the President’s Committee on Civil Rights in 1946, marking a federal shift toward racial equality. Roosevelt had died the previous year, so he could not have appointed the committee; Eisenhower was not yet president; Kennedy entered office much later. Recognizing Truman’s role illustrates how an executive decision can launch nationwide reforms, a pattern still seen in modern government‑led equity initiatives.

Q2. Which Supreme Court decision declared separate schools for blacks and whites unconstitutional?

A. Brown v. Board of Education ✅
B. Plessy v. Ferguson
C. Roe v. Wade
D. Miranda v. Arizona

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Brown v. Board of Education is the correct choice because the 1954 ruling expressly held that state‑enforced segregation in public schools violated the Equal Protection Clause. Plessy v. Ferguson upheld “separate but equal” and therefore does not fit; Roe v. Wade concerns abortion rights; Miranda v. Arizona deals with police interrogations. Knowing this case shows how judicial action can overturn discriminatory practices, a principle that continues to shape contemporary civil‑rights litigation.

Q3. Why did the United States seek civil‑rights reforms during the early Cold War era?

A. To improve domestic morale only
B. To counter Soviet propaganda highlighting American racism ✅
C. To appease Southern politicians
D. To reduce military spending

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The United States pursued civil‑rights reforms partly to deny the Soviet Union a moral weapon; the USSR pointed to American racial discrimination as evidence of hypocrisy. Enhancing civil rights reduced that criticism. Improving morale was a secondary benefit, not the primary driver; Southern politicians often resisted change, and reforms did not directly cut military budgets. This context explains why governments today may adjust internal policies to bolster international credibility.

Q7. How did adding Black citizens to juries affect the American legal system?

A. It eliminated all bias in trials
B. It increased representation and fairness in verdicts
C. It slowed down court proceedings ✅
D. It had no impact on case outcomes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Including Black jurors improved representation, making verdicts more likely to reflect community diversity and enhancing perceived fairness. It did not erase all bias, nor did it guarantee faster trials; the process could lengthen deliberations as jurors adjusted to new dynamics. Dismissing any impact ignores the constitutional principle of a jury of one’s peers. This change illustrates how procedural reforms can gradually improve justice, a lesson applied in current jury‑selection reforms.

This demonstrates how international rivalry can drive internal social change, a pattern observable in contemporary geopolitical contexts.

Q15. When the Supreme Court bans school segregation, what immediate legal change occurs?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ruling obliges public schools to integrate, ensuring that students of all races attend the same institutions. It does not force private schools to shut down, nor does it allow states to maintain separate systems; rather, it overrides state laws that permitted segregation. Teacher salaries are unrelated to the decision. This illustrates how a landmark ruling can directly reshape public education policy, a precedent still cited in current civil‑rights litigation.

Q19. A Supreme Court case challenges the exclusion of Black jurors. Which constitutional principle is at stake?

Recognizing this principle underscores how representation in juries upholds constitutional fairness, a concept that continues to guide modern judicial review.
