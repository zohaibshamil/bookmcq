What is Kennedy New Frontier social policies?

President John F. Kennedy’s New Frontier proposed domestic reforms including federal aid for education, medical care for the elderly, and urban renewal to combat poverty and stimulate the economy, but many proposals stalled in Congress until after his assassination.

Q1. In which year did John F. Kennedy assume the presidency of the United States?

A. 1960
B. 1961 ✅
C. 1962
D. 1963

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

John F. Kennedy was sworn in on January 20, 1961, making 1961 the year he became president. The correct answer is B because 1961 marks the start of his administration, while the other years either precede or follow his inauguration. Understanding the inauguration date helps place Kennedy’s policies in historical context, linking his actions on poverty and civil rights to the early 1960s rather than later decades.

Q2. Which executive action created the Peace Corps during Kennedy's administration?

A. Civil Rights Act
B. Economic Opportunity Act
C. Peace Corps Act ✅
D. Social Security Amendments

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Peace Corps was created by Executive Order 10924, issued by President Kennedy in March 1961. Option C is correct because the Peace Corps Act specifically refers to that order; the other options refer to unrelated legislation. Knowing the origin of the Peace Corps clarifies how Kennedy used volunteer service to project American values abroad and to address domestic concerns about youth unemployment, linking foreign service with home‑front policy goals.

Q12. A researcher wants to compare poverty rates before and after the Economic Opportunity Act of 1964. Which statistical test is most appropriate for comparing the two means?

A. Regression analysis
B. Chi‑square test
C. t‑test for independent samples ✅
D. ANOVA

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Comparing two means—poverty rates before and after the Economic Opportunity Act—calls for a t‑test for independent samples, which assesses whether the difference is statistically significant. Option C is correct; regression analysis examines relationships, chi‑square tests categorical data, and ANOVA compares more than two groups. Selecting the appropriate test ensures valid conclusions about policy impact, linking statistical methodology to the evaluation of Kennedy’s anti‑poverty legislation.
