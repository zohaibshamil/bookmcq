What is Eisenhower presidency social policies?

President Dwight D. Eisenhower maintained a moderate approach to social policy by expanding Social Security coverage, creating the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare, and supporting the Interstate Highway System to boost economic growth while avoiding large-scale new welfare programs.

Q1. In which year did Dwight D. Eisenhower assume the presidency of the United States?

A.1951
B.1953 ✅
C.1955
D.1957
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is 1953 because Eisenhower was inaugurated on January 20, 1953 after winning the 1952 election. The other years are incorrect: 1951 predates the election, 1955 and 1957 refer to later periods when Eisenhower was already in office or when his successor took over. Knowing the exact start year helps place his domestic policies, such as infrastructure projects, in the correct historical timeline, which is essential for understanding the era's economic context.

Q2. What was the unemployment rate in the United States in 1960 expressed as a percentage of the labor force?

A.0.05
B.0.06
C.0.07 ✅
D.0.08
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is 7 percent because official labor statistics show unemployment reaching roughly 5.5 million workers, about 7 % of the labor force, in 1960. The 5 % and 6 % options underestimate the true level, while 8 % exaggerates it. Understanding this rate is crucial since it demonstrates that high industrial output coexisted with notable joblessness, underscoring the need for policies that address employment gaps even in prosperous economies.

Q3. What was the primary purpose of the Fair Deal program proposed by President Truman?

A.Expand military spending
B.Continue New Deal reforms ✅
C.Reduce taxes on corporations
D.Promote isolationist foreign policy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Fair Deal aimed to extend and modernize the New Deal, so option B is correct. It sought higher minimum wages, social security expansions, and health initiatives, reflecting Truman's desire to build on Roosevelt's legacy. Options A, C, and D mischaracterize the program: it did not focus on military expansion, corporate tax cuts, or isolationism. Recognizing the Fair Deal's continuity with the New Deal helps explain why many of its components later became law and why opposition centered on new social proposals.

Q4. How did the Taft–Hartley Act of 1947 affect labor unions in the United States?

A.It limited union powers ✅
B.It granted unions the right to strike unlimitedly
C.It increased union membership automatically
D.It eliminated collective bargaining
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Taft–Hartley Act limited union powers, making A the correct answer. It prohibited secondary boycotts, required union leaders to affirm they were not Communists, and gave the president authority to intervene in strikes, thereby curbing union influence. Options B and C are opposite of the act's intent, while D overstates its effect by suggesting collective bargaining was abolished, which was not the case. Understanding these restrictions clarifies why many workers felt their interests were unprotected, influencing later labor‑policy debates.

Q5. Why did many Southern Democrats oppose Truman's Fair Deal proposals?

A.They opposed higher taxes
B.They disapproved of his support for black civil rights ✅
C.They wanted more government spending on agriculture
D.They favored isolationist foreign policy
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Southern Democrats primarily opposed Truman because he championed civil‑rights measures, so B is correct. Their resistance stemmed from fears that expanding rights for African Americans would disrupt the segregationist status quo in the South. Options A, C, and D do not capture the core political tension; while tax and agricultural concerns existed, the decisive factor was civil‑rights opposition. Recognizing this motive explains the regional split within the Democratic Party and why certain New Deal‑style reforms faced stiff resistance.

Q6. How did the lack of a national health system contribute to poverty among Americans over 65 in the 1960s?

A.It increased taxes on the young
B.It forced older adults to pay out‑of‑pocket medical costs ✅
C.It reduced employment opportunities
D.It led to higher inflation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The absence of a national health system meant seniors often faced high out‑of‑pocket expenses, so B is correct. Without universal coverage, many retirees relied on limited private insurance or paid directly for care, draining limited incomes and pushing them below the poverty line. Options A, C, and D describe indirect or unrelated effects; taxes on younger workers, job scarcity, and inflation were not the primary pathways linking health policy to senior poverty. This link illustrates why health‑care reform became a central issue in later welfare legislation.

Q7. If the U.S. population in 1966 was approximately 200 million, and 30 million were living below the poverty line, what percentage of the total population was impoverished?

A.0.1 ✅
B.0.12
C.0.15
D.0.18
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Dividing the 30 million impoverished by the 200 million total yields \(30/200 = 0.15\), or 15 %, making C correct. Option A (10 %) and B (12 %) underestimate the share, while D (18 %) overstates it. Calculating this proportion helps illustrate the scale of poverty relative to the whole nation, showing that a sizable minority lived in hardship despite overall economic growth. Such quantitative reasoning is essential for policymakers assessing the impact of anti‑poverty programs.

Q8. Assume the unemployment rate rose from 5 % in 1955 to 7 % in 1960, and the labor force remained at 80 million. How many additional people were unemployed in 1960 compared to 1955?

A.1.6 million ✅
B.2.0 million
C.2.4 million
D.3.0 million
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: At 5 % unemployment, 0.05 × 80 million = 4 million workers were jobless; at 7 % it is 0.07 × 80 million = 5.6 million. The difference is 1.6 million, so A is correct. Options B, C, and D represent larger differences that do not match the arithmetic. This calculation demonstrates how modest percentage changes can translate into large absolute numbers, highlighting why even small shifts in unemployment rates demand significant policy responses.

Q9. A policy analyst argues that implementing a universal health care system in the 1950s could have reduced the number of elderly living in poverty by 20 %. Using the 1966 figure of 30 million impoverished Americans, estimate the potential reduction in individuals. Which figure is closest?

A.4 million
B.5 million
C.6 million ✅
D.7 million
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Twenty percent of 30 million equals \(0.20 \times 30 = 6\) million, so option C is correct. Options A and B underestimate the impact, while D overestimates it. This estimation shows how a sizable health‑care reform could have directly lifted millions out of poverty, illustrating the tangible benefits of universal coverage and reinforcing arguments for later programs like Medicare.

Q10. Suppose the minimum wage was raised from $0.75 in 1949 to $1.00 in 1955. If a full‑time worker earned 40 hours per week, calculate the increase in weekly earnings.

A.5
B.10 ✅
C.15
D.20
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The hourly increase is $1.00 − $0.75 = $0.25. Multiplying by 40 hours gives $0.25 × 40 = $10, so B is correct. Options A, C, and D either understate or overstate the arithmetic. Understanding this wage rise quantifies the real‑world effect of policy changes on household income, helping students see how modest legislative adjustments can meaningfully improve workers’ standards of living.

Q11. Consider that the Taft–Hartley Act reduced union membership growth by 2 % per year for five years. If there were 10 million union members in 1947, what would be the estimated membership in 1952?

A.8.5 million ✅
B.9.0 million
C.9.5 million
D.9.04 million
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Applying a 2 % annual decline over five years: \(10 \times (0.98)^5 \approx 10 \times 0.9039 = 9.04\) million, making D correct. Option A assumes a larger drop, B approximates a round figure but misses the precise calculation, and C is too high. This exercise demonstrates how modest yearly reductions compound, leading to a noticeable decline in union strength, which helps explain the political environment of the era.

A.0.005
B.0.01 ✅
C.0.015
D.0.02
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The unemployment increase is 2 percentage points; multiplying by the 0.5 % effect gives \(2 \times 0.5 = 1.0\) %, so B is correct. Options A and C represent half and three‑half of the calculated effect, while D doubles it, all inaccurate. This relationship underscores how labor market shifts can directly influence poverty levels, reinforcing the importance of employment‑focused policies during periods of economic growth.
