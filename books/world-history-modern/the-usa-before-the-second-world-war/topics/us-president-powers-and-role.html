What is US President powers and role?

The US President serves as the head of the executive branch and commander-in-chief of the armed forces, responsible for enforcing federal laws, conducting foreign policy, appointing officials, and vetoing or signing legislation passed by Congress.

Q7. According to the passage, after the President makes a treaty with a foreign state, what additional step is required for it to become binding?

A.Approval by the Supreme Court
B.Ratification by the House of Representatives
C.Consent of the state governors
D.Ratification by the Senate ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Although the passage mentions the President’s ability to make treaties, the U.S. constitutional framework requires that any treaty be ratified by a two‑thirds vote in the Senate to become legally binding. This check ensures that the legislative branch shares responsibility for international commitments. Hence, the correct answer is the Senate ratification option.

Q8. If the President vetoes a bill, what majority is needed in Congress to override the veto according to constitutional practice?

A.Simple majority
B.Three‑quarters majority
C.Two‑thirds majority ✅
D.Unanimous consent
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Constitution provides that Congress can override a presidential veto only if both the House and the Senate achieve a two‑thirds supermajority vote. This high threshold preserves the balance of power while allowing Congress to enact legislation despite executive opposition. Therefore, the correct answer is the two‑thirds majority option.

Q12. During a military conflict, the President decides to order an airstrike without prior congressional approval. Which constitutional principle is most at stake?

A.Separation of powers ✅
B.Federalism
C.Freedom of speech
D.Judicial review
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The President’s role as Commander‑in‑Chief allows swift military action, but Congress holds the power to declare war and fund operations. Acting without congressional input raises concerns about the separation of powers, a core constitutional principle designed to prevent any single branch from dominating. Thus, the correct answer is separation of powers.

Q15. If the President wishes to remove a federal judge, what constitutional mechanism must be used?

A.Presidential decree
B.Senate confirmation
C.Supreme Court decision
D.Impeachment by Congress ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Federal judges have life tenure and can only be removed through the impeachment process, which requires a majority vote in the House of Representatives and a two‑thirds conviction in the Senate. The President does not possess unilateral removal authority. Therefore, the correct answer is impeachment by Congress.

Q16. If a President repeatedly uses the veto power to block legislation, what constitutional concern might arise, and how could Congress respond?

A.Concern about executive overreach; Congress could amend the Constitution
B.Concern about judicial activism; Courts could intervene
C.Concern about legislative stagnation; Congress could attempt to override vetoes with the required two‑thirds majority ✅
D.Concern about fiscal imbalance; Treasury could intervene
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Frequent vetoes may stall the legislative agenda, prompting worries about executive dominance. The Constitution provides a remedy: Congress can override a veto if it reaches a two‑thirds supermajority in both chambers, thereby reasserting legislative authority. Hence, the correct answer highlights the override mechanism.

Q17. How does the President’s appointment power interact with the Senate’s 'advice and consent' role in the appointment process?

A.The President appoints, and the Senate has no role
B.The President nominates, and the Senate must confirm the nominee before the appointment becomes effective
C.The Senate appoints, and the President merely advises
D.Both branches jointly appoint without any confirmation ✅

📖 Explanation: The Constitution assigns the President the power to nominate individuals for key positions, while the Senate exercises its advice‑and‑consent function by voting to confirm or reject those nominees. This shared responsibility ensures checks and balances in the appointment process. Therefore, the correct answer describes nomination followed by Senate confirmation.

Q18. If a treaty signed by the President conflicts with an existing federal statute, which legal instrument prevails under the Supremacy Clause?

A.The treaty, because international agreements are supreme over conflicting statutes ✅
B.The federal statute, because Congress holds legislative authority
C.The Supreme Court, which decides case‑by‑case
D.The President’s executive order, which overrides both
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Under the Supremacy Clause, duly ratified treaties become part of the supreme law of the land, taking precedence over any conflicting statutes unless Congress enacts a subsequent law to modify the treaty. Consequently, the treaty would normally prevail, making option A the correct choice.

Q20. When a President issues an executive order to achieve a policy goal without congressional approval, what constitutional limitation can restrict that order?
