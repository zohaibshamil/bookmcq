What is US Supreme Court powers and function?

The US Supreme Court is the highest judicial body in the nation, tasked with interpreting the Constitution, reviewing the constitutionality of laws and government actions, and serving as the final court of appeal to ensure justice and legal consistency across the country.

📝 All US Supreme Court powers and function MCQs

Q1. How many judges serve on the Supreme Court?

A. Seven
B. Eight
C. Nine ✅
D. Ten

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Constitution establishes a bench of nine members, often referred to as the \nine‑judge\ Court. This number, \(9\), was set early in American history to balance regional representation and to provide a manageable panel for deliberation. The fixed size helps maintain consistency in jurisprudence and workload distribution.

Q2. Who has the authority to appoint Supreme Court judges?

A. President ✅
B. Congress
C. Supreme Court itself
D. State governors

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Under Article II, Section 2 of the Constitution, the President nominates candidates for the Supreme Court. This executive power is a key check in the appointment process, ensuring that the highest judicial branch reflects the elected leadership's perspective while still requiring legislative oversight to prevent unilateral selections.

Q3. What is required for a Supreme Court appointment to be finalized?

A. Senate approval
B. House approval
C. Supreme Court approval
D. Senate approval ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The appointment process concludes only after the Senate gives its advice and consent. This Senate approval, mandated by the Constitution, acts as a legislative check on the President’s nominee, preventing unchecked executive influence and ensuring that the nominee meets broader national standards of competence and integrity.

Q4. Under what condition must a Supreme Court judge leave office?

A. Until age 70
B. Life unless ill health or scandal ✅
C. Ten‑year term
D. Renewable five‑year term

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Supreme Court Justices hold their positions during good behavior, effectively granting them lifetime tenure. They may only be removed through impeachment, resignation due to serious health problems, or scandal that undermines public confidence. This permanence safeguards judicial independence, allowing judges to decide cases without fear of political retaliation.

Q5. A dispute arises between the President and Congress over constitutional authority. Which court resolves the conflict?

A. Supreme Court ✅
B. State Supreme Court
C. Federal district court
D. International Court

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Supreme Court possesses original jurisdiction in cases involving disputes between the executive and legislative branches. By interpreting the Constitution, it can determine whether the President’s actions exceed statutory limits or whether Congress has overstepped its legislative powers, thereby maintaining the balance of federal authority.

Q6. If a state enacts a law that conflicts with the federal Constitution, which entity resolves the conflict?

A. State legislature
B. Supreme Court ✅
C. President
D. Senate

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When a state law appears to violate the Supremacy Clause, the Supreme Court steps in to adjudicate. Through judicial review, it assesses whether the state statute infringes on federally protected rights or powers, ensuring uniform application of constitutional standards across all states and preventing fragmented legal regimes.

Q7. A Supreme Court justice resigns after a scandal. How is the resulting vacancy filled?

A. New appointment by President with Senate approval ✅
B. Election by the public
C. Appointment by the Chief Justice
D. Vacancy filled by senior judge

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Constitution requires the President to nominate a successor, subject to Senate confirmation. This process mirrors the original appointment procedure, preserving the balance of powers. No public election or internal court promotion occurs, ensuring that the new justice receives both executive and legislative legitimacy.

Q8. Two states dispute water rights from a shared river. Which court hears the case?

A. Supreme Court ✅
B. Federal appellate court
C. State court
D. International tribunal

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Original jurisdiction over controversies between states is vested in the Supreme Court. Such interstate disputes, especially involving natural resources, are brought directly before the Court, which issues binding decisions to resolve the conflict and uphold the Constitution’s provision for peaceful resolution among states.

Q9. Which principle allows the Court to hear cases arising from the Constitution?

A. Judicial review ✅
B. Legislative supremacy
C. Executive privilege
D. Federalism

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Judicial review, established in Marbury v. Madison, empowers the Supreme Court to examine the constitutionality of statutes and executive actions. This doctrine ensures that any law or policy conflicting with the Constitution can be invalidated, reinforcing the judiciary’s role as the ultimate interpreter of constitutional limits.

Q10. A justice with a mild health issue wishes to continue serving. Under what condition can they be compelled to retire?

A. Only if they resign voluntarily
B. Only if impeached
C. Only if they cannot discharge duties due to health ✅
D. No condition; they serve for life

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

While justices hold lifetime appointments, they may be required to step down if their health prevents them from performing essential duties. The Constitution’s “good behavior” clause is interpreted to include the ability to fulfill judicial responsibilities; severe or progressive health problems can trigger voluntary or forced retirement to preserve court function.

Q11. Which constitutional provision governs the Senate’s role in confirming a Supreme Court nominee?

A. Article II, Section 2 ✅
B. Article I, Section 5
C. Article III, Section 1
D. Article V, Section 2

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Article II, Section 2 explicitly grants the President the power to nominate judges, “with the Advice and Consent of the Senate.” This clause forms the legal basis for Senate confirmation hearings and votes, creating a critical check on executive nominations and ensuring that the judiciary reflects a broader consensus.

Q12. Under what circumstance can a Supreme Court judge be compelled to retire due to health?

A. Only if they resign voluntarily
B. Only if impeached
C. Only if they cannot discharge duties due to health ✅
D. No condition; they serve for life

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Constitution’s tenure language—“during good Behaviour”—is interpreted to include the capacity to perform judicial duties. If a justice’s health deteriorates to the point where they cannot hear cases or write opinions, the judiciary may pressure retirement to maintain effective operation, though formal removal requires impeachment.

Q13. Which landmark case established the principle of dual sovereignty between federal and state governments?

A. Marbury v. Madison
B. McCulloch v. Maryland ✅
C. Worcester v. Georgia
D. United States v. Nixon

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

McCulloch v. Maryland (1819) affirmed that states could not impede valid constitutional actions of the federal government, reinforcing the concept of dual sovereignty. The decision articulated that federal laws made pursuant to the Constitution are supreme, while states retain independent powers, shaping the federal‑state balance that persists today.

Q14. How many justices must agree for a majority decision in a case involving a constitutional amendment?

A. 5 ✅
B. 6
C. 7
D. 9

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With nine sitting justices, a simple majority requires more than half, i.e., at least five votes. This threshold ensures that decisions reflect a clear majority, preventing deadlocks and guaranteeing that constitutional interpretations are supported by the majority of the Court’s members.
