Q1. Which of the following Founding Fathers was NOT directly involved in drafting the Constitution at the 1787 Philadelphia Convention?

🔹 A. George Washington
🔹 B. Thomas Jefferson
🔹 C. Benjamin Franklin
🔹 D. James Madison

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Thomas Jefferson was serving as minister to France in \(1787\) and did not attend the Constitutional Convention. Washington presided, Franklin was a delegate, and Madison was a principal author. Jefferson later contributed to the Bill of Rights, but his absence means he did not take part in the drafting process.

Q2. Which branch of the federal government has the constitutional authority to declare war?

🔹 A. Executive
🔹 B. Judicial
🔹 C. Legislative
🔹 D. State

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Article I, Section 8 of the Constitution grants Congress the power to declare war, reflecting the Framers' intent to keep the decision in the legislative branch rather than in a single individual. The President may command forces, but only Congress can formally declare war, ensuring collective deliberation.

Q3. Who possesses the power to veto legislation passed by Congress?

🔹 A. President
🔹 B. Supreme Court
🔹 C. Vice President
🔹 D. Speaker of the House

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The presidential veto is outlined in Article I, Section 7, allowing the President to reject bills sent to the executive. Congress can override the veto with a two‑thirds majority in both chambers, but the initial check originates with the President, exemplifying the executive's role in the balance of powers.

Q4. How many distinct branches comprise the federal government under the Constitution?

🔹 A. Two
🔹 B. Four
🔹 C. Three
🔹 D. Five

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The United States operates under a tripartite system: legislative (Congress), executive (President and administration), and judicial (Supreme Court and lower courts). This three‑branch structure was designed to prevent any single entity from accumulating excessive authority, embodying the principle of separation of powers.

Q5. Which amendment explicitly reserves powers not delegated to the federal government to the states or the people?

🔹 A. First Amendment
🔹 B. Tenth Amendment
🔹 C. Fifth Amendment
🔹 D. Fourteenth Amendment

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Tenth Amendment, part of the Bill of Rights ratified in \(1791\), states that powers not granted to the United States nor prohibited to the states are reserved for the states or the people. This provision reinforces the concept of limited federal authority and supports the broader separation of powers.

Q6. Which branch is primarily responsible for interpreting the constitutionality of federal statutes?

🔹 A. Legislative
🔹 B. Executive
🔹 C. State
🔹 D. Judicial

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Judicial review, established by the Supreme Court case Marbury v. Madison in \(1803\), empowers the judicial branch to assess whether laws align with the Constitution. This authority enables courts to invalidate statutes that conflict with constitutional principles, serving as a critical check on legislative and executive actions.

Q7. Which chamber of Congress holds the sole power to impeach a federal official?

🔹 A. House of Representatives
🔹 B. Senate
🔹 C. Both chambers jointly
🔹 D. Supreme Court

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Article I, Section 2 grants the House of Representatives the exclusive authority to bring articles of impeachment. This initiates the removal process, after which the Senate conducts a trial. The division of duties between the two chambers distributes the power to remove officials, preventing unilateral action.

Q8. Which body confirms the President's appointments to the Supreme Court?

🔹 A. House of Representatives
🔹 B. President's Cabinet
🔹 C. Senate
🔹 D. Supreme Court itself

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Constitution's Appointments Clause in Article II, Section 2 requires that the President obtain the advice and consent of the Senate for Supreme Court nominations. This Senate confirmation serves as a legislative check on the executive's appointment power, ensuring collaborative participation in shaping the judiciary.

Q9. Congress passes a bill, the President vetoes it, and Congress subsequently overrides the veto with the required majority. What does this sequence illustrate about checks and balances?

🔹 A. Legislative supremacy
🔹 B. Judicial review
🔹 C. Executive dominance
🔹 D. Mutual oversight

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The scenario demonstrates mutual oversight: the President checks Congress by vetoing, while Congress checks the President by overriding with a two‑thirds majority. This interplay ensures that neither branch can unilaterally impose its will, embodying the Framers' intent for a balanced distribution of authority.

Q10. When the Supreme Court declares a statute unconstitutional, which branch's power is being exercised?

🔹 A. Legislative
🔹 B. Judicial
🔹 C. Executive
🔹 D. State

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Judicial review allows the Supreme Court, part of the judicial branch, to nullify laws that conflict with the Constitution. This power, rooted in Marbury v. Madison, acts as a check on the legislative branch, ensuring that enacted statutes adhere to constitutional limits and protecting individual rights.

Q11. In Federalist No. 51, Madison argues that ambition must be made to counteract ambition. How does this principle relate to the separation of powers?

🔹 A. It promotes a single, strong leader
🔹 B. It suggests merging branches
🔹 C. It endorses mutual checks among branches
🔹 D. It ignores legislative authority

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Madison's statement underscores the need for each branch to have sufficient self‑interest to resist encroachments by the others. By designing overlapping powers—such as vetoes, confirmations, and impeachment—the Constitution ensures that ambition in one branch serves as a check on the ambitions of the others, preserving liberty.

Q12. If a President attempts to appoint a Supreme Court justice without Senate confirmation, which constitutional principle is being violated?

🔹 A. Advice and Consent
🔹 B. Judicial Review
🔹 C. Executive Privilege
🔹 D. Legislative Oversight

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Appointments Clause requires the President to seek the Senate's advice and consent before filling judicial vacancies. Bypassing this process would breach the constitutional separation of powers by concentrating appointment authority solely in the executive, undermining the Senate's role as a legislative check.

Q13. Why does the Constitution split the impeachment process between the House and the Senate rather than assigning it to a single body?

🔹 A. To simplify removal
🔹 B. To involve the judiciary
🔹 C. To ensure bipartisan deliberation
🔹 D. To distribute authority and prevent abuse

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Dividing impeachment between the House (which brings charges) and the Senate (which conducts the trial) diffuses power, preventing any single chamber from unilaterally removing officials. This structural safeguard reflects the Framers' wariness of concentrated authority and aligns with the broader doctrine of checks and balances.

Q14. A state enacts a law that conflicts with a federal statute on immigration. Which branch ultimately resolves the conflict?

🔹 A. Legislative
🔹 B. Executive
🔹 C. Judicial
🔹 D. State

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Under the Supremacy Clause in Article VI, federal law prevails over conflicting state statutes. When disputes arise, the judicial branch interprets and enforces this hierarchy, often through federal courts, thereby ensuring uniform application of national policies across all states.

Q15. How does the system of checks and balances prevent legislative dominance over the executive and judicial branches?

🔹 A. By giving Congress unlimited power
🔹 B. By allowing the President to ignore laws
🔹 C. By granting veto, appointment, and judicial review powers
🔹 D. By restricting the Senate's role

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Constitution equips Congress with law‑making authority, but the President can veto legislation, and the Senate confirms judicial appointments. Meanwhile, the judiciary can invalidate statutes that breach constitutional norms. These reciprocal powers restrict any single branch from overwhelming the others, maintaining institutional equilibrium.

Q16. Congress passes a budget, and the President refuses to sign it. What constitutional mechanism resolves this impasse?

🔹 A. Supreme Court injunction
🔹 B. Congressional override
🔹 C. Pocket veto
🔹 D. Judicial review

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

If the President takes no action on a bill for ten days while Congress is in session, the bill becomes law. However, if Congress adjourns during that period, the President may employ a pocket veto, effectively rejecting the budget without a formal veto, illustrating executive discretion within the budgetary process.

Q17. In practice, how do 'checks' differ from 'balances' within the separation of powers framework?

🔹 A. Checks are temporary, balances are permanent
🔹 B. Checks involve direct actions, balances refer to overall power distribution
🔹 C. Checks apply only to the judiciary
🔹 D. Balances are irrelevant

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Checks are specific mechanisms—such as vetoes, confirmations, or impeachment—that allow one branch to limit another's actions. Balances refer to the broader allocation of authority among the three branches, ensuring that no single entity holds disproportionate power. Together they create a dynamic equilibrium that sustains democratic governance.

Q18. If a Supreme Court justice faces allegations of ethical misconduct, which branch initiates the removal process?

🔹 A. Legislative
🔹 B. Executive
🔹 C. Judicial
🔹 D. State

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Under the Constitution, federal judges, including Supreme Court justices, can be removed only through impeachment by the House of Representatives and conviction by the Senate. Thus, the legislative branch—specifically the Senate after impeachment—carries out the removal, reflecting a check on lifetime judicial appointments.

Q19. The Twenty‑fifth Amendment addresses presidential succession and incapacity. How does this amendment interact with the separation of powers?

🔹 A. It consolidates power in Congress
🔹 B. It allows the judiciary to appoint a new President
🔹 C. It provides a clear mechanism for executive continuity while preserving checks
🔹 D. It eliminates the Vice President's role

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Twenty‑fifth Amendment clarifies the line of succession, ensuring that executive authority can be transferred smoothly without legislative or judicial interference. By defining procedures for vacancy and disability, it reinforces the stability of the executive branch while preserving the overall system of checks and balances.

Q20. A President issues an executive order that conflicts with an existing statute passed by Congress. The Supreme Court rules the order unconstitutional. Which principle determines the hierarchy of authority in this case?

🔹 A. State sovereignty
🔹 B. Legislative supremacy
🔹 C. Supremacy of the Constitution
🔹 D. Executive privilege

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Constitution, as the supreme law, outranks both statutes and executive actions. When an executive order contradicts a congressional statute, the judiciary assesses the conflict and, under the Supremacy Clause, may invalidate the order if it exceeds constitutional limits, reaffirming the hierarchy that places the Constitution at the top.

Q21. Evaluate the effectiveness of the War Powers Resolution of \(1973\) in limiting presidential authority to conduct military operations without congressional approval.

🔹 A. It fully restricts the President
🔹 B. It provides a balanced compromise
🔹 C. It is rarely enforced, reducing its impact
🔹 D. It grants Congress sole war‑making power

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The War Powers Resolution sought to curb unilateral executive action by requiring notification and limiting engagements to \(60\) days unless Congress authorizes continuation. While it establishes a framework for shared decision‑making, presidents have often circumvented its provisions, and Congress has been hesitant to assert enforcement, resulting in a mixed record of effectiveness.

Q22. Congress passes a law attempting to limit the Supreme Court's jurisdiction over certain types of cases. Which constitutional doctrine does this action potentially violate?

🔹 A. Full Faith and Credit
🔹 B. Necessary and Proper
🔹 C. Separation of Powers
🔹 D. Judicial Review

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Article III grants the Supreme Court appellate jurisdiction, and while Congress may make rules concerning the Court's jurisdiction, outright restrictions on core functions can upset the balance among branches. Such a move would intrude on judicial independence, contravening the separation of powers principle designed to keep each branch within its constitutional sphere.

Q23. Discuss the tension between judicial review and congressional supremacy when Congress enacts a law that appears to expand its own powers beyond those enumerated in the Constitution.

🔹 A. Judicial review always prevails
🔹 B. Congress can override the Court
🔹 C. The tension highlights the need for checks
🔹 D. There is no tension

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When Congress passes legislation that seemingly exceeds its constitutional authority, the judiciary may invalidate the law through judicial review, asserting its interpretive role. This clash underscores the system of checks, where the courts restrain legislative overreach while Congress retains the power to amend the Constitution, preserving democratic balance.

Q24. A President is impeached for obstruction of Congress. Outline the sequence of actions each branch must take to complete the removal process.

🔹 A. House votes, Supreme Court decides
🔹 B. Senate votes, then House confirms
🔹 C. House drafts articles, Senate conducts trial, then votes to convict
🔹 D. President resigns immediately

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The impeachment process begins in the House of Representatives, which investigates and votes on articles of impeachment. If passed, the matter moves to the Senate, which holds a trial presided over by the Chief Justice. Conviction requires a two‑thirds vote, after which the President is removed from office.

Q25. Propose a constitutional amendment to adjust the balance of appointment power between the President and the Senate. Which existing constraint would such an amendment most likely challenge?

🔹 A. The Supremacy Clause
🔹 B. The Appointments Clause
🔹 C. The Commerce Clause
🔹 D. The Bill of Rights

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Any amendment altering the appointment process would directly modify the Appointments Clause in Article II, Section 2, which currently mandates Senate advice and consent. Changing this framework—such as by allowing unilateral presidential appointments or expanding Senate confirmation—would require careful constitutional justification to avoid disrupting the established checks between the executive and legislative branches.
