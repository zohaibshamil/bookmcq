What is Causes of Great Depression 1929?

The Great Depression was caused by the stock market crash, bank failures, collapse of international trade due to protectionist tariffs, severe drought, and structural weaknesses in the economy that turned a financial crisis into a prolonged nationwide economic catastrophe.

📝 All Causes of Great Depression 1929 MCQs

Q1. On which date did the Wall Street Crash known as Black Tuesday occur?

A.24-Oct-29
B.29-Oct-29 ✅
C.31-Oct-29
D.01-Nov-29
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Black Tuesday refers to the day when the stock market collapsed dramatically, which was on 29 October 1929. This date marks the peak of panic selling and the massive loss of wealth, setting the stage for the Great Depression.

Q2. Which Republican won the overwhelming victory in the 1928 presidential election?

A.Herbert C. Hoover ✅
B.Calvin Coolidge
C.Franklin D. Roosevelt
D.Warren G. Harding
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Herbert C. Hoover was the Republican candidate who won the 1928 election by a large margin. His administration later faced the onset of the Great Depression, although the crash itself was not directly caused by his policies.

Q3. What immediate effect did the rush to withdraw savings have on banks?

A.Increased profits
B.Bank closures ✅
C.Higher interest rates
D.More loans issued
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The sudden demand for cash caused many banks to run out of reserves, forcing thousands to close. This wave of bank failures further deepened the financial crisis and reduced public confidence in the banking system.

Q4. By what percentage did industrial profits increase between 1923 and 1929?

A.0.08
B.0.3
C.0.72 ✅
D.1
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Industrial profits rose dramatically, by about \(72\%\) over the six‑year period. This outsized profit growth, contrasted with modest wage gains, contributed to unequal income distribution and weakened consumer purchasing power.

Q5. During the same period, how much did the average industrial wage rise?

A.0.01
B.0.05
C.0.08 ✅
D.0.12
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The average wage for industrial workers increased by roughly \(8\%\) from 1923 to 1929, which translated to only about \(1.4\%\) in real terms after inflation, limiting workers' ability to buy the surplus goods produced.

Q6. What term is commonly used to describe the 1929 stock market collapse?

A.The Great Recession
B.The Black Thursday Crash
C.The Wall Street Crash ✅
D.The 1929 Financial Shock
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The 1929 event is most frequently called the Wall Street Crash. It marked the abrupt end of the roaring twenties and signaled the beginning of a prolonged economic downturn known as the Great Depression.

Q7. Which sector’s overproduction most directly contributed to the Great Depression?

A.Agriculture
B.Automobile manufacturing
C.Industrial manufacturing ✅
D.Textiles
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Industrial manufacturing overproduced goods beyond domestic demand, leading to unsold inventories. This excess production forced firms to cut labor, reducing wages and spending, thereby intensifying the economic downturn.

Q8. Which group lacked unemployment benefits during the early 1930s?

A.Industrial workers ✅
B.Farmers
C.Bankers
D.All of the above
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Industrial workers did not receive unemployment benefits at the time. When factories reduced staff, these workers and their families faced immediate loss of income, curbing consumer demand and worsening the depression.

Q9. What phrase did President Coolidge use to describe the nation's condition in 1928?

A.America the Golden ✅
B.America the Strong
C.America the Proud
D.America the Resilient
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Coolidge’s optimistic statement, “America the Golden,” reflected the widespread belief that prosperity would continue unabated, a sentiment that was shattered by the 1929 crash.

Q10. In which year did the Wall Street Crash that triggered the Great Depression occur?

A.1927
B.1928
C.1929 ✅
D.1930
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The crash happened in 1929, with the most infamous day, Black Tuesday, on 29 October 1929. This event marked the transition from the prosperous 1920s to the severe economic contraction of the 1930s.

Q11. Which of the following was a direct result of factories closing after the crash?

A.Higher wages
B.Increased production
C.Rising unemployment ✅
D.Lower taxes
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Factory closures led to layoffs, which caused unemployment to rise sharply. With fewer people earning wages, overall consumer spending fell, deepening the economic slump.

Q12. What happened to the demand for goods as the depression spread?

B.It stayed the same
C.It fell dramatically ✅
D.It became unpredictable
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Demand for goods plummeted as consumers lost income and confidence. The resulting inventory buildup forced producers to cut back production, creating a vicious cycle of reduced employment and spending.

Q13. Which phrase best describes the global impact of the 1929 crash?

C.A minor recession
D.An isolated incident
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The crash quickly spread beyond the United States, turning into a worldwide economic crisis that affected many nations, not just the U.S., and is often referred to as the world economic crisis.

Q14. Which of the following best explains why the Wall Street Crash was a symptom rather than the cause of the Depression?

A.It caused all bank failures
B.It reflected deeper structural problems ✅
C.It was the sole factor in unemployment
D.It was unrelated to the economy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The crash revealed underlying issues such as overproduction, unequal income distribution, and weak consumer purchasing power. These structural problems existed before the market plunge and continued to drive the downturn after the crash.

Q15. If industrial profits grew by \(72\%\) while wages grew by \(8\%\), what was the ratio of profit growth to wage growth?

📖 Explanation: Dividing the profit increase (\(72\%\)) by the wage increase (\(8\%\)) yields a ratio of \(72/8 = 9\). This nine‑to‑one disparity highlights the widening gap between corporate earnings and worker earnings.

Q16. A factory produced 10,000 units in 1928 and 12,000 units in 1929. If unsold inventory rose from 1,000 to 3,000 units, what percentage of the 1929 production remained unsold?

A.0.2
B.0.25 ✅
C.0.3
D.0.33
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Unsold units in 1929 were 3,000 out of 12,000 produced, giving \(3,000/12,000 = 0.25\) or \(25\%\) of the output still in inventory, indicating excess production relative to demand.

Q17. Which policy would most directly address the unequal distribution of income described in the passage?

A.Increasing tariffs
B.Implementing progressive taxes ✅
C.Reducing interest rates
D.Expanding credit availability
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Progressive taxation can shift a larger share of profits from high‑earning industrialists to the broader public, potentially raising wages and consumer purchasing power, thereby mitigating the income inequality that contributed to the depression.

Q18. A consumer has a monthly income of $200 and spends $180 on goods. If their income falls by 15 % due to unemployment, how much can they now spend, assuming they keep the same spending ratio?

A.153 ✅
B.165
C.170
D.180
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The original spending ratio is \(180/200 = 0.9\). A 15 % income drop makes income \(200 \times 0.85 = $170\). Spending at the same ratio gives \(0.9 \times 170 = $153\).

Q19. Why did manufacturers hesitate to reduce prices during the late 1920s?

A.Fear of deflation
B.Strong competition
C.Desire to maintain profit margins ✅
D.Government price controls
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Manufacturers, often large corporations, prioritized preserving profit margins over cutting prices. Lowering prices could signal weakness, reduce revenues, and potentially trigger further layoffs, so they preferred to keep prices stable despite falling demand.

Q20. Which of the following best illustrates a vicious circle described in the passage?

B.Unemployment benefits increasing savings
C.Reduced production causing layoffs, which lowers demand further ✅
D.Government stimulus boosting employment
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The passage describes how layoffs reduced wages, which lowered consumer demand, leading to more layoffs—a self‑reinforcing downward spiral that perpetuated the depression.

Q22. Which economic indicator most directly captures the “vicious circle” of reduced production and rising unemployment?

Q24. Which of the following reforms was later introduced to prevent a repeat of the 1929 banking crisis?

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

D.400

Q26. Which of the following best explains why credit could no longer sustain consumer purchases by 1929?

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: By 1929, many households had maximized borrowing to maintain consumption. When credit could no longer expand, purchasing power stalled, exposing the economy’s reliance on borrowed money rather than real income.

Q31. If a factory reduced its workforce by 15 % and each remaining worker earned $120 per week, what was the total weekly payroll after the cut if the original payroll was $1 million?

B.900000

D.1000000

Q32. Which policy response directly addressed the lack of unemployment benefits highlighted in the passage?

Q34. Why might the Wall Street Crash be considered a “symptom” rather than the “cause” of the Depression, according to economic theory?

Q35. A country’s GDP fell from $500 billion to $425 billion between 1929 and 1930. What was the annual contraction rate?
