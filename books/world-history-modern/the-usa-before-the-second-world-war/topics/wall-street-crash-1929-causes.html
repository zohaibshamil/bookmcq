Q1. In which month and year did Black Tuesday occur?

🔹 A. Sept-29
🔹 B. Nov-29
🔹 C. Dec-29
🔹 D. Oct-29

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The crash climaxed on Black Tuesday, which occurred on 29 October 1929, marking the day when stock prices collapsed dramatically after a week of panic selling that began on 24 October. This date is widely remembered as the turning point that ushered the United States into the Great Depression.

🔹 A. Calvin Coolidge
🔹 B. Herbert C. Hoover
🔹 C. Franklin D. Roosevelt
🔹 D. Warren G. Harding

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Herbert C. Hoover, a Republican, secured a landslide victory in the 1928 presidential election, reflecting the nation's confidence in continued prosperity. His administration soon faced the unprecedented economic challenges triggered by the stock market collapse, which began only months after his inauguration.

📖 Explanation:

The immediate aftermath saw a rush of depositors withdrawing cash, overwhelming banks' liquidity. Unable to meet the surge in withdrawals, many institutions were forced to suspend operations, leading to a cascade of bank closures that amplified the economic shock and deepened public panic.

Q7. If an investor bought shares at $120 per share before the crash and sold at $30 per share after, what percent loss did they incur?

🔹 A. 0.75
🔹 B. 0.8
🔹 C. 0.85
🔹 D. 0.9

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The investor purchased shares at \( \$120 \) each and later sold them for \( \$30 \) each. The loss per share is \(120 - 30 = \$90\). The percentage loss is \((90 / 120) \times 100 = 75\%\). This illustrates the severe financial impact many investors faced during the crash.

Q8. A factory produced 10,000 units monthly but could only sell 6,000 after the crash, leaving excess inventory. What percentage of production remained unsold?

🔹 A. 0.4
🔹 B. 0.3
🔹 C. 0.2
🔹 D. 0.5

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The factory produced 10,000 units but could only sell 6,000 after demand fell, leaving 4,000 units unsold. The unsold inventory represents \((4,000 / 10,000) \times 100 = 40\%\) of total production. This excess capacity contributed to reduced profits and layoffs during the Depression.

🔹 D. It was unrelated to economic conditions.

📖 Explanation:

The passage characterizes the crash as a symptom rather than the cause because it manifested existing vulnerabilities such as overproduction, speculative finance, and income inequality. These deeper problems eroded economic stability, and when the market collapsed, they accelerated the transition from a boom to a prolonged depression.

Q10. If a bank had reserves equal to 20% of its deposits before the panic and withdrawals amounted to 15% of total deposits, what fraction of its reserves would be used?

🔹 A. 0.5
🔹 B. 0.6
🔹 C. 0.75
🔹 D. 0.9

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

With reserves equal to \(20\%\) of deposits, a withdrawal of \(15\%\) of total deposits consumes \(\frac{15\%}{20\%}=0.75\) or \(75\%\) of the bank’s reserves. This high utilization rate explains why many banks were forced to close when faced with massive withdrawals during the panic.

Q15. If a household's savings were $5,000 before the crash and they withdrew 80% during the panic, what would be the remaining balance?

🔹 A. 1000
🔹 B. 4000
🔹 C. 500
🔹 D. 0

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Starting with a savings balance of \( \$5,000 \), an 80% withdrawal leaves \(20\%\) remaining. Calculating \(0.20 \times 5,000 = \$1,000\) shows the household retained only \( \$1,000 \) after the panic, illustrating the severe reduction in personal wealth experienced by many.

Q17. Considering the passage, which policy response would most directly address the overproduction problem that contributed to the Depression?

🔹 A. Raising interest rates
🔹 B. Implementing protectionist tariffs
🔹 C. Expanding public works to absorb excess output
🔹 D. Reducing government spending

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

To address overproduction, a government could launch public works projects that absorb excess industrial output and provide employment, thereby reducing inventory buildup while stimulating demand. This policy directly targets the surplus capacity that contributed to the economic downturn, unlike measures focused solely on monetary or fiscal tightening.
