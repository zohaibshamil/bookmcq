What is Who was responsible for Wall Street Crash 1929?

Responsibility for the 1929 crash is shared among reckless investors, unregulated financial institutions, policymakers who ignored warning signs, and a laissez-faire economic philosophy that failed to prevent speculative excesses and systemic vulnerabilities in the financial system.

📝 All Who was responsible for Wall Street Crash 1929 MCQs

Q1. According to the passage, which political party is said to share responsibility for the economic disaster?

A.Republican Party ✅
B.Democratic Party
C.Whig Party
D.Green Party
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The author argues that while contemporary opinion often singled out President Hoover, the underlying causes were broader. By highlighting the Republican Party’s policies—such as tariff decisions and laissez‑faire attitudes—the text makes clear that the party collectively bears significant responsibility for the downturn.

Q2. What was the intended effect of the Smoot–Hawley Tariff as described in the text?

A.Reduce agricultural exports
B.Protect domestic farmers from foreign competition ✅
C.Increase foreign investment
D.Lower consumer prices
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Smoot‑Hawley Tariff, passed in 1930, was intended to shield American farmers by raising import duties on agricultural products. By making foreign goods more expensive, the legislation aimed to reduce competition and protect domestic farm incomes, even though it later provoked retaliatory tariffs.

Q3. Which organization created by Hoover was designed to persuade large banks to lend to smaller banks?

A.Reconstruction Finance Corporation
B.Federal Reserve Bank
C.Securities and Exchange Commission
D.National Credit Corporation ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Hoover’s National Credit Corporation was created to encourage large banks to extend loans to smaller, struggling banks. However, the larger institutions were wary of further risk, fearing that any defaults would jeopardize their own stability. This reluctance illustrates why the NCC failed to achieve its intended stabilizing effect.

Q4. If the government had lowered tariffs by 10% in 1929 and foreign imports of American goods increased by 15%, which of the following outcomes would be most consistent with the passage’s argument about tariff policy?

A.Domestic employment would likely rise ✅
B.Domestic employment would likely fall
C.Tariff reduction would have no effect on employment
D.Export earnings would double
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A 10 % reduction in tariffs would lower the cost of American exports, making them more attractive abroad. If foreign imports of U.S. goods rose by 15 %, the increased demand would likely translate into higher production and, consequently, more domestic jobs, aligning with the passage’s argument that lower tariffs could have mitigated the crisis.

Q5. Considering the reluctance of large banks to lend to smaller banks, which economic principle best explains the failure of the National Credit Corporation?

A.Liquidity trap
B.Crowding out
C.Moral hazard ✅
D.Supply‑side economics
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Moral hazard describes a situation where one party takes greater risks because another party bears the cost of those risks. In the case of the National Credit Corporation, large banks feared that lending to vulnerable small banks would expose them to potential losses, leading to a reluctance to provide credit despite the program’s goals.

Q6. In 1931 Hoover declared a one‑year moratorium on war debts. What was the primary hoped‑for benefit of this policy?

A.Allow foreign governments to save money for American goods ✅
B.Reduce U.S. government spending
C.Increase domestic tax revenue
D.Strengthen the gold standard
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Hoover’s one‑year moratorium on war debts was meant to give foreign governments a temporary reprieve, allowing them to conserve cash that could then be redirected toward purchasing American goods. The policy hoped that this financial breathing space would stimulate demand for U.S. exports, thereby helping to alleviate the domestic economic slump.

Q7. Suppose American exports fell from 100 units in 1929 to 30 units in 1932, a decline of 70%. If the Smoot‑Hawley Tariff reduced foreign demand by 25% and the moratorium failed to boost purchases, what proportion of the export decline can be attributed to tariff retaliation according to the passage’s analysis?

A.Approximately 25%
B.Approximately 35% ✅
C.Approximately 45%
D.Approximately 55%
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: American exports fell by 70 % from 1929 to 1932. The passage attributes part of this decline to the Smoot‑Hawley Tariff and the resulting retaliation, which reduced foreign demand by roughly a quarter. If we assign the 25 % tariff impact to the total loss, about one‑third (≈ 35 %) of the export collapse can be linked to tariff retaliation.

Q8. Weighing the evidence presented, which of the following statements most accurately reflects the passage’s view on assigning blame for the Great Depression?

A.Only President Hoover is responsible
B.The Republican Party as a whole bears significant blame ✅
C.Blame should be placed on foreign governments’ tariffs
D.The disaster was solely due to private sector speculation
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The passage stresses that blaming only President Hoover oversimplifies the crisis. It points to systemic issues within the Republican Party—such as protectionist tariffs and a laissez‑faire stance—as major contributors. Therefore, the most accurate view presented is that the Republican Party as a whole bears considerable responsibility for the disaster.

Q9. The Reconstruction Finance Corporation began showing results toward the end of 1932. If the RFC provided loans totaling \( \$2 \) billion and each loan created on average 5 jobs, approximately how many jobs were generated?

A.10000 ✅
B.20000
C.30000
D.40000
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The RFC’s total loan portfolio of \( \$2 \) billion, when divided into average loans that each support five jobs, yields an estimated employment impact. Assuming each loan represents a unit that creates five positions, the simple multiplication results in roughly ten thousand jobs generated, illustrating the modest but tangible effect of RFC financing.
