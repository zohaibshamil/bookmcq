📝 All US Congress structure and powers MCQs

Q1. What is the total number of members in the House of Representatives?

🔹 A. \(400\)
🔹 B. \(425\)
🔹 C. \(435\)
🔹 D. \(450\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The House of Representatives is fixed at \(435\) members by law. Each member represents a congressional district that is drawn to contain roughly equal population, ensuring proportional representation across the United States. This number has remained constant since the Reapportionment Act of 1929, providing stability in the lower chamber.

Q2. How many total Senators serve in the United States Senate?

🔹 A. \(50\)
🔹 B. \(75\)
🔹 C. \(100\)
🔹 D. \(150\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Senate is composed of \(100\) members, with each of the fifty states sending two senators regardless of population size. This equal representation was designed by the framers of the Constitution to balance the influence of larger and smaller states and remains a core feature of the bicameral legislature.

Q3. For how many years does a Representative serve before facing re‑election?

🔹 A. \(4\) years
🔹 B. \(2\) years
🔹 C. \(6\) years
🔹 D. \(8\) years

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Members of the House of Representatives serve a term of \(2\) years. All seats are up for election every two years, allowing the chamber to reflect current public opinion more quickly than the Senate. This short term was intended to keep Representatives closely accountable to their constituents.

Q4. What is the length of a Senator's term?

🔹 A. \(2\) years
🔹 B. \(6\) years
🔹 C. \(4\) years
🔹 D. \(8\) years

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Senators are elected for a term of \(6\) years. The Senate is divided into three classes, with approximately one‑third of the seats up for election every two years. This staggered system provides continuity and stability in the upper chamber, preventing a complete turnover at any single election.

Q5. How many Senators does each state send to the Senate?

🔹 A. \(1\)
🔹 B. \(3\)
🔹 C. \(2\)
🔹 D. \(4\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Every state, regardless of its population size, sends exactly \(2\) senators to the Senate. This arrangement balances the influence of populous and less‑populous states and was a key compromise in the Constitutional Convention, ensuring that each state has an equal voice in the upper chamber.

Q6. What voting threshold is required for a bill to become law in each house of Congress?

🔹 A. Simple majority
🔹 B. Two‑thirds majority
🔹 C. Three‑quarters majority
🔹 D. Unanimous consent

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A bill must receive a simple majority of the members present and voting in both the House and the Senate to pass. This means more votes in favor than against, without the need for a supermajority, allowing the ordinary legislative process to move forward efficiently under most circumstances.

Q7. What fraction of the Senate must approve a treaty for it to be ratified?

🔹 A. \(1/2\)
🔹 B. \(3/4\)
🔹 C. \(5/6\)
🔹 D. \(2/3\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Treaties require the approval of a two‑thirds (\( \frac{2}{3} \)) majority of the Senate. With \(100\) senators, at least \(67\) must vote in favor for the treaty to be ratified. This supermajority requirement reflects the importance of foreign agreements and ensures broad consensus before the United States binds itself internationally.

Q8. Which party has traditionally drawn strong support from businessmen and industrialists in the North?

🔹 A. Republicans
🔹 B. Democrats
🔹 C. Libertarians
🔹 D. Green Party

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Historically, the Republican Party has attracted support from Northern businessmen and industrialists. The party’s early platform emphasized protective tariffs and limited government interference in the economy, appealing to those who sought a stable environment for commerce and manufacturing. This alignment shaped much of the party’s policy focus during the early twentieth century.

Q9. From which region has the Democratic Party historically drawn much of its support?

🔹 A. Midwest
🔹 B. South
🔹 C. West Coast
🔹 D. New England

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Democratic Party has traditionally enjoyed strong backing from the Southern United States, as well as from immigrant communities in large Northern cities. This geographic base contributed to the party’s coalition, influencing its policy priorities and electoral strategies throughout much of American history, especially prior to the civil‑rights realignments of the 1960s.

Q10. Which President asked Congress to declare war on Germany in 1917?

🔹 A. Theodore Roosevelt
🔹 B. William Howard Taft
🔹 C. Woodrow Wilson
🔹 D. Calvin Coolidge

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

President Woodrow Wilson sought Congressional approval to enter World War I against Germany in 1917. He believed that a formal declaration of war by Congress was essential for constitutional legitimacy and to rally public support. Congress responded by passing the war resolution, marking a pivotal moment in U.S. foreign‑policy history.

Q11. Which President is noted for favoring non‑interventionist policies during the early Great Depression?

🔹 A. Herbert Hoover
🔹 B. Franklin D. Roosevelt
🔹 C. Harry S. Truman
🔹 D. John F. Kennedy

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Herbert Hoover, who served from 1929 to 1933, is often cited for his non‑interventionist stance during the early years of the Great Depression. He believed that the federal government should not directly intervene in economic and social problems, preferring a laissez‑faire approach that placed responsibility on private enterprise and local authorities.

Q12. Which President advocated a more active governmental role in social and economic problems?

🔹 A. Franklin D. Roosevelt
🔹 B. Dwight D. Eisenhower
🔹 C. Richard Nixon
🔹 D. Ronald Reagan

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Franklin D. Roosevelt, President from 1933 to 1945, championed an active federal government through his New Deal programs. He believed that the government should intervene to alleviate unemployment, provide social safety nets, and regulate the economy, fundamentally reshaping the relationship between the state and American citizens during the Great Depression and World War II.

Q13. How often does a third of the Senate seats become available for election?

🔹 A. Every year
🔹 B. Every two years
🔹 C. Every three years
🔹 D. Every six years

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Senate is divided into three classes, with approximately one‑third of its \(100\) seats up for election every two years. This staggered schedule ensures continuity within the chamber, as only a portion of senators may change at each election, preventing a complete turnover and preserving institutional memory.

Q14. Which chamber has the constitutional authority to originate revenue bills?

🔹 A. House of Representatives
🔹 B. Senate
🔹 C. Both chambers equally
🔹 D. Neither chamber

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Constitution assigns the House of Representatives the exclusive right to originate revenue‑raising bills. This provision reflects the framers’ intent that the chamber most directly elected by the people should control taxation matters, ensuring greater democratic accountability for fiscal decisions that affect the nation’s budget.

Q15. If the House passes a bill with \(218\) yes votes, \(217\) no votes, and one member abstains, does the bill pass?

🔹 A. Yes, it passes
🔹 B. No, it fails
🔹 C. It depends on the abstention rule
🔹 D. It requires a joint conference

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A simple majority is calculated based on members who actually cast a vote. With \(218\) affirmative votes and \(217\) negative votes, the yes side has more than half of the \(435-1=434\) voting members, so the bill meets the simple‑majority threshold and is considered passed despite one abstention.

Q16. How many affirmative votes are needed to ratify a treaty if all \(100\) Senators vote?

🔹 A. \(66\)
🔹 B. \(67\)
🔹 C. \(68\)
🔹 D. \(67\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A treaty requires a two‑thirds (\( \frac{2}{3} \)) majority of the Senate. Multiplying \( \frac{2}{3} \) by \(100\) yields \(66.\overline{6}\), which must be rounded up to the next whole number, \(67\). Therefore, at least \(67\) senators must vote yes for the treaty to be ratified.

Q17. What is a likely consequence of weak party discipline in the U.S. Congress?

🔹 A. Frequent party‑line votes
🔹 B. Frequent cross‑party coalitions
🔹 C. Increased executive control
🔹 D. Reduced legislative activity

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Weak party discipline encourages members to vote according to personal convictions or constituency interests rather than strict party directives. This environment often leads to cross‑party coalitions forming around specific issues, making legislative outcomes less predictable and sometimes fostering bipartisan compromise on contentious legislation.

Q18. After a joint conference resolves differences between the two houses, what is the next step?

🔹 A. Submit to the Supreme Court
🔹 B. Return to the originating house
🔹 C. Both houses vote on the compromise
🔹 D. Discard the bill

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Once a joint conference committee produces a compromise version of a bill, the revised text is sent back to both the House and the Senate for a final vote. Both chambers must approve the conference report before the legislation can proceed to the President for signature or veto.

Q19. If a bill fails in the Senate but has passed the House, what can Congress do?

🔹 A. Send it back to the House for revision
🔹 B. The bill dies permanently
🔹 C. The President can sign it anyway
🔹 D. The Senate can reconsider it without amendment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When a bill is rejected by the Senate, the House may choose to amend the measure and resend it, or it may re‑introduce a new version. The legislative process allows for further negotiation, but the original bill cannot become law without Senate approval.

Q20. How does representation differ between a state with \(5\) million people and one with \(20\) million people?

🔹 A. Both have equal representation in both houses
🔹 B. Only the larger state gets more senators
🔹 C. Only the larger state gets more representatives
🔹 D. Both have equal Senate representation but differ in House seats

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Each state, regardless of population, sends exactly \(2\) senators, providing equal representation in the Senate. However, the House of Representatives apportions seats based on population, so the state with \(20\) million people receives roughly four times as many representatives as the state with \(5\) million.

Q21. Which house must approve funding for a war after a declaration of war?

🔹 A. Only the House of Representatives
🔹 B. Both the House and the Senate
🔹 C. Only the Senate
🔹 D. The President alone

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Constitution requires that any appropriation of funds, including those needed to conduct a war, must be authorized by both chambers of Congress. After a war is declared, the House and the Senate must each pass legislation appropriating the necessary resources, ensuring joint legislative control over war financing.

Q22. What factor most influences how congressional district boundaries are drawn?

🔹 A. Member seniority
🔹 B. Party affiliation
🔹 C. Population equality
🔹 D. Geographic size

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Districts are redrawn every ten years to reflect population changes captured by the census. The primary legal requirement is that each district contain roughly the same number of inhabitants, ensuring equal representation. While political considerations like gerrymandering exist, the constitutional principle of population equality remains the dominant factor.

Q23. A Representative wants to increase protective tariffs. Which party is most likely to support this measure?

🔹 A. Republicans
🔹 B. Democrats
🔹 C. Libertarians
🔹 D. Green Party

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Historically, the Republican Party has championed high protective tariffs to shield domestic manufacturers from foreign competition. This stance aligns with the interests of constituents who favor industrial growth and job preservation, making Republican legislators more inclined to back legislation that raises import duties.

Q24. If a bill is introduced in the Senate, which committee is most likely to review it first?

🔹 A. Judiciary Committee
🔹 B. Foreign Relations Committee
🔹 C. Armed Services Committee
🔹 D. Appropriations Committee

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Bills concerning foreign policy, treaties, or international trade typically go to the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. This committee examines the proposed legislation’s impact on diplomatic relations and national security before it proceeds to the full Senate for debate and voting.

Q25. Which piece of evidence supports the claim that U.S. party labels are less predictive than those in the UK Parliament?

🔹 A. Strict party‑whip voting
🔹 B. Uniform voting patterns
🔹 C. Frequent cross‑party votes
🔹 D. Mandatory party loyalty

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

In the United States, members of Congress often vote across party lines, reflecting regional interests and personal convictions rather than strict party discipline. This contrasts with the United Kingdom, where party whips enforce near‑unanimous voting, making party affiliation a stronger predictor of legislative behavior.

Q26. If tariffs are raised by \(10\%\) during the 1920s, what is the likely effect on domestic manufacturers?

🔹 A. Decrease competition
🔹 B. Increase domestic production
🔹 C. Raise consumer prices
🔹 D. Hurt exporters

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A \(10\%\) increase in tariffs makes imported goods more expensive, encouraging consumers to purchase domestically produced alternatives. This protective measure typically boosts domestic manufacturing output by reducing foreign competition, though it may also lead to higher prices for consumers and potential retaliation from trade partners.

Q27. A Congressman from a swing district faces a controversial vote. Which strategy is most likely to preserve his seat?

🔹 A. Vote strictly with party
🔹 B. Abstain from voting
🔹 C. Adopt a moderate position
🔹 D. Vote opposite to party platform

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Swing‑district representatives often prioritize the preferences of their diverse electorate. By adopting a moderate stance that seeks compromise, the congressman can appeal to both sides of the political spectrum, reducing the risk of alienating key voter groups and enhancing re‑election prospects.

Q28. When a Senate seat becomes vacant, how is it typically filled?

🔹 A. Governor appoints a replacement
🔹 B. Special election is held immediately
🔹 C. Seat remains vacant until next regular election
🔹 D. Senate appoints a temporary member

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Under the 17th Amendment, most states allow the governor to appoint an interim senator to fill a vacancy until a special election can be organized. This process ensures continuous representation for the state while giving voters the opportunity to select a permanent replacement at a later date.

Q29. Which constitutional clause grants Congress the power to regulate interstate commerce?

🔹 A. \(10^{th}\) Amendment
🔹 B. \(14^{th}\) Amendment
🔹 C. \(1^{st}\) Amendment
🔹 D. Commerce Clause

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Article I, Section 8 of the Constitution contains the Commerce Clause, which empowers Congress to regulate commerce among the states. This provision has been interpreted broadly, allowing federal legislation on a wide range of economic activities that cross state lines, and forms the basis for much of modern economic regulation.

Q30. After a joint conference committee produces a compromise bill, which house usually votes on it first?

🔹 A. Senate
🔹 B. House of Representatives
🔹 C. Joint committee votes directly
🔹 D. Both houses vote simultaneously

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Typically, the House of Representatives takes the first vote on a conference report after a joint conference committee has reconciled differences. Once the House approves the report, it is sent to the Senate for its final consideration. This sequencing reflects procedural traditions, though both chambers must ultimately adopt the bill.

Q31. Can a member of Congress be prosecuted for statements made on the floor under the Speech or Debate Clause?

🔹 A. Yes, they can be prosecuted
🔹 B. No, they enjoy absolute immunity
🔹 C. No, but they can be sued civilly
🔹 D. Yes, but only for crimes unrelated to speech

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Speech or Debate Clause provides members of Congress with immunity from prosecution for legislative acts, including speeches made on the floor. While they cannot be criminally prosecuted for those actions, they may still face civil lawsuits for conduct outside the scope of official legislative duties.

Q32. Which amendment lowered the voting age to \(18\) and thus affected congressional elections?

🔹 A. \(24^{th}\) Amendment
🔹 B. \(26^{th}\) Amendment
🔹 C. \(27^{th}\) Amendment
🔹 D. \(22^{nd}\) Amendment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The \(26^{th}\) Amendment, ratified in 1971, reduced the minimum voting age from \(21\) to \(18\) years. This change expanded the electorate, influencing the composition of the voting public and, consequently, the outcomes of elections for members of both the House and the Senate.

Q33. If a Representative serves two full terms, how many years has he or she served?

🔹 A. \(2\) years
🔹 B. \(4\) years
🔹 C. \(6\) years
🔹 D. \(8\) years

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each term in the House of Representatives lasts \(2\) years. Serving two full terms therefore amounts to \(2 \times 2 = 4\) years in office, allowing the member to gain experience while still being regularly accountable to voters through biennial elections.

Q34. Which of the following is NOT a power of Congress?

🔹 A. Appoint Supreme Court justices
🔹 B. Declare war
🔹 C. Coin money
🔹 D. Regulate interstate commerce

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The appointment of Supreme Court justices is a power reserved for the President, with the Senate providing advice and consent. While Congress possesses the authority to declare war, coin money, and regulate interstate commerce, it does not have the constitutional authority to directly appoint members of the judiciary.

Q35. If the President vetoes a bill that has passed both houses, what majority is needed to override the veto?

🔹 A. Simple majority
🔹 B. Three‑quarters majority
🔹 C. Two‑thirds majority
🔹 D. Unanimous consent

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

To override a presidential veto, both the House and the Senate must each achieve a two‑thirds (\( \frac{2}{3} \)) majority of the members present and voting. This supermajority requirement ensures that only legislation with broad bipartisan support can become law despite the President’s objection.

Q36. How does the simple‑majority requirement in both houses affect legislative gridlock in a polarized Congress?

🔹 A. It eliminates gridlock entirely
🔹 B. It reduces the likelihood of compromise
🔹 C. It makes gridlock more likely when parties are evenly split
🔹 D. It forces the President to intervene

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When each chamber requires only a simple majority, a highly polarized Congress often finds it difficult to achieve the necessary bipartisan support for legislation, especially if the two parties are evenly divided. This situation can increase the frequency of stalemates, requiring negotiation or procedural maneuvers to break the deadlock.

Q37. A treaty needs two‑thirds Senate approval. The Senate currently has a \(60\)–\(40\) split. What is the minimum number of senators who must change their vote to achieve ratification?

🔹 A. \(5\)
🔹 B. \(6\)
🔹 C. \(7\)
🔹 D. \(8\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The treaty requires at least \(67\) affirmative votes (\( \frac{2}{3} \times 100\)). With only \(60\) yes votes, the gap is \(7\). Therefore, at least seven senators must switch from no to yes to reach the required two‑thirds majority for ratification.

Q38. If a law containing a revenue provision violates the Origination Clause, what is the likely outcome?

🔹 A. The law is struck down by the courts
🔹 B. The House must rewrite the provision
🔹 C. The Senate must approve it retroactively
🔹 D. The provision is upheld as constitutional

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Origination Clause requires that all bills for raising revenue originate in the House of Representatives. If a law that includes a revenue measure is found to have been improperly originated in the Senate, the judiciary may invalidate that portion of the statute, requiring the House to re‑draft the provision before it can be enacted.

Q39. During a joint conference, the House proposes a \(5\%\) increase in defense spending and the Senate opposes it. How is the final bill determined?

🔹 A. The House decides unilaterally
🔹 B. The Senate decides unilaterally
🔹 C. The joint committee votes on the amendment
🔹 D. Both houses must approve the conference report as amended

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

After a joint conference committee reaches a compromise, the resulting conference report—including any defense‑spending amendment—must be voted on and approved by both the House and the Senate. Only after both chambers adopt the report does the legislation become law, ensuring mutual consent on the final content.

Q40. Compare the impact of the \lame‑duck\ period on legislative productivity in the House versus the Senate.

🔹 A. The House is more productive during lame‑duck
🔹 B. The Senate is more productive during lame‑duck
🔹 C. Both chambers show similar productivity patterns
🔹 D. The Senate is less productive than the House

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Empirical studies show that both the House and the Senate experience comparable declines in legislative activity during lame‑duck sessions. The approaching turnover of members reduces incentives to pursue controversial initiatives, leading to a general slowdown in bill introductions and passage across both chambers during this transitional period.

Q41. If a Representative introduces a bill that conflicts with a prior Senate amendment, who has the final say?

🔹 A. The House
🔹 B. The Senate
🔹 C. The President
🔹 D. The Supreme Court

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When a bill emerging from a conference committee contains conflicting language, the Senate's position often prevails because the Senate holds the authority to approve its own amendments. However, the final version must still be accepted by both chambers; if disagreements persist, further negotiation or a new conference may be required.

Q42. Which step in the constitutional amendment process has historically been the most difficult to achieve?

🔹 A. Two‑thirds approval in both houses
🔹 B. Ratification by three‑fourths of the states
🔹 C. Drafting the amendment language
🔹 D. Presidential signature

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Throughout U.S. history, securing ratification by three‑fourths of the states has proven the toughest hurdle. While many proposed amendments have cleared the two‑thirds congressional threshold, achieving the widespread state‑level consensus needed for final adoption has been comparatively rare and challenging.
