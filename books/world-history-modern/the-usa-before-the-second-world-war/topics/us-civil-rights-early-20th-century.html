What is US civil rights early 20th century?

In the early twentieth century, African American activists and organizations like the NAACP challenged segregation and discrimination through legal challenges, protests, and advocacy, laying the groundwork for the later civil rights movement despite widespread resistance.

Q1. Who was the first Black man to earn a Ph.D. from Harvard University?

A.W. E. B. Du Bois ✅
B.Booker T. Washington
C.Marcus Garvey
D.Frederick Douglass
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: W. E. B. Du Bois earned his doctorate from Harvard in 1895, becoming the first African‑American to achieve this distinction. His academic success challenged prevailing stereotypes and inspired later generations of Black scholars, establishing a model for intellectual achievement that the NAACP later emphasized in its advocacy for education and civil rights.

Q2. In what year was the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People (NAACP) founded?

A.1909
B.1910 ✅
C.1911
D.1912
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The NAACP was formally established in 1910, emerging from a series of meetings among Black and white activists who sought a coordinated response to racial violence and discrimination. Its founding marked a shift toward organized, national advocacy, and the organization quickly became a central vehicle for legal challenges against segregation and disenfranchisement.

Q4. What was the name of the organization Marcus Garvey established in 1914 in Jamaica?

A.Southern Christian Leadership Conference
B.National Urban League
C.Congress of Racial Equality
D.Universal Negro Improvement Association ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Garvey founded the Universal Negro Improvement Association (UNIA) in 1914 while still in Jamaica. The UNIA promoted Black pride, economic self‑reliance, and a vision of a united African diaspora. Its rapid expansion into the United States laid the groundwork for Garvey’s later activities in Harlem, including his newspaper and the Black Star Line.

Q5. What was the title of the NAACP’s magazine edited by W. E. B. Du Bois?

A.The Crisis ✅
B.The Liberator
C.The Voice
D.The Advocate
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: \The Crisis\ was launched in 1910 under the editorship of W. E. B. Du Bois. The magazine featured essays, news, and literary works that highlighted African‑American achievements and exposed racial injustices. By circulating powerful ideas and documenting discrimination, it helped galvanize support for the NAACP’s legal and educational campaigns.

Q6. Du Bois criticized the vocational education at Tuskegee because he believed it was designed to keep Black youth in which region?

A.The North
B.The Midwest
C.The Rural South ✅
D.The West
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Du Bois argued that Tuskegee’s focus on industrial training served to confine African‑American workers to the agrarian South, limiting their opportunities for professional advancement in emerging urban economies. He believed that higher education and broader skill development were essential for Black people to compete in the rapidly industrializing North and achieve full civil rights.

Q7. A Black lawyer in 1912 wants to challenge segregation in public schools. Which organization provides the most effective legal support?

A.NAACP ✅
B.UNIA
C.Black Star Line
D.Guardian Newspaper
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The NAACP, founded that same year, quickly assembled a team of lawyers dedicated to confronting segregation through the courts. Its early legal strategy emphasized test cases that could set precedents, making it the most suitable organization for a lawyer seeking judicial remedies against discriminatory school policies.

Q8. Which statement best captures Du Bois’s critique of Booker T. Washington’s approach?

A.Washington’s focus on industrial education was sufficient for racial progress.
B.Washington’s strategy was too aggressive and threatened white interests.
C.Washington’s emphasis on accommodation ignored the demand for political rights. ✅
D.Washington’s plan prioritized political rights over economic stability.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Du Bois contended that Washington’s accommodationist stance, which emphasized vocational training and social deference, neglected the fundamental need for political enfranchisement and civil liberties. By ignoring the push for voting rights and legal equality, Washington’s approach, in Du Bois’s view, perpetuated a system that kept African‑Americans subordinate.

Q9. Which Garvey venture was a steamship company owned and operated by Black investors?

A.Black Star Line ✅
B.Harlem Renaissance Press
C.Negro World Publishing
D.African Trade Co.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Black Star Line, launched in 1919, was Garvey’s ambitious attempt to create a Black‑owned maritime fleet that could facilitate trade and transport for people of African descent. Though it ultimately faltered financially, the line symbolized Garvey’s commitment to economic independence and his broader vision of a self‑sufficient Black diaspora.

Q10. The 1905 Niagara conference statement emphasized that the voice of protest must never cease. Which later civil‑rights tactic most directly reflects this principle?

A.Sit‑in protests
B.Legal challenges to segregation
C.March on Washington ✅
D.Boycott of bus system
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The phrase “the voice of protest must never cease” anticipates the large, peaceful demonstrations such as the 1963 March on Washington, where thousands gathered to publicly demand civil‑rights reforms. This mass mobilization embodied the continuity of protest that the Niagara statement called for, linking early rhetoric to later iconic actions.

Q11. Approximately how many African Americans lived in the United States at the turn of the twentieth century?

A.\(8\) million
B.\(9\) million
C.\(10\) million ✅
D.\(12\) million
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Census data from the 1900 United States Census recorded roughly \(10\) million African‑American residents. This figure underscores the demographic weight of the Black population, particularly as the majority—about nine million—were concentrated in the Southern states, where they faced entrenched segregation and economic oppression.

Q13. Which phrase from the Niagara group’s founding statement reflects a rejection of passive acceptance?

B.The voice of protest...
C.America is unjust...
D.Assail the ears of their fellows...
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The opening clause, “We refuse to allow the impressions to remain…,” explicitly repudiates any notion that African‑Americans would passively accept inferiority. It signals an active stance against oppression, setting a tone of defiance that would inform subsequent protest movements.

Q14. Marcus Garvey’s idea of a ‘return to Africa’ is most closely associated with which later movement?

A.Civil Rights Movement of the 1960s
B.Black Power movement
C.Pan‑Africanism ✅
D.Integrationist approach
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Garvey’s advocacy for a diaspora return to Africa presaged Pan‑Africanism, a transnational movement that emphasized unity among peoples of African descent worldwide. Later leaders such as Kwame Nkrumah and organizations like the Organization of African Unity drew inspiration from Garvey’s vision of a shared African heritage and political solidarity.

Q15. What was the primary purpose of the Black Factories Corporation founded by Garvey?

A.To provide industrial jobs for Black workers ✅
B.To fund legal battles against segregation
C.To publish Black newspapers
D.To organize political rallies
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Garvey created the Black Factories Corporation to stimulate economic development by establishing manufacturing enterprises staffed by African‑American workers. This initiative aimed to foster economic independence, reduce reliance on white‑owned businesses, and demonstrate the capacity of Black communities to run successful industrial operations.

Q16. Which of the following best describes the NAACP’s strategy for achieving civil rights?

A.Legal action and education ✅
B.Separate Black institutions
C.Economic self‑reliance through businesses
D.Armed resistance
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: From its inception, the NAACP pursued a dual strategy of litigation—challenging discriminatory laws in court—and education—promoting academic achievement to dismantle racist myths. This approach sought to secure constitutional protections while reshaping public perceptions of African‑American capabilities.

Q17. If a Black community in 1918 wanted to promote racial pride through cultural events, which leader’s model would they most likely emulate?

A.W. E. B. Du Bois
B.Booker T. Washington
C.Marcus Garvey ✅
D.Frederick Douglass
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Garvey’s emphasis on Black nationalism, pride, and cultural celebration resonated with communities seeking to affirm their identity. His use of festivals, parades, and a dedicated newspaper provided a template for communal events that highlighted African heritage and fostered a sense of collective empowerment.

Q18. The term ‘Black nationalism’ as used by Garvey primarily meant:

A.Political integration into the US
B.Cultural assimilation with whites
C.Advocacy for a separate Black nation ✅
D.Economic cooperation with white businesses
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Garvey defined Black nationalism as the pursuit of political, economic, and cultural self‑determination for people of African descent, often envisioning a separate nation or autonomous territories. This stance contrasted sharply with integrationist strategies and underscored a desire for independence from white‑controlled institutions.

Q19. Which of the following was NOT a goal of the NAACP during its early years?

A.Ending lynching
B.Promoting segregation ✅
C.Advocating higher education
D.Legal challenges to Jim Crow
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The NAACP’s early agenda focused on combating lynching, securing higher education opportunities, and mounting legal challenges against Jim Crow laws. Promoting segregation was antithetical to its mission; therefore, it was never a goal of the organization.

Q20. The editorial stance of The Crisis under Du Bois can be best described as:

A.Conservative and accommodationist
B.Radical and militant
C.Moderate and reformist ✅
D.Isolationist
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: While Du Bois advocated for assertive civil‑rights action, his writings in \The Crisis\ balanced radical ideas with pragmatic reformism. He emphasized legal challenges, education, and moral arguments, positioning the magazine as a moderate yet forward‑looking voice within the broader struggle for equality.

Q21. During the Great Migration, many Southern Blacks moved north seeking better opportunities. Which statement aligns with Garvey’s view of this migration?

A.It would inevitably lead to full equality
B.It would cause cultural dilution
C.It highlighted the need for Black self‑determination ✅
D.It was a temporary phenomenon
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Garvey interpreted the mass movement of African‑Americans to northern cities as evidence that Black people required their own institutions and economic structures. He argued that reliance on white‑controlled systems would perpetuate oppression, thus reinforcing his call for self‑determination and separate Black enterprises.

Q22. Which legal victory was achieved by the NAACP in the 1930s, setting a precedent for later desegregation cases?

A.Brown v. Board of Education
B.Buchanan v. Warley ✅
C.Plessy v. Ferguson
D.Shelley v. Kraemer
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In 1917, the NAACP successfully argued the case Buchanan v. Warley, which struck down racially restrictive housing covenants. This decision established an important legal precedent that segregationist policies could be invalidated in court, paving the way for later victories such as Brown v. Board of Education.

Q24. The phrase ‘the voice of protest must never cease’ from the Niagara statement anticipates which later rhetorical strategy?

A.Non‑violent resistance ✅
B.Militant armed uprising
C.Legal incrementalism
D.Economic boycotts
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Niagara declaration’s insistence on constant protest foreshadows the philosophy of non‑violent resistance championed by leaders like Martin Luther King Jr. This strategy relied on persistent, peaceful demonstrations to maintain moral pressure on oppressors, echoing the earlier call for an unending protest voice.

C.They never met
D.They co‑authored The Crisis
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Du Bois and Trotter worked together to organize the 1905 Niagara conference, which produced the Niagara group’s founding statement. Their partnership combined Du Bois’s scholarly influence with Trotter’s activist zeal, demonstrating a collaborative effort to articulate a more confrontational civil‑rights agenda.

Q26. If a Black student in 1913 wanted to attend a Northern university, which NAACP initiative would most directly support him?

A.Scholarship fund ✅
B.Business incubator
C.Shipping line
D.Cultural festival

📖 Explanation: The NAACP established scholarship programs early on to assist promising African‑American students in gaining access to higher education. By providing financial aid, these scholarships directly facilitated enrollment at Northern institutions, aligning with the organization’s broader emphasis on education as a pathway to equality.

Q27. Garvey’s emphasis on ‘Black pride’ most directly challenged which prevailing societal belief?

A.That Blacks were intellectually inferior ✅
B.That Blacks should integrate economically
C.That Blacks were politically neutral
D.That Blacks were culturally homogeneous
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Garvey’s rhetoric celebrated African heritage and demanded respect, directly confronting the pervasive stereotype that Black people lacked intellectual capacity. By asserting cultural and racial dignity, he sought to overturn the belief that African‑Americans were inherently inferior.

Q28. Which statement reflects the NAACP’s view on segregation during the early 1900s?

A.Segregation was a temporary necessity
B.Segregation should be challenged through the courts
C.Segregation was beneficial for Black communities
D.Segregation could be solved by vocational training alone ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The NAACP consistently argued that segregation violated constitutional rights and should be dismantled via legal action. By filing lawsuits and supporting test cases, the organization aimed to overturn “separate but equal” doctrines, reflecting its belief that judicial avenues were essential to achieving civil‑rights goals.

Q29. Analyze how Du Bois’s concept of the ‘Talented Tenth’ influenced the NAACP’s early leadership composition. Which outcome best illustrates this influence?

A.Predominance of clergy in leadership
B.Leadership dominated by highly educated professionals ✅
C.Leadership focused on labor union organization
D.Leadership mainly composed of business owners
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Du Bois’s ‘Talented Tenth’ ideal advocated that a minority of highly educated African‑Americans would lead the community toward progress. Consequently, the NAACP’s early executive board consisted largely of scholars, lawyers, and physicians, reflecting the belief that intellectual elites could effectively challenge segregation and inspire broader societal change.

Q30. Considering Garvey’s economic ventures, evaluate why the Black Star Line failed to achieve its intended impact. Which factor was most decisive?

A.Lack of sufficient capital ✅
B.Government sabotage
C.Resistance from white labor unions
D.Overreliance on African ports
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Black Star Line suffered from chronic underfunding; insufficient capital hindered ship acquisition, maintenance, and operational efficiency. While government scrutiny and racial opposition contributed, the primary obstacle was the inability to raise enough financial resources to sustain a viable trans‑Atlantic shipping enterprise.

Q31. Compare the legal strategies of the NAACP with the political tactics of the UNIA. Which best captures the key distinction?

A.NAACP used courtroom litigation while UNIA pursued mass rallies ✅
B.NAACP focused on economic boycotts while UNIA emphasized legal reforms
C.Both relied primarily on violent protest
D.Both avoided any form of public demonstration
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The NAACP’s hallmark was strategic litigation aimed at overturning discriminatory laws, whereas the UNIA, led by Garvey, emphasized large public gatherings, parades, and a charismatic appeal to Black nationalism. This contrast highlights the NAACP’s institutional, court‑centered approach versus the UNIA’s mass‑mobilization, populist methodology.
