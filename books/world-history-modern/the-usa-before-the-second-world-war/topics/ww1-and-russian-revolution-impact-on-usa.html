Q1. What campaign slogan did Woodrow Wilson use to win the 1916 presidential election?

🔹 A. He Kept Us Out of the War
🔹 B. Make America Great Again
🔹 C. Peace, Not War
🔹 D. America First

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Wilson’s 1916 platform centered on keeping the United States out of the growing conflict in Europe. The slogan “He Kept Us Out of the War” highlighted his neutrality stance, resonating with voters weary of overseas entanglements and helping secure his narrow re‑election.

Q2. In which year did the February/March Russian Revolution that overthrew Tsar Nicholas II occur?

🔹 A. 1915
🔹 B. 1916
🔹 C. 1917
🔹 D. 1918

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The February/March Revolution unfolded in 1917, when masses of workers, soldiers, and peasants forced Tsar Nicholas II to abdicate. This pivotal event ended centuries of autocratic rule and ushered in a provisional government, dramatically altering Russia’s political landscape during World War I.

Q3. Why were many Americans initially reluctant to support entry into World War I before 1917?

🔹 A. Because they feared alliance with Germany
🔹 B. Because they considered Russia the most undemocratic state
🔹 C. Because they did not want to fight a democratic nation
🔹 D. Because of isolationist sentiment

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A strong tradition of isolationism dominated U.S. public opinion, as many citizens believed that involvement in European wars would threaten domestic priorities and entangle America in foreign disputes. This sentiment, combined with skepticism about the democratic credentials of Russia, made many Americans hesitant to endorse war.

Q4. What was the primary purpose of the Espionage Act passed by Congress in June 1917?

🔹 A. To regulate war profits
🔹 B. To punish those encouraging draft evasion
🔹 C. To expand voting rights
🔹 D. To fund the Red Cross

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Espionage Act was crafted to suppress dissent that might undermine the war effort, specifically targeting individuals who urged others to avoid military service. By criminalizing attempts to influence draft‑age men, the law aimed to maintain troop levels and prevent the spread of anti‑war sentiment deemed harmful to national security.

Q5. If Germany had resumed unrestricted submarine warfare in early 1917, which of the following actions would have most closely aligned with Wilson’s stated policy objectives?

🔹 A. Immediate declaration of war
🔹 B. Diplomatic protest followed by limited naval escort
🔹 C. Maintaining strict neutrality
🔹 D. Increasing domestic propaganda only

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Resuming unrestricted submarine attacks would have threatened American lives and commerce, prompting Wilson to issue a formal diplomatic protest while bolstering naval protection for U.S. vessels. This measured response reflected his desire to avoid premature war while defending American interests and upholding the principle of neutral rights.

Q6. How did the Bolsheviks’ order for a cease‑fire and peace talks with Germany in late 1917 influence American public opinion toward the new Russian government?

🔹 A. Strengthened support for the Bolsheviks
🔹 B. Led to accusations of German collusion
🔹 C. Prompted immediate US recognition
🔹 D. Had no impact

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Bolsheviks’ unilateral cease‑fire and negotiations with the German Empire alarmed Allied governments and the American public, who interpreted the move as favoring Germany. Consequently, U.S. officials branded the new regime as agents of German imperialism, eroding any goodwill that might have arisen from the overthrow of the autocratic Tsar.

Q7. By how many percentage points did the socialist vote in Chicago increase between the 1915 and 1917 municipal elections?

🔹 A. 30.1
🔹 B. 31
🔹 C. 31.1
🔹 D. 31.2

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The socialist share rose from 3.6 % in 1915 to 34.7 % in 1917. Subtracting the earlier figure gives \(34.7 - 3.6 = 31.1\) percentage points, illustrating a dramatic surge in support for socialist candidates during a period of heightened class consciousness and anti‑war activism.
