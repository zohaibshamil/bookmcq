What is Spanish Civil War 1936-1939?

From 1936 to 1939, the Spanish Civil War raged as a brutal conflict between Nationalist forces led by Franco and Republican defenders, characterized by foreign intervention from Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy supporting the rebels while the Soviet Union aided the Republic, ultimately ending in a decisive Nationalist victory.

📝 All Spanish Civil War 1936-1939 MCQs

Q1. Who was the monarch of Spain when the Second Republic was proclaimed in 1931?

A.Alfonso XIII ✅
B.Juan Carlos I
C.Felipe VI
D.Charles V
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Alfonso XIII had been king since 1885, but after the municipal elections of April 1931 showed overwhelming Republican support, he chose to abdicate to avoid bloodshed. His departure cleared the way for the proclamation of the Second Spanish Republic, marking the end of the constitutional monarchy without violent conflict.

Q2. Which military figure ruled Spain from 1923 to 1930 before the 1931 elections?

A.General Francisco Franco
B.General Miguel Primo de Rivera ✅
C.General José Sanjurjo
D.General Manuel Azaña
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: General Miguel Primo de Rivera seized power in a bloodless coup in 1923 with the king’s approval and governed as a military dictator for seven years. Although not a fascist, he pursued public works and ended the Rif war, but his mishandling of the economy during the 1930 crisis led to his resignation.

Q3. What major colonial conflict did Primo de Rivera successfully conclude in 1925?

A.The Spanish‑American War
B.The Rif War in Morocco ✅
C.The Cuban War of Independence
D.The Philippine Revolution
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Primo de Rivera’s most notable foreign achievement was ending the Rif War, a long‑running rebellion in Spanish Morocco led by Abd‑el‑Krim. By 1925 Spanish forces had suppressed the uprising, restoring colonial control and allowing the regime to claim a military victory, even as domestic problems persisted.

Q4. Which sector experienced a roughly one‑third decline in production during the early 1930s depression?

A.Textiles
B.Iron ✅
C.Agriculture
D.Shipbuilding
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Industrial output fell sharply during the worldwide economic crisis. In Spain, iron production dropped by about one‑third, reflecting reduced demand and investment. This contraction contributed to rising unemployment and heightened social tensions, setting the stage for political instability that later erupted into civil war.

Q5. Which of the following regions sought independence from Spain in the 1930s?

A.Andalusia
B.Catalonia ✅
C.Extremadura
D.La Rioja
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Catalonia, along with the Basque provinces, pursued greater autonomy and even outright independence during the turbulent 1930s. Their aspirations clashed with central government policies, fueling political polarization and contributing to the broader social conflicts that culminated in the 1936 civil war.

Q6. What was the primary reason the Roman Catholic Church opposed the Second Republic?

A.Support for socialism
B.Desire for land reform
C.Efforts to reduce its political power ✅
D.Alignment with fascist Italy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Catholic Church viewed the Republic’s secular reforms—such as limiting religious education, confiscating church lands, and curbing clerical influence—as direct attacks on its traditional authority. This antagonism deepened societal divisions, as many conservatives rallied to defend the Church, while left‑leaning groups pushed for further secularization.

Q7. How did the 1930 world economic crisis affect Spain’s currency?

A.It strengthened the peseta
B.It caused hyperinflation
C.It led to depreciation of the peseta ✅
D.It had no effect
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The global downturn reached Spain in 1930, triggering rising unemployment and fiscal mismanagement. Primo de Rivera’s government failed to stabilize finances, resulting in the depreciation of the Spanish peseta. The weakened currency eroded public confidence and strained the economy, contributing to the political unrest of the era.

Q8. Which of the following best describes Primo de Rivera’s political ideology?

A.Fascist
B.Communist
C.Military dictatorship without fascist elements ✅
D.Liberal democracy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Although Primo de Rivera exercised authoritarian control as a military dictator, he did not adopt fascist doctrines such as extreme nationalism or totalitarian party structures. His regime focused on technocratic modernization and public works, distinguishing it from the later fascist movements that emerged in Europe.

Q9. A Spanish farmer in 1932 faces falling wine prices, reduced export demand, and rising unemployment. Which of the following policies would most directly alleviate his situation?

A.Nationalizing all farms
B.Implementing price supports for wine ✅
C.Increasing military conscription
D.Abolishing property taxes
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Price supports would guarantee a minimum income for wine producers, counteracting market declines and helping farmers sustain livelihoods. While broader reforms like land redistribution might address structural issues, immediate financial relief is best achieved through targeted price stabilization mechanisms.

Q10. A labor union leader in 1935 argues that the decline in steel production (down by almost half) threatens workers’ rights. Which argument best supports his claim?

A.Steel’s decline shows the economy is healthy
B.Reduced steel output means fewer jobs and lower wages for workers ✅
C.Higher steel output would increase unemployment
D.Steel decline is unrelated to labor conditions
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A halving of steel production directly reduces the demand for labor, leading to layoffs, wage cuts, and weakened bargaining power. The union leader can therefore argue that the industry’s contraction jeopardizes workers’ livelihoods and necessitates protective labor legislation.

Q11. What event triggered the abdication of King Alfonso XIII?

A.A military coup
B.Municipal elections where Republicans won major cities ✅
C.A popular referendum
D.A foreign invasion
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In April 1931, municipal elections across Spain saw Republican candidates dominate the major urban centers. Massive crowds gathered in Madrid, and fearing civil conflict, King Alfonso XIII chose to abdicate, allowing the proclamation of the Second Republic without bloodshed.

Q12. Which of the following was NOT a public work undertaken by Primo de Rivera?

B.Irrigation schemes
C.Building of massive pyramids ✅
D.Road development
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Primo de Rivera’s domestic agenda emphasized modern infrastructure: expanding rail networks, constructing roads, and implementing irrigation projects to boost agriculture. He never pursued the fantastical notion of building pyramids, a clearly absurd option that highlights the other three genuine achievements.

Q13. During the early 1930s, which economic sector suffered the greatest proportional decline?

While agriculture also faced difficulties, the magnitude of the decline in iron output was particularly severe, reflecting broader industrial contraction during the depression.

Q14. A political analyst in 1934 predicts that the army’s discontent could lead to a coup. Which factor most strengthens this prediction?

📖 Explanation: The perception that the military wielded too much political power created resentment within the ranks, especially as civilian governments sought to limit its role. This tension, combined with economic instability, made the prospect of an army‑led coup increasingly plausible.

Q15. Which of the following best explains why the 1936 civil war was postponed until that year despite earlier tensions?

A.Immediate foreign intervention
B.A sudden economic boom
C.The monarchy’s peaceful transition in 1931
D.The army’s continued support for the Republic until 1936 ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Although the Republic faced numerous challenges, the army initially remained neutral or supportive, preventing an immediate outbreak of conflict. It was only after growing polarization, radicalization, and an increasingly hostile military that the situation escalated into full‑scale civil war in 1936.

Q16. A historian argues that the 1925 end of the Moroccan war reduced the likelihood of civil war in Spain. Which reasoning is most convincing?

A.Colonial victories boost national unity and distract from internal divisions ✅
B.Ending the war depleted Spain’s finances, increasing internal unrest
C.The war’s end removed a source of military experience that later fueled rebellion
D.All of the above
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A successful colonial campaign can temporarily foster national pride and unity, diverting attention from domestic grievances. By concluding the Rif conflict, Primo de Rivera’s regime gained short‑term legitimacy, which may have delayed, though not prevented, the later escalation of internal conflicts.

Q17. What percentage decline did steel production experience during the early 1930s depression?

📖 Explanation: The Republic’s secular reforms and attempts to curtail the Church’s influence generated bitter opposition from the Catholic hierarchy. This antagonistic relationship deepened societal divisions, as the Church rallied conservative forces while the Republic pursued further anti‑clerical measures, fueling the polarization that preceded the war.

Q19. An economist in 1933 argues that the depreciation of the peseta will lead to inflation. Which mechanism best supports this claim?

A.A weaker currency makes imports more expensive, raising prices ✅
B.Depreciation reduces government spending
C.A stronger peseta reduces export competitiveness
D.Currency depreciation has no effect on inflation
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When a currency loses value, imported goods become costlier, transmitting higher prices to domestic consumers. This import‑price inflation, combined with domestic monetary expansion, can erode purchasing power, supporting the economist’s forecast of rising overall price levels.

This shift aligns with the Republic’s objective of diminishing clerical influence over education and promoting a more neutral, non‑religious learning environment.

This rapid growth helped modernize the economy, though later financial mismanagement undermined these gains.

These entrenched divisions suggest that, despite temporary peace, the structural tensions made large‑scale conflict highly probable.
