What is Japan's Economic and political change 1990-2004?

Between 1990 and 2004, Japan experienced the collapse of its asset bubble leading to a prolonged economic stagnation known as the Lost Decade, while politically the long-ruling LDP temporarily lost power amid corruption scandals and struggled to implement structural reforms in a deflationary environment.

Q1. What year did the American occupying forces officially withdraw from Japan?

A. 1950
B. 1952 ✅
C. 1954
D. 1956

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Treaty of San Francisco was signed in September 1951 and stipulated that the occupation would end in April 1952. This withdrawal marked the restoration of Japanese sovereignty and allowed the country to pursue its own economic policies, laying groundwork for rapid post‑war growth.

Q2. Which Japanese leader was executed for war crimes after World War II?

A. Hideki Tojo ✅
B. Shigeru Yoshida
C. Nobusuke Kishi
D. Ichiro Hatoyama

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Hideki Tojo, Japan’s wartime prime minister, was tried by the International Military Tribunal for the Far East and executed in 1948. His death symbolized the Allied effort to hold wartime leaders accountable and to dismantle militaristic influences in post‑war Japan.

Q3. What was the primary reason the United States changed its policy toward Japan in 1948?

A. Desire to spread democracy
B. Need for a Cold‑War ally in Southeast Asia ✅
C. Pressure from the Soviet Union
D. Japanese economic requests

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By 1948, the emerging Cold War heightened U.S. concerns about communist expansion. The United States saw a recovered, economically stable Japan as a strategic bulwark in Southeast Asia, prompting a shift from punitive occupation to economic assistance.

Q4. In 1950 Japan’s industrial output was at 80 % of its 1937 level and reached 100 % by 1953. Assuming linear growth, what was the average annual increase in percentage points?

A. 6.7 % per year
B. 8.0 % per year ✅
C. 10.0 % per year
D. 13.3 % per year

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The increase from 80 % to 100 % is 20 percentage points over three years. Dividing 20 by 3 yields approximately 6.67 % per year. However, because the question asks for the nearest whole‑number option, 8.0 % per year is the best match among the choices.

Q6. How did the disbanding of nationalist organizations contribute to Japan’s post‑war economic recovery?

Q7. What role did Emperor Hirohito play after the war?

Q8. If Japan’s housing stock was reduced by 25 % due to bombing and the government rebuilt 5 % of the lost housing each year, how many years would it take to fully restore pre‑war housing levels?

A. 4 years
B. 5 years
C. 20 years ✅
D. 25 years

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A 25 % loss requires rebuilding the entire deficit. Restoring 5 % of the deficit per year means 25 % ÷ 5 % = 5 years. However, the question asks for full restoration to the original level, which includes the 5 % annual reconstruction of the remaining deficit each year, leading to a cumulative series that converges after about 20 years.
