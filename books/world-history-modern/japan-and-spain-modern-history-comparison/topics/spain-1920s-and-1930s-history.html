What is Spain 1920s and 1930s history?

During the 1920s and 1930s, Spain endured extreme political instability marked by the fall of the monarchy, the establishment of a fragile Second Republic, and intense polarization between leftist reformers and right-wing traditionalists over land reform, regional autonomy, and the role of the Catholic Church.

📝 All Spain 1920s and 1930s history MCQs

Q1. Who was the reigning monarch of Spain at the start of the 1920s?

A.Alfonso XIII ✅
B.Juan Carlos I
C.Félix VI
D.Charles V
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Alfonso XIII had been king since 1885 and remained on the throne throughout the early 1920s. His reign was marked by political instability and eventually led to the proclamation of the Second Republic in 1931 after widespread municipal election defeats.

Q2. In which year did General Primo de Rivera successfully end the war in Morocco?

A.1923
B.1925 ✅
C.1927
D.1929
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The conflict in Spanish Morocco concluded in 1925 when Primo de Rivera negotiated a cease‑fire and deployed additional forces, ending the long‑running guerrilla war. This victory boosted his domestic standing, even though his later economic policies would later undermine his regime.

Q3. If industrial production grew at three times the pre‑1923 rate, and the output in 1922 was \(100\) units, what would be the expected output by 1929 assuming linear growth?

A.300 ✅
B.400
C.500
D.600
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A three‑fold increase means the production would be \(3 \times 100 = 300\) units. This simple proportional calculation illustrates the dramatic industrial expansion credited to Primo de Rivera’s public‑works program, even though the growth was not sustained after the economic crisis of 1930.

Q4. The 1930 economic crisis raised unemployment by 20% in a labor force of 10 million. How many additional people became unemployed?

A.1 million
B.2 million ✅
C.3 million
D.4 million
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A 20 % rise on a 10 million workforce adds \(0.20 \times 10{,}000{,}000 = 2{,}000{,}000\) unemployed workers. This sharp increase contributed to social unrest and eroded confidence in Primo de Rivera’s government, prompting the army to withdraw its support.

Q5. Which political event in April 1931 led to King Alfonso XIII’s abdication?

A.General election
B.municipal elections ✅
C.referendum
D.parliamentary vote
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Municipal elections held on 12 April 1931 saw Republicans win in major cities, triggering massive street demonstrations in Madrid. Fearing bloodshed, Alfonso XIII chose to abdicate, allowing the Second Spanish Republic to be proclaimed without armed conflict.

Q6. How did the Roman Catholic Church’s opposition to the Republic most directly increase political polarization?

A.By supporting land reform
B.By financing right‑wing militias
C.By resisting separation of church and state
D.By endorsing regional independence ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Church’s bitter hostility, especially its opposition to measures such as the separation of church and state and the expulsion of Jesuits, rallied conservative forces. This deepened the divide between left‑leaning republicans and right‑wing traditionalists, setting the stage for the 1936 civil war.

Q7. If a landowner lost 15 % of his 200‑hectare estate due to nationalization, how many hectares does he retain?

A.170 ✅
B.185
C.190
D.195
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Losing 15 % of 200 ha leaves \(200 \times (1-0.15) = 170\) hectares. The redistribution of large estates was a key reform of the Republic, but it provoked strong backlash from conservative landowners who felt their property rights were being undermined.

Q8. When iron production fell by one‑third from an original \(300{,}000\) tons, what was the new production level?

A.200 000 tons
B.100 000 tons
C.150 000 tons ✅
D.250 000 tons
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A reduction of one‑third means the output becomes two‑thirds of the original: \(300{,}000 \times \frac{2}{3}=200{,}000\) tons. However, the correct answer reflecting the stated drop is \(300{,}000 - 100{,}000 = 200{,}000\) tons, which corresponds to option C.

Q9. Which nickname did King Alfonso XIII give to General Primo de Rivera, reflecting his view of the dictator?

A.'My Mussolini' ✅
B.'My Father'
C.'My Savior'
D.'My General'
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Alfonso XIII referred to Primo de Rivera as ‘my Mussolini’, indicating both admiration for his strong leadership and a willingness to accept a military figure as a stabilizing force, despite the latter’s lack of fascist ideology.

Q10. Which reform most directly angered right‑wing groups during the early Republic?

A.Land nationalization
B.Separation of church and state ✅
C.Compulsory retirement of officers
D.Wage increases
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The separation of church and state—ending government payments to priests, expelling Jesuits, and halting religious education—directly challenged the traditional influence of the Catholic Church, provoking fierce opposition from conservative and right‑wing factions.

Q11. If the Republic aimed to raise the average industrial wage by 10 % from 8 000 pesetas, what would be the new wage?

A.8 800 pesetas ✅
B.9 000 pesetas
C.9 200 pesetas
D.9 500 pesetas
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A 10 % increase on 8 000 pesetas yields \(8{,}000 \times 1.10 = 8{,}800\) pesetas. Raising wages was intended to secure workers’ loyalty, but the financial strain of such policies contributed to the fiscal instability that plagued the Republic.
