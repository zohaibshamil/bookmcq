What is Spanish Civil War 1936 breakout?

The Spanish Civil War broke out in July 1936 when a group of conservative military generals launched a coup against the democratically elected Republican government, triggering immediate widespread violence as workers' militias resisted the uprising and split the country into opposing zones.

📝 All Spanish Civil War 1936 breakout MCQs

Q1. In which year did the Spanish Civil War begin?

A.1935
B.1936 ✅
C.1937
D.1938
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The conflict erupted on 17 July 1936 when a faction of the Spanish army, led by nationalist officers, launched a coup against the democratically elected Second Republic. This date marks the official start of the three‑year civil war that would devastate the country and draw in international powers.

Q2. Which region was granted limited self‑government before the war, fueling separatist aspirations?

A.Catalonia ✅
B.Andalusia
C.Galicia
D.Murcia
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Catalonia received a degree of autonomy through the Statute of Autonomy in 1932, which allowed its own parliament and some control over education and finances. This concession was intended to placate regional nationalist movements but ultimately heightened tensions with other right‑wing factions opposed to any form of decentralization.

Q3. Which political party was created specifically to defend the interests of the Catholic Church and large landowners?

A.CEDA ✅
B.PSOE
C.UGT
D.CNT
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Confederación Española de Derechas Autónomas (CEDA) was founded in 1933 by José María Gil‑Robles. It positioned itself as the defender of traditional Catholic values and the property rights of large estate owners, becoming the dominant right‑wing force in the Cortes during the early 1930s.

Q4. Who was the prime minister whose government lost working‑class support after the Casas Viejas massacre?

A.Manuel Azaña ✅
B.Francisco Franco
C.Alejandro Lerroux
D.José María Gil‑Robles
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Manuel Azaña, a leading figure of the Republican government, faced a severe blow to his credibility after the army’s brutal suppression of an anarchist gathering in Casas Viejas in January 1933, which resulted in 25 deaths and alienated many workers.

Q5. What action was taken against Jesuit priests during the Republic’s early reforms?

A.They were expelled from Spain ✅
B.They were given higher salaries
C.They were allowed to teach in public schools
D.They were made military chaplains
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: One of the most controversial secularizing measures was the expulsion of the Jesuit order in 1932. The government viewed the Jesuits as a bastion of ultracatholic influence, and their removal was intended to diminish the Church’s political power.

Q6. Which industry experienced a production decline of roughly one third during the early 1930s depression?

A.Iron ✅
B.Textile
C.Coal
D.Agriculture
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Industrial output suffered dramatically; iron production fell from about 300,000 tons to roughly 200,000 tons, a reduction of approximately 33 %. This slump reflected the broader economic malaise that strained the Republic’s ability to maintain social stability.

Q7. What was the immediate result of the 1932 attempted coup by a group of army officers?

A.It was quickly suppressed ✅
B.It succeeded and overthrew the government
C.It led directly to the 1936 civil war
D.It caused a permanent split in the army
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The 1932 rebellion, led by General José Sanjurjo and other disgruntled officers, was quashed within days because the majority of the armed forces remained loyal to the constitutional order, preventing an early breakdown of the Republic.

Q8. Which two left‑wing groups favored a general strike and the overthrow of the capitalist system?

A.Anarchists and syndicalists ✅
B.Monarchists and conservatives
C.Falangists and Carlists
D.Nationalists and fascists
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The anarchist movement, represented by the CNT, and the syndicalist trade unions, such as the UGT, pushed for direct action, including general strikes, with the ultimate aim of dismantling the existing capitalist framework and establishing a more egalitarian society.

Q9. Which right‑wing party won a majority of seats in the November 1933 elections?

A.CEDA ✅
B.Falange
C.Carlist
D.Nationalist
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The 1933 elections saw the Catholic CEDA emerge as the largest parliamentary group, capitalising on widespread disillusionment with the left‑wing government and promising to restore traditional values and protect property owners.

Q10. What economic problem primarily affected Spanish peasants during the early 1930s?

A.Rising unemployment
B.Falling wages ✅
C.Increased land ownership
D.Higher taxes
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The agrarian crisis was characterised by a surge in rural unemployment as falling agricultural prices and declining export markets left many farm labourers without work, exacerbating social tensions in the countryside.

Q11. If the government nationalised 200,000 hectares, representing 12 % of total arable land, how many hectares remained privately owned?

A.1400000
B.1466667
C.1500000 ✅
D.1600000
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Total arable land can be calculated as 200,000 ÷ 0.12 ≈ 1,666,667 hectares. Subtracting the nationalised portion leaves 1,666,667 − 200,000 ≈ 1,466,667 hectares still in private hands. This illustrates the limited scale of the Republic’s land‑reform efforts.

Q12. Agricultural unemployment rose from 150,000 to 225,000. What was the percentage increase?

A.0.25
B.0.33
C.0.4
D.0.5 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The increase equals (225,000 − 150,000) ÷ 150,000 = 75,000 ÷ 150,000 = 0.5, or 50 %. This sharp rise heightened social unrest and contributed to the polarization of rural Spain in the lead‑up to civil war.

Q13. If iron output fell from 300,000 tons to a third less, what was the new output?

A.100000
B.200000 ✅
C.250000
D.300000
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A reduction of one‑third means a loss of 100,000 tons, leaving 300,000 − 100,000 = 200,000 tons. This decline underscores the severe industrial contraction that the Republic struggled to reverse.

Q14. Which statement best explains why the Roman Catholic Church opposed the Second Republic?

A.It perceived loss of property and influence ✅
B.It wanted to support socialism
C.It favoured regional independence
D.It advocated for industrial nationalisation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Church saw the Republic’s secular reforms—such as separating Church and State, expelling religious orders, and ending state salaries for priests—as direct attacks on its traditional privileges, prompting fierce opposition.

Q15. If industrial workers’ wages rose by 15 % while inflation was 20 %, what was the real wage change?

A.+5 % increase
B.+20 % increase
C.-5 % decrease
D.No change ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Real wages equal nominal wage growth minus inflation: 15 % − 20 % = ‑5 %. Despite nominal gains, workers actually experienced a decrease in purchasing power, fueling dissatisfaction with the Republic’s economic policies.

Q16. Suppose 5,000 officers were compulsorily retired, reducing the officer corps by 8 %. Approximately how many officers existed before the retirements?

A.50000
B.55000
C.62500 ✅
D.70000
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: If 5,000 represents 8 % of the total, the original size is 5,000 ÷ 0.08 = 62,500 officers. This calculation shows the scale of the purge and its impact on military morale.

Q17. Which factor most directly helped CEDA win the 1933 elections?

A.Economic boom
B.Church support ✅
C.Military coup
D.International pressure
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Catholic Church’s endorsement of CEDA gave the party moral legitimacy among devout voters, allowing it to consolidate the right‑wing electorate and secure a parliamentary majority.

Q18. A general strike shut down 70 % of factories for ten days, reducing monthly output by 8 %. If normal output is 1,000,000 units, how many were produced that month?

A.920000 ✅
B.930000
C.940000
D.950000
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An 8 % reduction leaves 92 % of the usual output: 1,000,000 × 0.92 = 920,000 units. The strike’s economic impact was significant, weakening the Republic’s fiscal base.

Q19. Why did granting self‑government to Catalonia fail to appease broader right‑wing opposition?

A.It satisfied all demands
B.It increased central authority
C.It alienated Basque nationalists ✅
D.It was seen as a concession to separatism undermining national unity
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: While Catalonia gained autonomy, the move was perceived by many right‑wing factions as a dangerous precedent that could embolden other separatist movements, particularly in the Basque Country, thereby aggravating fears of national disintegration.

Q20. If 10 % of cultivated land was abandoned, representing 200,000 hectares, what was the original cultivated area?

A.1500000
B.1800000
C.2200000
D.2000000 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Abandoned land equals 10 % of the total, so the full area is 200,000 ÷ 0.10 = 2,000,000 hectares. This loss reflected the severe agrarian crisis that contributed to rural unrest.

Q21. Which alliance most threatened the Republic’s stability in early 1936?

C.Falange‑Military
D.Communist‑Syndicalists
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The coalition of CEDA with monarchist elements created a powerful right‑wing bloc that could mobilise both political and paramilitary resources, posing a direct challenge to the fragile Republican government.

Q22. Steel production fell by almost half, from 120,000 tons to what amount?

B.65000

📖 Explanation: A near‑50 % decline means production dropped to roughly 60,000 tons, illustrating the depth of industrial contraction that hampered the Republic’s capacity to fund social programmes and maintain military readiness.

Q23. The killing of 25 people in Casas Viejas primarily eroded support for the Republic among which group?

A.Landowners
B.Industrialists
C.Peasants
D.Working‑class voters ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The massacre was widely condemned by urban workers and trade‑union members, who saw the government’s heavy‑handed response as betrayal of the working class, leading many to withdraw their political backing.

Q24. In the 1933 election, CEDA received 30 % of the vote. If 1,000,000 votes were cast, how many votes did CEDA obtain?

A.200000
B.250000
C.300000 ✅
D.350000
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Thirty percent of one million equals 0.30 × 1,000,000 = 300,000 votes, giving CEDA a decisive plurality and enabling it to dominate the right‑wing parliamentary bloc.

Q25. Why did the majority of the army remain loyal to the Republic prior to the 1932 coup attempt?

A.Strong ideological alignment
B.Professionalism and oath to the constitution ✅
C.Lack of funding
D.Influence of foreign powers
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Most officers adhered to a professional code and had sworn to uphold the constitutional order, which discouraged them from joining conspiracies despite political disagreements, thereby preserving the Republic’s military legitimacy.

Q26. If the Republic’s reforms increased Church hostility by 40 % and right‑wing mobilisation by 25 %, while left‑wing militancy rose by 30 %, which combination most plausibly explains the rapid escalation to civil war in July 1936?

A.Moderate reforms with limited opposition
B.Limited opposition and balanced policies
C.Aggressive secularisation with polarized mobilisation
D.No change in political dynamics ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The simultaneous rise in anti‑Church sentiment (40 %), right‑wing mobilisation (25 %) and left‑wing militancy (30 %) created a volatile environment where both sides felt existential threat, making a violent breakout in July 1936 highly likely.

Q27. How did the 1936 Spanish Civil War influence the policy of non‑intervention adopted by Britain and France?

A.It reinforced the policy to avoid entanglement ✅
B.It led them to provide direct military aid
C.It caused them to support the Nationalists openly
D.It prompted immediate sanctions against fascist powers
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The brutal conflict convinced Britain and France that direct involvement risked a wider European war, so they pursued a policy of non‑intervention—though it was inconsistently enforced and ultimately favoured the Nationalist side.

Q28. Assume agricultural export value fell from €500 million to €350 million, a 30 % drop. If the government introduced a subsidy of €20 million, what percentage of the original export value does the subsidy represent?

A.0.02
B.0.03
C.0.04 ✅
D.0.05
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The subsidy as a share of the original €500 million equals 20 ÷ 500 = 0.04, or 4 %. This modest assistance was insufficient to offset the severe loss in export earnings.

Q29. Which ideological difference most sharply divided the anarchists from the socialist parties during the early 1930s?

A.Support for monarchy
B.Attitude toward private property ✅
C.Preference for parliamentary democracy
D.Alignment with the Catholic Church
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Anarchists demanded the immediate abolition of private property and hierarchical structures, while many socialists were willing to work within a parliamentary framework and accept regulated private ownership, creating a deep strategic rift.

Q31. What role did radio broadcasts play in shaping public opinion during the 1936 conflict?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Both sides used radio to disseminate their narratives, but the Republican government maintained a network of stations that broadcast speeches, news, and cultural programs, helping to mobilise popular support and counter Nationalist messaging.

Q32. By August 1936, estimates suggested 10,000 civilians had been killed, representing 0.5 % of the total population of 2 million in the affected areas. Verify this percentage.

A.0.0025
B.0.005
C.0.01 ✅
D.0.02
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The proportion is calculated as 10,000 ÷ 2,000,000 = 0.005, which equals 0.5 %. This figure illustrates the early human cost of the conflict, even before large‑scale battles began.

Q33. Which long‑term political outcome can be directly linked to the Republic’s failure to resolve the Church‑state conflict?

A.Establishment of a secular monarchy
B.Rise of a military dictatorship under Franco ✅

📖 Explanation: The inability to reconcile the Church’s privileges with republican secularism contributed to the polarization that enabled General Francisco Franco to seize power, establishing a right‑wing authoritarian regime that lasted until 1975.
