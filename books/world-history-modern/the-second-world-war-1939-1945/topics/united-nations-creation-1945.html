What is United Nations creation 1945?

The United Nations was founded in 1945 to promote international cooperation, prevent future conflicts, and address humanitarian issues, replacing the ineffective League of Nations with a more inclusive and enforceable global organization.

📝 All United Nations creation 1945 MCQs

Q1. In which year did the German invasion of Poland begin?

A. 1939 ✅
B. 1940
C. 1941
D. 1942

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The invasion began on 1 September 1939, marking the start of World War II. Germany’s rapid assault on Poland prompted Britain and France to declare war, setting the stage for the international cooperation that later culminated in the United Nations.

Q2. Which battle prevented German air superiority over Britain?

A. Battle of Midway
B. Battle of Britain ✅
C. Battle of El Alamein
D. Battle of Stalingrad

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Battle of Britain, fought from July to September 1940, saw the Royal Air Force successfully defend the United Kingdom against the Luftwaffe. This defeat halted Germany’s plans for invasion and underscored the need for collective security, a principle later embedded in the UN Charter.

Q3. Which country declared war on Germany in June 1940?

A. Japan
B. USA
C. Italy ✅
D. Soviet Union

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Italy, under Mussolini, entered the war on 10 June 1940, aligning with Germany and Japan. This expansion of the Axis highlighted the dangers of unchecked aggression, motivating Allied leaders to consider a post‑war organization that could prevent similar coalitions, eventually leading to the UN.

Q4. The “phoney war” refers to the period after September 1939 and before which month?

A. March 1940
B. July 1940
C. June 1940
D. May 1940 ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The “phoney war” describes the lull in major ground operations on the Western Front between September 1939 and the German offensive in May 1940. The inactivity demonstrated how diplomatic failures could lead to prolonged tension, reinforcing the argument for a stronger international body like the United Nations.

Q5. Which conference first proposed the idea of a post‑war international organization?

A. Atlantic Charter ✅
B. Yalta
C. Tehran
D. Potsdam

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Atlantic Charter, announced in August 1941 by Roosevelt and Churchill, outlined common goals for the post‑war world, including the creation of a broader international organization. This early vision directly influenced the later establishment of the United Nations.

Q6. The United Nations was officially founded in which year?

A. 1943
B. 1945 ✅
C. 1944
D. 1946

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The UN officially came into existence on 24 October 1945 after the Charter was ratified by the required number of signatories. Its formation marked the culmination of Allied cooperation during World II and the desire to prevent future global conflicts.

Q7. Which nation did NOT sign the original UN Charter?

A. United Kingdom
B. Soviet Union
C. China ✅
D. Italy

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Italy was not among the original signatories of the UN Charter in 1945; it had been an Axis power and only joined the organization later, after its government was reconstituted as a democratic state.

Q8. Which country joined the Axis powers after the invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941?

A. Italy
B. Japan
C. Germany
D. Hungary ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Hungary entered the Axis in November 1940 and formally joined the war against the Soviet Union after Germany launched Operation Barbarossa in June 1941, expanding the coalition that the future United Nations would later seek to contain.

Q9. A diplomat in 1945 must argue why the UN should have a Security Council with veto power for certain members. Which rationale was historically used?

A. To give the most powerful Allies the ability to prevent unilateral actions ✅
B. To ensure equal representation
C. To allow smaller nations to dominate decisions
D. To eliminate the need for a General Assembly

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The veto was granted to the five permanent members—United States, United Kingdom, Soviet Union, France, and China—so that the most powerful Allied nations could block actions that might threaten their vital interests, a compromise designed to secure their participation in the new organization.

Q10. Considering the four phases of WWII, which phase most directly set the stage for the creation of the United Nations?

A. Opening moves (1939‑1940)
B. Axis powers defeated (1943‑1945) ✅
C. Offensives held in check (1942‑1943)
D. Axis offensive widens (1941‑1942)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The final phase, when the Allied powers began to defeat the Axis (1943‑1945), created the political momentum and collaborative frameworks necessary for establishing a permanent international body, culminating in the UN’s formation in 1945.

Q11. During the Yalta Conference, leaders discussed the post‑war organization. Which of the following was NOT a point of agreement?

A. Establishment of the UN
B. Division of Germany into occupation zones
C. Creation of NATO ✅
D. Soviet entry into the war against Japan

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Yalta agreements covered the UN, the division of Germany, and Soviet commitments against Japan, but the formation of NATO would not be discussed until a decade later; therefore, NATO was not an agreed‑upon point at Yalta.

Q12. The Atlantic Charter of August 1941 laid groundwork for the UN. Which principle was NOT included?

A. Self‑determination of peoples
B. Free trade
C. Disarmament
D. Right to colonial expansion ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Atlantic Charter emphasized self‑determination, economic cooperation, and post‑war peace, but it never endorsed a right to colonial expansion; instead, it called for respect of existing territories, making the notion of expanding empires inconsistent with its vision.

Q13. A historian argues that the Battle of Midway was pivotal for the UN's formation because it shifted power balance. Which reasoning best supports this claim?

A. It demonstrated US naval supremacy ✅
B. It forced Japan to surrender
C. It reduced Axis influence in Europe
D. It inspired the UN's charter

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Midway, fought in June 1942, showcased American naval power and halted Japanese expansion in the Pacific. This shift in the balance of power underscored the United States’ emerging role as a global leader, a factor that later influenced the design and leadership of the United Nations.

Q14. Which of the following statements best explains why the United Nations replaced the League of Nations?

A. The League was too powerful
B. The League failed to prevent WWII ✅
C. The League had too many members
D. The League focused on economic issues only

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The League of Nations could not stop the outbreak of World II, revealing its structural weaknesses. This failure motivated the Allied powers to craft a more robust organization, leading to the United Nations, which incorporated stronger enforcement mechanisms and broader membership.

Q15. In drafting the UN Charter, which principle ensured that member states could not be forced to accept decisions against their will?

A. Collective security
B. Veto power
C. Non‑intervention
D. Sovereign equality ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Sovereign equality, articulated in Article 2 of the Charter, guarantees that all members retain equal rights and cannot be compelled to accept measures that contravene their national interests, preserving state sovereignty while enabling collective action.

Q16. The San Francisco Conference in April 1945 produced the UN Charter. Approximately how many nations signed it?

A. 51 ✅
B. 38
C. 72
D. 100

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

At the San Francisco Conference, 51 nations signed the Charter, reflecting a broad coalition of Allied and neutral states committed to establishing a new global organization after the devastation of World II.

Q17. If a country wanted to join the UN in 1946, which of the following criteria would it need to meet?

A. Must be a permanent member of the Security Council
B. Must accept the obligations of the Charter ✅
C. Must have a nuclear arsenal
D. Must be a former Allied power

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Article 4 of the UN Charter requires prospective members to be peace‑loving states that accept the obligations contained in the Charter and are able and willing to carry out those obligations, regardless of their prior wartime alignment.

Q18. Which of the following best describes the impact of the Battle of Stalingrad on the formation of the UN?

A. It directly led to the creation of the UN
B. It weakened the Axis allowing Allied cooperation
C. It caused the US to withdraw from European affairs ✅
D. It resulted in the division of Germany

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Battle of Stalingrad, fought from August 1942 to February 1943, marked a decisive Soviet victory that turned the tide against Germany. This weakening of the Axis powers fostered greater Allied coordination, a prerequisite for the collaborative efforts that produced the United Nations.

Q19. During the Potsdam Conference, leaders discussed post‑war order. Which decision was most relevant to the future structure of the UN?

A. Division of Berlin
B. Declaration of the Truman Doctrine
C. Establishment of the Marshall Plan
D. Agreement on the Security Council composition ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

At Potsdam, the Allies agreed on the composition and voting procedures of the Security Council, ensuring that the major powers would have a decisive role in maintaining international peace—a cornerstone of the United Nations system.

Q20. The term \United Nations\ was first used in which context?

A. Declaration by United Nations (January 1942) ✅
B. Atlantic Charter
C. Yalta declaration
D. San Francisco Conference

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The phrase “United Nations” first appeared in the Declaration by United Nations on 1 January 1942, when 26 nations pledged to fight together against the Axis, establishing a collective identity that later evolved into the formal organization.

Q21. Evaluate how the principle of \collective security\ embedded in Chapter VII of the UN Charter reflects lessons learned from the failure of the League of Nations.

A. It allows unilateral action
B. It requires Security Council authorization for force ✅
C. It limits the use of force to self‑defence only
D. It imposes economic sanctions without Security Council consent

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Chapter VII institutionalizes collective security by granting the Security Council authority to determine when force may be used, correcting the League’s inability to act decisively. This framework ensures that any use of force is backed by a binding, multilateral decision rather than individual state initiatives.

Q22. Compare the voting mechanisms of the League of Nations Assembly and the UN General Assembly. Which statement is accurate?

A. Both required a two‑thirds majority
B. The UN allows each member one vote while the League weighted votes by population
C. Both bodies had equal veto powers for major powers
D. The League used unanimous consent while the UN uses simple majority ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The League of Nations required unanimous consent for many decisions, making action difficult, whereas the UN General Assembly employs a simple majority (or two‑thirds for important matters), allowing more flexible and democratic decision‑making.

Q23. If the UN Security Council were to be restructured to remove veto power, which of the following consequences would most likely occur, based on historical debates?

A. Decreased participation of major powers ✅
B. Increased effectiveness in peacekeeping
C. Strengthening of the General Assembly
D. Elimination of the UN altogether

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Historical discussions suggest that removing the veto could alienate the permanent members, reducing their willingness to engage fully and potentially weakening the Council’s legitimacy and operational capacity.

Q24. The concept of \self‑determination\ in the UN Charter was heavily influenced by which WWII event?

A. The liberation of Paris
B. The Atlantic Charter ✅
C. The Yalta Conference
D. The fall of Berlin

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Atlantic Charter’s commitment to the right of all peoples to choose their own government directly inspired the inclusion of self‑determination in the UN Charter, reflecting a wartime promise to reshape the post‑war international order.

Q25. During the drafting of the UN Charter, the issue of minority rights was controversial. Which article addresses this concern?

A. Article 1
B. Article 2
C. Article 55 ✅
D. Article 8

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Article 55 calls upon member states to promote higher standards of living, full employment, and conditions of economic and social progress, implicitly encompassing the protection of minority rights as part of broader human‑rights objectives.

Q26. The inclusion of economic and social councils in the UN system reflects an attempt to avoid which criticism of the League of Nations?

A. Its focus on military matters
B. Its lack of a judicial branch
C. Its exclusion of non‑European states
D. Its inability to enforce sanctions ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The League was often criticized for concentrating on political and security issues while neglecting economic and social development. By establishing bodies such as ECOSOC, the UN sought to address this imbalance and provide a more comprehensive platform for global cooperation.

Q27. Assess why the UN Charter does not include a provision for compulsory military service of member states.

A. To respect national sovereignty ✅
B. To avoid duplication with NATO
C. Because the League already had it
D. Because it was deemed unnecessary after WWII

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Charter respects the principle of sovereign equality, allowing each nation to determine its own defense policies. Compulsory military service would infringe on national autonomy and was therefore omitted to preserve state sovereignty while encouraging voluntary contributions to collective security.
