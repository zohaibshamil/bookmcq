Q1. Which Indonesian leader later became the first president of Indonesia after WWII?

🔹 A. Sukarno
🔹 B. Ho Chi Minh
🔹 C. Mahatma Gandhi
🔹 D. Kwame Nkrumah

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Sukarno is the correct answer because he led the independence movement in the Dutch East Indies and was elected Indonesia’s first president in 1949. Ho Chi Minh was a Vietnamese leader, Gandhi led India’s non‑violent movement, and Nkrumah was a Ghanaian nationalist, so they do not fit the Indonesian context. This highlights how WWII experience propelled local leaders like Sukarno into post‑war nation‑building roles.

Q2. In which year was the Atlantic Charter signed?

🔹 A. 1941
🔹 B. 1942
🔹 C. 1943
🔹 D. 1944

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Atlantic Charter was signed in 1941, outlining Allied goals for the post‑war world. The other years are incorrect: 1942 saw the Battle of Midway, 1943 the Casablanca Conference, and 1944 the D‑Day invasion. Knowing the Charter’s date shows how early Allied leaders began planning a new international order, influencing decolonisation expectations in Asia and Africa.

Q4. Which European colony did the Japanese capture that later saw a nationalist guerrilla campaign?

🔹 B. British India
🔹 C. Portuguese Angola
🔹 D. Spanish Morocco

The capture demonstrated that European powers could be challenged, encouraging local insurgencies.

📖 Explanation:

Economic aid and promises of independence were limited, and democratic constitutions were not broadly offered. This shift in perception energized nationalist leaders, who began to believe they could achieve self‑rule through armed struggle.

Q8. Why did European powers find it difficult to retain their empires after WWII?

🔹 A. Their economies and militaries were severely weakened
🔹 B. They voluntarily gave up colonies out of goodwill
🔹 C. International law banned empire after 1945
🔹 D. All colonies declared independence simultaneously

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The war drained European economies and destroyed much of their military capacity, making it hard to enforce distant control. There was no universal legal ban on empires, and while some colonies declared independence at different times, the primary obstacle was the weakened ability of the colonial powers, not altruistic surrender.
