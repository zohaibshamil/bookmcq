What is Algerian War of Independence 1954-1962?

The Algerian War of Independence was a brutal eight-year conflict between French forces and the National Liberation Front that resulted in hundreds of thousands of deaths and ultimately forced France to grant Algeria independence in 1962 despite fierce opposition from European settlers in Algeria.

📝 All Algerian War of Independence 1954-1962 MCQs

Q1. What term was used for the French settlers who owned large farms in Algeria before 1962?

A. pieds noirs ✅
B. colons
C. habous
D. metropolitans

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is “pieds noirs,” the French phrase meaning “black feet,” which described European settlers who took over fertile land. The other options refer to different concepts: “colons” is a general term for colonists, “habous” relates to Islamic endowments, and “metropolitans” describes people from mainland France. Knowing this term helps students recognize the social hierarchy that fueled Algerian resentment and later conflict.

Q2. Approximately how many French troops were deployed in Algeria by 1960?

A. 300000
B. 500000
C. 700000 ✅
D. 1000000

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The French sent about 700,000 soldiers to Algeria by 1960, reflecting the scale of the conflict. The lower figures (300,000 and 500,000) underestimate the commitment, while 1,000,000 exaggerates it. Understanding the troop level shows why the war strained French resources and influenced political debate at home, illustrating the high cost of trying to maintain colonial control.

Q3. How many members comprised the Algerian assembly created by the French government?

A. 60
B. 90
C. 120 ✅
D. 150

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The assembly consisted of 120 members, split evenly between settlers and Muslims. Options 60 and 90 are too small, and 150 is too large. Recognizing the size of the assembly clarifies how the French attempted a limited power‑sharing arrangement while still preserving settler dominance, a factor that intensified nationalist frustration.

Q4. Who was the leader of the National Liberation Front (FLN) that launched the guerrilla war in 1954?

📖 Explanation:

Ben Bella headed the FLN, initiating the armed struggle. Messali Hadj led an earlier peaceful nationalist movement, Mendès‑France was a French prime minister, and De Gaulle later became French president. Distinguishing these figures helps students track the shift from peaceful protest to violent resistance and understand leadership roles in decolonization.

Q6. How did the unequal voting system in the Algerian assembly contribute to the rise of the FLN?

D. It eliminated any political representation for Muslims

This imbalance frustrated peaceful political avenues, pushing radicals toward the FLN. The other statements are inaccurate: Muslims did not control the assembly, power was not shared equally, and Muslims still had some representation, albeit limited.

Q8. Explain how the French policy of treating Algeria as an extension of metropolitan France, rather than a colony, affected the political aspirations of Muslim Algerians.

📖 Explanation:

The other options are misleading: Muslims were not given full citizenship rights, partnership was superficial, and integration was limited. Q9. If a French settler owned 30 % of the fertile land and decided to halve grain production to grow vines, what would be the likely effect on food availability for the native Algerian population?

A. Food supply would increase by 15 %
B. Food supply would stay the same
C. Food supply would decrease by roughly 15 % ✅
D. Food supply would double

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Halving grain on 30 % of land cuts overall production by about half of that portion, i.e., 0.5 × 30 % = 15 % of total output, reducing food availability for locals. The other choices miscalculate the impact: an increase is impossible, staying the same ignores the loss, and doubling is contrary to the reduction.
