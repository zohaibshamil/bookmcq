Q1. In what year did the Gold Coast become the independent nation of Ghana?

🔹 A. 1955
🔹 B. 1957
🔹 C. 1960
🔹 D. 1962

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is 1957 because Ghana achieved full independence that year. 1955 and 1960 are incorrect; 1955 was before the independence movement succeeded, and 1962 is too late. 1962 also does not match the historical timeline. Knowing the exact year helps place Ghana’s independence in the post‑World War II decolonisation wave, showing how early it was compared with many other African states.

Q2. Who was the primary nationalist leader who guided Ghana to independence?

🔹 A. Julius Nyerere
🔹 B. Kwame Nkrumah
🔹 C. Jomo Kenyatta
🔹 D. Patrice Lumumba

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Kwame Nkrumah is the correct answer; he founded the Convention People’s Party and became prime minister in 1952, leading Ghana to independence in 1957. Julius Nyerere was Tanzania’s leader, Jomo Kenyatta led Kenya, and Patrice Lumumba was a Congolese figure. Understanding the key personalities clarifies who shaped Ghana’s political direction and why his education abroad mattered for the independence strategy.

Q3. Which colony adopted a federal constitution in 1954 to manage regional diversity?

🔹 A. Kenya
🔹 B. Uganda
🔹 C. Tanganyika
🔹 D. Nigeria

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Nigeria adopted the 1954 federal constitution to accommodate its large, ethnically diverse population, especially the Muslim north, Yoruba west, and Igbo east. Kenya, Uganda, and Tanganyika did not implement a federal system at that time. Recognising Nigeria’s choice illustrates how colonial powers tried to balance regional interests, a factor that later influenced post‑independence governance challenges.

Northern Rhodesia and Nyasaland had fewer settlers, and Western Kenya is unrelated. Q5. Why did the ‘settler factor’ create particular difficulties for Kenya’s path to independence?

🔹 A. It caused economic growth
🔹 B. It led to fear among settlers of black rule
🔹 C. It increased literacy rates
🔹 D. It reduced agricultural output

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The ‘settler factor’ in Kenya meant European and Asian settlers feared losing political and economic dominance under a black government, causing resistance to rapid decolonisation. Economic growth, literacy, and agricultural output are not direct consequences of the settler factor. Understanding this fear explains why Kenya experienced more negotiation and occasional violence compared with colonies lacking a large settler class.

This distinction clarifies why Kenya’s independence negotiations were more complex than Tanganyika’s.
