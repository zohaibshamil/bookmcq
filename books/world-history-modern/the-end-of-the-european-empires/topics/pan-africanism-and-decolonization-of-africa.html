What is Pan-Africanism and decolonization of Africa?

Pan-Africanism was an intellectual and political movement that promoted unity among people of African descent worldwide and provided the ideological foundation for African decolonization by emphasizing self-determination, racial pride, and collective resistance against European domination.

Q1. Which organization did Marcus Garvey found?

A. Universal Negro Improvement Association
B. African Union ✅
C. OAU
D. NAACP

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Universal Negro Improvement Association (UNIwas founded by Marcus Garvey to promote Black pride and self‑reliance. Option B (African Union) is a modern continental body, not Garvey’s creation. Option C (OAU) was established decades later. Option D (NAACP) is an American civil‑rights group. Knowing the correct organization shows how early diaspora leaders organized pan‑African ideas, a foundation for later movements.

Q2. W. E. B. Du Bois earned his doctorate from which university?

A. Yale
B. Harvard ✅
C. Oxford
D. Columbia

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Du Bois received his Ph.D. from Harvard, making him the first African American to do so. Yale, Oxford, and Columbia are all prestigious institutions but none awarded his doctorate. Recognizing Harvard highlights the academic achievement that bolstered Du Bois’s authority in promoting pan‑Africanism, influencing educated African elites during the independence era.

Q3. Why did Ethiopia oppose Nkrumah's idea of a United States of Africa?

A. Because they wanted economic dominance
B. Because they wanted to adopt the US model
C. Because they feared loss of sovereignty
D. Because they sought cultural isolation ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Ethiopia, having retained independence for centuries, feared that joining a federal Africa would diminish its autonomous status. Options A and B misstate motivations, focusing on economics or imitation of the US, which were not primary concerns. Option D suggests cultural isolation, which does not explain Ethiopia’s political stance. This opposition reflects the tension between national sovereignty and pan‑African unity.

Q4. How did pan‑Africanist ideas influence nationalist movements in British colonies?

Understanding this ideological bridge helps explain why many British colonies rapidly moved toward independence after World War II.

Recognizing this contrast explains why the OAU became the dominant, rather than a federal, continental body.
