Q1. On which date were India and Pakistan declared independent, marking the official start of partition?

🔹 A. 15-Aug-47
🔹 B. 26-Jan-50
🔹 C. 01-Jan-48
🔹 D. 02-Oct-47

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is A because both nations celebrated independence on 15 August 1947, as stated in the Indian Independence Act. Options B and D refer to unrelated dates: B is India’s republic day, D is Pakistan’s later national day. Option C is a random date with no historical link. Knowing the exact date helps students place the partition timeline in real‑world history.

Q2. Which two provinces were divided by the Indian Independence Act, creating separate Hindu‑majority and Muslim‑majority regions?

🔹 A. Madras and Bombay
🔹 B. Punjab and Bengal
🔹 C. Gujarat and Assam
🔹 D. Delhi and Rajasthan

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Answer B is correct because the Act split Punjab and Bengal, both having mixed populations, leading to massive migrations. The other pairs were not divided: Madras, Bombay, Gujarat, Assam, Delhi, and Rajasthan remained whole. Recognizing the provinces involved clarifies why the partition caused such widespread communal displacement.

Q3. Why did the hurried drawing of borders during partition lead to large‑scale communal violence?

🔹 A. It left many people on the 'wrong' side of the new frontier, prompting panic migrations.
🔹 B. It created economic competition over scarce resources.
🔹 C. It allowed British troops to intervene directly in local disputes.
🔹 D. It encouraged political leaders to adopt isolationist policies.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct answer is C because the rapid border delineation placed millions of Hindus and Muslims opposite to their preferred nation, causing panic‑driven migrations and violent clashes, especially in Punjab. Options A and B mischaracterize the primary cause; economic competition existed but was secondary, and British troops largely withdrew. Option D is unrelated. Understanding this cause links border decisions to the ensuing violence.
