What is Spanish decolonization of Africa?

Spanish decolonization of Africa occurred late and incompletely as Spain relinquished Spanish Morocco in 1956 and Equatorial Guinea in 1968 but retained Western Sahara until 1975, leaving the territory disputed between Morocco and the Polisario Front without resolved sovereignty.

Q1. If General Franco had agreed to grant independence to Spanish Sahara in \(1970\) instead of waiting until after his death, which of the following outcomes would have been most likely?

A.Immediate UN recognition of an independent Sahara ✅
B.Morocco would annex the territory without resistance
C.A prolonged conflict with Algeria supporting the Polisario Front
D.Spain retaining economic benefits from phosphates
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The correct answer is A because an early decolonisation would have satisfied the Saharan nationalist claim and allowed the United Nations to admit the new state, effectively ending the territorial dispute. Option B is inaccurate because Morocco’s claim still required a legal basis that was not automatically granted. Option C assumes the conflict would continue, but the primary trigger was Spain’s refusal to decolonise, not Algerian involvement. Option D ignores that Spain would lose phosphate revenue once the colony became sovereign, making early independence most likely to lead to UN recognition.

Therefore, legal penalties are the most critical missing element, explaining why the ceasefire stalled and the referendum never occurred.
