Q1. In which year did the Gold Coast become the independent nation of Ghana?

🔹 A. 1955
🔹 B. 1957
🔹 C. 1960
🔹 D. 1962

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Ghana achieved full independence in \(1957\). The British granted sovereignty after the CPP won the 1951 elections and Nkrumah became prime minister in 1952. Options A, C, and D are incorrect because they either predate or postdate the actual year. Knowing the correct year helps place Ghana’s independence in the broader post‑World War II decolonisation timeline, showing how quickly the Gold Coast transitioned from self‑government to nationhood.

Q2. Which Nigerian nationalist organized the 1945 general strike that pressured the British?

🔹 A. Obafemi Awolowo
🔹 B. Nnamdi Azikiwe
🔹 C. Ahmadu Bello
🔹 D. Jaja Wachuku

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The 1945 general strike in Nigeria was led by Nnamdi Azikiwe, a prominent nationalist educated in the USA. Azikiwe’s activism forced the British to consider constitutional reforms. Options A, C, and D name other notable figures but none led that particular strike. Recognising Azikiwe’s role clarifies how labour mobilisation became a catalyst for political change, linking the strike directly to Nigeria’s path toward federal independence.

Q3. Which British West African colony achieved independence in 1961 without major conflict?

🔹 A. The Gambia
🔹 B. Sierra Leone
🔹 C. Ghana
🔹 D. Nigeria

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Sierra Leone became independent in 1961, following a relatively peaceful transition compared with other colonies. The Gambia achieved independence later in 1965, while Ghana and Nigeria gained independence earlier (1957 and 1960 respectively). This answer highlights the varied timelines of decolonisation in West Africa and shows that not all colonies experienced the same level of unrest, underscoring Sierra Leone’s smoother path to sovereignty.

Q4. Why did the British choose a federal system for Nigeria's independence?

🔹 A. Because Nigeria's size required decentralised administration
🔹 B. Because the British wanted to retain control
🔹 C. Because federalism was popular in Europe
🔹 D. Because regional leaders demanded it

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The British adopted a federal system primarily because Nigeria’s vast territory contained distinct ethnic and religious regions—Hausa‑Fulani in the north, Yoruba in the west, and Igbo in the east—making power‑sharing essential to avoid domination by any single group. Options A and D are partially true but D is vague; option C is irrelevant; option A misstates the reason as merely administrative size. Understanding this choice explains later tensions that led to civil war.

Q6. What was the main reason the Gold Coast’s experience under British supervision was considered unique?

🔹 A. It allowed Ghanaian politicians to gain governance experience before full independence
🔹 B. It gave the Gold Coast unlimited economic aid
🔹 C. It prevented any political dissent
🔹 D. It eliminated all colonial laws

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Gold Coast’s period of self‑government (1952‑1957) let local leaders practice parliamentary procedures, budgeting, and policy‑making under British oversight, a rarity among newly independent African states. Options A, C, and D are inaccurate: the colony did not receive unlimited aid, dissent persisted, and colonial laws were not wholly removed. This unique apprenticeship is cited as a factor that helped Ghana avoid the immediate post‑independence chaos seen elsewhere.
