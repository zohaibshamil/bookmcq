What is Mobile IP addressing home address and care-of address?

In Mobile IP, the home address is the permanent IP address identifying a mobile node on its home network, while the care-of address is a temporary address acquired when visiting a foreign network that indicates the mobile node’s current location for packet forwarding.

Q1. What is the formula for end‑to‑end throughput when a path consists of several serial links?

A.Throughput = min{TR₁, TR₂, …, TRₙ} ✅
B.Throughput = sum of all TRᵢ
C.Throughput = average of all TRᵢ
D.Throughput = max{TR₁, TR₂, …, TRₙ}
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The end‑to‑end throughput of a series of links is limited by the slowest link, i.e., the minimum transmission rate among the links. This is expressed as Throughput=min⁡{TR1,TR2,…,TRn} \text{Throughput} = \min\{TR_1, TR_2, \dots, TR_n\} Throughput=min{TR1​,TR2​,…,TRn​}. Any faster link cannot increase the overall rate beyond the bottleneck, making option A correct.

Q2. Link1 = 200 kbps, Link2 = 100 kbps, Link3 = 150 kbps. Which link determines the end‑to‑end throughput?

A.Link1
B.Link2 ✅
C.Link3
D.All equally
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Because throughput is constrained by the slowest link, the 100 kbps link (Link2) becomes the bottleneck. Even though the other links can carry more data, the overall rate cannot exceed 100 kbps, so option B is the correct choice.

Q3. A dial‑up line provides 40 kbps and is shared equally by two independent flows. Assuming no other constraints, what is the maximum throughput each flow can obtain?

A.40 kbps
B.20 kbps ✅
C.10 kbps
D.30 kbps
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When a 40 kbps line is divided equally between two flows, each flow can at most use half of the capacity, i.e., 40/2=2040/2 = 2040/2=20 kbps. Therefore option B correctly reflects the maximum per‑flow throughput.

Q4. Three sources send 80 kbps each into a router that forwards traffic over a single 200 kbps backbone link. What is the most likely outcome for the flows if the backbone is the only bottleneck?

A.All three flows receive 80 kbps
B.Each flow is limited to about 66 kbps ✅
C.Two flows are dropped
D.The backbone automatically expands its capacity
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The total offered load (3 × 80 = 240 kbps) exceeds the 200 kbps backbone capacity. Fair sharing would allocate roughly 200/3≈66200/3 \approx 66200/3≈66 kbps to each flow, causing congestion for the original rates. Hence option B is correct.

Q5. Compare a dedicated 100 kbps link used by a single flow with a shared 100 kbps link used by four concurrent flows. Which scenario can provide a higher per‑flow throughput?

A.Dedicated link ✅
B.Shared link
C.Both equal
D.Depends on packet size
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When a link is dedicated to one flow, that flow can use the full 100 kbps. In a shared environment, the 100 kbps must be divided among four flows, reducing each flow’s share. Thus the dedicated link yields higher per‑flow throughput, making option A correct.

Q6. If the bottleneck link in a three‑link path is increased from 100 kbps to 150 kbps while the other two links remain at 200 kbps, what is the new end‑to‑end throughput?

A.100 kbps
B.150 kbps ✅
C.200 kbps
D.250 kbps
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: With the two non‑bottleneck links at 200 kbps and the former bottleneck now at 150 kbps, the minimum transmission rate becomes 150 kbps. The path’s throughput is therefore limited to 150 kbps, so option B is correct.

Q7. How does introducing a 5 % packet‑loss rate on the bottleneck link affect the measured throughput compared to simply reducing the link’s transmission rate by 10 %?

A.Loss reduces throughput more than the rate reduction ✅
B.Both have identical effect
C.Rate reduction reduces throughput more
D.Loss has no impact on throughput
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Packet loss forces retransmissions, effectively consuming additional bandwidth and lowering usable throughput more than a pure 10 % reduction in raw rate. Consequently, the loss scenario yields a greater throughput drop, making option A correct.

Q9. When multiple flows share a link of 100 kbps, how does the aggregate throughput relate to the individual flow rates?

Q13. In a star topology where each edge router connects to a central hub with 250 kbps links, how does the hub’s aggregate outgoing capacity compare to the sum of the individual inbound capacities?

A.Equal
B.Greater

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

Q14. Synthesize a scenario where two parallel paths exist between source and destination: Path A has links 100 kbps, 120 kbps; Path B has links 80 kbps, 150 kbps. If traffic can be split arbitrarily, what is the maximum achievable end‑to‑end throughput?

A.180 kbps ✅
B.200 kbps
C.230 kbps
D.250 kbps
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Each path’s throughput is limited by its slowest link (100 kbps for Path A, 80 kbps for Path B). By sending traffic over both paths, the total achievable throughput is the sum of these minima: 100+80=180100 + 80 = 180100+80=180 kbps. Hence option A is correct.
