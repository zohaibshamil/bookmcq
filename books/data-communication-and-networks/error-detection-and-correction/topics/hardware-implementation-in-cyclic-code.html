What is Hardware Implementation in Cyclic Code?

Hardware implementation of cyclic codes typically utilizes linear feedback shift registers (LFSRs) composed of XOR gates and flip-flops, providing a highly efficient, low-latency, and cost-effective method for real-time encoding and decoding in network interface cards.

📝 All Hardware Implementation in Cyclic Code MCQs

Q1. What is the purpose of a 1‑bit shift register in the CRC encoder?

A.Store the data permanently
B.Hold a bit for one clock cycle ✅
C.Perform XOR operations
D.Generate the system clock
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A 1‑bit shift register is designed to retain the incoming bit for exactly one clock period. At each clock edge it captures the new input, presents it on the output, and then waits for the next bit. This one‑cycle storage is essential for the serial shifting behavior required by CRC encoding.

Q2. If the divisor polynomial contains many zero coefficients, how does this affect the hardware implementation?

A.It reduces the number of XOR devices required ✅
B.It increases the number of XOR devices required
C.It has no effect on the number of XOR devices
D.It doubles the number of shift registers
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Zero coefficients mean that the corresponding XOR taps are not needed, so fewer XOR gates are instantiated. Each non‑zero term (except the leading term) normally requires an XOR gate; eliminating terms directly cuts down the gate count, making the circuit smaller and often lower‑power.

Q3. In the decoder, when does the syndrome become available?

A.After the first bit is received
B.After k bits have been processed
C.After n bits have been processed
D.After the entire codeword has been fed into the decoder ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The syndrome is the remainder of dividing the received codeword by the generator polynomial. Because the division is performed serially, the remainder cannot be finalized until every bit of the codeword has passed through the shift‑register cascade, so the syndrome appears only after the full codeword is input.

Q4. Compare the hardware resource usage when the encoder uses n shift registers versus n−k shift registers. Which statement is correct?

A.Using n shift registers consumes more resources than using n−k
B.Using n−k shift registers consumes more resources than using n ✅
C.Both configurations use the same number of registers
D.The required number depends on the generator polynomial
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The encoder only needs to store the extra n−k zero bits that are appended to the dataword. Therefore, implementing n−k 1‑bit shift registers is sufficient, whereas using n registers would add unnecessary hardware, increasing area and power without any functional benefit.

Q5. Given a CRC encoder with n=8 and k=4, how many zero bits are appended to the dataword before encoding?

A.4 ✅
B.8
C.0
D.2
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The number of appended zeros equals the degree of the generator polynomial, which is n−k. With n=8 and k=4, the difference is 4, so four zero bits are added to the original dataword to form the augmented dataword that is fed to the encoder.

Q6. Which statement best describes the waiting time for check bits in the basic design versus an improved scheme where check bits are ready after k steps?

A.The basic design requires a longer waiting time than the improved scheme ✅
B.Both designs have the same waiting time
C.The basic design has a shorter waiting time than the improved scheme
D.Waiting time is independent of the design approach
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: In the straightforward serial implementation, the encoder must process all n−k appended zeros before the right‑most XOR can produce the check bits, causing a delay of n−k cycles. Optimized designs, often using parallelism or feedback, can generate the check bits after only k cycles, thus shortening the latency.

Q7. Why must the extra n−k zero bits be processed before the right‑most XOR becomes unaffected?

A.They propagate through the shift registers and reach the XOR after n−k cycles ✅
B.They alter the generator polynomial during that period
C.They reset the clock before the XOR can operate
D.They are discarded by the decoder immediately
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The zero bits travel through the cascade of 1‑bit shift registers just like any other data bit. Only after they have advanced n−k positions do they arrive at the right‑most XOR input, at which point the XOR sees the true data bits and the zeros no longer influence its output.

Q8. Suppose the divisor polynomial is \(x^{3}+x+1\). How many XOR gates are required at minimum for the encoder?

A.3
B.2 ✅
C.1
D.0
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Each non‑zero coefficient of the generator polynomial, except the leading term, requires an XOR tap. The polynomial \(x^{3}+x+1\) has non‑zero terms for \(x^{3}, x^{1},\) and the constant term. Excluding the highest‑degree term, two XOR gates are needed to implement the taps for \(x\) and the constant.

Q9. What is the effect of feeding the augmented dataword (dataword plus zeros) into the encoder on the right‑most XOR after the zeros have passed?

A.The XOR becomes active and toggles each cycle
B.The XOR becomes inactive and stops toggling
C.The XOR output remains unchanged because zeros have no effect ✅
D.The XOR output alternates between 0 and 1 regardless of input
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Zeros do not alter the result of an XOR operation; when a zero reaches the right‑most XOR input, the gate simply passes the other input unchanged. Consequently, after all appended zeros have moved through the shift registers, the right‑most XOR behaves as if the zeros were never present, leaving its output unchanged.

Q10. If the shift registers are implemented with flip‑flops, what is the minimum clock frequency required to process a 1024‑bit codeword in under 1 ms?

A.1 MHz ✅
B.2 MHz
C.500 kHz
D.1.5 MHz
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Processing 1024 bits in 1 ms implies a throughput of 1,024,000 bits per second, which corresponds to a clock frequency of about 1.024 MHz. The nearest standard frequency among the options is 1 MHz, which satisfies the requirement within a small margin of error.

Q11. Assume a CRC encoder uses n=16, k=12. If a single‑bit error occurs at position 5 of the transmitted codeword, what is the probability that the error will be detected, assuming the generator polynomial is primitive?

A.1 ✅
B.0.5
C.0.75
D.0.25
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A primitive generator polynomial guarantees detection of all single‑bit errors because any single‑bit error pattern is not divisible by the polynomial. Therefore, the detection probability for a single‑bit error is 1 (i.e., 100 %).

Q12. Explain why the check bits in a CRC encoder become available after k steps in an optimized design rather than after n−k steps. Which technique primarily enables this improvement?

A.Using parallel processing of bits
B.Employing feedback registers that update check bits continuously ✅
C.Applying early termination of the division algorithm
D.Increasing the clock speed of the shift registers
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Feedback registers allow the encoder to update the remainder as each data bit arrives, effectively performing the division in a rolling fashion. This continuous update means that after the k original data bits have been processed, the remainder (which forms the check bits) is already computed, eliminating the need to wait for the additional n−k zero bits.

Q13. Given a CRC generator polynomial \(G(x)=x^{4}+x+1\), derive the remainder when the augmented dataword \(D(x)=x^{7}+x^{3}+1\) is divided by \(G(x)\). Which of the following is the correct remainder?

A.\(x\) ✅
B.\(x^{2}\)
C.\(1\)
D.\(x^{3}+x\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Performing binary polynomial division: first align \(G(x)\) with the highest term of \(D(x)\) and XOR, yielding \(x^{4}+1\). Align \(G(x)\) again and XOR, leaving \(x\). The final remainder has degree less than 4 and equals \(x\).

Q14. If a CRC decoder receives a codeword with an error pattern equal to the generator polynomial, what will be the syndrome?

A.Zero ✅
B.The generator polynomial itself
C.A non‑zero random pattern
D.Undefined
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When the error pattern is exactly the generator polynomial, the error vector is divisible by the generator. Division of the received word by the generator therefore yields a remainder of zero, indicating that the syndrome is zero and the decoder would (incorrectly) consider the word error‑free.

Q15. Consider two CRC encoders with generator polynomials \(G_{1}(x)=x^{3}+x+1\) and \(G_{2}(x)=x^{3}+x^{2}+1\). Which encoder provides better burst‑error detection for bursts up to length 3?

A.\(G_{1}\)
B.\(G_{2}\)
C.Both provide equal detection capability ✅
D.Neither provides burst‑error detection
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Both polynomials have degree 3, and a CRC of degree r guarantees detection of all burst errors of length ≤ r. Consequently, each encoder detects any burst up to three bits, making their burst‑error detection capabilities equivalent.

Q16. Why does the decoder need to receive the entire codeword before the syndrome can be computed?

A.Because the shift registers need to align the bits
B.Because the XOR gates need the full input simultaneously
C.Because the syndrome depends on all bits of the codeword ✅
D.Because the clock cycles are limited to one per bit
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The syndrome is the remainder after dividing the whole received codeword by the generator polynomial. This remainder can only be determined after every bit has contributed to the division process; partial input yields an incomplete remainder, so the full codeword must be processed first.

Q17. If a shift register receives a '1' at its input, what will be the output after one clock cycle?

A.0
B.1 ✅
C.The previous bit stored in the register
D.Undefined
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A 1‑bit shift register outputs the value that was present at its input during the preceding clock edge. Therefore, after a single clock transition, the output directly reflects the newly received '1'.

Q18. The time steps show the remainder evolving. Which property of the CRC ensures that the remainder after processing all bits is the check bits?

A.Linearity of the division operation ✅
B.Cyclicity of the generator polynomial
C.Redundancy introduced by the extra bits
D.Randomness of the data pattern
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: CRC computation is based on polynomial division over GF(2), which is a linear operation. Because the division is linear, the final remainder after processing the augmented dataword is precisely the set of check bits that, when appended to the original data, make the overall polynomial divisible by the generator.

Q19. Synthesize a design improvement that reduces the waiting time for check bits from \(n-k\) to \(k\) steps without increasing hardware complexity. Which technique could be employed?

A.Use parallel prefix adders
B.Implement feedback registers that update the remainder each cycle ✅
C.Employ early termination of the division algorithm
D.Use larger shift registers to store more bits
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Feedback registers allow the encoder to maintain and update the current remainder as each data bit arrives, effectively performing the division in a rolling manner. This approach eliminates the need to wait for the appended zeros, delivering the check bits after only k data bits have been processed while keeping the hardware count comparable to the basic design.

Q20. What does XOR stand for in digital logic?

A.Exclusive OR ✅
B.Exclusive AND
C.Extended OR
D.Exact OR
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: XOR is the abbreviation for Exclusive OR, a logical operation that outputs true only when an odd number of its inputs are true. In binary hardware, an XOR gate implements this function by producing a 1 when its two inputs differ and a 0 when they are the same.

Q21. If the generator polynomial contains a factor of \(x+1\), what type of error patterns are guaranteed to be detected?

A.Any error pattern with an odd number of bit errors ✅
B.Any error pattern with an even number of bit errors
C.All single‑bit errors only
D.Burst errors only
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A factor of \(x+1\) means the polynomial evaluates to zero for the input pattern of all ones, which corresponds to an odd number of flipped bits. Consequently, any error pattern that flips an odd number of bits will not be divisible by the generator and will be detected by the CRC.

Q22. Given a CRC system with generator polynomial \(G(x)=x^{5}+x^{2}+1\) and dataword length \(k=11\), how many check bits are appended?

A.5 ✅
B.4
C.6
D.7
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The number of check bits equals the degree of the generator polynomial, which is the highest exponent (5 in this case). Therefore, five check bits are appended to the 11‑bit dataword, resulting in a total codeword length of 16 bits.

Q23. Explain how the presence of multiple zeros in the divisor polynomial influences the power consumption of the hardware implementation. Which effect is correct?

A.It reduces XOR gate activity and thus lowers power consumption ✅
B.It increases the length of shift registers, raising power usage
C.It has no effect on power consumption
D.It forces a higher clock speed, increasing power draw
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Each zero coefficient eliminates a corresponding XOR tap, meaning fewer XOR gates toggle during operation. Since dynamic power in CMOS is proportional to switching activity, fewer active gates directly translate to reduced power consumption, making a divisor with many zeros advantageous from an energy‑efficiency standpoint.

Q24. During decoding, if the syndrome computed is non‑zero, what is the most likely conclusion about the received codeword?

A.The codeword is error‑free
B.The codeword contains a detectable error ✅
C.The codeword contains an undetectable error
D.It cannot be determined whether an error exists
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A non‑zero syndrome indicates that the received codeword is not divisible by the generator polynomial, meaning an error has been introduced during transmission. The CRC is designed to detect such errors, so a non‑zero syndrome reliably signals the presence of a detectable error.
