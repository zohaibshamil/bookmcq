What is Concept of Checksum Error Detecting Technique?

The core concept of the checksum is based on arithmetic redundancy, where the sender computes a complement of the sum of all data segments, and the receiver adds all segments including the checksum; a result of zero indicates the data is likely error-free.

📝 All Concept of Checksum Error Detecting Technique MCQs

Q1. If a generator polynomial includes the factor \(x+1\), which type of errors is guaranteed to be detected?

A.All odd-numbered errors ✅
B.All even-numbered errors
C.All burst errors length \(\le r\)
D.All errors
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The factor \(x+1\) forces the generator to have a root at \(x=1\), which means any error polynomial with an odd number of 1‑bits will produce a non‑zero remainder, guaranteeing detection of all odd‑numbered errors while other error types are not assured.

Q2. Suppose two isolated errors are exactly four bits apart. Which statement about detection by the generator \(g(x)=x^{14}+x^{3}+1\) is true?

A.Always detected
B.Detected only if distance < r
C.Detected only if distance > r
D.Undetected if distance exactly 4 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The given generator cannot detect two errors whose separation equals four positions; the error polynomial becomes a multiple of the generator, leaving a zero syndrome, so such a pattern remains invisible to the detector.

Q3. For \(r=14\), what is the probability that a burst error of length \(L=r+1\) goes undetected?

A.\((1/2)^{14}\)
B.\((1/2)^{13}\) ✅
C.\((1/2)^{15}\)
D.\((1/2)^{12}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When \(L=r+1\), the undetected‑burst probability is \((1/2)^{r-1}\). Substituting \(r=14\) gives \((1/2)^{13}\), meaning roughly one chance in eight thousand thousand for such an error to escape detection.

Q4. Which statement correctly compares a generator that contains the factor \(x+1\) with one that does not?

A.A generator without the factor cannot detect odd errors
B.A generator with the factor detects even errors
C.A generator with the factor \(x+1\) detects all odd-numbered errors ✅
D.A generator with the factor detects all burst errors
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Including \(x+1\) ensures that any error polynomial with an odd number of 1‑bits yields a non‑zero remainder, so the CRC will always flag odd‑count errors, a property the generator lacking this factor does not possess.

Q5. A generator that cannot divide \(x^{t}+1\) for any \(t<32768\) can detect all double isolated errors up to 32768 bits apart. Is this statement true?

A.true ✅
B.false
C.Partially true
D.Not enough information
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: If the generator never divides \(x^{t}+1\) for \(t\) below 32768, then the error polynomial formed by two isolated bits separated by any distance under that limit cannot be a multiple of the generator, guaranteeing detection of such double errors.

Q6. Given \(G_{1}=x^{14}+x^{3}+1\) and \(G_{2}=x^{4}+x^{2}+x+1\), which can guarantee detection of burst errors of length up to 14 bits?

A.\(G_{1}\)
B.\(G_{2}\) ✅
C.Both
D.None
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The guarantee depends on the degree \(r\) of the generator. \(G_{1}\) has degree 14, so any burst of length \(\le14\) is certainly detected, whereas \(G_{2}\) has degree 4 and cannot provide that guarantee.

Q7. How many check bits \(r\) are needed to guarantee detection of any burst error whose length \(L\le r\)?

A.\(r+1\)
B.\(r\) ✅
C.\(2r\)
D.\(r-1\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The CRC theorem states that a generator of degree \(r\) will always detect burst errors whose length does not exceed \(r\); thus exactly \(r\) check bits are sufficient for that guarantee.

Q8. For a generator of degree \(r\), what is the probability that a burst error of length \(L=r+1\) goes undetected?

A.\((1/2)^{r-1}\) ✅
B.\((1/2)^{r}\)
C.\((1/2)^{r+1}\)
D.0
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When the burst length is one greater than the degree, the syndrome may be zero with probability \((1/2)^{r-1}\); this follows from the uniform distribution of remainders for random error patterns.

Q9. To catch all odd‑numbered errors and any double isolated errors up to 5000 bits apart, which factor must be in the generator and what minimum degree \(r\) is required?

A.Include \(x+1\); \(r\ge10\)
B.Include \(x+1\); \(r\ge12\)
C.Include \(x+1\); \(r\ge13\) ✅
D.Include \(x+1\); \(r\ge14\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The factor \(x+1\) handles odd‑count errors. To separate two isolated errors up to 5000 bits, the generator’s degree must satisfy \(2^{r}>5000\); the smallest integer meeting this is \(r=13\) because \(2^{13}=8192\).

Q10. If \(j-i=r\) (so \(L=r+1\)), a burst error may be undetected with probability \((1/2)^{r-1}\). Does this probability depend on the specific generator polynomial?

A.Yes, depends on polynomial
B.No, independent
C.Depends only on \(r\) ✅
D.Not enough information
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The derivation of the undetected‑burst probability uses only the degree \(r\) of the generator; the exact coefficients do not affect the statistical chance, so the result hinges solely on \(r\).

Q11. The remainder of \((x^{j-i}+...+1)/(g(x))\) being zero indicates what about error detection?

A.Error detected
B.Error undetected ✅
C.Error corrected
D.None of the above
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A zero remainder (syndrome) means the error polynomial is divisible by the generator, so the CRC fails to flag the error; thus the error remains undetected.

Q12. Why must a generator that can detect a single error not divide the term \(x^{i}\) in a burst error?

A.Because dividing \(x^{i}\) would imply detection of a single‑bit error at position \(i\), contradicting the minimum condition. ✅
B.Because \(x^{i}\) is not part of the error polynomial.
C.Because the generator must be primitive.
D.Because burst errors are always longer than single errors.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: If the generator divided \(x^{i}\), the CRC would report a zero syndrome for a single‑bit error, violating the fundamental requirement that any single error be detectable; therefore the generator cannot contain \(x^{i}\) as a factor.

Q13. Given \(G_{a}=x^{14}+x^{3}+1\) and \(G_{b}=x^{14}+x^{2}+1\), which provides a lower probability of undetected burst errors of length 15?

B.\(G_{b}\)
C.Both equal ✅
D.Cannot determine
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Both generators have the same degree \(r=14\); the undetected‑burst probability for length \(r+1\) depends only on \(r\), yielding \((1/2)^{13}\) for each, so their performance is identical.

Q14. If a generator can detect all isolated double errors, which condition must hold for any \(t<32768\)?

B.It must divide \(x^{t}+1\)
C.It must have factor \(x+1\)
D.It must be primitive
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: To guarantee detection of any two isolated errors separated by up to 32768 bits, the generator must never be a divisor of \(x^{t}+1\) for those values of \(t\); otherwise the error polynomial could be a multiple of the generator and go unnoticed.

Q15. What is the primary purpose of including the factor \(x+1\) in a CRC generator polynomial?

B.Detect all even‑numbered errors

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The factor \(x+1\) forces the generator to have a root at \(x=1\), which ensures that any error pattern containing an odd number of 1‑bits yields a non‑zero remainder, thus guaranteeing detection of all odd‑count errors.

Q16. In CRC terminology, what does the degree \(r\) of the generator polynomial represent?

A.Number of data bits
B.Number of check bits ✅
C.Length of burst error detectable
D.Number of parity bits
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The degree \(r\) equals the number of parity (check) bits appended to the message; these bits form the CRC and determine the error‑detecting capability of the code.

Q17. If the length \(L\) of a burst error satisfies \(L\le r\), what is the detection outcome?

📖 Explanation: A fundamental property of CRCs states that any burst error whose length does not exceed the degree of the generator polynomial is guaranteed to produce a non‑zero syndrome, so it is always detected.

Q18. Given a generator polynomial of degree \(r=10\), what is the probability that a burst error of length 11 goes undetected?

Q19. For a generator polynomial that includes the factor \(x^{1}+1\), what can be inferred about detection of a single‑bit error?

Q20. How does increasing the degree \(r\) of the generator polynomial affect the probability of undetected burst errors longer than \(r+1\)?
