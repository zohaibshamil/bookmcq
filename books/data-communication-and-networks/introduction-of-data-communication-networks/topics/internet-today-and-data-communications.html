Q1. What does the acronym DSL stand for in the context of Internet connectivity?

🔹 A. Digital Subscriber Line
🔹 B. Direct Stream Line
🔹 C. Data Service Link
🔹 D. Digital Signal Loop

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

DSL stands for Digital Subscriber Line, a technology that uses existing telephone copper pairs to deliver high‑speed data while preserving voice service. It is distinct from other acronyms such as Direct Stream Line or Digital Signal Loop, which are not standard terminology in networking. Recognizing the correct expansion is fundamental when discussing broadband options.

Q2. If a household uses dial‑up service to connect to the Internet, which of the following is a direct consequence?

🔹 A. The connection supports simultaneous voice calls
🔹 B. Data transfer rates exceed 1 Mbps
🔹 C. The line remains permanently occupied for data only
🔹 D. Voice calls cannot be placed while the data session is active

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Dial‑up modems occupy the telephone line for the duration of the data session, preventing any concurrent voice calls. This limitation is a direct result of the line being repurposed for data transmission, unlike newer broadband methods that allow voice and data to coexist on the same pair.

Q3. Which statement correctly contrasts cable Internet and DSL regarding shared bandwidth among users?

🔹 A. Cable Internet provides dedicated bandwidth per household
🔹 B. DSL bandwidth is shared among all users on the same cable segment
🔹 C. Cable bandwidth is shared among neighbors, leading to variable speeds
🔹 D. DSL offers variable speeds based on neighbor usage

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Cable Internet typically uses a shared coaxial segment where multiple subscribers draw from the same pipe, so individual speeds can fluctuate with the number of active users. DSL, by contrast, assigns a dedicated portion of the line to each subscriber, resulting in more consistent performance regardless of neighboring traffic.

Q4. A small business needs simultaneous voice and data over a single telephone line. Which technology should it adopt?

🔹 A. Dial‑up modem
🔹 B. DSL service
🔹 C. Wireless WAN
🔹 D. Satellite Internet

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

DSL (Digital Subscriber Line) is designed to carry both voice and high‑speed data on the same copper pair, using frequency‑division multiplexing to keep the two streams separate. This makes it the appropriate choice for a small business that cannot install separate lines for voice and Internet connectivity.

Q5. A neighborhood experiences a surge in cable Internet traffic during evening hours. What is the most likely impact on an individual subscriber’s connection speed?

🔹 A. Speed will decrease because bandwidth is shared
🔹 B. Speed will increase due to network optimization
🔹 C. Speed remains unchanged as each subscriber has a dedicated channel
🔹 D. Speed fluctuates randomly independent of traffic

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When many households use the same cable segment simultaneously, the total available bandwidth must be divided among them, causing each user’s throughput to drop. This shared‑medium behavior explains why individual speeds tend to fall during peak usage periods, reflecting congestion on the local node.

Q6. Evaluate the advantages of a large corporation becoming its own local ISP compared with leasing a high‑speed WAN from an external provider.

🔹 A. Leasing offers complete control over routing
🔹 B. Becoming an ISP eliminates all maintenance costs
🔹 C. Leasing provides higher reliability than self‑managed infrastructure
🔹 D. Owning the ISP can reduce long‑term costs and allow customized peering

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

By operating as its own ISP, a corporation can negotiate peering agreements, tailor routing policies, and potentially lower recurring lease expenses. While initial capital outlay and ongoing maintenance increase, the strategic control and cost efficiencies over time often outweigh the simplicity of leasing a third‑party WAN.

Q7. How does peering between provider networks influence latency for end users accessing global services?

🔹 A. Peering creates shorter routing paths, reducing latency
🔹 B. Peering increases the number of hops, raising latency
🔹 C. Peering has no effect on latency, only on bandwidth
🔹 D. Peering diverts traffic through satellite links, increasing latency

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When two providers peer, traffic can be exchanged directly at a peering point rather than traversing multiple intermediate networks. This shortcut shortens the path, often reducing round‑trip time and improving user experience for services hosted across the Internet, especially when compared with indirect routing.

Q8. Assume a DSL line provides a downstream rate of 10 Mbps and an upstream rate of 1 Mbps. Ignoring protocol overhead, approximately how many seconds will it take to upload a 500 MB file?

🔹 A. 250 seconds
🔹 B. 2000 seconds
🔹 C. 4000 seconds
🔹 D. 800 seconds

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The upstream capacity of 1 Mbps equals \(1\text{ Mbps}=0.125\text{ MB/s}\). Dividing the file size by this rate gives \(500\text{ MB} / 0.125\text{ MB/s}=4000\text{ seconds}\). This calculation neglects protocol overhead, which would slightly increase the actual transfer time, but the estimate remains close to 4000 s.

Q9. Compare the security implications of using a wireless WAN versus a wired WAN for a corporate network.

🔹 A. Wired WAN is less susceptible to eavesdropping but both require proper security measures
🔹 B. Wireless WAN inherently provides stronger encryption than wired WAN
🔹 C. Wireless WAN eliminates the need for firewalls
🔹 D. Wired WAN cannot be segmented using VLANs

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Wired connections are physically confined, making them harder to intercept, yet they still need encryption, firewalls, and intrusion detection. Wireless WANs broadcast signals, exposing them to potential eavesdropping, so robust encryption (e.g., WPA3) and additional safeguards are essential. Both mediums demand comprehensive security strategies.

Q10. For a medium‑sized enterprise evaluating DSL, cable, and wireless WAN options, which recommendation best balances scalability, cost, and reliability?

🔹 A. Choose DSL for its low cost and highest reliability
🔹 B. Choose cable for maximum scalability despite variable cost
🔹 C. Choose wireless WAN for lowest latency
🔹 D. Adopt a hybrid approach using DSL for core traffic and wireless WAN for backup, ensuring scalability and cost‑effectiveness

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A hybrid strategy leverages DSL’s reliable baseline bandwidth for primary operations while employing wireless WAN as a redundant, on‑demand channel. This combination addresses scalability by adding capacity when needed, keeps costs moderate by avoiding full cable or premium wireless contracts, and enhances reliability through diversified pathways.
