📝 All Network Criteria in data communications MCQs

Q1. Which of the following is NOT one of the three most important network criteria?

🔹 A. Performance
🔹 B. Reliability
🔹 C. Security
🔹 D. Scalability

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Scalability, although a desirable attribute, is not listed among the three primary criteria—performance, reliability, and security—highlighted in the overview. Therefore option D correctly identifies the concept that falls outside the core trio, making it the correct answer.

Q2. If a network experiences increased throughput but also higher average delay, which of the following best explains the cause‑effect relationship?

🔹 A. Higher throughput always reduces congestion
🔹 B. Higher throughput can create congestion, leading to greater delay
🔹 C. Delay is independent of throughput
🔹 D. Both throughput and delay decrease as more users join

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When more data is injected into the network, the link utilization rises, which can cause queue buildup and packet waiting times. This congestion increases average delay even though the raw amount of data moved per unit time (throughput) grows, matching the described scenario.

Q3. A network designer observes that adding a second parallel link reduces the overall probability of total failure but increases the average recovery time after a failure. Which inference best explains the outcome?

🔹 A. Redundancy lowers failure probability and speeds recovery
🔹 B. Redundancy lowers failure probability but adds coordination overhead, lengthening recovery
🔹 C. Adding links always speeds recovery regardless of failure probability
🔹 D. Failure probability and recovery time are unrelated

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The addition of a parallel link provides an alternative path, decreasing the chance that a single point of failure disables the network. However, when a failure occurs, the system must detect, isolate, and re‑route traffic across the new topology, which introduces coordination overhead and can extend the mean time to recover.

Q4. Applying the principle of trade‑off between throughput and delay, which design choice would most likely improve real‑time voice quality?

🔹 A. Increase packet size to send more bits per frame
🔹 B. Decrease the number of routing hops between endpoints
🔹 C. Prioritize low‑delay traffic over bulk data transfers
🔹 D. Maximize overall bandwidth usage regardless of latency

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Real‑time voice is highly sensitive to delay; giving low‑delay traffic higher priority ensures packets traverse the network quickly, reducing jitter and latency. While larger packets or extra bandwidth can raise throughput, they may also increase queuing delay, harming voice quality. Prioritization directly addresses the delay component of the trade‑off.

Q5. Which statement correctly differentiates throughput and delay in the context of network performance?

🔹 A. Throughput measures the time a packet spends in a queue, while delay measures bits per second
🔹 B. Throughput is the rate of successful data delivery, delay is the time taken for a bit to travel from source to destination
🔹 C. Throughput and delay are interchangeable terms describing network speed
🔹 D. Delay determines how much data can be sent, whereas throughput is unrelated to time

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Throughput quantifies the volume of data successfully transmitted per unit time (often expressed as \(\text{bits/s}\)), whereas delay captures the latency experienced by a single unit of data from source to destination. The two metrics describe distinct aspects of performance and are not synonyms.

Q6. A company requires a network that is both highly reliable and secure. Which evaluation best reflects the trade‑off between these two criteria?

🔹 A. Increasing encryption strength always improves reliability
🔹 B. Stronger security protocols can add processing overhead, potentially raising latency and affecting reliability
🔹 C. Reliability and security are independent; improving one never impacts the other
🔹 D. Reducing redundancy enhances security by limiting attack surfaces

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Robust security mechanisms such as strong encryption or deep packet inspection add computational steps at each hop, which can increase processing time and susceptibility to bottlenecks. While this bolsters protection, the added latency may affect the network’s ability to meet strict reliability targets, illustrating the inherent trade‑off.

Q7. When comparing reliability and security, which of the following statements is most accurate?

🔹 A. Reliability focuses on data integrity, while security focuses on uptime
🔹 B. Reliability measures failure frequency and recovery time; security protects against unauthorized access and data loss
🔹 C. Security guarantees zero packet loss, whereas reliability does not
🔹 D. Both concepts are identical and interchangeable in network design

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Reliability is concerned with how often a network fails, how quickly it recovers, and its resilience to catastrophic events. Security, on the other hand, deals with preventing unauthorized access, ensuring confidentiality, and mitigating data breaches. The two address different risk domains, making option B the precise distinction.

Q8. If a network must guarantee a maximum end‑to‑end delay of less than \(50\,\text{ms}\) for critical messages, which combination of factors should be prioritized?

🔹 A. Maximize bandwidth while ignoring routing efficiency
🔹 B. Minimize number of hops and use low‑latency switching equipment
🔹 C. Increase packet size to reduce header overhead
🔹 D. Add redundant paths regardless of their speed

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Achieving a strict delay bound relies on reducing the time packets spend traversing the network. Fewer hops shorten the physical path, and low‑latency switches process frames quickly, both directly lowering the cumulative delay and helping meet the sub‑\(50\,\text{ms}\) requirement.

Q9. Design a network that balances high throughput, low delay, and strong security for a financial trading platform. Which integrated strategy best achieves this balance?

🔹 A. Deploy a single high‑speed link with no encryption to maximize speed
🔹 B. Use multiple parallel links with load‑balancing, prioritize latency‑sensitive traffic, and apply lightweight encryption such as TLS 1.3
🔹 C. Implement only robust firewalls and accept higher latency as a trade‑off
🔹 D. Rely on satellite links for redundancy despite their inherent high latency

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A multi‑link architecture spreads traffic, boosting aggregate throughput while load‑balancing prevents congestion. Prioritizing latency‑sensitive packets ensures low delay for time‑critical trades. Employing efficient, modern encryption (e.g., TLS 1.3) protects data without imposing excessive processing overhead, thereby satisfying the three competing objectives.

Q10. Explain the relationship between transit time and response time in a network that handles both data retrieval and command execution.

🔹 A. Transit time measures the delay for a single packet, while response time includes processing at the destination
🔹 B. Transit time and response time are identical concepts measured in different units
🔹 C. Response time only depends on server CPU speed, not on transit time
🔹 D. Transit time is always greater than response time

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Transit time is the pure propagation and queuing delay for a packet traveling from source to destination. Response time adds the additional interval required for the destination device to process the request and generate a reply. Thus, response time = transit time + processing time, linking the two metrics.

Q11. When the number of active users on a network doubles, which analytical outcome best describes the impact on throughput and delay, assuming the medium remains unchanged?

🔹 A. Throughput doubles while delay stays constant
🔹 B. Throughput remains the same but delay increases due to higher contention
🔹 C. Both throughput and delay improve because more users generate more traffic
🔹 D. Throughput decreases and delay decreases because the network becomes more efficient

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Doubling the user count raises the amount of traffic competing for the same bandwidth, leading to higher contention and queue lengths. While the raw capacity of the medium does not change, the effective throughput per user may stay similar or drop, and the average delay typically rises because packets wait longer for transmission.

Q12. A security breach caused encrypted traffic to be rerouted through a deep‑packet‑inspection appliance, adding an extra \(10\,\text{ms}\) of latency. Which inference about the effect on overall network performance is most logical?

🔹 A. The added latency will reduce throughput because packets are delayed longer
🔹 B. The extra latency only affects security, not performance metrics
🔹 C. Performance may degrade slightly due to increased delay, but throughput remains largely unchanged
🔹 D. Security improvements always increase both delay and throughput equally

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Introducing an inspection step adds a fixed processing delay, raising the overall end‑to‑end latency. However, the amount of data moved per unit time (throughput) is not directly reduced unless the appliance becomes a bottleneck. Therefore, the most logical outcome is a modest increase in delay while throughput stays essentially the same.
