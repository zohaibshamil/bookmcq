Q1. If the IAB decides to update the TCP/IP protocol suite, which body is primarily responsible for drafting the detailed specifications?

🔹 A. ISOC
🔹 B. IETF
🔹 C. IRTF
🔹 D. RG

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The IAB provides overall technical guidance, but the actual drafting of protocol specifications is carried out by the Internet Engineering Task Force. The IETF’s working groups develop the detailed documents that become RFCs, which the IAB later reviews. Therefore, the IETF is the primary entity responsible for drafting the new specifications.

Q2. What is the most likely consequence if the IETF fails to address a critical security vulnerability in the routing area?

🔹 A. Improved performance
🔹 B. Reduced research funding
🔹 C. No impact
🔹 D. Increased network outages

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

When a serious routing vulnerability remains unmitigated, routers may propagate malicious traffic or drop legitimate packets, leading to widespread service disruptions. Such outages affect many users and can cascade across the Internet, making reliability a major concern. Hence, the most probable outcome is an increase in network outages.

Q3. Given that the IRTF focuses on long‑term research, which of the following statements can be inferred about its working groups compared to IETF working groups?

🔹 A. They explore speculative protocols
🔹 B. They produce immediate standards
🔹 C. They prioritize short‑term fixes
🔹 D. They have no interaction with ISOC

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The IRTF’s mandate is to investigate future‑oriented topics, so its groups are inclined to study novel, often speculative protocols that may not be ready for immediate deployment. In contrast, IETF groups aim to produce operational standards that can be implemented promptly, reflecting the IRTF’s longer research horizon.

Q4. If the IAB serves as an external liaison between the Internet and other standards organizations, how would a change in the ISO/IEC standard for data encoding likely affect the IAB’s activities?

🔹 A. It would require the IAB to revise RFC editorial policies
🔹 B. It would have no effect
🔹 C. It would prompt IAB to coordinate with IETF to align new encoding with existing protocols
🔹 D. It would shift IAB’s funding

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A modification in an external encoding standard would create a compatibility issue for existing Internet protocols. The IAB, acting as the bridge, would work with the IETF to assess the impact, update relevant RFCs, and ensure that the new encoding is harmonized with current Internet specifications.

Q5. Which of the following best distinguishes the IETF from the IRTF?

🔹 A. IETF focuses on operational standards, IRTF focuses on long‑term research
🔹 B. IETF is funded by ISOC, IRTF is not
🔹 C. IETF is a standards body, IRTF is a funding agency
🔹 D. IETF works on hardware, IRTF works on software

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The primary difference lies in their objectives: the IETF concentrates on creating and refining operational Internet standards that can be deployed today, while the IRTF dedicates its effort to exploratory, long‑term research that may influence future standards. This distinction directly captures their respective missions.

Q6. Considering the roles of the IESG and the IRSG, which statement accurately reflects their relationship to their respective task forces?

🔹 A. IESG oversees IRTF, IRSG oversees IETF
🔹 B. Both report directly to ISOC
🔹 C. IESG oversees IETF, IRSG oversees IRTF
🔹 D. Neither has authority over task forces

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The IESG (Internet Engineering Steering Group) provides oversight and approval for the work of IETF working groups, ensuring standards meet quality criteria. Similarly, the IRSG (Internet Research Steering Group) governs the IRTF’s research groups, guiding their long‑term investigations. Thus, each steering group supervises the task forces within its own domain.

Q7. Which distinction correctly describes the funding mechanisms of ISOC versus the IAB?

🔹 A. ISOC receives government grants, IAB depends on membership fees
🔹 B. ISOC relies on donations and membership, while IAB operates primarily through volunteer effort supported by ISOC
🔹 C. ISOC is a for‑profit entity, IAB is nonprofit
🔹 D. ISOC has no budget, IAB controls all finances

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

ISOC sustains itself through membership dues, corporate sponsorships, and donations, providing a financial base for its activities. The IAB, in contrast, functions largely on volunteer contributions and receives support from ISOC rather than independent funding streams, reflecting its role as a technical advisory body.

Q8. If the IETF were to merge its routing area WG with the security area WG, what potential advantage and disadvantage would most likely result?

🔹 A. Advantage: unified standards; Disadvantage: diluted expertise
🔹 B. Advantage: faster deployment; Disadvantage: increased overhead
🔹 C. Advantage: reduced duplication; Disadvantage: loss of focus on security
🔹 D. Advantage: more funding; Disadvantage: regulatory hurdles

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Combining the two working groups could produce a more cohesive set of standards that address both routing and security together, simplifying implementation. However, the merger may also spread the expertise of each group thin, reducing the depth of focus on specialized security concerns and potentially weakening the quality of the resulting specifications.

Q9. When a new protocol is proposed within an IETF working group, which RFC series stage must it pass before becoming an Internet Standard?

🔹 A. Proposed Standard
🔹 B. Experimental
🔹 C. Draft Standard
🔹 D. Informational

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The IETF follows a staged publication process: a protocol first appears as a Proposed Standard, then advances to Draft Standard after sufficient implementation experience and testing, and finally attains the status of Internet Standard. Consequently, the protocol must successfully reach the Draft Standard stage before it can be elevated to an Internet Standard.

Q10. How does the editorial management of RFCs by the IAB influence the credibility of Internet standards?

🔹 A. It ensures peer review and consistency
🔹 B. It randomizes content
🔹 C. It limits access
🔹 D. It accelerates publication without review

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The IAB’s oversight of RFC editorial processes guarantees that each document undergoes rigorous peer review, adheres to formatting conventions, and maintains technical consistency. This systematic scrutiny builds confidence among implementers and stakeholders that the standards are reliable, well‑vetted, and suitable for widespread deployment.

Q11. Suppose an emerging technology requires modifications to both the application and network management areas of the IETF. Which coordination mechanism would best facilitate consistent development across these areas?

🔹 A. Separate area chairs working independently
🔹 B. Joint IETF plenary meeting
🔹 C. Ad‑hoc email list
🔹 D. External vendor committee

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A joint plenary session brings together representatives from the relevant IETF areas, allowing them to discuss interdependencies, align objectives, and synchronize timelines. This collaborative forum helps avoid contradictory specifications and ensures that changes in one area are reflected appropriately in the other, promoting coherent evolution of the technology.

Q12. If the ISOC decides to prioritize research on IPv6 adoption, which of the following sequences of actions best aligns with its mission and the existing structure of Internet governance?

🔹 A. Direct IETF to draft new standards → Ask IRTF to publish white papers → Seek ISOC membership increase
🔹 B. Have IAB create a new area → Allocate ISOC grants to IRTF → Task IETF with implementation specs
🔹 C. No action needed as IPv6 is already standardized
🔹 D. Fund IRTF research groups → Request IAB to update RFCs → Encourage IETF to develop deployment guidelines

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The ISOC can allocate grants to support IRTF research on IPv6, prompting the IAB to revise relevant RFCs to reflect new findings, and then task the IETF with creating practical deployment guidelines. This sequence leverages the existing governance hierarchy—research, editorial updating, and standards development—while staying true to ISOC’s role of fostering research and supporting standards processes.

Q13. What does the acronym IANA stand for?

🔹 A. Internet Assigned Numbers Authority
🔹 B. International Association of Network Administrators
🔹 C. Internet Architecture and Network Agency
🔹 D. Interconnected Autonomous Network Alliance

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

IANA stands for Internet Assigned Numbers Authority, the organization responsible for the global coordination of IP address allocation, autonomous system numbers, and other critical Internet protocol resources.
