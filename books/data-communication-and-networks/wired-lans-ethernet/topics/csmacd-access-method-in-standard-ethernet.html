📝 CSMA/CD access method in standard Ethernet (12 MCQs)

Shared Ethernet Access Through Carrier Sensing

Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection is the access method used by traditional shared, half-duplex Ethernet. Multiple stations connect to one shared communication medium and must coordinate transmission. A station listens before sending, monitors the medium while transmitting, and responds if another station transmits at the same time and causes a collision. This allows compatible interfaces to exchange frames across different wired media without changing upper layers.

Listening Before Transmission and Detecting Collisions

A station first senses the channel. If it is idle, it begins transmitting; if busy, it waits until the medium becomes available and the required interframe gap has passed. During transmission, it continues monitoring the medium. If a collision is detected, it stops the frame and sends a jam sequence so other stations recognize the event.

Jam Signals and Binary Exponential Backoff

After a collision, each station waits for a random number of slot times before retrying. Binary exponential backoff selects a random delay from an expanding range after repeated collisions, reducing the chance of another simultaneous attempt. The attempt counter and retry limit prevent endless retransmission under persistent contention. The receiver reverses these steps before examining the Ethernet frame.

The 512 Bit Time Collision Slot at Ten Megabits

At 10 Mb/s, the classic Ethernet slot time is 512 bit times, equal to 51.2 microseconds. This interval is related to the maximum collision-detection time for a valid collision domain. A station that sends a frame must keep transmitting long enough for a distant collision to return and be detected.

Where CSMA CD Operates in Classic Ethernet

For example, two workstations connected to a shared hub sense an idle channel and begin transmitting nearly together. Their signals collide, both detect the collision, send a jam sequence, and wait independently selected backoff periods. One may then transmit successfully while the other continues waiting for the channel. This separation keeps physical transmission distinct from MAC frame delivery.

Why Full Duplex Switch Links Do Not Use CSMA CD

'CSMA/CD prevents every collision' is incorrect because it detects collisions after simultaneous transmission begins. 'Switches always use CSMA/CD' is false because modern full-duplex point-to-point Ethernet has no shared collision domain. 'Backoff uses a fixed delay' is incorrect because binary exponential backoff chooses a random slot count from a growing range.

Tracing the CSMA CD Algorithm in Examinations

Trace the sequence of carrier sensing, transmission, collision detection, jam signalling, backoff, and retransmission. Know the 512-bit-time slot value for classic Ethernet and explain why minimum frame size matters. Contrast shared half-duplex operation with a switched full-duplex link, where simultaneous sending and receiving are supported without CSMA/CD. Always state the applicable rate, medium, encoding, and duplex mode.

CSMA/CD access method in standard Ethernet: Key Terms and Definitions

  • Carrier sense - Checks if medium is busy.
  • Collision - Overlapping signals corrupt transmission.
  • Jam signal - Announces a detected collision.
  • Backoff - Random delay before retransmission.
  • Slot time - Collision detection timing interval.
  • Half duplex - One direction at a time.

Q1. What does the variable \( G \) represent in the given scenario?

A.Number of frames per millisecond ✅
B.Frames per second divided by 4
C.Throughput factor
D.Error rate
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: \( G \) is defined as the rate at which frames are generated per millisecond. In the example, the system creates 250 frames per second, which equals \( \frac{1}{4} \) frames per millisecond, so \( G \) directly measures that per‑millisecond frame creation rate.

Q2. Using \( G = \frac{1}{4} \), what is the value of \( S = G e^{-2G} \) (rounded to three decimal places)?

A.0.2
B.0.18
C.0.152 ✅
D.0.12
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: First compute the exponent: \( e^{-2G}=e^{-0.5}\approx0.607\). Multiplying by \( G =0.25\) gives \( S =0.25\times0.607\approx0.1518\), which rounds to 0.152. This is the probability that a frame is successfully transmitted under the given traffic load.

Q3. If the throughput is calculated as \( 250 \times S \) and equals 38 frames per second, what would the throughput be if the frame creation rate doubled to 500 frames per second while \( S \) remains unchanged?

A.38
B.19
C.152
D.76 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Doubling the frame creation rate multiplies the product \(250 \times S\) by two, because \( S\) is unchanged. Therefore the new throughput is \(2 \times 38 = 76\) frames per second. The other options either keep the original value or incorrectly halve it.

Q4. Suppose the system now creates 200 frames per second. What is the new \( G \) value in frames per millisecond?

A.0.25
B.0.2 ✅
C.0.15
D.0.1
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A millisecond is one‑thousandth of a second, so 200 frames per second correspond to \(200/1000 = 0.20\) frames per millisecond. This directly gives the new \( G\) value, while the other numbers represent different conversion errors.

Q5. Which statement best describes the difference between using the exact formula \( S = G e^{-2G} \) and a linear approximation \( S \approx G \) for small \( G \) values?

A.Linear approximation overestimates S for larger G ✅
B.Exact formula always yields higher S
C.Exact formula ignores exponential decay
D.Both give identical results
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When \( G \) grows, the exponential term \( e^{-2G}\) drops below 1, causing the exact product \( G e^{-2G}\) to be smaller than the linear estimate \( G\). Thus the linear model tends to overestimate the success probability for moderate‑size \( G\) values, while the exact formula captures the decay.

Q6. A network operates with \( G = 0.3 \) frames per millisecond. Calculate the throughput (frames per second) using the formula \( \text{Throughput} = 250 \times S \), where \( S = G e^{-2G} \). Choose the nearest integer.

A.40
B.45
C.41 ✅
D.50
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: First compute \( S =0.3 \times e^{-0.6}\). The exponential term \( e^{-0.6}\approx0.549\), so \( S\approx0.3 \times 0.549 =0.1647\). Multiplying by 250 gives \(250 \times 0.1647 \approx 41.2\) frames per second, which rounds to 41.

Q7. Which of the following best explains why throughput initially rises as \( G \) increases from zero?

A.Higher G means more frames are generated, increasing potential successful transmissions
B.Higher G reduces collision probability
C.Throughput is independent of G
D.Increasing G decreases system efficiency ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: When \( G\) is very small, adding more frames raises the chance that a frame finds a clear slot, so the successful transmission probability—and thus throughput—grows. This positive relationship holds until congestion causes collisions, after which additional frames no longer improve throughput.

Q8. To compute the throughput for a system creating 250 frames per second, which ordered sequence of steps is correct?

A.Multiply 250 by G, then apply exponential, then add
B.Compute G, compute S using \( S = G e^{-2G} \), multiply by 250 ✅
C.Compute S first, then find G, then multiply
D.Directly multiply 250 by G
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct procedure starts by finding \( G\) (frames per millisecond), then evaluating the success probability \( S = G e^{-2G}\). Finally, multiply the base frame rate (250) by \( S\) to obtain the effective throughput. Any other ordering skips essential calculations.

Q9. If network congestion causes the effective frame generation rate to drop to 150 frames per second, what is the expected qualitative effect on \( G \) and \( S \)?

A.Both G and S increase
B.G decreases while S increases
C.Both G and S decrease ✅
D.G decreases while S stays same
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A lower generation rate reduces the per‑millisecond frame count, so \( G\) falls. Because \( S = G e^{-2G}\) is a monotonically increasing function for small \( G\), a smaller \( G\) also yields a smaller \( S\). Hence both values decline.

Q10. Which adjustment is most appropriate to reach a target throughput of approximately 60 frames per second, assuming \( S \) cannot exceed 0.2?

A.Decrease frame creation rate to 200 frames per second
B.Keep rate same but improve S to 0.24
C.Switch to a different access method
D.Increase frame creation rate to 300 frames per second ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: With \( S\le0.2\), achieving 60 frames per second requires a frame rate of at least \(60/0.2 = 300\) frames per second. Raising the generation rate to 300 meets the target without needing to alter the success probability, while the other options either reduce throughput or demand an unattainable \( S\).

Q11. If the frame size is doubled while the system maintains the same number of frames per second, how does \( G \) change and what is the likely impact on throughput?

A.G doubles, throughput may increase or decrease depending on S
B.G doubles, and throughput doubles if S stays constant ✅
C.G halves, throughput decreases
D.G unchanged, throughput unchanged
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Doubling the frame size doubles the amount of channel time each frame occupies, which effectively doubles the traffic intensity \( G\). Assuming the success probability \( S\) does not change, the product \( \text{Throughput}=250 \times S\) will also double, yielding a higher throughput.

Q12. Given that \( S = G e^{-2G} \) reaches its maximum at \( G = \frac{1}{2} \), what would be the effect on throughput if the current system operates at \( G = 0.25 \) and the designer decides to increase \( G \) to 0.5?

A.Throughput will increase to its maximum possible value
B.Throughput will decrease
C.Throughput remains the same
D.Throughput will reach its theoretical maximum ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: At \( G =0.5\) the function \( S\) attains its peak, so the success probability is highest. Raising \( G\) from 0.25 to 0.5 therefore raises \( S\) and consequently the overall throughput, moving the system toward its optimal performance level.
