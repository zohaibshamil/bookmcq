Q1. If station B sends an RTS that is heard by station A but not by station C, which subsequent frame ensures that C refrains from transmitting?

🔹 A. ACK
🔹 B. CTS
🔹 C. DATA
🔹 D. NAK

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The CTS frame carries the reservation duration and is broadcast by A. Because C receives this CTS, it learns that the channel will be occupied for the indicated period and therefore must defer any transmission, preventing a hidden‑station collision.

Q2. Which statement best contrasts the reservation method with pure CSMA/CA in terms of channel access?

🔹 A. Reservation guarantees a deterministic slot before data, while CSMA/CA relies on random backoff after sensing
🔹 B. Reservation uses random backoff, whereas CSMA/CA uses fixed time slots
🔹 C. Both methods require a handshake before data transmission
🔹 D. Reservation eliminates the need for carrier sensing entirely

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Reservation provides a pre‑allocated minislot that all stations know in advance, eliminating the need for carrier sensing before each transmission. In contrast, pure CSMA/CA depends on each node listening to the medium and then backing off randomly, which can lead to collisions under heavy load.

Q3. In a network with three stations, each having a dedicated minislot, how many reservation frames are needed for all stations to send one data frame each?

🔹 A. 1
🔹 B. 2
🔹 C. 3
🔹 D. 4

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Each station must first place its reservation in its own minislot within a reservation frame. To allow three stations to transmit one data frame each, three separate reservation frames are required—one for each station’s reservation—so the total number of frames equals the number of stations.

Q4. What is the hidden‑station problem in wireless networks?

🔹 A. A situation where two stations are out of range of each other but interfere at a common receiver
🔹 B. A case where a station cannot detect any other stations
🔹 C. An issue where stations transmit simultaneously on the same frequency by design
🔹 D. A problem unique to wired Ethernet networks

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The hidden‑station problem occurs when two stations cannot hear each other's transmissions yet both can be heard by a third station. This creates a risk of collisions at the common receiver because the hidden stations are unaware of each other's activity.

Q5. If station C receives a CTS containing a duration of \(5\text{ ms}\), what can C infer about the channel?

🔹 A. The channel will be free for the next 5 ms
🔹 B. The channel will be occupied for the next 5 ms and C must defer
🔹 C. C should transmit immediately after the CTS
🔹 D. C must send an ACK after 5 ms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The CTS frame announces the time the medium will be used for the upcoming data exchange. Receiving a duration of 5 ms informs C that the channel will be occupied for that interval, so C must postpone any transmission until the period expires to avoid interfering.

Q6. If two hidden stations transmit RTS frames simultaneously, what is the most likely outcome?

🔹 A. Both RTS frames are successfully received by the AP
🔹 B. Both RTS frames collide at the AP, causing a retransmission after a random backoff
🔹 C. One RTS is received while the other is ignored
🔹 D. The AP sends a combined CTS for both stations

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

When hidden stations send RTS at the same instant, their signals arrive at the access point at overlapping times, resulting in a collision. The AP cannot decode either RTS, so it does not issue a CTS, and each station will invoke the backoff algorithm and retry after a random interval.

Q7. Under heavy traffic, which method typically yields higher throughput?

🔹 A. Pure CSMA/CA with exponential backoff
🔹 B. Reservation method with fixed minislots
🔹 C. TDMA with no reservation
🔹 D. Aloha protocol

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The reservation method allocates a dedicated minislot for each station before data transmission, preventing collisions that are common in CSMA/CA under heavy load. By guaranteeing a collision‑free interval, the reservation approach maintains higher effective throughput when many stations contend for the medium.

Q8. What is the primary advantage of using RTS/CTS over basic CSMA/CA in environments with hidden terminals?

🔹 A. It eliminates the need for carrier sensing
🔹 B. It provides a virtual carrier‑sensing mechanism that informs hidden stations of upcoming transmissions
🔹 C. It reduces the packet size for data frames
🔹 D. It allows simultaneous transmissions from multiple stations

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RTS/CTS introduces a handshake that conveys a reservation duration to all stations, including those that cannot hear the original sender. This virtual carrier‑sensing (via the Network Allocation Vector) lets hidden terminals defer transmission, substantially reducing collisions compared with plain CSMA/CA.

Q9. How could the reservation frame be extended to support variable‑length data bursts while still preventing collisions?

🔹 A. Add a length field that specifies the burst size
🔹 B. Include a checksum for each data frame
🔹 C. Use a separate control channel for each burst
🔹 D. Limit all bursts to a fixed size

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

By inserting a length or duration field into the reservation frame, each station announces how long its upcoming burst will occupy the medium. Other stations can then calculate the exact time window to remain silent, preserving the collision‑free guarantee even when burst lengths vary.

Q10. If a network has \(N\) stations and each reservation frame contains \(N\) minislots, what is the maximum number of data frames that can be transmitted in one interval?

🔹 A. \(N/2\)
🔹 B. \(N\)
🔹 C. \(2N\)
🔹 D. Unlimited

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each station can occupy its own minislot to place a reservation. Because there are \(N\) minislots, at most \(N\) stations can make reservations in a single frame, allowing up to \(N\) data frames to be sent after the reservation phase. Thus the ceiling is the number of stations, \(N\).

Q11. Which statement correctly differentiates the exposed‑terminal problem from the hidden‑terminal problem when using RTS/CTS?

🔹 A. Exposed terminals cause collisions, hidden terminals cause idle time
🔹 B. Hidden terminals cannot hear each other, exposed terminals can hear each other but are unnecessarily silenced
🔹 C. Both problems are resolved identically by RTS/CTS
🔹 D. Exposed terminals only occur in wired networks

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The hidden‑terminal issue occurs when two stations are out of range of each other, leading to collisions at a common receiver. In contrast, the exposed‑terminal scenario involves a station that can hear a neighboring transmission and therefore suppresses its own transmission despite not causing interference. RTS/CTS mainly addresses hidden terminals, while exposed terminals may still experience unnecessary deferral.

Q12. A wireless LAN employing CSMA/CA with RTS/CTS adds a new node D that is within range of node B but not of the AP. Which additional mechanism most effectively prevents D from causing collisions?

🔹 A. Increasing the RTS transmission power of the AP
🔹 B. Having node B broadcast an updated Network Allocation Vector (NAV) after sending CTS
🔹 C. Requiring D to perform carrier sensing before transmitting any frame
🔹 D. Disabling RTS/CTS for all nodes

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Node D cannot hear the AP’s CTS, so it would not know about B’s upcoming transmission. By having B broadcast an updated NAV after its CTS, D receives the virtual carrier‑sensing information indirectly, allowing it to defer its own transmission and thus avoiding collisions despite being out of the AP’s range.

Q13. If the duration field in a CTS frame is mistakenly set to a value shorter than the actual data transmission time, what cascading effects are most likely to occur?

🔹 A. All stations will correctly wait for the full transmission time
🔹 B. Hidden stations may begin transmitting early, causing collisions and subsequent backoff storms
🔹 C. The sender will abort transmission early
🔹 D. The network will switch to a different channel automatically

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

An underestimated CTS duration causes hidden stations to believe the medium will be free sooner than it actually is. They may start transmitting while the original data frame is still in progress, leading to collisions. Those collisions trigger exponential backoff algorithms, increasing contention and potentially degrading overall network performance.
