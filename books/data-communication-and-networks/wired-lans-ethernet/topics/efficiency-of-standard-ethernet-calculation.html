Q1. In the described collision scenario, station A transmits from time t1 to t4 while station C transmits from time t2 to t3. Which station sends bits for a longer interval?

🔹 A. Station C
🔹 B. Both stations transmit for equal intervals
🔹 C. Cannot be determined from the given times
🔹 D. Station A

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Station A starts at t1 and finishes at t4, so its transmission interval is t4‑t1. Station C transmits only from t2 to t3, which is shorter (t3‑t2). Therefore, station A occupies the channel for a longer period, making it the correct choice.

Q2. A station uses the p‑persistent CSMA method with persistence probability p = 0.2. What is the probability that it will defer to the next time slot because the channel is busy?

🔹 A. 0.8
🔹 B. 0.2
🔹 C. 0.5
🔹 D. 0

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The probability that the channel is busy and the station decides to wait is q = 1 – p. With p = 0.2, q = 1 – 0.2 = 0.8, so there is an 80 % chance the station will defer to the next slot. This follows directly from the definition of q.

Q3. Assuming each slot attempt is independent, what is the expected number of slots (including the successful one) that a p‑persistent station with p = 0.4 must wait before it successfully transmits?

🔹 A. 1.5
🔹 B. 0.4
🔹 C. 2.5
🔹 D. 4

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The number of trials until the first success in a geometric distribution has expected value 1/p. With p = 0.4, the expected number of slots is 1/0.4 = 2.5. This count includes the slot in which the transmission finally succeeds, reflecting the average waiting time before a successful attempt.

Q4. Which statement correctly contrasts 1‑persistent and non‑persistent CSMA regarding channel utilization under high traffic?

🔹 A. 1‑persistent yields lower utilization
🔹 B. 1‑persistent may cause more collisions
🔹 C. non‑persistent yields higher utilization
🔹 D. non‑persistent always leads to immediate transmission

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

In high‑traffic conditions, a 1‑persistent station transmits as soon as the channel becomes idle, increasing the chance that multiple stations will transmit simultaneously, leading to more collisions. Non‑persistent stations, by waiting a random time, reduce the probability of simultaneous transmissions, so the statement about 1‑persistent causing more collisions is correct.

Q5. When the persistence probability p is set to 1, how does p‑persistent CSMA behave relative to 1‑persistent CSMA?

🔹 A. More deferment
🔹 B. Less collisions
🔹 C. No transmission
🔹 D. Identical behavior

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Setting p = 1 removes the probabilistic waiting component; the station transmits immediately whenever the medium is idle. This is exactly how 1‑persistent CSMA operates, so the two methods become indistinguishable in behavior, making the answer 'Identical behavior' correct.

Q6. If the binary exponential backoff limits the maximum backoff window to 2^k slots after k collisions, how does increasing k from 3 to 6 affect the theoretical maximum efficiency of Ethernet?

🔹 A. Increases efficiency
🔹 B. Decreases efficiency
🔹 C. No effect
🔹 D. Efficiency becomes zero

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Increasing k expands the maximum backoff window from 2^3 = 8 slots to 2^6 = 64 slots. While a larger window reduces the probability of repeated collisions, it also introduces longer idle periods after a collision, which lowers the proportion of time the channel carries useful data, thus decreasing theoretical maximum efficiency.

Q7. Given a propagation delay of 25 µs and a transmission rate of 10 Mbps, what is the minimum Ethernet frame size required for CSMA/CD to guarantee collision detection?

🔹 A. 64 bytes
🔹 B. 128 bytes
🔹 C. 256 bytes
🔹 D. 32 bytes

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Collision detection requires that the transmission time be at least twice the propagation delay (2 × 25 µs = 50 µs). At 10 Mbps, 50 µs corresponds to 10 × 10⁶ bits · 50 × 10⁻⁶ s = 500 bits, which is 62.5 bytes. Ethernet rounds up to the nearest whole byte, giving a minimum frame size of 64 bytes.

Q8. How does the requirement that a transmitting station must detect a collision before the last bit is sent influence Ethernet frame length and network diameter design?

🔹 A. Longer frames allow larger diameters
🔹 B. Shorter frames increase reliability
🔹 C. Frame length is independent of diameter
🔹 D. Larger diameters require shorter frames

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To ensure a collision can be sensed before the frame ends, the transmission time of the frame must exceed the round‑trip propagation time across the network. Therefore, increasing the allowed network diameter (greater propagation delay) forces the use of longer frames, while longer frames also permit larger physical extents, linking the two design parameters.

Q9. If a station spends an average transmitting time \(T_t = 800\) µs and an average idle time \(T_i = 200\) µs between transmissions, what is the channel efficiency?

🔹 A. 0.6
🔹 B. 0.7
🔹 C. 0.8
🔹 D. 0.9

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Channel efficiency is defined as the fraction of time the channel carries useful data: \(\eta = \frac{T_t}{T_t + T_i}\). Substituting the values gives \(\eta = \frac{800}{800 + 200} = \frac{800}{1000} = 0.8\), which corresponds to 80 % efficiency.

Q10. What does CSMA/CD stand for in Ethernet networking?

🔹 A. Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance
🔹 B. Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection
🔹 C. Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Prevention
🔹 D. Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Handling

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

CSMA/CD is the protocol used in traditional Ethernet to manage access to the shared medium. The acronym expands to Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection, describing how stations listen before transmitting and detect collisions while transmitting.
