📝 Gigabit Ethernet basics (8 MCQs)

Ethernet Operation at One Gigabit per Second

Gigabit Ethernet extends IEEE 802.3 networking to a nominal data rate of 1 Gb/s while retaining the Ethernet MAC frame and 48-bit addressing system. It supports copper and optical physical implementations. Gigabit links are widely used for workstation access, switch uplinks, servers, and connections where Fast Ethernet capacity is insufficient. This allows compatible interfaces to exchange frames across different wired media without changing upper layers.

Gigabit Ethernet MAC and Physical Layer Architecture

The MAC sublayer continues to format Ethernet frames and manage delivery, while the PHY converts the bit stream for the selected medium. Gigabit Ethernet includes 1000BASE-T over twisted-pair copper and 1000BASE-X variants such as SX and LX over fibre. Encoding and signalling differ between PHYs, but the MAC service remains Ethernet.

Copper and Fibre Variants of Gigabit Ethernet

1000BASE-T transmits over four pairs of balanced twisted-pair cable, using all pairs for simultaneous bidirectional signalling. 1000BASE-SX and 1000BASE-LX use optical fibre with different wavelength and reach characteristics. 1000BASE-CX uses short shielded copper cabling. The medium and transceiver determine the physical link implementation. The receiver reverses these steps before examining the Ethernet frame.

A Gigabit Link Between Access and Distribution Switches

A server may connect to a switch using 1000BASE-T, while two switches use 1000BASE-SX fibre through compatible optical transceivers. Both links operate at 1 Gb/s and carry the same kind of Ethernet frames. The switch forwards frames based on MAC addresses rather than interpreting the application data. This example shows how the specified medium carries Ethernet data.

Full Duplex Communication and Backward Frame Compatibility

Gigabit Ethernet normally operates as a switched full-duplex link, allowing simultaneous transmission and reception. The Ethernet frame structure remains compatible with earlier Ethernet generations, so 10-Mb/s, 100-Mb/s, and 1-Gb/s devices can communicate through appropriate switching equipment. Link negotiation establishes compatible speed and duplex settings. This separation keeps physical transmission distinct from MAC frame delivery.

Distinguishing 1000BASE T from 1000BASE X

'Gigabit Ethernet always uses fibre' is incorrect because 1000BASE-T uses copper twisted pair. 'One gigabit means one gigabyte per second' is false because 1 Gb/s equals 125 MB/s before overhead. 'Gigabit Ethernet requires a new MAC address format' is incorrect because it retains standard Ethernet MAC addressing. That distinction prevents confusing frame handling with signal transmission.

Gigabit Ethernet Rates and Media in Examinations

Identify 1 Gb/s as the nominal rate and distinguish 1000BASE-T from 1000BASE-SX, 1000BASE-LX, and 1000BASE-CX. Explain the role of the MAC and PHY, recognize full-duplex switched operation, and convert bits to bytes correctly. Relate fibre selection to wavelength, fibre type, and required link distance. Always state the applicable rate, medium, encoding, and duplex mode.

Gigabit Ethernet basics: Key Terms

  • Gigabit Ethernet - Ethernet operating at one gigabit.
  • 1000BASE T - Gigabit Ethernet over twisted pair.
  • 1000BASE SX - Short wavelength fibre Gigabit PHY.
  • 1000BASE LX - Long wavelength fibre Gigabit PHY.
  • Transceiver - Converts electrical and optical signals.
  • Full duplex - Sends and receives simultaneously.

📝 All Gigabit Ethernet basics MCQs

Q1. Which effect best explains the reduction in collisions when upgrading from 100 Mbps to 1 Gbps Ethernet?

A.The frame size is increased, leading to longer transmission times.
B.The transmission time per frame is shorter, reducing the collision window. ✅
C.The Ethernet protocol disables CSMA/CD at higher speeds.
D.Higher bandwidth automatically eliminates collisions.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The shorter transmission time at 1 Gbps means each frame occupies the medium for a much briefer period. Because collisions can only occur while a frame is being transmitted, the window in which another station can cause a collision becomes significantly smaller, thus reducing the overall likelihood of collisions.

Q2. Which statement correctly compares the physical media requirements for 1000BASE‑T and 10GBASE‑T Ethernet?

A.1000BASE‑T requires Cat5e cabling, while 10GBASE‑T can operate over Cat5e as well.
B.Both standards use identical signaling methods, so no changes in transceiver design are needed.
C.1000BASE‑T works over Cat5e/6 up to 100 m, whereas 10GBASE‑T typically needs Cat6a or higher to achieve the same distance. ✅
D.10GBASE‑T uses fiber exclusively, while 1000BASE‑T uses copper.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: 1000BASE‑T is designed for copper cabling such as Cat5e or Cat6 and can reliably reach 100 m. In contrast, 10GBASE‑T operates at ten times the speed and therefore requires higher‑performance cabling, typically Cat6a or better, to maintain signal integrity over the same distance. This distinction directly reflects the media‑specific demands of each standard.

Q3. How long does it take to transmit a 1500‑byte Ethernet frame at 1 Gbps?

A.\(12 \,\mu\text{s}\) ✅
B.\(1.2 \,\text{ms}\)
C.\(120 \,\mu\text{s}\)
D.\(12 \,\text{ns}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A 1500‑byte frame contains 1500 × 8 = 12 000 bits. At a data rate of 1 Gbps (1 × 10⁹ bits s⁻¹), the transmission time is 12 000 / 1 × 10⁹ = 12 × 10⁻⁶ seconds, which equals 12 µs. This simple division demonstrates the relationship between frame size, link speed, and transmission delay.

Q4. Which statement best differentiates the responsibilities of the MAC sublayer from the PHY sublayer in Gigabit Ethernet?

A.MAC handles encoding, PHY handles addressing.
B.MAC provides frame delimiting and error detection, while PHY deals with signal transmission and media characteristics. ✅
C.MAC controls cable type, PHY manages IP routing.
D.MAC and PHY have identical functions.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The MAC sublayer is responsible for framing data, adding pre‑ambles, performing CRC checks, and managing access to the medium. The PHY sublayer, on the other hand, converts these frames into electrical or optical signals, handles line coding, and interacts with the physical medium. Thus, MAC focuses on logical framing, while PHY handles the actual transmission characteristics.

Q5. Given that the vulnerable period for a collision is the frame transmission time, what inference can be drawn about collision probability at 1 Gbps?

A.As data rate increases, the ratio of propagation delay to transmission time increases, making collisions more frequent.
B.The reduced transmission time at 1 Gbps lowers the vulnerable period, thus decreasing collision probability.
C.Propagation delay becomes negligible, so CSMA/CD is unnecessary at any speed.
D.Collision detection time remains constant regardless of speed. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: At 1 Gbps the time to push a frame onto the wire shrinks dramatically, which shortens the interval during which another station could interfere. Because the vulnerable period is directly tied to transmission time, a shorter frame transmission reduces the chance that two stations will overlap their transmissions, thereby lowering the probability of collisions.

Q6. When deploying 10‑Gigabit Ethernet in a metropolitan area network, which trade‑off best describes fiber versus copper cabling?

A.Fiber offers lower attenuation and longer reach but higher cost; copper offers cheaper installation but limited to 100 m. ✅
B.Copper provides higher bandwidth than fiber for MAN.
C.Fiber cannot support 10 GbE over distances greater than 10 km.
D.Copper has lower latency than fiber.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Fiber optic cables exhibit minimal signal loss and can carry 10 GbE over many kilometers, making them ideal for long‑haul MAN links despite higher material and installation expenses. Copper cabling, while less costly and easier to install, suffers from higher attenuation and is typically limited to about 100 m for 10 GbE, restricting its usefulness to short‑run connections.

Q7. How does IEEE 802.3ab enable Gigabit Ethernet over twisted‑pair cabling?

A.It uses 8B/10B encoding over two pairs.
B.It mandates shielded Cat7 cabling.
C.It utilizes 4D‑PAM5 signaling across all four twisted‑pair pairs. ✅
D.It halves the data rate to maintain compatibility with older devices.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The 802.3ab standard defines Gigabit Ethernet over Category 5e/6 twisted‑pair by employing 4‑dimensional pulse‑amplitude modulation with five voltage levels (4D‑PAM5). This scheme transmits data simultaneously on all four pairs, achieving 1 Gbps while maintaining backward compatibility with existing cabling infrastructures.

Q8. Why does operating in full‑duplex mode remove the need for CSMA/CD in Gigabit Ethernet?

A.Because frames are never transmitted simultaneously, collisions cannot occur. ✅
B.Full‑duplex uses separate frequencies for send/receive.
C.It disables the MAC sublayer.
D.It replaces Ethernet with Wi‑Fi.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: In full‑duplex mode each device has a dedicated transmit and receive path, allowing simultaneous bidirectional communication. Since each node transmits on its own channel, no two stations can interfere on the same medium at the same time, eliminating the possibility of collisions and thus rendering the CSMA/CD contention mechanism unnecessary.]
