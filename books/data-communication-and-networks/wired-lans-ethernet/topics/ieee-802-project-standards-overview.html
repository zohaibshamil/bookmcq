What is IEEE 802 project standards overview?

The IEEE 802 project is a family of international standards developed by the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers to define the physical and data link layers of wired and wireless networks, with IEEE 802.3 specifically governing Ethernet technologies to ensure interoperability between equipment from different manufacturers.

Q1. Which of the following best defines the term 'framing' in the IEEE 802 data link layer?

A.The process of establishing a physical connection between two nodes.
B.The method of encrypting data before transmission.
C.The technique of routing packets through multiple networks.
D.The organization of bits into recognizable units for transmission and reception. ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Framing is the data‑link function that groups a stream of bits into discrete frames, adding start and stop delimiters so the receiver can identify where each logical unit begins and ends. This enables synchronization and error detection. The other options describe link establishment, encryption, or routing, which are not the purpose of framing in IEEE 802.

Q2. If the physical layer fails to provide accurate bit synchronization, what is the most likely immediate effect on the framing process?

A.Frames will be transmitted faster than the receiver can process them.
B.The receiver may misinterpret frame boundaries, leading to corrupted frames. ✅
C.The link will automatically switch to a dedicated mode.
D.Error‑control mechanisms will correct all synchronization errors.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Without reliable bit synchronization, the receiver cannot reliably determine where each frame starts and ends. Consequently, it may interpret the delimiters incorrectly, causing frames to be read with shifted boundaries and resulting in corrupted or discarded data. The other choices describe unrelated behaviors that do not directly stem from synchronization loss.

Q3. Compare character‑oriented and bit‑oriented framing methods in IEEE 802. Which statement accurately reflects a key difference?

A.Character‑oriented framing uses flag bytes, while bit‑oriented framing inserts flag bits and employs bit stuffing. ✅
B.Bit‑oriented framing requires a fixed‑length header, whereas character‑oriented framing does not.
C.Character‑oriented framing cannot detect errors, while bit‑oriented framing can.
D.Bit‑oriented framing is only used in wireless links, while character‑oriented framing is for wired links.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Character‑oriented framing relies on special byte values (e.g., 0x7E) to mark frame boundaries, whereas bit‑oriented framing uses a unique bit pattern (01111110) as a flag and performs bit stuffing to prevent accidental flag appearance inside the payload. This distinction directly impacts how each method handles data transparency and synchronization.

A.Stop‑and‑wait ARQ
B.Go‑Back‑N ARQ with a window size of 2
C.Selective Repeat ARQ with an appropriately sized window ✅
D.No error control, relying on CRC alone
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Selective Repeat ARQ allows the sender to retransmit only the erroneous frames while correctly received frames continue progressing, reducing unnecessary retransmissions. With an error probability of 0.001, a modest window size can keep the overhead under 10 % and achieve higher throughput than stop‑and‑wait or Go‑Back‑N, which would waste bandwidth on redundant retransmissions.

Q5. What is the most direct consequence of omitting flow‑control mechanisms in an IEEE 802 link?

A.The sender may overwhelm the receiver, causing buffer overflow and frame loss. ✅
B.Frames will be automatically retransmitted until successful.
C.The link will switch to a higher transmission rate.
D.Error detection will become more accurate.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Without flow control, the transmitting node can send frames faster than the receiving node can process and store them. This mismatch leads to buffer overflow, resulting in dropped or corrupted frames and reduced overall reliability. The other options describe behaviors that are either automatic responses to errors or unrelated to flow‑control absence.

Q6. When comparing stop‑and‑wait ARQ to a sliding‑window protocol with a window size of 4, which performance metric is most improved?

A.Latency for a single frame
B.Channel utilization under high propagation delay ✅
C.Error detection capability
D.Frame size flexibility
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A sliding‑window protocol permits multiple frames to be in transit before acknowledgments are received, dramatically increasing the proportion of time the channel is used for data transmission, especially when propagation delay is large. Stop‑and‑wait must wait for each acknowledgment, leaving the channel idle for much of the round‑trip time.

Q7. Design a simple protocol that combines bit stuffing with a CRC of polynomial G(x)=x3+x+1G(x)=x^3+x+1G(x)=x3+x+1. Which step must be performed first to guarantee that the flag pattern does not appear in the transmitted data?

A.Calculate the CRC and append it before bit stuffing. ✅
B.Insert flag bits, then compute the CRC over the entire frame.
C.Apply bit stuffing to the payload, then compute and append the CRC.
D.Compute the CRC, then replace any flag pattern with a special escape sequence.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The CRC must be calculated on the original payload and appended first, because the subsequent bit‑stuffing process will modify the combined data (payload plus CRC) to eliminate any accidental flag pattern. Only after stuffing is complete are the start and end flag bits added, ensuring the flag sequence never appears within the transmitted bits.

Q8. In a broadcast link supporting IEEE 802, which of the following statements logically follows regarding frame delivery?

A.All frames are received by every node, but only the intended recipient processes them. ✅
B.Frames are duplicated and sent individually to each node.
C.Only the source node receives acknowledgment frames.
D.Error control is unnecessary because broadcasts guarantee reliability.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A broadcast medium transmits a single copy of each frame that all nodes can hear. Each node examines the destination address; only the node whose address matches the frame processes it, while others discard it. This behavior preserves bandwidth and matches the logical expectation for broadcast delivery in IEEE 802 networks.

Q10. When using bit stuffing in an IEEE 802 frame, after a sequence of five consecutive '1' bits, what is inserted?

A.A start flag
B.A '0' bit ✅
C.A CRC remainder
D.A pause signal
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Bit stuffing inserts a single '0' after any occurrence of five consecutive '1' bits to prevent the accidental creation of the flag pattern (01111110). This ensures that the receiver can reliably detect the flag delimiters without mistaking payload data for control information.

Q11. If the propagation delay for a link is D=2D = 2D=2 ms and the transmission rate is 1 Mbps, how does increasing the frame size from 500 bits to 2000 bits affect the theoretical throughput?

A.Throughput decreases proportionally to the frame size.
B.Throughput increases because the ratio of transmission time to propagation delay grows. ✅
C.Throughput remains unchanged, as it depends only on link speed.
D.Throughput becomes limited by the error‑control algorithm.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Larger frames require more time to transmit, so the proportion of time spent sending data relative to waiting for propagation (the idle time) becomes larger. This raises the effective utilization of the channel, thereby increasing theoretical throughput, especially when propagation delay dominates the round‑trip time.

Q12. Which DLC service defined in IEEE 802 most closely corresponds to the OSI Data Link Layer's 'Error Control' function?

A.Flow control
B.Framing
C.Addressing
D.Error detection and recovery ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The OSI Data Link Layer's error‑control function encompasses mechanisms that detect and correct errors, such as CRC checks and retransmission strategies. In IEEE 802, this role is explicitly provided by the 'Error detection and recovery' service, making it the direct counterpart to the OSI definition.
