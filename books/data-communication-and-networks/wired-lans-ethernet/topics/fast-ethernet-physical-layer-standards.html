What is Fast Ethernet physical layer standards?

Fast Ethernet physical layer standards include 100BASE-TX for twisted pair, 100BASE-FX for fiber optic, and 100BASE-T4 for lower-grade cabling, each specifying distinct encoding schemes, signaling methods, and maximum segment lengths to achieve 100 Mbps throughput across different media types while adhering to the common MAC sublayer.

📝 All Fast Ethernet physical layer standards MCQs

Q1. What does the Interframe Space (IFS) represent in the CSMA/CA protocol?

A.The fixed time a station must wait after transmitting before listening for an acknowledgment
B.The interval allowing a transmitted signal from a distant station to reach the local station before channel assessment ✅
C.The duration of the contention window
D.The time allocated for the RTS/CTS handshake
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: IFS is the period that lets the front of a transmitted signal from a remote station arrive at the local station before it checks the medium. This ensures that the station does not misinterpret a busy channel as idle, providing a timing guard before the contention window begins.

Q2. What is the primary purpose of the contention window in CSMA/CA?

A.To introduce a random backoff period that reduces the chance of simultaneous transmissions ✅
B.To define the maximum packet size allowed on the medium
C.To synchronize clocks of all stations
D.To replace the need for acknowledgment frames
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The contention window creates a random number of idle slots that each station must wait before transmitting. By randomizing the wait times, it spreads out transmission attempts, lowering the probability that two stations will start sending at the same instant, which helps avoid collisions on the shared medium.

Q3. If a station detects a busy channel during its backoff slot, what does it do?

A.Continue counting down the slots regardless of the busy state
B.Reset the contention window to its minimum size
C.Pause the timer and resume when the channel becomes idle again ✅
D.Immediately transmit a RTS frame
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: When the channel becomes busy while a station is counting down its backoff slots, the timer is halted. The station does not restart the backoff from zero; instead, it waits until the channel is sensed idle again and then resumes the countdown from where it left off. This behavior gives priority to stations that have waited longer.

Q4. How does assigning a shorter IFS to a station affect its priority in channel access?

A.It allows the station to begin its backoff earlier, increasing its chance to transmit first ✅
B.It reduces the transmission power required
C.It enlarges the contention window for that station
D.It forces the station to wait longer after the channel is idle
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A shorter IFS means the station finishes its waiting interval sooner after the medium becomes idle. Consequently, it can start its random backoff earlier than stations with longer IFS values, giving it a head‑start in the contention process and raising its likelihood of gaining access to the channel first.

Q5. In a binary exponential backoff scheme, after three consecutive busy detections, what is the maximum number of slots the contention window may contain?

A.2 slots
B.4 slots
C.6 slots
D.8 slots ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Binary exponential backoff starts with a window of 1 slot. After each busy detection the window size doubles: 1 → 2 after the first, 2 → 4 after the second, and 4 → 8 after the third. Thus, after three consecutive failures the maximum window size is 8 slots, allowing the station to choose a random backoff value between 0 and 7.

Q6. Which statement correctly compares the durations and purposes of SIFS and DIFS?

A.SIFS is longer than DIFS and is used for low‑priority traffic
B.SIFS is shorter than DIFS and is used for immediate response frames such as ACK and CTS ✅
C.Both have equal duration but serve different modulation schemes
D.DIFS is only used after a collision, while SIFS is used before any transmission
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: SIFS (Short Interframe Space) is the briefest interval, reserved for time‑critical control frames like ACK, CTS, and the data reply itself. DIFS (DCF Interframe Space) is longer, allowing stations to perform the random backoff after the medium has been idle. The difference in length reflects their distinct roles in the protocol.

Q7. What is the expected effect on collision probability if the contention window size is increased?

A.Collision probability remains unchanged because backoff is random
B.Collisions become more frequent because stations wait longer
C.Collision probability decreases as stations spread their transmission attempts over more slots ✅
D.Collisions are eliminated entirely
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Increasing the contention window expands the range of possible backoff slots. With more slots available, stations are less likely to choose the same slot simultaneously, which spreads transmission attempts over a broader time horizon and therefore reduces the overall chance of collisions.

Q8. How does the RTS/CTS exchange differ from a basic CSMA/CA transmission without RTS/CTS?

A.RTS/CTS reserves the channel for the data frame, reducing hidden‑node collisions ✅
B.RTS/CTS eliminates the need for the contention window
C.Basic CSMA/CA uses longer interframe spaces than RTS/CTS
D.RTS/CTS increases the data rate of the transmission
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The RTS/CTS handshake reserves the medium by having the receiver explicitly grant permission before the data frame is sent. This reservation mitigates hidden‑node problems because other stations that hear the CTS know to defer transmission. In contrast, a basic CSMA/CA transmission relies solely on carrier sensing and random backoff, which is more vulnerable to hidden‑node collisions.

Q9. When a station’s backoff timer is paused because the channel becomes busy, how does this behavior influence fairness among contending stations?

A.It gives advantage to stations that have already completed their backoff
B.It favors stations with longer remaining backoff times, allowing them to resume later ✅
C.It equally distributes access regardless of backoff duration
D.It causes stations with shorter backoff to lose the channel
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Pausing the timer preserves the amount of backoff a station has already waited. Stations that have already counted down many slots retain that advantage, while those with longer remaining backoff must wait longer after the channel frees. This mechanism prevents a station from losing its accrued waiting time, promoting fairness by respecting the elapsed backoff.

Q10. A station with the highest priority uses the shortest IFS. What sequence of timing intervals will it follow to transmit a data frame?

A.DIFS → RTS → SIFS → CTS → SIFS → Data → SIFS → ACK
B.SIFS → RTS → DIFS → CTS → Data → ACK
C.IFS → Backoff → RTS → Data → ACK
D.DIFS → RTS → SIFS → CTS → SIFS → Data → SIFS → ACK ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The high‑priority station first observes DIFS, then sends an RTS. After receiving CTS it waits SIFS before transmitting the data frame, and finally waits another SIFS before sending the ACK. This chain of short interframe spaces (SIFS) after each control exchange ensures rapid progression for the high‑priority traffic.

Q11. Explain how binary exponential backoff assists in resolving collisions in a densely populated network.

A.It forces all stations to retransmit after the same fixed delay
B.It reduces transmission power after each collision
C.It exponentially enlarges the contention window, spreading retransmission attempts over a larger time span ✅
D.It eliminates the need for acknowledgments
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: After a collision, each station doubles its contention window, giving a larger pool of possible backoff slots. This exponential growth quickly spreads the retransmission attempts of many stations across a wider temporal range, drastically lowering the odds that they will collide again, which is especially effective when many stations contend for the medium.

Q12. In a scenario where voice frames must be prioritized over data frames, how could IFS values be adjusted to achieve this?

A.Assign a shorter IFS to voice stations and a longer IFS to data stations ✅
B.Increase the contention window for voice frames
C.Use the same IFS for both but double the data frame size
D.Disable acknowledgments for voice traffic
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: By giving voice stations a shorter IFS, they complete the initial waiting period sooner and start their backoff earlier than data stations. This timing advantage lets voice traffic seize the channel first, ensuring lower latency for voice packets while still allowing data frames to access the medium when voice traffic is idle.

Q13. Three stations have IFS values of 10 µs, 20 µs, and 30 µs respectively, and all use the same initial contention window. Which station is most likely to win the contention and why?

A.The station with 30 µs IFS because it waits longer, ensuring the channel is idle
B.The station with 10 µs IFS because it begins its backoff earlier, giving it the first opportunity to transmit ✅
C.All stations have equal chance because the contention window randomizes access
D.The station with 20 µs IFS because it balances waiting time and backoff
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The station with the shortest IFS (10 µs) completes its interframe waiting first, so it can start its random backoff ahead of the others. This head‑start increases its probability of selecting a smaller slot count and thus of gaining access to the channel before the stations with longer IFS values.

Q14. If after waiting DIFS a station selects 0 slots in its contention window, what is the implication compared to selecting 3 slots?

A.Both selections result in the same transmission time because the slot count is ignored
B.Choosing 0 slots guarantees immediate transmission, while 3 slots introduce a delay
C.Selecting 0 slots reduces the chance of collision but may cause unfairness to stations with larger slot selections ✅
D.The station must still wait an additional SIFS period regardless of slot count
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Choosing 0 slots means the station will attempt to transmit as soon as the DIFS ends, giving it the earliest possible access. However, this can be unfair to stations that have selected larger slot counts because they must wait longer, potentially increasing the chance that the zero‑slot station captures the medium repeatedly.

Q15. Assess the trade‑off between collision avoidance and channel utilization when employing a large versus a small contention window in a high‑traffic environment.

A.A large window lowers collision probability but may leave the channel idle longer, reducing throughput; a small window increases collision risk but can improve utilization if collisions are infrequent ✅
B.Both large and small windows produce identical throughput in high‑traffic conditions
C.A small window eliminates collisions entirely, while a large window causes constant collisions
D.Channel utilization is unaffected by contention window size
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A larger contention window spreads retransmission attempts over many more slots, which curtails collisions but can cause the medium to remain idle while stations wait, lowering overall throughput. Conversely, a smaller window allows quicker access and higher utilization when collisions are rare, but the risk of repeated collisions rises dramatically as traffic density grows.

Q16. If the IFS for high‑priority traffic is reduced by 50 % while all other protocol parameters remain unchanged, what is the predicted impact on overall network throughput?

A.Throughput will decrease because high‑priority frames will dominate the channel, starving lower‑priority traffic
B.Throughput will remain unchanged as IFS does not affect data rates
C.Throughput will increase modestly due to faster access for high‑priority frames, assuming collisions do not rise sharply ✅
D.Throughput will double because the channel becomes twice as fast
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Reducing the IFS for high‑priority traffic shortens the waiting period before those frames can start their backoff, allowing them to capture the medium more quickly. If the network can handle the additional accesses without a significant rise in collisions, overall throughput improves modestly; however, the gain is limited and does not double the raw channel capacity.
