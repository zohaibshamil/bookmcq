What is Border Gateway Protocol Version 4 (BGP4) inter-domain routing?

Border Gateway Protocol Version 4 is the standard exterior gateway protocol used for routing between autonomous systems on the Internet, employing path-vector mechanics with extensive policy controls to exchange reachability information and enforce business relationships rather than optimizing solely for technical metrics.

Q1. What does the acronym BGP stand for?

A. Border Gateway Process
B. Border Gateway Protocol ✅
C. Basic Gateway Protocol
D. Broadband Gateway Protocol

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The acronym BGP expands to Border Gateway Protocol, which is the routing protocol used between autonomous systems on the Internet. It enables the exchange of reachability information and path attributes, allowing routers to make policy‑based decisions about how traffic should be forwarded across the global network.

Q2. Which TCP port does BGP use for establishing a session?

A. 179 ✅
B. 80
C. 443
D. 22

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

BGP sessions are established over TCP port 179. This dedicated port ensures reliable transport of BGP messages, as BGP relies on TCP’s connection‑oriented features for ordered delivery and error handling, which are essential for the exchange of routing information between peers.

Q3. In BGP, what is the purpose of the AS_PATH attribute?

A. It indicates the next hop’s IP address
B. It lists the autonomous systems a route has traversed ✅
C. It defines the metric for the route
D. It specifies the route’s origin type

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The AS_PATH attribute records the sequence of autonomous system numbers that a route advertisement has passed through. By examining this list, a router can detect loops, prefer shorter paths, and apply routing policies based on the autonomous systems involved.

Q4. What is the effect of the BGP attribute ‘LOCAL_PREF’ on route selection?

A. It prevents route advertisement to external peers
B. It influences the choice of outbound path within an AS ✅
C. It indicates the route’s origin as IGP
D. It determines the next‑hop IP address

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

LOCAL_PREF is a well‑known discretionary attribute that is propagated only within a single autonomous system. Higher LOCAL_PREF values are preferred, allowing network operators to influence which exit point is chosen for outbound traffic, thereby shaping intra‑AS routing decisions.

Q5. Why does BGP use TCP instead of UDP for its transport?

A. Because TCP provides faster transmission
B. Because TCP ensures reliable, ordered delivery of messages ✅
C. Because UDP cannot traverse firewalls
D. Because TCP uses less bandwidth

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

BGP relies on the reliability guarantees of TCP, such as retransmission of lost packets, in‑order delivery, and flow control. These features are critical for maintaining consistent routing tables across peers, as lost or out‑of‑order update messages could cause routing instability or loops.

Q6. If a BGP speaker receives a route with the AS_PATH attribute \( \{65001, 65002, 65003\} \), what can it infer?

A. The route originated from AS 65001
B. The route passed through three autonomous systems ✅
C. The route has a local preference of 65003
D. The route is invalid and must be discarded

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The AS_PATH lists each autonomous system the route advertisement has traversed, in order. In this case, the route has passed through AS 65001, then 65002, and finally 65003, indicating a three‑hop AS traversal before reaching the receiving speaker.

Q7. Which BGP attribute is used to prevent routing loops by checking for the presence of the local AS number?

A. NEXT_HOP
B. AS_PATH ✅
C. ORIGIN
D. COMMUNITY

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The AS_PATH attribute is examined by a BGP speaker to detect its own autonomous system number within the path. If the local AS appears in the AS_PATH, the route is rejected, thereby preventing routing loops that could otherwise arise from circular path advertisements.

Q8. A network administrator wants to prefer routes learned from a specific external BGP neighbor. Which attribute should they modify?

B. LOCAL_PREF
C. NEXT_HOP
D. ORIGIN

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

MED is a non‑transitive attribute that influences the inbound path selection of neighboring autonomous systems. By assigning a lower MED value to routes received from the preferred external neighbor, the administrator can make those routes more attractive to the remote AS, shaping inbound traffic.

Q9. What is the main difference between iBGP and eBGP sessions?

📖 Explanation:

iBGP peers belong to the same autonomous system and share routing information without altering the NEXT_HOP attribute, while eBGP peers are in different autonomous systems and convey the NEXT_HOP to indicate the exit point. This distinction impacts route propagation and policy enforcement across AS borders.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

BGP’s decision process first evaluates LOCAL_PREF; the route with the higher LOCAL_PREF is preferred regardless of AS_PATH length. Consequently, Route X with LOCAL_PREF = 200 is chosen even though its AS_PATH is longer, reflecting the policy‑driven nature of BGP.

A route reflector centralizes route distribution, allowing multiple iBGP peers to send updates to a single reflector instead of maintaining a full mesh of peerings. This design reduces configuration complexity and scalability issues while preserving the iBGP requirement that routes be reflected with the correct attributes.

The \(no‑export\) community instructs routers not to advertise the route to external peers, but it does not affect internal preference. When other attributes are identical, BGP selects the route without the restrictive community, making Peer 2’s advertisement the preferred internal path.

The ORIGIN attribute indicates the source of the route. An ORIGIN value of ‘IGP’ means the route was originated inside the router’s own autonomous system, typically via an internal IGP such as OSPF or IS‑IS, rather than being learned from an external BGP neighbor.

Within a confederation, internal AS numbers are concealed from external peers by replacing them with the confederation’s identifier in the AS_PATH. This maintains loop‑prevention while simplifying policy enforcement, as external routers see only the confederation as a single autonomous system.

📖 Explanation:

The Multi‑Exit Discriminator (MED) is a non‑transitive attribute used by neighboring autonomous systems to convey a preference for inbound traffic. A higher MED value signals a less preferred path, so the route with the lower MED is typically chosen for inbound traffic.

eBGP routes are exchanged between different autonomous systems and are advertised only to external peers. iBGP routes, by contrast, stay within the same AS and preserve the NEXT_HOP attribute, requiring a full‑mesh or route‑reflector design to ensure all internal routers receive the updates.

📖 Explanation:

BGP follows a deterministic hierarchy: first it compares LOCAL_PREF values (higher wins), then evaluates the shortest AS_PATH length, and subsequently considers MED among other tie‑breakers. This ordered evaluation ensures consistent and policy‑driven route selection across the network.

📖 Explanation:

When LOCAL_PREF values are identical, BGP proceeds to compare MED. The route with the lower MED (10) is preferred, making Route B the chosen path. If MED were also equal, BGP would move to subsequent tie‑breakers such as eBGP over iBGP or router ID.

Q23. Explain why BGP does not use a distance‑vector algorithm like RIP. Which characteristic of BGP necessitates a path‑vector approach?

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

BGP’s path‑vector mechanism carries the full AS_PATH attribute, enabling each router to detect loops across diverse autonomous systems. A pure distance‑vector algorithm lacks this visibility, risking routing loops when routes traverse multiple, independently administered domains.
