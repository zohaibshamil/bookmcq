📖 Introduction to Transport Layer

Introduction To Transport Layer Services

Transport Layer communication provides process-to-process data delivery between applications running on different hosts. Its services define how application data is transferred, while connectionless and connection-oriented protocols offer different approaches to communication. Simple transport protocols establish the foundations of reliable data transfer through acknowledgments, sequence numbers, and retransmission. Stop-and-Wait, Go-Back-N, and Selective Repeat demonstrate increasingly efficient flow-control mechanisms. Bidirectional piggybacking adds another approach to acknowledgment handling. Together, these mechanisms connect network-layer packet delivery with reliable application communication.


Network Packets And Reliable Transmission Basics

Students should understand IP addressing, network-layer packet delivery, data-link framing, and basic sender-receiver communication. Knowledge of transmission errors, acknowledgments, sequence numbers, timers, and flow control provides the necessary foundation. Familiarity with connection-oriented and connectionless communication also helps distinguish transport services and protocol behaviour.


Skills For Understanding Transport Protocol Operations

Students should be able to explain transport-layer services and distinguish connection-oriented from connectionless protocols. They should describe simple transport protocol operations, trace Stop-and-Wait ARQ, compare Go-Back-N with Selective Repeat, and explain sliding-window mechanisms. They should also interpret acknowledgment behaviour, calculate transmission efficiency in basic protocol scenarios, and explain how piggybacking combines data transmission with acknowledgment delivery.

  • Connectionless And Connection-Oriented Protocols - Compares independent message delivery with connection establishment and ongoing communication.
  • Simple Transport Protocols - Establishes basic sender-receiver operations using data transmission, acknowledgments, and protocol rules.

A Learning Sequence From Services To Windows

Start with transport-layer services and connection models to understand the communication requirements between applications. Continue with simple transport protocols to establish the role of acknowledgments and retransmissions. Study Stop-and-Wait before moving to sliding-window protocols. Compare Go-Back-N and Selective Repeat to understand how multiple outstanding frames improve transmission efficiency. Finish with piggybacking and its relationship to bidirectional acknowledgment handling in data-link communication.


How Acknowledgments And Windows Control Delivery

Transport services establish the communication requirements, while protocol design defines how senders and receivers exchange information. Stop-and-Wait permits one outstanding frame, making acknowledgment and timeout behaviour easy to trace. Go-Back-N expands transmission through a sliding window but retransmits subsequent unacknowledged frames after an error. Selective Repeat buffers correctly received frames and retransmits only missing data. Piggybacking combines acknowledgments with outgoing frames in bidirectional link communication.


A Lost Segment During Sliding Window Transmission

A sender transmits three 1,000-byte data units using a window size of three and sequence numbers 0, 1, and 2. Unit 1 is lost, while unit 2 reaches the receiver. Under Go-Back-N, the receiver discards the out-of-order unit and acknowledges the next expected sequence number, 1. The sender retransmits units 1 and 2. Under Selective Repeat, the receiver buffers unit 2 and requests only unit 1, reducing unnecessary retransmission.


Why Reliable Transport Prevents Data Loss

Without appropriate transport protocols, applications would lack standardized mechanisms for managing delivery, ordering, and communication failures. Uncontrolled transmission could overwhelm receivers, while missing acknowledgments could leave senders uncertain about successful delivery. Flow control and retransmission mechanisms support reliable communication and provide foundations for understanding TCP operation and end-to-end data transfer.


Separating Connection Types And Retransmission Methods

Students may say 'connectionless communication guarantees reliable delivery'; connectionless protocols such as UDP do not provide built-in retransmission and ordering guarantees. They may say 'Go-Back-N retransmits only the missing unit'; it retransmits the missing unit and subsequent unacknowledged units. They may also say 'piggybacking is a transport-layer ARQ algorithm'; it is an acknowledgment technique associated with bidirectional data-link communication.


Protocol Comparisons And Sliding Window Problems

Exam questions commonly distinguish connection-oriented from connectionless services and identify their communication characteristics. Sequence-based problems test Stop-and-Wait acknowledgments, timeout behaviour, and retransmission. Numerical and diagram-based questions compare Go-Back-N and Selective Repeat window operations, receiver buffering, and acknowledgment handling. Students should also identify piggybacking situations and explain why sliding-window protocols achieve better link utilization than single-frame transmission under suitable conditions.


How Transport Protocols Coordinate Data Exchange

Transport communication connects application requirements with the delivery services provided by network protocols. Simple protocols establish the foundations of acknowledgment and error recovery, while Stop-and-Wait introduces controlled transmission. Sliding-window mechanisms allow multiple data units to remain in transit, improving efficiency through different retransmission strategies. Piggybacking demonstrates how bidirectional communication can combine acknowledgments with outgoing data. These mechanisms collectively explain the principles behind dependable data exchange between communicating systems.


Specifications For TCP UDP And Protocol Design

  • RFC 9293 - Defines the Transmission Control Protocol, including connection establishment, reliable delivery, sequencing, and flow control.
  • RFC 768 - Specifies the User Datagram Protocol and its connectionless message-delivery service.
  • RFC 1122 - Describes Internet host communication requirements, including transport-layer protocol behaviour.
  • Data Communications and Networking Transport Layer Sections - Provides further study of simple protocols, Stop-and-Wait, sliding windows, and acknowledgment mechanisms.

Essential Terms For Transport Layer Communication

  • Transport Service - Communication functionality provided by the transport layer to application processes.
  • Connectionless Protocol - Transfers independent messages without establishing a persistent transport connection.
  • Connection-Oriented Protocol - Establishes a logical communication connection before exchanging application data.
  • ARQ - Automatic Repeat Request uses acknowledgments and retransmissions to recover from transmission failures.
  • Stop-and-Wait - A protocol that sends one data unit and waits for its acknowledgment before continuing.
  • Go-Back-N - A sliding-window protocol that retransmits a missing unit and subsequent unacknowledged units.
  • Selective Repeat - A sliding-window protocol that selectively retransmits missing units while buffering correctly received data.
  • Piggybacking - Combines acknowledgment information with outgoing data during bidirectional communication.
