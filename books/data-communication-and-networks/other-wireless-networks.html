📖 Other Wireless Networks

Other Wireless Networks Beyond Local Wi-Fi

WiMAX, cellular telephony, and satellite networks extend wireless connectivity beyond local Wi-Fi coverage. WiMAX provides broadband wireless access through IEEE 802.16, while cellular systems organize communication through geographically distributed cells and evolved from analog 1G to digital 2G and 3G, followed by 4G LTE. Satellite networks relay signals through GEO, MEO, and LEO spacecraft, each offering different coverage and propagation characteristics. These technologies support fixed broadband, mobile communication, navigation, and connectivity in remote areas.

Radio Access And Satellite Communication Fundamentals

Knowledge of radio waves, frequency bands, modulation, bandwidth, and wireless transmission provides the necessary foundation. Students should understand cellular access points, network coverage, and basic protocol layering. Familiarity with digital communication, IP networks, and wireless LAN architecture helps distinguish broadband wireless access, mobile cellular services, and satellite-based communication.

Skills For Comparing Wide Area Wireless Systems

Students should be able to describe WiMAX services, applications, IEEE 802.16 standards, and protocol-stack layers. They should distinguish cellular generations from 1G through 4G, compare GSM, CDMA, UMTS, CDMA2000, and LTE technologies, and explain basic cellular operation. They should also compare GEO, MEO, and LEO satellite systems, describe satellite communication principles, and identify GPS and Iridium applications.

A Study Path From Broadband To Orbit

Begin with WiMAX to understand fixed broadband wireless access and its IEEE 802.16 architecture. Continue with cellular network organization before tracing the transition from analog 1G to digital 2G, 3G, and 4G systems. Finish with satellite communication principles, then compare GEO, MEO, and LEO orbits. This sequence moves from terrestrial wireless access to mobile communication and worldwide satellite coverage.

How Access Networks And Satellite Systems Connect

WiMAX provides broadband connectivity through its physical and MAC-layer architecture. Cellular systems organize radio coverage into cells, allowing devices to connect through base stations and move between coverage areas through handoffs. Successive cellular generations improve communication capabilities and network services. Satellite architecture extends connectivity beyond terrestrial coverage, while orbital altitude determines coverage patterns, propagation delay, and satellite movement. GPS and Iridium demonstrate different applications of MEO and LEO systems.

A Field Report Across Cellular And Satellite Coverage

An emergency team records a 120 kB report and obtains its position from GPS satellites in MEO. GPS supplies navigation and timing signals, not the report's communication path. Within 4G LTE coverage, the handset sends the report through a cellular base station and core network to a control center. Beyond cellular coverage, a separate Iridium LEO terminal can transmit a short emergency message through a satellite and gateway. A fixed rural outpost could alternatively obtain broadband through an IEEE 802.16 WiMAX connection.

Why Wide Area Wireless Extends Connectivity

Terrestrial wireless infrastructure cannot economically provide complete coverage across every remote region, moving environment, or geographic obstacle. WiMAX and cellular technologies support broadband and mobile access, while satellite systems extend communication and navigation capabilities. Their different architectures help network designers select suitable technologies for coverage, mobility, capacity, and remote connectivity requirements.

Separating Cellular GPS And Satellite Communication Roles

Students may say 'GPS sends user reports through MEO satellites'; GPS broadcasts navigation and timing signals, while separate communication systems carry user data. They may say 'GEO satellites move rapidly across the sky'; their orbital period matches Earth's rotation, making them appear stationary relative to the surface. They may also say 'LTE and WiMAX are identical'; LTE belongs to the 3GPP cellular standards family, while WiMAX follows IEEE 802.16.

Comparisons Across Wireless Generations And Orbits

Exam questions commonly compare WiMAX architecture with cellular communication and identify IEEE 802.16 responsibilities. Generation-based questions distinguish analog 1G, GSM and CDMA in 2G, UMTS and CDMA2000 in 3G, and LTE in 4G. Satellite questions test GEO, MEO, and LEO characteristics, orbital applications, propagation delay, and coverage. Students should also trace basic cellular communication and satellite signal paths.

How Terrestrial And Satellite Links Complement Each Other

Wide-area wireless communication combines fixed broadband, mobile cellular access, and satellite connectivity to serve different communication requirements. WiMAX provides broadband access, cellular generations support evolving mobile services, and satellite systems extend navigation and communication beyond terrestrial infrastructure. Differences in protocol architecture, radio coverage, network organization, and orbital characteristics determine the appropriate technology for each application.

Technical Specifications For Broadband Cellular And GPS

  • IEEE 802.16 Standard - Defines broadband wireless access technologies, including WiMAX physical and MAC-layer specifications.
  • 3GPP TS 23.002 - Describes network architecture and functional organization across GSM, UMTS, and related mobile systems.
  • 3GPP TS 36.300 - Specifies the overall description of LTE E-UTRA and E-UTRAN architecture.
  • GPS Interface Specification IS-GPS-200 - Defines GPS navigation signal structures and associated space-segment interface requirements.

Essential Terms For Wide Area Wireless Networks

  • WiMAX - A broadband wireless access technology based on the IEEE 802.16 standards family.
  • Handoff - Transfers an active mobile connection from one cellular coverage area to another.
  • GSM - A widely adopted second-generation digital cellular communication standard.
  • CDMA2000 - A third-generation cellular technology family developed from earlier CDMA systems.
  • LTE - A 3GPP mobile broadband technology associated with fourth-generation cellular networks.
  • GEO - A geostationary Earth orbit in which satellites appear stationary relative to Earth's surface.
  • MEO - A medium Earth orbit used by navigation satellite constellations such as GPS.
  • LEO - A low Earth orbit used by communications constellations such as Iridium.
