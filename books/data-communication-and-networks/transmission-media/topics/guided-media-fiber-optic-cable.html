📝 All Guided media: Fiber Optic Cable MCQs

Q1. Which category of UTP cable is specified to support a data rate of at least 200 Mbps and requires all components to be from the same manufacturer?

🔹 A. Category 4
🔹 B. Category 5e
🔹 C. Category 6
🔹 D. Category 6A

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Category 6 is defined as a new category where all components must be matched and tested at a data rate of 200 Mbps, ensuring consistent performance across the cable system. This requirement distinguishes it from earlier categories that do not mandate manufacturer‑wide matching.

Q2. If a network designer needs to minimize crosstalk for a high‑density office environment, which cable category should they prioritize?

🔹 A. Category 3
🔹 B. Category 5e
🔹 C. Category 7
🔹 D. Category 2

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Category 7, also called SSTP, wraps each pair in a helical metallic foil and adds an overall shield, dramatically reducing crosstalk and electromagnetic interference, making it ideal for dense installations.

Q3. Given that attenuation sharply increases above 100 kHz, what effect would doubling the operating frequency from 150 kHz to 300 kHz likely have on signal loss over a fixed distance?

🔹 A. Loss would halve
🔹 B. Loss would stay the same
🔹 C. Loss would increase roughly proportionally
🔹 D. Loss would decrease proportionally

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Attenuation tends to rise with frequency; doubling the frequency typically leads to a noticeable increase in loss, often roughly proportional for the same medium, so signal degradation becomes more severe.

Q4. Compare Category 5e and Category 6 in terms of their typical maximum supported LAN speeds. Which statement is accurate?

🔹 A. Both support up to 100 Mbps only
🔹 B. Category 5e supports 1 Gbps, Category 6 supports 10 Gbps
🔹 C. Category 5e supports 100 Mbps, Category 6 supports 1 Gbps
🔹 D. Category 5e supports 1 Gbps, Category 6 supports up to 10 Gbps over limited distances

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Category 5e is rated for 100 Mbps and can reliably handle 1 Gbps, while Category 6 is engineered for up to 10 Gbps but only over shorter runs, reflecting their differing performance envelopes.

Q5. Why does shielding in STP cables reduce the effect of electromagnetic interference compared to UTP?

🔹 A. Shielding absorbs signals
🔹 B. Shielding reflects external fields
🔹 C. Shielding provides a Faraday cage that blocks external electromagnetic fields
🔹 D. Shielding increases cable thickness, which inherently reduces interference

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The metallic shield acts like a Faraday cage, containing and redirecting external electromagnetic fields away from the twisted pairs, thereby limiting induced noise and preserving signal integrity.

Q6. If a telephone company upgrades its local loop from Category 3 to Category 5e, what immediate benefit is most likely?

🔹 A. Higher voice clarity
🔹 B. Ability to provide DSL broadband services
🔹 C. Reduced cable cost
🔹 D. Elimination of all crosstalk

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Category 5e offers higher bandwidth than Category 3, enabling the higher frequencies required for DSL, thus allowing the provider to deliver broadband data services over the same telephone infrastructure.

Q7. Which of the following statements best differentiates the RJ45 connector from other Ethernet connectors?

🔹 A. It is keyed for single‑direction insertion
🔹 B. It uses a screw‑type locking mechanism
🔹 C. It supports fiber‑optic modules directly
🔹 D. It has eight pins instead of four

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The RJ45 connector is keyed, meaning it can be inserted only one way, which prevents misalignment and ensures proper pin contact, a distinctive feature compared with many other connector types.

Q8. How does increasing the gauge (i.e., using a thicker wire) of a twisted‑pair cable affect its attenuation characteristics?

🔹 A. Attenuation increases because of higher resistance
🔹 B. Attenuation decreases due to lower resistance
🔹 C. Attenuation is unaffected by gauge
🔹 D. Attenuation becomes frequency‑independent

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A thicker conductor (lower gauge number) has lower resistance, which reduces signal loss per unit length, thereby decreasing attenuation, especially at higher frequencies where resistive losses are more pronounced.

Q9. What does the abbreviation RJ stand for in RJ45?

🔹 A. Registered Jack
🔹 B. Remote Junction
🔹 C. Rapid Join
🔹 D. Radio Jitter

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RJ is short for Registered Jack, a standardized telecommunication interface that defines the wiring and physical layout of connectors like the RJ45.

Q10. If a network experiences frequent errors due to crosstalk, which corrective action is most likely to reduce those errors?

🔹 A. Replace the cable with a higher‑frequency rating
🔹 B. Add repeaters every 100 m
🔹 C. Upgrade to a shielded twisted‑pair (STP) cable
🔹 D. Increase the transmit power

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Switching to STP provides a metallic shield around each pair, which isolates them from one another and from external electromagnetic sources, thereby mitigating crosstalk‑related errors.

Q11. Evaluate the statement: “Category 5e can reliably support 10 Gbps over 100 m.” Which assessment is correct?

🔹 A. True, because Category 5e is designed for 10 Gbps
🔹 B. False, because 10 Gbps requires Category 6 or higher
🔹 C. True, but only in ideal lab conditions
🔹 D. False, because Category 5e cannot exceed 1 Gbps

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Category 5e is rated up to 1 Gbps; achieving 10 Gbps reliably demands the tighter specifications and reduced crosstalk of Category 6 or higher, especially over standard distances.

Q12. In a twisted‑pair cable, why does twisting the pairs reduce electromagnetic interference?

🔹 A. Twisting shortens the cable length
🔹 B. Twisting creates a balanced magnetic field that cancels external interference
🔹 C. Twisting increases the cable’s capacitance
🔹 D. Twisting changes the gauge of the wire

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The alternating orientation of the twists causes induced noise to be cancelled out over each twist, effectively balancing the magnetic fields and reducing the net interference received by the signal.

Q13. Which category is sometimes referred to as SSTP?

🔹 A. Category 4
🔹 B. Category 5e
🔹 C. Category 6
🔹 D. Category 7

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Category 7 is often called Shielded Screen Twisted‑Pair (SSTP) because each pair is individually wrapped in foil and then shielded, providing superior protection against crosstalk.

Q14. A LAN uses 10Base‑T Ethernet over Category 3 cable. If the network is upgraded to 100Base‑TX, what is the most likely outcome?

📖 Explanation:

100Base‑TX requires at least Category 5 cable to reliably carry 100 Mbps; Category 3 lacks the bandwidth and attenuation characteristics, causing the upgraded network to malfunction.

Q15. Which factor most directly influences the maximum data rate a UTP cable can support?

🔹 A. Color of the jacket
🔹 B. Number of twists per meter
🔹 C. Diameter of the outer sheath
🔹 D. Length of the cable

📖 Explanation:

The twist rate determines how well the cable can reject crosstalk and external noise; higher twist densities allow higher frequencies to be transmitted, thus raising the achievable data rate.

Q18. If attenuation at 150 kHz is 3 dB/km and at 300 kHz is 6 dB/km, what is the approximate attenuation increase per doubling of frequency?

🔹 A. 1 dB/km
🔹 B. 2 dB/km
🔹 C. 3 dB/km
🔹 D. 4 dB/km

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The attenuation doubled from 3 dB/km to 6 dB/km when frequency doubled, indicating an increase of roughly 3 dB/km per frequency doubling in this range.

Q19. Compare the impact of using a plastic cover versus a metal shield on a twisted‑pair cable’s susceptibility to electromagnetic interference. Which statement is correct?

🔹 A. Plastic cover provides better shielding than metal
🔹 B. Metal shield reduces interference more effectively than plastic cover
🔹 C. Both provide equal protection
🔹 D. Plastic cover eliminates crosstalk entirely

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Metal shielding creates a conductive barrier that reflects and absorbs external electromagnetic fields, whereas a plastic cover offers no conductive path and thus does little to mitigate interference.

Q20. How does the presence of a helical metallic foil around each pair in Category 7 improve signal integrity compared with Category 5e?

🔹 A. It increases the cable’s capacitance
🔹 B. It reduces the characteristic impedance
🔹 C. It adds an extra ground reference that isolates each pair
🔹 D. It shortens the cable length

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The foil acts as an individual shield for each pair, providing a dedicated ground reference that isolates the pair from neighboring pairs and external fields, thereby reducing crosstalk and preserving signal fidelity.

Q28. Why does the attenuation curve of a twisted‑pair cable typically rise sharply after a certain frequency threshold?

🔹 A. Because resistance drops at higher frequencies
🔹 B. Because skin effect and dielectric losses increase with frequency
🔹 C. Because the cable length shortens
🔹 D. Because the twist rate decreases

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

At higher frequencies, the skin effect forces current toward the conductor surface, and dielectric losses in the insulation rise, both of which increase attenuation dramatically beyond a threshold.

Q42. If a cable’s attenuation is 2 dB/km at 50 kHz and 8 dB/km at 200 kHz, what can be inferred about its suitability for a 500 m link operating at 150 kHz?
