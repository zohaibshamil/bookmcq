What is Unguided media: Radio Waves?

Radio waves are omnidirectional unguided signals with frequencies ranging from 3 kHz to 1 GHz that can penetrate walls and travel long distances, making them ideal for broadcasting, Wi-Fi, and general wireless communication.

Q1. What frequency range defines radio waves in the electromagnetic spectrum?

A. 30 kHz – 300 GHz ✅
B. 300 kHz – 3 THz
C. 3 MHz – 30 GHz
D. 300 MHz – 30 GHz

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Radio waves occupy the portion of the spectrum from about 30 kHz up to 300 GHz, bridging the gap between extremely low‑frequency signals and microwaves. This range is widely used for broadcasting, navigation, and wireless communication, making the first option the correct definition.

Q2. If the wavelength of a radio wave is doubled while propagation speed remains constant, how does its frequency change?

A. Frequency doubles
B. Frequency halves
C. Frequency remains unchanged ✅
D. Frequency quadruples

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Because the speed of a wave in a given medium is the product of its frequency and wavelength (\(c = f\lambda\)), doubling \(\lambda\) while keeping \(c\) constant forces \(f\) to be halved. Hence the second option correctly describes the inverse relationship.

Q3. Compare VHF (30‑300 MHz) and UHF (300 MHz‑3 GHz) regarding penetration through obstacles.

A. VHF penetrates better ✅
B. UHF penetrates better
C. Both equal
D. Neither penetrates

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

VHF signals have longer wavelengths, which diffract around objects more effectively than the shorter UHF wavelengths. Consequently VHF experiences less attenuation when passing through walls, foliage, or terrain, giving it superior obstacle penetration compared to UHF.

Q4. Which modulation is most suitable for long‑distance ionospheric propagation?

A. Frequency Modulation (FM)
B. Amplitude Modulation (AM) ✅
C. Phase Shift Keying (PSK)
D. Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

AM signals can be reflected by the ionosphere over very long distances because their bandwidth is relatively narrow and they tolerate the variable phase shifts introduced by ionospheric layers, making AM the preferred choice for HF skywave communication.

Q5. How does increasing wavelength (lower frequency) affect atmospheric attenuation?

A. Attenuation increases
B. Attenuation decreases ✅
C. No effect
D. Attenuation first decreases then increases

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Longer wavelengths suffer less absorption by atmospheric gases and water vapor, so as the frequency drops (wavelength grows) the overall attenuation through the atmosphere diminishes, allowing signals to travel farther with less loss.

Q6. When choosing between fiber‑optic cable and a 2.4 GHz radio link for a campus network, which statement is most accurate?

A. Fiber offers higher bandwidth but higher installation cost ✅
B. Radio offers lower latency and unlimited bandwidth
C. Fiber is less secure than radio
D. Radio link is immune to interference

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Fiber‑optic links provide extremely high data rates and immunity to electromagnetic interference, but laying the cable demands trenching, connectors, and skilled labor, raising capital expenses. Radio links are cheaper to deploy but have limited bandwidth and are subject to fading and interference.

Q7. Design a 10 km point‑to‑point link at 2.4 GHz using 0 dBm transmit power. Assuming 20 dBi antennas at both ends, what is the received power using the Friis equation? Use \( \lambda = \frac{c}{f} \) with \( c = 3\times10^8 \) m/s.

A. -60 dBm
B. -70 dBm
C. -80 dBm ✅
D. -90 dBm

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

With \( \lambda = 0.125\) m, the free‑space path loss is \(20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{4\pi R}{\lambda}\right)\approx120\) dB. Adding the 0 dBm transmit power and the two 20 dBi antenna gains gives \(0+20+20-120\approx-80\) dBm, matching option C.

Q8. Increasing transmitter power reduces bit‑error rate (BER) up to a point, after which BER stops improving. What is the primary cause?

A. Thermal noise dominance
B. Multipath fading
C. Interference from other users
D. Receiver saturation ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Beyond a certain power level the receiver front‑end becomes nonlinear, producing distortion and clipping that limit further BER improvement. This saturation effect dominates over the benefit of higher signal‑to‑noise ratio, causing the BER curve to flatten.

Q9. Which statement best contrasts line‑of‑sight (LOS) and non‑LOS radio propagation?

A. LOS requires diffraction, non‑LOS does not
B. LOS suffers from multipath, non‑LOS does not
C. LOS can be achieved at any frequency, non‑LOS only at low frequencies
D. LOS generally has lower path loss than non‑LOS ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

In LOS scenarios the signal travels directly, encountering minimal obstacles, so the path loss exponent is close to 2. Non‑LOS paths involve diffraction, reflection, and scattering, which increase loss dramatically, making LOS links typically more efficient.

Q10. Why does antenna size tend to decrease as frequency increases?

A. Higher frequency waves have longer wavelengths
B. Higher frequency waves have shorter wavelengths ✅
C. Material conductivity improves
D. Power requirements drop

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Antenna dimensions are generally proportional to a fraction of the wavelength. As frequency rises, the wavelength shortens (\(\lambda = c/f\)), allowing the same fractional design (e.g., quarter‑wave) to be realized with a physically smaller structure.

Q11. During a solar flare, HF (3‑30 MHz) communication often experiences sudden blackouts. What mechanism causes this?

A. Increased ionospheric absorption ✅
B. Reduced transmitter power
C. Antenna misalignment
D. Ground conductivity change

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Solar flares emit intense X‑ray and UV radiation that ionizes the D‑layer of the ionosphere, dramatically increasing electron density and thus absorption of HF signals. The heightened attenuation leads to rapid communication outages.

Q12. For a secure military link, which advantage does spread‑spectrum (e.g., DSSS) have over narrowband transmission?

A. Higher data rate
B. Lower power consumption
C. Resistance to jamming and interception ✅
D. Simpler receiver design

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Spread‑spectrum spreads the signal energy over a wide bandwidth, making it appear as low‑level noise to unintended receivers. This inherent redundancy and processing gain provide strong resistance to intentional jamming and makes eavesdropping difficult without the correct pseudo‑random code.

Q13. A receiver moving away from a stationary transmitter at 30 m/s receives a 100 MHz signal. What is the observed frequency shift? Use Doppler formula \( f' = f \left(1 - \frac{v}{c}\right) \).

A. ≈ 99.99 MHz ✅
B. ≈ 100.01 MHz
C. ≈ 99.90 MHz
D. ≈ 100.10 MHz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Substituting \(v = 30\) m/s and \(c = 3\times10^8\) m/s gives \(v/c = 1\times10^{-7}\). The received frequency is \(100\text{ MHz}\times(1-10^{-7}) = 99.99999\text{ MHz}\), which rounds to about 99.99 MHz, matching option A.

Q14. If a radio wave encounters a medium with a higher refractive index, what happens to its direction?

A. It bends away from the normal
B. It bends toward the normal ✅
C. It continues straight
D. It reflects completely

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

According to Snell’s law, when a wave passes from a lower to a higher refractive index medium, its speed decreases and the wave bends toward the normal line, making option B the correct description.

Q15. Considering rain attenuation, which frequency band suffers most at 60 GHz compared to 2.4 GHz?

A. Attenuation is roughly equal
B. 60 GHz experiences significantly higher attenuation ✅
C. 2.4 GHz experiences higher attenuation
D. Both negligible

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Rain droplets are comparable in size to 60 GHz wavelengths, causing strong scattering and absorption, whereas 2.4 GHz waves are much larger relative to droplets and thus experience minimal rain‑induced loss. Hence the higher frequency suffers far greater attenuation.

Q16. Explain how a parabolic dish antenna focuses radio energy and how this affects gain. Which relationship best describes gain \( G \) as a function of dish diameter \( D \) and wavelength \( \lambda \)?

A. \( G \propto \frac{D}{\lambda} \)
B. \( G \propto \left(\frac{D}{\lambda}\right)^2 \) ✅
C. \( G \propto \frac{\lambda}{D} \)
D. \( G \propto \left(\frac{\lambda}{D}\right)^2 \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A parabolic reflector concentrates incoming plane waves onto its focal point, and when used as a transmitter it radiates a narrow beam. The gain grows with the square of the aperture size relative to the wavelength, giving the proportionality \(G \propto (D/\lambda)^2\).

Q17. When two coherent radio signals combine in phase, what is the resulting power relative to each individual signal?

A. Same as one signal
B. Double the power
C. Four times the power ✅
D. Zero

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In‑phase addition doubles the electric field amplitude; since power is proportional to the square of the amplitude, the resulting power becomes four times that of a single signal, which corresponds to option C.

Q18. Which principle allows frequency reuse in cellular networks without causing interference?

A. Time Division Multiplexing
B. Spatial separation using directional antennas ✅
C. Polarization diversity
D. Error correction coding

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Cellular systems allocate the same frequency channels to cells that are sufficiently far apart, relying on directional antennas and path loss to keep signals from overlapping. This spatial reuse enables efficient spectrum utilization while keeping co‑channel interference low.
