What is Introduction to Transmission Media in Physical Layer?

Transmission media are the physical pathways that connect devices and carry data signals from a sender to a receiver, serving as the foundational hardware layer where information is transmitted either through guided cables or unguided wireless signals.

Q1. Why was telegraph communication limited to short distances?

A. Because the signals were digital
B. Because the metallic medium caused high resistance ✅
C. Because of lack of encryption
D. Because of satellite interference

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The telegraph relied on metallic conductors, which introduced significant resistance and signal loss over long spans. High resistance limited the distance a signal could travel without amplification, making short‑range connections the practical norm for early telegraph systems.

Q2. What inference can be drawn from Hertz's 1895 experiment with high‑frequency signals?

A. It proved that sound can travel faster than light
B. It demonstrated the feasibility of wireless transmission ✅
C. It showed that metallic wires were unnecessary for telegraph
D. It indicated that vacuum cannot carry signals

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Hertz's generation and detection of high‑frequency electromagnetic waves showed that information could be sent through space without a physical conductor. This breakthrough laid the groundwork for wireless telegraphy, indicating that radio‑frequency signals could propagate through the air and be received at a distance.

Q3. If early telephone lines were noisy, which cause most likely explains the noise?

A. Low voltage of voice signals
B. Imperfect insulation of the wires ✅
C. Use of fiber‑optic cables
D. Digital encoding errors

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Early copper and twisted‑pair wires often suffered from inadequate insulation, allowing external electromagnetic interference and crosstalk to enter the circuit. This lack of shielding introduced hiss and static, degrading voice quality and making the conversation less intelligible, which is why noise was a common complaint.

Q4. Given the rapid increase in data rates of fiber‑optic cables, what is the most plausible future trend for transmission media?

A. A return to copper cables due to cost
B. Hybrid systems combining fiber and wireless ✅
C. Exclusive use of free‑space optics in all environments
D. Elimination of physical media altogether

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Fiber‑optic technology continues to push bandwidth limits, yet certain deployment scenarios (e.g., last‑mile connections) benefit from wireless flexibility. Combining high‑capacity fiber backbones with wireless links for end‑users offers both performance and convenience, making hybrid architectures the most realistic evolution.

Q5. How did modulation and multiplexing improve the efficiency of free‑space transmission?

A. By increasing signal amplitude
B. By allowing multiple signals to share the same spectrum ✅
C. By reducing the need for antennas
D. By converting analog signals to digital

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Modulation encodes information onto carrier waves, while multiplexing assigns distinct frequency slots to different data streams. Together they enable many signals to travel simultaneously over the same physical path, maximizing the utilization of the available spectrum and reducing the need for separate transmission channels.

Q6. Which statement best compares twisted‑pair cable with coaxial cable?

A. Twisted‑pair has higher bandwidth but lower shielding than coaxial ✅
B. Coaxial is less expensive and more flexible than twisted‑pair
C. Both have identical impedance characteristics
D. Twisted‑pair cannot support any digital data

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Twisted‑pair cables are prized for flexibility and lower cost but provide less shielding and typically lower bandwidth than coaxial cables, which have a continuous conductive shield that reduces interference and supports higher frequency signals, making coaxial preferable for high‑speed applications.

Q7. Evaluate the primary advantage of fiber‑optic cable over metallic cables in long‑distance communication.

A. Higher attenuation
B. Lower bandwidth
C. Immunity to electromagnetic interference ✅
D. Greater susceptibility to corrosion

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Fiber‑optic fibers transmit light, which is not affected by external electromagnetic fields. This immunity eliminates noise caused by nearby equipment or lightning, preserving signal integrity over thousands of kilometers and allowing repeaters to be spaced farther apart than with metallic conductors that suffer from EMI.

Q8. Differentiate between air, vacuum, and water as free‑space media regarding signal attenuation.

A. Air attenuates the most, vacuum the least, water intermediate ✅
B. Vacuum attenuates the most, water the least
C. All three have identical attenuation properties
D. Water attenuates the least, air the most

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In an empty space, vacuum offers virtually no attenuation, allowing signals to travel unimpeded. Air contains molecules that cause scattering and absorption, leading to higher loss. Water, especially seawater, absorbs electromagnetic energy strongly, giving it the highest attenuation among the three.

Q9. Compare the evolution from telegraph to telephone to wireless in terms of required transmission media.

A. All three rely exclusively on fiber‑optic cables
B. Telegraph and telephone needed metallic wires; wireless uses free space ✅
C. Wireless still depends on metallic conductors
D. Telephone eliminated the need for any medium

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The telegraph and early telephone systems both depended on conductive metal lines to carry electrical signals. Wireless communication, pioneered by Hertz and Marconi, eliminated the need for physical conductors by using radio waves that propagate through the atmosphere, marking a shift from wired to untethered media.

Q10. Assess how poor wire quality affected signal integrity in early telephone systems.

A. It caused signal amplification
B. It introduced distortion and noise reducing intelligibility ✅
C. It increased the speed of transmission
D. It eliminated the need for modulation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Low‑grade copper and inadequate insulation introduced resistance, capacitance, and inductance variations that distorted the analog voice waveform. These imperfections generated hiss, static, and echo, which lowered the clarity of conversations and forced engineers to develop better materials and line‑conditioning techniques.

Q11. If a signal suffers high attenuation, which transmission medium should be chosen to minimize loss?

A. Long copper cable
B. Twisted‑pair cable
C. Fiber‑optic cable ✅
D. Standard coaxial cable

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Fiber‑optic cables guide light through glass or plastic cores, where attenuation per kilometer is far lower than for metallic conductors. Selecting fiber reduces signal loss, allowing longer uninterrupted spans and preserving the original signal strength without frequent amplification.

Q12. Explain how modulation enables multiple signals to occupy the same transmission medium.

A. By increasing the voltage of each signal
B. By shifting each signal to a distinct frequency band ✅
C. By converting signals to binary code
D. By reducing the wavelength of the carrier

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Modulation varies a carrier wave's amplitude, frequency, or phase according to the information being sent. By assigning each data stream a unique carrier frequency (frequency‑division multiplexing), several signals can coexist on the same physical path without interfering, effectively sharing the medium.

Q13. Synthesize a design that combines fiber‑optic and wireless technologies to achieve reliable long‑distance communication.

A. Use fiber for all segments and ignore wireless
B. Deploy wireless repeaters only, discarding fiber
C. Employ fiber links for backbone and wireless for last‑mile connectivity ✅
D. Rely solely on satellite links

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A practical hybrid solution uses high‑capacity fiber‑optic cables for the core network, ensuring low‑loss, high‑speed transport across long distances. Wireless links then connect end‑users in the last mile, offering flexibility where laying fiber is impractical, while the backbone maintains overall performance.

Q14. For underwater communication, which transmission medium is most appropriate?

A. Air
B. Vacuum
C. Fiber‑optic cable ✅
D. Standard twisted‑pair

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Water absorbs most radio frequencies, making wireless transmission ineffective underwater. Fiber‑optic cables, protected within watertight jackets, can transmit light signals with minimal loss, providing a reliable medium for submarine or deep‑sea communication where other media would fail.

Q15. Why does the physical layer have direct control over transmission media?

A. Because it defines electrical characteristics that determine how media carry signals ✅
B. Because it stores user data
C. Because it encrypts the data
D. Because it manages application protocols

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The physical layer specifies voltage levels, timing, and signaling methods, which directly dictate how a chosen medium (copper, fiber, or free space) conveys bits. By governing these electrical and optical properties, the layer ensures that the medium can reliably transport the raw bitstream.

Q16. What is the definition of a transmission medium?

A. A device that decodes data
B. Anything that can carry information from a source to a destination ✅
C. A type of computer memory
D. A protocol for routing packets

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A transmission medium is any physical substance or space—such as copper wire, fiber‑optic cable, or air—that enables the movement of information from a sender to a receiver, serving as the conduit for signals.

Q17. Who invented the telegraph?

A. Samuel Morse ✅
B. Thomas Edison
C. Nikola Tesla
D. James Watt

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Samuel Morse developed the first practical electric telegraph and the Morse code in the 1830s, creating a system that could send messages over long distances using electrical pulses transmitted through wired connections.

Q18. If a new modulation technique doubles the achievable data rate, what is the most likely effect on required bandwidth?

A. Bandwidth remains unchanged
B. Bandwidth must double ✅
C. Bandwidth can be reduced
D. Bandwidth becomes irrelevant

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

According to the Shannon‑Hartley theorem, data rate is proportional to bandwidth for a given signal‑to‑noise ratio. Doubling the data rate generally requires roughly twice the bandwidth, unless other factors such as coding gain or signal‑to‑noise improvements are introduced.
