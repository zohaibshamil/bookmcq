📝 Guided media: Twisted Pair Cable

What is Guided media: Twisted Pair Cable?

Twisted pair cable consists of two insulated copper wires twisted together to reduce electromagnetic interference and crosstalk, making it a cost-effective and widely used guided medium for telephone lines and local area networks.

📝 All Guided media: Twisted Pair Cable MCQs

Q1. If a UTP cable has 25 twists per meter and the twist density is increased to 30 twists per meter, how does this change affect susceptibility to external electromagnetic noise?

A. It increases noise susceptibility
B. It decreases noise susceptibility ✅
C. It has no effect on noise
D. It creates additional crosstalk

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Increasing the number of twists per unit length forces the two conductors to alternate positions relative to external fields, causing the induced noise voltages to cancel more effectively. Therefore the cable becomes less susceptible to external electromagnetic interference, reducing overall noise pickup.

Q2. Compare unshielded twisted‑pair (UTP) and shielded twisted‑pair (STP) regarding cost and noise immunity. Which statement is most accurate?

A. UTP is cheaper but provides better noise immunity
B. STP is cheaper but provides worse noise immunity
C. UTP is cheaper and provides lower noise immunity ✅
D. STP is cheaper and provides higher noise immunity

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

UTP lacks a metallic shield, making it less expensive to manufacture and purchase. However, without shielding it is more vulnerable to external electromagnetic interference, so its noise immunity is lower than that of STP, which includes a foil or braid that blocks unwanted signals.

Q3. How does differential signaling in a twisted‑pair cable help cancel out external noise?

A. The receiver averages the two signals
B. The receiver subtracts one signal from the other ✅
C. The receiver amplifies both signals equally
D. The receiver uses only the stronger signal

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Differential signaling sends the same information on both wires but with opposite polarity. External noise tends to affect both wires similarly (common‑mode). By subtracting one wire’s voltage from the other, the receiver removes the common‑mode component, leaving only the intended differential signal and thus cancelling the noise.

Q4. What does the abbreviation UTP stand for?

A. Universal Transmission Pair
B. Unshielded Twisted Pair ✅
C. Ultra Twisted Pair
D. Underground Transmission Protocol

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

UTP stands for Unshielded Twisted Pair, which is the most common type of twisted‑pair cable used in networking. It lacks any additional metallic shielding around the pairs, relying on the twisting of the conductors to reduce electromagnetic interference.

Q5. In a single twist of a pair, one conductor is closer to a noise source than the other. In the next twist the positions are reversed. What is the net effect on the noise seen by the receiver?

A. Noise is amplified
B. Noise is unchanged
C. Noise is largely cancelled ✅
D. Noise is transformed into crosstalk

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Because the closer conductor in the first twist picks up more noise and the farther conductor picks up less, while the next twist swaps these positions, the noise contributions tend to cancel when the receiver takes the difference between the two wires. This balanced arrangement reduces net noise.

Q6. Why does adding a metallic shield to twisted‑pair cable increase its bulk and cost?

A. The shield requires thicker insulation
B. The shield adds extra copper and a grounding layer ✅
C. The shield doubles the number of twists
D. The shield forces a larger diameter core

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A shield consists of a foil or braided mesh that surrounds each pair or the entire cable. This additional conductive material requires more copper, extra manufacturing steps, and a dedicated grounding termination, all of which increase material usage, labor, and consequently the overall bulk and price of the cable.

Q7. A network designer must choose between UTP and STP for a new office floor that contains many fluorescent lights and HVAC motors. Which recommendation best balances performance and cost?

A. Use only UTP because cost dominates
B. Use only STP because interference is severe
C. Use UTP for most runs and STP for high‑interference zones ✅
D. Use STP for all runs regardless of cost

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

UTP is sufficient in areas with modest electromagnetic activity, keeping costs low. However, zones near strong EMI sources such as motors and fluorescent ballasts benefit from the extra shielding of STP. Combining both types leverages the cost advantage of UTP while providing protection where it is most needed.

Q8. If a twisted‑pair cable exhibits noticeable crosstalk, which design parameter is most likely insufficient?

A. Twist rate per unit length ✅
B. Conductor gauge
C. Insulation material type
D. Cable jacket color

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Crosstalk arises when electromagnetic fields from one pair couple into an adjacent pair. A higher twist rate (more twists per length) reduces the exposure time of any segment to the interfering field, thereby diminishing coupling. Therefore, an insufficient twist density is the primary cause of observed crosstalk.

Q9. How does the bandwidth capability of twisted‑pair cable generally compare with that of coaxial cable of similar gauge?

A. Twisted‑pair offers higher bandwidth
B. Coaxial offers higher bandwidth ✅
C. Both provide identical bandwidth
D. Bandwidth depends only on length, not type

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Coaxial cable has a single central conductor surrounded by a shielding layer, providing a more uniform impedance and lower attenuation at high frequencies. Consequently, for comparable conductor sizes, coaxial cable typically supports greater bandwidth than twisted‑pair, which suffers more from skin effect and pair‑to‑pair coupling.

Q10. Explain how increasing the number of twists per foot influences signal attenuation in a twisted‑pair cable. Which effect dominates?

A. More twists increase resistance and raise attenuation
B. More twists reduce capacitance and lower attenuation ✅
C. More twists create additional reflections, raising attenuation
D. More twists have no impact on attenuation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Increasing twist density shortens the effective length of each conductor segment exposed to the surrounding dielectric, thereby reducing the cable’s capacitance per unit length. Lower capacitance diminishes the high‑frequency loss component, resulting in reduced attenuation. While resistance may increase slightly, the capacitance effect generally dominates, improving signal integrity.

Q11. What does the abbreviation STP stand for?

A. Standard Twisted Pair
B. Shielded Twisted Pair ✅
C. Serial Transmission Protocol
D. Super Twisted Pair

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

STP stands for Shielded Twisted Pair. It is a variant of twisted‑pair cable that includes a metal foil or braided mesh covering each pair (or the whole bundle), providing additional protection against external electromagnetic interference and crosstalk.

Q12. If a pair of wires in a twisted‑pair cable is not twisted at all, what is the most likely outcome when the cable is placed near an AC power line?

A. Noise will be completely cancelled
B. Noise will be amplified on both wires equally
C. Noise will appear as a common‑mode signal that cannot be removed by differential reception ✅
D. Noise will cause the cable to overheat

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When the wires are not twisted, they occupy fixed positions relative to the external field, so both conductors pick up nearly identical noise voltages (common‑mode). The receiver, which relies on the difference between the two wires, cannot subtract this common component, so the noise remains present in the received signal.

Q13. Compare the characteristic impedance of typical twisted‑pair cable with that of coaxial cable. Which statement is correct?

A. Twisted‑pair typically has 100 Ω while coaxial is around 50 Ω ✅
B. Both have the same impedance of 75 Ω
C. Coaxial typically has 100 Ω while twisted‑pair is 50 Ω
D. Impedance is unrelated to cable type

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Standard Ethernet twisted‑pair (e.g., Cat5e, Cat6) is designed for a characteristic impedance of about 100 Ω. In contrast, many coaxial cables used for video or broadband are specified at 50 Ω or 75 Ω, reflecting their different construction and signal‑transfer requirements.

Q14. Why does twisting the conductors in a pair reduce crosstalk between adjacent pairs?

A. It increases the distance between pairs
B. It constantly changes the relative position of each conductor with respect to external fields ✅
C. It adds magnetic shielding around each wire
D. It eliminates the need for insulation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The twist causes each conductor to alternately occupy positions closer to and farther from neighboring pairs. This continual swapping means that any induced voltage from an adjacent pair is equally applied to both conductors over each twist cycle, allowing the differential receiver to cancel the induced component and thus reducing crosstalk.

Q15. Which category of guided media does twisted‑pair cable belong to?

A. Radio wave
B. Microwave
C. Fiber‑optic
D. Copper conductor ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Twisted‑pair cable consists of copper conductors that carry electrical signals, placing it in the category of guided (wired) media that use metallic conductors. It is distinct from wireless categories such as radio or microwave and from optical media that transmit light.

Q16. A network uses 1000 ft of Cat5e UTP cable rated for 100 MHz. Approximate attenuation can be estimated by the formula \(A = 0.5 \times \sqrt{L}\) dB, where \(L\) is length in feet. What is the estimated attenuation?

A. 15 dB
B. 22 dB
C. 31 dB ✅
D. 45 dB

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

First compute \(\sqrt{1000}\) ≈ 31.62. Multiply by 0.5 gives about 15.81 dB. However, the formula provided is \(A = 0.5 \times \sqrt{L}\); applying it yields roughly 15.8 dB, which matches answer choice B. Since none of the options exactly match, the closest value is 15 dB, making option A the best selection.

Q17. Assess why fiber‑optic cable generally outperforms twisted‑pair cable for long‑distance transmissions. Which factor is most decisive?

A. Higher electrical resistance of copper
B. Greater susceptibility to electromagnetic interference
C. Higher bandwidth and lower attenuation ✅
D. Lower installation cost

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Fiber‑optic cables transmit light, which experiences virtually no electrical resistance and is immune to electromagnetic interference. Moreover, they support much higher bandwidths with attenuation that grows far more slowly with distance than copper‑based twisted‑pair. These attributes make fiber the preferred medium for long‑haul links.

Q18. In a high‑EMI environment, twisted‑pair cable may be unsuitable. Which alternative medium should be selected and why?

A. Coaxial cable, because its shielding is cheaper
B. Fiber‑optic cable, because it is immune to EMI ✅
C. UTP cable, because it is lighter
D. Wireless link, because it avoids cables

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Fiber‑optic cable carries signals as light, rendering it completely immune to electromagnetic interference that degrades twisted‑pair performance. Its high bandwidth and low attenuation also make it suitable for demanding environments, whereas coaxial still relies on conductive paths and wireless introduces other reliability concerns.

Q19. If the shield in an STP cable is improperly grounded, what is the most likely consequence?

A. The shield will enhance noise rejection
B. The shield may act as an antenna and introduce additional noise ✅
C. The cable will become completely immune to crosstalk
D. The cable will short out and stop transmitting

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A correctly grounded shield provides a low‑impedance path for unwanted currents, diverting them away from the signal conductors. When grounding is faulty, the shield can pick up external fields and re‑radiate them, effectively turning the shield into an antenna that adds noise rather than removing it.

Q20. Compare the cost‑benefit of upgrading an office from UTP to STP in a building with high electromagnetic interference. Which statement best reflects the trade‑off?

A. STP costs twice as much but reduces errors by less than 1 %
B. STP adds moderate cost and can reduce error rates significantly in noisy zones ✅
C. STP is cheaper and provides the same performance as UTP
D. STP eliminates the need for any network maintenance

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While STP is more expensive due to shielding material and termination requirements, the added protection can markedly lower error rates caused by EMI in high‑interference areas. The investment is justified when the performance gain outweighs the incremental cost, especially for mission‑critical applications.

Q21. Explain the difference between differential‑mode and common‑mode noise in a twisted‑pair cable and how the cable design mitigates each. Which description is accurate?

A. Differential‑mode noise is cancelled by twisting; common‑mode is ignored
B. Common‑mode noise appears equally on both conductors and is cancelled by the differential receiver; differential‑mode arises from imbalance and is reduced by maintaining equal twists ✅
C. Both noises are eliminated by shielding alone
D. Differential‑mode noise only affects shielded pairs

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Common‑mode noise induces identical voltages on both wires, so when the receiver subtracts one from the other, the common component disappears. Differential‑mode noise results from unequal coupling to each wire; the regular twisting ensures that any imbalance is averaged out over successive twists, minimizing its effect.

Q22. What is the typical copper gauge used in Cat6 twisted‑pair cable?

A. 24 AWG
B. 23 AWG ✅
C. 22 AWG
D. 20 AWG

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Cat6 cable commonly employs 23 AWG (American Wire Gauge) copper conductors. This gauge balances lower resistance for higher frequencies with flexibility and cost considerations, making it suitable for the 250 MHz performance specifications of the standard.

Q23. If adjacent pairs in a cable have different twist rates, how does this affect crosstalk between them?

A. Crosstalk increases because the mismatch creates resonant coupling
B. Crosstalk decreases because differing rates disrupt coupling patterns ✅
C. Crosstalk is unaffected by twist rate differences
D. Crosstalk is eliminated entirely

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When adjacent pairs twist at different frequencies, the relative positions of the conductors change at non‑matching intervals, breaking up the consistent coupling that would otherwise allow energy to transfer. This disruption reduces the steady‑state crosstalk between the pairs.

Q24. Differentiate between balanced and unbalanced transmission lines. Which characteristic applies to a twisted‑pair cable?

A. Balanced lines have a single conductor and ground; twisted‑pair is unbalanced
B. Balanced lines use two conductors with equal impedance to ground; twisted‑pair is balanced ✅
C. Unbalanced lines use differential signaling; twisted‑pair is unbalanced
D. Balanced lines require shielding; twisted‑pair does not

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A balanced transmission line consists of two conductors that have equal impedance to ground and to each other, allowing common‑mode noise to be cancelled by differential reception. Twisted‑pair cables meet this definition, as each pair carries opposite signals and the twisting maintains equal exposure to external fields.
