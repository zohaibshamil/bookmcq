📝 Unguided media: Microwaves (17 MCQs)

High-Frequency Radio Links for Directional Communication

Microwaves are high-frequency electromagnetic waves used in wireless communication, particularly for directional point-to-point links and satellite systems. Their shorter wavelengths allow practical use of focused antennas such as parabolic dishes. Many terrestrial microwave links rely on a clear line of sight between antennas, making tower placement, alignment, distance, and obstacles central to link planning. through the atmosphere or open space.

Directional Antennas and Line-of-Sight Paths

A dish antenna concentrates transmitted energy into a narrow beam and provides gain in a selected direction. The receiving dish must be aligned with the incoming beam. Buildings, hills, and other obstructions can block the direct path; atmospheric conditions, especially rain at some higher microwave frequencies, can also affect signal strength.

Establishing a Terrestrial Microwave Link

Digital data is modulated onto a microwave carrier, amplified, and radiated through a directional antenna. At the far end, a matching antenna collects the signal and a receiver filters, amplifies, and demodulates it. Repeaters may be installed when a long route requires intermediate reception and retransmission because the path cannot span the full distance.

A Point-to-Point Link Between Two Buildings

Two office buildings separated by several kilometres can use aligned microwave dishes when a clear line-of-sight path is available. The first radio converts network data into a modulated microwave signal; the second recovers the data and passes it to the local network. Trees or new construction entering the path may degrade or interrupt the link.

Microwave Backhaul and Satellite Communication

Microwave links carry telecommunications backhaul, connect remote sites, and provide satellite uplinks and downlinks. Their directional beams can reduce interference with other directions and support concentrated energy, but alignment and unobstructed paths are critical. Satellite microwave links introduce long propagation distances, so their delay can be significantly greater than a short terrestrial link.

Mistakes About Microwave Links

'Microwave links work through any obstacle' is incorrect because many terrestrial links require line of sight. 'A dish receives equally from all directions' is false because it is highly directional. 'Rain never affects microwaves' is incorrect because rain attenuation can be significant in some frequency bands and link conditions. sometimes.

Line-of-Sight and Satellite Link Comparisons

Know why microwave dishes are aligned and how line of sight differs from general radio coverage. Identify transmit and receive antennas, repeaters, uplinks, and downlinks. Compare terrestrial and satellite paths by coverage, obstruction, propagation delay, and weather effects, and explain why a blocked path or poor alignment can reduce received signal quality.

Microwave Link and Antenna Terms

  • Microwave - High-frequency radio wave.
  • Line of sight - Unobstructed path between antennas.
  • Dish antenna - Reflector focusing a narrow beam.
  • Uplink - Earth station sends to satellite.
  • Downlink - Transmission from satellite to Earth.
  • Rain attenuation - Rain-caused signal loss.

📝 All Unguided media: Microwaves MCQs

Q1. What frequency range is commonly used for microwave communication in data networks?

A.1–10 GHz
B.30–300 GHz ✅
C.300 MHz–3 GHz
D.3–30 GHz
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The microwave band allocated for most data‑communication links lies between 30 GHz and 300 GHz. This range offers high bandwidth while still being manageable with existing antenna and amplifier technology, making it the standard choice for point‑to‑point and wireless backhaul applications.

Q2. At which OSI layer are transmission media directly controlled?

A.Physical ✅
B.Data Link
C.Network
D.Transport
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The physical layer (Layer 1) is responsible for the actual transmission of raw bits over a medium. It defines voltage levels, timing, and the characteristics of the transmission media themselves, so media control is a direct function of this layer.

Q3. If a microwave link operates at 12 GHz, what is the approximate wavelength in free space?

A.\(\approx 2.5 \text{cm}\) ✅
B.\(\approx 25 \text{cm}\)
C.\(\approx 0.25 \text{cm}\)
D.\(\approx 5 \text{cm}\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Using \(\lambda = c/f\) with \(c\approx3\times10^{8}\text{ m/s}\) and \(f=12\times10^{9}\text{ Hz}\) gives \(\lambda\approx0.025\text{ m}=2.5\text{ cm}\). This short wavelength is characteristic of microwave frequencies and determines antenna size and beamwidth.

Q4. Why are line‑of‑sight conditions essential for microwave transmission?

A.Microwaves can diffract around obstacles
B.They are highly absorbed by atmospheric gases
C.They travel in straight paths with limited diffraction ✅
D.They require a vacuum
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Microwave signals have wavelengths that are too short to bend significantly around objects, so they propagate essentially in straight lines. Any obstruction blocks the path, making a clear line of sight between antennas a prerequisite for reliable communication.

Q5. Compare microwave and optical fiber links in terms of bandwidth. Which statement is true?

A.Microwave offers higher bandwidth than fiber
B.Fiber offers higher bandwidth ✅
C.Both have equal bandwidth
D.Bandwidth depends only on modulation
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Optical fibers support terabits per second because light can carry many wavelengths simultaneously, whereas microwave radio links are limited to a few gigahertz of spectrum. Consequently, fiber provides substantially greater bandwidth for high‑capacity backbones.

Q6. A microwave link suffers a 3 dB increase in path loss due to rain fade. How does this affect the received power?

A.Power halves ✅
B.Power doubles
C.No change
D.Power reduces by 3 dB
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A 3 dB loss corresponds to a factor of two reduction in power. Since path loss directly subtracts from the link budget, the received power will be half of its previous value, which can degrade signal quality if the margin is small.

Q7. Which factor most significantly limits the maximum distance of a terrestrial microwave link?

A.Antenna size
B.Transmit power
C.Atmospheric attenuation ✅
D.Cable losses
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Atmospheric attenuation, especially due to rain, fog, and gases, increases with distance and frequency. Even with large antennas and high power, excessive attenuation eventually reduces the received signal below usable levels, making it the primary distance‑limiting factor.

Q8. If the antenna gain of a microwave system is increased from 20 dBi to 30 dBi, how does the link budget change?

A.Increases by 10 dB ✅
B.Decreases by 10 dB
C.No change
D.Increases by 20 dB
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A gain increase of 10 dB adds directly to the link budget, raising the received power by the same amount. This improvement can compensate for additional losses or extend the feasible link distance without altering other system parameters.

Q9. Which of the following best differentiates microwave terrestrial links from satellite microwave links?

A.Terrestrial links require line‑of‑sight, satellite links do not ✅
B.Satellite links use lower frequencies
C.Terrestrial links have higher latency
D.Satellite links cannot use polarization
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Terrestrial microwave links must have an unobstructed line of sight between ground stations, while satellite links communicate with a spacecraft above the atmosphere, eliminating the line‑of‑sight constraint on the ground side.

Q10. A microwave system uses a modulation scheme that requires a minimum signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) of 12 dB for a bit error rate of \(10^{-6}\). If the measured SNR is 9 dB, what is the likely outcome?

A.Error rate improves
B.Error rate worsens beyond spec ✅
C.No effect
D.System will shut down
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An SNR below the required 12 dB means the link cannot meet the target BER of \(10^{-6}\). The error rate will increase, potentially causing data corruption or requiring retransmissions, which is contrary to the design specification.

Q11. When a microwave link operates at 60 GHz, atmospheric oxygen absorption peaks. What is the primary consequence for link design?

A.Increased range
B.Reduced range
C.No impact
D.Higher power needed to maintain range ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Oxygen absorption at 60 GHz significantly attenuates the signal, shortening the viable link distance. Designers must compensate by either raising transmit power, using higher‑gain antennas, or shortening the hop length to achieve the desired performance.

Q12. Considering cost, bandwidth, and deployment time, which scenario favors the use of microwave links over fiber optics?

A.Urban core with existing ducts
B.Remote area with difficult terrain ✅
C.High‑density data center
D.Submarine connection
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In remote or rugged locations, laying fiber can be prohibitively expensive and time‑consuming. Microwave links can be installed quickly with minimal civil work, providing ample bandwidth at a lower overall cost compared to trenching fiber.

Q13. How does the use of frequency reuse in a cellular microwave network improve spectral efficiency?

A.It reduces interference
B.Allows same frequency in non‑adjacent cells ✅
C.Increases antenna size
D.Decreases transmit power
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Frequency reuse permits identical frequency channels to be assigned to cells that are sufficiently separated, effectively multiplying the amount of usable spectrum within the same geographic area and boosting overall capacity.

Q14. A microwave link is planned between two towers 40 km apart at 15 GHz. Given a free‑space path loss of \(L = 20\log_{10}(4\pi d f /c)\), calculate the path loss and determine if a 30 dBi transmit antenna and 35 dBi receive antenna with 20 W transmit power can achieve a received power above –80 dBm. Assume system losses of 2 dB.

A.Path loss ≈ 112 dB, received ≈ –70 dBm (OK)
B.Path loss ≈ 120 dB, received ≈ –78 dBm (OK) ✅
C.Path loss ≈ 112 dB, received ≈ –84 dBm (Not OK)
D.Path loss ≈ 120 dB, received ≈ –90 dBm (Not OK)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Using the formula yields a loss of about 120 dB. Transmit power of 20 W equals 43 dBm; adding antenna gains (30 + 35 = 65 dB) and subtracting 2 dB losses gives a received level of roughly –14 dBm, well above the –80 dBm threshold, confirming the link meets the requirement.

Q15. In designing a point‑to‑point microwave link, an engineer must choose between increasing antenna diameter or increasing transmit power to meet a required link margin. Which decision generally yields a more efficient improvement in link budget, and why?

A.Increasing power is more efficient because it directly raises received power
B.Increasing antenna size improves gain quadratically with diameter ✅
C.Both have equal effect
D.Decreasing frequency is better
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Antenna gain grows with the square of the aperture diameter, so a modest increase in size can provide many more decibels of gain than a comparable increase in transmit power, which adds linearly. Therefore, enlarging the antenna is usually the more efficient way to improve the budget.

Q16. Explain how adaptive modulation can be employed in a microwave link to maintain service quality during varying weather conditions.

A.By fixing modulation regardless of conditions
B.By switching to lower‑order modulation when SNR drops ✅
C.By increasing carrier frequency during rain
D.By using error‑correcting codes only
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Adaptive modulation monitors the instantaneous SNR; when adverse weather reduces signal quality, the system automatically selects a more robust, lower‑order modulation (e.g., QPSK instead of 64‑QAM). This trade‑off preserves link reliability at the expense of reduced data rate, ensuring continuous service.

Q17. A microwave system uses dual‑polarization to double capacity. If each polarization experiences independent fading with probability 0.1 of outage, what is the probability that the overall link is in outage?

A.0.01 ✅
B.0.1
C.0.19
D.0.2
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Since the two polarizations fade independently, the link fails only when both are simultaneously in outage. The joint probability is \(0.1 \times 0.1 = 0.01\), or 1 %, which is the overall outage probability for the dual‑polarized system.
