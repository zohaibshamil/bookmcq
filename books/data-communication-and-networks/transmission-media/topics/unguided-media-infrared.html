📝 All Unguided media: Infrared MCQs

Q1. What is the typical wavelength range used for infrared data communication?

🔹 A. 700 nm – 900 nm
🔹 B. 900 nm – 1.2 µm
🔹 C. 850 nm – 950 nm
🔹 D. 1.2 µm – 2.5 µm

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The 850–950 nm band is the standard infrared window for short‑range data links and remote‑control applications, making it the most commonly cited range.

Q2. If an infrared transmitter and receiver are placed in a room with a strong source of visible light, which effect most directly reduces the signal‑to‑noise ratio?

🔹 A. Increased ambient temperature
🔹 B. Photodiode saturation due to visible light
🔹 C. Interference from radio frequencies
🔹 D. Physical obstruction by furniture

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Visible light creates a background photocurrent in the detector, raising the noise floor and thus lowering the signal‑to‑noise ratio.

Q3. An IR link uses pulse‑width modulation (PWM) to encode data. If the duty cycle is reduced while keeping the carrier frequency constant, what is the most likely impact on the average received power?

🔹 A. It increases linearly with duty cycle
🔹 B. It remains unchanged
🔹 C. It decreases proportionally to the duty cycle
🔹 D. It oscillates unpredictably

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Average power equals carrier power multiplied by the duty cycle; lowering the duty cycle reduces the average received power in direct proportion.

Q4. Considering eye safety standards, why are infrared wavelengths between 800 nm and 1400 nm classified as Class 1 (safe) for typical consumer devices?

🔹 A. They are absorbed by the cornea, preventing retinal damage
🔹 B. They are reflected by the iris, reducing exposure
🔹 C. Their photon energy is too low to cause ionization
🔹 D. The blink reflex is faster at these wavelengths

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

These wavelengths are strongly absorbed by the cornea, so the retina is protected, allowing them to be regarded as safe for everyday use.

Q5. Which characteristic best distinguishes infrared communication from visible‑light communication?

🔹 A. IR signals can travel through walls
🔹 B. IR uses longer wavelengths that are invisible to the human eye
🔹 C. IR requires higher transmission power
🔹 D. IR is less susceptible to atmospheric absorption

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Infrared operates at longer wavelengths (≈800 nm–1 µm) that are not visible, which is the primary distinction from visible‑light systems.

Q6. Between on‑off keying (OOK) and frequency‑shift keying (FSK) for IR links, which statement accurately evaluates bandwidth efficiency?

🔹 A. FSK occupies a larger bandwidth than OOK for the same data rate
🔹 B. OOK provides higher bandwidth efficiency because it uses only one frequency
🔹 C. Both schemes have identical bandwidth efficiency under ideal conditions
🔹 D. FSK provides higher bandwidth efficiency because it encodes bits in frequency shifts

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

FSK conveys information by shifting between frequencies, allowing more bits per hertz of spectrum than simple on‑off keying.

Q7. When choosing between an IR LED and an IR laser diode for a 10 Gbps link over 5 m, which factor is most decisive?

🔹 A. Laser diodes offer higher optical power density and narrower beam, supporting higher data rates
🔹 B. LEDs have narrower beam divergence, allowing higher data rates
🔹 C. LEDs consume less power, making them preferable despite lower data rates
🔹 D. Laser diodes are cheaper and more robust for short distances

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Laser diodes provide much higher power density and a very narrow beam, both essential for achieving multi‑gigabit rates over a few metres.

Q8. Why must an infrared communication system maintain line‑of‑sight between transmitter and receiver?

🔹 A. IR waves are reflected by all surfaces, causing multipath interference
🔹 B. IR photons are absorbed by atmospheric gases, limiting range
🔹 C. IR propagation is highly directional with minimal diffraction
🔹 D. IR signals can only travel through optical fibers

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Infrared radiation is highly directional and does not diffract significantly, so a clear line‑of‑sight is required for reliable reception.

Q9. If an IR carrier frequency is \(38\text{ kHz}\) and the modulation bandwidth is \(5\text{ kHz}\), what is the approximate spectral efficiency in bits per second per hertz assuming binary modulation?

🔹 A. 0.2 bits/s/Hz
🔹 B. 0.5 bits/s/Hz
🔹 C. 1 bit/s/Hz
🔹 D. 2 bits/s/Hz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

With binary modulation the achievable data rate ≈ bandwidth (5 kHz), giving a spectral efficiency of 5 kHz / 5 kHz = 1 bit/s/Hz.

Q10. Design a basic IR communication system that must operate in a bright indoor environment, support 2 Mbps over 3 m, and meet eye‑safety Class 1. Which combination of components best satisfies these requirements?

🔹 A. A 940 nm LED, OOK modulation, and a simple photodiode
🔹 B. A 850 nm laser diode, FSK modulation, and a PIN photodiode with optical filter
🔹 C. A 1300 nm LED, PWM modulation, and an avalanche photodiode
🔹 D. A 950 nm LED array, DSSS modulation, and a broadband photodiode

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A 850 nm laser diode with FSK and a filtered PIN photodiode provides the needed bandwidth, rejects ambient light, and stays within safe power limits.

Q11. An IR link experiences intermittent drops when a moving person crosses the beam. Which inference logically explains this phenomenon?

🔹 A. The person’s body reflects IR, enhancing the signal
🔹 B. The person absorbs IR energy, temporarily attenuating the link
🔹 C. The motion induces electromagnetic interference in the IR frequency
🔹 D. Temperature changes caused by the person alter the carrier frequency

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Human tissue strongly absorbs infrared radiation, so when a person blocks the line‑of‑sight the received power drops, causing the observed interruptions.

Q12. Compare the impact of using a narrowband optical filter at the receiver versus increasing the transmitter’s output power to improve SNR in noisy ambient light conditions.

🔹 A. Both methods equally improve SNR without trade‑offs
🔹 B. A filter reduces background noise without increasing power consumption, while higher power may cause safety issues
🔹 C. Increasing power always yields better SNR than filtering
🔹 D. Filtering degrades the signal more than increasing power improves it

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A narrowband filter suppresses ambient light, improving SNR without raising emitted power, whereas raising power can breach eye‑safety limits and waste energy.

Q13. How does the Beer‑Lambert law apply to infrared attenuation in fog, and what implication does it have for link budgeting?

🔹 A. Attenuation is independent of distance, so link budget need not consider fog
🔹 B. Attenuation increases linearly with distance, requiring exponential power increase
🔹 C. Attenuation follows an exponential decay with distance, necessitating higher transmitter power or shorter range
🔹 D. Beer‑Lambert law does not apply to IR wavelengths

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Beer‑Lambert predicts exponential loss with distance in a scattering medium like fog, so designers must allocate extra power or limit range to maintain link reliability.
