What is Unguided media: Wireless?

Wireless transmission refers to unguided media that propagate electromagnetic signals through air, vacuum, or water without a physical conductor, enabling mobile communication and flexible network connectivity over varying distances.

📝 All Unguided media: Wireless MCQs

Q1. What frequency range defines the Very Low Frequency (VLF) band used for ground propagation?

A.3–30 kHz ✅
B.30–300 kHz
C.300 kHz–3 MHz
D.3–30 MHz
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The VLF band is defined as 3 kHz to 30 kHz. These low‑frequency signals travel close to the Earth's surface, making them suitable for long‑range navigation and submarine communication, which matches the description in the provided table.

Q2. Which propagation method is primarily used for signals whose frequencies lie between 2 MHz and 30 MHz?

A.Ground propagation
B.Sky propagation ✅
C.Line‑of‑sight propagation
D.Satellite propagation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Sky propagation dominates in the 2 MHz–30 MHz range because the waves are reflected by the ionosphere back to Earth, allowing longer distances with lower power. Ground propagation is limited to frequencies below about 2 MHz, while line‑of‑sight requires higher frequencies.

Q3. If a transmitter increases its output power, which propagation method will experience the most noticeable increase in communication range?

A.Ground propagation ✅
B.Sky propagation
C.Line‑of‑sight propagation
D.All equally
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Ground propagation relies on low‑frequency waves that hug the Earth's surface; their range is highly sensitive to power level. Raising power boosts the signal strength along the curved path, extending the distance more noticeably than for sky or line‑of‑sight methods, which already benefit from ionospheric reflection or direct paths.

Q4. Two antennas are placed beyond the radio horizon. What is the most likely outcome for a line‑of‑sight signal?

A.Signal becomes stronger
B.Signal is reflected by the ionosphere
C.Communication fails unless a relay is used ✅
D.Signal diffracts around the Earth
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Line‑of‑sight signals travel in straight lines and cannot bend around the Earth's curvature. When the antennas are beyond the horizon, the direct path is blocked, so communication generally fails unless an intermediate relay or repeater provides a new line‑of‑sight segment.

Q5. A 5 MHz radio wave is transmitted upward toward the ionosphere. Why is it reflected rather than absorbed?

A.Its wavelength is too short to interact with ions
B.The ionosphere's plasma frequency is lower than 5 MHz, allowing reflection
C.Atmospheric gases completely block the wave
D.The wave is above the ionospheric resonance frequency, causing absorption ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The ionosphere reflects frequencies lower than its plasma frequency; for a 5 MHz wave, the plasma frequency is typically higher, so the wave passes through and is absorbed. Therefore, reflection occurs when the transmitted frequency is below the plasma frequency, not above it.

Q6. Which statement correctly contrasts ground and sky propagation?

A.Both use the same frequency range
B.Ground propagation follows Earth's curvature, sky propagation relies on ionospheric reflection ✅
C.Sky propagation works only at VLF, ground works only at microwave
D.Ground propagation requires directional antennas, sky propagation uses omnidirectional antennas
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Ground propagation follows the Earth's surface, allowing low‑frequency waves to hug the planet. Sky propagation, in contrast, sends higher‑frequency waves upward where they encounter the ionosphere and are reflected back, enabling longer distances without needing the same low‑frequency band.

Q7. Why are microwave frequencies (1–300 GHz) preferred for point‑to‑point links despite requiring line‑of‑sight?

A.They penetrate obstacles easily
B.They have low atmospheric attenuation
C.They can be tightly focused into narrow beams ✅
D.They are not regulated by authorities
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Microwave wavelengths are short enough to be captured by high‑gain, narrow‑beam antennas. This tight focusing concentrates power, reduces interference, and supports high data rates over a clear line‑of‑sight path, making microwaves ideal for dedicated point‑to‑point communication despite the need for unobstructed sight.

Q8. How do the applications of VLF and LF bands differ based on their propagation characteristics?

A.VLF for short‑range, LF for long‑range
B.VLF for long‑range navigation, LF for radio beacons ✅
C.Both used for high‑speed data transmission
D.LF cannot travel through ground
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: VLF waves travel great distances by hugging the Earth, making them suitable for long‑range navigation systems. LF waves, while still ground‑propagated, are typically employed for radio beacons and locators because they balance range with manageable antenna size, reflecting their distinct practical uses.

Q10. Which frequency band would you select for a long‑range radio navigation beacon requiring ground propagation?

A.Very Low Frequency (VLF)
B.Low Frequency (LF) ✅
C.Medium Frequency (MF)
D.High Frequency (HF)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Low Frequency (30–300 kHz) is commonly used for navigation beacons because its ground‑propagation characteristics allow signals to travel extensive distances with modest power, fitting the needs of long‑range beacon systems.

Q11. How does the required physical size of an antenna relate to the wavelength of the transmitted signal?

A.Inversely proportional to frequency ✅
B.Directly proportional to frequency
C.Independent of frequency
D.Proportional to the square of frequency
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Antenna size is generally on the order of half the wavelength; since wavelength λ=c/f\lambda = c/fλ=c/f, higher frequency (shorter wavelength) permits smaller antennas, making size inversely proportional to frequency.

Q12. To achieve reliable 500 km coverage both day and night, which combination of band and propagation method is most suitable?

A.VLF ground propagation
B.LF sky propagation
C.HF sky propagation ✅
D.EHF line‑of‑sight propagation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: HF (2–30 MHz) waves reflected by the ionosphere (sky propagation) can cover several hundred kilometers regardless of time of day, providing the required range for a 500 km area while avoiding the severe attenuation of higher‑frequency EHF signals.

Q13. For a 20 km link that needs high data rate while minimizing interference, which technique best exploits propagation characteristics?

A.Use microwave band with directional antennas ✅
B.Use VLF with omnidirectional antenna
C.Use LF with sky propagation
D.Use EHF with ground propagation
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Microwave frequencies allow the use of highly directional antennas, concentrating power toward the receiver, which raises data rates and reduces the chance of interfering with other systems over a relatively short 20 km line‑of‑sight path.

Q14. If the transmitted power is halved, what is the expected effect on the maximum range of sky‑propagated signals?

A.Range halves
B.Range reduces by √2 ✅
C.Range unchanged
D.Range doubles
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Propagation range is roughly proportional to the square root of transmitted power because signal strength decays with distance. Halving the power therefore reduces the achievable range by a factor of √2, not by a full 50 % loss.

Q15. Which band is subject to the most stringent civilian regulatory restrictions due to potential interference with critical services?

A.Very Low Frequency (VLF)
B.Low Frequency (LF)
C.High Frequency (HF) ✅
D.Extremely High Frequency (EHF)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The HF band (2–30 MHz) is heavily regulated because it is used for aviation, maritime, and military communications; civilian use must avoid causing harmful interference, leading to the strictest licensing and operational constraints among the listed bands.

Q16. When a system uses both ground and sky propagation, how can frequency hopping improve reliability?

A.By avoiding ionospheric disturbances ✅
B.By increasing antenna size
C.By reducing power consumption
D.By eliminating line‑of‑sight requirements
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Frequency hopping rapidly changes the carrier frequency, allowing the system to bypass temporary ionospheric irregularities or fading that affect specific frequencies. This adaptability enhances overall reliability without altering hardware, making it an effective strategy for combined ground‑and‑sky propagation scenarios.
