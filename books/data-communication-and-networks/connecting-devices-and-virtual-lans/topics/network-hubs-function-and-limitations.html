What is Network Hubs function and limitations?

A network hub is a simple physical-layer device that broadcasts incoming data to all connected ports without filtering, which creates a single collision domain and limits network efficiency due to excessive traffic and lack of security compared to intelligent switching devices.

Q1. When a hub receives a frame from station A destined for station B, what does the hub do with the frame?

A.Regenerates and forwards only to the port connected to B
B.Regenerates and broadcasts out all other ports ✅
C.Drops the frame
D.Modifies the destination address
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The hub works as a physical‑layer repeater; it does not inspect MAC addresses. After regenerating the incoming signal, it transmits the frame out every port except the one it arrived on, effectively broadcasting the frame to all other stations.

Q2. If a hub introduces no filtering, what is the effect on network traffic when multiple stations send frames simultaneously?

A.Collisions are avoided due to hub's broadcast nature
B.Frames are queued and sent sequentially
C.Frames may collide causing retransmissions ✅
D.Hub routes frames to correct destinations
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Because a hub does not manage access to the medium, all stations share the same collision domain. When two or more stations transmit at the same time, their signals interfere, producing a collision. The collided frames are discarded, and the stations must retransmit after a random back‑off period, increasing overall traffic overhead.

Q3. A hub operates at the physical layer. Which statement correctly describes its ability to detect errors?

A.It can detect and correct bit errors using CRC
B.It can only regenerate the signal without checking for errors
C.It discards frames with errors based on MAC address
D.It forwards the signal without checking for errors ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A hub simply amplifies and reshapes the incoming electrical signal; it does not interpret the frame contents. Consequently, it cannot verify checksums or CRC values. It forwards whatever it receives, whether the bits are correct or corrupted, relying on higher‑layer protocols to handle error detection and correction.

Q4. Assume a LAN of 5 stations connected to a hub. If station X sends a broadcast frame, how many copies of the frame will be transmitted on the physical medium?

A.1 copy
B.4 copies ✅
C.5 copies
D.0 copies
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When a hub receives a frame, it retransmits the signal out every port except the one it arrived on. With five stations, the incoming port is excluded, leaving four remaining ports. Therefore the hub generates four identical copies, one for each of the other stations, resulting in four transmissions on the medium.

Q5. Which characteristic distinguishes a hub from a link‑layer switch?

A.Hub regenerates signals but cannot filter frames ✅
B.Hub operates at Layer 2 and can filter MAC addresses
C.Hub maintains a forwarding table for destinations
D.Hub provides VLAN segmentation
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A hub is a pure physical‑layer device; it amplifies incoming signals and rebroadcasts them without examining MAC addresses. In contrast, a link‑layer switch works at both the physical and data‑link layers, maintaining a MAC address table and forwarding frames only to the appropriate port, thereby providing filtering capability.

Q6. Compare the broadcast behavior of a hub with that of a link‑layer switch. Which statement is accurate?

A.Both broadcast frames to all ports
B.Hub broadcasts, switch forwards only to the destination port ✅
C.Switch broadcasts, hub forwards selectively
D.Both forward frames only to the source port
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A hub lacks any intelligence to inspect frame headers, so it indiscriminately forwards received frames out all other ports, creating a broadcast. A link‑layer switch, however, examines the destination MAC address and forwards the frame solely to the port associated with that address, eliminating unnecessary broadcasts.

Q7. Evaluate the impact on collision domains when replacing a hub with a link‑layer switch in a LAN.

A.Number of collision domains remains the same
B.Collision domain splits into multiple, one per port ✅
C.Collision domain expands to include all ports
D.Switch creates a single large collision domain
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A hub places all attached stations in a single collision domain, so any simultaneous transmission can cause a collision. Substituting a switch creates a separate collision domain for each individual port, isolating traffic and preventing collisions between devices connected to different ports, thereby improving network efficiency.

Q8. A network designer argues that using a hub simplifies troubleshooting because all frames are visible to every station. Critically assess this claim.

A.True, because hubs provide full visibility without filtering
B.False, because hubs cause collisions that obscure diagnostics ✅
C.True, but only for unicast traffic
D.False, because switches provide even greater visibility
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: While a hub indeed allows every station to see all traffic, the lack of filtering also means that collisions occur frequently, making it harder to isolate problems. Collided frames are discarded, and the resulting retransmissions can mask the root cause of errors, so the perceived simplicity is outweighed by diagnostic difficulty.

Q9. Apply the concept of signal regeneration to explain why a hub can extend the physical length of a LAN segment.

A.Regeneration amplifies the original signal, overcoming attenuation ✅
B.Regeneration compresses data to fit longer cables
C.Regeneration adds error‑checking bits to the frame
D.Regeneration changes the protocol to a higher layer
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A hub receives a weakened electrical signal, cleans it up, and then retransmits a fresh, full‑strength version. This regeneration compensates for signal loss due to cable length, allowing the LAN segment to be extended beyond the distance a single cable could reliably carry without amplification.

Q10. Synthesize how the absence of MAC address awareness in a hub influences its role in a VLAN environment.

A.Hub can enforce VLAN boundaries using MAC tags
B.Hub creates separate collision domains per VLAN
C.Hub cannot differentiate VLAN traffic, so VLANs span through it unchanged ✅
D.Hub encapsulates frames with VLAN IDs
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Because a hub operates only at the physical layer, it does not inspect or modify MAC addresses or VLAN tags. Consequently, any VLAN tagging applied by switches is transparent to the hub, and the hub forwards all frames regardless of VLAN, causing all VLANs to share the same broadcast domain across the hub.

Q11. If a hub is placed between two switches, what effect does it have on the overall network topology?

A.It creates a loop that must be prevented
B.It merges the two switch domains into one broadcast domain ✅
C.It isolates the switches, preventing any traffic exchange
D.It adds a routing function between the switches
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The hub acts as a simple repeater, forwarding frames received from one switch to the other without any filtering. This effectively connects the two switch segments into a single collision and broadcast domain, making them behave as one larger LAN and eliminating any segmentation that the switches might otherwise provide.

Q12. Design a scenario where deploying a hub instead of a switch could be advantageous despite its limitations. Which condition best justifies this choice?

A.When low latency is critical for real‑time applications
B.When cost constraints outweigh performance concerns in a small, static network
C.When the network requires advanced security filtering
D.When the network must provide simple connectivity without the need for MAC‑based filtering ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: In a very small, static environment where only a few devices communicate and sophisticated features are unnecessary, the low cost and ease of installation of a hub may outweigh its performance drawbacks. The simplicity of a hub can be sufficient when MAC‑level filtering or VLAN separation is not required.

Q13. What layer of the OSI model does a hub operate on?

A.Layer 1 (Physical) ✅
B.Layer 2 (Data Link)
C.Layer 3 (Network)
D.Layer 4 (Transport)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A hub functions purely as a physical‑layer device; it receives electrical signals, amplifies them, and retransmits them without interpreting any higher‑layer information such as MAC addresses. Therefore, it resides at Layer 1 of the OSI reference model.
