What is Bluetooth architecture piconet and scatternet?

Bluetooth networks are organized into piconets, where one master device synchronizes and controls communication with up to seven active slave devices in a star topology, and scatternets, which interconnect multiple piconets by allowing a device to act as a slave in one piconet and a master or slave in another, enabling extended mesh-like connectivity beyond a single piconet’s limitations.

Q1. In a one‑plus‑one APS scheme, what occurs when the working line fails?

A.The protection line continues to carry the same data without any switch.
B.The receiver automatically switches to the protection line, preserving the transmission. ✅
C.Both lines stop transmitting until manual reconfiguration.
D.The system discards the current frame and resumes with the next frame.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When the working line fails, the APS mechanism detects the loss and the receiver selects the protection line, which has been transmitting the same data simultaneously. This automatic switch maintains continuity of the transmission without loss, which is the core behavior of one‑plus‑one protection.

Q2. Given the multipoint SONET network where T1 can send to T2 and T3 simultaneously but T2 can only send to T3, which inference about bandwidth usage is most accurate?

A.T1 requires twice the bandwidth of T2.
B.T2 must use a lower‑order STS signal than T1.
C.T1's upstream link is shared equally among all terminals.
D.T1's downstream capacity must be at least the sum of the capacities needed for T2 and T3. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Because T1 must be able to deliver data to both T2 and T3 at the same time, its downstream link must have enough capacity to handle the combined traffic of those two destinations. This implies that T1's downstream bandwidth must be at least the sum of the individual bandwidths required for T2 and T3.

Q3. In a one‑to‑many APS configuration with one protection line shared by three working lines, what is the most likely outcome if two working lines fail at the same time?

A.Both failed lines recover automatically using the single protection line.
B.The protection line can only carry one of the failed streams, leaving the other lost. ✅
C.The system switches to a secondary protection line that was reserved for such events.
D.All three working lines are rerouted through the remaining active line.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A single protection line can only carry one data stream at a time; therefore, when two working lines fail simultaneously, only one of the failed streams can be restored using the shared protection line while the other remains unavailable until an alternate recovery method is employed.

Q4. Which statement best compares linear SONET networks with mesh SONET networks regarding redundancy?

A.Mesh networks provide multiple alternate paths, while linear networks rely on a single protection line. ✅
B.Linear networks use ADMs at every node, whereas mesh networks do not use ADMs.
C.Redundancy in linear networks is achieved through higher‑order STS signals, while mesh networks use lower‑order signals.
D.Both linear and mesh networks have identical redundancy mechanisms.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Mesh topologies inherently contain several distinct routes between any two nodes, allowing traffic to be rerouted around a failure. Linear topologies, by contrast, have only one primary path and depend on a dedicated protection line to supply redundancy, making mesh networks more resilient.

Q5. When evaluating resource utilization, how does a one‑plus‑one APS scheme compare to a one‑to‑one scheme?

A.One‑plus‑one uses less fiber but more bandwidth.
B.One‑to‑one provides higher reliability at the cost of more idle capacity.
C.One‑plus‑one doubles the bandwidth for the working line.
D.One‑to‑one reserves a dedicated protection line for each working line, reducing idle capacity. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: In a one‑to‑one arrangement each working line has its own dedicated protection line, so the protection resources are not shared and idle capacity is minimized. This contrasts with one‑plus‑one where the same protection line can be used for multiple working lines, potentially leaving unused capacity.

Q6. Which distinction correctly describes ADM usage in point‑to‑point versus multipoint SONET networks?

A.ADMs are required only in point‑to‑point networks.
B.Multipoint networks employ ADMs to add and drop signals, while point‑to‑point networks typically have none. ✅
C.Both network types use the same number of ADMs per terminal.
D.Point‑to‑point networks use ADMs only for protection switching.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In a multipoint SONET configuration, each node uses an Add‑Drop Multiplexer to insert its own traffic into the shared stream and extract traffic destined for it. A simple point‑to‑point link does not need this add‑drop functionality, so ADMs are generally absent.

Q7. To protect a SONET ring using APS, which design choice correctly applies the principle of automatic protection switching?

A.Use a single protection fiber that is shared by all nodes without any switching logic.
B.Deploy separate protection fibers for each node but never switch them.
C.Implement APS at each node so that when a fiber fails, traffic is rerouted in the opposite direction around the ring. ✅
D.Disable APS on the ring and rely solely on manual reconfiguration.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: APS is installed at each node so that when a failure is detected on the working fiber, the node instantly switches to the protection fiber, causing traffic to flow the opposite way around the ring and preserving connectivity without manual intervention.

Q8. An STS‑3c frame carries 44 ATM cells per SPE. If a frame contains 10 SPEs, how many ATM cells are transmitted in one full STS‑3c frame?

A.\(44 \times 10 = 440\) cells ✅
B.\(44 + 10 = 54\) cells
C.\(44 \times 3 = 132\) cells
D.\(44 \times 12 = 528\) cells
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Each SPE holds 44 ATM cells. With 10 SPEs in the frame, the total number of cells is the product of the two quantities: \(44 \times 10 = 440\) cells. This straightforward multiplication yields the complete cell count for the STS‑3c frame.

Q9. How does a Bidirectional Line Switched Ring (BLSR) incorporate APS mechanisms to ensure survivability?

A.BLSR uses APS only on the outer ring, ignoring the inner ring.
B.APS is not used in BLSR; protection relies on manual switching.
C.The ring switches to a pre‑configured backup wavelength without monitoring line quality.
D.Each span contains both working and protection fibers; APS monitors the working fiber and automatically switches to the protection fiber on a span‑by‑span basis when a failure is detected. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: In a BLSR, every span includes a working fiber and a protection fiber. APS continuously monitors the health of the working fiber; upon detecting a fault, the APS logic instantly switches that span to its protection fiber, allowing traffic to continue around the ring with minimal disruption.

Q10. What does ADM stand for in SONET terminology?

A.Automatic Data Multiplexer
B.Asynchronous Digital Module
C.Add‑Drop Multiplexer ✅
D.Advanced Demultiplexing Machine
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: ADM is the abbreviation for Add‑Drop Multiplexer, a device used in SONET networks to insert (add) or extract (drop) traffic at specific nodes without disturbing the rest of the optical signal.
