What is 802.11 physical layer standards a/b/g/n/ac/ax?

The 802.11 physical layer has evolved through successive amendments offering increasing speeds and efficiency: 802.11a/g provided 54 Mbps in 5 GHz/2.4 GHz bands, 802.11n introduced MIMO for up to 600 Mbps, 802.11ac added wider channels and multi-user MIMO for gigabit speeds in 5 GHz, and 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) improved spectral efficiency and capacity in dense environments using OFDMA and higher-order modulation across both frequency bands.

Q1. What is the fixed number of frames transmitted per second for any STS‑n signal in SONET?

A.8000 ✅
B.1000
C.5000
D.12000
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The SONET standard defines a constant frame rate of 8000 frames per second for every STS‑n signal. This rate matches the 8 kHz sampling frequency used for digitizing voice, ensuring that each voice sample fits exactly into one frame. Consequently, the transmission speed is independent of the STS level.

Q2. How many rows does each SONET frame matrix contain?

A.8
B.9 ✅
C.10
D.12
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A SONET frame is organized as a two‑dimensional matrix of bytes. The vertical dimension is fixed at nine rows, regardless of the STS level. This structural consistency simplifies hardware design and allows the same framing logic to be used for all SONET signals.

Q3. In SONET transmission, after which element is the third bit of the second byte sent?

A.It is the third bit transmitted overall in the frame.
B.It is transmitted after the first two bits of the second byte, but before any bits of the third byte. ✅
C.It is transmitted after all bits of the first row.
D.It is transmitted after the most significant bit of the first byte.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Bytes are sent left‑to‑right across the frame, and within each byte the bits travel from the most significant to the least significant. Therefore, the third bit of the second byte follows the first two bits of that same byte and precedes the remaining bits of the second byte and any later bytes.

Q10. Which statement correctly describes the transmission order of bytes and bits in a SONET frame?

A.Bytes are sent top to bottom while bits are sent left to right.
B.Bytes are transmitted left to right across the frame, and within each byte the bits are sent from most significant to least significant. ✅
C.Bits are transmitted before bytes are assembled.
D.Both bytes and bits are transmitted simultaneously.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: SONET specifies that bytes are placed sequentially from the leftmost column to the rightmost, moving row by row from top to bottom. Within each byte, the bits travel from the most significant bit (MSB) to the least significant bit (LSB), preserving the conventional ordering used in digital communications.

C.Frame size does not affect bandwidth
D.Increasing columns reduces bandwidth
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The frame rate is fixed, so each frame occupies the same time interval. Adding columns enlarges each frame, which means more bits are sent within the same time window, thereby raising the overall bit‑rate (bandwidth) without changing the duration of an individual frame.

Q12. Why does SONET use a fixed frame rate of 8000 frames per second?

A.To match video frame rates
B.Because 8000 Hz is the PCM voice sampling rate ✅
C.Arbitrary design choice
D.To simplify Ethernet compatibility
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The 8000 fps rate directly corresponds to the 8 kHz sampling frequency employed in pulse‑code modulation (PCM) for voice. By aligning the SONET frame period with the voice sample period, each voice sample can be placed into a single frame, ensuring seamless integration of telephony traffic.

Q14. Why is transmitting bits from most significant to least significant important for SONET compatibility?

A.Ensures compatibility with little‑endian systems
B.Allows direct mapping to ASCII
C.Facilitates synchronization with legacy TDM equipment ✅
D.No significance
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Legacy time‑division multiplexing (TDM) equipment, which SONET was designed to interoperate with, expects bits to arrive MSB‑first. Maintaining this order preserves timing relationships and enables seamless integration with existing telephony infrastructure, avoiding the need for costly re‑encoding.

Q15. How can the fixed frame structure of SONET be used to carry both voice and data traffic?
