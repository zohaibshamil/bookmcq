Q1. If a regional cable head (RCH) serves up to 400,000 subscribers and each distribution hub serves up to 40,000, what is the minimum number of distribution hubs required to serve the full capacity of one RCH?

🔹 A. 5
🔹 B. 8
🔹 C. 10
🔹 D. 12

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The total capacity of an RCH is 400,000 subscribers. Dividing this by the 40,000 subscribers that each hub can serve gives \(400{,}000 ÷ 40{,}000 = 10\). Therefore at least ten hubs are needed to cover the entire RCH capacity, assuming perfect load balancing.

Q2. Given that a fiber node splits the analog signal so the same signal is sent to each coaxial cable serving up to 1,000 subscribers, what is the total number of subscribers that can be served if a distribution hub connects to 20 fiber nodes?

🔹 A. 20000
🔹 B. 10000
🔹 C. 15000
🔹 D. 25000

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each fiber node can serve 1,000 subscribers. With 20 fiber nodes, the total is \(20 × 1{,}000 = 20{,}000\) subscribers. This calculation assumes each node is fully utilized and there is no overlap, which is typical in capacity‑planning scenarios for HFC distribution hubs.

Q3. If an HFC network reduces the number of amplifiers needed to eight or less per segment, and each amplifier adds an average latency of 0.3 ms, what is the maximum additional latency introduced by amplifiers in a segment?

🔹 A. 1.5 ms
🔹 B. 2.4 ms
🔹 C. 3.0 ms
🔹 D. 3.6 ms

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The worst‑case scenario uses eight amplifiers. Multiplying the number of amplifiers by the per‑amplifier latency yields \(8 × 0.3 \text{ms} = 2.4 \text{ms}\). This represents the upper bound on latency contributed by amplification, assuming all amplifiers are active and their latencies are additive.

Q4. Suppose the downstream data band occupies the frequency range 50–500 MHz, while the upstream band occupies 5–30 MHz. If a new service requires a 20 MHz contiguous block within the upstream band, which of the following allocations would be feasible?

🔹 A. 10–30 MHz
🔹 B. 5–25 MHz
🔹 C. 15–35 MHz
🔹 D. 25–45 MHz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The upstream band spans 5–30 MHz, giving a total width of 25 MHz. A 20 MHz block must fit entirely inside this range. The interval 5–25 MHz satisfies the requirement, staying within the limits, whereas the other options either exceed the upper bound or start below the lower bound.

Q5. A cable operator wants to increase the downstream bandwidth by 25 % without altering the existing fiber node architecture. If the current downstream range is 50–500 MHz, what new upper frequency limit must be achieved?

🔹 A. 550 MHz
🔹 B. 600 MHz
🔹 C. 612.5 MHz
🔹 D. 650 MHz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Current downstream bandwidth is \(500‑50 = 450 \text{MHz}\). A 25 % increase yields \(450 × 1.25 = 562.5 \text{MHz}\) total bandwidth. Adding this to the lower limit of 50 MHz gives a new upper limit of \(50 + 562.5 = 612.5 \text{MHz}\). Therefore the operator must extend the spectrum to at least 612.5 MHz.

Q6. Which statement correctly compares the directionality of traditional cable TV networks and hybrid fiber‑coaxial (HFC) networks?

🔹 A. Traditional is bidirectional, HFC is unidirectional
🔹 B. Both are unidirectional
🔹 C. Traditional is unidirectional, HFC is bidirectional
🔹 D. Both are bidirectional

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Traditional cable TV systems were designed for one‑way transmission: video downstream only, with limited return paths. HFC networks introduced fiber back‑haul and bidirectional amplifiers, enabling two‑way communication for both video and data. Hence, the traditional system is unidirectional while the HFC system supports bidirectional traffic.

Q7. Which of the following statements best differentiates the role of a DSLAM from that of a fiber node in an HFC network?

🔹 A. DSLAM aggregates DSL signals from many subscribers onto fiber, while the fiber node splits analog signals for coaxial distribution
🔹 B. DSLAM splits analog signals, while the fiber node aggregates DSL signals
🔹 C. Both perform identical functions in their respective networks
🔹 D. Neither handles signal conversion between media

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A DSLAM (Digital Subscriber Line Access Multiplexer) collects multiple DSL streams from copper pairs and forwards them over a single fiber link toward the ISP. Conversely, a fiber node in an HFC system receives a high‑capacity optical signal and then splits it into multiple coaxial outputs for downstream distribution, serving many subscribers. Their functions are thus complementary but distinct.

Q8. Evaluate the impact on signal attenuation if the coaxial portion of an HFC network is replaced with a second segment of fiber optic cable of equal length, assuming fiber attenuation is 0.2 dB/km and coax attenuation is 2 dB/km.

🔹 A. Attenuation reduces by 90 %
🔹 B. Attenuation reduces by 80 %
🔹 C. Attenuation reduces by 50 %
🔹 D. Attenuation reduces by 10 %

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The reduction factor is \((2 \text{dB/km} - 0.2 \text{dB/km}) / 2 \text{dB/km} = 0.9\), meaning a 90 % decrease in attenuation. This dramatic improvement stems from fiber’s much lower loss per kilometer, which directly translates into higher signal‑to‑noise ratios and longer reach without repeaters.

Q9. Differentiate the maximum subscriber capacity per node between a traditional cable TV head end and a hybrid fiber‑coaxial (HFC) distribution hub.

🔹 A. 1000 vs 40,000
🔹 B. 400,000 vs 40,000
🔹 C. 1000 vs 1000
🔹 D. 40,000 vs 400,000

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In a legacy coax‑only head end, each coaxial cable typically serves about 1,000 homes before signal quality degrades. An HFC distribution hub, enabled by fiber back‑haul and advanced amplification, can support up to 40,000 subscribers per hub, representing a 40‑fold increase in the number of customers that can be served from a single node.

Q10. In the context of address mechanisms, which layer is primarily responsible for assigning IP addresses to end devices?

🔹 A. Physical layer
🔹 B. Data Link layer
🔹 C. Network layer
🔹 D. Transport layer

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

IP addressing is a function of the Network layer (Layer 3) of the OSI model. This layer handles logical addressing, routing, and packet forwarding, whereas the Physical and Data Link layers deal with hardware addresses (MAC) and signal transmission, and the Transport layer manages end‑to‑end communication services.

Q11. Apply the principle of frequency‑division multiplexing (FDM) to explain why the cable TV bandwidth is divided into video, downstream data, and upstream data bands.

🔹 A. FDM allows simultaneous transmission of different services by allocating separate frequency ranges, preventing interference
🔹 B. FDM merges all signals into one band to simplify processing
🔹 C. FDM uses time slots to separate services
🔹 D. FDM is unrelated to cable TV bandwidth allocation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

FDM works by assigning each service its own slice of the frequency spectrum, so video, downstream, and upstream signals can travel concurrently without colliding. By keeping each type of traffic in a distinct band, the system avoids inter‑modulation and ensures that receivers can filter out unwanted portions, preserving signal integrity for all services.

Q12. Synthesize a design where a downstream data service requires 300 Mbps and each MHz of downstream bandwidth can support 0.8 Mbps. Using the HFC downstream range of 50–500 MHz, determine whether the existing allocation suffices, and justify your answer.

🔹 A. Sufficient
🔹 B. Insufficient
🔹 C. Exactly sufficient
🔹 D. Cannot determine

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The available downstream bandwidth is \(500‑50 = 450 \text{MHz}\). Multiplying by the capacity per MHz gives \(450 × 0.8 \text{Mbps} = 360 \text{Mbps}\). Since 360 Mbps exceeds the required 300 Mbps, the current allocation can comfortably support the service, leaving a margin for overhead or future growth.

Q13. If a subscriber experiences high upstream latency, which component in the HFC architecture is most likely responsible, and what modification could reduce the latency?

🔹 A. Fiber node (replace with higher‑speed fiber)
🔹 B. DSLAM (add more DSL lines)
🔹 C. Distribution hub (add bidirectional amplifiers)
🔹 D. Drop cable (shorten length)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The final coaxial segment—the drop cable—adds propagation delay proportional to its length. Reducing the length of this cable, or using higher‑quality, lower‑delay coax, directly cuts the round‑trip time experienced by upstream packets, thereby lowering perceived latency for the subscriber.

Q14. Propose a method to increase the number of upstream subscribers without expanding the frequency band, considering the constraints of the HFC network. Which technique best meets this goal?

🔹 A. Implement time‑division multiple access (TDMA) within the upstream band
🔹 B. Increase power of upstream signals
🔹 C. Reduce modulation order
🔹 D. Add more fiber nodes

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

TDMA partitions the existing upstream frequency into time slots, allowing multiple users to share the same spectral slice sequentially. By coordinating transmission times, the network can accommodate more upstream users without requiring additional spectrum, making efficient use of the limited upstream bandwidth while preserving signal quality.

Q15. What does DSLAM stand for?

🔹 A. Digital Subscriber Line Access Multiplexer
🔹 B. Direct Signal Line Access Module
🔹 C. Data Service Line Allocation Manager
🔹 D. Digital Signal Light Amplifier

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

DSLAM is the acronym for Digital Subscriber Line Access Multiplexer, a device that aggregates multiple DSL connections from customers and forwards the combined traffic over a high‑capacity link, typically fiber, toward the service provider’s core network.

Q16. Which component splits the incoming optical signal into multiple coaxial outputs?

🔹 A. Splitter
🔹 B. Modulator
🔹 C. Amplifier
🔹 D. Router

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In an HFC system, the splitter (often called a fiber‑to‑coax splitter) receives the optical signal from the fiber node and divides it into several lower‑frequency coaxial feeds, each of which carries the same set of services to a group of subscribers.

Q17. Synthesize a spectrum allocation plan that maximizes downstream capacity while preserving at least 50 MHz for upstream use within the 5–750 MHz HFC band. Which allocation achieves this?

🔹 A. Downstream 55–750 MHz, Upstream 5–55 MHz
🔹 B. Downstream 5–700 MHz, Upstream 700–750 MHz
🔹 C. Downstream 100–750 MHz, Upstream 5–100 MHz
🔹 D. Downstream 5–650 MHz, Upstream 650–750 MHz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To keep a minimum of 50 MHz for upstream, the upper bound of the upstream band can be set at 55 MHz. Allocating 5–55 MHz for upstream leaves 55–750 MHz (695 MHz) for downstream, which maximizes downstream capacity while meeting the upstream requirement.
