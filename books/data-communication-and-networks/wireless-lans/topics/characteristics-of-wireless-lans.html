What is Characteristics of wireless LANs?

Wireless LANs are characterized by their use of unlicensed radio frequency bands, limited coverage range due to signal attenuation, susceptibility to environmental interference and eavesdropping, variable data rates depending on distance and obstacles, and support for mobile device connectivity without physical tethers, making them ideal for flexible access but requiring careful planning for security and performance.

Q1. What is the line rate of an STS‑3 (OC‑3) signal as listed in the SONET/SDH table?

A. A) \(51.840\) Mbps
B. B) \(155.520\) Mbps ✅
C. C) \(466.560\) Mbps
D. D) \(622.080\) Mbps

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The SONET/SDH specification lists the line rate for an STS‑3 (also called OC‑3) as \(155.520\) Mbps. This value is exactly three times the basic STS‑1 rate of \(51.840\) Mbps, confirming the scaling rule presented in the table. Therefore option B is the correct choice.

Q2. If eighteen STS‑1 channels can be multiplexed into one STS‑18, how many STS‑3 channels can be multiplexed into the same STS‑18?

A. A) 3
B. B) 9
C. C) 12
D. D) 6 ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

An STS‑18 container holds eighteen STS‑1 channels. Since each STS‑3 is built from three STS‑1 channels, the number of STS‑3 channels that fit into an STS‑18 is \(18 ÷ 3 = 6\). This division directly yields six STS‑3 channels, making option D correct.

Q3. Which statement correctly compares the data rates of STS‑9 and STS‑18?

A. A) STS‑9 is twice the rate of STS‑18
B. B) STS‑18 is exactly twice the rate of STS‑9 ✅
C. C) Both have the same rate
D. D) STS‑9 is half the rate of STS‑18

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

According to the table, STS‑9 transports \(466.560\) Mbps while STS‑18 transports \(933.120\) Mbps. Observing the numbers shows that \(933.120 = 2 × 466.560\), meaning the STS‑18 rate is exactly twice that of STS‑9. Consequently, option B correctly describes the relationship.

Q4. Given that an STS‑12 signal transports \(622.080\) Mbps, what is the expected rate of an STS‑24 signal assuming linear scaling?

A. A) \(1244.160\) Mbps ✅
B. B) \(933.120\) Mbps
C. C) \(1555.200\) Mbps
D. D) \(622.080\) Mbps

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

SONET rates increase linearly with the multiplexing factor. Doubling the factor from 12 to 24 therefore doubles the bandwidth, so the expected rate for STS‑24 is \(2 × 622.080 = 1244.160\) Mbps. This matches the value given in the table, confirming option A.

Q5. Each STS‑3 channel carries three times the bandwidth of an STS‑1. What total bandwidth do four STS‑3 channels provide?

A. A) \(311.040\) Mbps
B. B) \(466.560\) Mbps
C. C) \(622.080\) Mbps ✅
D. D) \(933.120\) Mbps

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

One STS‑3 equals three STS‑1 channels, giving a bandwidth of \(3 × 51.840 = 155.520\) Mbps. Multiplying this by four yields \(4 × 155.520 = 622.080\) Mbps, which coincides with the rate listed for STS‑12. Therefore the correct total is option C.

Q6. Which STM hierarchical level corresponds to the OC‑48 rate of \(2488.320\) Mbps?

A. A) STM‑8
B. B) STM‑12
C. C) STM‑16 ✅
D. D) STM‑32

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The table aligns OC‑48 with an STM‑16 signal, both carrying a payload of \(2488.320\) Mbps. STM‑8 carries half that rate, while STM‑32 carries double, so the only level matching the OC‑48 rate is STM‑16. Hence option C is the accurate correspondence.

Q7. A network requires a minimum capacity of at least \(10\) Gbps. Which listed SONET/SDH multiplexing level first meets or exceeds this requirement?

A. A) STS‑96
B. B) STS‑192
C. C) STS‑384 ✅
D. D) STM‑64

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The highest listed rate, STS‑192, provides \(9953.280\) Mbps, which is slightly below the required \(10\) Gbps threshold. Since no listed multiplexing level reaches or exceeds \(10\) Gbps, the next standard step—STS‑384—would be needed, offering roughly \(19906.560\) Mbps. Option C correctly identifies this level.

Q8. Which statement accurately describes the relationship between STS‑9 and OC‑9?

A. A) Both represent the same payload of \(466.560\) Mbps
B. B) STS‑9 is half the rate of OC‑9
C. C) OC‑9 includes additional overhead not present in STS‑9
D. D) STS‑9 and OC‑9 are unrelated standards ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

In SONET/SDH terminology, STS‑9 and OC‑9 refer to the same multiplexed signal, each transporting a payload of \(466.560\) Mbps. They are simply different naming conventions for the identical rate, so the statement that both represent the same payload is correct. Option A therefore is accurate.

Q9. If two STS‑24 signals are combined, what is the resulting data rate?

A. A) \(1244.160\) Mbps
B. B) \(2488.320\) Mbps ✅
C. C) \(3110.400\) Mbps
D. D) \(1555.200\) Mbps

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each STS‑24 channel carries \(1244.160\) Mbps. When two such channels are combined, their rates add, giving \(2 × 1244.160 = 2488.320\) Mbps, which corresponds to the STS‑48 rate. This summed value matches option B, making it the correct answer.
