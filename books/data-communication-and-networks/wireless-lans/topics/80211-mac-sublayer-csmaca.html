What is 802.11 MAC sublayer CSMA/CA?

The 802.11 MAC sublayer uses Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA) instead of collision detection because wireless devices cannot listen while transmitting; it prevents collisions by having stations first sense if the channel is idle, then wait a random backoff period before sending, optionally using Request-to-Send and Clear-to-Send frames to reserve the medium and avoid hidden terminal issues.

Q1. What is the duration of a SONET frame?

A.125 μs ✅
B.100 μs
C.150 μs
D.200 μs
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The SONET standard defines a fixed frame period of 125 microseconds, which allows a consistent timing reference for synchronous transmission across the network. This value is derived from the 8 kHz sampling rate used for voice digitization.

Q2. Which bytes are used for alignment in the section overhead?

A.A1 and A2 ✅
B.B1 and C1
C.D1 and D2
D.E1 and F1
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Alignment bytes A1 and A2 are placed at the beginning of the frame to provide a known bit pattern that receivers can lock onto for synchronization. They are not used for payload or other control functions.

📖 Explanation: An STS‑1 frame is organized as a 9 × 90 matrix of octets, giving a total of 810 bytes per frame. The nine rows are a fundamental structural element of SONET framing.

Q16. Compare the overhead allocation in SONET STS‑1 with that in Ethernet frames regarding efficiency.

📖 Explanation: Ethernet frames have a variable overhead (preamble, header, CRC) that can be a larger fraction of small frames, whereas SONET’s overhead is fixed (27 octets) regardless of payload size, making SONET more efficient for large, constant‑size frames.
