What is Bluetooth in Networking?

Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology designed for personal area networking that enables low-power, low-cost communication between nearby devices such as phones, headphones, and sensors, operating in the 2.4 GHz ISM band with frequency hopping to reduce interference, and serving as a cable replacement for ad-hoc connections rather than infrastructure-based LAN access.

📝 All Bluetooth in Networking MCQs

Q1. If a Bluetooth device operates in the 2.4 GHz ISM band and experiences interference from Wi‑Fi, what is the most likely effect on its connection?

A.Increased range
B.Decreased latency
C.Higher packet error rate ✅
D.Lower power consumption
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Wi‑Fi and Bluetooth share the 2.4 GHz ISM band, so overlapping transmissions cause collisions. When collisions occur, the Bluetooth link must retransmit corrupted packets, which raises the packet error rate. Consequently, the connection becomes less reliable and may experience temporary pauses, even though range and power are unchanged.

Q2. Compare Bluetooth and Wi‑Fi regarding typical data rate and power consumption for short‑range communication.

A.Bluetooth offers a higher data rate and higher power consumption
B.Bluetooth provides a lower data rate and lower power consumption ✅
C.Bluetooth has a higher data rate and lower power consumption
D.Both have the same data rate and power consumption
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Bluetooth is designed for low‑energy, short‑range applications, offering data rates up to a few megabits per second, which are lower than Wi‑Fi’s tens or hundreds of megabits. To preserve battery life, Bluetooth devices use less transmit power, making the pairing of lower data rate with lower power consumption the correct description.

Q3. In a piconet, what is the role of the master device concerning frequency hopping?

A.Generates the hopping sequence and synchronizes slaves ✅
B.Passively follows slaves' hopping decisions
C.Uses a fixed frequency for the whole session
D.Randomly selects a new channel for each packet
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The master defines the hopping pattern used by the entire piconet and broadcasts the timing information in the access code. All slaves must follow this sequence to stay synchronized, ensuring that each device tunes to the same channel at the same time. The master therefore controls and coordinates the frequency‑hopping process.

Q4. If a Bluetooth device moves out of range but remains within the same piconet, which protocol component helps manage the connection status?

A.Adaptive frequency hopping
B.Power control mechanism
C.Sniff mode operation
D.Link Manager Protocol ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Link Manager Protocol (LMP) oversees link establishment, maintenance, and termination. When a device drifts beyond reliable signal strength, the LMP detects the loss of link quality and can initiate procedures such as reconnection attempts or graceful disconnection, thereby handling the change in connectivity within the piconet.

Q5. Which statement correctly differentiates a scatternet from a piconet?

A.A scatternet consists of multiple piconets linked via shared devices ✅
B.A scatternet is a single piconet with more than eight devices
C.A scatternet uses wired connections between devices
D.A scatternet operates at 5 GHz
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A piconet is limited to one master and up to seven active slaves. When a device participates in more than one piconet, it can act as a bridge, creating a scatternet—a collection of interconnected piconets. The defining feature is the shared device(s) that link the separate piconets together.

Q6. Applying the concept of time‑division multiplexing, how does Bluetooth achieve simultaneous voice and data transmission in a headset?

A.By using separate frequency bands for voice and data
B.By interleaving packets in time slots ✅
C.By increasing transmission power for voice packets
D.By compressing voice into data packets
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Bluetooth divides the radio channel into short time slots (typically 625 µs). Voice and data packets are placed into alternating slots, allowing the headset to carry both streams over the same frequency without interference. This time‑division multiplexing lets the device alternate rapidly, giving the impression of simultaneous transmission.

Q8. Evaluate the impact of increasing the number of active slaves in a piconet from 2 to 7 on the effective throughput per device, assuming the master’s total bandwidth is fixed.

A.Throughput per device doubles
B.Throughput per device remains unchanged
C.Throughput per device decreases roughly proportionally ✅
D.Throughput per device increases slightly
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The master’s total bandwidth is shared among all active slaves. When the number of slaves grows from 2 to 7, the same aggregate capacity must be divided among more devices, so each device receives a smaller share. The reduction is approximately proportional to the increase in participants, leading to a noticeable drop in per‑device throughput.

Q9. Synthesize the security mechanisms of Bluetooth (pairing, encryption, authentication) to explain why a man‑in‑the‑middle attack is more difficult compared to unsecured Wi‑Fi.

A.Bluetooth uses fixed keys that never change
B.Bluetooth never transmits data unencrypted
C.The combination of Secure Simple Pairing and link‑level encryption provides mutual authentication ✅
D.Bluetooth operates on a different frequency
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Bluetooth’s Secure Simple Pairing establishes a shared secret using Elliptic‑Curve Diffie‑Hellman, followed by link‑layer encryption that protects all subsequent traffic. Both devices authenticate each other, making it hard for an external entity to insert itself without detection. In contrast, unsecured Wi‑Fi often lacks mutual authentication, leaving it vulnerable to simple interception.

Q10. What is the maximum number of active slaves that a Bluetooth master can support in a single piconet?

A.4
B.7 ✅
C.10
D.12
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Bluetooth specification defines a piconet as having one master and up to seven active slaves. Additional devices can be parked, but only seven can actively communicate with the master at any given time, establishing the limit of seven active slaves.

Q11. If a Bluetooth device uses Adaptive Frequency Hopping (AFH) to avoid a channel occupied by a microwave oven, what is the most likely outcome on the connection's reliability?

A.Reliability decreases due to more hops
B.Reliability improves because interfered channels are avoided ✅
C.No change because AFH does not affect reliability
D.Device disconnects immediately
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: AFH monitors channel quality and dynamically removes those experiencing high interference, such as the one used by a microwave oven. By steering traffic away from the noisy channel, the device reduces collisions and packet loss, thereby enhancing the overall reliability of the Bluetooth link.
