📝 Wireless access control mechanisms

📖 From Data Communication and Networks • 15. Wireless LANs • 12 questions available

What is Wireless access control mechanisms?

Wireless access control mechanisms regulate how devices gain permission to transmit on the shared wireless medium to prevent collisions and ensure fair usage, primarily using protocols like CSMA/CA with RTS/CTS handshakes or polling methods where an access point coordinates transmissions, since traditional collision detection used in wired networks is ineffective in wireless environments due to hidden node problems and inability to sense collisions while transmitting.

Q1. In SONET terminology, what does the abbreviation STS represent?

A. Synchronous Transport Signal ✅
B. Synchronous Transfer System
C. Signal Timing Standard
D. Serial Transmission Service

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The abbreviation STS stands for Synchronous Transport Signal, which is the standard naming convention for SONET payload containers. This term is used throughout SONET specifications to identify the hierarchical levels of transport signals, distinguishing them from optical carrier designations such as OC‑x.

Q2. Given that STS‑9 is exactly one‑half the rate of STS‑18, if an STS‑18 container carries 18 STS‑1 channels, how many STS‑3 channels can it carry?

A. 9
B. 6 ✅
C. 12
D. 18

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

An STS‑18 container holds 18 STS‑1 channels. Since each STS‑3 consists of three STS‑1 channels, the container can accommodate 18 ÷ 3 = 6 STS‑3 channels. This deduction follows directly from the defined relationship between STS‑1, STS‑3, and STS‑18 rates.

Q3. If the data rate of an STS‑1 signal is 51.840 Mbps, what is the data rate of an STS‑3 signal?

A. 155.520 Mbps ✅
B. 103.680 Mbps
C. 207.360 Mbps
D. 51.840 Mbps

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

STS‑3 is defined as three times the bandwidth of STS‑1. Multiplying the base rate 51.840 Mbps by three yields 155.520 Mbps, which is the standard data rate for an STS‑3 container in the SONET hierarchy.

Q4. Compare the bandwidth of STM‑4 (622.080 Mbps) and STM‑6 (933.120 Mbps). Which statement is correct?

A. STM‑6 provides twice the bandwidth of STM‑4
B. STM‑6 provides the same bandwidth as STM‑4
C. STM‑6 provides 1.5 times the bandwidth of STM‑4 ✅
D. STM‑4 provides more bandwidth than STM‑6

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the STM‑6 rate (933.120 Mbps) by the STM‑4 rate (622.080 Mbps) gives 1.5, meaning STM‑6 offers exactly one‑and‑a‑half times the bandwidth of STM‑4. This proportional relationship is a direct consequence of the SONET scaling rules.

Q5. A network uses an add/drop multiplexer rated for up to STS‑12. Can it directly interface with an OC‑48 line without additional equipment?

A. Yes, because OC‑48 is equivalent to STS‑12
B. No, because OC‑48 corresponds to STS‑48 which exceeds the multiplexer’s capacity ✅
C. Only if a wavelength converter is added
D. Not applicable

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

OC‑48 corresponds to an STS‑48 container, which carries 48 × STS‑1 channels. Since the multiplexer is limited to STS‑12, it cannot directly handle the larger OC‑48 payload; an intermediate conversion or a higher‑capacity multiplexer would be required.

Q6. Which of the following best explains why SONET employs STS multiplexers/demultiplexers?

A. To encrypt the payload
B. To combine lower‑order STS signals into a higher‑order container ✅
C. To convert electrical signals to optical
D. To regenerate degraded signals

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The primary function of STS multiplexers/demultiplexers is to assemble several lower‑order STS signals (e.g., STS‑1, STS‑3) into a higher‑order container (e.g., STS‑12, STS‑24). This hierarchical aggregation enables efficient bandwidth utilization and simplifies network provisioning.

Q7. Evaluate the impact on latency if a regenerator is inserted after every 500 km in a 2000 km SONET link using OC‑12, assuming each regenerator adds 10 µs of processing delay.

A. Total latency increases by 40 µs ✅
B. Total latency decreases by 40 µs
C. Latency remains unchanged
D. Latency cannot be determined

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A 2000 km link with a regenerator every 500 km results in four regenerators. Each adds 10 µs, so the cumulative processing delay is 4 × 10 µs = 40 µs. This additional time is added to the propagation delay, producing a modest increase in overall latency.

Q8. How many STS‑1 channels are required to fill an OC‑96 container?

A. 96 ✅
B. 48
C. 192
D. 144

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

OC‑96 is the optical carrier equivalent of an STS‑96 container. By definition, an STS‑96 comprises 96 individual STS‑1 channels, each carrying 51.840 Mbps. Therefore, exactly 96 STS‑1 signals are needed to occupy the full capacity of an OC‑96 line.

Q9. To enable dynamic bandwidth allocation for both STM‑8 and STM‑12 traffic on a single OC‑48 line, which architecture is most appropriate?

A. Hierarchical add/drop multiplexing ✅
B. Ring topology
C. Bus topology
D. Mesh topology

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A hierarchical add/drop architecture allows the OC‑48 line to be partitioned into lower‑order containers (e.g., STM‑8, STM‑12) that can be independently added or dropped. This design supports flexible bandwidth assignment while preserving the SONET hierarchy, making it ideal for mixed‑rate traffic management.

Q10. If a failure occurs in the STS‑24 multiplexer and redundancy is provided only at the regenerator level, what is the approximate reduction in overall system throughput on an OC‑48 link?

A. 25 % reduction
B. 50 % reduction ✅
C. 75 % reduction
D. No reduction

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

An OC‑48 link carries 2488.320 Mbps. The STS‑24 container contributes half of this capacity (1244.160 Mbps). With no multiplexer redundancy, loss of the STS‑24 element removes roughly 50 % of the total throughput, assuming the remaining portions continue to operate.

Q11. A single add/drop multiplexer rated for STM‑16 can drop how many STS‑3 channels simultaneously without exceeding its capacity?

A. 8
B. 16
C. 32 ✅
D. 64

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

STM‑16 supports 4976.640 Mbps. Each STS‑3 carries 155.520 Mbps. Dividing the total capacity by the per‑channel rate yields 4976.640 ÷ 155.520 ≈ 32, meaning the multiplexer can safely handle up to 32 STS‑3 channels concurrently.

Q12. When comparing the scalability of SON ET hierarchy to Ethernet over fiber for a carrier targeting up to 10 Gbps, which statement is most accurate?

A. SONET scales linearly but requires multiple OC‑192 containers
B. Ethernet scales more flexibly and can reach 10 Gbps with fewer hardware changes ✅
C. SONET cannot support 10 Gbps without a complete redesign
D. Ethernet cannot achieve 10 Gbps over fiber

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Ethernet over fiber can evolve from 1 Gbps to 10 Gbps by upgrading transceivers and using existing fiber, whereas SONET would need to aggregate several OC‑192 (≈10 Gbps) containers or adopt newer optical line codes, making Ethernet a more flexible scaling path for many carriers.
