What is IEEE 802.11 architecture BSS and ESS?

The IEEE 802.11 standard defines two fundamental network architectures: a Basic Service Set (BSS) consists of a single access point and its associated wireless stations forming one cell, while an Extended Service Set (ESS) connects multiple BSSs through a distribution system like a wired backbone, allowing seamless roaming across larger areas under the same network name without losing connectivity.

Q1. In an asymmetric 56K modem, the upstream rate is limited to 33.6 kbps while downstream can reach 56 kbps. What primarily causes the lower upstream rate?

A.Higher voltage
B.Quantization noise ✅
C.Greater bandwidth
D.Longer cable
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The upstream path must be sampled and digitized at the telephone exchange, which introduces quantization error. This error adds noise, reducing the signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) and therefore limits the achievable data rate to 33.6 kbps. The downstream direction does not suffer this quantization noise, allowing the higher 56 kbps rate.

Q4. Compared with a traditional 33.6 kbps modem, which statement correctly describes the symmetry of a 56K modem?

A.33.6 kbps is symmetric
B.56K is symmetric
C.33.6 kbps is asymmetric
D.56K is asymmetric ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A 56K modem provides different maximum rates for downstream (56 kbps) and upstream (33.6 kbps), making it an asymmetric device. In contrast, a traditional 33.6 kbps modem offers the same rate in both directions, which is symmetric. Therefore the correct description of a 56K modem is that it is asymmetric.

Q5. Why does the downstream direction of a 56K modem not violate the Shannon capacity limit while the upstream direction does?

A.Both A and B ✅
B.No quantization noise downstream
C.Higher SNR downstream
D.Neither
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: In the downstream path the signal is not re‑sampled at the central office, so quantization noise is absent, and the effective SNR is higher than in the upstream direction where quantization introduces noise. Both the lack of quantization noise and the resulting higher SNR together keep the downstream rate within Shannon’s bound.

Q6. How does quantization noise affect the signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) in the upstream versus downstream directions of a 56K modem?

A.Introduces noise only upstream
B.Reduces SNR upstream ✅
C.Both directions equally
D.Improves SNR downstream
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Quantization noise is added when the upstream analog signal is sampled and digitized at the switching station, which degrades the SNR for the upstream path. The downstream signal bypasses this sampling step, so its SNR remains higher. Consequently, quantization noise specifically reduces the upstream SNR, limiting its data rate.

Q7. If the bandwidth of a telephone line cannot be increased, which parameter must be improved to raise the modem’s data rate?

A.Use longer cable
B.Decrease voltage
C.Reduce sampling rate
D.Increase SNR ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: According to Shannon’s theorem, capacity grows with both bandwidth and the logarithm of the signal‑to‑noise ratio. When bandwidth is fixed, the only way to increase capacity is to improve the SNR, for example by reducing noise sources or increasing signal power, thereby allowing a higher data rate.

Q8. Which factor primarily explains the asymmetry observed in many DSL services, where downstream rates exceed upstream rates?

A.Sampling frequency
B.Bandwidth allocation ✅
C.Quantization bits
D.Noise floor
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: DSL providers allocate a larger portion of the available spectrum to the downstream channel, reserving less bandwidth for upstream transmissions. This deliberate bandwidth allocation creates higher downstream capacity while limiting upstream throughput, producing the characteristic asymmetry of many DSL connections.

Q11. If quantization error were eliminated entirely, could the downstream rate of a 56K modem exceed the advertised 56 kbps without violating Shannon’s limit, assuming the same bandwidth?

A.Yes, by increasing bits per sample
B.No, limited by bandwidth ✅
C.Yes, by reducing noise
D.No, limited by physical line
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Even without quantization noise, the channel capacity is bounded by the product of bandwidth and the logarithmic term of the SNR (Shannon’s theorem). Since the bandwidth is fixed, the maximum achievable rate cannot surpass the value determined by that bandwidth, which is approximately 56 kbps for the given line.
