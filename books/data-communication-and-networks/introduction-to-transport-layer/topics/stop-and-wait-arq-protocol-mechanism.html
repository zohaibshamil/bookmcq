📝 All Stop and Wait ARQ protocol mechanism MCQs

Q1. What is the primary purpose of the Stop and Wait protocol?

🔹 A. Ensure reliable delivery of frames
🔹 B. Increase overall network throughput
🔹 C. Reduce end‑to‑end latency
🔹 D. Enable multiplexing of multiple connections

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The protocol’s main goal is to guarantee that each transmitted frame is correctly received before the next one is sent, providing reliability through acknowledgments and retransmissions. This contrasts with mechanisms that aim to boost speed or share resources.

Q2. In Stop and Wait, after sending a data frame the sender must wait for which type of signal from the receiver?

🔹 A. Ready‑to‑Send (RTS)
🔹 B. Clear‑to‑Send (CTS)
🔹 C. Acknowledgment (ACK)
🔹 D. Negative Acknowledgment (NAK)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The sender pauses until it receives an ACK, confirming successful reception. RTS and CTS are used in hardware flow control, while NAK indicates a problem; ACK is the definitive signal for proceeding to the next frame.

Q3. If a timeout occurs before an ACK is received, what is the most likely cause?

🔹 A. The data frame was lost or corrupted
🔹 B. The receiver sent a duplicate ACK
🔹 C. Network congestion caused delay
🔹 D. A protocol implementation error

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A timeout indicates that the expected acknowledgment did not arrive within the prescribed interval, which typically means the original frame never reached the receiver or was damaged, prompting a retransmission.

Q4. How does the Stop and Wait protocol detect errors in a transmitted frame?

🔹 A. By using a checksum attached to the frame
🔹 B. By employing a parity bit only
🔹 C. By calculating a cyclic redundancy check (CRC) at the receiver
🔹 D. By relying solely on sequence numbers

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Although checksums, CRCs, and parity can all detect errors, many textbook descriptions of Stop and Wait highlight the use of a simple checksum field to verify data integrity before an ACK is sent.

Q5. Compared to a sliding‑window protocol, Stop and Wait typically has lower ___?

🔹 A. Throughput
🔹 B. Latency
🔹 C. Protocol overhead
🔹 D. Implementation complexity

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because only one frame can be in transit at any time, the link is idle for much of the round‑trip time, reducing the amount of useful data transferred per unit time, i.e., its throughput.

Q6. If the round‑trip time (RTT) doubles while all other parameters stay the same, what happens to the utilization of Stop and Wait?

🔹 A. It roughly halves
🔹 B. It doubles
🔹 C. It stays unchanged
🔹 D. It becomes zero

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Utilization follows \(U = \frac{L}{L + 2B \times RTT}\). Doubling RTT increases the denominator, causing the fraction to shrink dramatically; the link appears almost idle, though it never truly reaches zero.

Q7. What role does the sequence number play in Stop and Wait?

🔹 A. It distinguishes new frames from retransmissions
🔹 B. It indicates the priority of a frame
🔹 C. It determines the size of the sliding window
🔹 D. It sets the timeout interval

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A single‑bit sequence number toggles between 0 and 1, allowing the receiver to recognize whether an incoming frame is a fresh transmission or a duplicate caused by a lost ACK.

Q8. When multiple connections share the same physical link and all use Stop and Wait, what effect is most noticeable?

🔹 A. Increased number of collisions
🔹 B. Decreased fairness among connections
🔹 C. Reduced end‑to‑end latency
🔹 D. Higher overall throughput

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Because each connection can occupy the link for the full round‑trip time, the link becomes a bottleneck, and some flows may dominate the medium while others wait longer, reducing fairness.

Q9. If the data frame arrives correctly but its ACK is lost, what will the sender do after the timeout expires?

🔹 A. Retransmit the data frame
🔹 B. Wait indefinitely for the missing ACK
🔹 C. Send a NAK to the receiver
🔹 D. Abort the transmission

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The sender cannot differentiate between a lost ACK and a lost data frame; after the timeout it assumes the frame was not acknowledged and retransmits it, ensuring reliability.

Q10. Suppose a channel has bandwidth \(B = 1\) Mbps and frame size \(L = 1000\) bits. The round‑trip time is 20 ms. What is the utilization of Stop and Wait?

🔹 A. ≈ 2.4 %
🔹 B. ≈ 5 %
🔹 C. ≈ 10 %
🔹 D. ≈ 20 %

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Utilization \(U = \frac{L}{L + 2B \times RTT}\). Convert RTT to seconds (0.02 s): \(2BRTT = 2 \times 10^{6} \times 0.02 = 40{,}000\) bits. Thus \(U = 1000/(1000+40000) \approx 0.0244\) or 2.4 %.

Q11. Which scenario would most benefit from replacing Stop and Wait with a Go‑Back‑N protocol?

🔹 A. A high‑latency satellite link with frequent errors
🔹 B. A low‑latency LAN with minimal errors
🔹 C. An error‑free fiber‑optic connection
🔹 D. A single‑frame transmission over a reliable channel

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Go‑Back‑N shines when the channel has long round‑trip times and non‑negligible error rates; the satellite link example fits this description, whereas the other options either already have low latency or do not need pipelining.

Q12. How does the sender determine the appropriate timeout interval in a Stop and Wait system?

🔹 A. By continuously estimating the round‑trip time (RTT)
🔹 B. By using a fixed 1‑second value for all links
🔹 C. By selecting a random interval for each frame
🔹 D. By receiving a timeout value from the receiver

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Dynamic timeout calculation uses measured RTT samples (often via exponential weighted moving average) to adapt to changing network conditions, ensuring the interval is neither too short nor excessively long.

Q13. If the probability of a frame error is \(p = 0.01\) and errors are independent, what is the expected number of transmissions per successful frame?

🔹 A. 1.01
🔹 B. 1.02
🔹 C. 1.1
🔹 D. 1.2

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The expected transmissions equal \(1/(1-p) = 1/0.99 \approx 1.0101\), meaning on average just a little more than one transmission is needed for each successful delivery.

Q14. When the timeout is set too low relative to the actual RTT, which performance metric suffers most?

🔹 A. Throughput
🔹 B. Reliability
🔹 C. Latency
🔹 D. Bandwidth utilization

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A premature timeout forces unnecessary retransmissions, filling the channel with duplicate frames and drastically lowering the amount of useful data transferred per unit time, i.e., the throughput.

Q15. How does using a single‑bit sequence number prevent acceptance of duplicate frames?

🔹 A. It lets the receiver differentiate new frames from old ones
🔹 B. It provides error‑detection capability
🔹 C. It signals the end of a transmission session
🔹 D. It determines the priority of a frame

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The bit toggles between 0 and 1 for each new transmission; if a frame arrives with the same bit as the previously accepted one, the receiver knows it is a duplicate and discards it.

Q16. If a network experiences burst errors that cause three consecutive frame losses, how does the average delay change in Stop and Wait?

🔹 A. It increases linearly with the number of losses
🔹 B. It increases quadratically
🔹 C. It remains unchanged
🔹 D. It actually decreases due to back‑off mechanisms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each lost frame triggers a timeout and retransmission, adding roughly the same timeout duration per loss; thus the total delay grows proportionally to the number of consecutive losses.

Q17. Which modification would most improve efficiency of Stop and Wait without altering its fundamental behavior?

🔹 A. Increase the size of each data frame
🔹 B. Reduce the timeout interval to match measured RTT
🔹 C. Add pipelining of multiple frames
🔹 D. Use a larger sequence‑number field

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A shorter, more accurate timeout reduces idle waiting time between frames, directly raising utilization while preserving the single‑frame‑in‑flight nature of the protocol.

Q18. Why might ACK frames be transmitted using a shorter time than data frames on a wireless link?

🔹 A. To lower the power consumption of the sender
🔹 B. To reduce the amount of channel time occupied by control traffic
🔹 C. To increase the probability of ACK errors
🔹 D. To simplify the hardware design of the receiver

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

ACKs are typically small packets; sending them quickly frees the shared wireless medium for subsequent data frames, improving overall channel efficiency.

Q19. If a sender’s timeout is exactly equal to the measured RTT, what risk does this pose?

🔹 A. The sender may timeout before the ACK arrives
🔹 B. Retransmissions will be delayed unnecessarily
🔹 C. There is no risk; this is optimal
🔹 D. The connection may enter a deadlock state

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Network jitter can make the actual RTT slightly larger than the measured value; setting timeout equal to RTT leaves no margin, so occasional variations cause premature timeout and unnecessary retransmissions.

Q20. Given two links: Link X (10 Mbps, RTT = 5 ms) and Link Y (1 Mbps, RTT = 50 ms). Assuming identical frame size, which link yields higher utilization under Stop and Wait?

🔹 A. Link X
🔹 B. Link Y
🔹 C. Both links provide equal utilization
🔹 D. Cannot be determined without more data

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Utilization \(U = \frac{L}{L + 2BRTT}\). For both links the product \(B \times RTT\) equals 50 kb, making the denominator identical; thus the utilization is the same for the two links.

Q21. Design a hybrid protocol that uses Stop and Wait for control frames and a sliding window for data frames. Which advantage does this design primarily provide?

🔹 A. Reduced latency for control messages
🔹 B. Increased throughput for data transmission
🔹 C. Simplified error handling for all frames
🔹 D. Better fairness among multiple flows

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By applying a pipelined sliding‑window approach to bulk data, the protocol can keep the link busy, while still using the simple, reliable Stop‑and‑Wait method for small control packets.

Q22. Assume a Stop and Wait system with frame size \(L\), bandwidth \(B\), and propagation delay \(D\). Derive the expression for the optimal timeout \(T_{opt}\) that maximizes utilization. Which formula is correct?

🔹 A. \(T_{opt}=2D\)
🔹 B. \(T_{opt}= \frac{L}{B}\)
🔹 C. \(T_{opt}=2D+\frac{L}{B}\)
🔹 D. \(T_{opt}= \frac{L}{2B}+D\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The round‑trip time consists of the propagation delay in both directions plus the transmission time of the frame: \(RTT = 2D + \frac{L}{B}\). Setting the timeout equal to this RTT maximizes the chance of receiving the ACK before retransmission.

Q23. If a malicious node consistently drops every third ACK in a Stop and Wait connection, what pattern of retransmissions will the sender observe?

🔹 A. Retransmission of every third data frame
🔹 B. Retransmission of every second data frame
🔹 C. Random retransmissions with no clear pattern
🔹 D. No retransmissions because the sender cannot detect the loss

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because the sender waits for an ACK before sending the next frame, the loss of every third ACK forces a timeout and retransmission of the corresponding third data frame, creating a predictable periodic pattern.

Q24. Compare the effect of increasing frame size versus decreasing timeout on utilization when RTT dominates the transmission time. Which yields a greater relative improvement?

🔹 A. Increasing the frame size
🔹 B. Decreasing the timeout interval
🔹 C. Both produce equal improvements
🔹 D. Neither has a noticeable effect

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When RTT is large, utilization \(U = \frac{L}{L+2BRTT}\) is more sensitive to changes in \(L\); enlarging the frame reduces the proportion of time spent waiting, whereas a modest reduction in timeout cannot overcome the dominant RTT component.

Q25. Why can the concept of ‘piggybacking’ not be directly applied to Stop and Wait? Which protocol element prevents it?

🔹 A. Lack of bidirectional data flow
🔹 B. The single outstanding frame restriction
🔹 C. A fixed timeout value that blocks combined messages
🔹 D. The limited size of the sequence number field

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Piggybacking requires the ability to send acknowledgments together with outgoing data. Stop and Wait permits only one unacknowledged frame at a time, so there is never a simultaneous data packet to carry the ACK.

Q26. In a network where propagation delay is negligible but processing delay at each node is significant, how should the timeout be set to minimize idle time?

🔹 A. Equal to the processing delay only
🔹 B. Twice the processing delay
🔹 C. Sum of processing delay and transmission time
🔹 D. Ignore processing delay and use a fixed value

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The timeout must cover the time the frame spends being processed plus the time needed to transmit it, ensuring the sender does not wait longer than necessary while still accounting for the dominant processing overhead.

Q27. Given a Stop and Wait link with frame error probability \(p\), frame length \(L\), bandwidth \(B\), and timeout \(T\), which expression correctly represents the expected throughput \(S\)?

🔹 A. \(S = \frac{L}{L + B\,T}\,(1-p)\)
🔹 B. \(S = \frac{L\,(1-p)}{L + B\,T}\)
🔹 C. \(S = \frac{L}{L + 2B\,T}\,(1-p)\)
🔹 D. \(S = \frac{L\,(1-p)}{L + 2B\,T}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Throughput equals the fraction of time spent transmitting useful data. The effective cycle time includes the frame transmission \(L/B\) plus the timeout \(T\); factoring the success probability \((1-p)\) yields \(S = \frac{L\,(1-p)}{L + 2B\,T}\).
