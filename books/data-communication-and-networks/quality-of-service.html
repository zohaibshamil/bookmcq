About 30. Quality of Service

Quality of Service mechanisms prioritize critical traffic over less sensitive flows to meet performance guarantees when network resources are insufficient for unconstrained demand. Classification and marking (DiffServ DSCP, 802.1p) identify traffic classes, queuing disciplines (WFQ, CBWFQ, LLQ) schedule transmission order, shaping and policing enforce rate limits, and admission control reject excess loads proactively, collectively enabling differentiated service levels within shared infrastructure.

End-to-end QoS requires coordinated policy across all network segments, complicated by administrative boundaries, encryption obscuring classifiers, and WiFi’s contention-based MAC undermining wired assumptions. Integrated Services (RSVP) promised per-flow guarantees but failed to scale, leaving DiffServ’s coarse-grained aggregation as practical compromise, while emerging determinism standards (TSN, DetNet) target industrial precision, reflecting persistent tension between statistical multiplexing efficiency and predictable performance guarantees.
