What is Amplitude Modulation(AM) during Analog to Analog conversion?

Amplitude Modulation (AM) is an analog-to-analog conversion technique where the amplitude of a high-frequency carrier wave is varied in direct proportion to the instantaneous amplitude of a lower-frequency analog message signal, while the carrier’s frequency and phase remain unchanged, enabling the transmission of voice or music over radio channels but remaining vulnerable to amplitude-based noise and interference.

Q1. In amplitude modulation, increasing the amplitude of the carrier while keeping the modulating signal constant primarily affects which characteristic of the transmitted signal?

A.Frequency
B.Power ✅
C.Phase
D.Bandwidth
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Increasing the carrier amplitude raises the overall signal power, because power is proportional to the square of amplitude. The frequency and phase remain unchanged, and the bandwidth is dictated by the modulating signal, not by carrier amplitude. Hence power is the directly affected characteristic.

Q2. Compare standard AM with DSB‑SC (double‑sideband suppressed‑carrier). Which statement correctly describes a key difference?

A.DSB‑SC requires twice the bandwidth of standard AM
B.Standard AM transmits the carrier
C.DSB‑SC uses frequency modulation
D.Standard AM includes the carrier while DSB‑SC suppresses it ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Standard AM always transmits the carrier along with the two sidebands, which consumes power even though the sidebands carry the information. DSB‑SC suppresses the carrier, sending only the sidebands, thus reducing transmitted power while preserving the same bandwidth.

Q3. If a sinusoidal modulating signal with peak amplitude \A_m = 0.5A_c\ is applied, what is the modulation index \m\?

A.0.5 ✅
B.1
C.0.25
D.0.75
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The modulation index for AM is defined as \m = \\frac{A_m}{A_c}\. Substituting \A_m = 0.5A_c\ gives \m = 0.5\. This value indicates that the carrier amplitude varies by 50 % around its nominal level, staying within the linear modulation range.

Q4. Suppose an AM transmitter uses a carrier frequency of 1 MHz and a modulation index of 0.8. If the modulating signal frequency doubles, what happens to the overall occupied bandwidth?

A.Remains unchanged
B.Increases by 50 kHz
C.Doubles ✅
D.Decreases
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The occupied bandwidth of an AM signal equals twice the highest modulating frequency: \BW = 2f_{m,max}\. Doubling the modulating frequency therefore doubles the bandwidth, while the carrier frequency and modulation index do not affect the bandwidth directly.

Q5. Evaluate the power efficiency of standard AM versus DSB‑SC when both convey the same audio signal with modulation index 0.6. Which statement is true?

A.Standard AM has higher efficiency
B.DSB‑SC has higher efficiency ✅
C.Both have equal efficiency
D.Efficiency depends only on carrier power
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Power efficiency of standard AM is limited because a large portion of transmitted power resides in the carrier, which carries no information. DSB‑SC suppresses the carrier, sending only sidebands that contain the audio, thereby delivering more of the transmitted power to useful information and achieving higher efficiency.

Q6. Apply the concept of envelope detection: Which condition must be satisfied for a simple diode envelope detector to correctly recover the modulating signal?

A.Carrier frequency must be lower than modulating frequency
B.Modulation index must be exactly 1
C.RC time constant must satisfy \1/f_c \\ll RC \\ll 1/f_m\ ✅
D.No carrier suppression allowed
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: An envelope detector works when the RC constant is long enough to follow the carrier envelope but short enough to track the fastest modulation. This requirement is expressed as \1/f_c \\ll RC \\ll 1/f_m\, ensuring the diode conducts each carrier cycle while the filter smooths the envelope without distortion.

Q7. If an AM signal suffers from over‑modulation (modulation index >1), what is the most likely observable effect on the received waveform?

A.Increased carrier power
B.Envelope distortion leading to signal clipping ✅
C.Reduced bandwidth
D.Phase reversal
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When the modulation index exceeds 1, the instantaneous amplitude of the carrier can drop to zero or negative, causing the envelope to cross the zero line. This produces clipping and distortion in the recovered audio, a hallmark of over‑modulation that degrades intelligibility.

Q8. Compare the spectral content of an AM signal with modulation index 0.3 to one with index 0.9. Which of the following statements is accurate?

A.Both have identical sideband amplitudes
B.Higher index results in larger sideband amplitudes ✅
C.Lower index reduces carrier amplitude
D.Sideband width decreases with higher index
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The carrier amplitude remains essentially constant, while the sideband amplitudes are proportional to the modulation index. Raising the index from 0.3 to 0.9 triples the sideband magnitude, making the sidebands more prominent relative to the carrier in the frequency spectrum.

Q9. When designing an AM transmitter for a maximum deviation of 75 % to avoid over‑modulation, what is the maximum allowable peak amplitude of the modulating signal relative to the carrier amplitude?

A.0.75 A_c ✅
B.1.33 A_c
C.0.5 A_c
D.0.25 A_c
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The modulation index limit of 0.75 corresponds to \m = A_m/A_c = 0.75\. Solving for \A_m\ gives \A_m = 0.75A_c\. Keeping the modulating signal below this peak ensures the envelope never reaches zero, preventing over‑modulation and preserving signal fidelity.

Q10. What is the formula for the modulation index \m\ in amplitude modulation using peak carrier and sideband amplitudes?

A.\m = \\frac{A_{max}-A_{min}}{A_{max}+A_{min}}\ ✅
B.\m = \\frac{A_{max}+A_{min}}{A_{max}-A_{min}}\
C.\m = \\frac{A_{c}}{A_{m}}\
D.\m = A_{c} \\times A_{m}\
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The standard definition of modulation index for AM uses the maximum and minimum envelope amplitudes: \m = (A_{max}-A_{min})/(A_{max}+A_{min})\. This ratio quantifies the extent of amplitude variation relative to the carrier, with values between 0 and 1 for linear modulation.

Q11. Consider an AM system where the carrier frequency drifts by \+5\ kHz due to oscillator instability, while the modulating signal remains at 5 kHz. How does this drift affect the positions of the sidebands relative to the original carrier?

A.Sidebands shift by +5 kHz ✅
B.Sidebands remain unchanged
C.Upper sideband shifts by +5 kHz and lower sideband shifts by –5 kHz
D.Both sidebands merge into the carrier
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Both sideband frequencies are defined as \f_c \\pm f_m\. If the carrier frequency increases by 5 kHz, each sideband frequency also increases by the same amount, preserving the spacing of \\\pm f_m\ from the new carrier. Thus the entire spectrum translates upward by 5 kHz.

Q12. Analyze the impact on signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) when the modulation index is reduced from 0.9 to 0.3 in a standard AM broadcast, assuming constant transmitted power. Which outcome is correct?

A.SNR improves proportionally to the square of the index
B.SNR degrades proportionally to the square of the index ✅
C.SNR remains unchanged
D.SNR improves because carrier power dominates noise
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In AM, the useful signal power resides in the sidebands and varies as \m^2\. Reducing the modulation index from 0.9 to 0.3 cuts the sideband power by a factor of \(0.3/0.9)^2 = 1/9\, causing the SNR to deteriorate proportionally to the square of the index.

Q13. Synthesize a design for an AM transmitter that limits the modulation index to 0.8 using a limiter circuit. Which component configuration best achieves this?

A.Series resistor with diode clamp at \\\pm0.8A_c\ ✅
B.Parallel LC filter tuned to carrier
C.Variable gain amplifier with feedback proportional to output amplitude
D.Phase‑locked loop controlling carrier amplitude
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A diode limiter positioned in series with a resistor can clip the instantaneous amplitude of the modulating signal to a preset level, such as \\\pm0.8A_c\. This hard‑limiting action enforces a maximum modulation index of 0.8, preventing envelope distortion while preserving carrier integrity.

Q14. If an AM signal with carrier amplitude \A_c\ and modulation index \m\ passes through a nonlinear amplifier that squares the input, what new frequency components appear in the output spectrum?

A.Only the original carrier frequency
B.Carrier and its second harmonic
C.Carrier, second harmonic, and sum/difference frequencies at \f_c \\pm f_m\ ✅
D.No frequencies due to cancellation
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Squaring an AM waveform generates terms like \A_c^2\\cos^2(2\\pi f_c t)\ which produce a second‑harmonic component at \2f_c\, as well as cross‑product terms that create frequencies at \f_c \\pm f_m\ and \2f_c \\pm f_m\. Hence the output contains the original carrier, its harmonic, and the sum/difference sidebands.

Q15. Which of the following statements correctly identifies an advantage of DSB‑SC over standard AM regarding power consumption?

A.DSB‑SC transmits half the power of standard AM for the same audio level ✅
B.DSB‑SC requires more power because carrier is suppressed
C.Both use identical power
D.DSB‑SC eliminates sidebands, reducing power
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: In DSB‑SC, the carrier— which carries no information— is removed, so all transmitted power is devoted to the sidebands that contain the audio. Consequently, for a given audio intensity, DSB‑SC needs roughly half the total power of a conventional AM system that still transmits the full‑power carrier.

Q16. In an AM system, if the carrier frequency is 600 kHz and the highest audio frequency is 5 kHz, what is the minimum required transmission bandwidth?

A.5 kHz
B.10 kHz ✅
C.15 kHz
D.20 kHz
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The bandwidth of an AM signal equals twice the highest audio (modulating) frequency: \BW = 2f_{m,max}\. With \f_{m,max}=5\ kHz, the required bandwidth is \2\\times5\ kHz = 10 kHz, regardless of the carrier frequency.
