What is Aspects of Digital to Analog Conversion during Analog Transmission?

Digital-to-analog conversion is the process of transforming discrete digital data into a continuous analog signal suitable for transmission over analog media, where key aspects include selecting an appropriate modulation technique, ensuring sufficient bandwidth, maintaining signal integrity against noise, and synchronizing the receiver to accurately reconstruct the original digital information from the received analog waveform.

Q1. Which of the following is NOT one of the three fundamental characteristics of a sine wave used in digital-to-analog modulation?

A.Amplitude
B.Frequency
C.Phase ✅
D.Wavelength
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Wavelength is not a primary characteristic of a sine wave; the three defining parameters are amplitude, frequency, and phase. Wavelength is related to frequency and propagation speed, but it does not directly describe the instantaneous shape of the sinusoid used for modulation.

Q2. In digital-to-analog conversion, what does the term \modulating\ refer to?

A.Changing digital bits to analog voltage levels

C.Encoding binary data into a digital signal
D.Filtering noise from the analog signal
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Modulating means altering one of the sine‑wave attributes (amplitude, frequency, or phase) in accordance with the digital information. This process creates an analog waveform that carries the digital data, rather than simply converting bits to static voltage levels.

Q5. If frequency modulation is used and the carrier frequency is increased, what is the immediate impact on the bandwidth required for a given data rate?

A.Bandwidth decreases
B.Bandwidth increases ✅
C.Bandwidth unchanged
D.Bandwidth becomes infinite
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Increasing the carrier frequency raises the frequency deviation required for a given modulation index, which in turn widens the occupied spectrum. Consequently, the required bandwidth grows, typically following Carson’s rule, which is proportional to the sum of the carrier deviation and the modulating frequency.

📖 Explanation: The low‑pass filter will suppress much of the carrier’s spectral components, leaving only a weakened version of the baseband signal. This attenuation distorts the amplitude levels that represent the digital bits, leading to errors in the recovered data.

Q8. Compare amplitude modulation (AM) and frequency modulation (FM) regarding susceptibility to additive white Gaussian noise. Which statement is correct?

A.AM is more resistant to noise than FM ✅
B.FM is more resistant to noise than AM
C.Both are equally susceptible to noise
D.Neither is affected by noise
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: FM conveys information in frequency deviations, which are less affected by amplitude fluctuations caused by noise. Consequently, FM generally offers better noise immunity than AM, where the information is carried directly in amplitude and is therefore more vulnerable to noise.

Q9. Evaluate the trade‑off between using amplitude versus phase modulation for a DAC in terms of implementation complexity and spectral efficiency. Which option best describes the relationship?

A.Amplitude modulation is simpler but less spectrally efficient ✅
B.Phase modulation is simpler but less spectrally efficient
C.Amplitude modulation is both simpler and more efficient
D.Phase modulation is more complex but more efficient
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Amplitude modulation requires only a variable gain stage, making the hardware straightforward, but it uses a single‑bit symbol and thus wastes spectral capacity. Phase modulation needs precise phase‑control circuitry, increasing complexity, yet it can encode multiple bits per symbol, improving spectral efficiency.

Q10. Given two DAC designs—one using amplitude modulation with a linear amplifier, the other using phase modulation with a voltage‑controlled oscillator— which design is likely to exhibit lower harmonic distortion and why?

A.The amplitude‑modulated design because of its linearity
B.The phase‑modulated design because VCOs are inherently nonlinear
C.Both designs produce equal distortion
D.The phase‑modulated design because phase changes produce less amplitude variation ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Phase modulation alters the instantaneous angle of the carrier without changing its magnitude, so the output waveform remains sinusoidal. Harmonic distortion mainly arises from amplitude non‑linearity; therefore, a phase‑modulated DAC typically generates a cleaner spectrum than an amplitude‑modulated counterpart.

Q11. Contrast the effect on power consumption when increasing the carrier frequency for FM versus increasing the amplitude for AM, assuming the same data rate. Which statement is accurate?

B.Increasing AM amplitude consumes more power ✅
C.Both increase power consumption equally
D.Increasing FM carrier frequency consumes less power
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Power in an AM system is proportional to the square of the amplitude (P∝A2P \propto A^{2}P∝A2). Raising the amplitude therefore raises power substantially. In FM, raising carrier frequency mainly changes the signal’s spectral position without directly affecting the transmitted power, so the power impact is far smaller.

Q12. Differentiate between a DAC that modulates frequency and one that modulates amplitude in terms of required post‑conversion filtering. Which filter type is appropriate for each?

A.Frequency DAC needs a low‑pass filter
B.Amplitude DAC needs a band‑pass filter
C.Frequency DAC needs a band‑pass filter ✅
D.Amplitude DAC needs no filter
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A frequency‑modulated output contains the carrier plus sidebands spread around the carrier frequency, requiring a band‑pass filter to isolate the desired band. In contrast, an amplitude‑modulated output can be recovered with a low‑pass filter that removes high‑frequency carrier components, leaving the baseband signal.

Q13. Apply the principle of sine‑wave characteristics: If a digital system varies only the phase of a carrier to represent bits, which characteristic remains constant?

📖 Explanation: When only phase is altered, the carrier’s amplitude and frequency stay unchanged. The information is encoded solely in the instantaneous angle of the sine wave, leaving the magnitude of the waveform identical for all symbols.

Q17. Explain why phase modulation can be more spectrally efficient than amplitude modulation for the same data rate. Which reason is correct?

A.Because phase changes require less bandwidth
B.Because amplitude changes need larger voltage swings
C.Because FM inherently occupies more bandwidth
D.Because phase modulation allows more bits per symbol ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D
