Q1. What does the abbreviation FM stand for in communication systems?

🔹 A. Fast Modulation
🔹 B. Frequency Modulation
🔹 C. Fading Modulation
🔹 D. Fixed Modulation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Frequency Modulation (FM) refers to the technique where the instantaneous frequency of a carrier wave varies in proportion to the amplitude of the input (modulating) signal. This definition distinguishes FM from other schemes such as amplitude modulation, and is the standard meaning used in radio and communication contexts.

Q2. If the amplitude of the modulating signal is doubled while the carrier frequency remains constant, how does the maximum frequency deviation change?

🔹 A. It remains the same
🔹 B. It doubles
🔹 C. It halves
🔹 D. It quadruples

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

In FM, the peak frequency deviation Δf is directly proportional to the amplitude of the modulating signal. Therefore, doubling the amplitude causes Δf to double as well, while the carrier frequency stays unchanged. This relationship is fundamental for designing FM systems with desired deviation characteristics.

Q3. Which of the following best compares FM and AM regarding susceptibility to noise in a typical terrestrial channel?

🔹 A. FM has higher noise susceptibility than AM
🔹 B. Both have equal susceptibility
🔹 C. FM is less susceptible because noise primarily affects amplitude
🔹 D. AM is less susceptible because it uses a constant amplitude

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Noise in terrestrial channels usually appears as amplitude fluctuations. Since FM conveys information through frequency variations, amplitude noise has minimal impact, making FM more robust. AM, on the other hand, directly encodes information in amplitude, so any amplitude disturbance degrades the received signal.

Q4. Using Carson's rule, what is the approximate total bandwidth required for an FM signal with a peak deviation of \(75\ \text{kHz}\) and a maximum modulating frequency of \(15\ \text{kHz}\)?

🔹 A. 180 kHz
🔹 B. 210 kHz
🔹 C. 240 kHz
🔹 D. 300 kHz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Carson's rule states that the bandwidth \(BW \approx 2(\Delta f + f_m)\). Substituting \(\Delta f = 75\ \text{kHz}\) and \(f_m = 15\ \text{kHz}\) gives \(BW = 2(75+15) = 180\ \text{kHz}\). This estimate captures the majority of the signal's spectral energy.

Q5. A carrier at \(100\ \text{MHz}\) is frequency‑modulated by a sinusoidal tone of \(5\ \text{kHz}\). Which pair of frequencies represents the first upper and lower sidebands generated by this modulation?

🔹 A. 100.005 MHz and 99.995 MHz
🔹 B. 100.010 MHz and 99.990 MHz
🔹 C. 100.5 MHz and 99.5 MHz
🔹 D. 105 MHz and 95 MHz

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In FM, the first-order sidebands appear at the carrier frequency plus and minus the modulating frequency. Adding and subtracting \(5\ \text{kHz}\) from \(100\ \text{MHz}\) yields \(100.005\ \text{MHz}\) (upper) and \(99.995\ \text{MHz}\) (lower), representing the closest spectral components to the carrier.

Q6. Increasing the modulation index from 0.5 to 5 in a wideband FM system primarily affects which aspect of the transmitted signal?

🔹 A. Reduces the required transmission power
🔹 B. Narrows the occupied bandwidth
🔹 C. Expands the spectrum with more significant sidebands
🔹 D. Eliminates phase distortion

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The modulation index \(β = \Delta f / f_m\) determines how many sidebands carry appreciable power. Raising \(β\) from 0.5 to 5 dramatically increases the number and amplitude of higher‑order sidebands, thereby expanding the occupied spectrum while improving noise performance.

Q7. Design an FM transmitter that must not exceed a total bandwidth of \(200\ \text{kHz}\). If the highest audio frequency is \(5\ \text{kHz}\), what is the maximum allowable peak deviation?

🔹 A. 45 kHz
🔹 B. 95 kHz
🔹 C. 105 kHz
🔹 D. 120 kHz

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Carson's rule gives \(BW = 2(\Delta f + f_m)\). Solving for \(\Delta f\) with \(BW = 200\ \text{kHz}\) and \(f_m = 5\ \text{kHz}\) yields \(\Delta f = 100 - 5 = 95\ \text{kHz}\). This deviation ensures the signal stays within the prescribed bandwidth limit.

Q8. What is the most likely outcome if the instantaneous frequency deviation exceeds the maximum allowed deviation specified for a given FM system?

🔹 A. The carrier frequency will shift permanently
🔹 B. Spectral regrowth causing adjacent channel interference
🔹 C. The modulation index will drop to zero
🔹 D. The system will become amplitude modulated

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When deviation surpasses the design limit, the FM spectrum spreads beyond its allocated channel, producing unwanted sidebands that can encroach on neighboring channels. This phenomenon, known as spectral regrowth, degrades system coexistence and may violate regulatory spectral masks.

Q9. How does narrowband FM differ from wideband FM in terms of modulation index and resulting bandwidth?

🔹 A. Narrowband FM has a larger modulation index and wider bandwidth
🔹 B. Both have identical modulation indices but different carrier frequencies
🔹 C. Narrowband FM uses a small modulation index, resulting in bandwidth close to twice the modulating frequency
🔹 D. Wideband FM reduces bandwidth by limiting deviation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Narrowband FM (NBFM) operates with a modulation index \(β \ll 1\). Consequently, its bandwidth is approximately \(2f_m\), only slightly larger than the modulating frequency. Wideband FM employs a larger \(β\), producing many sidebands and a substantially wider occupied spectrum.

Q10. For an FM system operating in a noisy environment, which relationship between modulation index \(β\) and signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) is most advantageous?

🔹 A. SNR improves linearly with decreasing \(β\)
🔹 B. SNR is independent of \(β\)
🔹 C. Higher \(β\) generally yields better SNR up to a practical limit
🔹 D. Lower \(β\) eliminates noise completely

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In FM, a larger modulation index spreads the signal power over more sidebands, which enhances the system’s ability to average out amplitude noise, thereby improving the effective SNR. Practical limits exist due to bandwidth constraints, but within those limits, increasing \(β\) is beneficial.

Q11. An FM signal’s spectrum shows significant sidebands up to the 7th order. Which approximate modulation index does this observation suggest?

🔹 A. \(β \approx 0.5\)
🔹 B. \(β \approx 1\)
🔹 C. \(β \approx 3\)
🔹 D. \(β \approx 7\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The number of observable sidebands roughly corresponds to the modulation index plus one. Detecting sidebands up to the 7th order implies a modulation index near 6–7, indicating a relatively wide deviation and a broad spectral footprint.

Q12. When two FM transmitters operate at carrier frequencies of \(90\ \text{MHz}\) and \(110\ \text{MHz}\) respectively, which one is more affected by a Doppler shift caused by a moving receiver?

🔹 A. Both are affected equally
🔹 B. The 90 MHz signal experiences greater absolute frequency shift
🔹 C. The 110 MHz signal experiences greater absolute frequency shift
🔹 D. Doppler shift does not depend on carrier frequency

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Doppler shift \(Δf = (v/c) f_c\) is proportional to the carrier frequency \(f_c\). Therefore, a higher carrier frequency (110 MHz) experiences a larger absolute frequency change for the same relative motion, making it more susceptible to Doppler‑induced errors.

Q13. Why is FM generally more resistant to amplitude‑based noise compared to AM?

🔹 A. Because FM encodes information in amplitude variations
🔹 B. Because noise adds only phase errors which are ignored
🔹 C. Because information is conveyed by frequency variations, so amplitude fluctuations have little effect
🔹 D. Because FM uses higher carrier power

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In FM the transmitted information resides in variations of the carrier’s instantaneous frequency. Amplitude noise, which primarily alters signal strength, does not change the frequency trajectory, so the demodulated audio remains largely unaffected, granting FM superior noise immunity over AM.

Q14. If an FM broadcast must accommodate a stereo audio signal with a 15 kHz pilot tone, what is the minimum required deviation ratio to ensure proper transmission of both left‑right channels without exceeding a 300 kHz bandwidth?

🔹 A. Deviation ratio of 5
🔹 B. Deviation ratio of 10
🔹 C. Deviation ratio of 15
🔹 D. Deviation ratio of 20

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

For stereo FM, the highest baseband component is about \(15\ \text{kHz}\) (pilot) plus the L‑R subcarrier at \(38\ \text{kHz}\). Using Carson’s rule, \(BW = 2(\Delta f + 53\ \text{kHz}) \le 300\ \text{kHz}\) gives \(\Delta f \le 97\ \text{kHz}\). The deviation ratio \(\Delta f / f_m\) ≈ \(97/15 \approx 6.5\), so a ratio of 10 comfortably satisfies the bandwidth constraint while providing adequate deviation.
