What is Frequency Shift Keying during Analog Transmission?

Frequency Shift Keying (FSK) is a digital-to-analog modulation method that encodes binary data by shifting the frequency of a carrier wave between two distinct frequencies, where one frequency represents a binary 1 and the other represents a binary 0, offering better noise immunity than ASK because amplitude variations caused by interference do not affect the frequency-based detection at the receiver.

Q1. What does FSK stand for?

A. Frequency Shift Keying ✅
B. Fast Signal Kinetics
C. Frequency Signal Kinematics
D. Fourier Signal Kernel

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Frequency Shift Keying is the correct expansion of the acronym FSK. It denotes a modulation method where binary data is conveyed by shifting the carrier frequency between two distinct values, typically representing logical ‘0’ and ‘1’. This definition is fundamental to understanding the technique.

Q2. In binary FSK, which pair of frequencies typically represents a logical ‘0’ and ‘1’?

A. \(f_c\) and \(f_c+Δf\)
B. \(f_c-Δf\) and \(f_c+Δf\) ✅
C. \(f_c\) and \(f_c-Δf\)
D. \(f_c-2Δf\) and \(f_c+2Δf\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Binary FSK uses two carrier frequencies spaced by a deviation Δf. The lower frequency (\(f_c-Δf\)) encodes a ‘0’, while the higher frequency (\(f_c+Δf\)) encodes a ‘1’. Selecting these symmetric frequencies simplifies demodulation and maintains a constant average power, which is essential for reliable digital communication.

Q3. The frequency deviation in FSK is commonly denoted by which symbol?

A. \(δf\)
B. \(Δf\)
C. \(f_d\)
D. \(γ\) ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

In the literature on frequency shift keying, the symbol \(Δf\) is conventionally used to represent the frequency deviation between the two carrier tones. This notation appears in bandwidth calculations and performance analyses, making it a standard reference for engineers designing FSK systems.

Q4. If the frequency separation \(Δf\) is doubled while keeping the data rate constant, what is the most likely impact on the required transmission bandwidth?

A. It remains unchanged
B. It decreases slightly
C. It doubles ✅
D. It increases but less than double

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Doubling the frequency separation directly widens the spacing between the two carrier tones. According to Carson's rule, the total bandwidth is roughly twice the sum of the carrier deviation and the modulating signal bandwidth, so a larger \(Δf\) roughly doubles the contribution from the deviation, leading to a near‑doubling of overall bandwidth.

Q5. When interference causes the received frequency to drift upward by \(0.1Δf\), which logical decision becomes most error‑prone?

A. Detecting a ‘0’ as a ‘1’
B. Detecting a ‘1’ as a ‘0’ ✅
C. Both decisions are equally affected
D. Neither decision is affected

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

An upward drift reduces the separation between the intended ‘1’ frequency (\(f_c+Δf\)) and the ‘0’ frequency (\(f_c-Δf\)). Consequently, the receiver is more likely to misinterpret a transmitted ‘1’ as a ‘0’, because the shifted ‘1’ tone moves closer to the ‘0’ tone, increasing the probability of error.

Q6. Assuming ideal band‑limited channels, how does increasing the data rate while keeping \(Δf\) constant affect the probability of symbol error in coherent FSK detection?

A. Error probability decreases
B. Error probability remains constant
C. Error probability increases
D. Error probability first decreases then increases ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

When data rate rises, the symbol duration shortens, reducing the time for accurate frequency discrimination. With a fixed frequency deviation, the signal‑to‑noise ratio per symbol declines, causing the probability of symbol error to rise. Coherent detection relies on sufficient observation time to resolve the frequency shift, which is compromised at higher rates.

Q7. If a non‑coherent detector is used for binary FSK, which performance trade‑off is most accurate compared to a coherent detector?

A. Higher bandwidth efficiency
B. Lower bit‑error rate
C. Higher tolerance to phase noise ✅
D. Reduced implementation complexity

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Non‑coherent detectors do not track the carrier phase, making them less sensitive to phase noise and frequency offsets. While they typically exhibit a higher bit‑error rate than coherent detectors, their robustness to phase variations and simpler hardware make them attractive for environments where phase stability cannot be guaranteed.

Q8. Consider an FSK system where the carrier frequencies are \(f_0\) and \(f_0+2Δf\). How does this affect the minimum Euclidean distance between signal points compared to conventional binary FSK?

A. It doubles ✅
B. It remains the same
C. It halves
D. It increases by \(\sqrt{2}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Increasing the separation between the two carrier frequencies to \(2Δf\) widens the spectral distance, effectively doubling the Euclidean distance between the corresponding signal vectors in the frequency domain. A larger distance improves error performance because the receiver can more reliably distinguish the two tones, assuming equal power and noise conditions.

Q9. When the symbol rate equals the frequency deviation (\(R_s = Δf\)), which statement best describes the spectral efficiency of binary FSK?

A. It is maximized
B. It is minimized
C. It equals that of BPSK ✅
D. It is independent of \(Δf\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Spectral efficiency, measured in bits per second per Hertz, for binary FSK with \(R_s = Δf\) yields the same efficiency as binary phase‑shift keying (BPSK). Both schemes occupy roughly the same bandwidth under this condition, making their efficiencies comparable despite differing modulation principles.

Q10. If a channel introduces a constant frequency offset of \(0.25Δf\), which receiver design modification most effectively restores correct demodulation?

A. Increase the detection bandwidth
B. Add a frequency‑offset correction loop ✅
C. Switch to amplitude shift keying
D. Use a higher‑order FSK scheme

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A frequency‑offset correction loop (often implemented as a phase‑locked loop) can track and compensate for the constant shift, aligning the receiver’s local oscillator with the incoming carrier frequencies. This adjustment preserves the intended frequency separation, allowing the detector to correctly distinguish between the logical ‘0’ and ‘1’ tones.

Q11. In a scenario where the transmitted FSK signal experiences Rayleigh fading, which modulation order provides the most resilience to deep fades?

A. Binary FSK ✅
B. 4‑FSK
C. 8‑FSK
D. 16‑FSK

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Binary FSK, with only two distinct frequencies, offers the greatest resilience under Rayleigh fading because the probability that both tones simultaneously experience deep fades is lower than for higher‑order FSK schemes. As the modulation order increases, the likelihood that at least one tone is severely attenuated rises, degrading overall reliability.

Q12. When employing Gaussian‑filtered binary FSK (GFSK) for Bluetooth Low Energy, what is the primary advantage of the Gaussian filter?

A. It reduces peak power
B. It narrows the occupied bandwidth ✅
C. It eliminates inter‑symbol interference
D. It increases the frequency deviation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Gaussian filter smooths the abrupt frequency transitions inherent in binary FSK, effectively limiting the spectral side‑lobes and compressing the occupied bandwidth. This narrower spectrum enables more efficient use of the limited 2 MHz ISM band allocated for Bluetooth Low Energy, while still preserving adequate data integrity.

Q13. In comparing binary FSK to binary PSK, which factor most strongly influences the choice of modulation for a low‑power sensor network?

A. Maximum data rate
B. Power consumption per bit ✅
C. Resistance to multipath fading
D. Implementation cost

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Low‑power sensor networks prioritize minimizing energy use, and binary FSK typically requires less complex circuitry and lower peak power than binary PSK, especially when non‑coherent detection is acceptable. While PSK may offer better spectral efficiency, the power savings of FSK often dominate design decisions for battery‑operated devices.

Q14. How does the required signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) for a target bit‑error rate of \(10^{-5}\) compare between coherent binary FSK and non‑coherent binary FSK?

A. Coherent requires higher SNR
B. Both require identical SNR
C. Non‑coherent requires about 2 dB higher SNR ✅
D. Non‑coherent requires about 6 dB higher SNR

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Non‑coherent detection lacks phase information, leading to a performance penalty. For a BER of \(10^{-5}\), the non‑coherent binary FSK typically needs roughly 2 dB more SNR than its coherent counterpart to achieve the same error probability, reflecting the trade‑off between complexity and performance.

Q15. When the carrier frequency \(f_c\) is doubled while keeping the frequency deviation \(Δf\) constant, what is the effect on the relative bandwidth efficiency of binary FSK?

A. Efficiency doubles
B. Efficiency halves
C. Efficiency remains unchanged ✅
D. Efficiency improves slightly

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Bandwidth efficiency depends on the ratio of data rate to carrier frequency. Doubling \(f_c\) without altering \(Δf\) or the data rate does not change the required bandwidth, so the relative efficiency—bits per Hertz—remains essentially the same, assuming the same modulation index.

Q16. If a system designer replaces binary FSK with 4‑ary FSK while maintaining the same symbol rate, how does the required bandwidth change?

A. It halves
B. It stays the same
C. It doubles
D. It increases by 1.5× ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Increasing the modulation order to 4‑ary FSK introduces three frequency separations instead of one, effectively expanding the occupied spectrum. With the same symbol rate, the bandwidth roughly scales with the number of distinct tones, leading to an approximate 1.5‑times increase compared with binary FSK.

Q17. Consider two FSK transmitters: one uses a modulation index \(h = 0.5\), the other uses \(h = 1.0\). Which transmitter exhibits a lower probability of error for a given SNR?

A. The one with \(h = 0.5\)

C. The one with \(h = 1.0\) ✅
D. Error probability depends only on data rate

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A larger modulation index increases the frequency separation between logical symbols, enhancing distinguishability at the receiver. Consequently, the transmitter with \(h = 1.0\) provides a greater Euclidean distance between signal points, yielding a lower error probability for the same signal‑to‑noise ratio.

Q18. When evaluating the power spectral density of binary FSK, which component dominates the shape of the spectrum?

A. The carrier amplitude
B. The frequency deviation \(Δf\) ✅
C. The symbol duration
D. The phase noise

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The frequency deviation determines how far the two tones are spaced from the central carrier, directly influencing the width and side‑lobe structure of the power spectral density. Larger \(Δf\) spreads energy over a broader frequency range, making it the dominant factor shaping the spectrum.

Q19. How does the principle of orthogonality apply to multi‑tone FSK schemes such as 8‑FSK?

A. All tones share the same phase
B. Each tone is a time‑shifted replica
C. Tones are spaced to maintain zero cross‑correlation ✅
D. Orthogonal tones are not required

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In higher‑order FSK, orthogonal tones are selected so that the integral of the product of any two distinct carrier sinusoids over a symbol interval equals zero. This zero cross‑correlation ensures that each tone can be distinguished without interference, enabling reliable demodulation of multiple bits per symbol.

Q20. Why might an engineer choose continuous‑phase FSK (CPFSK) over standard binary FSK for a mobile communication system?

A. It simplifies transmitter design
B. It reduces out‑of‑band emissions ✅
C. It improves resistance to Doppler shift
D. It increases data rate

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

CPFSK maintains phase continuity between successive symbols, which smooths the signal waveform and significantly reduces spectral side‑lobes. This lower out‑of‑band emission is advantageous in mobile environments where regulatory masks are strict and adjacent channel interference must be minimized.

Q21. In a digital communication link, what is the primary reason for preferring FSK over ASK when the channel experiences severe amplitude fading?

A. FSK offers higher data rates
B. FSK is less susceptible to amplitude variations ✅
C. ASK provides better spectral efficiency
D. FSK requires less bandwidth

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Frequency shift keying encodes information in frequency rather than amplitude, making it inherently robust against amplitude fading caused by multipath or shadowing. Since the receiver discriminates between distinct frequencies, variations in signal strength have less impact on the correct detection of logical symbols.

Q22. When designing an FSK modem for a power‑line communication system, which characteristic of the carrier frequencies is most critical to avoid interference with existing AC mains signals?

A. Choosing frequencies well below 50 Hz
B. Selecting frequencies far above 60 Hz
C. Ensuring carrier frequencies are integer multiples of the mains frequency
D. Using frequencies that do not overlap the harmonics of the mains ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Power‑line communication must coexist with the 50/60 Hz AC supply and its harmonics. Selecting carrier frequencies that avoid these harmonic frequencies minimizes coupling and interference, allowing the FSK signal to propagate without being masked by the dominant power‑line spectrum.

Q23. A binary FSK system operates with a carrier frequency of 2 GHz and a deviation of 50 kHz. What is the approximate minimum bandwidth required according to Carson’s rule?

A. 100 kHz
B. 150 kHz
C. 200 kHz ✅
D. 250 kHz

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Carson’s rule states that the total bandwidth ≈ 2(Δf + \(f_m\)), where \(f_m\) is the maximum modulating frequency, which for binary signaling equals half the data rate. Assuming a typical data rate of 100 kHz, the bandwidth ≈ 2(50 kHz + 50 kHz) = 200 kHz.

Q24. In an FSK system employing Gaussian filtering with a BT product of 0.5, how does the filter affect the inter‑symbol interference compared to an unfiltered FSK signal?

A. It eliminates interference completely
B. It reduces interference moderately ✅
C. It increases interference
D. It has no effect on interference

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A Gaussian filter with a BT product of 0.5 smooths frequency transitions, thereby reducing the abruptness of symbol changes. This smoothing lessens the overlap between adjacent symbols in the frequency domain, diminishing inter‑symbol interference while preserving most of the signal’s energy.

Q25. If the symbol duration is halved while the frequency deviation \(Δf\) is kept constant, what is the impact on the modulation index \(h = Δf / R_s\) (where \(R_s\) is the symbol rate)?

A. \(h\) doubles ✅
B. \(h\) halves
C. \(h\) remains unchanged
D. \(h\) becomes zero

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The modulation index \(h\) is defined as the ratio of frequency deviation to symbol rate (\(R_s\)). Halving the symbol duration doubles the symbol rate, thus increasing \(h\) proportionally. A larger index indicates greater frequency separation relative to symbol timing, affecting bandwidth and error performance.

Q26. When an FSK signal is transmitted over a channel with additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN), which detection method yields the lowest bit‑error rate?

A. Non‑coherent detection
B. Coherent detection with matched filtering ✅
C. Envelope detection
D. Zero‑crossing detection

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Coherent detection using a matched filter aligns the receiver’s reference phase with the incoming carrier, maximizing the signal‑to‑noise ratio for each frequency tone. This optimal processing minimizes the probability of symbol error in AWGN, outperforming non‑coherent and simpler detection schemes that lack phase information.

Q27. What is the primary advantage of using Minimum Shift Keying (MSK) over conventional binary FSK?

A. It doubles the data rate
B. It eliminates the need for a carrier
C. It provides constant envelope with minimum frequency separation ✅
D. It allows for amplitude modulation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

MSK is a special case of continuous‑phase FSK where the frequency deviation is set to half the data rate, yielding the smallest possible frequency separation while maintaining a constant envelope. This results in spectral efficiency close to that of PSK while retaining the robustness of FSK.

Q28. In a multi‑user environment employing FSK, how can orthogonal frequency allocation reduce mutual interference?

A. By using identical carrier frequencies for all users
B. By assigning each user a unique frequency offset that satisfies orthogonality conditions ✅
C. By time‑multiplexing users on the same frequency
D. By employing spread spectrum techniques

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Allocating distinct, orthogonal carrier frequencies to each user ensures that the cross‑correlation between any two users’ signals is zero over a symbol interval. This orthogonality prevents one user’s transmission from interfering with another’s, enabling simultaneous independent communication within the same spectral band.

Q29. When the carrier frequency is increased from 1 GHz to 4 GHz while maintaining the same absolute frequency deviation, how does the modulation index change?

A. It quadruples
B. It halves
C. It remains the same ✅
D. It doubles

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The modulation index \(h = Δf / R_s\) depends on the frequency deviation and symbol rate, not directly on the absolute carrier frequency. Keeping \(Δf\) and the symbol rate unchanged means the index stays constant, even though the carrier frequency itself has increased fourfold.

Q30. If an FSK transmitter uses a frequency deviation of 25 kHz and a data rate of 50 kbps, what is the modulation index \(h\)?

A. 0.25
B. 0.5 ✅
C. 1
D. 2

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The symbol rate \(R_s\) for binary signaling equals the data rate, 50 kbps. The modulation index \(h = Δf / R_s = 25 kHz / 50 kHz = 0.5\). This index reflects the ratio of frequency separation to symbol timing, influencing bandwidth and error performance.

Q31. In an FSK system subject to Doppler shift, which modulation parameter can be adjusted to mitigate the impact of the shift on detection accuracy?

A. Increase the carrier amplitude
B. Reduce the symbol duration
C. Increase the frequency deviation \(Δf\) ✅
D. Decrease the modulation index

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A larger frequency deviation widens the separation between the logical tones, making the system more tolerant to frequency offsets caused by Doppler effects. By increasing \(Δf\), the relative shift becomes a smaller proportion of the total separation, preserving detection reliability.

Q32. Why does binary FSK typically require a larger bandwidth than binary PSK for the same data rate?

A. FSK uses higher carrier frequencies
B. FSK’s frequency deviation adds extra spectral components ✅
C. PSK employs amplitude modulation
D. FSK signals have longer guard intervals

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Binary FSK introduces two distinct carrier frequencies separated by the deviation \(Δf\). This separation adds side‑bands to the spectrum, expanding the occupied bandwidth. In contrast, binary PSK varies only the phase of a single carrier, resulting in a more compact spectrum for the same data rate.

Q33. When implementing FSK on a low‑cost microcontroller, which design choice most significantly reduces power consumption?

B. Implementing non‑coherent detection ✅
C. Increasing the frequency deviation
D. Employing a high‑resolution DAC

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Non‑coherent detection eliminates the need for a phase‑locked loop or complex carrier recovery circuitry, simplifying the receiver architecture and lowering the overall power draw. This approach is especially advantageous in resource‑constrained microcontroller environments where energy efficiency is paramount.

Q34. In a scenario where the channel bandwidth is limited to 200 kHz, which FSK configuration maximizes data throughput while staying within the bandwidth constraint?

A. Binary FSK with \(Δf = 100 kHz\)
B. 4‑FSK with \(Δf = 25 kHz\)
C. 8‑FSK with \(Δf = 12.5 kHz\)
D. Binary FSK with \(Δf = 50 kHz\) ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Binary FSK requires a bandwidth roughly equal to \(2(Δf + R_s)\). Selecting \(Δf = 50 kHz\) and a symbol rate of 50 kbps yields a total bandwidth of about 200 kHz, fitting the constraint while providing the highest possible data rate among the listed options.

Q35. If an FSK system uses a modulation index \(h = 0.75\) and a symbol rate of 20 kbps, what is the required frequency deviation \(Δf\)?

A. 10 kHz
B. 15 kHz ✅
C. 20 kHz
D. 25 kHz

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The modulation index \(h = Δf / R_s\). Rearranging gives \(Δf = h \times R_s = 0.75 \times 20 kHz = 15 kHz\). This deviation defines the spacing between the two carrier frequencies, influencing both bandwidth and error performance.
