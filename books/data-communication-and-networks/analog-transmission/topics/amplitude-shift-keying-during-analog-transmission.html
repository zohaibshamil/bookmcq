What is Amplitude Shift Keying during Analog Transmission?

Amplitude Shift Keying (ASK) is a digital-to-analog modulation technique where the amplitude of a carrier wave is varied between two or more discrete levels to represent binary data, with one amplitude level typically representing a binary 1 and another level (often zero) representing a binary 0, making it simple to implement but highly susceptible to noise and attenuation during analog transmission.

B.Increased probability of detecting '0' correctly
C.Increased bit error rate ✅

Q2. Suppose bandwidth is limited, leading to intersymbol interference (ISI). How does ISI affect ASK performance?

A.It decreases required bandwidth
B.It improves signal amplitude discrimination
C.It raises the probability of symbol errors due to amplitude distortion ✅
D.It eliminates need for carrier synchronization
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Inter‑symbol interference causes the tail of one pulse to overlap the next, altering the amplitude at the sampling instant. In ASK, where information is encoded purely in amplitude, such distortion directly changes the perceived level, making the decision more error‑prone and thus raising the symbol‑error probability.

Q4. Compare ASK with FSK in terms of susceptibility to noise. Which statement is correct?

A.ASK is more susceptible to noise than FSK ✅
B.ASK is less susceptible than FSK
C.Both have equal susceptibility
D.Susceptibility depends solely on carrier frequency
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: ASK conveys information through amplitude changes, making it vulnerable to amplitude‑modulating noise such as AWGN. FSK, on the other hand, relies on frequency shifts, which are less affected by amplitude fluctuations. Consequently, for comparable signal conditions, ASK exhibits a higher susceptibility to noise than FSK.

Q5. Evaluate the bandwidth efficiency of ASK versus QAM given the same symbol rate. Which is more efficient?

A.ASK and QAM have identical bandwidth efficiency
B.ASK is more bandwidth efficient than QAM
C.QAM provides higher bandwidth efficiency than ASK ✅
D.Bandwidth efficiency cannot be compared without modulation index
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Bandwidth efficiency is measured as bits per second per Hertz. QAM conveys multiple bits per symbol by using both amplitude and phase, effectively transmitting more bits for the same symbol rate. ASK transmits only one bit per symbol, so its bits/Hz ratio is lower than that of QAM.

Q6. Differentiate coherent vs non-coherent detection in ASK regarding required SNR for a target BER of .

A.Coherent requires 2 dB higher SNR than non-coherent
B.Non-coherent requires 2 dB higher SNR than coherent
C.Both require identical SNR
D.Coherent requires about 3 dB lower SNR than non-coherent ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Coherent detection synchronizes the receiver’s phase with the transmitted carrier, allowing optimal demodulation and a lower required SNR for a given BER. Non‑coherent detection lacks phase alignment, which degrades performance and typically needs about 3 dB more SNR to achieve the same error rate.
