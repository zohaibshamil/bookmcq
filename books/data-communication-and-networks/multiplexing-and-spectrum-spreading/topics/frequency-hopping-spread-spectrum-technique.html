What is Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum Technique?

Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum is a spread spectrum method where the transmitter rapidly switches carrier frequencies according to a pseudorandom sequence known to both sender and receiver, making the signal resistant to narrowband interference and difficult to intercept.

📝 All Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum Technique MCQs

Q1. What does FHSS stand for?

A. Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum ✅
B. Frequency High-Speed Signaling
C. Fast Hopping Signal System
D. Fiber High Spectrum Service

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The acronym FHSS expands to Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum. This term describes the technique of rapidly changing the carrier frequency during transmission, which spreads the signal over a wider band and provides resistance to interference and eavesdropping. The other options are fabricated terms that do not correspond to the standard definition.

Q2. Typical hop rate in FHSS systems is in the range of:

A. 1–10 hops/s
B. 10–100 hops/s
C. 100–1000 hops/s ✅
D. 1000–10,000 hops/s

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Most practical FHSS implementations operate with hop rates between a few hundred and a few thousand hops per second, commonly expressed as 100–1000 hops per second. This range balances the need for rapid frequency changes with the limitations of hardware timing and synchronization, making option C the correct choice.

Q3. If a jammer stays on a single frequency, what is the most likely effect on an FHSS link?

A. Complete loss of communication
B. Brief interruption lasting one hop ✅
C. No effect at all
D. Improved signal quality

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Because FHSS hops away from any single frequency after a short dwell time, a jammer that occupies only one channel can disrupt at most one hop. The communication may experience a brief interruption during that hop, after which the system resumes on a different frequency, limiting the impact.

Q4. Increasing the number of hopping frequencies most directly reduces which of the following?

A. Transmission power required ✅
B. Probability of collision with other users
C. Latency of each packet
D. Complexity of modulation scheme

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When more frequencies are available for hopping, each transmission occupies a smaller fraction of the total set, thereby decreasing the chance that two users select the same channel at the same time. This reduction in collision probability is the primary benefit of expanding the hop set.

Q5. A system uses FHSS with 50 channels each 1 MHz wide. What is the total hopping bandwidth?

A. 10 MHz
B. 25 MHz
C. 50 MHz ✅
D. 100 MHz

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The hopping bandwidth is the product of the number of channels and the bandwidth per channel. With 50 channels × 1 MHz per channel, the total is 50 MHz, making option C the correct answer.

Q6. Compared to Direct‑Sequence Spread Spectrum (DSSS), FHSS is generally more resistant to which type of interference?

A. Wideband noise
B. Narrowband jamming
C. Multipath fading
D. Thermal noise ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

FHSS spreads the signal across many frequencies over time, making it especially effective against narrowband jamming that targets a single frequency. DSSS, by contrast, spreads the signal over a wide band simultaneously, offering better protection against wideband noise but less against narrowband attacks.

Q7. Which scenario most favors the use of FHSS over DSSS?

A. High‑speed video streaming in a controlled environment
B. Low‑power sensor networks operating in crowded ISM bands ✅
C. Long‑range satellite links with fixed frequency allocation
D. Fiber‑optic backbone connections

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

FHSS excels in environments with many potential interferers and limited power budgets, such as low‑power IoT devices in the ISM band. Its ability to hop away from interferers while using modest power makes it preferable to DSSS in this context.

Q8. How is time synchronization between transmitter and receiver typically achieved in FHSS?

A. Through a separate GPS signal
B. By exchanging a pre‑shared seed and using a deterministic pseudo‑random generator
C. Via continuous phase‑locked loops on each hop ✅
D. Through manual configuration before each session

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Both ends generate the same hopping sequence from a shared seed, but they must also align their hop timing. This alignment is usually accomplished with a phase‑locked loop that tracks the hop clock, ensuring that both devices change frequencies simultaneously.

Q9. If the hopping sequence is pseudo‑random but known to both ends, how does security depend on it?

A. Security relies solely on the sequence length ✅
B. Security is provided by the difficulty of predicting future hops without the seed
C. Security is independent of the sequence and depends on encryption only
D. Security increases with the number of hops per second

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Even though the sequence is deterministic, an outsider without the initial seed cannot predict the next hop. Thus, the secrecy of the seed (or key) provides the primary security, making the difficulty of predicting future hops the critical factor.

Q10. A FHSS system hops at rate \(R_h = 200\) hops/s over 100 channels. What is the average dwell time per channel?

A. 5 ms
B. 0.5 ms ✅
C. 0.05 ms
D. 0.005 ms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The dwell time equals the inverse of the hop rate: \(1 / 200\) s = 0.005 s, which is 5 ms. However, because the system cycles through 100 channels, each individual channel is visited once every 0.5 s, giving an average occupancy of 0.5 ms per hop. Option B reflects this calculation.

Q11. If the hop set includes 250 channels and each channel occupies 5 MHz, what is the total spectrum required?

A. 250 MHz
B. 1250 MHz ✅
C. 500 MHz
D. 125 MHz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Total spectrum equals the number of channels multiplied by the bandwidth per channel: 250 × 5 MHz = 1250 MHz. However, FHSS does not need to occupy the entire span simultaneously; it only requires the bandwidth of a single channel at any instant, which is 5 MHz. The question asks for the required span to accommodate all hops, so 1250 MHz (option B) is correct.

Q12. In a high multipath delay‑spread environment, FHSS mitigates intersymbol interference by:

A. Increasing transmit power
B. Reducing symbol duration
C. Changing carrier frequency each hop ✅
D. Using error‑correcting codes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

By hopping to a new frequency for each symbol (or group of symbols), FHSS ensures that the multipath components from one hop do not align with symbols on the next hop, effectively decorrelating the interference and reducing intersymbol interference.

Q13. How does increasing the hop rate affect system throughput and latency?

A. Higher hop rate increases throughput but also raises latency
B. Higher hop rate reduces both throughput and latency
C. Higher hop rate improves throughput while slightly increasing latency ✅
D. Higher hop rate decreases throughput and latency equally

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A faster hop rate allows more frequent use of fresh spectral resources, which can raise the effective data rate (throughput). However, each hop requires synchronization overhead, slightly increasing the end‑to‑end delay (latency). Therefore, option C captures the trade‑off accurately.

Q14. What is the trade‑off between hop set size and probability of interception?

A. Larger hop set reduces interception probability but increases hardware complexity ✅
B. Larger hop set increases interception probability and reduces bandwidth efficiency
C. Smaller hop set improves security but requires more power
D. Hop set size does not affect interception probability

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A larger hop set provides more possible frequencies, making it harder for an adversary to guess the current channel, thereby lowering the chance of successful interception. The downside is increased complexity in managing a larger set, which is reflected in option A, the correct answer.

Q15. Two FHSS radios use different pseudo‑random sequences over a 100‑channel set. What is the probability that they collide on the same channel in a single hop?

A. Jan-50
B. 1/100 ✅
C. 1/200
D. 1/250

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

If each radio independently selects one of 100 channels, the probability that both choose the same channel is \(1/100\). The choice of different sequences does not affect the per‑hop collision probability, making option B the correct answer.

Q16. How does FHSS support multiple users in the same band without causing excessive collisions?

A. By assigning each user a unique fixed frequency
B. Through coordinated time‑division multiplexing
C. By using different pseudo‑random hopping sequences for each user ✅
D. By limiting each user to a single channel per day

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When each user employs a distinct pseudo‑random hopping pattern, the likelihood that two users occupy the same frequency at the same instant is reduced. This statistical separation allows many users to share the same spectral band while keeping collision rates acceptably low.

Q17. Explain how FHSS can be combined with adaptive power control to improve link reliability.

A. By fixing power and varying hop rate ✅
B. By increasing power on congested channels only
C. By reducing hop set size when power is low
D. By disabling hopping when power is low

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Adaptive power control adjusts transmit power based on channel conditions, while FHSS provides frequency diversity. Together, they allow the system to boost power on channels experiencing high loss and simultaneously hop away from persistently poor frequencies, enhancing overall reliability without excessive interference.

Q18. A jammer can jam up to 10 % of the hopping set simultaneously. If the FHSS system uses 200 channels, what is the probability that a given transmission is jammed?

A. 0.1 ✅
B. 0.05
C. 0.2
D. 0.15

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Ten percent of 200 channels equals 20 jammed frequencies. The probability that the transmitter selects one of those jammed frequencies on a given hop is \(20/200 = 0.1\), or 10 %, which corresponds to option A.

Q19. If the hop duration is \(t_h = 2\) ms and the propagation delay is 0.5 ms, what is the maximum allowable processing delay to maintain synchronization?

A. 1.5 ms ✅
B. 2.0 ms
C. 2.5 ms
D. 3.0 ms

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To keep the total time within the hop duration, the processing delay must satisfy \(t_{proc} + t_{prop} \le t_h\). Substituting \(t_{proc} + 0.5 \le 2\) ms yields \(t_{proc} \le 1.5\) ms. Therefore, the maximum processing delay is 1.5 ms, option A.

Q20. Doubling the FHSS hop rate while keeping SNR constant most likely results in which BER change?

A. BER halves
B. BER doubles
C. BER remains unchanged
D. BER increases slightly due to reduced dwell time ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Increasing the hop rate reduces the time spent on each frequency, which can diminish the energy per symbol and make the receiver more vulnerable to noise fluctuations. Even though the SNR is unchanged, the shorter dwell time leads to a modest rise in bit‑error rate, making option D correct.

Q21. Given a hop set of 128 frequencies, what minimum number of hops must a receiver monitor to keep interception probability below 0.001?

A. 8 hops ✅
B. 16 hops
C. 32 hops
D. 64 hops

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The interception probability after monitoring \(k\) hops is approximately \(k/128\). To keep this below 0.001, solve \(k/128 < 0.001\) → \(k < 0.128\). Since at least one hop must be observed, the practical approach is to monitor a small fraction; however, the closest option that satisfies the constraint is 16 hops, yielding a probability of \(16/128 = 0.125\), which is still above 0.001. Therefore, the correct answer is 8 hops (option A).

Q22. Which scenario best illustrates FHSS combined with MIMO to enhance both spectral efficiency and anti‑jamming capability?

A. Using a single antenna with rapid hopping only
B. Employing multiple antennas that transmit the same hop sequence on all ports
C. Utilizing multiple antennas each following independent hopping patterns, enabling spatial multiplexing and diversity against jamming ✅
D. Applying MIMO without hopping, relying solely on beamforming

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When each MIMO antenna follows its own pseudo‑random hopping pattern, the system gains spatial multiplexing (increasing spectral efficiency) and spatial diversity (providing resilience against jamming on any single frequency). This dual benefit aligns with option C, making it the correct description.

Q23. Why is FHSS particularly suitable for low‑power IoT devices operating in crowded ISM bands?

A. Because FHSS eliminates the need for any regulatory compliance
B. Because FHSS allows devices to transmit at high power without interference
C. Because FHSS spreads transmissions over many channels, reducing the chance of persistent interference while keeping average power low, meeting ISM‑band regulations ✅
D. Because FHSS requires complex, power‑hungry hardware unsuitable for IoT

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

FHSS’s ability to quickly hop among many narrow channels means each transmission occupies a small fraction of time on any given frequency, limiting interference and complying with duty‑cycle limits imposed on ISM bands. This approach also permits low transmit power, making FHSS ideal for energy‑constrained IoT devices. Option C correctly captures these advantages.
