What is Multiplexing techniques and its role in Physical Layer?

Multiplexing is a physical layer method that combines multiple signals into one shared medium to maximize bandwidth efficiency, allowing several users or data streams to transmit simultaneously over a single communication channel without interference.

Q1. Which of the following best defines multiplexing in data communications?

A.Combining multiple signals into one transmission medium to share bandwidth. ✅
B.Increasing the transmission speed of a single signal.
C.Dividing a signal into smaller packets for error correction.
D.Encrypting data before transmission.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Multiplexing is the technique of combining several independent data streams onto a single physical medium, allowing them to share the available bandwidth. The definition that mentions combining multiple signals to share bandwidth directly captures this idea, making option A the correct choice.

Q2. A network requires simultaneous transmission of voice (64 kbps) and video (4 Mbps) over a single link with 5 Mbps capacity. Which multiplexing method is most appropriate?

A.Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM)
B.Time Division Multiplexing (TDM)
C.Code Division Multiple Access (CDMA)
D.Statistical Multiplexing ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Statistical multiplexing dynamically allocates bandwidth based on instantaneous demand, allowing low‑rate voice and high‑rate video to share the 5 Mbps link without fixed reservations. This flexibility makes it the most suitable technique for mixed traffic, whereas FDM, TDM, and CDMA would either waste bandwidth or require rigid allocation.

Q3. If a link has 100 MHz total bandwidth and three channels require 20 MHz, 30 MHz, and 40 MHz respectively, can Frequency Division Multiplexing be applied without exceeding the link capacity?

A.Yes, because the sum is 90 MHz which is less than 100 MHz. ✅
B.No, because guard bands will exceed the capacity.
C.Only if guard bands are zero.
D.Only if the channels are time‑shared.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The three channels together need 20 + 30 + 40 = 90 MHz, leaving 10 MHz margin for guard bands. Since the total required bandwidth is below the available 100 MHz, FDM can be implemented safely. Therefore, the statement that it is possible is correct.

Q4. In a TDM system with a frame size of 8 slots, a device requires a data rate of 2 Mbps. What is the minimum link bandwidth needed to support this device?

A.2 Mbps
B.4 Mbps
C.16 Mbps ✅
D.8 Mbps
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Each TDM slot provides an equal share of the link capacity. To deliver 2 Mbps in one slot, the total link must supply 2 Mbps × 8 = 16 Mbps. This ensures that the device can transmit its data in its allocated slot while the remaining slots serve other users.

Q5. Which advantage is unique to Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM) compared to Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM)?

A.Allows simultaneous transmission over the same fiber without electrical conversion ✅
B.Requires less guard band between channels
C.Works only in copper media
D.Reduces latency of each channel
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: WDM operates directly on optical wavelengths, enabling multiple light channels to travel together in a single fiber without converting the signals to electrical form. This optical‑only handling is a distinct benefit not shared by traditional FDM, which typically works in the electrical domain and needs conversion.

Q6. In Code Division Multiple Access (CDMA), orthogonal codes separate users. If two users transmit with codes that are not perfectly orthogonal, what is the likely effect?

A.Increased signal‑to‑noise ratio
B.Inter‑user interference ✅
C.Reduced bandwidth usage
D.Higher data rate
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When codes are not fully orthogonal, their cross‑correlation produces unwanted overlap, causing one user's signal to appear as noise to the other. This inter‑user interference degrades performance and can increase error rates, which is the primary consequence of imperfect code orthogonality.

Q7. Assume a TDM system with 5 channels each requiring 1 Mbps. If the link capacity is 4 Mbps, what must be done to accommodate all channels?

A.Increase the frame size
B.Reduce each channel's data rate
C.Use statistical multiplexing ✅
D.Add a second link
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Statistical multiplexing shares the link dynamically, allowing channels to transmit only when they have data, effectively increasing utilization beyond the static 4 Mbps limit. This approach can accommodate the five 1 Mbps streams without permanently allocating more bandwidth than is available.

Q8. Consider two scenarios: (1) Using FDM on a copper pair with 10 MHz bandwidth, (2) Using TDM on a fiber link with 10 Gbps capacity. Which scenario provides higher spectral efficiency (bits per Hz)?

A.Scenario 1
B.Scenario 2 ✅
C.Both are equal
D.Cannot be determined from the information given
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Spectral efficiency measures bits transmitted per unit of bandwidth. Fiber with 10 Gbps capacity over a very wide optical spectrum yields far more bits per Hertz than a copper link limited to 10 MHz, making the TDM‑on‑fiber scenario considerably more efficient.

Q9. When guard bands are introduced in Frequency Division Multiplexing, what is the primary trade‑off?

A.Increased latency
B.Reduced total usable bandwidth ✅
C.Higher error rate
D.Lower signal power
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Guard bands occupy a portion of the total spectrum to prevent adjacent channel interference. While they improve signal isolation, the space they consume reduces the amount of bandwidth available for actual data transmission, representing the main compromise when implementing guard bands.

Q10. If a TDM frame of 4 slots is transmitted every 1 ms, and two slots are corrupted, what fraction of the frame is lost?

A.0.1
B.0.25
C.0.5 ✅
D.0.75
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A frame consists of four equal slots; losing two of them removes half of the transmitted information. Therefore, the loss fraction is 2 / 4 = 0.5, or 50 %, which corresponds to option C.

Q11. Which configuration provides more flexibility for variable‑bit‑rate traffic?

A.TDM ✅
B.FDM
C.Both are equal
D.Neither
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Time Division Multiplexing allocates time slots on demand, allowing each user’s bitrate to change dynamically without altering the physical frequency plan. This adaptability makes TDM more suitable for variable‑bit‑rate applications, whereas FDM’s fixed frequency allocations are less responsive to traffic fluctuations.

Q12. Why can the aggregate required bandwidth in statistical multiplexing be less than the sum of peak rates of individual streams?

A.Because packets are compressed
B.Because error correction reduces data
C.Because guard bands are removed
D.Because not all streams peak simultaneously ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Statistical multiplexing relies on the observation that individual streams rarely reach their maximum rate at the same instant. By sharing the same channel and allocating bandwidth only when needed, the overall required capacity can be lower than the simple sum of all peak rates.

Q13. If a multiplexed system experiences a 5 % increase in total traffic, and the link's utilization was originally 70 %, what will the new utilization be?

A.0.735 ✅
B.0.7
C.0.75
D.0.8
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An increase of 5 % applied to the original utilization yields 70 % × 1.05 = 73.5 %. This arithmetic directly reflects the new proportion of the link’s capacity that is occupied after the traffic growth.

Q14. When implementing WDM over a single optical fiber, how does channel spacing affect system performance?

A.Larger spacing reduces crosstalk but limits number of channels ✅
B.Smaller spacing increases power
C.Spacing has no effect
D.Larger spacing increases attenuation
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Increasing the wavelength separation between channels lowers the risk of inter‑channel crosstalk, improving signal integrity. However, wider spacing reduces the total number of channels that can fit within the fiber’s usable spectrum, presenting a trade‑off between performance and capacity.

Q15. In a hybrid FDM/TDM system, each of 3 frequency bands carries 4 TDM slots of 1 Mbps each. If one frequency band fails, what is the remaining total capacity?

A.4 Mbps
B.8 Mbps ✅
C.12 Mbps
D.16 Mbps
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The full system provides 3 × 4 × 1 = 12 Mbps. Losing one band removes its 4 Mbps contribution, leaving 2 × 4 × 1 = 8 Mbps. Thus the remaining capacity after the failure is 8 Mbps, corresponding to option B.
