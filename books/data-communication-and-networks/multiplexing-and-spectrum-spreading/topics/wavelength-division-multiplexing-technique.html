What is Wavelength Division Multiplexing Technique?

Wavelength Division Multiplexing is a fiber-optic technique that combines multiple light signals of different wavelengths onto a single optical fiber, effectively acting as frequency division multiplexing for light to dramatically increase data capacity in high-speed networks.

📝 All Wavelength Division Multiplexing Technique MCQs

Q1. What does FDM stand for?

A.Frequency Division Multiplexing ✅
B.Fiber Data Modulation
C.Fast Digital Multiplexing
D.Frequency Direct Multiplex
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: FDM is the abbreviation for Frequency Division Multiplexing, a technique that divides a transmission medium into separate frequency bands for simultaneous data streams. The other options are fabricated terms that do not correspond to the standard definition used in communications engineering.

Q2. In FDM, each channel is allocated a distinct ______.

A.time slot
B.frequency band ✅
C.phase angle
D.amplitude level
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: FDM separates channels by assigning each a unique frequency band within the overall spectrum. Unlike time-division multiplexing, which uses time slots, FDM relies on frequency separation, making the term “frequency band” the correct completion of the sentence.

Q3. If two analog signals overlap in frequency, what is the most likely outcome in an FDM system?

A.Increased signal-to-noise ratio
B.Automatic channel separation
C.Interference causing distortion ✅
D.Signal amplification
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: When spectra overlap, the signals interfere with each other, producing distortion and making it impossible to recover the original data cleanly. This interference degrades the signal-to-noise ratio rather than improving it, and no automatic separation occurs without additional processing.

Q4. Given that digital signals are first converted to analog before FDM, what would be the effect of using a low‑pass filter with cutoff below the lowest carrier frequency?

A.All channels pass unchanged
B.No effect on any channel
C.All channels are completely blocked
D.Channels near cutoff are attenuated ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A low‑pass filter whose cutoff lies below the lowest carrier will attenuate the carrier components of each channel, especially those close to the cutoff frequency. This reduces signal amplitude and can cause loss of information, whereas channels well above the cutoff remain largely unaffected.

Q5. Suppose three channels are spaced 20 nm apart in wavelength, centered at 1550 nm, 1570 nm, and 1590 nm. If the source drifts by +5 nm, which channel(s) will experience overlap?

A.Only first
B.First and second ✅
C.Second and third
D.All three
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: After a +5 nm drift the wavelengths become 1555 nm, 1575 nm, and 1595 nm. Assuming each channel occupies ±10 nm, the first channel now reaches 1565 nm, exactly the lower edge of the second channel, causing overlap between the first and second channels.

Q6. Compare FDM and TDM in terms of bandwidth utilization for three channels each requiring 4 MHz. Which statement is true?

A.FDM uses 12 MHz total, TDM uses 4 MHz ✅
B.Both use 12 MHz
C.FDM uses 4 MHz, TDM uses 12 MHz
D.Both use 4 MHz
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: In FDM each channel occupies its own 4 MHz slice, so three channels need 12 MHz of spectrum. TDM shares a single 4 MHz slot sequentially, requiring only 4 MHz overall. Hence the first statement correctly describes the bandwidth consumption of the two multiplexing methods.

Q7. If the number of channels doubles from 3 to 6 while total available bandwidth stays constant, which effect is most accurate?

A.Each channel's bandwidth doubles
B.Overall signal‑to‑noise ratio improves
C.System latency increases
D.Channel spacing must decrease ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Keeping the total spectrum fixed while adding more channels forces each channel to occupy a narrower portion of the spectrum, meaning the spacing between adjacent channels must be reduced. This does not inherently improve SNR or latency, and individual channel bandwidth does not increase.

Q8. Differentiate analog FDM from digital FDM (baseband). Which characteristic distinguishes analog FDM?

A.Requires digital‑to‑analog conversion
B.Uses separate carrier frequencies ✅
C.Employs time slots
D.Needs error‑correction coding
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Analog FDM relies on assigning distinct carrier frequencies to each channel, allowing simultaneous transmission over the same medium. Digital baseband multiplexing typically uses time or code division, not separate carriers, making the use of separate carrier frequencies the defining trait of analog FDM.

Q9. Given two FDM systems: System X uses guard bands of 2 MHz between channels; System Y uses no guard bands but employs adaptive filtering. Which system is more likely to maintain channel integrity when channel count is high?

A.System X ✅
B.System Y
C.Both equal
D.Neither
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Guard bands provide a safety margin that prevents adjacent‑channel interference, which becomes increasingly critical as channel count rises. Adaptive filtering can mitigate interference but cannot guarantee isolation for many tightly packed channels, so the system with explicit guard bands (System X) better preserves channel integrity.

Q10. Apply the principle of superposition to explain why FDM can combine three analog signals without mutual interference if their spectra do not overlap. Which statement best captures this?

A.Signals add linearly, and non‑overlapping spectra remain separable ✅
B.Signals cancel each other
C.Signals are multiplied
D.Signals are encoded in phase only
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The superposition principle states that the sum of linear, non‑interacting signals equals their individual contributions. When each signal occupies a unique frequency band with no overlap, the combined signal can be decomposed back into the original components without cross‑talk, preserving data integrity.

Q11. If a digital signal is encoded using PCM with a sampling rate of 8 kHz before FDM, what is the minimum bandwidth required for each channel assuming Nyquist bandwidth?

A.4 kHz ✅
B.8 kHz
C.16 kHz
D.32 kHz
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: According to the Nyquist theorem, to avoid aliasing the highest frequency component must be less than half the sampling rate. With an 8 kHz sampling rate, the highest baseband frequency is 4 kHz, so each channel needs at least 4 kHz of bandwidth.

Q12. Synthesize a scenario where FDM is combined with WDM in an optical fiber network. What advantage does this hybrid approach provide?

A.Reduces need for optical amplifiers
B.Increases total number of independent channels ✅
C.Eliminates need for modulation
D.Simplifies network topology
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Combining FDM (frequency division) with WDM (wavelength division) multiplexes multiple frequency‑division groups onto distinct optical wavelengths, effectively multiplying the number of channels that can be carried simultaneously. This expands capacity without altering the underlying modulation schemes.

Q13. In a system where three channels are modulated onto carriers at wavelengths λ₁, λ₂, λ₃, derive the condition for non‑overlapping spectra given each channel occupies bandwidth Δλ. Which inequality must hold?

A.|λᵢ − λⱼ| > Δλ for all i≠j
B.|λᵢ − λⱼ| ≥ 2Δλ ✅
C.|λᵢ − λⱼ| ≤ Δλ
D.|λᵢ − λⱼ| = Δλ
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: To prevent spectral overlap, the separation between any two carrier wavelengths must be at least the sum of their half‑bandwidths, i.e., a full bandwidth Δλ on each side. This requirement translates to a minimum spacing of 2Δλ between carrier centers, ensuring the spectra remain distinct.

Q14. If guard band is reduced causing adjacent channels to partially overlap, what is the immediate effect on bit error rate?

A.BER decreases
B.BER unchanged
C.BER increases ✅
D.BER becomes zero
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Partial overlap introduces inter‑channel interference, which corrupts the received symbols and raises the probability of incorrect bit decisions. Consequently, the bit error rate rises, reflecting degraded performance; it does not decrease or remain unchanged, and it certainly does not drop to zero.

Q15. Compare FDM using fixed carriers versus OFDM regarding spectral efficiency. Which statement is true?

A.FDM is more spectrally efficient
B.OFDM is more spectrally efficient due to overlapping orthogonal subcarriers ✅
C.Both have equal efficiency
D.OFDM requires larger guard bands
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: OFDM arranges many closely spaced subcarriers that are mathematically orthogonal, allowing their spectra to overlap without interference, thereby achieving higher spectral efficiency than traditional FDM, which must maintain guard bands between fixed carriers.

Q16. Apply frequency planning: If the total available spectrum is 100 MHz and each channel requires 8 MHz plus a 2 MHz guard band, what is the maximum number of channels that can be accommodated?

A.8
B.9
C.10 ✅
D.11
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Each channel plus its guard band consumes 10 MHz of spectrum (8 MHz + 2 MHz). Dividing the total 100 MHz by 10 MHz per channel yields 10 channels that can be placed without exceeding the available bandwidth.
