What is Frequency Division Multiplexing Analog Technique?

Frequency Division Multiplexing is an analog multiplexing method that assigns each signal a unique frequency band within the total available spectrum, enabling multiple signals to travel at the same time over one medium by keeping their frequencies separated with guard bands.

Q3. FDM is applicable when the link bandwidth exceeds which of the following?

📖 Explanation: The fundamental condition for FDM is that the total available bandwidth must be larger than the combined bandwidths of all signals to be multiplexed, ensuring each channel can be allocated its required spectral slice without crowding.

Q4. In an analog FDM system, each input signal is combined with a distinct carrier. What type of modulation is most commonly used for this purpose?

B.Phase modulation
C.Frequency modulation
D.Pulse‑code modulation

📖 Explanation: Amplitude modulation (AM) is traditionally employed in analog FDM because it directly varies the carrier amplitude in proportion to the baseband signal, creating sidebands that occupy the allocated channel. Phase and frequency modulation are used in other multiplexing schemes, while PCM is a digital technique.

Q5. If three channels each require \(2\text{ MHz}\) of bandwidth and a guard band of \(0.5\text{ MHz}\) separates each pair, what is the minimum total bandwidth needed for the FDM link?

C.\(5.5\text{ MHz}\)
D.\(8\text{ MHz}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The total bandwidth equals the sum of channel bandwidths plus two guard bands (one between each adjacent pair). Thus, \(3 \times 2\text{ MHz}=6\text{ MHz}\) and \(2 \times 0.5\text{ MHz}=1\text{ MHz}\); together they give \(7\text{ MHz}\).
