What is Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum Technique?

Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum is a spread spectrum method that multiplies the original data signal by a high-rate pseudorandom chip sequence to spread it across a wide bandwidth, allowing the receiver to recover the signal using the same sequence while rejecting noise and other users' signals.

📝 All Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum Technique MCQs

Q1. What is the main function of Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum (DSSS) in a communication system?

A.Increase data rate
B.Spread signal over wider bandwidth for resistance to interference ✅
C.Reduce power consumption
D.Convert analog signals to digital
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The purpose of DSSS is to expand a narrow‑band data signal over a much wider frequency range using a pseudo‑random chip sequence. This spreading makes the signal more tolerant to narrowband interference and jamming, while also providing a degree of security, which matches option B.

Q2. In the context of multiplexed systems, what does the term \channel\ specifically denote?

A.The physical cable connecting devices
B.The frequency band allocated to a user
C.Portion of a link that carries a transmission between a given pair of lines ✅
D.The multiplexing device itself
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A channel refers to the logical portion of a physical link that is dedicated to a particular communication pair. It is the slice of bandwidth or time that transports the data stream between two endpoints, not the hardware itself, matching option C.

Q3. If a DSSS system uses a chip rate of 1 Mbps and the original data rate is 100 kbps, what is the processing gain, and how does this affect interference resistance?

A.Processing gain of 0.1, reducing resistance to interference
B.Processing gain of 10, but it does not affect interference resistance
C.Processing gain of 100, providing tenfold improvement
D.Processing gain of 10, enhancing resistance to narrowband interference by roughly ten times ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Processing gain is defined as the ratio of chip rate to data rate, here \( \frac{1\,\text{Mbps}}{100\,\text{kbps}} = 10\). A gain of 10 means the signal’s energy is spread ten times wider, making any narrowband interferer only affect a small fraction of the chips, thereby improving resistance by about an order of magnitude.

Q4. Suppose a link has total bandwidth of 200 MHz, and each of 5 users is allocated an equal portion via DSSS. If each user’s spread signal occupies 30 MHz, what can be inferred about the remaining bandwidth?

A.50 MHz remains unused and could serve as guard band or future channels ✅
B.No bandwidth remains; system is fully utilized
C.Remaining bandwidth must be allocated equally to each user, reducing their portion
D.The system must reduce each user’s bandwidth to fit within 200 MHz
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Five users each using 30 MHz consume 150 MHz of the 200 MHz link, leaving 50 MHz free. This spare spectrum can be reserved for guard bands, future expansion, or other services, which is precisely the inference described in option A.

Q5. A DSSS transmitter increases its chip rate while keeping the data rate constant. How does this change affect the probability of a narrowband interferer corrupting a bit, assuming interferer bandwidth stays the same?

A.Probability increases because higher chip rate widens the signal
B.Probability decreases because higher chip rate spreads the signal more, making narrowband interference less likely to affect a bit ✅
C.Probability remains unchanged because interferer bandwidth is constant
D.Probability fluctuates unpredictably
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Increasing the chip rate raises the processing gain (\( \text{gain}= \frac{\text{chip rate}}{\text{data rate}} \)). A higher gain means each bit is represented by more chips, so a narrowband interferer overlaps only a small portion of those chips, reducing the chance that enough chips are corrupted to flip the bit, as stated in option B.

Q6. In a multiplexed system using time‑division multiplexing (TDM), if the frame time is reduced by half while keeping the number of channels constant, what is the impact on each channel’s data rate?

A.Data rate halves
B.Data rate stays the same
C.Data rate doubles ✅
D.Data rate becomes zero
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Halving the frame time means each channel gets half the time to transmit its data. Since the amount of data per frame remains unchanged, the transmission must occur twice as fast, effectively doubling the per‑channel data rate, which is reflected in option C.

Q7. Compare the bandwidth efficiency of frequency‑division multiplexing (FDM) and direct sequence spread spectrum (DSSS) when both are applied to the same number of channels. Which statement is most accurate?

A.FDM generally uses bandwidth more efficiently than DSSS because DSSS spreads each signal over a wider spectrum ✅
B.DSSS is more bandwidth‑efficient because it allows overlapping of signals without guard bands
C.Both have identical bandwidth efficiency under ideal conditions
D.DSSS uses less bandwidth than FDM when the spreading factor is low
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: FDM assigns distinct non‑overlapping frequency slots to each channel, so the total occupied bandwidth is roughly the sum of the individual channel widths plus small guard bands. DSSS, by design, spreads each channel across a much larger spectrum, leading to higher overall bandwidth consumption, making option A correct.

Q8. Evaluate the trade‑off between processing gain and power spectral density in DSSS. Which choice best reflects the relationship?

A.Increasing processing gain raises power spectral density
B.Increasing processing gain lowers power spectral density, making the signal harder to detect
C.Processing gain does not affect power spectral density
D.Higher processing gain reduces power spectral density, which improves resistance to interference while keeping total power constant ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: When processing gain rises, the same total transmitted power is distributed over a wider frequency range, reducing the power per hertz (power spectral density). This reduction makes the signal appear weaker to unintended receivers while still providing robustness against interference, matching option D.

Q9. Differentiate between the effect of increasing the spreading factor versus increasing the transmit power on the bit error rate (BER) in a DSSS system under Gaussian noise. Which option correctly describes the impact?

A.Raising transmit power always yields lower BER than increasing spreading factor
B.Increasing spreading factor reduces BER more efficiently than a comparable increase in transmit power under Gaussian noise ✅
C.Both have identical impact on BER
D.Decreasing spreading factor improves BER while increasing power degrades it
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In Gaussian noise, the BER depends on the signal‑to‑noise ratio after despreading. A higher spreading factor provides greater processing gain, effectively improving the post‑despread SNR more than a modest increase in raw transmit power, thus lowering BER more efficiently, as described in option B.

Q10. Given two DSSS systems: System X uses a spreading factor of 16 and transmit power of 0 dBm; System Y uses a spreading factor of 64 and transmit power of –10 dBm. Assuming identical noise conditions, which system will achieve lower BER?

A.System X will have lower BER because of higher transmit power
B.System Y will have higher BER due to lower transmit power
C.System Y will achieve lower BER thanks to its higher spreading factor outweighing the power reduction ✅
D.Both systems will have identical BER
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The processing gain of System Y is four times that of System X (\(64/16 = 4\)). This gain translates into a 6 dB improvement in effective SNR, which more than compensates for the 10 dB loss in transmit power, resulting in a lower BER for System Y, as indicated in option C.

Q11. Assess why DSSS is more suitable than FDM for satellite communication where the spectrum is limited. Which rationale is most compelling?

A.DSSS allows overlapping of multiple users in the same frequency band using unique spreading codes, conserving spectrum ✅
B.DSSS requires wider bandwidth than FDM, making it less suitable
C.FDM provides better security, which is unnecessary for satellites
D.DSSS cannot operate in the high‑frequency bands used by satellites
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Satellite links have strict bandwidth constraints. DSSS enables many users to share the same narrow frequency band by assigning orthogonal pseudo‑random codes, thereby maximizing spectral utilization without needing separate frequency slots, which directly addresses the limitation, making option A the best justification.
