What is Time Division Multiplexing Technique?

Time Division Multiplexing is a digital multiplexing method that allocates specific time slots to each signal in a repeating cycle, allowing multiple data streams to share the same channel by transmitting in rapid succession rather than simultaneously.

Q1. What is the primary purpose of multiplexing in communication systems?

A.To increase signal power
B.To combine multiple signals into a single channel ✅
C.To encrypt data
D.To reduce noise
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Multiplexing allows several lower‑rate data streams to share a single higher‑rate transmission medium, maximizing bandwidth utilization while keeping each stream recoverable at the receiver.

Q2. In Frequency‑Division Multiplexing, each channel is shifted to a different carrier frequency. Which term describes the frequency range added between adjacent channels to avoid overlap?

A.Guard band ✅
B.Pilot tone
C.Sideband
D.Null interval
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A guard band is a small unused portion of spectrum placed between adjacent channels to prevent interference caused by imperfect filtering or drift.

Q3. A voice channel occupies 4 kHz. If three such channels are combined without guard bands, what is the total required bandwidth?

D.20 kHz

📖 Explanation: Each voice channel needs 4 kHz, so three channels require 3 × 4 kHz = 12 kHz. No guard band means the bandwidth is exactly the sum of the individual channels.
