What is Packet loss causes and effects?

Packet loss occurs when router buffers overflow during congestion, links experience bit errors, or TTL expires, causing reduced application performance, increased latency from retransmissions, and degraded quality for real-time applications like voice and video.

📝 All Packet loss causes and effects MCQs

Q1. If a VLAN is misconfigured causing broadcast storms, what is the most likely impact on packet loss?

A.Packet loss decreases because traffic is spread out.
B.Packet loss increases due to overwhelming the network. ✅
C.Packet loss remains unchanged.
D.Broadcast storms have no effect on packet loss.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Broadcast storms flood the network with excessive frames, saturating bandwidth and causing queues to overflow. When queues overflow, frames are dropped, leading to higher packet loss. Therefore the misconfiguration that creates a broadcast storm directly results in an increase in packet loss.

Q2. How does enabling the Spanning Tree Protocol (STP) on VLAN trunks affect packet loss during a temporary network loop?

A.STP accelerates the loop, increasing loss.
B.STP ignores loops, so loss is unchanged.
C.STP blocks the loop quickly, reducing loss.
D.STP disables the loop by blocking ports, thereby minimizing loss. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: STP detects loops by exchanging Bridge Protocol Data Units and disables selected ports to break the loop. By blocking the looping ports, STP prevents the continuous circulation of frames that would otherwise consume bandwidth and cause buffer overflows, thus reducing the amount of packet loss during the transient loop condition.

Q3. A trunk link between two switches drops untagged frames while packet loss spikes. What is the most plausible inference?

A.Untagged frames are being discarded because the trunk expects tagged frames. ✅
B.Untagged frames are prioritized, causing loss of tagged frames.
C.Untagged frames cause loops, leading to loss.
D.Untagged frames are converted to tagged frames, eliminating loss.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Trunk ports are configured to forward only frames that carry VLAN tags. When untagged frames arrive, the switch treats them as invalid for the trunk and discards them, contributing to observed packet loss. This behavior aligns with standard trunking rules, making the dropping of untagged frames the most logical explanation.

Q4. Compared to a legacy flat LAN, how does VLAN tagging influence susceptibility to packet loss?

A.Tagging adds overhead, increasing loss.
B.Tagging has no effect on loss.
C.Tagging isolates traffic, reducing congestion‑related loss. ✅
D.Tagging forces all traffic through a single path, raising loss.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: VLAN tagging separates broadcast domains, so each VLAN carries only its own traffic. This isolation reduces the amount of unnecessary traffic on any single segment, decreasing the chance of congestion and buffer overflow, which are primary causes of packet loss. Consequently, VLAN tagging tends to lower susceptibility to loss compared with a flat LAN.

Q5. During a distributed denial‑of‑service (DDoS) attack targeting a single department, what is the effect of VLAN isolation on overall packet loss across the campus network?

A.Loss spreads to all VLANs regardless of isolation.
B.Loss is confined to the attacked VLAN, limiting campus‑wide impact. ✅
C.VLAN isolation amplifies loss by adding extra processing.
D.VLANs have no impact on DDoS‑induced loss.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: VLANs create separate broadcast domains, so traffic generated by a DDoS attack remains within the compromised VLAN. Other VLANs continue to operate normally because their frames are filtered by VLAN tags, preventing the attack traffic from overwhelming shared resources. This containment limits packet loss to the affected VLAN rather than the entire campus.

Q6. In a multi‑layer switch architecture, which layer typically experiences greater packet loss due to congestion: the access layer or the distribution layer, and why?

A.Access layer, because end devices generate bursts.
B.Access layer, due to limited port bandwidth.
C.Distribution layer, because it aggregates traffic from many access ports.
D.Distribution layer, because it aggregates traffic from many access ports and often becomes the bottleneck, leading to higher packet loss. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The distribution layer consolidates traffic from numerous access switches, increasing the volume of frames that must be processed and forwarded. If the distribution links or devices lack sufficient capacity, buffers fill more quickly, causing higher rates of packet drops. Therefore, congestion‑induced loss is usually more pronounced at the distribution layer.

Q7. How can implementing VLANs in a campus network help reduce packet loss caused by broadcast traffic?

A.By segmenting broadcast domains, VLANs limit unnecessary traffic on each segment. ✅
B.VLANs increase broadcast traffic, worsening loss.
C.VLANs convert broadcasts to unicast, eliminating loss.
D.VLANs have no effect on broadcast traffic.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: VLANs partition a physical network into multiple logical broadcast domains. Broadcast frames are confined to the VLAN where they originate, preventing them from flooding unrelated segments. This reduction in unnecessary broadcast traffic lessens the load on switches and links, thereby decreasing the likelihood of congestion‑related packet loss.

Q8. When a VLAN trunk connects two switches with different MTU settings, what is the most likely cause of packet loss?

A.The larger MTU frames are fragmented and reassembled without loss.
B.MTU mismatch leads to frame truncation, causing drops.
C.MTU mismatch causes oversized frames to be discarded, leading to packet loss. ✅
D.MTU mismatch does not affect VLAN trunks.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: If one side of a trunk supports a larger Maximum Transmission Unit than the other, frames exceeding the smaller MTU cannot be transmitted. Many switches will drop these oversized frames rather than fragment them, resulting in packet loss. Proper MTU alignment across trunk links is essential to avoid this issue.

Q9. Which VLAN design best minimizes packet loss while preserving redundancy when spanning‑tree protocol is used?

A.Place all devices in a single VLAN and rely on STP for loop prevention.
B.Create separate VLANs for each department and disable STP.
C.Use multiple VLANs with STP enabled, ensuring each VLAN has a designated root bridge and backup links. ✅
D.Assign VLANs randomly without considering STP topology.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: By assigning devices to multiple VLANs and enabling STP for each, the network can provide redundant paths while keeping traffic isolated. Designating a root bridge per VLAN ensures optimal forwarding, and backup links activate only when primary links fail, reducing congestion and packet loss during normal operation.

Q10. What does the IEEE 802.1Q standard specify?

A.Physical cabling types for Ethernet.
B.Routing algorithms for IP networks.
C.Wireless security protocols.
D.The format for VLAN tagging in Ethernet frames. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: IEEE 802.1Q defines the method for inserting a VLAN tag into Ethernet frames, including the tag's structure, EtherType field, and how switches interpret the tag. This standard enables multiple VLANs to coexist on a single physical link by providing a consistent tagging format.
