What is Network delay components propagation transmission queuing?

Total network delay consists of propagation delay for signals to travel through media, transmission delay for pushing bits onto the link, processing delay for examining headers, and queuing delay waiting in router buffers before transmission.

📝 All Network delay components propagation transmission queuing MCQs

Q1. In which OSI layer does a hub operate?

A.Physical ✅
B.Data Link
C.Network
D.Transport
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A hub functions solely as a physical‑layer device; it merely repeats the electrical signal without interpreting frame headers, so it belongs to the Physical layer of the OSI model.

Q2. Which device regenerates and retimes the original bit pattern of a signal?

A.Hub
B.Switch
C.Repeater ✅
D.Router
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A repeater receives a weakened signal, restores its original waveform, and retransmits it, thereby regenerating and retiming the bit pattern before attenuation degrades the data.

Q3. If signal attenuation exceeds the acceptable limit before reaching a hub, what is the most likely immediate consequence for the transmitted frame?

A.The frame is received with errors ✅
B.The frame is discarded by the hub
C.The hub amplifies the signal without regeneration
D.Transmission is delayed until attenuation reduces
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When attenuation is too great, the signal arriving at the hub no longer meets the required voltage levels, so the hub cannot correctly interpret the bits and the frame is effectively corrupted, leading to errors.

Q4. In a star topology using a single hub, if the cable connecting one host fails, how does this affect communication among the remaining hosts?

A.All communication stops
B.Only the failed host loses connectivity ✅
C.Hub reroutes traffic through another port
D.Network automatically switches to wireless mode
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A hub simply forwards bits between its ports; a broken cable isolates only the host attached to that port, leaving the other hosts connected to the hub able to communicate normally.

Q5. Adding an additional hub to a LAN increases the size of the collision domain. What effect does this have on average network delay?

A.Delay decreases because of more paths
B.Delay remains unchanged
C.Delay increases due to higher chance of collisions ✅
D.Delay becomes unpredictable but always lower
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Each hub creates a single shared medium; more hubs mean more hosts share the same collision domain, raising the probability of collisions and retransmissions, which in turn raises the average time a frame spends waiting to be successfully transmitted.

Q6. Compare a hub and a link‑layer switch in terms of collision domains and their impact on end‑to‑end delay.

A.Both create a single collision domain, but switches add processing delay
B.Hubs create one collision domain while switches create separate domains, reducing queuing delay ✅
C.Switches increase collision domains, causing higher delay
D.Hubs isolate collisions, leading to lower delay than switches
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A switch isolates each port into its own collision domain, so frames are less likely to collide and wait in queue, whereas a hub places all ports in one domain, increasing contention and potentially raising delay.

Q7. Evaluate the use of a hub versus a router for connecting two separate LANs when the maximum acceptable end‑to‑end delay is 5 ms. Which device is more suitable and why?

A.Hub, because it adds no routing delay
B.Router, because it can segment traffic and reduce collision‑related delays ✅
C.Hub, because repeaters inside compensate for delay
D.Router, because its processing delay is negligible compared to hub’s broadcast nature
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A router forwards packets based on network addresses and can isolate traffic, preventing collisions that would otherwise increase delay on a shared hub; the processing delay of modern routers is typically well under a millisecond, keeping total delay within the 5 ms limit.

Q8. Differentiate the functional role of a repeater from that of a hub in a star‑topology Ethernet network, focusing on their contributions to overall latency.

A.Repeaters amplify signals without adding delay, while hubs introduce queuing delay
B.Repeaters regenerate signals and add propagation delay, hubs merely forward bits with minimal processing ✅
C.Both repeaters and hubs add identical delay
D.Hubs perform signal regeneration, repeaters only switch frames
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A repeater restores signal integrity and therefore adds a small regeneration time plus the propagation time of its own segment; a hub simply copies the incoming electrical signal to all ports, contributing only a negligible switching time.

Q9. Analyze how increasing the length of a cable segment beyond the recommended maximum, while inserting a repeater at the midpoint, influences the total transmission delay.

A.Total delay decreases because the repeater shortens the path
B.Delay remains the same as original segment
C.Delay increases due to added propagation plus repeater processing ✅
D.Delay is unaffected because repeaters eliminate propagation delay
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The longer physical distance adds propagation delay proportionally, and the repeater introduces its own regeneration time; together they raise the overall latency compared with a shorter, unamplified segment.

Q10. Given three cable segments each with a propagation delay of \(2\,\text{ms}\) and two repeaters each introducing a regeneration delay of \(0.5\,\text{ms}\), what is the total end‑to‑end delay?

A.\(6.0\,\text{ms}\)
B.\(7.0\,\text{ms}\) ✅
C.\(8.0\,\text{ms}\)
D.\(9.0\,\text{ms}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The three segments contribute \(3 \times 2 = 6\) ms of propagation delay; the two repeaters add \(2 \times 0.5 = 1\) ms of regeneration delay, giving a total of \(6 + 1 = 7\) ms.

Q11. Explain how the shared bandwidth nature of a hub influences the effective transmission delay experienced by multiple hosts sending data simultaneously.

A.Bandwidth sharing reduces delay for each host
B.All hosts experience the same delay regardless of traffic
C.Increased contention leads to higher queuing delay per host ✅
D.Hub allocates dedicated channels, eliminating delay variation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Because all ports share the same medium, when several hosts transmit at once they must contend for the same bandwidth; collisions and subsequent back‑off periods increase the time each host waits, raising the effective delay for each transmission.

Q12. Design a small office network that must not exceed an end‑to‑end delay of \(10\,\text{ms}\). You may use hubs, repeaters, and cables with known propagation delays. Which configuration satisfies the requirement?

A.Three 3 ms cables with one hub
B.Two 4 ms cables with a hub and a repeater ✅
C.Four 2 ms cables with two hubs
D.One 9 ms cable with a hub only
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Two 4 ms cable segments give 8 ms of propagation; adding a repeater (≈0.5 ms) and the hub’s negligible switching delay keeps the total under 10 ms, meeting the requirement.

Q13. Apply the OSI model to explain why a hub, despite operating only at the physical layer, can still affect the performance of transport‑layer protocols.

A.Physical layer errors cause retransmissions at transport layer ✅
B.Hubs encrypt data affecting transport
C.Hubs modify TCP headers
D.Transport layer does not depend on physical devices
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When a hub introduces errors or collisions, higher‑layer protocols such as TCP detect lost or corrupted packets and trigger retransmissions, flow control, and congestion avoidance mechanisms, thereby influencing transport‑layer performance even though the hub itself resides at the physical layer.

Q14. If a hub introduces a propagation delay of \(0.2\,\text{ms}\) per port and a frame must traverse three ports to reach its destination, what is the cumulative delay contributed by the hub?

A.\(0.2\,\text{ms}\)
B.\(0.4\,\text{ms}\)
C.\(0.6\,\text{ms}\) ✅
D.\(0.8\,\text{ms}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Each hop through the hub adds \(0.2\) ms, so three successive ports contribute \(3 \times 0.2 = 0.6\) ms of delay before the frame exits the hub.

Q15. Which of the following statements correctly describes the effect of using a hub on collision detection in an Ethernet LAN?

A.Collisions are eliminated because the hub isolates each port
B.Collisions can occur on the shared medium, requiring CSMA/CD ✅
C.Hubs convert collisions into errors that are ignored
D.Hubs provide full‑duplex communication, preventing collisions
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A hub connects all ports to a single collision domain; therefore, when two devices transmit simultaneously, a collision occurs and the CSMA/CD protocol must detect and resolve it, unlike switches that can provide full‑duplex links.
