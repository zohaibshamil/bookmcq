About 22. Next Generation IP

Next-generation IP addresses IPv4 exhaustion and architectural limitations through IPv6 adoption and complementary transition technologies. IPv6 expands address space to 128 bits, simplifies header processing, integrates IPsec mandatorily, and improves autoconfiguration via SLAAC, yet decades-long migration necessitates coexistence mechanisms like dual-stack, tunneling (6to4, GRE), and translation (NAT64/DNS64) to maintain connectivity during gradual transition periods.

Beyond addressing, next-gen initiatives include segment routing for source-directed traffic engineering, SRv6 for unified data plane programmability, and deterministic networking extensions for industrial IoT requiring bounded latency and jitter. These developments reflect shifting priorities from mere connectivity toward predictable performance, operational simplicity, and application-aware networking, suggesting future internet evolution will emphasize service guarantees and programmability alongside traditional best-effort forwarding paradigms.
