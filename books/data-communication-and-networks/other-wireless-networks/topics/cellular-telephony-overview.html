What is Cellular Telephony overview?

Cellular telephony is a mobile communication system that divides a large geographic area into smaller cells, each served by a base station, allowing many users to make calls simultaneously using limited radio frequencies.

Q1. When a mobile station moves from its current cell to an adjacent cell, which network action is required to keep the call active?

A.Increase transmission power
B.Perform a handoff ✅
C.Change the mobile’s phone number
D.Disconnect the call
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A handoff transfers the ongoing call from the original base station to the one covering the new cell, preserving the call session. Raising power or changing the number would not maintain continuity, and disconnecting ends the call, making handoff the only correct solution.

Q2. What is the primary function of the Mobile Switching Center (MSC) in a cellular network?

A.Provide antenna power
B.Store user contacts
C.Coordinate calls and manage billing ✅
D.Encrypt voice data
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The MSC acts as the central hub that routes calls between base stations, interfaces with the public switched telephone network, logs call details, and handles billing. It does not supply power, store contacts, or perform encryption; its core responsibility is call coordination and related administrative tasks.

Q3. How does a frequency‑reuse pattern with factor 4 differ from one with factor 7 in terms of co‑channel interference and network capacity?

A.Factor 4 uses more distinct frequencies, reducing interference but lowering capacity
B.Factor 4 places reusing cells closer together, increasing capacity but raising interference ✅
C.Factor 7 spreads reusing cells farther apart, decreasing interference and increasing capacity
D.Both patterns have identical interference and capacity
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A reuse factor of 4 means the same frequencies are reused after only one intervening cell, so cells are nearer each other, which raises co‑channel interference but allows more simultaneous calls per area. A factor of 7 spaces reusing cells further apart, reducing interference but limiting the number of cells that can share the same spectrum.

Q5. If the transmission power of a base station is increased to improve coverage, what is the most likely effect on neighboring cells?

A.Reduced interference
B.Increased co‑channel interference ✅
C.No change in interference
D.Immediate handoff of all mobiles
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Increasing power expands the signal’s reach, causing it to overlap more with adjacent cells that use the same frequency set. This overlap creates higher co‑channel interference, degrading the quality of communications in neighboring cells. Power reduction or handoffs are not direct consequences of simply raising transmission power.

Q6. Evaluate the trade‑off between decreasing cell size to handle high traffic and the resulting increase in handoff frequency. Which statement best describes the impact on network performance?

A.Smaller cells improve capacity with negligible handoff overhead
B.More frequent handoffs can degrade call quality despite higher capacity ✅
C.Reducing cell size eliminates handoffs
D.Larger cells always provide better performance regardless of traffic
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Shrinking cells raises the number of simultaneous users a region can support, but mobiles must cross cell boundaries more often, causing more handoffs. Each handoff introduces a brief period of vulnerability; frequent handoffs can lead to increased call drops or latency, offsetting some capacity gains. Therefore, the net effect is a balance between capacity and call quality.

Q7. A city contains a dense downtown area and a sparsely populated suburb. Which allocation strategy best balances interference and capacity?

A.Use reuse factor 7 downtown and 4 in suburbs ✅
B.Apply reuse factor 4 downtown and 7 in suburbs
C.Use the same reuse factor 5 everywhere
D.Assign a unique frequency to each cell
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Downtown requires many simultaneous calls, so a smaller reuse factor (more aggressive frequency reuse) is needed, but this raises interference; using a moderate factor like 7 keeps interference manageable while still providing capacity. Suburban areas have lower demand, allowing a larger reuse factor (4) to reduce interference. Option A reflects this optimal balance.

Q9. Compare hard handoff and soft handoff mechanisms. Which advantage is unique to soft handoff?

A.Simpler implementation
B.Lower latency
C.Simultaneous connection to multiple base stations ✅
D.Reduced power consumption
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Soft handoff allows a mobile device to maintain active links with two or more base stations simultaneously during a transition, providing a smoother transfer and reducing the chance of dropped calls. Hard handoff breaks the connection before establishing a new one, lacking this overlap. Implementation complexity and latency are not unique advantages of soft handoff, nor is power consumption necessarily lower.

Q11. A mobile user experiences a call drop immediately after crossing into a new cell. Which cause is most consistent with this symptom?

A.Insufficient power in the original cell
B.Failure to execute a proper handoff ✅
C.Interference from neighboring cells
D.Battery depletion of the handset
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When a call ends precisely at the cell boundary, the most plausible explanation is that the handoff procedure—responsible for transferring the call to the new base station—did not complete correctly. Power issues or interference would typically cause gradual degradation, while battery depletion would affect the handset regardless of location.

Q12. What is the expected effect on overall network capacity if the reuse factor is increased from 4 to 9 while keeping the total number of channels constant?

A.Capacity doubles
B.Capacity triples
C.Capacity decreases because each cell gets fewer channels ✅
D.Capacity remains unchanged
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Increasing the reuse factor spreads the same set of frequencies over more cells, so each individual cell receives a smaller share of the total channels. With a fixed channel pool, the per‑cell allocation drops, reducing the number of simultaneous calls each cell can support, thus overall capacity declines.
