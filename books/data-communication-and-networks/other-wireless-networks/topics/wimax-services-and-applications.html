Q1. If the STS‑9 rate is exactly one‑half the STS‑18 rate, how many STS‑1 channels are contained in an STS‑9 signal? \( \text{STS‑9}= \tfrac{1}{2}\text{STS‑18}\)

A. 9 ✅
B. 6
C. 12
D. 18

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because STS‑18 carries 18 STS‑1 channels, half of that capacity (STS‑9) must carry 9 STS‑1 channels. The relationship directly scales the number of elementary channels, so the correct answer is 9.

Q2. Compare the payload capacity of an STS‑32 signal to an STS‑16 signal. How does the capacity change?

A. Half
B. Same
C. Double ✅
D. Quarter

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

An STS‑32 signal is defined as having twice the bandwidth of an STS‑16 signal (32 = 2 × 16). Therefore its payload capacity is exactly double that of STS‑16, making the answer “Double.”

Q3. In a SONET link that uses an STS multiplexer, a demultiplexer, and a regenerator, what primary benefit does adding the regenerator provide?

A. Reduces latency
B. Extends the reach of the optical signal ✅
C. Changes the data rate
D. Eliminates the need for demultiplexing

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A regenerator functions as a repeater that reshapes and retimes the optical signal, compensating for attenuation and dispersion over long distances. Its main purpose is to extend the maximum distance a SON ET link can travel without degrading signal quality, not to alter rate or latency.

Q4. An optical link is experiencing dispersion‑induced errors. Which SONET device should be inserted to most directly mitigate this problem?

A. Regenerator ✅
B. Add/Drop multiplexer
C. Demultiplexer
D. STS multiplexer

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Dispersion causes signal distortion that can be corrected by reshaping the waveform. A regenerator performs optical‑electrical‑optical conversion, restoring the signal’s timing and amplitude, thereby mitigating dispersion‑related errors. The other devices do not provide this corrective function.

Q5. Evaluate the trade‑off between using an add/drop multiplexer versus a separate demultiplexer for extracting a single channel from an OC‑48 stream. Which statement is most accurate?

A. Add/drop multiplexers add more latency than demultiplexers
B. Demultiplexers provide better signal quality than add/drop multiplexers
C. Both solutions have identical performance and cost
D. Add/drop multiplexers allow selective insertion or removal of a channel without full demultiplexing of the entire stream ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

An add/drop multiplexer can tap a specific tributary while the rest of the OC‑48 stream continues unchanged, avoiding the need to fully demultiplex and remultiplex the whole signal. This selective capability reduces equipment count and processing overhead compared with a full demultiplexer.

Q6. Synthesize the relationship: 18 STS‑1 channels can be multiplexed into an STS‑18, and each STS‑3 carries three STS‑1 channels. How many STS‑3 channels fit into one STS‑18?

A. 3
B. 9
C. 6 ✅
D. 12

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Since an STS‑18 contains 18 STS‑1 channels and each STS‑3 contains 3 STS‑1 channels, the number of STS‑3 channels that can be accommodated is \( \frac{18}{3}=6\). Thus six STS‑3 channels can be multiplexed into a single STS‑18.

Q7. A network employs an STS‑64 link with a total capacity of 4020 Mbps. If this link is divided into four equal‑capacity STS‑16 sub‑links, what is the capacity of each STS‑16 sub‑link?

A. 805 Mbps
B. 1005 Mbps ✅
C. 1205 Mbps
D. 1505 Mbps

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The capacity of each STS‑16 sub‑link is the total capacity divided by four: \( \frac{4020\text{ Mbps}}{4}=1005\text{ Mbps}\). Therefore each STS‑16 carries 1005 Mbps.

Q8. Analyze the impact of using a higher‑order multiplexing format such as STS‑96 instead of multiple lower‑order links (e.g., several STS‑24). Which effect is most significant?

A. Higher‑order formats increase overhead because more framing bits are needed ✅
B. Higher‑order formats reduce overhead by decreasing the number of required regenerators and interface conversions
C. Higher‑order formats have the same overhead as lower‑order formats
D. Higher‑order formats make network management more complex without any bandwidth benefit

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Higher‑order SONET frames incorporate larger payload blocks, which actually **reduce** the relative amount of overhead compared with many smaller frames. Consequently, using STS‑96 lowers overhead, making answer A incorrect; the correct effect is a reduction in overhead, so answer B is the accurate statement.

Q9. You must transport 12 STS‑3 channels across a SONET network while minimizing equipment count. Which configuration uses the fewest multiplexers?

A. Three STS‑3 multiplexers feeding an STS‑9
B. Two STS‑9 multiplexers feeding an STS‑18 ✅
C. One STS‑12 multiplexer directly to the optical link
D. Four STS‑3 multiplexers connected directly to an OC‑48

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each STS‑3 carries three STS‑1 channels, so twelve STS‑3 channels equal four STS‑9 groups. Two STS‑9 multiplexers can combine these into a single STS‑18, requiring only two multiplexers total, which is fewer than the other alternatives.
