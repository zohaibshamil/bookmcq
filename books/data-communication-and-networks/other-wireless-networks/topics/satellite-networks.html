What is Satellite Networks?

Satellite networks use orbiting spacecraft as relay stations to transmit voice, data, and video signals across vast distances, providing communication coverage to remote areas where ground-based infrastructure is unavailable.

📝 All Satellite Networks MCQs

Q1. A GEO satellite completes one orbit every 24 hours. If the satellite drifts eastward by 2°, what is the most likely impact on its coverage area over a 24‑hour period?

A. Coverage shifts westward, leaving a coverage gap.
B. Coverage shifts eastward, maintaining continuous service. ✅
C. Coverage remains unchanged because the orbit period matches Earth's rotation.
D. Coverage fluctuates randomly due to atmospheric effects.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Geostationary satellites orbit at the same angular speed as Earth, keeping their footprint fixed relative to the ground. A 2° eastward drift moves the satellite’s position, causing its coverage area to shift east as well. Without corrective station‑keeping, this creates a temporary gap on the western edge, reducing continuous service.

Q2. Which statement best compares GEO and LEO satellites regarding latency and coverage footprint?

A. GEO satellites have lower latency but smaller footprints than LEO satellites.
B. GEO satellites have higher latency and larger footprints than LEO satellites. ✅
C. Both GEO and LEO satellites provide similar latency and footprint sizes.
D. LEO satellites have higher latency but larger footprints than GEO satellites.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Geostationary satellites reside at approximately 35,786 km altitude, so a signal must travel roughly 71,572 km round‑trip, producing a latency of about 240 ms. Low‑Earth‑Orbit satellites are only 500–2,000 km high, yielding a round‑trip distance of 1,000–4,000 km and latency near 30 ms. Consequently, GEO provides a very large footprint but higher latency, whereas LEO offers low latency with a much smaller coverage area.

Q3. How does a beam‑steering adaptive antenna system improve the link budget of a fixed WiMAX subscriber station?

A. By concentrating transmitted power toward the subscriber, increasing received signal strength. ✅
B. By spreading the signal uniformly, reducing interference.
C. By rotating the satellite to align with the base station.
D. By using frequency hopping to avoid fading.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Beam‑steering adaptive antennas dynamically direct the main lobe toward the subscriber, increasing the effective isotropic radiated power in that direction. This gain raises the received signal strength, improving the link budget and allowing higher data rates or longer distances while reducing interference from other directions.

Q4. If a satellite’s orbital altitude is increased from 2,000 km to 20,000 km, how does the round‑trip signal delay change? Assume speed of light \(c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}\).

A. Delay decreases by a factor of 10.
B. Delay remains the same because propagation speed is constant.
C. Delay increases roughly by a factor of 10. ✅
D. Delay increases by 5 ms regardless of altitude.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The round‑trip distance is twice the orbital radius. At 2,000 km the distance is \(2 \times 2{,}000 = 4{,}000\) km, giving a delay of \(4{,}000 \times 10^3 / (3 \times 10^8) \approx 13\) ms. At 20,000 km the distance becomes \(40{,}000\) km, yielding roughly 133 ms. The delay therefore increases about tenfold.

Q5. For an IoT application requiring frequent short messages, which satellite tier (GEO, MEO, LEO) offers the most appropriate balance of latency, power consumption, and network availability?

A. GEO, because its constant visibility reduces power needs.
B. LEO, because low latency and short passes minimize power use. ✅
C. MEO, because it provides moderate latency with longer visibility than LEO.
D. Any tier is equally suitable.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

IoT devices typically transmit short packets infrequently and must conserve power. LEO satellites orbit close to Earth, giving propagation delays of only a few tens of milliseconds and allowing the device to use low‑power transmitters. Although passes are brief, the frequent constellation ensures regular contact, making LEO the most suitable tier for such applications.

Q6. Design a hybrid satellite network that uses GEO satellites for broadcast services and LEO constellations for broadband access. Which of the following statements correctly describes the synergy between the two layers?

A. GEO provides low‑latency backhaul for LEO, while LEO distributes high‑bandwidth content locally.
B. LEO satellites relay GEO signals to remote areas, eliminating the need for ground stations.
C. GEO handles time‑critical control messages, and LEO supplies high‑throughput data to end users. ✅
D. Both layers operate independently, offering redundant services without interaction.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In a hybrid architecture, GEO satellites broadcast wide‑area, low‑latency services such as television or emergency alerts because their constant visibility covers entire continents. LEO constellations complement this by delivering high‑throughput broadband to end users, benefiting from low latency and large aggregate capacity. The two layers cooperate, with GEO providing ubiquitous coverage and LEO handling data‑intensive traffic.

Q7. During a handoff between two LEO satellites, a user experiences a 300 ms interruption. Which of the following is the most plausible cause?

A. The handoff algorithm failed to predict the satellite’s orbital position, causing a brief loss of line‑of‑sight. ✅
B. The user’s device battery was depleted, leading to a temporary shutdown.
C. Atmospheric scattering increased signal attenuation during the handoff.
D. The ground station switched to a different frequency band.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

LEO handoffs rely on precise orbital predictions to maintain line‑of‑sight. If the algorithm miscalculates the satellite’s position, the user may lose the signal briefly until the next satellite acquires the link, producing an interruption of a few hundred milliseconds. Device battery status or atmospheric effects would not cause such a deterministic, short‑duration outage.

Q8. Compared to IEEE 802.11, IEEE 802.16 (WiMAX) is better suited for wide‑area deployments because:

A. It uses a connectionless protocol that reduces overhead.
B. Its PHY layer operates only at 2.4 GHz, enabling better penetration.
C. It defines a connection‑oriented service with QoS mechanisms for large cells. ✅
D. It limits the maximum range to a few hundred meters, enhancing security.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

IEEE 802.16 defines a connection‑oriented MAC layer with explicit QoS parameters (e.g., UGS, ertPS) that guarantee bandwidth, latency, and jitter for each flow. This design supports large cell radii of tens of kilometers, unlike 802.11’s connectionless, best‑effort approach limited to short‑range WLANs. Hence, WiMAX is better suited for wide‑area networks.

Q9. What does the acronym GEO stand for in satellite communications?

A. Global Earth Orbit
B. Geostationary Earth Orbit ✅
C. Geosynchronous Earth Orbit
D. Ground‑based Emergency Operation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The acronym GEO stands for Geostationary Earth Orbit. It describes satellites that orbit the Earth at approximately 35,786 km altitude, matching the planet’s rotation period so that they appear stationary over a fixed point on the equator, enabling continuous coverage of a specific region.
