What is Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites Iridium?

LEO satellites orbit close to Earth to reduce signal delay and enable global mobile voice and data communication, with constellations like Iridium providing seamless coverage including polar regions where other satellites cannot reach.

Q1. What altitude range defines Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites?

A.160 km to 2,000 km ✅
B.2,000 km to 35,786 km
C.35,786 km to 42,164 km
D.Above 42,164 km
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: LEO satellites operate between roughly 160 km and 2,000 km above Earth’s surface. This range is low enough to benefit from reduced signal latency while remaining above most atmospheric drag zones. Altitudes higher than 2,000 km transition toward Medium Earth Orbit, and the much higher GEO regime begins at about 35,786 km.

Q3. Compare the impact of propagation delay on real‑time applications for LEO versus GEO satellites. Which statement best captures the difference?

A.LEO delay is roughly 10 ms, GEO about 250 ms, making LEO far better for latency‑sensitive traffic. ✅
B.Both LEO and GEO have similar delays because the speed of light is constant.
C.GEO delay is lower because the signal travels a shorter distance.
D.LEO delay is higher due to atmospheric drag.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Signals to a GEO satellite travel about 35,786 km each way, producing a round‑trip delay near 250 ms. A LEO at ~500 km altitude yields a round‑trip of roughly 10 ms. This large difference makes LEO far more suitable for interactive services such as voice over IP or online gaming, where low latency is crucial.

Q4. Given the need for continuous coverage, a LEO constellation uses multiple orbital planes. If each plane contains 4 satellites evenly spaced, how many satellites are required for 3 planes to guarantee at least one satellite above any point on Earth at all times?

A.12 ✅
B.8
C.6

📖 Explanation: With three orbital planes each holding four satellites, the total number of satellites equals 3×4=123 \times 4 = 123×4=12. Even spacing within each plane and proper phasing between planes ensures that as the Earth rotates, at least one satellite is visible from any location, providing uninterrupted global coverage.

Q6. Evaluate the trade‑off between increasing the number of LEO satellites in a constellation and the resulting inter‑satellite link bandwidth. Which outcome is most accurate?

A.More satellites always increase total bandwidth because each adds a new link.
B.Adding satellites can raise aggregate bandwidth but may saturate inter‑satellite links, reducing per‑satellite throughput. ✅
C.Bandwidth is independent of satellite count; it depends solely on ground station technology.
D.Increasing satellites decreases overall bandwidth due to higher orbital interference.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Adding more satellites expands the total network capacity, yet each satellite must share finite inter‑satellite link resources. As the constellation grows, contention for these links can become a bottleneck, causing the average bandwidth available to each satellite to drop if the infrastructure is not upgraded accordingly.

At 600 km, a constellation of about 72 satellites, evenly distributed across several planes, meets the coverage requirement while maintaining manageable launch and operational costs.

Q8. Compare the header efficiency of a fixed‑size 64‑byte frame versus a variable‑size frame that averages 128 bytes, given a payload of 1 Mbps. Which framing strategy yields higher payload‑to‑overhead ratio?

A.Fixed‑size 64‑byte frames
B.Variable‑size average 128‑byte frames ✅
C.Both have equal efficiency
D.Efficiency depends only on link speed
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Larger frames amortize the fixed header overhead over more payload bits, reducing the proportion of bits spent on headers. With an average frame of 128 bytes, the header-to-payload ratio is roughly half that of a 64‑byte frame, resulting in a higher effective data‑to‑overhead efficiency for the same 1 Mbps payload.

Q11. Explain why LEO satellites are preferred for megabit‑per‑second video streaming over GEO satellites, considering atmospheric attenuation and latency.

A.LEO suffers less atmospheric attenuation and lower latency, improving video quality. ✅
B.GEO offers lower latency, making it better for streaming.
C.Atmospheric attenuation is identical for both, but LEO is cheaper.
D.LEO satellites cannot support megabit video due to limited bandwidth.
