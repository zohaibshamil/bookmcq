What is Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) satellites GPS?

MEO satellites orbit at medium altitudes and are primarily used for global positioning systems, providing accurate location, navigation, and timing data to receivers anywhere on Earth's surface.

Q1. Why does a Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) satellite typically exhibit lower signal latency than a Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO) satellite?

A.MEO satellites are closer to the Earth's surface, reducing the propagation distance. ✅
B.MEO satellites use faster modulation schemes.
C.MEO satellites operate at higher frequencies.
D.MEO satellites have more powerful onboard processors.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Because propagation delay is directly proportional to distance, a MEO satellite at roughly 20,000 km travels a shorter path than a GEO satellite at 35,786 km, resulting in noticeably lower latency for voice and data services.

Q2. If the orbital altitude of a MEO satellite is increased, which of the following effects is most directly expected?

A.Decrease in orbital period
B.Increase in coverage footprint ✅
C.Reduction in signal power at ground
D.Increase in required launch energy
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A higher altitude expands the satellite’s line‑of‑sight cone, enlarging the ground area it can serve. While period and launch energy also change, the most immediate and observable impact is the broader coverage footprint.

Q17. Synthesize a mitigation strategy for the Doppler shift experienced by user terminals communicating with MEO satellites moving at high relative velocities.

C.Increase transmit power to overcome Doppler effects
D.Deploy additional ground repeaters to eliminate Doppler
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Adaptive frequency tracking continuously estimates the instantaneous Doppler offset and retunes the local oscillator, allowing the terminal to maintain lock despite rapid frequency changes, which is far more effective than simply raising power or adding repeaters.
