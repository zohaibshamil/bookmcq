Q1. If WiMAX employs Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), what is the primary effect on signal robustness in multipath environments?

🔹 A. Increases intersymbol interference
🔹 B. Reduces intersymbol interference
🔹 C. Eliminates need for error correction
🔹 D. Requires larger antenna size

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

OFDM divides a wideband channel into many narrow, orthogonal sub‑carriers, converting a frequency‑selective fading channel into several flat‑fading ones. This structure dramatically reduces intersymbol interference caused by multipath delays, allowing simple equalization at the receiver. Hence the correct answer is that OFDM reduces intersymbol interference.

Q2. Cellular networks typically reuse frequencies every 3 cells. If WiMAX cells are placed twice as far apart but maintain the same reuse factor, what can be inferred about the expected co‑channel interference compared with the cellular case?

🔹 A. Interference will double
🔹 B. Interference will be halved
🔹 C. Interference will remain unchanged
🔹 D. Interference will be unpredictable

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Increasing the distance between co‑channel cells reduces the received power of interfering signals according to the path‑loss exponent. Doubling the separation roughly squares the attenuation, so the interference level drops substantially. Therefore, with the same reuse factor, the WiMAX deployment would experience roughly half the co‑channel interference of the cellular arrangement.

Q3. A WiMAX channel provides 10 MHz of bandwidth and employs a modulation scheme with a spectral efficiency of 4 bits / s / Hz. Under ideal conditions, what is the maximum theoretical downlink data rate?

🔹 A. 20 Mbps
🔹 B. 40 Mbps
🔹 C. 80 Mbps
🔹 D. 100 Mbps

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The theoretical data rate equals bandwidth multiplied by spectral efficiency. Multiplying 10 MHz by 4 bits / s / Hz yields \(10\times10^{6}\times4 = 40\times10^{6}\) bits per second, or 40 Mbps. This assumes perfect channel conditions and no protocol overhead, making 40 Mbps the correct maximum rate.

Q4. Which of the following best characterizes the difference between the MAC layer of WiMAX (IEEE 802.16) and that of LTE?

🔹 A. WiMAX MAC is connection‑oriented while LTE MAC is connection‑less
🔹 B. WiMAX MAC uses a scheduling‑based approach, whereas LTE MAC relies on random access only
🔹 C. WiMAX MAC supports both TDD and FDD in the same layer, while LTE MAC is limited to TDD
🔹 D. WiMAX MAC provides QoS classes with explicit bandwidth requests, whereas LTE MAC integrates QoS within the scheduler

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

WiMAX MAC explicitly defines service classes and requires subscriber stations to request bandwidth, giving fine‑grained QoS control. LTE MAC, by contrast, embeds QoS decisions into its scheduler without separate bandwidth‑request messages. This distinction makes the statement about explicit QoS classes with bandwidth requests the accurate characterization.

Q5. When evaluating Time Division Duplex (TDD) versus Frequency Division Duplex (FDD) for a WiMAX deployment, which statement most accurately reflects a key advantage of TDD?

🔹 A. TDD can adapt the uplink/downlink ratio to traffic asymmetry
🔹 B. TDD allows simultaneous uplink and downlink transmission
🔹 C. TDD eliminates the need for guard bands between uplink and downlink
🔹 D. TDD requires twice the spectrum of FDD for the same data rate

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

TDD uses the same frequency band for both directions but at different times, allowing the network operator to allocate more time slots to the direction with higher traffic demand. This flexibility to adjust the uplink/downlink ratio makes TDD especially suitable for asymmetric data patterns, which is the primary advantage described.

Q6. IEEE 802.16d (fixed WiMAX) and IEEE 802.16e (mobile WiMAX) differ in several PHY parameters. Which of the following statements correctly distinguishes them?

🔹 A. 802.16d uses OFDMA only for downlink, while 802.16e uses OFDMA for both downlink and uplink
🔹 B. 802.16d supports adaptive modulation, whereas 802.16e does not
🔹 C. 802.16e operates only in licensed spectrum, while 802.16d operates in unlicensed bands
🔹 D. 802.16e introduces support for handover and Doppler‑resistant pilots, features absent in 802.16d

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The mobile standard 802.16e adds capabilities needed for users in motion, such as handover procedures and pilot designs that tolerate Doppler shifts. These features are not part of the fixed 802.16d specification, making the statement about handover and Doppler‑resistant pilots the correct distinction.

Q7. A network planner wishes to determine the maximum cell radius \(R\) for a WiMAX base station given a transmit power \(P_t\), antenna gains \(G_t\) and \(G_r\), a minimum received power \(P_{\text{min}}\), and a path‑loss exponent \(\alpha\). Which expression correctly represents \(R\) under a simple path‑loss model?

🔹 A. \(R = \left( \frac{P_t G_t G_r}{P_{\text{min}}} \right)^{\alpha}\)
🔹 B. \(R = \left( \frac{P_t G_t G_r}{P_{\text{min}}} \right)^{1/\alpha}\)
🔹 C. \(R = \left( \frac{P_{\text{min}}}{P_t G_t G_r} \right)^{1/\alpha}\)
🔹 D. \(R = \log\left( \frac{P_t G_t G_r}{P_{\text{min}}} \right)^{1/\alpha}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Under the basic path‑loss model, received power equals transmit power times gains divided by distance raised to \(\alpha\). Solving for distance gives \(R = \left( \frac{P_t G_t G_r}{P_{\text{min}}} \right)^{1/\alpha}\). This formula directly relates the cell radius to power and the path‑loss exponent, making option B the correct expression.

Q8. Consider a scenario where a metropolitan area uses a cellular 4G network for mobile access, while a municipal WiMAX network provides high‑capacity backhaul to remote base stations. Which of the following best illustrates how the two systems can be integrated to improve overall service?

🔹 A. WiMAX base stations replace cellular towers, eliminating the need for handovers
🔹 B. Cellular users connect directly to WiMAX spectrum, sharing the same carrier frequency
🔹 C. Cellular and WiMAX operate independently, with no data exchange
🔹 D. WiMAX links serve as dedicated backhaul, carrying aggregated traffic from multiple cellular sites to the core network

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

In many deployments, WiMAX is used as a high‑throughput backhaul solution, linking dispersed cellular sites to the core network. This arrangement leverages WiMAX’s broadband capabilities to transport aggregated mobile traffic, reducing reliance on wired infrastructure and enhancing coverage in remote areas. Therefore, the dedicated backhaul description is the accurate integration example.

Q9. What does the acronym WiMAX stand for?

🔹 A. Worldwide Interoperability for Microwave Access
🔹 B. Wireless Multiplexing Access
🔹 C. Wideband Integrated Mobile Access
🔹 D. Wireless Interference Management and Access

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The term WiMAX was coined to denote “Worldwide Interoperability for Microwave Access,” reflecting its goal of providing a globally compatible broadband wireless technology. This definition matches option A, making it the correct answer.
