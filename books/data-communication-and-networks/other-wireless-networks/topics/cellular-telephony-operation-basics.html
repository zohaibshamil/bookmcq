What is Cellular telephony operation basics?

Cellular networks operate by assigning specific frequency channels to individual cells and transferring active calls between adjacent cell towers as users move, ensuring continuous coverage through a process called handoff.

Q1. What is the data rate of an STS‑1 (OC‑1) signal?

A.51.840 Mbps ✅
B.155.520 Mbps
C.622.080 Mbps
D.1.544 Mbps
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The STS‑1, also known as OC‑1, is defined to carry exactly 51.84051.84051.840 Mbps of payload. This value appears directly in the SONET/SDH rate table and is the baseline for higher‑order STS levels, making option A the correct choice.

Q2. Which SONET/SDH standard corresponds to an OC‑12 line?

A.STS‑1
B.STS‑3
C.STS‑9
D.STS‑12 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: OC‑12 is the ITU‑T designation for a line that transports twelve times the basic STS‑1 rate, which is the same as the SONET STS‑12 level. Therefore the correct mapping is STS‑12, listed as option D.

Q9. When a 10 Mb payload travels over a single STS‑12 versus three cascaded STS‑3 stages, each stage adding 0.5 ms processing delay, which path yields lower latency?

A.STS‑12 path has lower latency ✅
B.STS‑3 cascade has lower latency
C.Both have equal latency
D.Latency cannot be determined
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The single‑stage STS‑12 incurs only one processing delay of 0.5 ms. The cascaded STS‑3 route introduces at least two stages (two delays), totaling at least 1 ms. Consequently, the direct STS‑12 path provides the lower latency, making option A correct.

Q10. Assuming identical modulation, how does the spectral efficiency of an OC‑9 (STS‑9) compare to an OC‑3 (STS‑3)?
