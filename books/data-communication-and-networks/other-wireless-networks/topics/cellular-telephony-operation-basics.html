What is Cellular telephony operation basics?

Cellular networks operate by assigning specific frequency channels to individual cells and transferring active calls between adjacent cell towers as users move, ensuring continuous coverage through a process called handoff.

Q1. What is the data rate of an STS‑1 (OC‑1) signal?

A. 51.840 Mbps ✅
B. 155.520 Mbps
C. 622.080 Mbps
D. 1.544 Mbps

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The STS‑1, also known as OC‑1, is defined to carry exactly \(51.840\) Mbps of payload. This value appears directly in the SONET/SDH rate table and is the baseline for higher‑order STS levels, making option A the correct choice.

Q2. Which SONET/SDH standard corresponds to an OC‑12 line?

A. STS‑1
B. STS‑3
C. STS‑9
D. STS‑12 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

OC‑12 is the ITU‑T designation for a line that transports twelve times the basic STS‑1 rate, which is the same as the SONET STS‑12 level. Therefore the correct mapping is STS‑12, listed as option D.

Q3. If three STS‑3 signals are multiplexed into one STS‑9, what is the total data rate of the resulting STS‑9?

A. \(155.520\) Mbps
B. \(311.040\) Mbps
C. \(466.560\) Mbps ✅
D. \(622.080\) Mbps

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Each STS‑3 carries \(155.520\) Mbps. Multiplying by three gives \(3 \times 155.520 = 466.560\) Mbps, which matches the defined rate for STS‑9. Hence option C correctly represents the total bandwidth after multiplexing.

Q4. Replacing an OC‑3 circuit with three separate OC‑1 circuits will have what effect on overall bandwidth?

A. No change ✅
B. Increase by a factor of two
C. Decrease the bandwidth
D. Increase by a factor of three

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

An OC‑3 is equivalent to three OC‑1 circuits, each providing \(51.840\) Mbps. Substituting three OC‑1 links simply distributes the same total capacity (\(3 \times 51.840 = 155.520\) Mbps) across separate paths, so the aggregate bandwidth remains unchanged.

Q5. A transport network uses six STS‑12 circuits at full utilization. If one STS‑12 fails, what fraction of the total capacity is lost?

A. \(8.33\%\)
B. \(16.67\%\) ✅
C. \(25\%\)
D. \(33.33\%\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The network’s total capacity is \(6 \times 622.080 = 3\,732.48\) Mbps. Losing one STS‑12 removes \(622.080\) Mbps, which is \(622.080 / 3\,732.48 = 0.1667\) or \(16.67\%\) of the overall capacity. Thus option B is correct.

Q6. How many STS‑3 signals can be accommodated within a single STS‑12 circuit?

A. 2
B. 3
C. 4 ✅
D. 6

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

An STS‑12 carries twelve times the basic STS‑1 rate, while an STS‑3 carries three times that rate. Dividing the capacities gives \(12 / 3 = 4\); therefore four STS‑3 signals fit into one STS‑12, making option C correct.

Q7. What is the bandwidth ratio of STS‑3 to STS‑1?

A. 1 : 1
B. 1 : 2
C. 1 : 3 ✅
D. 1 : 4

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

STS‑1 transmits \(51.840\) Mbps, whereas STS‑3 transmits \(155.520\) Mbps. The ratio \(155.520 / 51.840 = 3\) corresponds to a 1 : 3 relationship, so option C accurately describes the bandwidth ratio.

Q8. A network currently using only STS‑1 links wants the greatest capacity increase. Which addition provides the largest boost?

A. Add one STS‑3
B. Add two STS‑1
C. Add one STS‑9
D. Add one STS‑12 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Comparing the extra capacity: one STS‑3 adds \(155.520\) Mbps, two STS‑1 add \(2 \times 51.840 = 103.680\) Mbps, one STS‑9 adds \(466.560\) Mbps, and one STS‑12 adds \(622.080\) Mbps. The STS‑12 yields the highest increase, so option D is correct.

Q9. When a 10 Mb payload travels over a single STS‑12 versus three cascaded STS‑3 stages, each stage adding 0.5 ms processing delay, which path yields lower latency?

A. STS‑12 path has lower latency ✅
B. STS‑3 cascade has lower latency
C. Both have equal latency
D. Latency cannot be determined

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The single‑stage STS‑12 incurs only one processing delay of 0.5 ms. The cascaded STS‑3 route introduces at least two stages (two delays), totaling at least 1 ms. Consequently, the direct STS‑12 path provides the lower latency, making option A correct.

Q10. Assuming identical modulation, how does the spectral efficiency of an OC‑9 (STS‑9) compare to an OC‑3 (STS‑3)?

A. OC‑9 is three times higher ✅
B. OC‑3 is three times higher
C. Both have equal efficiency
D. OC‑9 is lower than OC‑3

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Spectral efficiency is proportional to data rate when modulation is unchanged. Since OC‑9 carries three times the bitrate of OC‑3 (\(466.560 / 155.520 = 3\)), its efficiency is threefold higher, so option A is correct.

Q11. Using hierarchical multiplexing, how many STS‑1 signals can be combined to form an STS‑12?

A. 6
B. 8
C. 12 ✅
D. 24

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

An STS‑12 is defined as twelve times the basic STS‑1 rate. Therefore, exactly twelve STS‑1 signals are multiplexed together to create an STS‑12, which corresponds to option C.

Q12. A provider upgrades from an OC‑3 infrastructure to support a new 400 Mbps service. Which minimum SONET level should be adopted?

A. STS‑3
B. STS‑9 ✅
C. STS‑12
D. STS‑1

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The required bandwidth (400 Mbps) exceeds the STS‑3 rate (\(155.520\) Mbps) but is below the STS‑9 rate (\(466.560\) Mbps). Selecting STS‑9 meets the demand while minimizing excess capacity, so option B is correct.

Q13. In a SONET ring, two STS‑12 links share traffic equally. If one STS‑12 fails and traffic is rerouted onto an STS‑9 link, what is the additional load the STS‑9 must carry?

A. \(51.840\) Mbps
B. \(103.680\) Mbps
C. \(155.520\) Mbps
D. \(311.040\) Mbps ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

With equal sharing, each STS‑12 initially carries half of the total load: \(622.080 / 2 = 311.040\) Mbps. When one fails, the surviving STS‑12 continues its 311.040 Mbps, and the STS‑9 must take over the lost 311.040 Mbps, making option D correct.

Q14. To increase capacity by exactly a factor of four using SONET levels, which combination should be employed?

A. STS‑1 → STS‑4 (non‑existent)
B. STS‑3 → STS‑12 ✅
C. STS‑1 → STS‑9
D. STS‑1 → STS‑12

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A four‑fold increase is achieved by moving from STS‑3 (\(155.520\) Mbps) to STS‑12 (\(622.080\) Mbps) because \(622.080 / 155.520 = 4\). The pair STS‑3 → STS‑12 therefore satisfies the requirement, making option B correct.
