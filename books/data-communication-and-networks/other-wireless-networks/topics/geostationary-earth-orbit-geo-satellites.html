📝 Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO) satellites (11 MCQs)

Geostationary Orbit and Apparent Fixed Position

A geostationary Earth orbit satellite circles above the equator with an orbital period equal to Earth’s rotation, approximately one sidereal day. At an altitude of about 35,786 kilometres above the equator, it appears nearly fixed in the sky to an observer on the ground. This permits earth stations to use fixed directional antennas. Its scope includes both radio-interface behavior and broadband service delivery.

Orbital Period, Altitude and Equatorial Geometry

To remain geostationary, the satellite must orbit in a circular equatorial path in the same direction as Earth’s rotation. Its angular velocity matches Earth’s rotation, so its ground position remains approximately constant. Inclined or eccentric geosynchronous orbits do not appear perfectly stationary and show movement relative to a ground observer.

How GEO Satellites Provide Continuous Regional Coverage

A ground dish is aligned toward the satellite’s fixed apparent position and maintains a continuous radio link. The satellite receives the uplink, processes or repeats the carrier and transmits toward its coverage footprint. Because the satellite remains visible from the service region, the terminal generally does not need continuous tracking.

GEO Television and VSAT Link Example

A broadcast provider uplinks television channels to a GEO satellite above the equator. The satellite retransmits them across a large regional footprint, and home dishes remain pointed at one orbital slot. The same downlink can reach many receivers, making GEO useful for one-to-many broadcasting and fixed satellite service. The example also shows the role of gateway backhaul capacity.

Coverage Advantages and Propagation Delay Trade-Offs

GEO satellites provide broad coverage and persistent visibility over a region, reducing the need for satellite handovers. However, the long propagation path adds noticeable delay to interactive communication, and high latitudes may receive poor viewing geometry. Large path loss and rain attenuation also influence link budgets and antenna design. These relationships shape deployment choices and the quality users experience.

Misconceptions About Geostationary Satellites

'A GEO satellite is above any point on Earth' is incorrect because geostationary orbit is equatorial. 'Its orbital period is exactly 24 solar hours' is imprecise because it matches a sidereal day. 'A GEO link has no delay' is incorrect because radio waves still travel tens of thousands of kilometres.

GEO Orbital Characteristics and Link Delay in Examinations

Know the approximate altitude of 35,786 km, equatorial orbit, eastward direction and sidereal orbital period. Explain why the satellite appears stationary and why fixed dishes can be used. Estimate one-way propagation using distance divided by the speed of light, and recognize the resulting round-trip delay in interactive services. A clear sequence helps distinguish control operations from user-plane transfer.

GEO Satellite Orbit Terms

  • GEO - Geostationary Earth orbit.
  • Altitude - About 35,786 km above equator.
  • Sidereal day - Earth rotation period relative to stars.
  • Equatorial orbit - Orbit aligned with Earth equator.
  • Orbital slot - Assigned longitude for satellite placement.
  • Footprint - Region illuminated by satellite beam.

📝 All Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO) satellites MCQs

Q1. What is the frame transmission rate of any SONET STS‑n signal?

A.8000 frames per second ✅
B.1000 frames per second
C.10 000 frames per second
D.5000 frames per second
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: SONET defines a constant frame rate of 8000 frames per second for all STS‑n signals. This rate matches the 8‑kHz sampling frequency used in PCM voice digitization, ensuring synchronization between the transport layer and voice channels. Therefore the correct answer is 8000 frames per second.

Q2. If the overhead bytes in an STS‑1 frame increase by 9 bytes, how many simultaneous voice channels can be carried?

A.774
B.765 ✅
C.756
D.747
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Increasing overhead bytes reduces the payload available for voice samples. An STS‑1 frame originally carries 810 total bytes, of which 36 are reserved for overhead, leaving 774 voice channels. Adding nine more overhead bytes removes nine voice‑channel bytes, so the remaining capacity becomes 774 − 9 = 765 channels. Hence the answer is 765.

Q3. By what factor does the data rate of an STS‑3 signal exceed that of an STS‑1 signal?

A.2
B.4
C.3 ✅
D.5
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: STS‑n signals are defined such that the data rate scales linearly with n. An STS‑3 consists of three STS‑1 containers multiplexed together, so its data rate is exactly three times that of an STS‑1. The factor is therefore 3.

Q4. Given that each frame lasts 1⁄8000 s, what is the maximum amount of data (in megabits) that can be transmitted in one second by an STS‑5 signal?

A.259.2 megabits per second ✅
B.300 megabits per second
C.400 megabits per second
D.500 megabits per second
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Each frame lasts 1⁄8000 s, and an STS‑5 contains five times the payload of an STS‑1. The STS‑1 data rate is 8000 × 810 × 8 = 51.84 Mbit s⁻¹. Multiplying by 5 yields 259.2 Mbit s⁻¹, which is the maximum amount of data that can be sent in one second by an STS‑5 signal.

Q5. If a SONET link is configured to drop every 5th frame for maintenance, what is the effective frame rate?

A.8000 frames per second
B.6400 frames per second ✅
C.7200 frames per second
D.5600 frames per second
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: If every fifth frame is omitted, the system transmits only four out of every five frames. The original rate of 8000 frames s⁻¹ multiplied by 4⁄5 gives an effective rate of 8000 × 0.8 = 6400 frames s⁻¹. Thus the effective frame rate is 6400 frames per second.

Q6. Compare the total number of bits transmitted in one second by an STS‑1 and an STS‑4 signal. What is the difference?

A.155.52 million bits ✅
B.207.36 million bits
C.51.84 million bits
D.100 million bits
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An STS‑1 transmits 8000 frames s⁻¹ × 810 bytes frame⁻¹ × 8 bits byte⁻¹ = 51.84 million bits per second. An STS‑4 carries four times that amount, i.e., 207.36 million bits per second. The difference between them is 207.36 − 51.84 = 155.52 million bits per second, which matches option A.

Q7. Why is the duration of an STS‑n frame independent of n?

A.Because the frame size scales with n
B.Because the transmission rate is constant ✅
C.Because overhead changes
D.Because payload is variable
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: SONET’s frame duration is tied to the fixed frame rate of 8000 frames s⁻¹, which does not depend on the multiplexing level n. Regardless of whether the signal is STS‑1, STS‑3, or any STS‑n, each frame occupies exactly 1⁄8000 s. Hence the constancy of duration stems from the constant transmission rate.

Q8. If each byte in a SONET frame is transmitted most‑significant‑bit first, which bit of the third byte is sent first?

A.Bit 7
B.Bit 0 ✅
C.Bit 1
D.Bit 6
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: SONET transmits each byte from the most‑significant bit (MSB) to the least‑significant bit (LSB). Numbering bits from 7 (MSB) down to 0 (LSB), the first bit sent from the third byte is bit 7. Therefore the correct answer is bit 7.

Q9. An STS‑2 signal carries twice the payload of an STS‑1. If an STS‑1 can support 774 voice channels, how many voice channels can an STS‑2 support assuming the same overhead proportion?

A.1548 ✅
B.1500
C.1400
D.1600
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An STS‑2 contains twice the payload of an STS‑1 while keeping the same overhead proportion. Since an STS‑1 supports 774 voice channels, doubling the payload yields 2 × 774 = 1548 channels for an STS‑2, assuming the overhead scales similarly. Thus 1548 is the correct number.

Q10. Synthesize a reason why SONET uses a fixed frame rate of 8000 frames / s, which matches the standard PCM voice sampling rate.

A.To simplify buffering
B.To align with audio digitization ✅
C.To reduce latency
D.To match Ethernet
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: SONET was designed to transport PCM‑encoded voice, which is sampled at 8 kHz. By fixing the frame rate at 8000 frames s⁻¹, SONET aligns its transport timing with the voice sampling interval, simplifying multiplexing and reducing the need for additional timing conversion. This alignment explains why the frame rate matches the PCM sampling rate.

Q11. If a network engineer mistakenly configures an STS‑3 link to operate at the data rate of an STS‑1, what is the percentage reduction in throughput?

A.0.3333
B.0.6667000000000001 ✅
C.0.5
D.0.75
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An STS‑3 should transmit three times the data of an STS‑1. Operating it at the STS‑1 rate delivers only one‑third of the intended throughput. The reduction relative to the intended rate is (1 − 1⁄3) × 100 % = 66.67 %. Hence the throughput is reduced by 66.67 %.
