What is Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO) satellites?

GEO satellites orbit at an altitude where their rotation matches Earth's spin, appearing stationary above a fixed point to provide continuous coverage for television broadcasting and weather monitoring over large regions.

Q1. What is the frame transmission rate of any SONET STS‑n signal?

A. 8000 frames per second ✅
B. 1000 frames per second
C. 10 000 frames per second
D. 5000 frames per second

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

SONET defines a constant frame rate of 8000 frames per second for all STS‑n signals. This rate matches the 8‑kHz sampling frequency used in PCM voice digitization, ensuring synchronization between the transport layer and voice channels. Therefore the correct answer is 8000 frames per second.

Q2. If the overhead bytes in an STS‑1 frame increase by 9 bytes, how many simultaneous voice channels can be carried?

A. 774
B. 765 ✅
C. 756
D. 747

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Increasing overhead bytes reduces the payload available for voice samples. An STS‑1 frame originally carries 810 total bytes, of which 36 are reserved for overhead, leaving 774 voice channels. Adding nine more overhead bytes removes nine voice‑channel bytes, so the remaining capacity becomes 774 − 9 = 765 channels. Hence the answer is 765.

Q3. By what factor does the data rate of an STS‑3 signal exceed that of an STS‑1 signal?

A. 2
B. 4
C. 3 ✅
D. 5

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

STS‑n signals are defined such that the data rate scales linearly with n. An STS‑3 consists of three STS‑1 containers multiplexed together, so its data rate is exactly three times that of an STS‑1. The factor is therefore 3.

Q4. Given that each frame lasts 1⁄8000 s, what is the maximum amount of data (in megabits) that can be transmitted in one second by an STS‑5 signal?

A. 259.2 megabits per second ✅
B. 300 megabits per second
C. 400 megabits per second
D. 500 megabits per second

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each frame lasts 1⁄8000 s, and an STS‑5 contains five times the payload of an STS‑1. The STS‑1 data rate is 8000 × 810 × 8 = 51.84 Mbit s⁻¹. Multiplying by 5 yields 259.2 Mbit s⁻¹, which is the maximum amount of data that can be sent in one second by an STS‑5 signal.

Q5. If a SONET link is configured to drop every 5th frame for maintenance, what is the effective frame rate?

A. 8000 frames per second
B. 6400 frames per second ✅
C. 7200 frames per second
D. 5600 frames per second

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

If every fifth frame is omitted, the system transmits only four out of every five frames. The original rate of 8000 frames s⁻¹ multiplied by 4⁄5 gives an effective rate of 8000 × 0.8 = 6400 frames s⁻¹. Thus the effective frame rate is 6400 frames per second.

Q6. Compare the total number of bits transmitted in one second by an STS‑1 and an STS‑4 signal. What is the difference?

A. 155.52 million bits ✅
B. 207.36 million bits
C. 51.84 million bits
D. 100 million bits

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

An STS‑1 transmits 8000 frames s⁻¹ × 810 bytes frame⁻¹ × 8 bits byte⁻¹ = 51.84 million bits per second. An STS‑4 carries four times that amount, i.e., 207.36 million bits per second. The difference between them is 207.36 − 51.84 = 155.52 million bits per second, which matches option A.

Q7. Why is the duration of an STS‑n frame independent of n?

A. Because the frame size scales with n
B. Because the transmission rate is constant ✅
C. Because overhead changes
D. Because payload is variable

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

SONET’s frame duration is tied to the fixed frame rate of 8000 frames s⁻¹, which does not depend on the multiplexing level n. Regardless of whether the signal is STS‑1, STS‑3, or any STS‑n, each frame occupies exactly 1⁄8000 s. Hence the constancy of duration stems from the constant transmission rate.

Q8. If each byte in a SONET frame is transmitted most‑significant‑bit first, which bit of the third byte is sent first?

A. Bit 7
B. Bit 0 ✅
C. Bit 1
D. Bit 6

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

SONET transmits each byte from the most‑significant bit (MSB) to the least‑significant bit (LSB). Numbering bits from 7 (MSB) down to 0 (LSB), the first bit sent from the third byte is bit 7. Therefore the correct answer is bit 7.

Q9. An STS‑2 signal carries twice the payload of an STS‑1. If an STS‑1 can support 774 voice channels, how many voice channels can an STS‑2 support assuming the same overhead proportion?

A. 1548 ✅
B. 1500
C. 1400
D. 1600

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

An STS‑2 contains twice the payload of an STS‑1 while keeping the same overhead proportion. Since an STS‑1 supports 774 voice channels, doubling the payload yields 2 × 774 = 1548 channels for an STS‑2, assuming the overhead scales similarly. Thus 1548 is the correct number.

Q10. Synthesize a reason why SONET uses a fixed frame rate of 8000 frames / s, which matches the standard PCM voice sampling rate.

A. To simplify buffering
B. To align with audio digitization ✅
C. To reduce latency
D. To match Ethernet

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

SONET was designed to transport PCM‑encoded voice, which is sampled at 8 kHz. By fixing the frame rate at 8000 frames s⁻¹, SONET aligns its transport timing with the voice sampling interval, simplifying multiplexing and reducing the need for additional timing conversion. This alignment explains why the frame rate matches the PCM sampling rate.

Q11. If a network engineer mistakenly configures an STS‑3 link to operate at the data rate of an STS‑1, what is the percentage reduction in throughput?

A. 0.3333
B. 0.6667000000000001 ✅
C. 0.5
D. 0.75

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

An STS‑3 should transmit three times the data of an STS‑1. Operating it at the STS‑1 rate delivers only one‑third of the intended throughput. The reduction relative to the intended rate is (1 − 1⁄3) × 100 % = 66.67 %. Hence the throughput is reduced by 66.67 %.
