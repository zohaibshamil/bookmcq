📝 Structure of Packet Switches

📖 From Data Communication and Networks • 8. Switching • 35 questions available

What is Structure of Packet Switches?

Packet switches contain input/output ports, high-speed switching fabrics, buffer memory, and control processors that examine packet headers to make forwarding decisions, enabling store-and-forward or cut-through operations that dynamically route individual packets across shared network infrastructure.

Q1. What does the abbreviation FDM stand for in networking?

A. Frequency Division Multiplexing
B. Fast Data Management ✅
C. Fiber Data Modulation
D. Fixed Delay Multiplexing

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

FDM is a technique that divides a communication link into separate frequency bands, each carrying its own signal. The phrase “Frequency Division Multiplexing” precisely describes this method, making it the correct expansion of the abbreviation.

Q2. In the example network, each link uses FDM to connect a maximum of how many voice channels?

A. One ✅
B. Two
C. Three
D. Four

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The description states that each link can support a maximum of two voice channels using FDM. Therefore, the correct number of channels that can be simultaneously accommodated on a link is two.

Q3. What is the total bandwidth of each link in the eight‑telephone example?

A. \(4\text{ kHz}\)
B. \(8\text{ kHz}\) ✅
C. \(12\text{ kHz}\)
D. \(16\text{ kHz}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each voice channel occupies \(4\text{ kHz}\). Since a link carries two such channels, the link bandwidth is the sum, \(2 \times 4\text{ kHz}=8\text{ kHz}\). This value is explicitly given in the example.

Q4. If a link of \(8\text{ kHz}\) bandwidth already carries two \(4\text{ kHz}\) voice channels, what happens when a third channel attempts to use the same link?

A. It would be allocated a new time slot
B. It would be blocked ✅
C. It would share the existing bandwidth
D. It would cause interference

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Because the link’s bandwidth is fully occupied by the two existing \(4\text{ kHz}\) channels, there is no remaining spectrum for a third channel, so the attempt is rejected (blocked).

Q5. In a TDM‑based circuit‑switched network, increasing the number of time slots per frame while keeping the frame duration constant will have what effect on the bandwidth allocated to each slot?

A. Increase per‑slot bandwidth
B. Decrease per‑slot bandwidth ✅
C. No change to per‑slot bandwidth
D. Effect depends on traffic load

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The total frame bandwidth is fixed; dividing it among more slots reduces the portion each slot can carry, thus the per‑slot bandwidth decreases.

Q6. Two links each have \(8\text{ kHz}\) bandwidth and are placed in series. What is the end‑to‑end bandwidth available for a single voice channel?

A. \(4\text{ kHz}\)
B. \(8\text{ kHz}\)
C. \(16\text{ kHz}\)
D. Limited by the smallest link ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

When links are cascaded, the narrowest link determines the maximum continuous bandwidth for a flow. Since both links have the same bandwidth, the end‑to‑end bandwidth cannot exceed \(8\text{ kHz}\).

Q7. If the end‑to‑end addressing fails during the setup phase of a circuit‑switched call, what is the most likely outcome?

A. Data will be routed using FDM
B. Connection will be established without addressing
C. Connection will be aborted ✅
D. Switches will assign dynamic addresses

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Successful call setup requires the exchange of addressing information to reserve a circuit. A failure to obtain this information prevents reservation, causing the connection attempt to be aborted.

Q8. When telephone 1 wishes to call telephone 5 in the example network, which action must the switches perform?

A. A new circuit must be reserved ✅
B. An existing circuit can be reused
C. No circuit is needed; data is broadcast
D. The call is handled by packet switching

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Circuit switching requires a dedicated path for each call. Since telephone 5 does not share an existing circuit with telephone 1, the network must reserve a new circuit for this specific call.

Q9. If each link can support two voice channels via FDM, how many simultaneous calls can the simple star topology with four links support?

A. 4
B. 6
C. 8 ✅
D. 2

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Each link carries two independent channels, and the star’s central node can handle all four links simultaneously. Thus the total number of independent voice channels—and therefore simultaneous calls—is \(4 \times 2 = 8\).

Q10. One link is upgraded to support three voice channels via FDM while the other three links remain at two channels each. What is the maximum number of simultaneous calls the network can support?

A. 9 ✅
B. 10
C. 11
D. 12

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The upgraded link contributes three channels, and the remaining three links contribute \(3 \times 2 = 6\) channels, giving a total of \(3 + 6 = 9\) concurrent calls.

Q11. If a TDM switch allocates time slots statically, what is the impact on bandwidth utilization when traffic is bursty?

A. Utilization is high
B. Utilization is low ✅
C. Utilization remains unchanged
D. Utilization depends on number of slots

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Static slot allocation assigns fixed time intervals regardless of actual traffic. When traffic arrives in bursts, many slots remain empty, leading to low overall utilization of the available bandwidth.

Q12. Compared with TDM, how does FDM differ in susceptibility to noise?

A. FDM is more susceptible ✅
B. TDM is more susceptible
C. Both are equally susceptible
D. Susceptibility depends on implementation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because FDM separates signals into distinct frequency bands, any noise that falls within a band directly corrupts that channel. TDM, by contrast, separates signals in time, making it less vulnerable to frequency‑specific noise.

Q13. What is a primary advantage of circuit‑switched networks for voice communication over packet‑switched networks?

A. Lower latency
B. Higher bandwidth
C. Easier routing
D. Better quality‑of‑service (QoS) ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Circuit switching reserves a dedicated path, guaranteeing constant delay and bandwidth, which translates into consistent QoS for voice traffic—often considered the chief benefit over packet switching.

Q14. Which statement correctly describes the role of end‑to‑end addressing in circuit‑switched networks?

A. It is used only during the setup phase ✅
B. It is used in both setup and data phases
C. No address is needed at any stage
D. It is used only during data transfer

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

During circuit establishment, the source and destination addresses are exchanged to reserve a path. Once the circuit is in place, data flows without further address inspection, so addressing is confined to the setup stage.

Q15. When two voice channels share the same frequency band using FDM versus the same time slot using TDM, which requires more synchronization?

A. FDM requires more synchronization
B. TDM requires more synchronization ✅
C. Both require equal synchronization
D. Neither requires synchronization

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

TDM depends on precise timing to ensure each slot arrives at the correct instant, demanding tighter clock alignment across switches. FDM merely separates signals spectrally, needing less stringent synchronization.

Q16. Why do circuit‑switched networks not need addressing during the data‑transfer phase?

A. Because the path is pre‑established ✅
B. Because switches use MAC addresses
C. Because data is broadcast to all nodes
D. Because IP routing is employed

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Once a circuit is reserved, every switch knows the exact outgoing link for the flow, eliminating the need to examine destination addresses for each packet.

Q17. If the number of telephones in the example doubles from eight to sixteen while link bandwidth remains \(8\text{ kHz}\), what happens to scalability?

A. Scalability remains unchanged
B. Scalability decreases ✅
C. Scalability improves
D. Scalability depends on protocol choice

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Doubling the number of endpoints without increasing link capacity forces more calls to compete for the same limited bandwidth, reducing the network’s ability to support additional users—hence scalability drops.

Q18. How does the concept of occupied bandwidth in FDM influence the internal architecture of a packet switch?

A. It determines the number of parallel processors
B. It determines the need for frequency filters ✅
C. It determines buffer size requirements
D. It determines the routing algorithm used

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

FDM allocates distinct frequency bands to different flows; therefore, a switch must incorporate filtering hardware to isolate each band, shaping the physical design around frequency‑selective components.

Q19. When comparing end‑to‑end addressing with hop‑by‑hop addressing for call setup, which typically yields a faster setup time?

A. End‑to‑end addressing is faster ✅
B. Hop‑by‑hop addressing is faster
C. Both have identical setup times
D. Speed depends on number of hops

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

End‑to‑end addressing allows the source to include the destination address in a single message, reducing intermediate processing. Hop‑by‑hop schemes require each node to add its own address, adding latency at each hop.

Q20. How does resource reservation differ between circuit switching and packet switching?

A. Circuit switching reserves fixed resources ✅
B. Packet switching reserves dynamic resources
C. Both reserve fixed resources
D. Neither reserves resources

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Circuit switching allocates a dedicated slice of bandwidth for the entire call duration, guaranteeing constant resources. Packet switching, by contrast, allocates resources on demand, sharing capacity among many flows.

Q21. When a link fails, how do failure modes differ between circuit‑switched and packet‑switched networks?

A. Circuit‑switched loses the entire call, packet‑switched reroutes ✅
B. Both lose the affected flow
C. Both reroute automatically
D. Both continue unaffected

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A circuit‑switched call relies on a single reserved path; loss of any link tears down the call. Packet‑switched networks can dynamically find alternate routes, preserving the flow despite a link failure.

Q22. If each voice channel needs \(4\text{ kHz}\), how many such channels can fit into an \(8\text{ kHz}\) link using FDM?

A. 1
B. 2 ✅
C. 3
D. 4

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the total bandwidth \(8\text{ kHz}\) by the per‑channel requirement \(4\text{ kHz}\) yields \(8/4 = 2\) channels that can be placed side‑by‑side without overlap.

Q23. Why is end‑to‑end addressing required only during the setup phase in circuit‑switched networks?

A. Because the path is static after setup ✅
B. Because routing becomes dynamic later
C. Because NAT is applied later
D. Because DHCP assigns addresses later

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

During setup the switches need source and destination identifiers to carve out a dedicated circuit. Once the circuit exists, the path is fixed, eliminating the need for further address checks.

Q24. Increasing the number of time slots in a TDM system generally affects latency for voice packets how?

A. It increases latency ✅
B. It decreases latency
C. It has no effect on latency
D. Latency depends on traffic pattern only

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

More slots per frame mean each voice sample may have to wait longer before its assigned slot arrives, thereby raising the end‑to‑end delay (latency) for each packet.

Q25. Can a \(12\text{ kHz}\) link support three \(4\text{ kHz}\) voice channels via FDM?

A. Yes ✅
B. No
C. Only with compression
D. Depends on guard bands

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The total required bandwidth is \(3 \times 4\text{ kHz}=12\text{ kHz}\), which exactly matches the link capacity. Assuming negligible guard bands, the three channels can be placed side‑by‑side.

Q26. In the example network, the relationship between the number of simultaneous calls and total bandwidth required is best described as:

A. Linear ✅
B. Quadratic
C. Exponential
D. Logarithmic

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each additional call consumes a fixed amount of bandwidth (one voice channel). Therefore, total bandwidth grows proportionally with the number of calls—a linear relationship.

Q27. If a packet switch internally uses both FDM and TDM, which resource‑allocation strategy best optimizes voice and data traffic?

A. Use FDM for voice, TDM for data
B. Use TDM for voice, FDM for data
C. Use a hybrid per‑flow approach ✅
D. Use static allocation for all traffic

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A hybrid scheme lets the switch assign frequency bands to constant‑bit‑rate voice (leveraging FDM) while allocating time slots to bursty data (leveraging TDM), achieving efficient multiplexing for both traffic types.

Q28. When a circuit‑switched call ends, what happens to the reserved bandwidth?

A. It is released for other calls ✅
B. It remains occupied until manually cleared
C. It doubles for the next call
D. It halves for the next call

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Circuit switching releases the allocated resources as soon as the call terminates, making the bandwidth immediately available for new connections.

Q29. Why might FDM be less efficient than TDM when traffic is intermittent?

A. Because of fixed frequency slots ✅
B. Because of fixed time slots
C. Both are equally efficient
D. Neither has efficiency concerns

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

FDM allocates a continuous spectrum to each channel regardless of activity. If traffic is intermittent, many frequency slots sit idle, wasting capacity—whereas TDM can dynamically fill empty time slots with other traffic.

Q30. A link using FDM carries two channels, each capable of \(6\text{ kbps}\). What is the maximum aggregate data rate per link?

A. \(12\text{ kbps}\) ✅
B. \(8\text{ kbps}\)
C. \(24\text{ kbps}\)
D. \(6\text{ kbps}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The aggregate rate is simply the sum of the two channels: \(2 \times 6\text{ kbps}=12\text{ kbps}\).

Q31. During call setup, how does a message travel using end‑to‑end addressing?

A. It is broadcast to all nodes
B. It is routed hop‑by‑hop using source/destination addresses ✅
C. It is multicast to a group
D. It is stored and forwarded without addressing

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Even though the message contains end‑to‑end addresses, each intermediate switch forwards it based on those addresses, performing hop‑by‑hop routing until the destination is reached.

Q32. If a circuit‑switched network cannot allocate a circuit due to insufficient bandwidth, which mitigation strategy is most appropriate?

A. Reduce voice quality for all calls
B. Switch to packet‑switched fallback for the call ✅
C. Increase link bandwidth immediately
D. Drop the call without notification

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When a circuit cannot be provisioned, falling back to packet switching allows the call to continue using shared resources, preserving connectivity while avoiding outright denial.

Q33. How does the principle “no addressing during data transfer” affect the design of switch tables?

A. Tables store only path identifiers ✅
B. Tables store MAC addresses
C. Tables store IP addresses
D. Tables store no information

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since the data phase does not require address inspection, switches maintain simple tables that map a circuit identifier (or path ID) to an outgoing link, avoiding the overhead of full address tables.

Q34. In TDM, each time slot carries one voice sample. If the frame rate is \(125\) frames s\(^{-1}\) and each slot carries \(8\) bits, what is the data rate per channel?

A. \(1\text{ kbps}\) ✅
B. \(2\text{ kbps}\)
C. \(0.5\text{ kbps}\)
D. \(8\text{ kbps}\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Data rate = frame rate × bits per slot = \(125 \times 8 = 1000\) bits s\(^{-1}\) = \(1\text{ kbps}\).

Q35. If each link’s bandwidth is doubled while the number of channels per link stays the same, what happens to the network’s call capacity?

A. Call capacity doubles ✅
B. Call capacity increases by 50 %
C. Call capacity remains unchanged
D. Call capacity decreases

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Doubling the total available bandwidth while keeping the channel count constant effectively provides each channel with twice the original bandwidth, allowing twice as many simultaneous calls.
