What is Three Phases of Circuit Switching Network?

Circuit switching involves three distinct phases: setup, where a dedicated path is reserved through intermediate switches; data transfer, where information flows continuously over the established connection; and teardown, where the allocated resources are released after communication ends.

📝 All Three Phases of Circuit Switching Network MCQs

Q1. Which of the following lists the three phases of communication in a circuit-switched network?

A. Setup, Data Transfer, Teardown ✅
B. Connection, Routing, Release
C. Handshake, Transmission, Closure
D. Initiation, Transmission, Termination

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The three distinct stages are Setup (circuit establishment), Data Transfer (actual payload exchange), and Teardown (release of resources). This sequence is fundamental to circuit‑switched communication, making option A the correct description.

Q2. During which phase are the network resources reserved exclusively for a connection?

A. Data-Transfer Phase
B. Teardown Phase
C. Setup Phase ✅
D. Idle Phase

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Resources are allocated only during the Setup phase, where a dedicated path is created and held for the entire duration of the connection. The other phases either use the reserved path (Data Transfer) or release it (Teardown). Hence the correct answer is Setup Phase.

Q3. If a switch cannot find a free channel during the setup phase, what is the most likely outcome?

A. Connection is established after delay
B. Connection is rejected ✅
C. Data transfer proceeds with shared channels
D. Teardown occurs immediately

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When no free channel exists, the circuit‑switched network cannot create a dedicated path, so the request is denied. The system will inform the caller that the connection cannot be established, making rejection the expected result.

Q4. If a new connection request requires an additional output port, what inference can be made about the network's ability to accommodate the request?

A. It can always accommodate by reusing a folded port
B. It will automatically expand the switch
C. It will convert to packet switching
D. It may need to reject or wait for a free port ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Because the switch has a fixed number of output ports, an extra request may exceed available capacity. The network must either reject the request or hold it until a port becomes free, reflecting the correct inference.

Q5. When only one party sends a teardown signal after data transfer, what logical consequence follows regarding resource release?

A. All switches immediately free resources
B. Resources remain allocated until both signals are received ✅
C. Resources are partially released, causing deadlock
D. The network reassigns resources to new connections

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Circuit‑switched teardown requires acknowledgment from both ends; until both signals are processed, the reserved channels stay allocated. This prevents premature release and ensures a clean termination of the circuit.

Q6. Which statement best captures why circuit‑switched networks are generally considered less efficient than packet‑switched networks?

A. They allocate resources only during data transfer
B. They use dynamic routing for each packet
C. Resources are reserved for the entire connection duration ✅
D. They share bandwidth among many users

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Circuit‑switched networks hold the entire path for the whole session, blocking other traffic even when no data is being sent. This static reservation reduces overall utilization compared with packet‑switched systems that share links dynamically.

Q7. If the teardown phase is delayed by 2 seconds after the last data byte is sent, which effect is most likely on overall network throughput?

A. Throughput decreases because resources stay occupied ✅
B. Throughput increases due to longer connection
C. Throughput unchanged as data already sent
D. Throughput improves because of better error checking

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Delaying teardown keeps the dedicated channels occupied, preventing other connections from using them. Consequently, the effective throughput of the network drops because the same bandwidth cannot be reassigned to new traffic during the idle period.

Q8. Which of the following correctly differentiates the three phases of circuit switching from TCP's three‑way handshake?

A. Setup corresponds to SYN, Data Transfer to ACK, Teardown to FIN
B. Setup is analogous to SYN‑SYN‑ACK, Data Transfer to data exchange, Teardown to FIN‑ACK
C. All phases are identical in function
D. Setup is analogous to SYN‑SYN‑ACK, Data Transfer to data exchange, Teardown to FIN‑ACK ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

In TCP, the SYN‑SYN‑ACK exchange establishes a connection (analogous to circuit‑switched Setup), the subsequent data packets correspond to Data Transfer, and the FIN‑ACK sequence terminates the session (analogous to Teardown). Option D captures this mapping accurately.

Q9. A network with two 4×8 switches supports three simultaneous connections, each using two dedicated channels. If a fourth connection request arrives, what can be inferred about the network's capacity?

A. It can always accommodate by reassigning channels
B. It must reject or queue the request due to insufficient channels ✅
C. It will split channels among connections
D. It will automatically upgrade to packet switching

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

With each connection occupying two channels, the three existing connections consume six of the eight available channels. Only two channels remain free, insufficient for a fourth two‑channel request, so the network must reject or queue the new request.

Q10. Why is end‑to‑end addressing essential during the connection‑setup phase in circuit‑switched networks?

A. It ensures the correct dedicated path is reserved between the specific terminals ✅
B. It allows switches to route packets dynamically
C. It enables encryption of data
D. It reduces latency by bypassing switches

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

End‑to‑end addresses identify the exact source and destination, enabling each switch to allocate a dedicated channel that directly links those terminals. Without this information, a switch cannot reserve the proper path, making the connection impossible.

Q11. Which sequence correctly represents the order of actions from the moment system A initiates a call to system M until the circuit is fully established?

A. A sends request → I finds channel → IV finds channel → III informs M → M acknowledges → connection established
B. A sends request → I finds channel → IV finds channel → III informs M → M acknowledges → connection established
C. A sends request → I finds channel → IV finds channel → III informs M → M acknowledges → connection established ✅
D. A sends request → I finds channel → III informs M → IV finds channel → M acknowledges → connection established

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The correct flow is: A sends request to I; I locates a channel to IV; IV locates a channel to III; III informs M; M sends an acknowledgment back, completing the circuit. Option C follows this exact order.

Q12. If four output ports are folded into input ports to enable intra‑office communication, how does this design choice affect the number of inter‑office channels available?

A. It doubles the inter‑office channels
B. It reduces inter‑office channels by four ✅
C. It leaves inter‑office channels unchanged
D. It eliminates the need for inter‑office channels

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Folding four output ports into inputs repurposes them for internal traffic, thereby removing those ports from the pool that could have been used for inter‑office links. Consequently, the count of inter‑office channels is reduced by four.

Q13. Consider a circuit‑switched network where each link provides 8 kHz bandwidth divided into 4‑kHz channels. If a connection requires a 4‑kHz channel for the entire duration of a 5‑second transfer, how many such connections can the link support simultaneously without affecting each other?

A. Two connections ✅
B. Four connections
C. Eight connections
D. One connection

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The 8 kHz link can be split into two independent 4 kHz channels. Each connection occupies one channel, so at most two connections can coexist without sharing bandwidth, making the answer two.

Q14. When a teardown signal is sent to each switch in reverse order of setup, what inference can be drawn about the network's state after all switches receive the signal?

A. Resources remain allocated until a manual reset
B. Only the last switch releases its resources
C. All dedicated channels become available for new connections
D. All dedicated channels become available for new connections ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

After every switch processes the teardown message, the previously reserved channels are released, making them immediately available for subsequent connection requests.
