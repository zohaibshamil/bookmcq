What is Circuit Switched Network in Physical Layer?

A circuit-switched network in the physical layer establishes a dedicated, continuous electrical or optical path between two communicating endpoints before any data transfer begins, ensuring consistent bandwidth and minimal jitter but reserving resources even when no data is being sent.

📝 All Circuit Switched Network in Physical Layer MCQs

Q1. Which statement correctly describes a circuit-switched network?

A. Establishes a dedicated physical path for the duration of a communication session. ✅
B. Routes each packet independently based on destination address.
C. Shares bandwidth among users using statistical multiplexing.
D. Uses dynamic bandwidth allocation per frame.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A circuit-switched network creates a fixed, exclusive path between the communicating parties for the entire call, ensuring that the same physical resources are used throughout. This contrasts with packet switching where each packet may travel a different route and share bandwidth with other traffic.

Q2. In a circuit-switched link employing Time Division Multiplexing (TDM) with 48 time slots, how many distinct circuits can be simultaneously supported?

A. 24
B. 48 ✅
C. 96
D. 12

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

TDM divides a link into discrete time slots, each representing an independent circuit. With 48 slots available, the link can host 48 simultaneous circuits, because each circuit occupies one slot for the duration of the call.

Q3. Two stations A and B are connected via three parallel links, each link has 8 channels. If a call between A and B consumes one channel on each traversed link, what is the maximum number of concurrent calls that can be established without sharing channels?

A. 8
B. 16
C. 24 ✅
D. 32

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Each link provides 8 independent channels. With three parallel links, the total number of channels available for calls is \(3 \times 8 = 24\). Since each call uses one channel on a single link, up to 24 calls can be supported concurrently.

Q4. If a circuit-switched network experiences a failure of one link that originally provided 10 channels, how does this affect the maximum number of end‑to‑end connections that can be maintained assuming alternative routing is unavailable?

A. Reduces by 10 ✅
B. Reduces by 1
C. No change
D. Increases by 10

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When the failed link carried 10 channels, those channels are no longer usable for any connection that would have traversed that link. Because no alternate path exists, the overall capacity drops exactly by the number of channels lost, i.e., ten connections.

Q5. Consider a network where each link is divided into n channels using FDM. If n = 20 and a call traverses 4 links, how many channels are occupied by that single call?

A. 4 ✅
B. 20
C. 80
D. 1

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In FDM each link has 20 separate frequency channels. A call uses one channel on each link it traverses, so for four links the call occupies \(4\) distinct channels—one per link.

Q6. A circuit-switched network uses FDM with each channel having bandwidth 64 kHz. If a voice channel requires 4 kHz, how many voice channels can be multiplexed onto a single physical link?

A. 8
B. 12
C. 16 ✅
D. 20

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The number of voice channels that fit on the link is the total bandwidth divided by the bandwidth per voice channel: \( \frac{64\text{ kHz}}{4\text{ kHz}} = 16\). Therefore sixteen independent voice channels can coexist on that link.

Q7. In a network with 5 nodes arranged in a ring, each link provides 12 TDM slots. Assuming each call occupies one slot on each link it traverses, what is the maximum number of simultaneous calls that can be set up between opposite nodes?

A. 12
B. 24
C. 30 ✅
D. 60

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A ring of 5 links yields \(5 \times 12 = 60\) total slots. Calls between opposite nodes must cross two links, consuming two slots each. The maximum number of such calls is \( \left\lfloor \frac{60}{2} \right\rfloor = 30\).

Q8. If a circuit-switched link has 16 channels and 4 of them are reserved for priority traffic, how many channels remain for regular traffic?

A. 12 ✅
B. 16
C. 4
D. 8

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Subtracting the reserved priority channels from the total gives the remaining capacity for ordinary traffic: \(16 - 4 = 12\) channels are available for non‑priority calls.

Q9. A call requires a continuous bandwidth of 2 Mbps. If each channel on a link provides 2 Mbps, how many channels are needed for the call?

A. 1 ✅
B. 2
C. 0.5
D. 4

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because one channel already supplies the required 2 Mbps, the call can be carried entirely on a single channel. No additional channels are needed.

Q10. Which advantage is most directly associated with circuit switching compared to packet switching?

A. Lower latency for steady streams ✅
B. Better utilization of bandwidth
C. Ability to share links among many users
D. Dynamic routing flexibility

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Circuit switching reserves a fixed path, eliminating the need for per‑packet routing decisions and buffering. This yields consistently low latency for continuous traffic such as voice, which is a primary benefit over packet‑switched approaches.

Q11. Compare FDM and TDM in circuit-switched networks: Which statement accurately reflects a key difference?

A. FDM allocates separate frequency bands, while TDM allocates time slots. ✅
B. TDM uses frequency division, FDM uses time division.
C. Both allocate identical resources.
D. FDM requires synchronization, TDM does not.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Frequency Division Multiplexing (FDM) separates channels by assigning each a unique frequency band, whereas Time Division Multiplexing (TDM) separates channels by assigning each a unique time slot within a repeating frame. This distinction is fundamental to how each technique partitions the shared medium.

Q12. Evaluate the impact of increasing the number of channels per link on call blocking probability in a circuit-switched network.

A. Blocking probability increases linearly.
B. Blocking probability decreases, approaching zero. ✅
C. Blocking probability remains unchanged.
D. Blocking probability becomes random.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

More channels provide a larger pool of resources for incoming call requests. As the number of available channels grows, the likelihood that all channels are occupied at the moment a new call arrives diminishes, causing the blocking probability to fall toward zero.

Q13. Given a circuit-switched network where each link carries 30 channels, and traffic demand requires 450 simultaneous connections, determine whether the network can satisfy the demand assuming a single-path routing and each connection uses one channel per traversed link.

A. Yes, sufficient capacity
B. No, insufficient capacity ✅
C. Depends on routing
D. Cannot be determined

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

If each connection uses one channel on each link, the total available channels equals the number of links times 30. Even with five links, the capacity would be \(5 \times 30 = 150\) channels, far below the required 450 connections, so the demand cannot be met.

Q14. Differentiate between dedicated and shared channels in circuit switching: Which scenario exemplifies a dedicated channel?

A. A channel reserved for a single call for its entire duration. ✅
B. A channel that alternates among multiple calls each frame.
C. A channel used only for control signaling.
D. A channel that is dynamically allocated per packet.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A dedicated channel is one that is allocated exclusively to a particular call and remains reserved for that call until it terminates, ensuring uninterrupted use of that physical resource.

Q15. In a circuit-switched network using TDM, what is the effect on latency if the number of time slots per frame is doubled while keeping slot duration constant?

A. Latency halves
B. Latency doubles ✅
C. Latency unchanged
D. Latency becomes variable

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Doubling the number of slots lengthens the frame duration proportionally because each slot length stays the same. Since a call must wait for its slot to appear in each frame, the overall latency experienced by the call roughly doubles.

Q16. Which of the following is a limitation of circuit-switched networks?

A. Inefficient use of bandwidth when traffic is bursty ✅
B. High flexibility in routing
C. Low setup time
D. Ability to support many simultaneous users

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Circuit-switched networks allocate a fixed path for the entire call, which can leave large portions of the reserved bandwidth idle during periods of low or bursty traffic, leading to poor overall utilization.

Q17. Apply the concept of circuit establishment to explain why a voice call experiences a noticeable setup delay before conversation begins.

A. The network must allocate a dedicated path, which takes time. ✅
B. The call must wait for packet assembly.
C. Bandwidth is shared, causing congestion.
D. Encryption keys are exchanged.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Before communication can start, the circuit-switched system must locate a free channel on each link along the route and reserve those resources for the call. This reservation process introduces a brief delay, perceived by users as the call‑setup time.

Q18. Synthesize how circuit-switched networks could be combined with modern IP routing to support legacy telephony services, describing the role of a media gateway.

A. Media gateway converts circuit‑switched signaling into IP packets while preserving a virtual circuit. ✅
B. Media gateway replaces all circuit switching with pure IP.
C. Media gateway only handles billing.
D. Media gateway adds extra channels to the circuit.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A media gateway acts as an interface that translates traditional circuit‑switched signaling (e.g., SS7) and voice streams into IP‑based packets (e.g., RTP) while maintaining the illusion of a continuous circuit for legacy equipment, enabling integration with contemporary packet networks.

Q19. Explain why increasing the number of parallel links between two nodes reduces call blocking probability, assuming each link provides the same number of channels.

A. More total channels increase the pool of available resources. ✅
B. Parallel links cause interference, raising blocking.
C. It does not affect blocking.
D. It reduces latency only.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each additional parallel link contributes its full set of channels to the total capacity. With more channels overall, the chance that all channels are busy when a new call arrives drops, thereby lowering the probability that the call will be blocked.

Q20. If a circuit-switched network uses FDM with each channel occupying 10 kHz, how many channels can be placed on a 100 kHz link?

A. 10 ✅
B. 5
C. 20
D. 100

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the total bandwidth by the bandwidth per channel gives the number of channels: \( \frac{100\text{ kHz}}{10\text{ kHz}} = 10\) channels can be allocated on that link.

Q21. Consider a scenario where a circuit-switched network must support both voice and video streams, each requiring different bandwidths. Propose a strategy for allocating channels to meet quality requirements while maximizing link utilization.

A. Allocate higher‑bandwidth channels to video and lower‑bandwidth to voice, possibly using hierarchical FDM. ✅
B. Use only voice channels for both services.
C. Assign all channels to video only.
D. Randomly distribute channels.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

By assigning channels with larger bandwidth (or grouping multiple voice‑size channels) to video, and reserving smaller‑bandwidth channels for voice, the network can satisfy each service’s quality needs. Hierarchical FDM or mixed‑capacity channel pools enable efficient use of the link while preserving the dedicated‑circuit model.

Q22. How does the principle of 'one channel per link per connection' affect scalability when the network topology expands to many nodes?

A. Scalability is limited because each new connection consumes a channel on every traversed link. ✅
B. Scalability improves linearly with nodes.
C. Scalability is unaffected.
D. Scalability depends only on processing power.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since every connection must reserve a distinct channel on each link it traverses, adding more nodes and longer paths quickly exhausts available channels, constraining the ability of the network to scale to large numbers of simultaneous calls.

Q23. Using the concept of circuit reservation, predict the effect on network performance if traffic patterns become highly bursty.

A. Performance degrades due to idle reserved channels. ✅
B. Performance improves because bursts fit reserved circuits.
C. No effect on performance.
D. Burstiness eliminates the need for reservation.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When traffic is bursty, many of the channels that were pre‑reserved for ongoing calls may sit idle for periods of inactivity, reducing overall efficiency and leading to poorer performance compared with a more dynamic sharing scheme.

Q24. Relate the concept of dedicated paths in circuit switching to the reliability of the communication session during link failures.

A. A dedicated path must be re‑established if any link fails, causing interruption. ✅
B. Dedicated paths automatically reroute without interruption.
C. Reliability is independent of path dedication.
D. Dedicated paths prevent any failures.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because the circuit is fixed for the call’s duration, a failure on any link that forms part of the path breaks the circuit. The call must then be torn down and a new circuit established, resulting in a temporary loss of service.

Q26. If a network operator wants to reduce the call setup time in a circuit-switched system, which technique would be most effective?

A. Implement fast signaling protocols to reserve channels more quickly. ✅
B. Increase the number of channels per link.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Accelerating the signaling phase—such as using streamlined call‑setup messages and faster handshake procedures—directly shortens the time needed to allocate and confirm the dedicated path, thereby decreasing overall call‑setup latency.
