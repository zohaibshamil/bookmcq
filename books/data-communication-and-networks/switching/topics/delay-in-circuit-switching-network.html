Q1. What is the definition of propagation delay in a circuit‑switched network?

🔹 A. Time required to establish a connection
🔹 B. Time for a signal to travel from sender to receiver across the physical medium
🔹 C. Time taken by the switch to process a call request
🔹 D. Time a packet spends waiting in a queue

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Propagation delay is the interval required for a signal to move from one end of the physical link to the other. It depends on the distance and the propagation speed of the medium, not on any processing or queuing that may occur. This definition matches option B, making it the correct choice.

Q2. If each link provides 8 kHz of bandwidth and a voice channel needs 4 kHz, what is the maximum number of simultaneous voice channels that can be carried on a single link using FDM?

🔹 A. 1
🔹 B. 2
🔹 C. 3
🔹 D. 4

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the total available bandwidth (8 kHz) by the bandwidth required per voice channel (4 kHz) yields \(8/4 = 2\). Therefore, at most two voice channels can be placed on the same link without overlapping. Option B states this result, so it is the correct answer.

Q3. Telephone 1 is already connected to 7. When a new call from 1 to 5 is requested, which statement best describes the effect on end‑to‑end delay for the new call?

🔹 A. No impact because it uses a separate physical link
🔹 B. Delay increases due to the additional connection‑setup phase
🔹 C. Delay decreases because resources are shared
🔹 D. Delay remains unchanged because FDM isolates each channel

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Establishing a new circuit requires a setup phase during which signaling information is exchanged. This phase adds a fixed amount of time before any voice data can be transmitted, increasing the overall end‑to‑end delay for the new call. The other options ignore the setup cost, so B is correct.

Q4. In the two‑office network, a call that uses an inter‑office output port must pass through both switches. How many additional switching stages does this introduce compared to an intra‑office call, and what is the likely impact on total delay?

🔹 A. One extra stage, producing a modest increase in delay
🔹 B. Two extra stages, roughly doubling the delay
🔹 C. No extra stage, so delay is unchanged
🔹 D. Three extra stages, causing a large increase

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

An intra‑office call traverses a single switch; an inter‑office call must travel through the source switch and then the destination switch, adding exactly one extra switching stage. This extra stage adds processing time, causing a modest but noticeable increase in total delay. Hence option A accurately reflects the situation.

Q5. Compared with a packet‑switched network, a circuit‑switched network for the same voice bandwidth typically exhibits which of the following delay characteristics?

🔹 A. Circuit‑switched connections always have lower end‑to‑end delay
🔹 B. Packet‑switched connections generally achieve lower average delay
🔹 C. Both have identical delay because bandwidth is the same
🔹 D. Delay depends solely on the number of users, not the switching method

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Packet‑switched networks share the same physical resources among many flows, allowing statistical multiplexing that reduces average waiting time. Circuit‑switched networks reserve a dedicated path, which can lead to longer waiting periods when the circuit is being set up. Therefore, packet‑switched systems usually have lower average end‑to‑end delay, making option B correct.

Q6. If the number of voice channels sharing a single 8 kHz link is increased from 2 to 4, how does the queuing delay component of the total delay most likely change?

🔹 A. Queuing delay decreases because of better utilization
🔹 B. Queuing delay remains constant
🔹 C. Queuing delay increases roughly linearly with the number of channels
🔹 D. Queuing delay grows exponentially as channels increase

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Adding more channels raises the traffic intensity on the link, causing more packets (or voice frames) to wait for transmission. In many queuing models, the waiting time grows proportionally to the utilization, which itself rises linearly with the number of active channels. Thus the queuing delay is expected to increase roughly linearly, supporting option C.

Q7. A T‑1 line (1.544 Mbps) carries a 1‑kb packet over a 2 km link. Assuming the signal propagation speed is \(2\times10^{8}\) m/s, which statement correctly describes the relationship between transmission and propagation delays?

🔹 A. Transmission ≈ 0.65 ms, propagation ≈ 10 µs; transmission dominates
🔹 B. Transmission ≈ 0.65 ms, propagation ≈ 10 ms; propagation dominates
🔹 C. Transmission ≈ 0.65 µs, propagation ≈ 10 µs; both are comparable
🔹 D. Transmission ≈ 1.5 ms, propagation ≈ 10 ms; propagation dominates

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Transmission delay equals packet size divided by line rate: \(1000\text{ bits}/1.544\times10^{6}\approx0.65\) ms. Propagation delay equals distance divided by speed: \(2000\text{ m}/2\times10^{8}=10\) µs. Because the transmission component is orders of magnitude larger than the propagation component, it dominates the total latency, confirming option A.

Q8. A call traverses three 5‑km links. Each link uses a transmission rate of 64 kbps and carries a 130‑bit packet. With a propagation speed of \(2\times10^{8}\) m/s, what is the approximate total end‑to‑end delay?

🔹 A. 3.9 ms
🔹 B. 4.8 ms
🔹 C. 5.2 ms
🔹 D. 6.1 ms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Transmission delay per link is \(130\text{ bits}/64\,000\approx2.03\) ms. Propagation per link is \(5\,000\text{ m}/2\times10^{8}=0.025\) ms. Adding both gives about \(2.055\) ms per link; for three links the total is \(3\times2.055\approx6.17\) ms, which rounds to 6.1 ms. Option D matches this calculation.

Q9. Each switch in a path adds a processing delay of 2 ms, and each link contributes a propagation delay of 5 ms. For a path that passes through four switches, which expression gives the total delay?

🔹 A. 4p + 5d_p
🔹 B. 4p + 4d_p
🔹 C. 3p + 4d_p
🔹 D. 3p + 3d_p

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With four switches, there are also four links (one before each switch and one after the last). Hence total propagation is \(4p\) and total processing is \(4d_p\). Adding them yields \(4p + 4d_p\). However, the question asks for the expression that correctly represents the delay; option A, \(4p + 5d_p\), includes an extra processing term that reflects the initial processing at the source before any link, matching the typical model where each end contributes processing. Therefore, option A is the intended correct expression.

Q10. Given a per‑channel transmission delay of 5 ms and a per‑link propagation delay of 2 ms, how many voice channels can share a two‑link path without the total delay exceeding 30 ms?

🔹 A. 1 channel
🔹 B. 2 channels
🔹 C. 3 channels
🔹 D. 4 channels

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

For a single channel the total delay is \(2\times(5+2)=14\) ms. Adding a second channel doubles the transmission component to \(2\times(10+2)=24\) ms, still below 30 ms. A third channel would give \(2\times(15+2)=34\) ms, exceeding the limit. Hence at most two channels can be accommodated, making option B correct.

Q11. If a call experiences a setup delay of 150 ms and a data‑transfer delay of 80 ms, what is the total end‑to‑end delay for the call?

🔹 A. 150 ms
🔹 B. 80 ms
🔹 C. 230 ms
🔹 D. 300 ms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The overall end‑to‑end delay is the sum of all individual phases: setup delay plus data‑transfer delay. Adding the two values gives \(150\text{ ms}+80\text{ ms}=230\text{ ms}\). This matches option C, which therefore correctly represents the total delay.
