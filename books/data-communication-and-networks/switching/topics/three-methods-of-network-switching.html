What is Three Methods of Network Switching?

The three fundamental methods of network switching are circuit switching, which dedicates a physical path; packet switching, which breaks data into routed units; and message switching, which stores and forwards entire messages, each offering different trade-offs between resource usage and transmission efficiency.

📝 All Three Methods of Network Switching MCQs

Q1. Which switching method establishes a dedicated physical path for the entire duration of a communication session?

A. Packet switching
B. Circuit switching ✅
C. Message switching
D. Datagram switching

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Circuit switching reserves a fixed route and bandwidth for the whole session, so the path is dedicated from start to finish. Packet and message switching share network resources, and datagram switching is a variant of packet switching without a predetermined path.

Q2. If a network experiences frequent short bursts of traffic that are interleaved with idle periods, which switching method typically yields the lowest average latency?

A. Circuit switching
B. Message switching
C. Hybrid switching
D. Packet switching ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Packet switching breaks bursts into small packets, allowing each to be forwarded immediately, reducing waiting time compared with storing whole messages (message switching) or waiting for a reserved circuit.

Q3. A node receives a complete message before it can forward any part of it to the next node. Which switching technique is being employed, and what is its main disadvantage?

A. Packet switching; high header overhead
B. Message switching; increased end‑to‑end delay ✅
C. Circuit switching; inflexible routing
D. Datagram switching; no reliability

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Message switching stores the entire message before forwarding, causing larger end‑to‑end delay because the next node must wait for the whole message. Packet switching forwards each packet immediately, avoiding this delay.

Q4. Assume a network where each link has a capacity of 10 Mbps and the average packet size is 10 KB. Using the formula \( \text{utilization} = \frac{\text{average\_packet\_size}}{\text{capacity} \times \text{inter‑arrival\_time}} \), which switching method would most likely keep utilization below 30 % under bursty traffic?

A. Circuit switching
B. Message switching
C. Packet switching ✅
D. Static time‑division multiplexing

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Packet switching allows packets to be interleaved, and with small packets the link can handle bursts without saturating, keeping utilization low. Circuit switching reserves a fixed bandwidth, leading to higher utilization during bursts; message switching holds whole messages, causing spikes.

Q5. Which statement correctly contrasts circuit switching with packet switching regarding resource allocation?

A. Both allocate resources dynamically per packet
B. Circuit switching allocates a fixed path; packet switching shares resources ✅
C. Packet switching reserves a dedicated channel; circuit switching does not
D. Both require end‑to‑end acknowledgments

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Circuit switching sets up a dedicated circuit for the whole session, guaranteeing bandwidth, whereas packet switching treats each packet independently, sharing the same links among many flows.

Q6. Evaluate the impact of packet switching on Quality‑of‑Service (QoS) for real‑time video compared with circuit switching.

A. Packet switching always provides lower jitter
B. Circuit switching guarantees constant delay, benefiting real‑time video ✅
C. Packet switching eliminates the need for buffering
D. Circuit switching cannot support video traffic

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Circuit switching reserves bandwidth, giving predictable latency and minimal jitter, which is crucial for real‑time video. Packet switching can introduce variable delay and jitter, requiring additional QoS mechanisms to meet video requirements.

Q7. Differentiate a virtual‑circuit packet‑switching network from a datagram network in terms of routing information stored at each node.

A. Virtual‑circuit stores a connection identifier; datagram stores full destination address ✅
B. Both store only the destination address
C. Datagram stores a connection identifier; virtual‑circuit stores full path
D. Neither stores any routing information

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In virtual‑circuit networks, a connection identifier (VCI) is used after setup, so intermediate nodes only need that ID. Datagram networks forward each packet based on the complete destination address, requiring full routing decisions at each hop.

Q8. Apply the principle of multiplexing: In a network that carries both voice (circuit‑switched) and data (packet‑switched) traffic, which hybrid architecture best optimizes bandwidth usage?

A. Pure circuit switching for all traffic
B. Pure packet switching for all traffic
C. Separate dedicated links for voice and data
D. Integrated switching where voice uses circuit‑switched sub‑channels and data shares remaining capacity ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

An integrated approach reserves fixed sub‑channels for voice, ensuring low latency, while allowing data packets to use any unused capacity, thus improving overall bandwidth utilization compared with separate or single‑mode architectures.

Q9. How does packet switching affect bandwidth utilization compared to circuit switching?

A. It reduces utilization because packets are smaller
B. It increases utilization by allowing statistical multiplexing ✅
C. It keeps utilization constant
D. It requires dedicated bandwidth per flow

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Packet switching enables many flows to share the same link, filling gaps left by idle periods of other flows, which raises overall bandwidth utilization through statistical multiplexing, unlike circuit switching which reserves fixed bandwidth regardless of actual use.

Q10. Synthesize a hybrid switching model that combines the reliability of message switching with the low latency of packet switching. Which of the following best describes such a model?

A. Store‑and‑forward only for control packets
B. Segment messages into packets, forward each packet immediately, but retain full‑message acknowledgment ✅
C. Use circuit switching for data and packet switching for control
D. Apply message switching on high‑capacity links and packet switching on low‑capacity links

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By breaking a large message into packets and forwarding each as soon as it arrives (packet switching), while still acknowledging the complete message at the destination, the model retains reliability of message acknowledgment and reduces latency compared with pure store‑and‑forward.

Q11. Analyze how the choice of switching method influences the scalability of a network as the number of nodes grows from 100 to 10 000.

A. Circuit switching scales linearly because each new node requires a new dedicated circuit
B. Message switching scales poorly due to increased storage requirements
C. Packet switching scales best because it shares links among many connections ✅
D. All methods scale equally

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Packet switching's statistical multiplexing allows many more nodes to share the same physical infrastructure, making it far more scalable than circuit switching, which would need a combinatorial increase in circuits, or message switching, which would stress storage buffers.

Q12. Given the end‑to‑end delay formula \( D = \frac{L}{R} + \frac{Q}{C} \) where \(L\) is packet length, \(R\) is transmission rate, \(Q\) is queuing delay, and \(C\) is link capacity, which switching method minimizes the \(\frac{Q}{C}\) term under heavy traffic?

A. Circuit switching
B. Message switching
C. Packet switching ✅
D. Broadcast switching

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Packet switching distributes traffic across many packets, reducing queue buildup and thus the queuing delay term \(\frac{Q}{C}\). Circuit switching can still experience queuing if the reserved circuit is overloaded, while message switching often creates larger queues due to whole‑message storage.

Q13. If a network designer must guarantee delivery of large files with minimal retransmission but also support interactive applications, which combination of switching methods should be prioritized?

A. Message switching for large files and packet switching for interactive traffic ✅
B. Circuit switching for all traffic
C. Packet switching for both types
D. Hybrid switching with packet switching for files and circuit switching for interactive

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Message switching, by storing the entire file before forwarding, reduces the chance of packet loss and retransmission for bulk transfers, while packet switching's low‑delay handling suits interactive applications, providing a balanced solution.
