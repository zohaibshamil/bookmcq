What is Line coding process of converting digital data to digital signals?

Line coding is the fundamental process of converting a stream of binary digital data into a specific pattern of voltage or current pulses so that the information can be physically transmitted over a digital communication medium.

📝 All Line coding process of converting digital data to digital signals MCQs

Q1. What is the primary purpose of line coding in digital communication systems?

A. To convert analog data to digital signals
B. To encrypt data for security
C. To convert digital data into a digital signal suitable for transmission ✅
D. To compress data for efficient storage

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Line coding's main role is to map a binary data stream into a waveform that can be transmitted over a physical medium. It does not involve encryption, compression, or analog‑to‑digital conversion; rather, it creates a digital signal that retains the original bit pattern while meeting channel requirements.

Q2. Which line coding scheme inherently produces a zero DC component in the transmitted signal?

A. NRZ-L
B. Manchester ✅
C. NRZ-I
D. Unipolar NRZ

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Manchester encoding always includes a transition in the middle of each bit period, causing equal amounts of positive and negative voltage over time. This balanced pattern eliminates any net DC component, unlike unipolar schemes that can accumulate a DC offset.

Q3. What determines the voltage level of the transmitted bit in NRZ‑I?

A. The current bit alone
B. The previous bit’s level and the current bit ✅
C. The clock frequency
D. The line impedance

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

In NRZ‑I, a logical ‘1’ triggers a transition while a ‘0’ leaves the level unchanged. Therefore the voltage at any instant depends on the previous level and whether the current bit forces a transition, making the prior state essential.

Q4. What does the abbreviation “AMI” stand for in line coding?

A. Alternating Mark Inversion ✅
B. Amplitude Modulation Inversion
C. Asynchronous Manchester Interface
D. Adaptive Multi‑Level Inversion

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

AMI refers to Alternate Mark Inversion, a bipolar line‑coding method where each ‘1’ (mark) alternates polarity, ensuring zero average DC while zeros are represented by no voltage.

Q5. If a data stream consists of a long sequence of zeros, what effect does NRZ‑L have on the baseline of the transmitted signal?

A. Baseline remains constant ✅
B. Baseline drifts upward
C. Baseline drifts downward
D. Baseline alternates each bit

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

NRZ‑L maps a binary ‘0’ to a low level (often 0 V) and a ‘1’ to a high level. A long run of zeros therefore holds the signal at the low level, keeping the baseline steady without drift.

Q6. Suppose a line coding scheme uses a transition for each ‘1’ and no transition for ‘0’ (NRZ‑I). If the receiver observes a transition at the start of a bit period, what can be inferred about the transmitted bit?

A. Bit is 0
B. Bit is 1 ✅
C. Bit could be either
D. Error

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A transition at the beginning of a bit period in NRZ‑I indicates that the current bit is a logical ‘1’, because only a ‘1’ causes a change of state. A ‘0’ would leave the voltage unchanged, so the presence of a transition uniquely identifies a ‘1’.

Q7. In Manchester encoding, each bit period contains a transition in the middle. If the receiver detects a transition at the start of a bit interval, what does this indicate about synchronization?

A. Receiver is correctly synchronized
B. Receiver is out of sync by half a bit period ✅
C. The transmitted bit is a ‘1’
D. The transmitted bit is a ‘0’

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Manchester’s defined transition occurs at the middle of the bit. Detecting a transition at the beginning suggests the receiver’s clock is misaligned by half a bit, causing it to sample the wrong edge and interpret the timing incorrectly.

Q8. A communication system uses Differential Manchester encoding. If a sequence of bits ‘1 0 0 1’ is transmitted, how many transitions will appear in the signal over the four bit periods?

A. Four
B. Five
C. Six ✅
D. Seven

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Differential Manchester always includes a mid‑bit transition. Additionally, a start‑of‑bit transition occurs for each ‘0’. The pattern ‘1 0 0 1’ therefore has two start transitions (for the two zeros) plus four mid‑bit transitions, totaling six transitions.

Q9. When using B8ZS (Bipolar with 8 Zero Substitution), what is the purpose of inserting a violation pattern after eight consecutive zeros?

A. To maintain synchronization ✅
B. To increase data rate
C. To reduce power consumption
D. To encode a ‘1’

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

B8ZS replaces a run of eight zeros with a predefined bipolar violation pattern. This artificial pattern creates transitions, allowing the receiver to stay synchronized and recover the clock despite the long zero run that would otherwise produce no edges.

Q10. If a line code has a guaranteed minimum of one transition per bit period, what impact does this have on clock recovery?

A. Makes clock recovery more difficult
B. Eliminates need for a separate clock
C. Allows reliable clock extraction from the data ✅
D. Has no impact

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Having at least one transition per bit ensures that timing information is embedded in the data stream, enabling the receiver to lock onto the bit rate without a separate clock channel, which simplifies hardware and improves robustness.

Q11. Consider a channel that cannot pass DC components. Which line coding scheme would be unsuitable for this channel?

A. Manchester
B. NRZ‑I
C. Unipolar NRZ ✅
D. Differential Manchester

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Unipolar NRZ represents a ‘1’ with a positive voltage and ‘0’ with zero voltage, producing a DC component proportional to the data’s average level. Channels that block DC cannot convey this signal reliably, making unipolar NRZ inappropriate.

Q12. Which line coding scheme provides the highest bandwidth efficiency: NRZ‑L, Manchester, or Differential Manchester?

A. NRZ‑L ✅
B. Manchester
C. Differential Manchester
D. All equal

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Bandwidth efficiency is measured by the ratio of data rate to required bandwidth. NRZ‑L transmits one symbol per bit without extra transitions, whereas Manchester and Differential Manchester double the fundamental frequency due to mandatory mid‑bit transitions, making NRZ‑L the most efficient.

Q13. Compare the spectral characteristics of NRZ‑L and AMI. Which statement is true?

A. NRZ‑L has lower frequency components than AMI
B. AMI has a spectral null at zero frequency while NRZ‑L does not ✅
C. Both have identical spectra
D. NRZ‑L has a spectral null at zero frequency

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

AMI is a bipolar scheme that alternates polarity for ‘1’s, producing no net DC and thus a spectral null at zero frequency. NRZ‑L, being unipolar, retains a DC component, so its spectrum does not vanish at zero hertz.

Q14. Evaluate the suitability of Manchester encoding for a power‑line communication system where the channel attenuates high frequencies more than low frequencies.

A. Highly suitable
B. Unsuitable because of high frequency content ✅
C. Suitable because it eliminates DC
D. Neither suitable nor unsuitable

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Manchester doubles the required bandwidth by inserting a transition in the middle of each bit, placing significant energy at higher frequencies. In channels that heavily attenuate high frequencies, such as power‑line links, this leads to signal loss and reduced performance.

Q15. Given a data rate of 10 Mbps, calculate the minimum required bandwidth for NRZ‑L and Manchester line codes assuming ideal Nyquist bandwidth (i.e., \(B = \frac{R}{2}\) for NRZ and \(B = R\) for Manchester). Which of the following is correct?

A. NRZ‑L: 5 MHz, Manchester: 10 MHz ✅
B. NRZ‑L: 10 MHz, Manchester: 5 MHz
C. NRZ‑L: 5 MHz, Manchester: 5 MHz
D. NRZ‑L: 10 MHz, Manchester: 10 MHz

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using Nyquist’s rule, NRZ‑L requires half the data rate as its bandwidth, so \(B_{NRZ}=10/2=5\) MHz. Manchester needs a full‑rate transition, giving \(B_{Manchester}=10\) MHz. Therefore the correct pair is 5 MHz for NRZ‑L and 10 MHz for Manchester.

Q16. Differentiate between NRZ‑I and Differential Manchester in terms of transition rules and clock recovery capability.

A. Both use a transition at the start of each bit
B. NRZ‑I has transitions only for ‘1’, while Differential Manchester has a mandatory mid‑bit transition ✅
C. NRZ‑I provides better clock recovery than Differential Manchester
D. Differential Manchester does not use mid‑bit transitions

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

NRZ‑I triggers a transition only when a ‘1’ is transmitted, leaving the level unchanged for a ‘0’. Differential Manchester, however, guarantees a transition in the middle of every bit period and uses an optional start transition to encode the bit value, enhancing clock recovery.

Q17. Which of the following line coding methods inherently supports error detection by violating the bipolar rule?

A. NRZ‑L
B. Manchester
C. AMI with B8ZS ✅
D. Differential Manchester

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

B8ZS replaces a run of eight zeros with a predefined violation pattern that deliberately breaks the alternating‑mark rule of AMI. This violation is recognizable by the receiver and can be used to detect errors or synchronize after long idle periods.

Q18. Assume a channel with maximum allowable signal swing of ±1 V. For a bipolar AMI code, what is the peak‑to‑peak voltage requirement?

A. 1 V
B. 2 V ✅
C. 0.5 V
D. 4 V

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Bipolar AMI uses +V for a ‘1’ of one polarity and –V for the next ‘1’, with zeros at 0 V. If the permissible swing is ±1 V, the highest positive level is +1 V and the lowest negative level is –1 V, giving a peak‑to‑peak voltage of 2 V.

Q19. When comparing NRZ‑L and NRZ‑I, which scheme typically results in lower power consumption for a random data stream?

A. NRZ‑L
B. NRZ‑I ✅
C. Both equal
D. Depends on clock

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

NRZ‑I produces transitions only for ‘1’s, so on average fewer voltage changes occur compared to NRZ‑L, which may toggle for each bit depending on the data pattern. Fewer transitions generally translate to lower dynamic power consumption in the driver circuitry.

Q20. If a line code must minimize electromagnetic interference (EMI), which characteristic is most beneficial?

A. High DC component
B. Frequent transitions
C. Balanced polarity with zero DC ✅
D. Unbalanced levels

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A balanced bipolar waveform with equal positive and negative pulses eliminates net DC and reduces low‑frequency spectral components, which are major contributors to radiated EMI. Hence, zero‑DC, polarity‑balanced codes are preferred for EMI mitigation.

Q21. A system uses 4B/5B block coding followed by NRZ‑L line coding. What is the overall line coding efficiency (ratio of data bits to transmitted bits)?

A. 0.8 ✅
B. 1
C. 0.75
D. 0.9

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

Q22. Apply the principle of transition density: why does Manchester encoding guarantee at least one transition per bit period?

A. Because it encodes each bit as a high‑low or low‑high pattern ✅
B. Because it uses bipolar levels
C. Because it relies on clock edges
D. Because it suppresses DC

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Manchester represents a ‘0’ as a high‑to‑low transition and a ‘1’ as a low‑to‑high transition within each bit interval. This mandatory mid‑bit change ensures that every bit contains at least one transition, providing inherent timing information.

Q23. Explain how clock recovery is achieved in a differential Manchester system without a separate clock channel.

A. By detecting the mid‑bit transition that occurs in every bit ✅
B. By using a dedicated pilot tone
C. By measuring the amplitude of the signal
D. By interpreting the polarity of the signal

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Differential Manchester always inserts a transition at the middle of each bit period, regardless of the data value. The receiver locks onto these regular mid‑bit edges to reconstruct the clock, eliminating the need for an auxiliary clock line.

Q24. Synthesize the effect of line coding on signal integrity: how does a coding scheme with a guaranteed transition help mitigate intersymbol interference?

A. It reduces ISI by limiting low‑frequency components ✅
B. It increases ISI by adding more transitions
C. It has no effect on ISI
D. It only affects noise

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A guaranteed transition forces the signal to change polarity regularly, which suppresses low‑frequency energy that can cause baseline wander and intersymbol interference. By keeping the spectrum away from DC, the receiver can more easily distinguish successive symbols, improving integrity.

Q25. Given a scenario where a communication link suffers from baseline wander due to long runs of zeros, propose a line coding modification to alleviate this problem and justify its effectiveness.

A. Switch to NRZ‑L
B. Use Manchester encoding ✅
C. Apply unipolar encoding
D. Increase signal amplitude

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Manchester inserts a transition in the middle of every bit, ensuring that even long sequences of zeros generate regular edges. These edges prevent the baseline from drifting, because the average voltage remains centered, directly counteracting baseline wander.

Q26. Apply the concept of violation pulses in B8ZS: what logical condition triggers the insertion of the eight‑zero substitution pattern?

A. When a ‘1’ follows eight zeros
B. When eight consecutive zeros occur ✅
C. When the line is idle
D. When a framing error is detected

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

B8ZS is designed to replace a run of eight consecutive zeros with a specific bipolar violation pattern. The pattern introduces controlled transitions, allowing the receiver to maintain synchronization and detect the original zero sequence.

Q27. Explain why AMI line coding, despite having zero DC, can still suffer from long‑run problems and how scrambling addresses this issue.

A. Because AMI alternates polarity, long runs of zeros produce no transitions, leading to synchronization loss; scrambling randomizes the pattern to ensure frequent transitions ✅
B. Because AMI has high DC, scrambling removes it
C. Because AMI uses too many levels, scrambling reduces levels
D. Because AMI cannot be combined with NRZ

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Although AMI balances positive and negative pulses, a long string of zeros yields no voltage changes, making clock recovery difficult. Scrambling reorders the bit pattern to break up zero runs, inserting pseudo‑random transitions that preserve synchronization while retaining AMI’s DC‑balance.
