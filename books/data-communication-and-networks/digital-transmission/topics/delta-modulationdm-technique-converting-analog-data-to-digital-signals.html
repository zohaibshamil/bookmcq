Q1. What does DM stand for in digital communication?

🔹 A. Direct Modulation
🔹 B. Delta Modulation
🔹 C. Digital Modulation
🔹 D. Differential Modulation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Delta Modulation is abbreviated as DM. The correct expansion is Delta Modulation, which describes a technique that encodes the difference between successive samples using a single bit. This answer is correct because it directly matches the standard terminology used in communication theory, distinguishing it from other modulation types.

Q2. If the step size \\( \\Delta \\) is too large, what is the most likely effect on the reconstructed signal?

🔹 A. Increased slope overload distortion
🔹 B. Higher granular noise
🔹 C. Reduced signal amplitude
🔹 D. No effect on quality

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A large step size makes the modulator unable to follow rapid signal changes, causing slope overload distortion where the output jumps in large increments. This leads to noticeable distortion in the reconstructed waveform, which is the primary effect of an oversized step size in DM systems.

Q3. Which statement correctly compares DM to PCM regarding bandwidth?

🔹 A. DM requires more bandwidth than PCM
🔹 B. DM typically requires less bandwidth than PCM
🔹 C. Both require identical bandwidth
🔹 D. PCM uses less bandwidth only for high‑rate signals

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Delta Modulation encodes each sample with a single binary bit, while PCM uses multiple bits per sample. Consequently, DM transmits fewer bits per unit time, leading to a lower required bandwidth compared to PCM, assuming the same sampling rate. This makes DM more bandwidth‑efficient in many scenarios.

Q4. In DM, the output of the comparator at each sampling instant indicates what?

🔹 A. The absolute value of the input signal
🔹 B. The phase of the input signal
🔹 C. Whether the input is above or below the previous reconstructed value
🔹 D. The frequency of the input signal

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The comparator decides if the current input sample exceeds the previous reconstructed value. If it does, the output is a logical '1'; otherwise, it is '0'. This binary decision drives the integrator to increase or decrease the reconstructed signal, forming the core of DM operation.

Q5. What is the primary component that determines the quantization step in DM?

🔹 A. Sampling frequency
🔹 B. Signal amplitude
🔹 C. Noise variance
🔹 D. The step size \\( \\Delta \\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The quantization step in Delta Modulation is explicitly defined by the parameter \\( \\Delta \\). It sets the amplitude increment used by the integrator to track the input signal. While other factors like sampling rate influence performance, the step size alone dictates the granularity of each update.

Q6. If the sampling frequency is increased while keeping step size constant, what happens to the probability of slope overload?

🔹 A. It remains unchanged
🔹 B. It increases slightly
🔹 C. It fluctuates unpredictably
🔹 D. It decreases

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Increasing the sampling frequency provides more update opportunities per unit time, allowing the integrator to follow faster signal changes even with a fixed step size. Consequently, the chance that the signal slope exceeds the step size between samples (slope overload) diminishes, improving tracking accuracy.

Q7. Which of the following is a disadvantage of using a very small step size in DM?

🔹 A. Increased granular noise
🔹 B. Higher slope overload probability
🔹 C. Reduced transmission power
🔹 D. Longer system latency

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A very small step size causes the reconstructed signal to make many minute adjustments, which appear as random fluctuations known as granular noise. While this reduces slope overload, the fine granularity introduces audible noise, making the output sound less clean, especially for low‑amplitude signals.

Q8. In a DM system, the demodulator essentially performs which operation?

🔹 A. Differentiation of the binary stream
🔹 B. Integration of the binary output
🔹 C. Fourier transformation of the signal
🔹 D. Amplitude demodulation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The demodulator in Delta Modulation integrates the binary sequence generated by the comparator. Each '1' adds the step size \\( \\Delta \\) to the reconstructed signal, while each '0' subtracts it. This cumulative integration recreates an approximation of the original analog waveform.

🔹 A. Granular noise

📖 Explanation:

When \\( \\Delta \\) equals half the peak‑to‑peak amplitude, the modulator cannot follow the steep slopes of the sinusoid, especially near its peaks. The resulting error is slope overload, where the reconstructed signal lags behind the input, producing noticeable distortion that overshadows granular effects.

Q10. Compare the impact of increasing the sampling rate versus decreasing the step size on the signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) of a DM system.

🔹 A. Increasing the sampling rate degrades SNR, decreasing step size improves it
🔹 B. Both actions equally improve SNR
🔹 C. Increasing sampling rate improves SNR, decreasing step size has diminishing returns
🔹 D. Decreasing step size improves SNR, increasing sampling rate has diminishing returns

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Both higher sampling rates and smaller step sizes raise SNR by reducing slope overload and granular noise, respectively. However, once the step size becomes sufficiently small, further reduction yields only marginal SNR gains because granular noise dominates, whereas raising the sampling rate continues to provide noticeable improvement.

Q11. How does adaptive delta modulation (ADM) modify the step size during operation, and why is this beneficial for speech signals?

🔹 A. It varies \\( \\Delta \\) based on recent error trends, reducing both granular and slope overload
🔹 B. It keeps \\( \\Delta \\) constant to simplify design
🔹 C. It doubles \\( \\Delta \\) every frame regardless of signal dynamics
🔹 D. It selects \\( \\Delta \\) randomly to spread errors

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

ADM monitors the pattern of successive comparator decisions and adjusts \\( \\Delta \\) upward when many identical decisions occur (indicating slope overload) and downward when decisions alternate (indicating granular noise). This dynamic adaptation matches the varying amplitude and rate of speech, yielding lower overall distortion.

Q12. If a DM transmitter mistakenly uses a step size that is double the optimal value, which error type will likely increase most significantly?

🔹 A. Granular noise
🔹 B. Slope overload distortion
🔹 C. Phase jitter
🔹 D. Amplitude clipping

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Doubling the optimal step size makes the modulator prone to overshooting rapid signal changes, leading to slope overload distortion. The larger increments cause the reconstructed waveform to jump beyond the true signal, especially during steep slopes, while granular noise actually decreases with a larger step.

Q13. Consider two DM systems: one with fixed step size \\( \\Delta \\) and another with adaptive step size. Which system is more likely to maintain a constant SNR across varying input amplitudes?

🔹 A. The fixed‑step system
🔹 B. Both systems perform identically
🔹 C. The adaptive‑step system
🔹 D. Neither system can maintain constant SNR

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

An adaptive step size can increase \\( \\Delta \\) when the input has large amplitude changes and reduce it for small variations, thereby balancing slope overload and granular noise. This flexibility enables a more uniform SNR across a wide range of signal amplitudes compared to a fixed‑step design.

Q14. Explain why DM can be implemented with a simple binary comparator and integrator, whereas PCM requires a multi‑bit quantizer.

🔹 A. DM uses analog-to-digital conversion while PCM uses digital-to-analog conversion
🔹 B. PCM needs higher power consumption making binary components unsuitable
🔹 C. Binary decisions in DM eliminate the need for multi‑level quantization, simplifying hardware
🔹 D. PCM’s binary nature demands more complex circuitry than DM

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Delta Modulation encodes each sample as a single binary decision (increase or decrease), allowing a single comparator and an integrator to generate the output. PCM, on the other hand, quantizes each sample into multiple bits, requiring a multi‑bit quantizer and a DAC, which adds hardware complexity.

📖 Explanation:

A rapid amplitude jump exceeds the current step size, causing the modulator to lag behind the input. This results in slope overload distortion until the adaptive algorithm increases \\( \\Delta \\) to catch up with the new signal level.

Q16. In terms of bandwidth efficiency, how does DM compare to differential pulse‑code modulation (DPCM) when both use the same sampling frequency?

Both DM and DPCM aim to reduce redundancy, but DM transmits a single binary bit per sample, whereas DPCM may transmit multiple bits representing the difference. Consequently, DM generally occupies less spectral bandwidth under equal sampling conditions.

Oversampling raises the number of sampling instants per unit time, giving the integrator more opportunities to correct errors. This finer time resolution diminishes the appearance of granular noise, leading to a smoother reconstructed waveform, especially for slowly varying signals.

Therefore, the condition \\( m T_s > \\Delta \\) accurately captures the scenario where the modulator cannot keep up with the signal’s rate of change.

Q23. If a DM system is subjected to a channel that flips bits with probability 0.01, how does this affect the reconstructed signal compared to PCM with the same bit error rate?

🔹 A. DM error leads to cumulative drift, making distortion larger
🔹 B. PCM error causes more severe distortion than DM
🔹 C. Both systems suffer identical distortion levels
🔹 D. DM can correct bit errors automatically, so distortion is negligible

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

In DM, each bit determines whether the reconstructed signal steps up or down; a flipped bit causes the integrator to move in the wrong direction, creating a cumulative error that propagates until corrected. PCM errors affect individual sample values but do not accumulate, so DM generally experiences larger distortion for the same bit‑error probability.
