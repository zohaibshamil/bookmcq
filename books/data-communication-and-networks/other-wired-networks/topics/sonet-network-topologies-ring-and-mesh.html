📝 All SONET network topologies ring and mesh MCQs

Q1. What is the data rate of an OC‑3 link?

🔹 A. 155.520 Mbps
🔹 B. 51.840 Mbps
🔹 C. 622.080 Mbps
🔹 D. 466.560 Mbps

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The SONET standard defines OC‑3 as three times the OC‑1 rate, giving a line rate of 155.520 Mbps. This value is widely used for metropolitan-area connections and is listed directly in the OC‑x table. Therefore the correct choice is the 155.520 Mbps option.

Q2. Which STS level corresponds exactly to an OC‑12 signal?

🔹 A. STS‑9
🔹 B. STS‑12
🔹 C. STS‑18
🔹 D. STS‑24

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

OC‑12 is defined as the optical carrier that carries an STS‑12 payload. The numeric relationship is one‑to‑one, so the STM‑1 hierarchy maps OC‑12 to STS‑12. Recognizing this direct mapping helps identify the correct STS level, making option B the right answer.

Q3. If a SONET frame carries 18 STS‑1 channels, how many full STS‑3 channels can it contain?

🔹 A. 6
🔹 B. 9
🔹 C. 12
🔹 D. 3

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each STS‑3 channel is three times the bandwidth of an STS‑1 channel. Dividing the total of 18 STS‑1 channels by 3 yields 6 STS‑3 channels. This straightforward division demonstrates the multiplexing relationship, so the answer is 6 (Option A).

Q4. Suppose an OC‑48 link is upgraded to OC‑96. By what factor does its capacity increase?

🔹 A. 2
🔹 B. 3
🔹 C. 4
🔹 D. 1.5

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

OC‑96 carries twice the bandwidth of OC‑48 because the OC number directly indicates the multiple of the base OC‑1 rate. Hence the capacity doubles, which corresponds to a factor of 2. The correct answer is therefore the option labeled 2, which is placed in column C.

Q5. If an add/drop multiplexer removes 4 STS‑3 channels, how many STS‑1 channels are effectively removed?

🔹 A. 12
🔹 B. 8
🔹 C. 4
🔹 D. 16

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each STS‑3 channel comprises three STS‑1 channels. Removing four STS‑3 channels therefore eliminates 4 × 3 = 12 STS‑1 channels. The calculation shows that 12 STS‑1 channels are dropped, which is listed as option B.

Q6. A SONET system multiplexes an STS‑9 payload that uses 90 % of its capacity into an STS‑18 frame. What percentage of the STS‑18 payload is occupied by the STS‑9 payload?

🔹 A. 0.45
🔹 B. 0.9
🔹 C. 1.8
🔹 D. 0.6

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

An STS‑18 frame has twice the bandwidth of an STS‑9 frame. If the STS‑9 payload occupies 90 % of its own capacity, it will occupy 90 % ÷ 2 = 45 % of the larger STS‑18 capacity. Therefore the correct answer is 45 %, which appears in column D.

Q7. An OC‑24 link runs at 1244.160 Mbps. If optical overhead consumes 2.5 % of the total bandwidth, what is the effective data bandwidth?

🔹 A. 1213.056 Mbps
🔹 B. 1220 Mbps
🔹 C. 1200 Mbps
🔹 D. 1240 Mbps

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The usable data rate equals the total rate multiplied by (1 − overhead). Thus 1244.160 × 0.975 = 1213.056 Mbps. This precise calculation matches option A, confirming it as the correct choice.

Q8. Which statement correctly describes the relationship between OC‑3 and OC‑9 rates?

🔹 A. OC‑9 is exactly three times OC‑3
🔹 B. OC‑9 is double OC‑3
🔹 C. OC‑3 is half OC‑9
🔹 D. OC‑3 equals OC‑9

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

OC‑9 carries three times the bandwidth of OC‑3 because the OC number is a direct multiple of the base OC‑1 rate. Consequently, OC‑9 is three times OC‑3, making the statement in option C accurate.

Q9. Why does using a single STS‑12 channel often give lower overhead than three separate STS‑4 channels?

🔹 A. Lower overhead
🔹 B. Higher latency
🔹 C. Same capacity
🔹 D. More complexity

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Aggregating three STS‑4 channels into one STS‑12 eliminates the need for three separate framing structures, reducing the amount of overhead bits required. This consolidation therefore results in lower overhead compared with three independent channels, so option A is correct.

Q10. Which device changes the payload at intermediate nodes without altering other channels?

🔹 A. STS multiplexer/demultiplexer
🔹 B. Regenerator
🔹 C. Add/drop multiplexer
🔹 D. Terminal

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A regenerator amplifies and reshapes the optical signal but does not modify the payload. The device that inserts or extracts specific channels while leaving the remaining traffic untouched is the add/drop multiplexer, which is therefore the correct answer.

Q11. Between a single OC‑48 line and six OC‑8 lines (each 402.336 Mbps), which design offers better fault tolerance?

🔹 A. Design i (single OC‑48)
🔹 B. Design ii (six OC‑8)
🔹 C. Both are equal
🔹 D. Cannot determine

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Using multiple OC‑8 links creates redundancy; if one link fails, the others continue to carry traffic, preserving partial connectivity. A single OC‑48 line provides no such redundancy, so the six‑link architecture (Design ii) yields superior fault tolerance.

Q12. When multiplexing STS‑1 directly into STS‑18 versus hierarchically (STS‑1 → STS‑3 → STS‑9 → STS‑18), which method uses fewer multiplexing stages?

🔹 A. Direct STS‑1 → STS‑18
🔹 B. Hierarchical approach
🔹 C. Both have the same number
🔹 D. Neither is possible

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Directly combining 18 × STS‑1 into an STS‑18 bypasses intermediate stages, requiring only one multiplexing operation. The hierarchical method introduces additional stages (STS‑3 then STS‑9), increasing complexity. Hence the direct method uses fewer stages, corresponding to option A.

Q13. If optical overhead rises from 2 % to 4 % on an OC‑12 link, how does the usable capacity change?

🔹 A. Capacity halves
🔹 B. Capacity reduces by 2 %
🔹 C. Capacity reduces by 4 %
🔹 D. Capacity unchanged

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Increasing overhead from 2 % to 4 % subtracts an extra 2 % of the total bandwidth from the usable payload. Therefore the effective data capacity drops by 2 % of the original, which is reflected in option C.

Q14. Apply the relationship STS‑3 = 3 × STS‑1 to find the STS‑1 rate when STS‑3 is 155.520 Mbps.

🔹 A. 51.840 Mbps
🔹 B. 46.656 Mbps
🔹 C. 52.000 Mbps
🔹 D. 53.000 Mbps

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Dividing the STS‑3 rate by three gives the STS‑1 rate: 155.520 ÷ 3 = 51.840 Mbps. This straightforward calculation confirms that the STS‑1 channel operates at 51.840 Mbps, which is option A.

Q15. Why does SONET employ hierarchical STS levels rather than a single flat rate?

🔹 A. To simplify routing
🔹 B. To match Ethernet frames
🔹 C. To allow incremental bandwidth scaling
🔹 D. To reduce latency

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The hierarchy lets network operators add capacity in small steps (STS‑1, STS‑3, STS‑12, etc.) without redesigning the entire infrastructure. This incremental scaling fits diverse traffic demands and promotes efficient use of fiber, making the scaling advantage the key reason.

Q16. A network needs at least 800 Mbps of payload with no more than 10 % overhead. Which minimum OC level satisfies this requirement?

🔹 A. OC‑12
🔹 B. OC‑18
🔹 C. OC‑24
🔹 D. OC‑48

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

OC‑12 provides 622.080 Mbps, which is insufficient. OC‑18 offers 933.120 Mbps, exceeding 800 Mbps while still keeping overhead below 10 %. Therefore the smallest OC level that meets the demand is OC‑18, corresponding to option B.

Q17. Using a single STS‑24 channel in an OC‑24 link can reduce latency compared with multiple OC‑12 links. What is the latency effect?

🔹 A. Latency higher
🔹 B. Latency lower
🔹 C. Latency unchanged
🔹 D. Latency unpredictable

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Aggregating traffic into one higher‑order STS channel reduces the number of intermediate multiplexing and demultiplexing stages, shortening the signal’s path and thus lowering overall latency. Consequently, latency is lower, which is presented in option C.

Q18. If a signal loses 0.5 dB per 50 km and a regenerator is placed every 200 km, what is the maximum loss before regeneration?

🔹 A. 2 dB
🔹 B. 1 dB
🔹 C. 0.5 dB
🔹 D. 4 dB

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Over 200 km the loss accumulates as 0.5 dB × 4 = 2 dB. This is the maximum attenuation tolerated before the signal reaches a regenerator, matching option A.

Q19. Moving from STM‑4 (OC‑12) to STM‑8 (OC‑24) has what effect on network capacity?

🔹 A. Capacity doubles
🔹 B. Capacity triples
🔹 C. Capacity stays the same
🔹 D. Capacity halves

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

STM‑8 carries twice the bandwidth of STM‑4 because the OC number doubles (12 → 24). Therefore the overall transport capacity doubles, which is described by option D.

Q20. In a SONET network, which device is responsible for inserting and extracting payloads at intermediate nodes without affecting other channels?

🔹 A. STS multiplexer/demultiplexer
🔹 B. Regenerator
🔹 C. Add/drop multiplexer
🔹 D. Terminal

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The add/drop multiplexer selectively adds or removes specific STS channels while leaving the remaining traffic untouched, enabling flexible routing at intermediate points. This function matches option C, making it the correct answer.
