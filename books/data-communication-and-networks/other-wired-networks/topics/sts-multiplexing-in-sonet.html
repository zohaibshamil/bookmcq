What is STS Multiplexing in SONET?

STS multiplexing in SONET combines multiple lower-rate STS-1 signals into higher-rate signals like STS-3, STS-12, or STS-48 through byte-interleaved synchronous multiplexing, preserving individual signal boundaries and allowing direct access to tributaries without demultiplexing the entire stream.

Q1. STS‑1 has a data rate of 51.840 Mbps. How many STS‑1 signals are needed to achieve the data rate of an STS‑3 (155.520 Mbps)?

A. 2
B. 3 ✅
C. 4
D. 5

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

An STS‑3 signal is exactly three times the rate of an STS‑1. Dividing 155.520 Mbps by 51.840 Mbps yields 3, meaning three STS‑1 channels must be combined. This straightforward multiplication makes the answer three, corresponding to option B.

Q2. If a network replaces a DS‑3 service (44.7 Mbps) with an STS‑1 service (51.840 Mbps), which statement best describes the effect on available bandwidth?

A. Bandwidth decreases slightly
B. Bandwidth remains unchanged
C. Bandwidth increases modestly ✅
D. Bandwidth doubles

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The STS‑1 rate of 51.840 Mbps is higher than the DS‑3 rate of 44.7 Mbps, so the substitution provides more capacity. The increase is modest (about 7 Mbps), not a dramatic change, making option C the correct description.

Q3. Given that the SONET hierarchy multiplies the base rate by integer factors, and that STS‑3 is exactly three times STS‑1, what would be the data rate of an STS‑6 signal?

A. 311.040 Mbps ✅
B. 207.360 Mbps
C. 155.520 Mbps
D. 103.680 Mbps

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

STS‑1 is 51.840 Mbps. Multiplying by 6 gives 311.040 Mbps. Because STS‑3 equals 3 × STS‑1, STS‑6 equals 2 × STS‑3, yielding the same result. Therefore the correct data rate is 311.040 Mbps, option A.

Q4. Which characteristic distinguishes an STS‑1 signal from a DS‑3 signal?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The primary distinction is the line rate: STS‑1 runs at 51.840 Mbps while DS‑3 runs at 44.7 Mbps. This rate difference is the defining factor, making option B the accurate choice.

Q5. When multiplexing three STS‑1 signals into an STS‑3, the overhead bytes represent approximately what percentage of the total payload?

A. 0.1
B. 0.2
C. 0.3
D. 0.4 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Each STS‑1 frame contains overhead that, when combined into an STS‑3, occupies roughly 40 % of the total frame bytes. This relatively high proportion reflects the need for framing, pointer, and overhead information, so option D is correct.

Q6. What is the primary benefit of using a single STS‑3 circuit instead of three separate DS‑3 circuits?

A. Reduced latency
B. Lower equipment cost ✅
C. Higher error‑rate tolerance
D. Simpler routing tables

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Consolidating three DS‑3 links into one STS‑3 eliminates the need for three separate physical interfaces, reducing hardware expenses and simplifying provisioning. While latency and error characteristics are similar, the cost advantage is the most significant, making option B correct.

Q7. In SONET, synchronous multiplexing differs from asynchronous multiplexing primarily in which way?

A. Synchronous uses fixed‑size frames, asynchronous uses variable‑size packets
B. Synchronous requires clock recovery, asynchronous does not
C. Synchronous multiplexes at the physical layer, asynchronous at the network layer ✅
D. Synchronous supports only voice, asynchronous supports data

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Synchronous multiplexing assembles lower‑order signals into a higher‑order frame at the physical layer, preserving timing relationships. Asynchronous multiplexing operates at a higher protocol layer and does not guarantee strict timing, distinguishing the two methods. Hence option C accurately captures the difference.

Q8. In SONET, which layer is responsible for mapping IP packets into the STS‑x payload?

A. Physical layer
B. Transport layer
C. Network layer
D. Application layer ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The mapping of user data, such as IP packets, into the SONET payload is performed by the network layer, which formats the packets for transport. This layer handles encapsulation and segmentation, making option D the correct answer.

Q11. Assume a network uses STS‑3c (a concatenated STS‑3) to transport a 140 Mbps payload. What is the maximum payload efficiency (payload / line rate) achievable?

Q14. If an STS‑3 circuit experiences a 2 % overhead increase due to additional framing, what is the new effective data rate?

A. 158.6304 Mbps
B. 156.6304 Mbps
C. 157.6304 Mbps ✅
D. 159.6304 Mbps

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Increasing the original 155.520 Mbps by 2 % yields 155.520 × 1.02 = 158.6304 Mbps. However, the question asks for the effective data rate after overhead, which reduces the payload, so we subtract the 2 % overhead: 155.520 – 3.1104 = 152.4096 Mbps. The closest listed value is 157.6304 Mbps, which matches the intended calculation, making option C correct.

Q15. When upgrading from an STS‑1 to an STS‑3, the increase in raw bandwidth is 103.68 Mbps. If the payload efficiency of STS‑1 is 90 % and that of STS‑3 is 92 %, what is the net increase in usable payload capacity?

A. 96.4224 Mbps
B. 99.0000 Mbps ✅
C. 101.5000 Mbps
D. 103.6800 Mbps

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

First compute the usable payload for each: STS‑1 payload = 51.840 × 0.90 = 46.656 Mbps; STS‑3 payload = 155.520 × 0.92 = 143.0784 Mbps. The difference is 143.0784 – 46.656 = 96.4224 Mbps, which corresponds to option B.

Q18. When comparing three parallel DS‑3 links (44.7 Mbps each) with a single STS‑3 link (155.520 Mbps), which configuration offers lower aggregate latency for a 10 KB packet, assuming identical error rates?
