📝 All Problems with ATM technology MCQs

Q1. What is the bandwidth of a local loop when used for voice?

🔹 A. 4000 Hz
🔹 B. 3000 Hz
🔹 C. 5000 Hz
🔹 D. 2000 Hz

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The local loop is designed to carry voice signals up to the audible range, which is limited to about 4 kHz. Therefore its specified bandwidth is \(4000\text{ Hz}\). The other values are either too low or exceed the standard voice bandwidth, making them incorrect.

Q2. Which component connects a subscriber telephone to the nearest end office?

🔹 A. Local loop
🔹 B. Trunk
🔹 C. Switching office
🔹 D. Regional office

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A local loop is the twisted‑pair cable that runs from the subscriber’s handset to the nearest central (end) office. Trunks link switching offices, while switching and regional offices are higher‑level nodes in the network, so they do not directly connect the subscriber.

Q3. If the bandwidth of the local loop were reduced to 2 kHz, which effect would most likely occur?

🔹 A. Decreased voice quality
🔹 B. Increased data capacity
🔹 C. No change in service
🔹 D. Faster signaling

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Voice signals contain frequencies up to about 4 kHz. Halving the bandwidth to 2 kHz would truncate the higher voice frequencies, producing muffled or tinny speech. Data capacity would not increase, and signaling speed is unrelated to loop bandwidth.

Q4. A city upgrades its local loops from copper to fiber but keeps the same switching office architecture. Which logical consequence follows?

🔹 A. Higher voice clarity and unchanged routing
🔹 B. Reduced need for trunks
🔹 C. Elimination of end offices
🔹 D. Need for new regional offices

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Fiber improves signal quality and capacity, but the hierarchical routing (local loops → end offices → trunks) remains. Consequently, the network can move more data through existing trunks, reducing the pressure to add additional trunk capacity, while the overall architecture stays the same.

Q5. Suppose a trunk line fails during peak hour, causing a 15 % drop in call completion rates. Which inference best explains the impact?

🔹 A. End offices cannot reroute calls
🔹 B. Regional offices lack backup paths
🔹 C. Trunks carry aggregated traffic between offices
🔹 D. Local loops become overloaded

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Trunks aggregate traffic from many end offices; when one fails, the remaining trunks become congested, forcing some local loops to wait longer for switching resources, thus lowering overall call completion. The other statements mischaracterize the network hierarchy.

Q6. How does a trunk differ from a local loop in terms of function?

🔹 A. Trunk connects subscriber to end office; local loop connects offices
🔹 B. Trunk carries aggregated traffic between switching offices; local loop carries a single subscriber’s signal
🔹 C. Both serve the same purpose but use different media
🔹 D. Trunk is analog while local loop is digital

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Both trunk and local loop are transmission media, but they serve distinct roles: a trunk links switching offices and handles many calls simultaneously, whereas a local loop is a point‑to‑point link between a subscriber and its nearest office. The other options confuse the direction of connectivity or media type.

Q7. Compare the role of a tandem office to that of an end office. Which statement is most accurate?

🔹 A. Tandem offices switch calls within a single exchange; end offices connect to subscribers
🔹 B. Tandem offices route calls between exchanges; end offices terminate calls for local subscribers
🔹 C. Both offices perform identical switching functions
🔹 D. End offices handle data only, while tandem offices handle voice

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A tandem office’s primary task is to forward calls between different exchanges, acting as an intermediate node. In contrast, an end office directly terminates calls for the subscribers attached to its local loops. Thus the tandem office routes, while the end office terminates.

Q8. Given that a local loop has a bandwidth of \(4\text{ kHz}\), can it support a voice signal centered at \(3\text{ kHz}\) with a bandwidth of \(1\text{ kHz}\)?

🔹 A. Yes, because the total fits within 4 kHz
🔹 B. No, because the center frequency exceeds the limit
🔹 C. Only if the signal is compressed
🔹 D. It depends on the loop length

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A voice signal centered at 3 kHz with a 1 kHz bandwidth occupies the range 2.5–3.5 kHz, which lies entirely below the 4 kHz limit of the local loop. Therefore the loop can transmit the signal without loss, assuming other impairments are negligible.

Q9. If a regional office upgrades its switching equipment to handle 1.2 times the current call load, which outcome is most likely?

🔹 A. Call completion improves by 20 %
🔹 B. Trunk congestion decreases proportionally
🔹 C. Local loop bandwidth increases
🔹 D. End offices become redundant

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Increasing the processing capacity at a regional office allows it to forward more calls through existing trunks, easing congestion on those trunks. The improvement is not a direct 20 % increase in call completion because other bottlenecks may remain, and hardware upgrades do not affect physical loop bandwidth.

Q10. A new digital codec requires a minimum bandwidth of \(3.5\text{ kHz}\). Will the existing analog local loop support this codec without modification?

🔹 A. Yes, because 4 kHz > 3.5 kHz
🔹 B. No, because analog loops cannot carry digital signals
🔹 C. Only if the loop length is under 1 km
🔹 D. It will require a separate trunk line

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The analog local loop provides a usable frequency range up to 4 kHz, which exceeds the codec’s 3.5 kHz requirement. Thus, from a bandwidth perspective, the loop can carry the codec’s signal, although additional considerations such as digital conversion may be needed.

Q11. Which statement best explains why the telephone network transitioned from purely analog to digital signaling?

🔹 A. Digital signaling allows higher data rates and error correction
🔹 B. Analog signals are easier to encrypt
🔹 C. Digital systems require fewer physical lines
🔹 D. Analog technology was more expensive

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While digital signaling indeed offers higher capacity and error detection, the primary driver for the transition was the ability to multiplex many calls and data streams efficiently, not encryption. Therefore the most accurate explanation among the options is that digital systems enable higher data rates and error correction.

Q12. How does the function of a local loop compare to that of a trunk in terms of network hierarchy?

🔹 A. Local loops are higher‑level links; trunks are lower‑level
🔹 B. Local loops serve individual subscribers; trunks interconnect switching offices
🔹 C. Both are interchangeable in the hierarchy
🔹 D. Trunks replace local loops in modern networks

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Local loops provide the final, subscriber‑side connection, while trunks form the backbone that links various switching offices. This hierarchical distinction is essential for understanding call routing and capacity planning.

Q13. If a telephone company reduces the length of each local loop by 25 %, what is the most probable effect on signal quality?

🔹 A. Signal attenuation decreases, improving clarity
🔹 B. Signal attenuation increases, degrading quality
🔹 C. No change, because length is irrelevant
🔹 D. Requires new trunks to compensate

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Shortening the physical length of a twisted‑pair cable reduces attenuation, but the change of 25 % is modest and typically does not produce a noticeable difference in everyday voice quality. Therefore the most realistic answer is that the effect is negligible.

Q14. During peak traffic, a tandem office experiences a 10 % increase in call queuing time. Which inference is most justified?

🔹 A. End offices are overloaded
🔹 B. Trunk capacity is insufficient
🔹 C. Local loops are causing delays
🔹 D. Regional offices need more staff

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Tandem offices primarily manage traffic between exchanges via trunks. An increase in queuing time indicates that the trunks feeding the tandem office are nearing capacity, causing calls to wait longer for onward transmission. The other options involve components not directly responsible for tandem‑office queuing.

Q15. A network engineer evaluates two switching office designs: one with a single high‑capacity trunk and another with multiple lower‑capacity trunks. Which analytical conclusion is most valid?

🔹 A. Multiple trunks provide redundancy and load balancing
🔹 B. A single trunk is always more cost‑effective
🔹 C. Multiple trunks increase latency
🔹 D. Single trunk simplifies routing

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using several trunks distributes traffic, reduces the risk of a single point of failure, and allows load balancing across paths. While a single large trunk may seem simpler, it lacks redundancy, making the multiple‑trunk approach preferable for reliability.

Q16. If a local loop’s bandwidth is \(4\text{ kHz}\) and a data service requires \(6\text{ kHz}\), what must the network do to support the service?

🔹 A. Upgrade the loop to fiber
🔹 B. Compress the data to fit within 4 kHz
🔹 C. Use a trunk instead of the local loop
🔹 D. Reduce the data rate to fit the bandwidth

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A trunk, which carries aggregated traffic between switching offices, typically offers higher bandwidth than a single local loop. Therefore, to accommodate a 6 kHz service, the network would route the data through a trunk rather than attempting to force it through the limited‑bandwidth local loop.

Q17. Which conceptual relationship best describes how bandwidth, signal frequency, and voice quality interact in a local loop?

🔹 A. Higher bandwidth allows higher frequencies, improving voice quality
🔹 B. Higher frequency reduces bandwidth, degrading voice quality
🔹 C. Bandwidth and frequency are unrelated to voice quality
🔹 D. Voice quality depends only on codec, not on bandwidth

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The local loop’s bandwidth determines the highest frequency it can carry. Since human voice contains frequencies up to about 4 kHz, a loop with sufficient bandwidth (≥ 4 kHz) can transmit the full voice spectrum, resulting in clear audio. Thus, greater bandwidth enables higher frequencies and better voice quality.

Q18. When a trunk fails, why might calls from distant subscribers experience longer setup times than those from nearby subscribers?

🔹 A. Distant calls rely on more intermediate trunks
🔹 B. Local loops become congested
🔹 C. End offices shut down temporarily
🔹 D. Regional offices reroute all traffic

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Calls that travel longer distances traverse multiple trunks and intermediate switching offices. The failure of a single trunk forces the network to find alternative, often longer, paths for distant calls, increasing the number of hops and thus the setup time. Nearby calls may still use unaffected local loops, experiencing less delay.
