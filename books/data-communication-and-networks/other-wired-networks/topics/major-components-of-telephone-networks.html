📝 Major components of telephone networks

📖 From Data Communication and Networks • 14. Other Wired Networks • 10 questions available

What is Major components of telephone networks?

The major components of telephone networks include local loops connecting subscribers to central offices, trunk lines linking central offices for long-distance communication, switching systems that route calls, and signaling networks that manage call setup and teardown.

Q1. After the 1984 divestiture, into how many LATAs was the United States divided?

A. More than 100
B. More than 150 ✅
C. More than 200
D. More than 250

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The divestiture created a fragmented landscape, and official records indicate the nation was split into over \(150\) LATAs. This figure reflects the regulatory intent to increase local competition, making option B the accurate count.

Q2. If a particular state was split between two LATAs after the divestiture, which of the following is the most logical inference about the state's telecom regulation?

A. The state had a single regulatory agency overseeing all telecom.
B. The state required separate licensing for each LATA.
C. The state's telecom market was monopolized.
D. The state had to coordinate multiple carriers across LATA boundaries. ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

When a state falls into more than one LATA, it must manage relationships with carriers operating under different LATA jurisdictions. This necessitates coordination of interconnection and service standards, indicating that the state must work with multiple carriers across LATA boundaries, which matches option D.

Q3. A carrier operating solely within one LATA seeks to expand services to an adjacent LATA. Based on regulatory cause‑effect, what must the carrier obtain first?

A. A certificate of public convenience and necessity from the FCC. ✅
B. Approval from the state public utility commission.
C. A merger with an existing carrier in the adjacent LATA.
D. A waiver from the Department of Transportation.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Expanding across LATA lines triggers federal oversight because the service becomes interstate. The FCC requires a certificate of public convenience and necessity before a carrier can legally operate in a new LATA, making option A the correct prerequisite.

Q4. Compare the number of LATAs that existed before the 1984 divestiture with the number after it. Which statement best reflects this comparison?

A. The number of LATAs decreased after the divestiture.
B. The number remained the same.
C. The number increased, creating more than 150 LATAs. ✅
D. The number fluctuated but ultimately stabilized at exactly 100.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Prior to 1984, LATAs were few and loosely defined. The divestiture deliberately multiplied them to over \(150\) to fragment the market and promote competition. Therefore, the accurate comparison is that the number increased, as expressed in option C.

Q5. Evaluate the impact of creating more than 150 LATAs on competition among local exchange carriers. Which assessment is most accurate?

A. The proliferation of LATAs reduced competition by consolidating carriers.
B. More LATAs fragmented the market, fostering competition among local exchange carriers. ✅
C. The increase in LATAs had no measurable effect on competition.
D. Creating many LATAs led to a monopoly by the incumbent local exchange carriers.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By slicing the country into many LATAs, the policy forced incumbents to compete locally while new entrants could focus on specific regions. This fragmentation lowered barriers to entry and heightened rivalry, thereby fostering competition, which aligns with option B.

Q6. Differentiate the regulatory authority of the FCC over services that cross LATA boundaries versus those that remain within a single LATA.

A. The FCC regulates only services that cross LATA boundaries; intralata services are regulated by state commissions. ✅
B. The FCC has authority over all telecom services regardless of LATA boundaries.
C. State commissions regulate services crossing LATA boundaries, while the FCC handles intralata services.
D. Neither the FCC nor state commissions have authority over LATA‑related services.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Federal jurisdiction applies when a service traverses LATA borders, making it interstate and subject to FCC rules. In contrast, purely intralata services fall under state regulatory frameworks. This split of authority is precisely described in option A.

Q7. Apply the principle of LATA segmentation: How does the division into multiple LATAs affect the routing of interstate calls?

A. Calls must be routed through the nearest state capital.

📖 Explanation:

When a call moves from one LATA to another, it becomes an interstate transaction, invoking FCC interconnection standards. Therefore, such calls must obey federal interconnection rules, which is captured in option C.

Q8. A new telecommunications firm wishes to provide services in three different LATAs. Which of the following steps best synthesizes the regulatory process it must follow?

Each LATA represents a distinct interstate region, so the firm must seek separate FCC certificates for each LATA and then negotiate interconnection terms with incumbent carriers. This combined approach is reflected in option C.

Q9. Explain the relationship between LATA boundaries and FCC jurisdiction, especially in the context of emerging broadband services. Which statement captures this relationship most comprehensively?

A. LATA boundaries are irrelevant to FCC jurisdiction, which is based solely on state lines.
B. The FCC’s jurisdiction extends to all services that cross LATA boundaries, while intralata services may fall under state authority. ✅
C. The FCC only regulates broadband services within a single LATA.
D. LATA boundaries define the exact limits of the FCC’s regulatory power, excluding any state involvement.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The FCC’s authority is triggered by crossing LATA borders, regardless of technology. Emerging broadband that moves between LATAs falls under federal oversight, whereas services confined to one LATA may be regulated by the state. Option B accurately describes this nuanced relationship.
