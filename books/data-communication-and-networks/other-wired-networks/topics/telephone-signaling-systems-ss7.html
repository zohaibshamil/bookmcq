What is Telephone signaling systems SS7?

Signaling System 7 (SS7) is a set of telephony signaling protocols used in public switched telephone networks to control call setup, routing, billing, and advanced services like caller ID by exchanging messages over a separate packet-switched signaling network distinct from voice channels.

Q1. What does WAN stand for in the context of wired networks?

A. Wide Area Network ✅
B. Wireless Access Node
C. World Access Network
D. Wide Access Node

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

WAN stands for Wide Area Network, which describes networks that span large geographic areas and interconnect multiple LANs or access networks. The abbreviation captures the scope and purpose of these networks, distinguishing them from local or access‑level connections. Hence option A is correct.

Q2. Which technology uses existing telephone lines for data transmission in access networks?

A. Fiber‑optic
B. DSL ✅
C. Satellite
D. Wi‑Fi

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

DSL (Digital Subscriber Line) leverages the existing copper telephone lines to deliver high‑speed data services while still supporting voice calls. It modulates digital signals onto frequencies above the voice band, enabling simultaneous voice and data. Therefore the correct choice is DSL.

Q3. If an ISP upgrades a customer's connection from dial‑up (maximum 56 kbps) to DSL (up to 25 Mbps), which of the following immediate effects is most likely?

A. It decreases latency
B. It reduces signal attenuation
C. It increases bandwidth ✅
D. It eliminates the need for modems

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Upgrading from dial‑up (≈56 kbps) to DSL (up to 25 Mbps) directly raises the amount of data that can be transferred each second. While latency may improve slightly, the most noticeable immediate change is the dramatic increase in available bandwidth, making option C the right answer.

Q4. Given that fiber‑optic cables have attenuation of about 0.2 dB/km while copper cables attenuate about 2 dB/km, what can be inferred about the maximum feasible distance between repeaters in a fiber‑optic WAN compared to a copper‑based WAN?

A. They can be the same distance
B. Fiber can span roughly ten times farther ✅
C. Copper can span farther
D. Distance does not depend on attenuation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Fiber‑optic attenuation is roughly ten times lower than copper, allowing signals to travel much farther before needing regeneration. Consequently repeaters can be spaced about ten times farther apart in a fiber‑optic WAN than in a copper‑based WAN, confirming option B.

Q5. When a DSL signal experiences a signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR) drop from 30 dB to 15 dB, how does the theoretical maximum data rate change according to the Shannon‑Hartley theorem?

A. It halves
B. It reduces by factor of √2
C. It drops to one‑quarter ✅
D. It remains unchanged

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Shannon‑Hartley theorem, \(C = B \log_2(1+SNR)\), shows capacity grows with the logarithm of the signal‑to‑noise ratio. Halving the SNR (a 3 dB drop) roughly halves capacity; a 15 dB drop reduces SNR by a factor of about 32, causing capacity to fall to roughly one‑quarter of its original value. Hence option C is correct.

Q6. A cable network originally designed for video streams begins to carry internet traffic. Which layer of the OSI model primarily handles the multiplexing of these different data types?

A. Physical
B. Data Link
C. Network
D. Transport ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Transport‑layer protocols, such as TCP and UDP, use port numbers to multiplex different application streams over a single physical link. When a cable network carries both video and internet traffic, the transport layer distinguishes these flows, making option D the appropriate answer.

Q7. Compare the bandwidth capabilities of a typical coaxial cable used in cable TV networks (up to 1 Gbps) with a standard twisted‑pair telephone line used for DSL (up to 25 Mbps). Which statement best reflects this comparison?

A. Coaxial provides roughly 40× higher bandwidth ✅
B. DSL provides higher bandwidth
C. Both provide similar bandwidth
D. Bandwidth depends only on distance, not medium

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Typical coaxial cable used for cable TV can support gigabit‑scale speeds, whereas standard twisted‑pair DSL lines are limited to tens of megabits. The ratio of roughly 1 Gbps to 25 Mbps is about 40 : 1, so the coaxial cable provides roughly forty times more bandwidth, matching option A.

Q8. Evaluate why fiber‑optic cables are preferred for Internet backbone WANs over copper cables, considering factors such as attenuation, electromagnetic interference, and capacity. Which factor is least relevant to this preference?

A. Lower attenuation
B. Immunity to EMI
C. Higher capacity
D. Lower cost per meter ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Fiber‑optic's primary advantages are its low attenuation, immunity to electromagnetic interference, and extremely high capacity. Cost per meter is often higher for fiber than copper, so cost is the least relevant factor when choosing fiber for backbone WANs, making option D correct.

Access networks usually exhibit latencies under 20 ms and provide throughput in the 10‑100 Mbps range, while WAN links commonly show latencies between 30 ms and 150 ms with capacities from 100 Mbps up to 1 Gbps. Option C accurately reflects these typical ranges.

Q10. Which of the following best describes the primary difference between an access network and a wide area network?

A. Access networks connect end users to ISPs, WANs interconnect multiple ISPs ✅

D. Access networks operate only at Layer 1, WANs at Layer 7

An access network connects end users directly to an ISP, whereas a WAN links multiple ISP or enterprise sites over long distances. This distinction captures the core functional difference, making option A the correct statement.

Q11. DSL uses modulation techniques to transmit digital data over analog telephone lines. Which modulation method is commonly employed in ADSL to achieve downstream rates up to 24 Mbps?

A. Frequency Shift Keying (FSK)
B. Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM) ✅
C. Phase Shift Keying (PSK)
D. Pulse Code Modulation (PCM)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Asymmetric DSL (ADSL) commonly uses Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM) because it efficiently combines amplitude and phase variations to transmit multiple bits per symbol, enabling downstream rates up to 24 Mbps on ordinary telephone lines. Therefore option B is correct.

Q12. A university plans a hybrid network: fiber‑optic backbone for inter‑building connections, DSL for dormitory internet access, and coaxial cable for campus video streaming. Synthesize how signaling methods differ across these three segments and justify why each technology is chosen.

A. Use the same signaling (NRZ) across all segments to simplify management.
B. Employ QAM over fiber, FSK over DSL, and AM over coaxial because each matches the medium’s bandwidth.
C. Apply OFDM for fiber, PSK for DSL, and QPSK for coaxial to maximize throughput.
D. Use advanced modulation (e.g., coherent QAM) on fiber for high capacity, discrete multitone (DMT) on DSL to handle line impairments, and QAM on coaxial to support video streams, matching each medium’s physical constraints. ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Fiber backbone employs coherent QAM to exploit its high bandwidth, DSL uses Discrete Multitone (DMT) to adapt to line conditions, and coaxial cable for video uses QAM to carry multiple channels. Each signaling method aligns with the physical limits of its medium, so option D best synthesizes the design.

Q13. Explain how the concept of 'bandwidth' in signaling differs when referring to the frequency range of a carrier signal versus the data rate achievable on a wired medium.

A. They are identical definitions
B. Frequency range determines data rate through Shannon limit ✅
C. Bandwidth only applies to wireless
D. Bandwidth is unrelated to data rate

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Bandwidth can refer to the width of a frequency band (Hz) of a carrier signal, and through Shannon’s formula it determines the maximum achievable data rate (bits per second). Hence the frequency range directly influences the data rate, making option B correct.

Q14. In a scenario where a WAN employs both synchronous optical networking (SONET) and Ethernet over fiber, how can the signaling clocks be synchronized to avoid packet loss, and which protocol facilitates this synchronization?

A. Network Time Protocol (NTP)
B. Precision Time Protocol (PTP) ✅
C. Asynchronous transmission
D. Simple Network Management Protocol (SNMP)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Precise clock alignment between SONET and Ethernet over the same fiber is achieved using the Precision Time Protocol (PTP, IEEE 1588), which distributes timing information with sub‑microsecond accuracy, preventing packet loss due to timing drift. Therefore option B is the appropriate choice.

Q15. If a cable TV operator increases its upstream channel spacing from 6 MHz to 8 MHz, what is the most likely impact on the number of upstream channels available?

A. Increases number of channels
B. Decreases number of channels ✅
C. No change
D. Causes interference

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Increasing the channel spacing widens each channel, so fewer upstream channels can fit within the allocated spectrum. Expanding from 6 MHz to 8 MHz therefore reduces the total number of channels, making option B correct.

Q16. Which factor most directly limits the maximum length of a copper‑based DSL line?

A. Signal attenuation ✅
B. Fiber dispersion
C. Optical loss
D. Satellite latency

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Signal attenuation on copper lines grows with length, causing the received signal to weaken and error rates to rise. Attenuation is therefore the primary factor limiting how far a DSL line can extend before performance degrades, confirming option A.

Q17. Applying the principle of multiplexing, how can multiple DSL connections share a single telephone pair without interfering with each other?

A. By using different frequencies (frequency‑division multiplexing) ✅
B. By assigning unique IP addresses
C. By increasing voltage
D. By using time‑division duplex only

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Frequency‑division multiplexing (FDM) assigns separate frequency bands to each DSL connection on the same copper pair, allowing simultaneous transmission without interference. This technique directly applies the principle of multiplexing, so option A is correct.
