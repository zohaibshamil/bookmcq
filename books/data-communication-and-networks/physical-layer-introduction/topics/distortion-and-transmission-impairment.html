Q1. If the rms voltage of a household supply is 120 V, what is its peak amplitude?

🔹 A. 150 V
🔹 B. 170 V
🔹 C. 240 V
🔹 D. 120 V

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Using the relationship peak = √2 × rms, we compute peak = 1.414 × 120 V ≈ 169.7 V, which rounds to 170 V. Therefore the correct choice is 170 V, corresponding to option B. The other options either ignore the √2 factor or represent the rms value itself.

Q2. A signal has a measured peak amplitude of 155 V. What is its rms voltage?

🔹 A. 110 V
🔹 B. 120 V
🔹 C. 100 V
🔹 D. 130 V

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RMS voltage for a sinusoid is peak divided by √2. Thus rms = 155 V / 1.414 ≈ 109.7 V, which rounds to 110 V. Option A matches this calculation. The other values are either too high or result from using incorrect conversion factors.

Q3. Two waveforms have identical rms values. Which one is more likely to exhibit harmonic distortion? (choose the waveform description)

🔹 A. Signal with flat tops
🔹 B. Signal with sinusoidal shape
🔹 C. Signal with constant DC offset
🔹 D. Signal with sharp peaks

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Sharp peaks introduce abrupt changes in the waveform, which generate higher-order harmonics when analyzed in the frequency domain. A flat or sinusoidal shape contains fewer discontinuities, and a pure DC offset adds no alternating components, so the waveform with sharp peaks is the most prone to harmonic distortion.

Q4. A distorted waveform shows a total harmonic distortion (THD) of 5 % and a fundamental rms voltage of 100 V. What is the rms voltage contributed by the harmonic content?

🔹 A. 7.07 V
🔹 B. 20 V
🔹 C. 50 V
🔹 D. 5 V

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

THD is defined as the ratio of the rms voltage of the harmonic content to the rms voltage of the fundamental. Rearranging, harmonic rms = THD × fundamental rms = 0.05 × 100 V = 5 V. Thus option D correctly represents the harmonic rms voltage.

Q5. For two signals with the same peak amplitude, how do their rms values compare when one is a sine wave and the other a square wave?

🔹 A. The sine wave rms is higher
🔹 B. The square wave rms is higher
🔹 C. Both rms values are equal
🔹 D. The square wave rms is 0.707 times the sine rms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A sine wave’s rms value equals its peak divided by √2 (≈0.707 × peak). A square wave maintains its peak amplitude throughout the cycle, so its rms equals the peak itself. Consequently, the square wave’s rms is larger than the sine wave’s rms, making option B correct.

Q6. Adding a DC offset to an AC signal primarily affects its rms value in which way?

🔹 A. RMS unchanged
🔹 B. RMS increases
🔹 C. RMS decreases
🔹 D. RMS becomes zero

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

RMS calculation involves squaring the instantaneous voltage, averaging, then taking the square root. Introducing a DC offset adds a constant term, which when squared contributes positively to the average, thereby raising the overall rms value. Hence the rms increases, corresponding to option B.

Q7. How does clipping distortion differ from harmonic distortion regarding the frequencies introduced into the signal spectrum?

🔹 A. Clipping adds all harmonics
🔹 B. Clipping adds only odd harmonics
🔹 C. Harmonic distortion adds specific harmonics
🔹 D. Harmonic distortion adds no new frequencies

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Clipping creates abrupt discontinuities, which generate a broad set of harmonics—including both odd and even multiples of the fundamental frequency. In contrast, harmonic distortion typically involves selective amplification of particular integer multiples. Therefore, the statement that clipping adds all harmonics (option A) correctly characterizes the spectral effect.

Q8. Which measurement directly quantifies distortion present in a periodic waveform?

🔹 A. RMS voltage
🔹 B. Peak voltage
🔹 C. Total harmonic distortion
🔹 D. Average voltage

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Total harmonic distortion (THD) is a metric that explicitly measures the ratio of harmonic content to the fundamental component, providing a direct indication of waveform distortion. RMS, peak, and average voltages do not isolate the contribution of harmonics, making THD the appropriate choice.

Q9. If the voltage of an AA battery is treated as a sine wave with a peak of 1.5 V, what is its rms value?

🔹 A. 0.75 V
🔹 B. 1.06 V
🔹 C. 1.5 V
🔹 D. 2.12 V

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

For a sinusoidal waveform, rms = peak / √2. Substituting the given peak, rms = 1.5 V / 1.414 ≈ 1.06 V. This matches option B. The other choices either halve the peak, leave it unchanged, or apply an incorrect conversion factor.

Q10. A 120 V rms, 60 Hz power line experiences distortion that raises its peak to 180 V while the fundamental rms stays at 120 V. What is the resulting total harmonic distortion (THD) percentage?

🔹 A. 0.03
🔹 B. 0.09
🔹 C. 0.12
🔹 D. 0.06

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

First compute the fundamental peak: √2 × 120 V ≈ 169.7 V. The excess peak due to distortion is 180 V − 169.7 V ≈ 10.3 V. THD = (excess / fundamental peak) × 100 % ≈ (10.3 / 169.7) × 100 % ≈ 6 %. Option D reflects this calculation.

Q11. Why is rms voltage preferred over peak voltage for power calculations in AC circuits?

🔹 A. It reflects average power
🔹 B. It is easier to measure
🔹 C. It eliminates phase
🔹 D. It equals peak for all waveforms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Power in resistive AC loads depends on the square of the voltage, and the rms value directly yields the average power equivalent to a DC voltage of the same heating effect. Hence rms provides a meaningful measure for power, unlike peak voltage which does not account for the waveform’s time variation.

Q12. What is the period of a sinusoidal signal with a frequency of 50 Hz?

🔹 A. 0.5 s
🔹 B. 0.02 s
🔹 C. 20 s
🔹 D. 2 s

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Period T is the inverse of frequency f: T = 1/f. For f = 50 Hz, T = 1/50 s = 0.02 s. This matches option B, while the other options represent incorrect inverses.

Q13. According to the passage, the peak value equals how many times the rms value?

🔹 A. 1
🔹 B. √2
🔹 C. 2
🔹 D. 0.5

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The text states that the peak value is equal to √2 times the rms value. This relationship is a fundamental property of sinusoidal waveforms, making option B the correct answer. The other multipliers do not correspond to the described conversion.
