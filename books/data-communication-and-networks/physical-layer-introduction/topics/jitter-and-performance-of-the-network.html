Q1. What is the allocated bandwidth per FM radio station in the United States?

🔹 A. 10 kHz
🔹 B. 200 kHz
🔹 C. 500 kHz
🔹 D. 1 MHz

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

FM stations are assigned a much wider channel than AM stations to accommodate the larger frequency deviation of frequency‑modulated signals. In the United States each FM station occupies a 200 kHz slice of spectrum, which is the standard allocation defined by the FCC.

Q2. If jitter increases the effective frequency spread of a digital signal by 10 %, how does the required bandwidth change?

🔹 A. Increase by 10 %
🔹 B. Increase by 5 %
🔹 C. Remain unchanged
🔹 D. Decrease by 10 %

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Jitter adds uncertainty to the timing of signal transitions, effectively spreading the signal’s spectral components. If the spread increases by 10 %, the Nyquist bandwidth must also increase by roughly the same proportion to faithfully capture the signal without distortion, so the required bandwidth rises by 10 %.

Q3. Considering that AM stations have a 10‑kHz bandwidth while FM stations have a 200‑kHz bandwidth, which statement best explains the difference in jitter tolerance?

🔹 A. AM can tolerate more jitter because its bandwidth is narrower
🔹 B. FM tolerates less jitter due to larger bandwidth
🔹 C. FM’s larger bandwidth provides greater margin for timing variations, making it more tolerant to jitter
🔹 D. Both AM and FM have equal jitter tolerance

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

FM’s 200 kHz bandwidth provides a larger guard band around the carrier, allowing the system to tolerate greater timing variations (jitter) without causing inter‑symbol interference. AM’s narrow 10 kHz channel offers less margin, making FM more robust to jitter‑induced frequency excursions.

Q4. A signal has eight discrete amplitude levels. How many bits can each level represent?

🔹 A. 2 bits
🔹 B. 3 bits
🔹 C. 4 bits
🔹 D. 5 bits

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The number of bits that can be represented by each level is given by the binary logarithm of the number of levels: \(\log_2 8 = 3\). Therefore each of the eight amplitude levels can encode three bits of information per symbol.

Q5. Given a resolution of 525×700 scanned 30 times per second, what is the minimum bandwidth required assuming 2 pixels per cycle?

🔹 A. 5.5124 MHz
🔹 B. 3.85 MHz
🔹 C. 4 MHz
🔹 D. 6 MHz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The total pixel rate is \(525 \times 700 \times 30 = 11{,}025{,}000\) pixels per second. In the worst case two pixels can be sent per cycle, so the minimum cycle rate is \(11{,}025{,}000 / 2 = 5.5125 \times 10^6\) Hz, i.e., about 5.5124 MHz bandwidth.

Q6. If jitter causes a timing uncertainty of 0.2 µs in the pixel clock of the TV example, what is the maximum percentage error in the estimated bandwidth of 5.5124 MHz?

🔹 A. 0.1
🔹 B. 0.35
🔹 C. 1.1
🔹 D. 2

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The nominal period of a 5.5124 MHz signal is \(T = 1 / 5.5124\times10^{6} \approx 181.5\) ns. A jitter of 0.2 µs equals 200 ns, which is \(200 / 181.5 \approx 1.10\) or 110 % of the period. Thus the timing error could exceed the entire cycle, causing a >100 % error in the bandwidth estimate.

Q7. If a digital modulation scheme uses 16 levels, how many bits per symbol are transmitted?

🔹 A. 2 bits
🔹 B. 3 bits
🔹 C. 4 bits
🔹 D. 5 bits

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

For a modulation scheme with \(L = 16\) discrete levels, the bits per symbol are \(\log_2 16 = 4\). This means each transmitted symbol carries four bits of information, allowing higher data rates compared with lower‑level schemes.

Q8. A system requires synchronization within ±5 kHz of a 240 kHz carrier. If jitter introduces a frequency deviation of 7 kHz, what is the likely outcome?

🔹 A. Synchronization is maintained
🔹 B. Synchronization is lost
🔹 C. Carrier frequency is shifted
🔹 D. System crashes

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The system’s synchronization tolerance is ±5 kHz. A jitter‑induced deviation of 7 kHz exceeds this limit, causing the received carrier to fall outside the permitted range. Consequently the receiver cannot maintain proper synchronization, leading to loss of lock.

Q9. Analog color TV channel is allocated 6 MHz bandwidth. If the video portion uses 70 % of this bandwidth, how many MHz remain for audio and synchronization?

🔹 A. 1.8 MHz
🔹 B. 2.0 MHz
🔹 C. 2.1 MHz
🔹 D. 2.2 MHz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

If 70 % of the 6 MHz channel is used for video, the remaining portion is \(30\% \times 6\text{ MHz} = 1.8\text{ MHz}\). This residual bandwidth is allocated to audio, control, and synchronization signals, ensuring the overall channel meets broadcasting standards.

Q10. Jitter in a digital communication system refers to:

🔹 A. Amplitude variation
🔹 B. Timing variation
🔹 C. Frequency variation
🔹 D. Phase variation

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Jitter describes the short‑term variations in the timing of signal edges relative to their ideal positions. It is a temporal phenomenon, unlike amplitude, frequency, or phase variations, and directly impacts the reliability of digital data recovery.
