What is Noiseless Channel: Nyquist Bit Rate?

The Nyquist bit rate formula defines the maximum theoretical data rate for a noiseless channel as twice the bandwidth multiplied by the logarithm base two of the number of signal levels, establishing that even without noise there is a fundamental limit to how fast data can be transmitted based solely on available bandwidth and the complexity of the signaling scheme used.

📝 All Noiseless Channel: Nyquist Bit Rate MCQs

Q1. What is the Nyquist formula for the maximum bit rate of a noiseless channel that uses \(M\) discrete signal levels and has bandwidth \(B\) Hz?

A. \(2B \log_2 M\) ✅
B. \(B \log_2 M\)
C. \(2B \log_{10} M\)
D. \(B / \log_2 M\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Nyquist theorem states that in a noiseless channel the highest achievable data rate equals twice the bandwidth multiplied by the binary logarithm of the number of signal levels. This expression, \(2B\log_2 M\), directly gives the maximum bits per second, assuming ideal conditions without noise or distortion.

Q2. If the bandwidth of a noiseless channel is doubled while the number of signal levels \(M\) remains unchanged, how does the maximum achievable bit rate change according to the Nyquist theorem?

A. It remains the same.
B. It doubles. ✅
C. It quadruples.
D. It increases by factor of \\(\\sqrt{2}\\).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

According to Nyquist, the maximum bit rate is proportional to the bandwidth (\(2B\log_2 M\)). Doubling \(B\) therefore doubles the product, and consequently the maximum bit rate also doubles, provided the number of signal levels stays constant.

Q3. A channel with bandwidth 3 kHz uses 4 signal levels. What would be the effect on the bit rate if the number of signal levels is increased to 8 while keeping bandwidth constant?

A. Increases by 50 %. ✅
B. Increases by 100 %.
C. Increases by 25 %.
D. No change.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With \(M=4\), the Nyquist rate is \(2\cdot3000\cdot\log_2 4 =12{,}000\) bps. Raising \(M\) to 8 gives \(2\cdot3000\cdot\log_2 8 =18{,}000\) bps, a 50 % increase. Hence the bit rate grows by half of its original value.

Q4. Consider two noiseless channels: Channel X has bandwidth 4 kHz and uses 2 signal levels; Channel Y has bandwidth 2 kHz and uses 8 signal levels. Which channel provides the higher maximum bit rate according to Nyquist, and why?

A. Channel X, because its bandwidth is larger despite fewer levels.
B. Channel Y, because the higher number of levels outweighs the smaller bandwidth. ✅
C. Both provide the same bit rate.
D. Cannot be determined without noise information.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Channel X yields \(2\cdot4000\cdot\log_2 2 =8{,}000\) bps. Channel Y gives \(2\cdot2000\cdot\log_2 8 =12{,}000\) bps. The larger logarithmic factor from the eight levels more than compensates for the smaller bandwidth, making Channel Y the superior one.

Q5. If a noiseless channel operates with a bandwidth of 5 kHz and uses binary signaling (\(M=2\)), what is the theoretical maximum bit rate according to Nyquist?

A. 5 kbps
B. 10 kbps ✅
C. 20 kbps
D. 2.5 kbps

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Applying Nyquist, the maximum rate is \(2\cdot5000\cdot\log_2 2 =10{,}000\) bps, which equals 10 kbps. Binary signaling contributes a factor of one to the logarithm, leaving the rate directly proportional to twice the bandwidth.

Q6. Compare the spectral efficiency (bits per second per Hz) of a noiseless channel using binary signaling versus one using 16 signal levels, both with the same bandwidth. Which statement is correct?

A. Binary signaling has higher spectral efficiency.
B. 16-level signaling has twice the spectral efficiency of binary.
C. 16-level signaling has four times the spectral efficiency of binary. ✅
D. Both have identical spectral efficiency.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Spectral efficiency equals the bit rate divided by bandwidth, giving \(2\log_2 M\) bits/s/Hz. For binary (\(M=2\)), this is 2 bits/s/Hz; for 16 levels (\(M=16\)), it becomes \(2\cdot4 =8\) bits/s/Hz, which is four times larger.

Q7. An engineer wants to achieve a bit rate of 48 kbps over a noiseless channel with bandwidth 6 kHz. What is the minimum number of discrete signal levels \(M\) required?

A. 2
B. 4
C. 8
D. 16 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Using Nyquist, \(2\cdot6000\log_2 M \ge 48{,}000\) gives \(\log_2 M \ge 4\). Therefore \(M\) must be at least \(2^4 =16\). Levels fewer than 16 cannot meet the required data rate under ideal noiseless conditions.

Q8. Which of the following statements correctly describes the relationship between bandwidth and maximum bit rate in a noiseless channel when the number of signal levels is fixed?

A. Bit rate is directly proportional to the square of bandwidth.
B. Bit rate is inversely proportional to bandwidth.
C. Bit rate is directly proportional to bandwidth. ✅
D. Bit rate is independent of bandwidth.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When the number of signal levels \(M\) is constant, Nyquist reduces to \(C = 2B\log_2 M\). The term \(\log_2 M\) is a constant, so the maximum achievable bit rate varies linearly with the bandwidth \(B\).

Q9. A communication system uses a noiseless channel with bandwidth 10 kHz and employs \(M\) signal levels. To double the channel’s maximum bit rate without changing bandwidth, by what factor must \(M\) be increased if the channel originally uses binary signaling?

A. \\(\\sqrt{2}\\)
B. 2 ✅
C. 4
D. 8

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

With binary signaling (\(M=2\)), the Nyquist rate is \(2B\cdot1\). Doubling the rate requires \(\log_2 M_{\text{new}} = 2\), so \(M_{\text{new}} = 2^2 =4\). Thus the number of levels must be multiplied by a factor of two.

Q10. A noiseless channel with bandwidth 3 kHz is required to transmit a video stream encoded at 18 kbps. What is the minimum number of signal levels \(M\) that must be used to satisfy Nyquist’s limit?

A. 2
B. 4
C. 8 ✅
D. 16

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Nyquist gives \(2\cdot3000\log_2 M \ge 18{,}000\), so \(\log_2 M \ge 3\). The smallest integer \(M\) with a log base 2 of at least 3 is \(2^3 =8\). Hence eight distinct signal levels are necessary.

Q11. Explain how the Nyquist bit rate formula changes if a channel is no longer noiseless but has a known signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR). Which of the following expressions correctly incorporates SNR?

A. \(2B \log_2 (1+ \text{SNR})\) ✅
B. \(B \log_2 (1+ \text{SNR})\)
C. \(2B \log_2 M + \text{SNR}\)
D. \(2B \log_2 (M + \text{SNR})\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When noise is present, the Shannon capacity formula replaces Nyquist’s limit: \(C = B\log_2(1+\text{SNR})\). For a noiseless case, the term \((1+\text{SNR})\) reduces to the number of distinguishable levels, yielding the classic Nyquist expression. Option A reflects this transition.

Q12. A satellite link operates as a noiseless channel with bandwidth 12 kHz. If the system designer wants to achieve a spectral efficiency of 6 bits/s/Hz, how many discrete signal levels must be employed?

A. 4
B. 8 ✅
C. 16
D. 64

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Spectral efficiency equals \(2\log_2 M\). Setting \(2\log_2 M = 6\) gives \(\log_2 M = 3\), so \(M = 2^3 = 8\). Therefore eight distinct amplitude levels are needed to meet the 6 bits/s/Hz target.

Q13. Suppose a noiseless channel uses 8 signal levels and has a bandwidth of 2 kHz. If the channel is upgraded to use 64 signal levels while keeping the same bandwidth, by what factor does the maximum bit rate increase?

A. 2 ✅
B. 3
C. 4
D. 6

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Nyquist rates are proportional to \(\log_2 M\). For 8 levels, \(\log_2 8 = 3\); for 64 levels, \(\log_2 64 = 6\). The ratio \(6/3 = 2\) shows the bit rate doubles when the number of levels is increased from 8 to 64.

Q14. In a noiseless communication system, the engineer decides to use pulse amplitude modulation (PAM) with 32 levels over a bandwidth of 5 kHz. According to Nyquist, what is the theoretical maximum data rate, and which modulation order (bits per symbol) does it correspond to?

A. 50 kbps, 4 bits/symbol ✅
B. 80 kbps, 5 bits/symbol
C. 100 kbps, 5 bits/symbol
D. 160 kbps, 6 bits/symbol

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Nyquist yields \(C = 2\cdot5000\log_2 32 = 10{,}000\cdot5 = 50{,}000\) bps (50 kbps). Each PAM symbol carries \(\log_2 32 = 5\) bits, so the modulation order is five bits per symbol, matching option A.

Q15. A noiseless channel with bandwidth 8 kHz is required to support a data link of 64 kbps. If the system uses quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) with square constellation, what is the smallest square constellation size (M) that meets the requirement?

A. 4
B. 16 ✅
C. 64
D. 256

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Nyquist gives \(2\cdot8000\log_2 M \ge 64{,}000\) → \(\log_2 M \ge 4\). The smallest integer \(M\) satisfying this is \(2^4 =16\). A square QAM constellation of 16 points (4 × 4) therefore fulfills the 64 kbps demand.

Q16. Which of the following best describes why the Nyquist formula assumes a noiseless channel?

A. It ignores thermal noise to simplify calculations.
B. It assumes that signal levels can be distinguished without error. ✅
C. It considers only intersymbol interference.
D. It accounts for quantization noise only.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Nyquist derivation presumes an ideal scenario where each discrete signal level can be perfectly detected, meaning no errors due to noise. This assumption allows the formula to focus solely on bandwidth and the number of levels, without accounting for the degradations introduced by real‑world noise sources.
