What is Wavelength in Periodic Analog Signals?

Wavelength is the physical distance a signal travels during one complete cycle and is calculated by dividing the propagation speed by the frequency, meaning higher frequency signals have shorter wavelengths, which directly affects antenna design and transmission characteristics because the size of transmitting and receiving equipment must be proportional to the wavelength for efficient signal radiation and reception.

📝 All Wavelength in Periodic Analog Signals MCQs

Q1. What is the relationship between the propagation speed (v), frequency (f), and wavelength (λ) of a signal?

A.v = f λ
B.v = f / λ
C.λ = f v
D.f = λ / v ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The fundamental wave equation states that the speed of a wave equals the product of its frequency and wavelength, expressed as v = f λ. Rearranging gives the other forms, but only the first option directly matches the definition, making it the correct choice.

Q2. If the wavelength of a signal is halved while the propagation speed stays constant, what happens to its frequency?

A.Frequency remains the same
B.Frequency doubles ✅
C.Frequency halves
D.Frequency becomes zero
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Using v = f λ, keeping v constant while λ is reduced by half forces f to double to maintain the equality. Therefore the frequency must increase twofold, which is reflected in option B.

Q3. Which of the following transmission media typically supports the longest wavelengths in typical LAN environments?

A.Fiber‑optic cable
B.Coaxial cable
C.Twisted‑pair copper cable ✅
D.Wireless microwave link
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Copper twisted‑pair operates at relatively low frequencies (hundreds of MHz), yielding wavelengths of several meters—longer than the millimeter‑scale wavelengths of fiber optics or microwave links—so it supports the longest wavelengths among the choices.

Q4. Compared to an analog signal, a digital signal representing the same information usually

A.has a broader spectral width because of its discrete transitions ✅
B.has a single, fixed wavelength
C.does not occupy any bandwidth
D.uses lower frequency components only
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Digital signals consist of rapid transitions that introduce many harmonics, expanding the spectrum and producing a broader range of wavelengths than a smooth analog waveform, which tends to concentrate energy around a single carrier frequency.

Q5. When attenuation in a channel increases with frequency, which wavelength choice will most effectively reduce signal loss?

A.Very short wavelength (high frequency)
B.Mid‑range wavelength
C.Wavelength independent of frequency
D.Longer wavelength (lower frequency) ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Higher frequencies correspond to shorter wavelengths and greater attenuation. Selecting a longer wavelength lowers the operating frequency, thereby reducing the attenuation per unit distance, making option D the optimal choice.

Q6. Two signals travel through the same medium at the same propagation speed but have different wavelengths. Which one reaches the receiver first?

A.The signal with the higher frequency
B.The signal with the longer wavelength
C.Both arrive at the same time ✅
D.It depends on the modulation format
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Propagation speed in a given medium is independent of wavelength; both signals cover the same distance at the same speed, so they reach the receiver simultaneously regardless of their wavelength differences.

Q7. When comparing wavelength‑division multiplexing (WDM) to time‑division multiplexing (TDM) for optical fibers, which statement is most accurate?

A.WDM can increase capacity by sending multiple wavelengths simultaneously over the same fiber ✅
B.WDM reduces capacity because wavelengths interfere
C.TDM always provides higher capacity than WDM
D.WDM is only applicable to radio‑frequency links
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: WDM assigns distinct data streams to separate optical carrier wavelengths, allowing them to travel concurrently on a single fiber, effectively multiplying the usable bandwidth compared with TDM, which shares the same wavelength over successive time slots.

Q8. A microwave link operates at a frequency of 10 GHz. What is the approximate wavelength of the transmitted signal? (Assume the speed of light is 3×1083\times10^{8}3×108 m/s.)

A.3 mm
B.3 cm ✅
C.30 cm
D.300 cm
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Using λ = v/f, λ = 3×10^8 m/s ÷ 10×10^9 Hz = 0.03 m, which equals 3 centimeters. This matches option B, confirming the calculation.

Q9. How does chromatic dispersion affect signal integrity when extremely short optical wavelengths are used in fiber transmission?

A.It eliminates dispersion, improving signal quality
B.It converts all wavelengths to the same speed, eliminating pulse broadening
C.It causes the fiber to become opaque, stopping transmission
D.It leads to pulse broadening because different wavelengths travel at slightly different speeds, degrading the signal ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Chromatic dispersion causes each wavelength component to propagate at a slightly different velocity; with very short wavelengths the spread of these components becomes more pronounced, resulting in broader pulses and increased inter‑symbol interference, which harms signal integrity.

Q10. Two fiber links are considered: Link A uses a wavelength of 1550 nm with attenuation 0.2 dB/km, and Link B uses 1310 nm with attenuation 0.35 dB/km. Over an 80 km span, which link exhibits lower total loss?

A.Link A, because 0.2 dB/km × 80 km = 16 dB total loss ✅
B.Link B, because 0.35 dB/km × 80 km = 28 dB total loss
C.Both have the same total loss
D.Loss cannot be compared without knowing the launch power
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Multiplying attenuation per kilometer by the distance gives total loss. Link A’s 0.2 dB/km over 80 km yields 16 dB, whereas Link B’s 0.35 dB/km yields 28 dB, so Link A clearly suffers less attenuation.

Q11. A free‑space optical link must avoid atmospheric absorption peaks at 850 nm and 1550 nm. Which wavelength band is most suitable?

A.900–950 nm
B.1000–1100 nm ✅
C.1200–1300 nm
D.1400–1450 nm
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The 850 nm and 1550 nm windows are known absorption peaks; the spectral region between them, especially around 1000–1100 nm, experiences lower atmospheric attenuation, making it the optimal choice for reliable free‑space optical communication.

Q12. According to the Nyquist theorem, what is the maximum symbol rate achievable on a channel with 20 MHz bandwidth using binary signaling?

A.10 Msymbols/s
B.20 Msymbols/s
C.40 Msymbols/s ✅
D.80 Msymbols/s
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Nyquist states that the maximum symbol rate is twice the bandwidth for a noiseless channel with two-level (binary) signaling, so 2 × 20 MHz = 40 Msymbols per second, matching option C.

Q13. When choosing between long‑wavelength (low‑frequency) radio signals and high‑frequency microwave signals for a LAN, which trade‑off is most accurate?

A.Long wavelengths penetrate obstacles better but support lower data rates ✅
B.Long wavelengths provide higher data rates but have poor penetration
C.Both options yield identical data rates and penetration characteristics
D.Long wavelengths cannot be modulated for data transmission
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Lower‑frequency radio waves have larger wavelengths that can diffract around obstacles, improving penetration, yet the available bandwidth is limited, resulting in lower achievable data rates compared with higher‑frequency microwaves that offer more bandwidth but are more easily blocked.

Q14. For an antenna to be most efficient, its physical length is typically proportional to which fraction of the signal wavelength?

A.One full wavelength
B.Two full wavelengths
C.Three‑quarters of a wavelength
D.One‑half of a wavelength ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Resonant antennas, such as dipoles, achieve maximum radiation efficiency when their length is approximately half the wavelength of the transmitted signal, allowing constructive interference of the radiated fields; thus option D correctly reflects this principle.)
