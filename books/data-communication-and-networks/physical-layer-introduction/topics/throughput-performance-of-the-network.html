Q1. If the lower cutoff frequency \(f_1\) is close to zero and the upper cutoff frequency \(f_2\) is very high, what can be inferred about the channel’s ability to transmit high‑frequency components of a digital signal?

🔹 A. High‑frequency components are heavily attenuated
🔹 B. High‑frequency components pass with little attenuation
🔹 C. Both low and high frequencies are equally blocked
🔹 D. Only the mid‑range frequencies are transmitted

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Because a very high upper cutoff means the channel allows frequencies far beyond the signal’s highest needed component, while a near‑zero lower cutoff removes little low‑frequency content. Consequently, high‑frequency components encounter minimal attenuation, enabling them to be conveyed effectively across the medium.

Q2. In a star‑topology LAN where each station has a dedicated point‑to‑point channel to the hub, how does the maximum number of simultaneous full‑duplex communications scale with the number of stations \(S\)?

🔹 A. \(S\) communications
🔹 B. \(S-1\) communications
🔹 C. \(\frac{S}{2}\) communications
🔹 D. One communication at a time

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Each full‑duplex link consumes two stations. In a star, the hub can support a separate link for each pair of stations, but a station cannot belong to more than one pair simultaneously. Therefore the greatest number of disjoint full‑duplex pairs is \(\frac{S}{2}\) (rounded down), which limits simultaneous communications.

Q3. Consider a digital signal with bit rate \(N\) transmitted over a low‑pass channel whose maximum passband frequency is \(f_{\max}=N/2\). Which statement best describes the channel’s capability to reconstruct arbitrary bit patterns?

🔹 A. It can perfectly reconstruct any pattern
🔹 B. It can only reconstruct patterns without consecutive identical bits
🔹 C. It can reconstruct patterns but with distortion for high‑frequency transitions
🔹 D. It cannot reconstruct any pattern

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The channel passes frequencies up to \(N/2\), the highest frequency needed for the fastest possible transition (alternating bits). While this allows the most demanding pattern to be represented, any higher‑frequency components that might arise from abrupt changes are filtered, causing slight distortion. Hence arbitrary patterns can be reconstructed, but with some loss for rapid transitions.

Q4. Compare a dedicated‑channel LAN to a bus‑topology LAN in terms of throughput efficiency when only two stations need to communicate at a time.

🔹 A. Dedicated channel provides lower efficiency
🔹 B. Both provide equal efficiency
🔹 C. Dedicated channel provides higher efficiency
🔹 D. Bus topology provides higher efficiency

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A dedicated channel allocates the entire medium to the communicating pair, eliminating contention and overhead. In a bus topology the two stations must share the medium with all other nodes, requiring arbitration or time‑division, which introduces idle periods and collisions. Thus, when only two stations are active, the dedicated channel yields higher throughput efficiency.

Q5. Evaluate the impact of reducing the available bandwidth of a low‑pass channel on the effective throughput when approximating a digital signal with an analog waveform.

🔹 A. Throughput decreases because fewer frequency components can represent the digital signal
🔹 B. Throughput increases linearly with reduced bandwidth
🔹 C. Throughput remains unchanged
🔹 D. Throughput becomes independent of bandwidth

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A low‑pass channel’s bandwidth determines the highest frequency that can be transmitted. Fewer frequencies mean fewer harmonics to accurately model rapid bit transitions, forcing the analog approximation to lose detail. This loss reduces the number of distinguishable symbols per unit time, directly lowering the effective throughput of the communication system.

Q6. Differentiate between the worst‑case alternating‑bit pattern (0101…) and a random‑bit pattern regarding the number of frequency components required for analog simulation in a limited‑bandwidth channel.

🔹 A. The alternating pattern requires more components
🔹 B. Both require the same number
🔹 C. The random pattern always requires fewer components
🔹 D. The alternating pattern requires the maximum frequency \(N/2\) while the random pattern may need lower frequencies on average

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The alternating pattern switches every bit, producing the highest possible transition rate and therefore demanding the full Nyquist frequency \(N/2\). A random pattern contains runs of identical bits, reducing the average transition rate; consequently, many of its spectral components lie below the Nyquist limit, allowing it to be approximated with fewer high‑frequency components when bandwidth is constrained.

Q7. Applying the Nyquist criterion, what is the minimum bandwidth \(B_{\min}\) required to transmit a binary signal at bit rate \(N\) without intersymbol interference?

🔹 A. \(B_{\min}=N\)
🔹 B. \(B_{\min}=N/2\)
🔹 C. \(B_{\min}=2N\)
🔹 D. \(B_{\min}=N/4\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Nyquist’s sampling theorem states that to avoid aliasing, the sampling frequency must be at least twice the maximum signal frequency. For a binary signal, the highest frequency component is half the bit rate (\(N/2\)). Therefore the channel must support a bandwidth equal to that highest frequency, giving \(B_{\min}=N/2\).

Q8. Synthesize how time‑sharing (TDMA) in a bus‑topology LAN influences the aggregate network throughput compared to a scenario where each station transmits continuously without coordination.

🔹 A. Time‑sharing reduces aggregate throughput
🔹 B. Time‑sharing increases aggregate throughput by eliminating collisions
🔹 C. Time‑sharing has no effect on throughput
🔹 D. Time‑sharing doubles the throughput

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When stations transmit without coordination, collisions cause retransmissions and wasted channel time, lowering overall efficiency. By allocating distinct time slots to each station (TDMA), collisions are avoided, and every slot is used for successful data transfer. This organized access raises the effective aggregate throughput, even though individual stations may experience idle periods.

Q9. Explain the relationship between channel bandwidth \(B\) and achievable throughput \(T\) in a baseband transmission system that uses NRZ signaling.

🔹 A. \(T\) is independent of \(B\)
🔹 B. \(T\) is directly proportional to \(B\)
🔹 C. \(T\) is inversely proportional to \(B\)
🔹 D. \(T\) equals \(2B\) regardless of signaling

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

NRZ signaling transmits one bit per symbol, and the symbol rate is limited by the channel’s ability to carry the required frequency components. According to the Nyquist limit for baseband, the maximum symbol rate equals twice the bandwidth (\(2B\)), but because NRZ uses only one level change per bit, the practical throughput scales linearly with the available bandwidth.

Q10. Given a dedicated channel of total bandwidth \(B\) Hz, a digital bit rate \(N\) bits/s, and the fact that two bits are transmitted per analog cycle at frequency \(f=N/2\), derive the expression for the maximum achievable throughput \(T_{\max}\) in bits per second.

🔹 A. \(T_{\max}=B\)
🔹 B. \(T_{\max}=2B\)
🔹 C. \(T_{\max}=N\)
🔹 D. \(T_{\max}=B \times \frac{N}{2f}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Each analog cycle carries two bits, and the cycle frequency is \(f=N/2\). Multiplying the bits per cycle by the cycle frequency yields the throughput: \(T_{\max}=2 \times f = 2 \times (N/2) = N\). Thus, regardless of the channel bandwidth, the throughput cannot exceed the original digital bit rate \(N\) when the signaling scheme transmits two bits per cycle.

Q11. In the context of LANs, what does the term “dedicated channel” refer to?

🔹 A. A channel shared by all stations
🔹 B. A channel used exclusively by a pair of stations
🔹 C. A wireless link
🔹 D. A channel that alternates between transmit and receive modes

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A dedicated channel is a physical medium allocated to a specific communication pair, meaning no other devices contend for its use while the pair is active. This exclusivity distinguishes it from shared media, where multiple stations must coordinate access to the same channel.

Q12. If a low‑pass channel can only pass frequencies up to \(f_{\max}=0.4N\), what fraction of the original digital signal’s bandwidth is lost, and how does this loss affect the effective throughput?

🔹 A. 20% loss, throughput unchanged
🔹 B. 40% loss, throughput reduced proportionally
🔹 C. 60% loss, throughput doubles
🔹 D. No loss, throughput unchanged

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The full digital signal requires frequencies up to \(N/2\) (50% of the bit rate). A cutoff at \(0.4N\) represents a reduction of \((0.5N-0.4N)/0.5N = 20\%\) of the required bandwidth, but expressed relative to the original Nyquist limit it corresponds to a 40% loss of the usable spectrum. Because fewer frequency components can be transmitted, the symbol rate—and thus the effective throughput—decreases roughly in proportion to the lost bandwidth.
