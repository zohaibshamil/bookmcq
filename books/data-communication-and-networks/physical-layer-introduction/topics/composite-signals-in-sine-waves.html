What is Composite Signals in Sine Waves?

A composite signal consists of multiple sine waves of different frequencies, amplitudes, and phases combined together, and according to Fourier analysis any practical communication signal is composite rather than a single pure sine wave because real-world data requires a range of frequencies to represent changing information, making decomposition into simple sine waves essential for analyzing signal behavior and system requirements.

📝 All Composite Signals in Sine Waves MCQs

Q1. If an analog signal is sampled at a rate lower than twice its highest frequency component, which phenomenon is most likely to occur?

A.Quantization error
B.Aliasing ✅
C.Noise amplification
D.Phase distortion
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Aliasing occurs when the sampling frequency is insufficient to capture the rapid variations of the signal, causing higher‑frequency components to masquerade as lower‑frequency ones. This distortion is a direct result of violating the Nyquist criterion, which requires sampling at least twice the maximum signal frequency.

Q2. When a composite signal loses its highest‑frequency sine component, what immediate effect does this have on the signal’s waveform?

A.Increase in overall amplitude
B.Smoother appearance ✅
C.Introduce discontinuities
D.Shift in phase
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Removing the highest‑frequency component eliminates the rapid oscillations that contribute fine detail, so the remaining waveform appears smoother. The overall amplitude does not necessarily increase, and no new discontinuities or phase shifts are introduced solely by discarding that component.

Q3. If the frequency of one sine component in a composite signal is doubled while all other components remain unchanged, how does the period of the overall signal change?

A.It halves ✅
B.It doubles
C.It remains the same
D.It becomes unpredictable
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The period of a sine wave is the reciprocal of its frequency (\(T=1/f\)). Doubling the frequency of a component cuts its period in half. Although the composite signal contains other frequencies, the component that changed now repeats twice as fast, effectively halving its own period.

Q4. A sine wave described by \(x(t)=A\sin(2\pi f t + \phi)\) crosses zero at \(t=0\). If the phase \(\phi\) is increased by \(\pi/2\), at what time does the next zero crossing occur?

A.\(t = \frac{1}{4f}\) ✅
B.\(t = \frac{1}{2f}\)
C.\(t = \frac{3}{4f}\)
D.\(t = \frac{1}{f}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Setting the new phase to \(\phi+\pi/2\) gives \(2\pi f t + \pi/2 = \pi\) for the next zero. Solving for \(t\) yields \(t = (\pi - \pi/2)/(2\pi f)=1/(4f)\). This demonstrates how a phase shift advances the zero‑crossing point by a quarter of the original period.

Q5. Which of the following best illustrates why a digital signal appears as a series of vertical lines on a time‑value plot?

A.Continuous amplitude variation
B.Discrete voltage levels ✅
C.Infinite frequency components
D.Phase modulation
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A digital signal holds a constant value between sampling instants, creating a step‑like shape. When plotted, each sample is represented by a horizontal segment, and the abrupt change to the next value is shown as a vertical line, highlighting the discrete nature of the signal.

Q6. Two sine waves of equal amplitude and frequency are combined, one with phase \(0\) and the other with phase \(\pi\). What is the resulting signal?

A.A sine wave of double amplitude
B.A sine wave of zero amplitude (cancellation) ✅
C.A cosine wave
D.A constant DC offset
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When two identical sine waves are 180° out of phase (\(\pi\) radians), their instantaneous values are equal in magnitude but opposite in sign, causing complete cancellation at every point in time. The resultant waveform is therefore zero everywhere.

Q7. A composite analog signal contains frequency components up to 8 kHz. According to the Nyquist theorem, what is the minimum sampling rate required to avoid aliasing?

A.8 kHz
B.12 kHz
C.16 kHz ✅
D.20 kHz
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Nyquist theorem states that the sampling frequency must be at least twice the highest frequency present in the signal. With a maximum component of 8 kHz, the minimum safe sampling rate is \(2 \times 8\text{ kHz}=16\text{ kHz}\). Sampling below this limit would cause aliasing.

Q8. Which statement correctly contrasts analog and digital signals regarding the number of distinct amplitude levels they can represent?

A.Analog signals have a finite number of levels, while digital signals have infinite
B.Both have infinite levels
C.Analog signals have infinite levels, digital signals have a limited set ✅
D.Digital signals have infinite levels, analog signals have two levels
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Analog signals vary continuously, offering an unbounded set of possible amplitudes. Digital signals, by definition, are limited to a finite set of discrete levels (often binary 0 and 1), making option C the accurate contrast.

Q9. Why might engineers prefer transmitting music as a composite analog signal rather than a single sine wave?

A.Single sine wave cannot carry any information
B.Composite signals can represent complex timbres ✅
C.Composite signals require less bandwidth
D.Single sine waves are more susceptible to noise
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Music consists of many harmonics that define its timbre. A composite analog signal combines these multiple sine components, preserving the richness of the sound. A single sine wave lacks the harmonic content needed to convey the full character of musical notes.

Q10. In data communications, what distinguishes a periodic digital signal from a nonperiodic digital signal?

A.Presence of a repeating bit pattern ✅
B.Use of analog modulation
C.Higher voltage levels
D.Continuous amplitude variation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A periodic digital signal repeats a specific sequence of bits over regular intervals, creating a recognizable pattern. Nonperiodic digital signals lack such repetition, changing arbitrarily over time. The distinction lies in the existence of a repeatable pattern, not in voltage level or modulation type.

Q11. Adding a third harmonic (frequency triple the fundamental) to a fundamental sine wave primarily affects which characteristic of the resulting waveform?

A.Its average power
B.Its fundamental frequency
C.Its shape (sharpness) ✅
D.Its phase stability
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Higher‑order harmonics introduce steeper slopes and more abrupt transitions, sharpening the waveform and making it resemble non‑sinusoidal shapes (e.g., square or sawtooth). This alteration of shape directly influences perceived timbre, while average power and fundamental frequency remain unchanged.

Q12. If the amplitude of a component sine wave in a composite signal is doubled while its frequency remains unchanged, how does the signal’s power change?

A.Power doubles
B.Power quadruples ✅
C.Power remains the same
D.Power halves
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Signal power is proportional to the square of its amplitude (\(P \propto A^{2}\)). Doubling the amplitude (\(A \to 2A\)) results in \(P \to (2A)^{2}=4A^{2}\), which is four times the original power.

Q13. When a composite analog signal is digitized using an 8‑bit converter, which trade‑off most directly limits the accuracy of representing low‑amplitude components?

A.Sampling rate
B.Quantization noise ✅
C.Signal-to-noise ratio
D.Bandwidth
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An 8‑bit converter provides 256 discrete levels. Low‑amplitude components may fall between these levels, leading to quantization error (noise). This quantization noise limits how precisely small variations can be captured, making it the primary constraint on accuracy for low‑amplitude signals.

Q14. How does a composite signal differ from a simple sine wave in terms of frequency content?

A.Composite has only one frequency
B.Composite contains multiple frequencies ✅
C.Simple sine wave contains harmonics
D.Both contain the same frequencies
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A simple sine wave is a single‑frequency sinusoid. A composite signal is formed by adding several sinusoidal components, each with its own frequency, resulting in a richer frequency spectrum.

Q15. To construct a composite signal that approximates a given waveform, which principle should be applied?

A.Superposition of sine waves ✅
B.Multiplication of cosine waves
C.Differentiation of a step function
D.Integration of noise
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The principle of superposition states that any complex periodic waveform can be expressed as the sum of multiple sine (or cosine) waves with appropriate amplitudes, frequencies, and phases. This is the foundation of Fourier synthesis used to build composite signals.

Q16. Explain how the relative amplitudes of harmonics in a composite signal influence the perceived timbre of a musical note.

A.Higher amplitudes make the note louder only
B.Harmonic amplitudes shape the spectral envelope, affecting timbre ✅
C.Amplitudes have no effect on timbre
D.Only the fundamental amplitude matters
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Timbre is determined by the spectral envelope—the distribution of energy among harmonics. By adjusting each harmonic’s amplitude, the ear perceives differences in brightness, warmth, or richness, even if the fundamental pitch remains unchanged. Thus, harmonic amplitudes directly shape timbre.

Q17. Which series of sine waves can be summed to synthesize a square wave?

A.Only the fundamental sine wave
B.Odd harmonics with decreasing amplitudes proportional to \(1/n\) ✅
C.Even harmonics with equal amplitudes
D.Random frequencies with equal amplitudes
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A square wave can be approximated by adding odd‑order harmonics (1st, 3rd, 5th, …) where each harmonic’s amplitude is inversely proportional to its order (\(1/n\)). This series converges toward the characteristic sharp transitions of a square wave.

Q18. If two sine components of equal amplitude have a phase difference of \(\pi/3\), what is the amplitude of the resulting waveform?

A.\(A\)
B.\(2A\cos(\pi/6)\) ✅
C.\(2A\sin(\pi/3)\)
D.Zero
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When two equal‑amplitude sinusoids differ by \(\Delta\phi\), the resultant amplitude is \(2A\cos(\Delta\phi/2)\). Substituting \(\Delta\phi=\pi/3\) gives \(2A\cos(\pi/6)=2A\cdot\sqrt{3}/2=\sqrt{3}A\), matching option B.

Q19. A composite signal contains frequencies up to 5 kHz. According to the Nyquist criterion, what minimum sampling frequency ensures accurate digital reconstruction?

A.5 kHz
B.7.5 kHz
C.10 kHz ✅
D.15 kHz
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Nyquist requires a sampling rate at least twice the highest frequency component. With a maximum of 5 kHz, the minimum safe sampling frequency is \(2 \times 5\text{ kHz}=10\text{ kHz}\).

Q20. When a composite analog signal is sampled exactly at twice its highest frequency component, what type of distortion, if any, appears in the reconstructed signal?

A.Severe aliasing
B.No distortion
C.Slight amplitude attenuation ✅
D.Phase inversion
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Sampling at the exact Nyquist rate meets the theoretical minimum, but practical reconstruction filters have non‑ideal roll‑off, causing modest attenuation of higher frequencies. This leads to slight amplitude loss rather than severe aliasing or phase inversion.

Q21. Why does a digital signal plot consist of vertical line segments rather than smooth curves?

A.Because digital signals change continuously
B.Because each sample holds its value until the next sample ✅
C.Because of high frequency content
D.Because of phase modulation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In a digital representation, each sampled value remains constant until the subsequent sample arrives. This creates a step‑wise waveform where the transition between samples is depicted as a vertical line, emphasizing the discrete, piecewise‑constant nature of digital signals.

Q22. What is an analog signal?

A.A signal with discrete voltage levels
B.A signal that varies continuously over time ✅
C.A binary sequence of 0s and 1s
D.A digital representation of data
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An analog signal is defined by its continuous variation in amplitude (or another property) with respect to time, allowing it to assume any value within a range rather than being restricted to discrete levels.

Q23. Which three parameters fully describe a sine wave?

A.Amplitude, frequency, wavelength
B.Peak amplitude, frequency, phase ✅
C.Voltage, current, resistance
D.Period, duty cycle, offset
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A sine wave is uniquely defined by its peak amplitude (height), its frequency (how many cycles per second), and its phase (the horizontal shift relative to a reference point). These three parameters determine the wave’s shape and timing.

Q24. In a periodic signal, the repeated pattern is called a?

A.Cycle
B.Waveform
C.Period ✅
D.Frequency
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The term “period” denotes the duration of one complete repetition of a periodic signal’s pattern. It is the fundamental interval after which the signal’s shape repeats identically.
