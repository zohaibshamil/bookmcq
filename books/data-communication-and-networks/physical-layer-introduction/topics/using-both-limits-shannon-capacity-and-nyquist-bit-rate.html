Q1. A periodic composite signal has a fundamental frequency \( f \). In its frequency‑domain plot you notice a spike at \( 5f \). What does this spike represent?

🔹 A. A noise component unrelated to the signal
🔹 B. The fifth harmonic of the signal
🔹 C. The fundamental frequency shifted by a factor of five
🔹 D. An aliasing artifact caused by sampling

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The spike at \(5f\) corresponds to an integer multiple of the fundamental frequency, which by definition is a harmonic. Since 5 is an integer, this component is called the fifth harmonic, indicating that the signal contains a sine wave at five times the base frequency.

Q2. Which of the following best contrasts the frequency‑domain representation of a periodic composite signal with that of a non‑periodic composite signal?

🔹 A. The periodic signal shows discrete spikes at integer multiples of the fundamental, while the non‑periodic signal shows a continuous spread of frequencies.
🔹 B. Both have continuous spectra, but the periodic one shows higher peak amplitudes.
🔹 C. The non‑periodic signal has discrete spikes, whereas the periodic signal displays a smooth curve.
🔹 D. Both representations are identical; only the time‑domain waveforms differ.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A periodic signal repeats regularly, so its Fourier transform consists of isolated spectral lines at discrete frequencies (harmonics). A non‑periodic signal lacks this repeatability, producing a continuous spectrum where energy is spread over a range of frequencies without distinct gaps.

Q3. If a low‑pass filter removes all frequency components above \( 3f \) from a periodic composite signal, which statement best describes the resulting time‑domain waveform?

🔹 A. It will retain only the fundamental sine wave, eliminating higher harmonics.
🔹 B. It will become a constant DC level because all AC components are removed.
🔹 C. It will contain the fundamental and the third harmonic, producing a distorted waveform.
🔹 D. It will double the amplitude of the remaining components.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Removing frequencies above \(3f\) leaves the components at \(f\) and \(3f\). Their sum is still a periodic waveform but differs from the original because the higher harmonics (e.g., \(9f\)) are missing, causing a shape that is less sharp and somewhat distorted.

Q4. In Fourier analysis, what term is used for a frequency component that is an integer multiple of the fundamental frequency?

🔹 A. Sideband
🔹 B. Modulation index
🔹 C. Nyquist frequency
🔹 D. Harmonic

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A frequency that is an integer multiple of the fundamental is called a harmonic. Harmonics are the building blocks of periodic signals, each representing a sine wave whose frequency is an integer multiple of the base frequency.

Q5. A periodic composite signal has amplitudes \( A \) for the fundamental (\( f \)), \( \frac{A}{3} \) for the third harmonic (\( 3f \)), and \( \frac{A}{9} \) for the ninth harmonic (\( 9f \)). If the amplitude of the third harmonic is increased to \( \frac{2A}{3} \) while the others stay unchanged, what is the new total peak amplitude (sum of absolute amplitudes) of the three components?

🔹 A. \( \frac{13A}{9} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{19A}{9} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{17A}{9} \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{16A}{9} \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The new sum is \( A + \frac{2A}{3} + \frac{A}{9} = \frac{9A}{9} + \frac{6A}{9} + \frac{A}{9} = \frac{16A}{9} \). This calculation adds the absolute values of each harmonic’s amplitude, reflecting the increased contribution from the third harmonic while the fundamental and ninth harmonic remain unchanged.

Q6. Suppose a periodic composite signal originally contains only integer‑multiple harmonics of its fundamental frequency \( f \). If a new component at frequency \( 2.5f \) is added, which effect does this have on the signal’s periodicity?

🔹 A. The signal remains periodic with the same fundamental period.
🔹 B. The signal becomes non‑periodic because the added frequency is not an integer multiple of \( f \).
🔹 C. The signal’s period doubles, but it stays periodic.
🔹 D. The signal’s frequency domain now contains only discrete spikes.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Periodicity requires that all frequency components be integer multiples of a common fundamental. Introducing a component at \(2.5f\) breaks this condition, so the signal no longer repeats exactly over a fixed interval, making it non‑periodic.

Q7. How does Fourier decomposition enable data compression in communication systems?

🔹 A. By representing the signal with only the most significant harmonics, less data is required to approximate the original waveform.
🔹 B. It eliminates the need for error‑correction codes.
🔹 C. It converts analog signals into digital ones without any loss.
🔹 D. It increases the bandwidth of the transmitted signal.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Fourier analysis reveals that many signal components contribute little energy. By keeping only the strongest harmonics and discarding weaker ones, the signal can be approximated with far fewer coefficients, reducing the amount of data that must be stored or transmitted while preserving essential characteristics.

Q8. In the frequency‑domain plot of a periodic composite signal, the spikes appear at discrete frequencies. What logical conclusion can be drawn about the time‑domain signal?

🔹 A. The time‑domain signal must be a single sine wave.
🔹 B. The time‑domain signal is random noise.
🔹 C. The time‑domain signal repeats after a fixed interval, i.e., it is periodic.
🔹 D. The time‑domain signal cannot be reconstructed from the frequency plot.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Discrete spectral lines indicate that the underlying time‑domain waveform repeats at regular intervals. This is the hallmark of a periodic signal, because only periodic signals have Fourier transforms consisting of isolated spikes at harmonic frequencies.

Q9. Which statement correctly differentiates a discrete frequency spectrum from a continuous frequency spectrum?

🔹 A. A discrete spectrum contains infinitely many frequencies, while a continuous spectrum has only a finite number.
🔹 B. A discrete spectrum consists of isolated spikes at specific frequencies; a continuous spectrum fills an interval of frequencies without gaps.
🔹 C. Both spectra are identical; the terms refer to the same concept.
🔹 D. A continuous spectrum only occurs for periodic signals, whereas a discrete spectrum occurs for non‑periodic signals.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A discrete spectrum appears as separate lines (spikes) at distinct frequencies, typical of periodic signals. A continuous spectrum, in contrast, spreads smoothly over a range of frequencies without gaps, characteristic of non‑periodic or aperiodic signals.

Q10. Designing a band‑pass filter to isolate the fundamental frequency \( f \) of a periodic composite signal, which filter characteristic is most critical to avoid distortion of the fundamental component?

🔹 A. A flat passband that includes \( f \) but attenuates frequencies just above and below \( f \) minimally.
🔹 B. A very narrow stopband around \( f \) to suppress nearby harmonics.
🔹 C. A high‑gain amplification at \( f \) to increase its amplitude.
🔹 D. A steep roll‑off that starts at \( 0.5f \).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To preserve the shape of the fundamental component, the filter must allow \( f \) to pass with little attenuation and maintain a flat response across its bandwidth. This ensures the amplitude and phase of the fundamental are not altered, while higher harmonics can be suppressed as needed.

Q11. If two sine components of a composite signal have the same frequency \( f \) but a phase difference of \( \pi \) radians, what is the net effect on the amplitude of that frequency in the frequency‑domain representation?

🔹 A. The amplitudes add, doubling the peak.
🔹 B. The amplitudes cancel, resulting in zero amplitude at frequency \( f \).
🔹 C. The phase difference has no effect on amplitude; the peak remains unchanged.
🔹 D. The amplitudes cancel, resulting in zero amplitude at frequency \( f \).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A phase shift of \( \pi \) radians means the two sine waves are exactly out of phase, causing their instantaneous values to oppose each other. When summed, they cancel completely, producing zero net amplitude at that frequency, which appears as a missing or null spike in the spectrum.

Q12. When the amplitude of every harmonic in a periodic composite signal is multiplied by a constant factor \( k \), what happens to the overall shape of the time‑domain waveform?

🔹 A. The waveform’s shape changes to a different waveform.
🔹 B. The frequency components shift to higher frequencies.
🔹 C. The waveform is scaled in amplitude by \( k \) but retains its original shape.
🔹 D. The signal becomes a constant DC offset.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Multiplying all harmonic amplitudes by the same factor uniformly scales the signal’s amplitude without altering the relative contributions of each harmonic. Consequently, the waveform retains its original shape, only its overall magnitude is increased or decreased by the factor \( k \).
