What is Using Both Limits: Shannon Capacity and Nyquist Bit Rate?

Network engineers must consider both Nyquist and Shannon limits because Nyquist tells you the maximum symbol rate possible for a given bandwidth and signal levels in ideal conditions, while Shannon tells you the maximum reliable bit rate considering actual noise, so practical system design involves choosing signal levels that approach Shannon capacity without exceeding Nyquist constraints, often requiring trade-offs between speed, complexity, and error performance.

Q1. A periodic composite signal has a fundamental frequency f f f. In its frequency‑domain plot you notice a spike at 5f 5f 5f. What does this spike represent?

C.The fundamental frequency shifted by a factor of five
D.An aliasing artifact caused by sampling
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The spike at 5f5f5f corresponds to an integer multiple of the fundamental frequency, which by definition is a harmonic. Since 5 is an integer, this component is called the fifth harmonic, indicating that the signal contains a sine wave at five times the base frequency.

Q2. Which of the following best contrasts the frequency‑domain representation of a periodic composite signal with that of a non‑periodic composite signal?

A.The periodic signal shows discrete spikes at integer multiples of the fundamental, while the non‑periodic signal shows a continuous spread of frequencies. ✅

C.The non‑periodic signal has discrete spikes, whereas the periodic signal displays a smooth curve.
D.Both representations are identical; only the time‑domain waveforms differ.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A periodic signal repeats regularly, so its Fourier transform consists of isolated spectral lines at discrete frequencies (harmonics). A non‑periodic signal lacks this repeatability, producing a continuous spectrum where energy is spread over a range of frequencies without distinct gaps.
