What is Digital Signal as a Composite Analog Signal?

A digital signal is actually a composite analog signal composed of infinite sine waves because the sharp transitions between voltage levels require many frequency components to represent, meaning digital transmission fundamentally relies on analog principles and the available bandwidth limits how faithfully digital pulses can be reproduced, causing rounding of edges and potential intersymbol interference at high speeds.

Q1. What is the factor that relates the peak voltage to the rms voltage for a pure sinusoidal waveform?

A.Peak = rms
B.Peak = \( \sqrt{2} \) × rms ✅
C.Peak = 2 × rms
D.Peak = rms ÷ 2
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a sinusoidal waveform, the rms value is the square root of the average of the squared instantaneous values. The peak value is therefore \( \sqrt{2} \) times the rms value, giving a conversion factor of \( \sqrt{2} \). This relationship is standard for pure sine waves.

Q2. If the rms voltage of a household supply is 120 V, what is the peak voltage?

A.120 ÷ \( \sqrt{2} \) V
B.120 V
C.120 × 2 V
D.120 × \( \sqrt{2} \) V ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The peak voltage of a sinusoidal signal equals the rms voltage multiplied by \( \sqrt{2} \). Multiplying 120 V by \( \sqrt{2} \) (≈1.414) yields about 170 V, which is the maximum instantaneous value the waveform reaches each cycle.

Q3. Signal A has a frequency of 50 Hz, while Signal B has a frequency of 200 Hz. Which statement correctly compares their periods?

A.They have equal periods
B.Signal A has a shorter period than Signal B
C.Signal A has a longer period than Signal B ✅
D.Cannot determine without more information
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Period is the inverse of frequency: \( T = 1/f \). Therefore, the 50 Hz signal has a period of 0.02 s, and the 200 Hz signal has a period of 0.005 s. The lower‑frequency signal thus has the longer period.

Q4. When two sine waves of equal amplitude but frequencies 60 Hz and 120 Hz are added, what is the maximum possible amplitude of the resulting composite signal?

A.Same as the individual amplitude ✅
B.Double the individual amplitude
C.Zero amplitude
D.Depends on the relative phase
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: If the two sine waves are in phase at a given instant, their instantaneous values add, producing a peak amplitude that is the sum of the individual amplitudes—i.e., double the single‑wave amplitude. Phase differences can reduce the peak, but the maximum achievable is the sum.

Q5. A composite analog signal consists of a 10 V rms 60 Hz component and a 5 V rms 180 Hz component. What is the rms value of the combined signal assuming the components are orthogonal?

A.15 V
B.10 V
C.7.5 V
D.11.2 V ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: When uncorrelated (orthogonal) components are combined, the total rms value is the square root of the sum of the squares: \( \sqrt{10^{2}+5^{2}} = \sqrt{125} \approx 11.2 \) V. This follows from the power‑addition principle for independent signals.

Q6. Which of the following sampling rates is sufficient to accurately reconstruct a composite signal containing frequency components up to 8 kHz according to the Nyquist criterion?

A.8 kHz
B.16 kHz ✅
C.12 kHz
D.20 kHz
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Nyquist’s theorem requires a sampling frequency greater than twice the highest frequency component. For an 8 kHz maximum, the sampling rate must exceed 16 kHz. A 16 kHz rate meets the minimum requirement, ensuring no aliasing of the signal’s highest component.

Q7. Explain why increasing the sampling frequency improves the representation of a composite analog signal in the digital domain.

A.Increases quantization error
B.Decreases resolution
C.Reduces aliasing ✅
D.None of the above
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A higher sampling frequency pushes the Nyquist limit upward, allowing more of the signal’s spectral content to be captured without overlap. This reduces aliasing, which occurs when higher‑frequency components are misinterpreted as lower frequencies, thereby preserving the fidelity of the digital representation.

Q8. If a digital system samples a 60 Hz sinusoid at 90 Hz, what alias frequency will appear in the reconstructed signal?

A.60 Hz
B.90 Hz
C.0 Hz
D.30 Hz ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: When the sampling frequency (90 Hz) is lower than twice the signal frequency, aliasing occurs. The aliased frequency is the absolute difference between the sampling rate and the original frequency: |90 − 60| = 30 Hz. Thus a 30 Hz component is observed after reconstruction.

Q9. Two composite signals each contain a 5 V rms 50 Hz component. Signal X adds a 3 V rms 150 Hz component, while Signal Y adds a 4 V rms 150 Hz component. Which signal has the higher overall rms value?

A.Signal Y ✅
B.Signal X
C.Both are equal
D.Cannot determine
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Compute the total rms for each: Signal X: \( \sqrt{5^{2}+3^{2}} = \sqrt{34} \approx 5.83\) V. Signal Y: \( \sqrt{5^{2}+4^{2}} = \sqrt{41} \approx 6.40\) V. Because 6.40 V > 5.83 V, Signal Y possesses the greater overall rms voltage.

Q10. Synthesize how the principle of superposition allows a digital representation to model a composite analog signal composed of multiple sinusoidal components, and identify one limitation of this approach.

A.Superposition is irrelevant; limitation is aliasing
B.Superposition requires non‑linear combination; limitation is sampling rate
C.Superposition only works for DC signals; limitation is bandwidth
D.Superposition permits adding sampled waveforms; limitation is quantization noise ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Superposition states that the total response of a linear system equals the sum of individual responses. In digital signal processing, each sinusoidal component can be sampled and stored separately, then added to reconstruct the composite waveform. However, the finite word length of digital samples introduces quantization noise, limiting accuracy.

Q11. A house voltage waveform is distorted such that its rms value is measured at 115 V, but the peak value is observed to be 170 V. Assuming the distortion adds a harmonic component, what can be inferred about the harmonic’s contribution to the total power?

A.Harmonic increases rms more than peak
B.Harmonic increases peak more than rms
C.Harmonic decreases both rms and peak ✅
D.Harmonic has no effect on either measure
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The observed peak (170 V) exceeds the ideal sinusoidal peak of \(115 × \sqrt{2} ≈ 162.6 V\). This indicates that the added harmonic raises the instantaneous maximum more than it raises the average power (rms). Hence the harmonic contributes disproportionately to the peak, not to the rms‑derived power.

Q12. Given a composite signal consisting of three sinusoidal components: \(V_1 = 4 \text{ V rms at } 50 \text{ Hz}\), \(V_2 = 3 \text{ V rms at } 150 \text{ Hz}\), and \(V_3 = 2 \text{ V rms at } 250 \text{ Hz}\). If the components are mutually orthogonal, what is the total rms voltage?

A.4 V
B.5.4 V ✅
C.6 V
D.9 V
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For orthogonal (uncorrelated) components, the total rms voltage is the square root of the sum of the squares of the individual rms values: \( \sqrt{4^{2}+3^{2}+2^{2}} = \sqrt{29} \approx 5.4\) V. This follows directly from the power‑addition rule for independent sinusoidal signals.
