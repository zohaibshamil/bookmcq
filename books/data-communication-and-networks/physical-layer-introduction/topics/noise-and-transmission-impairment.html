What is Noise and Transmission Impairment?

Noise refers to unwanted random electrical signals inserted into the transmission medium from thermal agitation, electromagnetic interference, crosstalk, or impulse sources, and unlike attenuation or distortion which are deterministic, noise is stochastic and fundamentally limits communication reliability because it adds uncertainty to signal detection, making error correction and signal-to-noise ratio management essential for maintaining data integrity.

Q1. Express a period of 100 ms in microseconds.

A. 10,000 µs
B. 100,000 µs ✅
C. 1,000 µs
D. 1,000,000 µs

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A period of 100 ms equals 0.1 s. Since 1 s = 10⁶ µs, multiplying 0.1 s by 10⁶ gives 100,000 µs. Therefore the correct conversion is 100,000 µs, which corresponds to option B.

Q2. Which unit corresponds to a frequency of \(10^{6}\) Hz?

A. Kilohertz
B. Gigahertz
C. Megahertz ✅
D. Terahertz

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The prefix \mega-\ denotes a factor of 10⁶. Thus a frequency of \(10^{6}\) Hz is expressed as 1 megahertz (MHz). Among the choices, Megahertz is option C, making it the correct answer.

Q3. If two signals have identical amplitude and phase but one’s frequency is doubled, what happens to its period?

A. The period is halved ✅
B. The period is doubled
C. The period remains unchanged
D. The frequency remains unchanged

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Period \(T\) and frequency \(f\) are reciprocals (\(T=1/f\)). Doubling the frequency makes the period half as large. Consequently the period is halved, which is option A.

Q4. A noise source emits a signal with period \(2\text{ ms}\). Reducing the period to \(0.5\text{ ms}\) changes the frequency by what factor?

A. Four‑fold increase ✅
B. Four‑fold decrease
C. Two‑fold increase
D. Two‑fold decrease

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Frequency is the inverse of period. Changing the period from 2 ms to 0.5 ms divides the period by 4, so the frequency is multiplied by 4. Hence the frequency increases four‑fold, corresponding to option A.

Q5. Human perception cannot resolve amplitude changes faster than 1 ms. If a signal alternates amplitude every 0.4 ms, what is most likely perceived?

A. Distinct amplitude steps
B. A constant amplitude
C. A rapid flicker perceived as steady ✅
D. No sound at all

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Because the alternation is faster than the 1 ms temporal resolution, the eye integrates the rapid changes and perceives a continuous, steady flicker rather than separate steps. This description matches option C.

Q6. A sinusoidal signal with period \(T\) is corrupted by white noise of variance \(\sigma^{2}\). Halving \(T\) while keeping \(\sigma^{2}\) unchanged doubles the signal‑to‑noise ratio (SNR).

A. SNR doubles ✅
B. SNR halves
C. SNR quadruples
D. SNR unchanged

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When the period is halved, the signal frequency doubles, which concentrates the same signal power into a shorter time interval, effectively increasing the power per unit time. With noise power unchanged, the SNR therefore doubles, making option A correct.

Q7. Compare the conversion factor from milliseconds to seconds with that from microseconds to milliseconds.

A. Both are \(10^{-3}\) ✅
B. Both are \(10^{3}\)
C. First is \(10^{-3}\) and second is \(10^{-3}\)
D. First is \(10^{3}\) and second is \(10^{3}\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

1 ms = \(10^{-3}\) s and 1 µs = \(10^{-3}\) ms, so each step down the scale uses a factor of \(10^{-3}\). Therefore both conversions share the same factor, which is option A.

Q8. Evaluate which statement correctly describes the relationship between frequency and period for a sinusoidal signal.

A. Frequency is the square of period
B. Frequency is the reciprocal of period ✅
C. Frequency equals period multiplied by \(2\pi\)
D. Frequency is independent of period

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By definition, frequency \(f\) and period \(T\) are inverses: \(f = 1/T\). This reciprocal relationship is fundamental to periodic signals, making option B the accurate description.

Q9. Differentiate between kilohertz (kHz) and megahertz (MHz) in terms of the factor of 10.

A. 1 kHz = 10 MHz
B. 1 MHz = 1000 kHz ✅
C. 1 kHz = 1000 MHz
D. 1 MHz = 0.001 kHz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The prefix “kilo‑” denotes \(10^{3}\) and “mega‑” denotes \(10^{6}\). Hence 1 MHz equals \(10^{6}\) Hz, which is 1000 kHz. Option B correctly states this relationship.

Q10. Given a signal with frequency \(f = 2.5\text{ kHz}\), calculate its period in microseconds.

A. 0.4 µs
B. 400 µs ✅
C. 4000 µs
D. 0.04 µs

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Period \(T = 1/f = 1/2500\text{ Hz} = 0.0004\text{ s}\). Converting seconds to microseconds (\(1\text{ s}=10^{6}\text{ µs}\)) yields \(0.0004\text{ s} \times 10^{6}=400\text{ µs}\). Thus option B is correct.

Q11. Apply the principle of aliasing: If a sampling system can only detect frequencies up to 20 kHz, what happens to a component at 30 kHz?

A. It appears as 10 kHz ✅
B. It appears as 30 kHz
C. It is completely lost
D. It appears as 50 kHz

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Aliasing folds frequencies above the Nyquist limit back into the observable band. A 30 kHz component exceeds the 20 kHz limit by 10 kHz, so it is reflected and perceived as 10 kHz, which is option A.

Q12. Synthesize: How does increasing the bandwidth of a communication channel affect the thermal noise power, assuming temperature remains constant?

A. Noise power decreases
B. Noise power stays the same
C. Noise power increases proportionally to bandwidth ✅
D. Noise power is inversely proportional to bandwidth

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Thermal (Johnson‑Nyquist) noise power is given by \(P = kTB\), where \(B\) is bandwidth. With temperature \(T\) constant, expanding \(B\) raises \(P\) linearly. Therefore noise power increases proportionally to bandwidth, matching option C.

Q13. Explain why human eyes cannot distinguish amplitude changes occurring in less than 1 ms.

A. Photoreceptor response time is too slow ✅
B. Brain processing limit is too low
C. Light‑speed limit prevents faster changes
D. Retinal pigment density limits speed

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The photoreceptor cells in the retina have a finite response time, typically on the order of several milliseconds. Changes faster than this interval are integrated, preventing the visual system from resolving individual amplitude variations. Hence option A is correct.

Q14. In a digital system, jitter adds uncertainty to the period of a clock signal. If the nominal period is 100 ns and jitter is ±2 ns, what is the maximum possible frequency deviation in MHz?

A. 0.2 MHz ✅
B. 0.4 MHz
C. 0.8 MHz
D. 1.0 MHz

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Nominal frequency \(f_0 = 1/100\text{ ns}=10\text{ MHz}\). With jitter, the shortest period is 98 ns, giving \(f_{\max}=1/98\text{ ns}\approx10.204\text{ MHz}\). The deviation is about \(0.204\text{ MHz}\), which rounds to 0.2 MHz, option A.

Q15. In a noise measurement, SNR in decibels is \( \text{SNR}_{\text{dB}} = 10\log_{10}(P_s/P_n) \). If SNR improves from 20 dB to 30 dB, by what factor does the power ratio \(P_s/P_n\) increase?

A. 2
B. 5
C. 10 ✅
D. 100

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A 20 dB SNR corresponds to a power ratio of \(10^{20/10}=10^{2}=100\). A 30 dB SNR gives \(10^{30/10}=10^{3}=1000\). The ratio increases from 100 to 1000, a factor of 10. Hence option C is correct.
