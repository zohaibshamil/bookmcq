📝 All Attenuation and Transmission Impairment MCQs

Q1. What does the term 'attenuation' refer to in the context of signal transmission?

🔹 A. Loss of signal amplitude
🔹 B. Increase in signal frequency
🔹 C. Phase shift of the waveform
🔹 D. Additional time delay

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Attenuation is defined as the reduction in signal strength as it propagates through a medium. It primarily manifests as a loss of amplitude, meaning the received signal is weaker than the transmitted one. The other options describe different phenomena that are not attenuation.

Q2. In a transmission medium, the relationship between propagation speed \(c\), wavelength \(\lambda\), and frequency \(f\) is given by which equation?

🔹 A. \(c = \lambda + f\)
🔹 B. \(c = \lambda f\)
🔹 C. \(c = \frac{\lambda}{f}\)
🔹 D. \(c = \frac{f}{\lambda}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The fundamental wave relationship links speed, wavelength, and frequency by multiplication: \(c = \lambda f\). This equation shows that for a constant speed, wavelength and frequency are inversely related. The other listed expressions either add the quantities or invert the ratio, which are mathematically incorrect for wave propagation.

Q3. If a signal experiences higher attenuation over a fixed distance, what is the most direct effect on the received signal amplitude?

🔹 A. It increases
🔹 B. It stays the same
🔹 C. It decreases
🔹 D. It becomes more noisy

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Higher attenuation means more of the signal's power is lost while traveling, so the amplitude measured at the receiver will be lower than the transmitted amplitude. The other choices either describe unrelated changes or imply no effect, which contradicts the definition of attenuation.

Q4. Given two cables of equal length, Cable X has an attenuation of \(2 \text{ dB/km}\) and Cable Y has \(5 \text{ dB/km}\). Which statement best describes the relative received power assuming identical transmitted power?

🔹 A. Cable X will deliver more power
🔹 B. Cable Y will deliver more power
🔹 C. Both deliver equal power
🔹 D. Power cannot be compared without frequency

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Attenuation expressed in dB per kilometer indicates how much signal loss occurs per unit length. Since Cable X loses only 2 dB per km compared with 5 dB per km for Cable Y, the signal emerging from Cable X will be stronger, assuming all other factors are equal.

Q5. A fiber‑optic link operates at wavelength \(0.5 \,\mu\text{m}\) with an attenuation coefficient of \(0.2 \text{ dB/km}\). If the link length is doubled, by how many decibels does the total attenuation increase?

🔹 A. 0.2 dB
🔹 B. 0.4 dB
🔹 C. 0.8 dB
🔹 D. 1.0 dB

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Attenuation is proportional to distance; doubling the length doubles the total loss. The original loss is \(0.2 \text{ dB/km} \times L\). When \(L\) becomes \(2L\), the loss becomes \(0.2 \times 2L = 0.4 \text{ dB}\). Thus the increase is 0.4 dB.

Q6. Which of the following best explains why attenuation is generally lower in coaxial cable than in free space for the same frequency?

🔹 A. Coaxial cable confines the electromagnetic field reducing radiation losses
🔹 B. Coaxial cable amplifies the signal
🔹 C. Free space has higher permittivity
🔹 D. Coaxial cable uses higher propagation speed

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A coaxial cable shields the electromagnetic wave inside its conductors, limiting radiation into the surrounding environment. This confinement reduces loss mechanisms such as scattering and radiation, leading to lower attenuation compared with an unconfined wave propagating through free space where such losses are higher.

Q7. Consider two sine waves of equal amplitude, one at 2 GHz and another at 10 GHz traveling through the same medium with attenuation proportional to frequency. Which wave will experience greater attenuation?

🔹 A. 2 GHz wave
🔹 B. 10 GHz wave
🔹 C. Both equal
🔹 D. Depends on wavelength

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

If attenuation grows with frequency, the higher‑frequency wave (10 GHz) will suffer more loss per unit distance than the lower‑frequency (2 GHz) wave, because the proportional factor multiplies the larger frequency value, resulting in a larger attenuation coefficient.

Q8. A signal with initial power \(P_0\) passes through a medium with attenuation coefficient \(\alpha = 0.5 \text{ dB/m}\). After distance \(d\) the power is \(P = P_0 \times 10^{-\alpha d/10}\). If the power must drop to half of \(P_0\), what distance \(d\) is required?

🔹 A. \( \frac{10 \log_{10}2}{\alpha}\) m
🔹 B. \( \frac{5 \log_{10}2}{\alpha}\) m
🔹 C. \( \frac{10}{\alpha}\) m
🔹 D. \( \frac{\log_{10}2}{\alpha}\) m

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Setting \(P = P_0/2\) gives \(1/2 = 10^{-\alpha d/10}\). Taking \(\log_{10}\) of both sides yields \(-\alpha d/10 = \log_{10}(1/2) = -\log_{10}2\). Solving for \(d\) gives \(d = \frac{10 \log_{10}2}{\alpha}\). This expression directly relates the required distance to the attenuation coefficient.

Q9. If the propagation speed in a cable is reduced to half the speed of light, how does the wavelength of a 6 GHz signal change compared to free space?

🔹 A. It doubles
🔹 B. It halves
🔹 C. It stays same
🔹 D. It becomes zero

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Wavelength follows \(\lambda = c/f\). Halving the propagation speed \(c\) while keeping frequency constant halves the wavelength. Therefore the 6 GHz signal’s wavelength in the slower medium is half of its free‑space value.

Q10. A time‑domain plot of a sine wave shows a period of 0.2 s. What is the corresponding frequency in hertz?

🔹 A. 5 Hz
🔹 B. 0.2 Hz
🔹 C. 2 Hz
🔹 D. 10 Hz

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Frequency is the reciprocal of period: \(f = 1/T\). With \(T = 0.2\) s, \(f = 1/0.2 = 5\) Hz. The other options are either the period itself or incorrect reciprocals.

Q11. In the frequency domain, a single sine wave appears as a spike at its frequency. If a signal consists of two sine waves at 4 Hz and 7 Hz with equal amplitudes, how many distinct spikes will appear, and what does their relative height represent?

🔹 A. One spike; height is sum of amplitudes
🔹 B. Two spikes; height each equals individual amplitude
🔹 C. Two spikes; height indicates combined amplitude
🔹 D. Three spikes; one at each frequency plus a harmonic

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Each sinusoid contributes a separate spectral line, so two frequencies generate two spikes. Because the amplitudes are equal, each spike’s vertical height reflects that same amplitude, not a sum or a harmonic relationship.

Q12. When signal attenuation is high, designers may increase transmit power to maintain link reliability. Which of the following side effects is most likely?

🔹 A. Reduced noise figure
🔹 B. Increased nonlinear distortion
🔹 C. Decreased bandwidth
🔹 D. Lower antenna gain

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Boosting transmit power raises the signal level but also pushes amplifiers closer to their nonlinear operating region, which can introduce distortion. The other options either improve performance or are unrelated to power increase.

Q13. A coaxial cable exhibits attenuation that follows \( \alpha(f) = k f^2\) dB/m, where \(k = 1 \times 10^{-21}\) and \(f\) is in Hz. What is the attenuation per meter at \(f = 1 \text{ GHz}\)?

🔹 A. \(10^{-3}\) dB/m
🔹 B. \(1\) dB/m
🔹 C. \(10^{-6}\) dB/m
🔹 D. \(10^{-12}\) dB/m

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Insert \(f = 1\times10^{9}\) Hz into the formula: \(\alpha = 1\times10^{-21} \times (1\times10^{9})^{2} = 1\times10^{-21} \times 1\times10^{18} = 1\times10^{-3}\) dB/m. This yields a very small but non‑zero attenuation per meter.

Q14. In a frequency‑domain plot, what does the vertical height of a spike represent?

🔹 A. Phase shift
🔹 B. Wavelength
🔹 C. Peak amplitude
🔹 D. Propagation speed

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The magnitude of a spectral line (spike) corresponds to the signal’s peak amplitude at that frequency. Phase information is encoded elsewhere, while wavelength and propagation speed are related to frequency, not directly shown by spike height.

Q15. Two signals travel through the same medium: Signal A has wavelength \(0.75 \text{ m}\) and frequency \(400 \text{ MHz}\). Signal B has wavelength \(0.5 \text{ m}\). Assuming the same propagation speed, what is the frequency of Signal B?

🔹 A. \(300 \text{ MHz}\)
🔹 B. \(600 \text{ MHz}\)
🔹 C. \(800 \text{ MHz}\)
🔹 D. \(400 \text{ MHz}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Using \(c = \lambda f\), compute the speed from Signal A: \(c = 0.75 \times 400\text{ MHz} = 300\text{ MHz·m}\). For Signal B, \(f = c / \lambda = 300 / 0.5 = 600\) MHz.

Q16. If the attenuation of a channel doubles while the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) remains constant, what is the effect on the channel capacity according to Shannon's theorem \(C = B \log_2(1 + \text{SNR})\)?

🔹 A. Capacity halves
🔹 B. Capacity remains unchanged
🔹 C. Capacity decreases
🔹 D. Capacity increases

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Shannon’s capacity formula depends only on bandwidth \(B\) and SNR. If attenuation changes but SNR is held constant (perhaps by adjusting transmit power), the term inside the logarithm does not change, so capacity stays the same regardless of the increased loss.

Q17. A sine wave of 5 V peak amplitude and 6 Hz frequency appears as a spike in the frequency domain. If the amplitude doubles, how does the spike height change?

🔹 A. It halves
🔹 B. It stays the same
🔹 C. It doubles
🔹 D. It quadruples

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The spike height is directly proportional to the signal’s peak amplitude. Doubling the amplitude from 5 V to 10 V therefore doubles the height of the spectral line, while the frequency location remains unchanged.

Q18. A signal traveling through a medium experiences attenuation \(A = 20 \log_{10}(V_{in}/V_{out})\) dB. If the input voltage is 2 V and the output voltage is 0.5 V, what is the attenuation?

🔹 A. 6 dB
🔹 B. 12 dB
🔹 C. 3 dB
🔹 D. 9 dB

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The voltage ratio is \(2/0.5 = 4\). Applying the formula: \(A = 20 \log_{10}(4) = 20 \times 0.60206 \approx 12.04\) dB, which rounds to 12 dB.

Q19. Which of the following statements correctly describes the relationship between wavelength \(\lambda\) and frequency \(f\) for a wave traveling at constant speed \(c\)?

🔹 A. \(\lambda\) is directly proportional to \(f\)
🔹 B. \(\lambda\) is inversely proportional to \(f\)
🔹 C. \(\lambda\) is independent of \(f\)
🔹 D. \(\lambda = c+f\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

With constant speed \(c\), the fundamental wave equation \(c = \lambda f\) shows that wavelength and frequency are inversely related: as one increases, the other must decrease to keep the product equal to the constant speed.

Q20. When a high‑frequency signal (e.g., 10 GHz) passes through a medium with frequency‑dependent attenuation, which design strategy helps mitigate loss?

🔹 A. Use lower frequency carrier
🔹 B. Increase cable length
🔹 C. Reduce transmit power
🔹 D. Use a medium with higher dielectric constant

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Lowering the carrier frequency reduces the attenuation term if loss grows with frequency, thereby decreasing overall signal loss. Extending length or reducing power would worsen the link, while changing dielectric constant does not directly address frequency‑dependent loss.

Q21. In the time domain, a sine wave is described by \(v(t)=V_p \sin(2\pi f t + \phi)\). If the phase \(\phi\) is increased by \(\pi/2\), what is the effect on the waveform?

🔹 A. Frequency doubles
🔹 B. Amplitude doubles
🔹 C. Waveform shifts left by quarter period
🔹 D. No effect

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A phase increase of \(\pi/2\) radians corresponds to a time shift of \(\frac{1}{4}\) of the period (since \(\phi = 2\pi f \Delta t\)). This moves the entire waveform earlier in time (to the left) without altering its frequency or amplitude.

Q22. A transmission line has an attenuation of \(0.1 \text{ dB/m}\). Over what distance will the signal be attenuated by 3 dB?

🔹 A. 10 m
🔹 B. 30 m
🔹 C. 3 m
🔹 D. 0.3 m

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Total attenuation equals per‑meter loss multiplied by distance: \(A = 0.1 \text{ dB/m} \times d\). Setting \(A = 3\) dB gives \(d = 3 / 0.1 = 30\) m.

Q23. Suppose two identical channels differ only in their attenuation coefficients: Channel 1: \(0.05 \text{ dB/km}\), Channel 2: \(0.15 \text{ dB/km}\). If both transmit at 0 dBm, after 100 km which channel provides a higher received power, and by how many decibels?

🔹 A. Channel 1, 10 dB higher
🔹 B. Channel 2, 10 dB higher
🔹 C. Channel 1, 5 dB higher
🔹 D. Channel 2, 5 dB higher

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Attenuation for Channel 1 is \(0.05 \times 100 = 5\) dB, giving a received power of \(-5\) dBm. Channel 2 loses \(0.15 \times 100 = 15\) dB, resulting in \(-15\) dBm. The difference is \(15 - 5 = 10\) dB, so Channel 1 is 10 dB stronger.)
