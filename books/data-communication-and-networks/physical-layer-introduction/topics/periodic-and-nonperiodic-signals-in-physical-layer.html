What is Periodic and Nonperiodic signals in Physical Layer?

A periodic signal completes a predictable pattern within a fixed time frame and repeats indefinitely, making it suitable for continuous analog communication, while a nonperiodic signal does not repeat and has no fixed cycle, which is typical for digital data transmission where information is sent in irregular bursts rather than continuous waves.

Q1. Which layer of the OSI model is responsible for transmitting bits over the physical medium?

A.Application
B.Transport
C.Data‑link
D.Physical ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Physical layer is the lowest layer in the OSI model and handles the actual transmission of raw bits over a communication medium. It defines electrical, optical, and mechanical characteristics, making it the only layer that directly deals with the physical transmission of data.

Q3. Which statement must be true for a non‑periodic signal?

A.It has a defined fundamental frequency
B.It can be expressed as a sum of harmonics
C.Its Fourier series does not exist ✅
D.It repeats after some interval
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A non‑periodic signal does not repeat at regular intervals, so it lacks a fundamental period and cannot be represented by a Fourier series of discrete harmonics. Instead, it requires a continuous Fourier transform. Hence the Fourier series representation does not exist for such signals.

Q4. A jitter adds a random delay up to 1 ms to a signal with a 10 ms period. What is the maximum possible deviation from perfect periodicity?

A.1 ms ✅
B.10 ms
C.0.1 ms
D.5 ms
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Jitter introduces timing variations without changing the nominal period. The worst‑case scenario occurs when the delay reaches its maximum value of 1 ms, causing the interval between two consecutive edges to differ by up to 1 ms from the ideal 10 ms period.

Q5. Which characteristic best distinguishes periodic from non‑periodic signals in the frequency domain?

A.Periodic signals have infinite bandwidth
B.Periodic signals have a discrete spectrum ✅
C.Non‑periodic signals have a discrete spectrum
D.Both have the same bandwidth
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A periodic signal repeats regularly, producing a spectrum composed of discrete spectral lines at integer multiples of the fundamental frequency. In contrast, a non‑periodic signal yields a continuous spectrum. Therefore, the defining frequency‑domain property of periodic signals is a discrete spectrum.

Q6. Which statement best differentiates how the physical layer treats periodic and non‑periodic traffic?

A.Periodic traffic requires fixed‑slot timing, non‑periodic uses demand‑based access ✅
B.Both use identical encoding schemes
C.Non‑periodic traffic must be converted to periodic before transmission
D.Periodic traffic cannot experience jitter
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Periodic traffic often follows predetermined time slots (e.g., TDM), allowing the physical layer to schedule transmissions precisely. Non‑periodic traffic, however, is typically sent when the medium is idle, using contention‑based methods like CSMA. This distinction directly influences how the physical layer allocates resources.

Q7. Which of the following accurately describes the effect of signal periodicity on clock‑recovery mechanisms?

A.Periodic signals ease clock recovery ✅
B.Non‑periodic signals ease clock recovery
C.Both require equal effort
D.Synchronization is independent of periodicity
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Clock‑recovery circuits rely on regular transitions to lock onto a timing reference. A periodic signal provides predictable edges at known intervals, simplifying the extraction of a stable clock. Non‑periodic signals lack this regularity, making it harder for the receiver to maintain accurate synchronization.

Q9. In a WAN where routers introduce variable propagation delays, how can a periodic data stream be preserved?

B.Apply compression before transmission

Q12. For a non‑periodic digital signal, which encoding scheme guarantees no DC component?

A.NRZ‑L
B.Manchester ✅
C.AMI
D.Unipolar
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Manchester encoding combines a transition in the middle of each bit period, ensuring that every bit contains both a high and a low level. This guarantees a zero average (no DC) regardless of the data pattern, making it suitable for AC‑coupled physical media.
