📝 Analog and Digital Signals in Physical Layer (13 MCQs)

Physical Forms of Analog and Digital Signals

An analog signal changes continuously with time and can take an unlimited range of values within its operating limits. A digital signal represents information through a finite set of distinguishable signal elements, often voltage levels or pulses. The physical layer defines how these waveforms are placed on copper, optical fibre, or a wireless channel and how receivers recognize them. Compatible transmitter and receiver conventions are required.

Amplitude, Frequency, Phase and Signal Levels

A periodic analog waveform is described by amplitude, frequency, and phase. A digital waveform is described by its signal levels, symbol intervals, and transitions. Binary signaling commonly uses two levels, although multilevel schemes are possible. Real signals are affected by channel limits, so receivers use agreed thresholds and timing to distinguish valid symbols.

Generating and Detecting the Waveform

A transmitter converts bits or analog information into a waveform using a suitable encoding or modulation method. The signal propagates through the channel and may be weakened or altered. The receiver filters and detects the incoming waveform, recovers timing where needed, and decides which signal element was sent before reconstructing the data.

Comparing Two Signal Representations

Suppose a digital link represents bit 0 with a low voltage and bit 1 with a higher voltage. The transmitter sends a sequence such as 1, 0, 1 as corresponding signal levels. By contrast, a microphone output changes continuously with sound pressure. The examples show discrete versus continuous signal variation.

Signal Choice and Transmission System Design

Digital signals are common in computer networks because repeaters can regenerate defined levels and timing, limiting accumulated noise effects. Analog signals remain useful for carriers and many source waveforms. Modulation allows digital data to use bandpass channels, while digitization allows analog information to share digital networks with other data. It therefore influences end-to-end communication quality.

Avoiding Confusion Between Signal Categories

'Digital signals contain no physical variation' is false because voltage, light, or radio energy still changes. 'Every analog signal is periodic' is false because speech waveforms are generally nonperiodic. 'Two-level signaling is the only digital method' is false because a system may use multiple signal levels to encode more than one bit per symbol.

Interpreting Signal Diagrams and Parameters

Be able to classify a waveform as analog or digital from its shape and allowed values. Identify amplitude, frequency, phase, signal levels, and symbol intervals where applicable. For a digital sequence, relate each signal element to its encoded bits; for an analog waveform, describe continuous variation and avoid treating its instantaneous values as discrete symbols.

Analog and Digital Signal Terms

  • Waveform - Signal shape across time.
  • Amplitude - Signal's magnitude or strength.
  • Frequency - Cycles completed each second.
  • Phase - Position within a cycle.
  • Signal element - One transmitted signal unit.
  • Symbol interval - Time allocated per symbol.

📝 All Analog and Digital Signals in Physical Layer MCQs

Q1. If an analog signal experiences a 6 dB attenuation before reaching a digital receiver, which of the following is the most likely immediate effect?

A.Increase in signal‑to‑noise ratio
B.Decrease in required sampling rate
C.Reduced amplitude possibly causing bit errors ✅
D.No effect on digital decoding
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Attenuation lowers the signal amplitude, making it harder for the receiver to distinguish logical levels. When the amplitude drops, the decision threshold may be crossed incorrectly, leading to bit errors. The other options describe effects that are either opposite or unrelated to simple attenuation.

Q2. A network uses Manchester encoding for digital data transmission. If the clock frequency doubles, what is the impact on the bandwidth requirement of the transmitted signal?

A.Bandwidth halves
B.Bandwidth remains unchanged
C.Bandwidth increases by 1.5 times
D.Bandwidth doubles ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Manchester encoding produces a transition at each clock edge, so the fundamental frequency of the signal follows the clock. Doubling the clock therefore doubles the highest frequency component, which directly doubles the required bandwidth. The other choices underestimate or ignore this proportional relationship.

Q3. Consider a system where an ADC samples a sinusoidal signal at exactly twice its highest frequency component. According to the Nyquist criterion, what is the most probable consequence if the sampling rate drops by 10%?

A.No aliasing occurs
B.Minor amplitude distortion only
C.Aliasing introduces spurious frequency components ✅
D.The signal becomes completely unreadable
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Dropping the sampling rate below the Nyquist limit causes the sampled data to overlap in the frequency domain, producing aliasing. This creates false frequency components that corrupt the reconstructed signal. Minor distortion would not be sufficient, and total loss is unrealistic; aliasing is the expected outcome.

Q4. Which statement correctly contrasts analog and digital signals regarding noise susceptibility?

A.Analog signals are immune to noise
B.Digital signals degrade linearly with noise
C.Analog signals degrade continuously while digital signals tolerate noise up to a threshold ✅
D.Both are equally affected by noise
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Analog waveforms change continuously, so any added noise directly alters the signal amplitude. Digital signals, by representing discrete levels, can tolerate noise as long as it does not push the signal across the decision threshold. Hence digital signals are more robust until a certain noise level is exceeded.

Q5. In a point‑to‑point fiber optic link, the dispersion effect spreads an optical pulse over time. How does increasing the data rate from 1 Gb/s to 10 Gb/s affect the required dispersion compensation?

A.Compensation can be reduced by a factor of 10
B.Compensation must be increased proportionally ✅
C.No change is needed
D.Compensation must be increased by the square root of the data‑rate ratio
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Higher data rates shorten the symbol interval, making pulses more vulnerable to temporal spreading. To keep inter‑symbol interference within limits, the amount of dispersion compensation must grow in proportion to the data‑rate increase, i.e., roughly tenfold for a ten‑fold speedup.

Q6. A digital communication system employs pulse‑amplitude modulation (PAM) with 4 levels (4‑PAM). Compared to binary NRZ signaling at the same bit rate, which of the following accurately describes the required bandwidth?

A.4‑PAM requires twice the bandwidth
B.4‑PAM requires the same bandwidth
C.4‑PAM requires half the bandwidth ✅
D.Bandwidth is independent of modulation order
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Using 4‑PAM conveys two bits per symbol, so the symbol rate is half the bit rate. Bandwidth is roughly proportional to the symbol rate, meaning the required bandwidth is about half that of binary NRZ transmitting the same number of bits per second.

Q7. Applying the Nyquist sampling theorem, what minimum sampling frequency is required to faithfully reconstruct an audio signal with a maximum frequency of 20 kHz?

A.20 kHz
B.40 kHz ✅
C.10 kHz
D.30 kHz
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Nyquist states that the sampling frequency must be at least twice the highest frequency component. For a 20 kHz audio limit, the minimum sampling rate is \(2 \times 20\text{ kHz} = 40\text{ kHz}\). Lower rates would cause aliasing, while higher rates are unnecessary for basic reconstruction.

Q8. When converting an analog voice signal to a digital format using PCM with 8‑bit quantization and a sampling rate of 8 kHz, what is the resulting bit rate?

A.64 kbps ✅
B.8 kbps
C.128 kbps
D.16 kbps
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The PCM bit rate equals the product of sampling rate and bits per sample: \(8{,}000\ \text{samples/s} \times 8\ \text{bits} = 64{,}000\ \text{bits/s}\), i.e., 64 kbps. The other options arise from incorrect multiplication or omission of one factor.

Q9. In a communication system that alternates between analog FM and digital QPSK modulation on the same carrier, which parameter must be adjusted to maintain the same occupied bandwidth for both schemes?

A.Carrier frequency
B.Modulation index for FM
C.Symbol rate for QPSK
D.Both B and C appropriately ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: FM bandwidth is proportional to the product of modulation index and carrier frequency deviation, while QPSK bandwidth depends on the symbol rate. To keep the total occupied bandwidth equal, the FM modulation index and the QPSK symbol rate must be tuned together, making option D correct.

Q10. Which of the following best defines an analog signal?

A.A signal represented by discrete voltage levels
B.A continuous‑time waveform that can take any value within a range ✅
C.A binary sequence of ones and zeros
D.A digital representation of data
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An analog signal varies continuously over time and can assume any amplitude within its physical limits. The other options describe digital or quantized representations, which are not characteristic of true analog signals.

Q11. If a digital signal is transmitted over a channel with a maximum allowable frequency of 5 MHz, and the system uses baseband NRZ signaling, what is the highest achievable bit rate without violating the channel limit?

A.5 Mbps
B.10 Mbps ✅
C.2.5 Mbps
D.20 Mbps
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: NRZ baseband signaling occupies roughly half the channel bandwidth, so the maximum bit rate equals twice the channel’s highest frequency: \(2 \times 5\text{ MHz} = 10\text{ Mbps}\). This respects the Nyquist limit for baseband transmission.

Q12. A switch operates at the physical layer by converting incoming electrical pulses into optical signals for fiber transmission. Which of the following best explains why this conversion improves signal integrity over long distances?

A.Optical signals are immune to electromagnetic interference ✅
B.Electrical signals travel faster
C.Optical conversion reduces latency
D.Electrical signals require less power
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Fiber‑optic carriers are not affected by external electromagnetic fields, which eliminates crosstalk and noise that degrade electrical signals over long runs. While speed and latency are important, immunity to interference is the primary reason for superior integrity.

Q13. In a hybrid network where both analog and digital signals coexist, synchronization between the two domains is critical. Which technique is commonly used to align timing between analog modulation and digital sampling?

A.Phase‑locked loop (PLL) ✅
B.Amplitude modulation
C.Manchester coding
D.Frequency division multiplexing
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A PLL can lock the phase of a local oscillator to a reference, ensuring that the analog carrier and digital sampling clocks stay synchronized. This alignment is essential for coherent demodulation and accurate digital reconstruction, whereas the other techniques serve different purposes.
