📝 Time and Frequency Domains in Periodic Analog Signals (14 MCQs)

Two Views of the Same Signal

The time domain describes how a signal's amplitude changes with time, while the frequency domain describes the frequencies present and their magnitudes or phases. These are two representations of the same signal, not different physical signals. Both views are useful when studying periodic waveforms and the frequency limits of communication channels. This establishes the physical representation at both communication endpoints.

Reading Waveforms and Spectral Components

A time-domain graph places time on the horizontal axis and instantaneous amplitude on the vertical axis. A frequency-domain graph places frequency on the horizontal axis and indicates the strength of each frequency component. A pure sine wave has one frequency component, whereas a composite waveform can contain several. These properties are selected to match the physical medium.

Converting Between the Two Representations

Fourier analysis decomposes a suitable signal into sinusoidal components. For a periodic signal, this commonly produces a fundamental frequency and harmonics at integer multiples. The inverse process combines the components to reconstruct the time waveform. The frequency representation reveals spectral content that may be difficult to infer from the waveform alone.

Interpreting a Fundamental and Its Harmonics

A periodic waveform with a 1 kHz fundamental may contain components at 2 kHz and 3 kHz. Its spectrum shows lines at those frequencies, while its time-domain graph shows the combined repeating shape. The lowest component is the fundamental, and higher integer multiples are harmonics. This isolates the relevant principle using realistic link parameters.

Why Frequency-Domain Analysis Matters

Channel bandwidth is usually specified as a range of frequencies, so frequency-domain analysis helps determine whether a signal's components fit within a transmission channel. Time-domain analysis shows pulse shape, timing, and waveform distortion. Engineers use both views to connect signal composition with transmission quality. It therefore influences end-to-end communication quality.

Mistakes When Switching Domains

'The frequency domain shows a signal at a later time' is incorrect; it shows frequency content. 'A pure sine wave contains many harmonics' is incorrect because an ideal sine wave has one frequency component. 'A spectrum replaces the time waveform' is misleading because the representations answer different questions about the same signal.

Reading Time Plots and Frequency Spectra

For a time plot, determine period and amplitude from the waveform. For a spectrum, identify the fundamental, harmonics, and highest significant frequency. When asked about bandwidth, calculate the span between the highest and lowest frequency components present, rather than confusing it with the signal's period or data rate. Show the formula, units, assumptions, and intermediate steps.

Time-Frequency Analysis Terms

  • Time domain - Amplitude plotted against time.
  • Frequency domain - Components plotted by frequency.
  • Spectrum - Display of frequency components.
  • Fourier analysis - Decomposes signals into sinusoids.
  • Fundamental - Lowest component frequency.
  • Harmonic - Integer multiple of fundamental.

📝 All Time and Frequency Domains in Periodic Analog Signals MCQs

Q1. What is the primary difference between analog and digital signals in the time domain?

A.Analog signals are continuous in time, digital signals have discrete time intervals. ✅
B.Analog signals have discrete amplitude levels, digital signals are continuous.
C.Analog signals are represented by binary bits, digital signals by sine waves.
D.Analog signals vary only in frequency, digital signals vary only in amplitude.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Analog signals maintain a continuous waveform over time, meaning every instant has a defined value, whereas digital signals are defined only at distinct sampling moments, producing a step‑like representation. This distinction underlies how each type is processed and transmitted, affecting conversion, bandwidth, and error handling in communication systems.

Q2. If a signal is sampled at a rate lower than its Nyquist frequency, which of the following effects is most likely to occur?

A.Attenuation of the signal
B.Aliasing of frequency components ✅
C.Quantization noise addition
D.Phase shift of the waveform
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When the sampling rate falls below twice the highest frequency component (the Nyquist rate), the sampled data cannot uniquely represent the original waveform. Consequently, higher‑frequency components fold back into lower frequencies, producing spurious spectral copies known as aliasing. This distortion can corrupt the recovered signal and is a primary concern in digital communication system design.

Q3. Compare the time‑domain representation of a square wave with its frequency‑domain representation. Which statement is true?

A.The square wave has only a single frequency component.
B.The square wave's frequency spectrum is continuous.
C.The square wave has infinite harmonics decreasing as 1/n. ✅
D.The square wave contains only even harmonics.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A square wave is a periodic, non‑sinusoidal waveform whose Fourier series consists of an infinite set of odd harmonics. Each harmonic’s amplitude falls proportionally to the reciprocal of its order (1/n), giving the characteristic “rich” spectrum. This harmonic structure explains why square waves contain many frequency components, unlike a pure sine wave which has just one.

Q4. Applying the Fourier Transform to a time‑domain pulse results in which type of frequency‑domain shape?

A.Sinc function ✅
B.Gaussian function
C.Rectangular function
D.Delta spikes
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Fourier Transform of a rectangular time‑domain pulse yields a sinc function in the frequency domain, reflecting the reciprocal relationship between duration and bandwidth. The main lobe’s width is inversely proportional to the pulse width, while side lobes decay as 1/f. This property is fundamental for understanding bandwidth requirements of pulse‑based signaling schemes.

Q5. In a communication system, increasing the symbol rate while keeping the bandwidth constant will most directly affect which of the following?

A.Increase intersymbol interference
B.Reduce signal‑to‑noise ratio
C.Increase required filter complexity ✅
D.Decrease latency
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: When the symbol rate rises without expanding the available spectral bandwidth, the system must pack more symbols into the same frequency range. To preserve signal integrity, filters become steeper and more selective, demanding higher order designs or advanced techniques. This added filter complexity can raise implementation cost and power consumption, even though latency may not change directly.

Q6. Suppose a baseband signal with bandwidth B = 2 kHz is transmitted over a channel using quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) with 16 symbols. What is the minimum required Nyquist bandwidth for the transmitted signal?

A.2 kHz
B.4 kHz ✅
C.8 kHz
D.16 kHz
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a baseband signal, the Nyquist bandwidth equals twice the highest frequency component, so 2 × 2 kHz = 4 kHz. QAM transmits two bits per symbol, but the symbol rate must still satisfy the Nyquist criterion. Therefore, the minimum bandwidth needed to avoid intersymbol interference is 4 kHz, independent of the constellation size.

Q7. If a system exhibits a time‑domain impulse response that is symmetric, what can be inferred about its frequency response?

A.It is purely real
B.It is purely imaginary
C.It has linear phase ✅
D.It is non‑causal
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A symmetric (even) impulse response implies that the phase of its Fourier Transform varies linearly with frequency, while the magnitude remains even. This linear‑phase characteristic ensures that all frequency components experience the same time delay, preserving waveform shape after filtering. Hence, symmetry in the time domain directly translates to linear phase in the frequency domain.

Q8. Contrast the effects of windowing a signal before performing an FFT versus not windowing it. Which outcome is most accurate?

A.Windowing reduces spectral leakage ✅
B.Windowing increases leakage
C.No effect on the spectrum
D.Windowing changes time resolution only
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Applying a window (e.g., Hamming or Hann) tapers the signal edges, mitigating the abrupt discontinuities that cause spectral leakage when the FFT assumes periodic extension. This reduction spreads energy from strong bins into neighboring bins, yielding a cleaner spectrum. Without windowing, sharp edges generate sinc‑shaped sidelobes, obscuring weak frequency components.

Q9. Evaluate the trade‑off between time resolution and frequency resolution in a short‑time Fourier transform (STFT). Which statement correctly describes this trade‑off?

A.Shorter windows improve frequency resolution
B.Longer windows improve time resolution
C.Shorter windows improve time resolution but worsen frequency resolution ✅
D.There is no trade‑off
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: In an STFT, the analysis window length determines the resolution: a short window captures rapid temporal changes, giving good time resolution but a broad main lobe in frequency, thus poorer frequency resolution. Conversely, a long window provides narrow frequency bins but blurs rapid transitions. This inverse relationship is fundamental to time‑frequency analysis.

Q10. Applying a low‑pass filter with cutoff frequency f_c to a signal removes components above f_c. If the original signal contains a component at 1.5 f_c, what happens to that component?

A.It is attenuated to near zero ✅
B.It is shifted to 0.5 f_c
C.It remains unchanged
D.It doubles in amplitude
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A component whose frequency exceeds the filter’s cutoff lies in the stop‑band and is therefore strongly suppressed. Ideal low‑pass behavior would eliminate it completely; practical filters attenuate it according to their roll‑off, typically reducing its magnitude to a negligible level, while leaving lower‑frequency components largely intact.

Q11. In a digital communication link, the jitter of a recovered clock is measured to be 0.2 of the unit interval (UI). If the symbol period is 10 ns, what is the maximum timing error in picoseconds?

A.200 ps
B.2 ns ✅
C.20 ps
D.0.2 ns
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A jitter of 0.2 UI means the timing uncertainty equals 0.2 × 10 ns = 2 ns. Converting to picoseconds (1 ns = 1000 ps) yields 2000 ps. Therefore, the maximum timing error introduced by jitter is 2 ns, which can impact eye‑diagram opening and error rates in high‑speed links.

Q12. Consider a system where a signal x(t) is passed through a linear time‑invariant (LTI) filter with impulse response h(t). If the output y(t) is observed to be a delayed version of the input with no distortion, which property must h(t) satisfy?

A.It must be a pure delay (δ(t‑τ)) ✅
B.It must be an all‑pass filter
C.It must be a low‑pass filter
D.It must be causal and stable
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A delayed replica of the input indicates that the filter’s frequency response has constant magnitude (unity) and a linear phase term corresponding to the delay. The only impulse response that yields such behavior is a shifted Dirac delta, h(t)=δ(t‑τ), which imparts a pure time shift without altering the signal’s spectral content.

Q13. Given two signals x₁(t)=cos(2π·1000 t) and x₂(t)=cos(2π·1000 t+φ). When these signals are added, the resulting amplitude depends on φ. Derive the condition on φ that yields maximum constructive interference.

A.φ = 0 or multiples of 2π ✅
B.φ = π
C.φ = π/2
D.φ = π/4
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Adding the two cosines gives 2 cos(π·φ)·cos(2π·1000 t+φ/2). The resultant amplitude reaches its maximum when cos(π·φ) = 1, which occurs for φ = 0, ±2π, … . Thus, zero phase difference (or integer multiples of 2π) produces perfect constructive interference, doubling the original amplitude.

Q14. In the context of time‑frequency analysis, the Heisenberg uncertainty principle states that the product of the effective duration Δt and effective bandwidth Δf of a signal cannot be arbitrarily small. Which of the following best expresses this principle?

A.Δt·Δf ≥ 1/(4π) ✅
B.Δt·Δf = 0
C.Δt·Δf ≤ 1/2
D.Δt·Δf ≥ 1
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The continuous‑time version of the uncertainty principle asserts that the time‑bandwidth product is bounded below by 1/(4π). This inequality reflects the trade‑off that a signal cannot be simultaneously arbitrarily short in time and arbitrarily narrow in frequency, a fundamental limit that guides window design and modulation choices.)
