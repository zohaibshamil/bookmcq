What is Bandwidth performance of the network?

Bandwidth in networking performance refers to the maximum theoretical data transfer capacity of a communication link measured in bits per second, representing the upper limit of what the physical medium and protocols can support under ideal conditions, but students should distinguish this from actual achieved throughput since bandwidth alone does not guarantee real-world performance due to overhead, congestion, and protocol inefficiencies.

Q1. What is the formula that relates period \(T\) and frequency \(f\)?

A. \(T = 1/f\) ✅
B. \(T = f\)
C. \(T = f^{2}\)
D. \(T = \sqrt{f}\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The period is defined as the time required for one complete cycle, while frequency counts how many cycles occur per second. By definition, the two are inverses of each other, so the period equals the reciprocal of the frequency, expressed as \(T = 1/f\). This relationship holds for any periodic waveform.

Q2. For a pure sinusoidal voltage, the peak value is how many times the rms value?

A. 1
B. \\(\\sqrt{2}\\) ✅
C. 2
D. 2\\sqrt{2}

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

For a sinusoid, the rms value is the peak divided by the square‑root of two. This comes from integrating the squared waveform over a full cycle and taking the square root of the mean. Consequently, the peak value equals rms multiplied by \\(\\sqrt{2}\\), giving a conversion factor of about 1.414.

Q3. If the frequency of a sinusoidal signal is doubled, what happens to its period?

A. It is halved ✅
B. It stays the same
C. It doubles
D. It becomes one‑quarter

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Frequency and period are reciprocals: \(f = 1/T\). Doubling the frequency makes the denominator larger, so the period must shrink to keep the product equal to one. Mathematically, if the original period is \(T\), the new period becomes \(T/2\), i.e., it is halved.

Q4. A battery provides a constant voltage of 1.5 V rms. If the peak voltage is measured as 2.12 V, what can be inferred about the waveform?

A. It is a sine wave ✅
B. It is a square wave
C. It is a distorted sine wave
D. It is a triangular wave

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The ratio of peak to rms for a pure sine wave is \\(\\sqrt{2}\\) (≈1.414). Multiplying 1.5 V by \\(\\sqrt{2}\\) yields about 2.12 V, matching the measurement. Square or triangular waves have different ratios, so the observed values indicate a sinusoidal waveform.

Q5. An AC source has rms voltage 120 V and frequency 60 Hz. If a load draws 10 A rms, what is the apparent power and how does it compare to the real power if the load is purely resistive?

A. 1200 VA, equal to real power ✅
B. 1200 VA, less than real power
C. 1200 VA, greater than real power
D. 2400 VA, equal to real power

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Apparent power \(S\) equals rms voltage times rms current: \(S = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}} = 120\text{ V} \times 10\text{ A} = 1200\text{ VA}\). For a purely resistive load, the power factor is 1, so real power \(P\) equals apparent power, making them identical.

Q6. When a signal’s period is measured as 0.005 s, what is the frequency, and what would be the period if the frequency were increased to 500 Hz?

A. 200 Hz and 0.002 s ✅
B. 200 Hz and 0.004 s
C. 200 Hz and 0.001 s
D. 500 Hz and 0.002 s

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Frequency is the reciprocal of period: \(f = 1/T\). With \(T = 0.005\) s, \(f = 1/0.005 = 200\) Hz. Raising the frequency to 500 Hz gives a new period \(T = 1/500 = 0.002\) s, confirming the values in option A.

Q7. A signal has peak amplitude 10 V. Its rms value is measured as 7.07 V. If the signal passes through an ideal low‑pass filter that removes all harmonic components, what will be the rms voltage at the filter output and why?

A. 7.07 V, unchanged
B. 10 V, because peak becomes rms
C. 7.07 V, because the filter preserves the fundamental component ✅
D. 5 V, due to attenuation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

An ideal low‑pass filter eliminates harmonics, leaving only the fundamental sinusoidal component. The rms of that fundamental is exactly the original rms value (7.07 V). The peak value does not become the rms after filtering, and no attenuation is assumed, so the rms remains unchanged.

Q8. Compare the period of a 50 Hz European power signal with the period of a 60 Hz North American power signal. Which statement is correct?

A. The 50 Hz period is longer ✅
B. The 50 Hz period is shorter
C. Both periods are equal
D. The 60 Hz period is longer

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Period equals the reciprocal of frequency. For 50 Hz, \(T = 1/50 = 0.020\) s; for 60 Hz, \(T = 1/60 ≈ 0.0167\) s. The lower‑frequency (50 Hz) signal therefore has the longer period, making option A correct.

Q9. Evaluate the effect on bandwidth if the rise time \(t_r\) of a system is halved, assuming a first‑order relationship \(BW \\approx \\frac{0.35}{t_r}\\). What happens to the bandwidth?

A. It doubles ✅
B. It halves
C. It increases by 25 %
D. It remains unchanged

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The given formula shows bandwidth is inversely proportional to rise time. Halving \(t_r\) makes the denominator half as large, so the overall value doubles. Therefore the system’s bandwidth increases by a factor of two, confirming option A.

Q10. Differentiate between RMS and peak values for a sinusoid in terms of energy delivery to a resistive load.

A. RMS reflects average power while peak does not ✅
B. Peak reflects average power while RMS does not
C. Both are equal for a sinusoid
D. Neither relates to power

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

RMS voltage represents the equivalent DC voltage that would deliver the same average power to a resistor, because power depends on the square of the instantaneous voltage. Peak voltage is merely the maximum instantaneous value and does not convey average energy, so RMS is the appropriate measure for power calculations.

Q11. Given two signals with identical frequency and phase but amplitudes 3 V and 5 V respectively, analyze how their combined signal’s bandwidth is affected when they are summed.

A. Bandwidth remains unchanged; only amplitude adds ✅
B. Bandwidth doubles
C. Bandwidth halves
D. New bandwidth is the average of the individual

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When two sinusoids share the same frequency and phase, their spectra overlap completely; summing them merely increases the amplitude of that single frequency component. No new frequency components are created, so the overall bandwidth stays the same while the signal’s magnitude grows.

Q12. A communication channel has a specified bandwidth of 3 MHz. If the signal’s fundamental frequency is 1 MHz, determine the highest harmonic that can be transmitted without attenuation, assuming only integer harmonics.

A. 2nd harmonic
B. 3rd harmonic ✅
C. 4th harmonic
D. 5th harmonic

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The channel passes frequencies up to 3 MHz. Harmonics appear at integer multiples of the fundamental: 2 MHz (2nd), 3 MHz (3rd), 4 MHz (4th) exceeds the limit. Thus the 3rd harmonic, at exactly 3 MHz, is the highest that can be carried without attenuation.

Q13. Apply the relationship between rms and peak values to explain why a 120 V rms household outlet has a peak voltage of approximately 170 V.

A. Multiply rms by \\(\\sqrt{2}\\) ✅
B. Multiply rms by 2
C. Divide rms by \\(\\sqrt{2}\\)
D. Add 50 V

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

For a sinusoidal voltage, peak = rms × \\(\\sqrt{2}\\). Multiplying 120 V by \\(\\sqrt{2}\\) (≈1.414) yields about 170 V, which matches the observed peak. This conversion reflects the fact that the rms value represents the effective heating value, while the peak is the instantaneous maximum.

Q14. Synthesize how changing the frequency of an AC signal influences its period and consequently the design of a filter intended to pass only one cycle of the waveform.

A. Higher frequency shortens period, requiring faster filter response ✅
B. Higher frequency lengthens period, requiring slower response
C. Frequency does not affect period
D. Filter design is independent of period

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since period \(T = 1/f\), increasing frequency reduces the time per cycle. A filter that must pass exactly one cycle therefore needs a bandwidth and transient response fast enough to settle within the shortened period. Designers must therefore choose components with higher cutoff frequencies and lower group delay for higher‑frequency signals.

Q15. Explain why the period of a sinusoid is considered the reciprocal of its frequency, using the definition of Hertz.

A. Because Hz is cycles per second, so seconds per cycle = 1/f ✅
B. Because Hz is seconds per cycle
C. Because period and frequency are unrelated
D. Because frequency is measured in seconds

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

One hertz equals one cycle per second. If a waveform completes \(f\) cycles each second, the duration of a single cycle—its period—must be the fraction of a second per cycle, which is \(1/f\). This reciprocal relationship directly follows from the definition of the unit hertz.

Q16. If a signal’s RMS voltage is 20 V and its peak voltage is measured as 28.3 V, deduce the waveform shape and justify your conclusion using the RMS‑to‑peak ratio.

A. It is a sine wave ✅
B. It is a square wave
C. It is a triangular wave
D. It is a sawtooth wave

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The ratio \(V_{\text{peak}}/V_{\text{rms}} = 28.3/20 ≈ 1.415\), which is essentially \\(\\sqrt{2}\\). This ratio is characteristic of a pure sinusoid. Square, triangular, or sawtooth waves have different ratios (1, \\(\\sqrt{3}\\), etc.), so the measured values indicate a sinusoidal waveform.

Q17. A system’s bandwidth is defined as the frequency range where the output power falls within –3 dB of the maximum. If the –3 dB points are at 1.2 kHz and 3.8 kHz, calculate the bandwidth and discuss its significance for signal transmission.

A. 2.6 kHz, indicates a moderate range
B. 5 kHz, indicates a wide range
C. 2.6 kHz, indicates a narrow range ✅
D. 3.0 kHz, indicates an average range

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Bandwidth equals the upper –3 dB frequency minus the lower –3 dB frequency: \(3.8\text{ kHz} - 1.2\text{ kHz} = 2.6\text{ kHz}\). A 2.6 kHz span is relatively narrow for high‑speed communications, limiting the data‑rate and the ability to transmit rapidly changing signals without distortion.
