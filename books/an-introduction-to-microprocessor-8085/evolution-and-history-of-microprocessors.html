About 1. Evolution and History of Microprocessors

The Big Idea: Think of a microprocessor as the "brain" of a computer, but instead of being made of biological cells, it is made of millions of tiny electronic switches called transistors on a single piece of silicon. Before microprocessors, a computer's brain was a huge, room-sized machine made of separate parts. This chapter tells the story of how engineers kept shrinking those parts until they could fit an entire CPU (Central Processing Unit) onto one small chip, starting from the 4-bit Intel 4004 to the powerful 8-bit 8085 that we study today.

How It Works: You don't need to memorize every old chip name; instead, focus on the trend. Each new generation (like the 8008, 8080, and then 8085) did two things: it got faster and it could handle more data at once (going from 4 bits to 8 bits). The 8085 is famous because it simplified the design by using a single +5-volt power supply (unlike older chips that needed multiple voltages), making it cheaper and easier to use in teaching and small systems. In short, this chapter helps you appreciate that the 8085 isn't just an old chip—it is a successful product of this amazing "shrinking" journey.
