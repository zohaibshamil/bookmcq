📖 The Foundations of Biochemistry

Foundations of Biochemistry

is the opening chapter that establishes the core chemical and biological principles needed to understand life at a molecular level. It begins by defining the key classes of biomolecules—carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids—and explains how their unique structures determine their functions in cells. The chapter then introduces the concept of cellular organization, comparing prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, and highlights the importance of water as the solvent for all biochemical reactions, focusing on its polarity, hydrogen bonding, and role in maintaining pH through buffers.Moving from structure to energy, the chapter covers thermodynamics in living systems, explaining how cells obtain, store, and use energy via ATP and coupled reactions. It introduces enzymes as biological catalysts that speed up reactions without being consumed, and discusses factors like activation energy and specificity. Finally, the foundations touch on metabolic pathways—the organized sequences of chemical reactions—and the principle of dynamic steady state, where cells maintain internal balance while constantly exchanging matter and energy with their surroundings. These ideas form the essential toolkit for exploring all subsequent topics in biochemistry.
