What is Exergonic vs endergonic reactions?

Definition:
Exergonic reactions are chemical reactions that release free energy (ΔG<0\Delta G < 0ΔG<0) and are spontaneous, such as catabolic pathways like glycolysis and cellular respiration, while endergonic reactions absorb free energy (ΔG>0\Delta G > 0ΔG>0) and are non-spontaneous, requiring energy input, such as anabolic pathways like protein synthesis, and in living systems, energy from exergonic reactions (e.g., ATP hydrolysis) drives endergonic reactions.

Working:
Exergonic reactions work by decreasing the free energy of the system, often releasing heat and doing work, and they are thermodynamically favorable; endergonic reactions require an external energy source to proceed, and they are coupled to exergonic reactions in cells to make them proceed, where the coupling is achieved through shared intermediates like ATP, and the overall ΔG\Delta GΔG of a coupled reaction is the sum of the individual ΔG\Delta GΔG values.

Example:
A simple example is cellular respiration, where glucose oxidation has ΔG≈−686 kcal/mol\Delta G \approx -686 \text{ kcal/mol}ΔG≈−686 kcal/mol (exergonic), and the energy released is used to synthesize ATP from ADP and phosphate, which is endergonic (ΔG≈+7.3 kcal/mol\Delta G \approx +7.3 \text{ kcal/mol}ΔG≈+7.3 kcal/mol per ATP), and these reactions are coupled in mitochondria, demonstrating how energy is transferred and used in cells.

Reason:
Understanding exergonic and endergonic reactions is essential for grasping metabolism, as it explains how cells capture and use energy, and it is critical for understanding enzyme function, metabolic regulation, and the design of energy-efficient biotechnological processes.

Q1. Which statement best distinguishes an exergonic reaction from an endergonic reaction under a specified set of conditions?

A.An exergonic reaction always requires an external energy source, whereas an endergonic reaction never does
B.An exergonic reaction has ΔG<0\Delta G < 0ΔG<0, whereas an endergonic reaction has ΔG>0\Delta G > 0ΔG>0 ✅
C.An exergonic reaction always releases heat, whereas an endergonic reaction always absorbs heat
D.An exergonic reaction must be fast, whereas an endergonic reaction must be slow
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The key distinction is the sign of Gibbs free-energy change. An exergonic reaction has ΔG<0\Delta G < 0ΔG<0 and is thermodynamically favorable in the stated direction, whereas an endergonic reaction has ΔG>0\Delta G > 0ΔG>0 and requires energy input or coupling to proceed favorably.

Q2. A student argues that every reaction that releases heat must be exergonic. Which response most accurately evaluates this reasoning?

A.Correct, because heat release directly determines ΔG\Delta GΔG
B.Correct, because exergonic means exactly the same as exothermic
C.Incorrect, because ΔG\Delta GΔG depends on both enthalpy and entropy, not heat release alone ✅
D.Incorrect, because exergonic reactions can never release heat
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The reasoning confuses two different thermodynamic quantities. Heat release is associated primarily with enthalpy change, whereas spontaneity is determined by Gibbs free energy, ΔG=ΔH−TΔS\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta SΔG=ΔH−TΔS. Thus, heat release alone cannot establish whether a reaction is exergonic.

Since ΔG<0\Delta G < 0ΔG<0, the reaction is exergonic at this temperature. The negative enthalpy helps, while negative entropy opposes spontaneity.

Q4. An endergonic biosynthetic reaction is observed to proceed efficiently inside a cell. Which explanation best accounts for this observation?

A.The reaction becomes exergonic simply because enzymes are present
B.The cell can couple the endergonic reaction to an exergonic reaction so the combined process has favorable ΔG\Delta GΔG ✅
C.Endergonic reactions become spontaneous whenever their activation energy is low
D.The reaction must actually be exothermic, so it cannot be endergonic
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An endergonic reaction can proceed when it is coupled to a sufficiently exergonic process. The Gibbs free-energy changes of coupled reactions are additive, so a strongly negative contribution can make the overall process favorable even though one individual reaction has positive ΔG\Delta GΔG.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For coupled reactions, the Gibbs free-energy changes add algebraically. Therefore, −25+18=−7 kJ/mol-25 + 18 = -7\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}−25+18=−7 kJ/mol. Because the overall ΔG\Delta GΔG is negative, the coupled pathway is thermodynamically favorable even though the second reaction alone is endergonic.

Q7. A researcher compares two reactions. Reaction X has a large negative ΔG\Delta GΔG but a high activation barrier, while reaction Y has a smaller negative ΔG\Delta GΔG and a low activation barrier. Which prediction is most scientifically justified?

A.X must always occur faster because its ΔG\Delta GΔG is more negative
B.Y may proceed faster even though X is more thermodynamically favorable ✅
C.X cannot occur because high activation energy means positive ΔG\Delta GΔG
D.Y must release more energy because it has a lower activation barrier
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Thermodynamic favorability and reaction rate describe different aspects of a reaction. A more negative ΔG\Delta GΔG indicates greater thermodynamic driving force, but activation energy controls kinetics. Therefore, reaction Y can be faster despite having a less negative ΔG\Delta GΔG.

Q8. A student calculates ΔG=+8 kJ/mol\Delta G = +8\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}ΔG=+8 kJ/mol and concludes, 'The reaction cannot occur under any circumstances.' What is the most accurate correction?

A.Positive ΔG\Delta GΔG means the reaction cannot occur at all
B.Positive ΔG\Delta GΔG means the reaction is unfavorable in that direction under those conditions, but coupling or changed conditions can make it favorable ✅
C.Positive ΔG\Delta GΔG means the reaction must be extremely fast
D.Positive ΔG\Delta GΔG proves the reaction is exothermic
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A positive Gibbs free-energy change means the reaction is thermodynamically unfavorable in the specified direction under the stated conditions. It does not mean the reaction is impossible. Coupling, altered concentrations, temperature, or other changes can shift the thermodynamic balance.

Q11. A cell converts a nutrient through an exergonic reaction and uses the released free energy to drive an endergonic transport process. Which model best represents the overall strategy?

A.The cell reverses the sign of the nutrient reaction without changing anything else
B.The cell stores or transfers free energy through coupling so the combined reactions have a favorable ΔG\Delta GΔG ✅
C.The cell violates conservation of energy because the transport step gains energy
D.The transport process becomes exergonic simply because nutrients are present
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Cells do not create free energy. Instead, they couple favorable and unfavorable processes so that the sum of their Gibbs free-energy changes is negative. Energy released by an exergonic reaction can therefore be transferred through an appropriate coupling mechanism to support an endergonic process.

Q13. An experiment reports that an endergonic reaction proceeds after an enzyme is added. The investigator concludes that the enzyme changed the reaction's ΔG\Delta GΔG from positive to negative. Which evaluation is best?

A.Correct, because enzymes supply free energy directly
B.Correct, because every enzyme makes its reaction exergonic
C.Incorrect, because enzymes generally lower activation energy without changing the overall ΔG\Delta GΔG between initial and final states ✅
D.Incorrect, because enzymes prevent reactions from reaching equilibrium
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Enzymes accelerate reactions by lowering the activation-energy barrier. They do not normally change the overall Gibbs free-energy difference between reactants and products. Therefore, an enzyme can make an unfavorable reaction proceed faster, but it does not by itself make its ΔG\Delta GΔG negative.
