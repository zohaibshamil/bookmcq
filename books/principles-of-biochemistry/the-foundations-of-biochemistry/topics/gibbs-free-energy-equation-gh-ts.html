What is Gibbs free energy equation ΔG=ΔH-TΔS?

Definition:
The Gibbs free energy equation ΔG=ΔH−TΔS\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta SΔG=ΔH−TΔS is a fundamental thermodynamic equation that determines the spontaneity of a reaction, where ΔG\Delta GΔG is the change in free energy, ΔH\Delta HΔH is the change in enthalpy (heat content), TTT is the absolute temperature, and ΔS\Delta SΔS is the change in entropy; a negative ΔG\Delta GΔG means the reaction is exergonic (spontaneous), while a positive ΔG\Delta GΔG means it is endergonic (non-spontaneous) and requires energy input.

Working:
This equation works by balancing the enthalpy (energy stored in bonds) and entropy (disorder) contributions to determine if a reaction can proceed without external energy; at constant temperature and pressure, the sign of ΔG\Delta GΔG predicts the direction of a reaction, and in cells, endergonic reactions are coupled to exergonic ones (e.g., ATP hydrolysis with ΔG≈−30.5 kJ/mol\Delta G \approx -30.5 \text{ kJ/mol}ΔG≈−30.5 kJ/mol) to drive biosynthesis, and the equilibrium constant KeqK_{eq}Keq​ is related by ΔG∘=−RTln⁡Keq\Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K_{eq}ΔG∘=−RTlnKeq​.

Example:
A simple example is the conversion of glucose to glucose-6-phosphate, which has ΔG∘=+13.8 kJ/mol\Delta G^\circ = +13.8 \text{ kJ/mol}ΔG∘=+13.8 kJ/mol (endergonic), but is coupled to ATP hydrolysis (ΔG∘=−30.5 kJ/mol\Delta G^\circ = -30.5 \text{ kJ/mol}ΔG∘=−30.5 kJ/mol), making the overall reaction exergonic and feasible, illustrating how cells use coupled reactions to drive thermodynamically unfavorable processes.

Reason:
The Gibbs free energy equation is central to biochemistry because it allows prediction of reaction feasibility, energy coupling in metabolism, and the direction of biochemical pathways, and it is essential for understanding bioenergetics, enzyme function, and drug action, making it a cornerstone of biochemical thermodynamics.

Q1. For a process at constant temperature and pressure, which expression correctly predicts whether the process is thermodynamically favorable from the signs and magnitudes of ΔH\Delta HΔH and ΔS\Delta SΔS?

A.ΔG=ΔH+TΔS\Delta G=\Delta H+T\Delta SΔG=ΔH+TΔS
B.ΔG=ΔH−TΔS\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta SΔG=ΔH−TΔS ✅
C.ΔG=TΔH−ΔS\Delta G=T\Delta H-\Delta SΔG=TΔH−ΔS
D.ΔG=ΔH/T−ΔS\Delta G=\Delta H/T-\Delta SΔG=ΔH/T−ΔS
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The correct relationship is ΔG=ΔH−TΔS\Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta SΔG=ΔH−TΔS. The enthalpy change contributes directly, while the entropy term is multiplied by absolute temperature. A negative ΔG\Delta GΔG indicates thermodynamic favorability under the specified conditions.

Q2. A reaction has ΔH<0\Delta H<0ΔH<0 and ΔS>0\Delta S>0ΔS>0. Without knowing their numerical magnitudes, what can be concluded about ΔG\Delta GΔG as temperature changes?

A.It must always be positive
B.It must always equal zero
C.It must always be negative ✅
D.Its sign depends only on pressure
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: When ΔH<0\Delta H<0ΔH<0 and ΔS>0\Delta S>0ΔS>0, both terms make ΔG\Delta GΔG more negative because ΔH\Delta HΔH is negative and −TΔS-T\Delta S−TΔS is also negative. Therefore, ΔG\Delta GΔG is negative at every positive absolute temperature.

Q3. Two processes have the following properties: Process X has ΔH=−10\Delta H=-10ΔH=−10 kJ/mol and ΔS=−20\Delta S=-20ΔS=−20 J/(mol·K), while Process Y has ΔH=+5\Delta H=+5ΔH=+5 kJ/mol and ΔS=+40\Delta S=+40ΔS=+40 J/(mol·K). At 300 K, which process has the more favorable ΔG\Delta GΔG?

A.Process X, because its enthalpy is negative
B.Process Y, because its entropy contribution is sufficiently favorable ✅
C.Both have the same ΔG\Delta GΔG
D.Neither can have a negative ΔG\Delta GΔG
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For X, ΔG=−10−(300)(−0.020)=−4\Delta G=-10-(300)(-0.020)=-4ΔG=−10−(300)(−0.020)=−4 kJ/mol. For Y, ΔG=5−(300)(0.040)=−7\Delta G=5-(300)(0.040)=-7ΔG=5−(300)(0.040)=−7 kJ/mol. Thus Y is more favorable despite having positive enthalpy because its favorable entropy contribution is larger.

Q4. A student argues that any process with positive ΔH\Delta HΔH cannot occur spontaneously. A measured process has ΔH=+12\Delta H=+12ΔH=+12 kJ/mol and ΔS=+60\Delta S=+60ΔS=+60 J/(mol·K). At 300 K, how should the student's reasoning be evaluated?

A.Correct, because positive enthalpy always prevents spontaneity

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The student's reasoning ignores the entropy term. At 300 K, ΔG=12−(300)(0.060)=12−18=−6\Delta G=12-(300)(0.060)=12-18=-6ΔG=12−(300)(0.060)=12−18=−6 kJ/mol. Therefore, despite being endothermic, the process has a negative free-energy change under these conditions.

Q5. A researcher observes that a molecular assembly becomes more ordered while releasing heat. Which combination most strongly explains why the assembly can still have a negative ΔG\Delta GΔG?

A.Positive ΔH\Delta HΔH and negative ΔS\Delta SΔS
B.Negative ΔH\Delta HΔH and negative ΔS\Delta SΔS ✅
C.Positive ΔH\Delta HΔH and positive ΔS\Delta SΔS

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Greater molecular order commonly corresponds to a negative entropy change, which opposes favorable free energy. However, if the process releases enough heat so that the negative ΔH\Delta HΔH outweighs the positive −TΔS-T\Delta S−TΔS contribution, the overall ΔG\Delta GΔG can still be negative.

Q10. Two researchers evaluate the same reaction. Researcher A reports ΔH=−25\Delta H=-25ΔH=−25 kJ/mol and ΔS=−50\Delta S=-50ΔS=−50 J/(mol·K), while Researcher B reports ΔG=−10\Delta G=-10ΔG=−10 kJ/mol at 300 K. Which conclusion is best supported?

A.The measurements are inconsistent because ΔG\Delta GΔG must equal ΔH\Delta HΔH

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Using the reported enthalpy and entropy, ΔG=−25−(300)(−0.050)=−25+15=−10\Delta G=-25-(300)(-0.050)=-25+15=-10ΔG=−25−(300)(−0.050)=−25+15=−10 kJ/mol. Therefore both researchers' results are internally consistent. The entropy term raises ΔG\Delta GΔG by 15 kJ/mol because the entropy change is negative.
