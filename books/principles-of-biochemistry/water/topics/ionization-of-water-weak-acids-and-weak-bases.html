What is Ionization of Water, Weak Acids and Weak Bases?

Definition:
The ionization of water is the reversible process where water molecules spontaneously dissociate into hydronium ions (\(H_3O^+\)) and hydroxide ions (\(OH^-\)), represented by \(2H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+ + OH^-\), and this equilibrium establishes the foundation for the pH scale; weak acids and weak bases are substances that only partially ionize in water, establishing an equilibrium between the undissociated molecule and its ions, with dissociation constants (\(K_a\) and \(K_b\)) that quantify their strength, making them essential for buffering and pH regulation in biological systems.

Working:
Pure water ionizes to a very small extent: at 25°C, the concentration of \(H^+\) and \(OH^-\) are both \(1.0 \times 10^{-7}\) M, giving the ion product constant \(K_w = [H^+][OH^-] = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}\); weak acids (HA) partially dissociate according to \(HA \rightleftharpoons H^+ + A^-\), with \(K_a = \frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}\), and weak bases (B) partially ionize via \(B + H_2O \rightleftharpoons BH^+ + OH^-\), with \(K_b = \frac{[BH^+][OH^-]}{[B]}\), and these constants are used to calculate pH, predict ionization states, and design buffer systems using the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation \(pH = pK_a + \log\frac{[A^-]}{[HA]}\).

Example:
A simple example is acetic acid (\(CH_3COOH\)), a weak acid with \(K_a = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}\) (\(pK_a = 4.76\)), which partially ionizes in water to yield \(H^+\) and acetate (\(CH_3COO^-\)); another example is ammonia (\(NH_3\)), a weak base with \(K_b = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}\) (\(pK_b = 4.74\)), which accepts a proton from water to form \(NH_4^+\) and \(OH^-\), illustrating the behavior of weak acids and bases in aqueous solutions.

Reason:
Understanding the ionization of water and the behavior of weak acids and bases is fundamental to biochemistry and physiology because it explains the pH of biological fluids, the function of buffer systems, the ionization state of drugs and biomolecules, and the acid-base balance essential for life, making it a cornerstone of biochemistry and medicine.

📝 All Ionization of Water, Weak Acids and Weak Bases MCQs

Q1. At \(25^\circ\mathrm{C}\), a solution has \([\mathrm{H^+}]=1.0\times10^{-5}\mathrm{M}\). Which conclusion best follows from the water-ionization relationship?

A.The solution is basic because \([\mathrm{OH^-}]\) exceeds \([\mathrm{H^+}]\)
B.The solution is acidic because \([\mathrm{H^+}]>[\mathrm{OH^-}]\) ✅
C.The solution must contain a strong acid
D.The solution has equal concentrations of \(\mathrm{H^+}\) and \(\mathrm{OH^-}\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: At \(25^\circ\mathrm{C}\), \(K_w=1.0\times10^{-14}\), so \([\mathrm{OH^-}]=10^{-9}\mathrm{M}\). Because the hydrogen-ion concentration is much greater than the hydroxide-ion concentration, the solution is acidic. This conclusion does not require knowing whether the acid is strong or weak.

Q2. Which statement most accurately distinguishes a weak acid from a strong acid when both are present at the same analytical concentration?

A.A weak acid produces no ions in water
B.A weak acid has a smaller fraction of its molecules ionized at equilibrium ✅
C.A weak acid always has a higher pH than water
D.A weak acid cannot establish an equilibrium with water
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A weak acid partially ionizes in water, meaning a significant fraction of its molecules remains un-ionized at equilibrium. A strong acid at comparable concentration undergoes much more extensive ionization. Weak does not mean incapable of ionizing.

Q3. A student says, 'Because a weak acid ionizes only partially, adding more of it cannot substantially change the hydrogen-ion concentration.' What is the best evaluation?

A.Correct, because weak acids cannot affect pH significantly
B.Correct, because equilibrium prevents additional ionization
C.Incorrect, because increasing total weak-acid concentration can increase \([\mathrm{H^+}]\) even when ionization remains incomplete ✅
D.Incorrect, because every weak acid becomes completely ionized at high concentration
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Partial ionization does not mean negligible ion production. Increasing the analytical concentration of a weak acid generally increases the equilibrium hydrogen-ion concentration, even though the percentage ionized may decrease. Thus weak acids can produce substantial pH changes.

Q4. Two weak acids have the same concentration, but acid X has a larger \(K_a\) than acid Y. Which prediction is most defensible?

A.X has a smaller equilibrium concentration of \(\mathrm{H^+}\)
B.X ionizes to a greater extent and generally gives a lower pH ✅
C.Y must be completely ionized
D.Both acids must have identical pH values because their concentrations are equal
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For weak acids at comparable concentrations, a larger \(K_a\) indicates a greater tendency to donate protons to water. Therefore acid X generally produces more \(\mathrm{H^+}\), resulting in greater ionization and a lower pH than acid Y.

Q5. A weak acid solution is diluted tenfold. Which qualitative change is most likely, assuming ideal behavior and that water remains relevant?

A.The fraction of acid molecules ionized generally increases while \([\mathrm{H^+}]\) decreases ✅
B.Both the fraction ionized and \([\mathrm{H^+}]\) must increase tenfold
C.The fraction ionized becomes zero
D.The acid becomes a strong acid
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Dilution lowers the concentration of reactants, shifting the weak-acid equilibrium toward ionized species and increasing the fraction ionized. However, the total hydrogen-ion concentration still decreases because the solution contains substantially fewer acid molecules per unit volume.

Q6. A weak base is added to water. Measurements show that \([\mathrm{OH^-}]\) rises while \([\mathrm{H^+}]\) falls. Which interpretation best explains both observations?

A.The base consumes water completely
B.The base accepts protons from water, establishing an equilibrium that produces hydroxide ions ✅
C.The base directly converts all hydrogen ions into acid molecules without equilibrium
D.Water stops ionizing in the presence of any base
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A weak base reacts reversibly with water by accepting protons, producing its conjugate acid and hydroxide ions. The increased hydroxide concentration corresponds to a reduced hydrogen-ion concentration through the water-ionization relationship.

Q7. A researcher compares two \(0.10\mathrm{M}\) weak-base solutions. Base P produces \(1.0\times10^{-3}\mathrm{M}\) hydroxide, whereas base Q produces \(1.0\times10^{-4}\mathrm{M}\) hydroxide under identical conditions. What is the strongest conclusion?

A.Q is necessarily more concentrated than P
B.P has a greater tendency to interact with water to produce hydroxide under these conditions ✅
C.P must be a strong base
D.Q cannot ionize in water
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: With identical initial concentrations and conditions, the larger equilibrium hydroxide concentration indicates that base P undergoes greater reaction with water. This supports a stronger tendency toward proton acceptance, although it does not by itself prove complete ionization.

Q8. A \(0.010\mathrm{M}\) weak acid has an equilibrium \([\mathrm{H^+}]\) of approximately \(1.0\times10^{-4}\mathrm{M}\). A student concludes that \(1\%\) of the acid molecules ionized. Is the reasoning valid?

A.Yes, because \(10^{-4}/10^{-2}=10^{-2}\) ✅
B.No, because the percentage ionized must be calculated from hydroxide concentration
C.No, because hydrogen ions are not related to acid ionization
D.Yes, but only if the acid is strong
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: If each ionized acid molecule contributes one hydrogen ion, the fraction ionized is approximately \([\mathrm{H^+}]/[\mathrm{HA}]_0=10^{-4}/10^{-2}=10^{-2}\), or \(1\%\). The approximation assumes water's contribution is negligible.

Q9. A student calculates the pH of an acidic solution from \([\mathrm{OH^-}]\) alone and obtains a value of \(9.2\). The student therefore labels the solution basic. What is the likely error?

A.The student ignored the inverse relationship between \([\mathrm{H^+}]\) and \([\mathrm{OH^-}]\) ✅
B.The student assumed weak acids cannot ionize
C.The student used \(K_a\) instead of \(K_w\)
D.The student should always assume \([\mathrm{H^+}]=[\mathrm{OH^-}]\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For aqueous solutions at \(25^\circ\mathrm{C}\), \(K_w=[\mathrm{H^+}][\mathrm{OH^-}]\). Therefore, a low hydroxide concentration corresponds to a high hydrogen-ion concentration. Determining pH requires correctly converting between the two ion concentrations.

Q10. A graph plots percentage ionization of a weak acid on the y-axis against its initial concentration on the x-axis. Which curve shape is most consistent with expected behavior as concentration increases?

A.A steadily increasing curve approaching \(100\%\)
B.A generally decreasing curve as concentration increases ✅
C.A horizontal line at exactly \(50\%\)
D.A curve that remains exactly zero at every concentration
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a weak acid, dilution generally increases the fraction of molecules that ionize, whereas increasing initial concentration tends to decrease the percentage ionized. Therefore, percentage ionization generally falls as concentration increases, although the exact curve depends on the equilibrium constant.

Q11. A graph shows two weak-acid curves of \([\mathrm{H^+}]\) versus initial acid concentration. Curve X lies above curve Y across the same concentration range. If both acids are monoprotic, what is the most reasonable interpretation?

A.X likely represents the acid with greater ionization tendency under the plotted conditions ✅
B.Y must have the larger \(K_a\)
C.X must contain fewer acid molecules at every point
D.Both curves must represent strong acids
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: At the same initial acid concentration, a higher equilibrium hydrogen-ion concentration indicates greater proton production. For comparable monoprotic weak acids, this is consistent with curve X representing the acid with a larger tendency to ionize, often reflected by a larger \(K_a\).

Q12. A solution contains a weak acid and its conjugate base. After adding a small amount of strong acid, which immediate equilibrium response best explains the system's resistance to a large pH change?

A.The added hydrogen ions react with the conjugate base, reducing their direct effect on free \(\mathrm{H^+}\) concentration ✅
B.The weak acid instantly becomes completely ionized
C.Water stops participating in ionization
D.The strong acid is converted directly into hydroxide ions
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The conjugate base can accept added protons, converting into the weak acid. This consumes much of the added hydrogen-ion disturbance and limits the change in free \(\mathrm{H^+}\) concentration. The result is a smaller pH shift than in unbuffered water.

Q13. A solution has \([\mathrm{H^+}]=10^{-3}\mathrm{M}\) at \(25^\circ\mathrm{C}\). Another solution has \([\mathrm{OH^-}]=10^{-8}\mathrm{M}\). Which comparison is correct?

A.The first solution is more acidic ✅
B.The second solution is more acidic
C.Both have the same pH
D.The second solution is neutral because hydroxide is present
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For the first solution, \([\mathrm{OH^-}]=10^{-11}\mathrm{M}\), so its pH is \(3\). For the second, \([\mathrm{H^+}]=10^{-6}\mathrm{M}\), giving pH \(6\). Therefore the first solution has the higher hydrogen-ion concentration and is more acidic.

Q14. A weak acid has \(K_a=1.0\times10^{-5}\), while a weak base has \(K_b=1.0\times10^{-5}\). Each is prepared at the same concentration in separate solutions. Which statement is safest without additional information?

A.Their pH values must be identical
B.Their ionization percentages must be identical because \(K_a=K_b\)
C.Their acid-base behavior cannot be compared directly by treating \(K_a\) and \(K_b\) as the same type of constant ✅
D.Both solutions must be neutral
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: \(K_a\) describes acid ionization while \(K_b\) describes base ionization, so equal numerical values do not automatically imply identical pH or identical ionization behavior. Meaningful comparison requires considering the relevant equilibrium and the relationship to water.
