What is Henderson Hasselbalch equation pH pKa?

Definition:
The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation is a formula used to calculate the pH of a buffer solution, given by \( pH = pK_a + \log\frac{[A^-]}{[HA]} \), where \( pK_a \) is the negative logarithm of the acid dissociation constant, \( [A^-] \) is the concentration of the conjugate base, and \( [HA] \) is the concentration of the weak acid; this equation is widely used in biochemistry to predict the ionization state of molecules and design buffer systems.

Working:
The equation is derived from the equilibrium expression for a weak acid: \( K_a = \frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]} \), rearranged to \( [H^+] = K_a \frac{[HA]}{[A^-]} \), and taking the negative logarithm gives the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation; it shows that when \( [A^-] = [HA] \), pH = pK_a, and it is used to calculate the pH of a buffer when the ratio of salt to acid is known, or to determine the ratio needed for a desired pH; this equation is critical in protein chemistry, drug formulation, and understanding the behavior of biological molecules.

Example:
A simple example is a buffer containing acetic acid (\( pK_a = 4.76 \)) and sodium acetate; if the ratio \( [A^-]/[HA] = 10 \), then \( pH = 4.76 + \log(10) = 5.76 \); another example is the bicarbonate buffer, where the pH of blood (7.4) is maintained by a ratio of \( [HCO_3^-]/[CO_2] \approx 20 \), illustrating the application of the equation in physiology.

Reason:
The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation is indispensable in biochemistry because it quantifies the relationship between pH, pK_a, and the ionization state of acids and bases, and it is used in enzyme kinetics, molecular biology, and pharmacology to understand and control pH-dependent processes.

📝 All Henderson Hasselbalch equation pH pKa MCQs

Q1. A buffer contains equal concentrations of a weak acid and its conjugate base. If the \(pK_a\) is 4.8, what pH should be expected under ideal conditions?

A.3.8
B.4.8 ✅
C.5.8
D.9.6
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When the weak acid and conjugate base have equal concentrations, their concentration ratio is 1. The logarithm of 1 is zero, so the pH equals the \(pK_a\). Therefore, the expected pH is 4.8.

Q2. A buffer has \(pK_a=6.2\) and a conjugate-base-to-acid concentration ratio of 10. Which pH is most consistent with these conditions?

A.5.2
B.6.2
C.7.2 ✅
D.8.2
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The pH increases above the \(pK_a\) when conjugate base exceeds weak acid. A ratio of 10 contributes one pH unit, so \(pH=pK_a+1=7.2\). This reflects the logarithmic dependence on concentration ratio.

Q3. Two buffers have the same \(pK_a\). Buffer X contains equal amounts of acid and base, while Buffer Y contains ten times more base than acid. Which comparison is correct?

A.Both have the same pH because their \(pK_a\) values are equal
B.Buffer X has a higher pH because it contains equal concentrations
C.Buffer Y has a higher pH because its base-to-acid ratio is larger ✅
D.Their pH values cannot be compared without knowing total concentration
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The \(pK_a\) establishes the reference pH, but the concentration ratio determines how far the pH shifts from that reference. Because Buffer Y has ten times more base relative to acid, its pH is one unit higher.

Q4. A student doubles both the weak-acid concentration and conjugate-base concentration in a buffer while keeping their ratio unchanged. What is the most reasonable prediction for the pH?

A.It doubles
B.It increases by about 0.30 units
C.It remains approximately unchanged ✅
D.It decreases because the buffer becomes more concentrated
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The pH depends on the ratio of conjugate base to weak acid rather than their absolute concentrations. Doubling both concentrations leaves that ratio unchanged, so the pH remains approximately the same, although buffer capacity increases.

Q5. A buffer has \(pK_a=7.4\). Its pH is measured as 6.4. Which change would most directly produce this pH if the acid and base forms are otherwise ideal?

A.Make the base concentration ten times the acid concentration
B.Make the acid concentration ten times the base concentration ✅
C.Make acid and base concentrations equal
D.Remove all weak acid while keeping the base
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A pH one unit below \(pK_a\) corresponds to a base-to-acid ratio of one-tenth. Equivalently, the acid concentration must be ten times the conjugate-base concentration, shifting the logarithmic term by \(-1\).

Q6. Buffer A has \(pK_a=5.0\) and \([base]/[acid]=0.1\). Buffer B has \(pK_a=6.0\) and \([base]/[acid]=0.01\). Which buffer has the higher pH?

A.Buffer A, because its ratio is larger ✅
B.Buffer B, because its \(pK_a\) is larger
C.Both have pH 4.0
D.Both have the same pH of 5.0
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For Buffer A, the ratio contributes \(-1\), giving pH 4.0. For Buffer B, the ratio contributes \(-2\), giving pH 4.0 as well. Therefore, neither has a higher pH; both are predicted to have the same pH of 4.0.

Q7. A laboratory buffer initially has \(pH=7.0\) and \(pK_a=6.0\). After preparation, analysis shows the base concentration is approximately ten times the acid concentration. A student claims the measured pH is impossible because it exceeds the \(pK_a\). What is the best evaluation?

A.The student is correct because pH must always equal \(pK_a\)
B.The student is correct because buffers cannot have pH above \(pK_a\)
C.The student is incorrect because a tenfold base-to-acid ratio gives a pH about one unit above \(pK_a\) ✅
D.The student is incorrect because pH depends only on total buffer concentration
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A buffer can have a pH above its \(pK_a\) when the conjugate base predominates. A tenfold base-to-acid ratio contributes approximately one positive pH unit, making a pH near 7.0 entirely reasonable.

Q8. A researcher calculates a buffer pH using \(pH=pK_a+\log([acid]/[base])\). The correct concentration ratio is \([base]/[acid]\). Which consequence follows from this error?

A.The predicted pH will always equal the correct pH
B.The logarithmic term has the wrong sign, reversing whether excess base raises or lowers pH ✅
C.The \(pK_a\) is automatically changed
D.Only total buffer concentration is affected
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The concentration ratio must compare conjugate base with weak acid in that order. Reversing the ratio changes the sign of the logarithm, causing an excess of base to be incorrectly predicted as lowering pH instead of raising it.

Q9. A buffer's pH is plotted against \(\log([base]/[acid])\). The graph is approximately a straight line passing through pH 6.3 when the horizontal-axis value is zero. What does the intercept most directly indicate?

A.The total buffer concentration
B.The \(pK_a\) of the weak acid ✅
C.The concentration of hydroxide ions
D.The buffer capacity
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When the horizontal value is zero, the base-to-acid ratio is 1 because \(\log(1)=0\). At that point, pH equals \(pK_a\). Therefore, the graph's intercept at 6.3 identifies the approximate \(pK_a\).

Q10. A graph shows pH values of 5.0, 6.0, and 7.0 when \(\log([base]/[acid])\) is \(-1,0,\) and \(1\), respectively. A new sample has a horizontal-axis value of 2. What pH should the linear relationship predict?

A.5
B.6
C.7
D.8 ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The graph increases by one pH unit for every one-unit increase in the logarithmic concentration ratio. Extending that linear pattern from \(x=0\), where pH is 6.0, to \(x=2\) gives a predicted pH of 8.0.

Q11. A biochemist needs a buffer near pH 7.2 and has two weak-acid systems: System A with \(pK_a=5.0\), and System B with \(pK_a=7.1\). Which is generally the better starting choice if the goal is to minimize the required concentration imbalance between acid and base?

A.System A, because the smaller \(pK_a\) gives stronger buffering at every pH
B.System A, because a large difference between pH and \(pK_a\) is preferred
C.System B, because its \(pK_a\) is close to the desired pH ✅
D.Either system, because \(pK_a\) does not affect buffer composition
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A buffer is most naturally balanced when the desired pH is close to its \(pK_a\). System B differs by only 0.1 pH unit, requiring a ratio close to 1, whereas System A would require a much larger base-to-acid imbalance.

Q12. A technician prepares two buffers at the same pH using the same weak-acid system. Buffer P has total concentration 0.02 M, while Buffer Q has total concentration 0.20 M. Which statement best distinguishes them?

A.Their pH must differ by exactly one unit
B.Their pH can be similar because their acid-to-base ratios are the same, while Q generally has greater resistance to added acid or base ✅
C.P must have a higher pH because it is more dilute
D.Q must have a lower pH because concentrated buffers suppress ionization completely
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For the same weak-acid system, equal acid-to-base ratios produce approximately the same pH regardless of total concentration. Increasing total concentration generally increases the amount of added acid or base the buffer can absorb before its pH changes substantially.

Q13. A buffer has \(pK_a=8.0\). Initially, \([base]/[acid]=1\). A small addition of acid converts some base into acid, making the ratio approximately \(0.1\). What is the approximate pH change?

A.Increase by 1 unit
B.Decrease by 1 unit ✅
C.Increase by 0.1 unit
D.No change because \(pK_a\) is fixed
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Initially, equal concentrations give pH approximately equal to 8.0. After the addition, the base-to-acid ratio becomes 0.1, contributing \(-1\) to the logarithmic term. The pH therefore decreases by about one unit.

Q14. A student argues that increasing the total concentration of a buffer from 0.01 M to 1.0 M must increase its pH by 100-fold because pH depends on concentration. Which response best identifies the flaw?

A.pH depends only on \(pK_a\), never concentration
B.The relevant concentration effect is the logarithm of the base-to-acid ratio, not the absolute total concentration ✅
C.Increasing concentration always decreases pH
D.pH changes linearly with total buffer concentration
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The key variable is the ratio of conjugate base to weak acid. Multiplying both components by the same factor does not change that ratio, so it does not directly change pH even though it substantially increases total buffer concentration.

Q15. Two buffers use weak acids with \(pK_a\) values of 4.0 and 9.0. Their base-to-acid ratios are \(10^5\) and \(10^{-5}\), respectively. What surprising conclusion follows under the ideal relationship?

A.The first buffer has pH 9.0 and the second has pH 4.0 ✅
B.Both buffers have pH 9.0
C.Both buffers have pH 4.0
D.Both buffers must have pH 7.0 because their ratios are opposite
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For the first system, a ratio of \(10^5\) adds five pH units to \(pK_a=4.0\), giving pH 9.0. For the second, \(10^{-5}\) subtracts five units from \(pK_a=9.0\), also giving pH 4.0. Thus they do not share the same pH; the first is 9.0 and the second is 4.0.
