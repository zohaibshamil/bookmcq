What is Titration curves and pKa?

Definition:
A titration curve is a graphical representation of pH changes as a titrant (acid or base) is added to a solution, showing the equivalence point and the buffering region, and the pKapK_apKa​ of a weak acid is the pH at the midpoint of the buffer region, where [A−]=[HA][A^-] = [HA][A−]=[HA], and it is the pH at which the acid is half-dissociated; titration curves are used to determine pKapK_apKa​ values and understand acid-base properties of biomolecules.

Working:
In a titration, the pH is plotted against the volume of titrant, and the curve has a characteristic S-shape; at the midpoint, the concentrations of the acid and its conjugate base are equal ([HA]=[A−][HA] = [A^-][HA]=[A−]), and the Henderson-Hasselbalch equation gives pH=pKapH = pK_apH=pKa​; the buffering region is where the pH changes slowly, and this is crucial for biological systems; the equivalence point is where the moles of base equal the moles of acid; these curves are used to analyze amino acids, proteins, and other biological buffers, and the pKapK_apKa​ values are determined experimentally.

Example:
A simple example is the titration of acetic acid (CH₃COOH) with sodium hydroxide, where the pKapK_apKa​ is 4.76, and at the midpoint, pH = 4.76; another example is the titration of histidine, which has three pKapK_apKa​ values (1.82, 6.0, and 9.17), corresponding to its ionizable groups, illustrating how titration curves can characterize biological molecules.

Reason:
Titration curves and pKapK_apKa​ are essential for biochemistry, as they reveal the acid-base properties of molecules, guide the design of buffer systems, and are used in drug development and metabolic studies, making them a key concept in biochemistry.

📝 All Titration curves and pKa MCQs

Q1. During a titration of a weak monoprotic acid with a strong base, the pH at the half-equivalence point is measured as 4.76. Which conclusion is most justified?

A.The acid concentration is 4.76 M
B.The acid has pKa=4.76pK_a=4.76pKa​=4.76 under the experimental conditions ✅
C.The equivalence-point pH must be exactly 7.00
D.The acid is completely dissociated before titration
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: At the half-equivalence point, equal amounts of the weak acid and its conjugate base are present. Under these conditions, the Henderson–Hasselbalch relationship reduces to pH=pKapH=pK_apH=pKa​, so the measured pH directly estimates pKapK_apKa​.

Q2. A student wants to estimate the pKapK_apKa​ of a weak acid from a titration curve. Which region of the curve provides the most direct estimate?

A.The initial pH before titrant is added
B.The equivalence point
C.The half-equivalence point ✅
D.The region far beyond equivalence
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The half-equivalence point is especially useful because the concentrations of the weak acid and conjugate base are approximately equal there. Consequently, the measured pH equals the acid's pKapK_apKa​, making this region diagnostically valuable.

Q3. Two weak acids are titrated under identical conditions. Acid X has pKa=3.2pK_a=3.2pKa​=3.2, while Acid Y has pKa=5.8pK_a=5.8pKa​=5.8. Which prediction best explains how their titration curves differ before equivalence?

A.Acid X should generally have a higher pH because its pKapK_apKa​ is smaller
B.Acid Y should generally have a higher pH because it is the weaker acid ✅
C.Both curves must have identical pH values until equivalence
D.Acid X cannot produce a buffer region
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A larger pKapK_apKa​ corresponds to a weaker acid and generally means less dissociation at comparable concentrations. Therefore, Acid Y tends to have a higher pH before equivalence than Acid X under otherwise comparable conditions.

Q4. A weak acid is titrated with strong base. At one point, the solution contains substantial amounts of both HAHAHA and A−A^-A−. Why does the pH change relatively slowly when additional base is added in this region?

A.The strong base becomes chemically inactive
B.The solution behaves as a buffer because HAHAHA and A−A^-A− can consume added base or acid ✅
C.Water stops ionizing in this region
D.The weak acid becomes completely insoluble
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A mixture containing appreciable HAHAHA and A−A^-A− resists pH changes because added hydroxide can react with HAHAHA, while added acid can react with A−A^-A−. This buffering produces the flatter portion of the titration curve.

Q5. A titration curve has a broad, relatively flat region centered around pH 6.4. The equivalence point occurs later at a much higher pH. What is the strongest interpretation of the flat region?

A.It is likely near the buffer region where pHpHpH approaches pKapK_apKa​ ✅
B.It proves the acid is strong
C.It identifies the equivalence point exactly
D.It proves no conjugate base is present
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The broad flat region occurs while both weak acid and conjugate base are present in meaningful concentrations. Its midpoint corresponds approximately to equal concentrations, where pHpHpH is close to pKapK_apKa​.

Q6. A 25.0 mL sample of weak acid is titrated with 0.100 M NaOH. The equivalence point occurs after 20.0 mL of NaOH has been added. At 10.0 mL, the measured pH is 4.20. What is the best estimate of the acid's pKapK_apKa​?

A.2.1
B.4.2 ✅
C.8.4
D.12.6
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The equivalence point requires 20.0 mL of base, so 10.0 mL represents half-equivalence. At that point, the weak acid and conjugate base have equal concentrations, giving pH=pKapH=pK_apH=pKa​. Therefore, pKapK_apKa​ is approximately 4.20.

Q7. A researcher obtains two titration curves for the same weak acid but uses different initial acid concentrations. The half-equivalence pH values are 4.75 and 4.78. What does this similarity most strongly suggest?

A.The acid's pKapK_apKa​ is reasonably consistent despite the concentration change ✅
B.The acid concentration must have been identical
C.The equivalence points must occur at the same volume
D.The strong base concentration was necessarily identical
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The pKapK_apKa​ is an intrinsic equilibrium property under fixed conditions, whereas concentrations primarily affect titration volumes and curve dimensions. Similar half-equivalence pH values therefore support a consistent pKapK_apKa​ estimate.

Q8. A student claims, 'The pH at the equivalence point is the pKapK_apKa​ because that is where the acid has been neutralized.' Which correction is most accurate?

A.The pKapK_apKa​ is determined most directly at half-equivalence, not necessarily at equivalence ✅
B.The pKapK_apKa​ is always equal to the initial pH
C.The pKapK_apKa​ is always 7.00 for weak acids
D.The pKapK_apKa​ can only be determined after adding excess acid
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: At equivalence, most of the original weak acid has been converted into its conjugate base, so the pH is influenced strongly by conjugate-base hydrolysis. The half-equivalence point instead gives pH=pKapH=pK_apH=pKa​.

Q9. A student reads a graph and identifies the steepest point of the titration curve as the pKapK_apKa​. The steepest point occurs near equivalence, while the midpoint of the buffer region occurs earlier. What is the student's main error?

A.Confusing the equivalence point with the half-equivalence point ✅
B.Assuming strong bases contain hydroxide ions
C.Ignoring the initial acid concentration
D.Using volume rather than pH on the graph
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The steepest portion of a weak-acid/strong-base titration occurs near the equivalence region, whereas pKapK_apKa​ is estimated from the pH at half-equivalence. These are different locations with different chemical meanings.

Q10. An experimental titration gives a half-equivalence pH of 5.10. However, the student's graph shows the half-equivalence volume incorrectly placed at 60% of the equivalence volume. Which result should be trusted for estimating pKapK_apKa​?

A.The pH at the incorrectly selected 60% volume
B.The pH at the true half-equivalence volume ✅
C.The initial pH only
D.The equivalence-point volume without considering pH
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The pKapK_apKa​ estimate depends on identifying the point where exactly half of the original weak acid has been neutralized. That corresponds to half the equivalence volume, so an incorrectly positioned point can produce a misleading estimate.

Q11. Consider a titration curve where the pH values at added-base volumes of 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25 mL are approximately 4.0, 4.4, 4.8, 5.2, and 6.0. The equivalence volume is 30 mL. Which estimate of pKapK_apKa​ is most appropriate?

A.4
B.4.4
C.4.8 ✅
D.6
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The equivalence volume is 30 mL, so half-equivalence occurs at 15 mL. The graph gives a pH of approximately 4.8 at that volume. Since pH=pKapH=pK_apH=pKa​ at half-equivalence, the best estimate is 4.8.

📖 Explanation: At half-equivalence, the pH approximates pKapK_apKa​. Thus Curve A corresponds to pKa≈3.5pK_a\approx3.5pKa​≈3.5, while Curve B corresponds to pKa≈6.0pK_a\approx6.0pKa​≈6.0. The larger pKapK_apKa​ indicates the weaker acid.

Q13. A weak acid has pKa=4.0pK_a=4.0pKa​=4.0. During titration, a student chooses a point where the acid-to-conjugate-base ratio is approximately 10:1 and concludes that the pH must be 4.0. Which evaluation is best?

A.Correct, because any buffer composition gives pH=pKapH=pK_apH=pKa​
B.Incorrect, because pHpHpH depends on the ratio of conjugate base to weak acid ✅
C.Correct, because the equivalence point has not been reached
D.Incorrect, because weak acids cannot form buffers
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The equality pH=pKapH=pK_apH=pKa​ occurs specifically when HAHAHA and A−A^-A− concentrations are equal. A 10:1 ratio is not equal, so the pH must differ from pKapK_apKa​ according to the logarithmic concentration ratio.

Q14. A chemist compares two weak-acid titration methods. Method 1 estimates pKapK_apKa​ from the half-equivalence pH. Method 2 estimates it from the initial pH alone. Which method is generally more reliable for this purpose, and why?

A.Method 2, because initial pH directly equals pKapK_apKa​
B.Method 1, because the half-equivalence condition isolates the pKapK_apKa​ relationship more directly ✅
C.Both are equally reliable under all concentrations
D.Method 2, because weak acids are completely ionized initially
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Initial pH depends on both acid dissociation and the starting concentration, so it does not generally equal pKapK_apKa​. At half-equivalence, equal HAHAHA and A−A^-A− concentrations make the pHpHpH-to-pKapK_apKa​ relationship much more direct.

Q15. A weak acid has a pKapK_apKa​ near 5.0. A titration experiment produces a smooth curve with a buffer region centered near pH 5.0, but the measured equivalence-point pH is also near 5.0. Which interpretation is most defensible?

A.The equivalence point must always equal pKapK_apKa​
B.The similar values may be coincidental; pKapK_apKa​ should still be obtained from half-equivalence ✅
C.The acid must actually be strong
D.The experiment proves that no conjugate base forms
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The equivalence-point pH of a weak-acid titration is affected by the conjugate base and generally does not directly equal pKapK_apKa​. The half-equivalence pH remains the appropriate diagnostic value, so similar numerical values alone do not establish equality.

Q16. An unknown monoprotic acid is titrated with a strong base. The equivalence point is at 40.0 mL, and the pH at 20.0 mL is 5.30. 📖 Explanation: Changing the starting concentration changes the amount of titrant required and can alter some curve features, but the pKapK_apKa​ is an equilibrium property. At half-equivalence, HAHAHA and A−A^-A− remain equal, so the pH should remain near 5.30 under unchanged conditions.
