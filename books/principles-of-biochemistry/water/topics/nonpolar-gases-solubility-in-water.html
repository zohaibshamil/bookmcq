What is Nonpolar gases solubility in water?

Definition:
Nonpolar gases like oxygen (O2O_2O2​), carbon dioxide (CO2CO_2CO2​), and nitrogen (N2N_2N2​) have low solubility in water because they lack polarity and cannot form hydrogen bonds or strong electrostatic interactions with water molecules, but their solubility is sufficient for biological needs, and they are essential for respiration and photosynthesis; the solubility is influenced by temperature, pressure, and the presence of salts, as described by Henry's law.

Working:
Nonpolar gases dissolve in water by occupying spaces between water molecules, without disrupting the hydrogen-bond network significantly, and their solubility follows Henry's law: C=kH×PC = k_H \times PC=kH​×P, where CCC is concentration, kHk_HkH​ is the Henry's law constant, and PPP is the partial pressure; oxygen solubility is low (about 0.25 mM at 25°C), but it is sufficient for aquatic organisms, and CO₂ is more soluble due to its reaction with water to form carbonic acid, which is important for pH regulation and transport of respiratory gases in blood.

Example:
A simple example is the oxygen dissolved in water that fish use for respiration, which is sufficient despite low solubility; another example is the carbon dioxide in soda, where high pressure increases CO₂ solubility, and when pressure is released, CO₂ bubbles out, illustrating Henry's law in action.

Reason:
Understanding nonpolar gas solubility is crucial for respiratory physiology, aquatic biology, and environmental science, as it explains gas exchange in lungs, gills, and ecosystems, and is key to understanding climate change effects on ocean chemistry.

📝 All Nonpolar gases solubility in water MCQs

Q1. A student compares the behavior of a nonpolar gas with a polar gas in water. Which molecular-level explanation best predicts that the nonpolar gas will have lower solubility?

A.Water molecules strongly attract and stabilize the nonpolar gas through hydrogen bonding
B.Water must disrupt favorable interactions among itself without gaining sufficiently strong interactions with the nonpolar gas ✅
C.Nonpolar gas molecules always have larger molecular masses than polar molecules
D.Nonpolar gases cannot physically enter spaces between water molecules
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Water is highly cohesive because its molecules form strong hydrogen-bonding interactions. A nonpolar gas cannot establish comparably favorable interactions with water, so transferring its molecules into water provides relatively little compensating stabilization.

Q2. Why does increasing the pressure of a nonpolar gas above water generally increase the amount of that gas dissolved in the water, even though its molecular interactions with water remain weak?

A.Higher pressure makes the gas molecules chemically polar
B.Higher pressure converts water into a nonpolar solvent
C.Higher pressure increases the tendency of gas molecules to enter the liquid phase ✅
D.Higher pressure causes permanent hydrogen bonds to form between gas and water
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Increasing gas pressure raises the number of gas molecules contacting the water surface and increases their tendency to enter the liquid phase. The underlying weak interaction with water remains, but greater pressure increases dissolved concentration.

Q3. A researcher wants to remove a dissolved nonpolar gas from water efficiently. Which change would most directly favor movement of the gas from the water into the surrounding atmosphere?

A.Increase the gas pressure above the solution
B.Decrease the temperature while maintaining all other conditions
C.Reduce the partial pressure of that gas above the water ✅
D.Add a small amount of another nonpolar gas
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Lowering the gas partial pressure above the solution reduces the driving force for gas molecules to remain dissolved. Molecules can then escape more readily until the system approaches a new equilibrium.

Q4. Two sealed containers contain equal volumes of water. Container X has a high partial pressure of a nonpolar gas, while container Y has a low partial pressure of the same gas. Which prediction is most reasonable after equilibrium is established?

A.Container X contains more dissolved gas because more gas molecules are available to dissolve ✅
B.Container Y contains more dissolved gas because lower pressure weakens water cohesion
C.Both contain identical dissolved concentrations because the gas is nonpolar
D.Neither contains measurable dissolved gas because nonpolar gases cannot dissolve at all
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Nonpolar gases are poorly soluble rather than completely insoluble. At equilibrium, a higher partial pressure generally produces a greater dissolved concentration because more gas molecules are driven into the aqueous phase.

Q5. A student claims, 'Because a nonpolar gas cannot hydrogen-bond with water not even one molecule of that gas can dissolve.' What is the main error in this reasoning?

A.Hydrogen bonding is unnecessary for any substance to dissolve
B.Poor solubility means the equilibrium strongly favors the gas phase, not that dissolution is impossible ✅
C.Nonpolar gases become ionic immediately after entering water
D.Water loses all polarity when exposed to gases
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Dissolution does not require strong hydrogen bonding in every case. Nonpolar gases can enter water, but the absence of strong favorable interactions makes the aqueous state comparatively unfavorable, resulting in low solubility.

Q6. A sealed bottle contains water and a nonpolar gas. The bottle is shaken vigorously for several minutes. Which conclusion is most defensible?

A.Shaking permanently increases the intrinsic solubility of the gas
B.Shaking can speed equilibration by increasing gas-liquid contact, but it does not necessarily change the equilibrium solubility at fixed conditions ✅
C.Shaking makes the gas chemically polar
D.Shaking eliminates the need for equilibrium
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Vigorous shaking increases contact between the gas and liquid and can therefore accelerate the approach to equilibrium. However, once equilibrium is reached, agitation alone does not necessarily change the equilibrium solubility.

Q7. A water sample is exposed separately to two gases under identical temperature and pressure conditions. Gas A is nonpolar and Gas B interacts more favorably with water. Which observation would provide the strongest evidence supporting the predicted difference?

A.Gas A dissolves more extensively because nonpolar molecules disrupt fewer water interactions
B.Gas B reaches a higher equilibrium concentration in water than Gas A ✅
C.Both gases must have identical solubilities because their pressures are equal
D.Gas A becomes an ion while Gas B remains molecular
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: If temperature and pressure are controlled, differences in water-gas interactions can produce different equilibrium concentrations. A gas capable of more favorable interactions with water is expected to dissolve more extensively.

Q8. A model predicts dissolved concentration of a nonpolar gas as pressure increases. Which graph shape would best represent a simple approximately proportional relationship over a moderate pressure range?

A.A line increasing upward from the origin ✅
B.A horizontal line at zero concentration
C.A line decreasing as pressure increases
D.A vertical line showing pressure never changes
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For a dilute gas under conditions where proportional behavior is a useful approximation, dissolved concentration increases with gas pressure. An upward-sloping relationship therefore captures the expected qualitative dependence.

Q9. A graph shows dissolved concentration of a nonpolar gas rising as gas pressure increases. A student concludes that the gas has become more chemically compatible with water at higher pressure. Why is this conclusion flawed?

A.Pressure can affect equilibrium concentration without changing the fundamental polarity or chemical identity of the gas ✅
B.Higher pressure always destroys water molecules
C.Solubility cannot depend on pressure under any circumstances
D.The gas must have undergone ionization before dissolving
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Pressure changes the physical equilibrium between gas and liquid phases. An increase in dissolved concentration does not imply that molecular polarity or chemical identity has changed; it reflects altered phase equilibrium.

Q10. A laboratory team compares two methods for increasing the dissolved amount of a nonpolar gas in water. Method 1 increases gas pressure, while Method 2 merely stirs the water briefly at constant pressure. Which method should generally produce the larger change in equilibrium dissolved concentration?

A.Method 1, because pressure changes the equilibrium condition ✅
B.Method 2, because stirring creates permanent gas-water bonds
C.Both methods must produce exactly the same equilibrium concentration
D.Method 2, because stirring makes the gas polar
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Increasing pressure changes the equilibrium condition and generally raises the dissolved concentration of a gas. Stirring mainly improves the rate of mass transfer, so its effect on final equilibrium concentration is much smaller under fixed conditions.

Q11. A student argues that a nonpolar gas should dissolve readily because both the gas and water are composed of very small molecules. Which principle best identifies the weakness in this argument?

A.Molecular size alone determines solubility
B.Solubility depends strongly on the relative favorability of interactions and the energetic cost of entering the solvent ✅
C.Small molecules are always insoluble in liquids
D.Water can dissolve only molecules containing carbon
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Molecular size is not sufficient to predict solubility. The interactions formed and disrupted during dissolution are crucial. A nonpolar gas lacks strong favorable interactions with polar water, making dissolution relatively unfavorable.

Q12. A researcher notices that a carbonated water sample loses gas rapidly after opening the container. Which sequence best explains the observation?

A.Opening raises gas pressure, causing more gas to dissolve, followed by rapid evaporation
B.Opening lowers the gas pressure above the liquid, shifting the equilibrium toward escape of dissolved gas ✅
C.Opening makes water nonpolar, immediately forcing all gas into solution
D.Opening creates hydrogen bonds between the gas and water, causing precipitation
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Opening the container sharply lowers the partial pressure of the dissolved gas above the water. The equilibrium then favors movement of gas molecules out of the liquid, producing bubbles and degassing.

Q13. A graph compares two gases and shows that Gas P has a steeper increase in dissolved concentration with pressure than Gas Q. If both gases are nonpolar and measured at the same temperature, what can reasonably be inferred?

A.Gas P has greater effective solubility under the tested conditions ✅
B.Gas Q must be polar
C.Gas P must have a larger molecular mass
D.The graph proves Gas P forms covalent bonds with water
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A steeper concentration-pressure relationship indicates that more of Gas P enters the aqueous phase for a given pressure increase. This supports greater effective solubility under those experimental conditions, without proving new chemical bonding.

Q14. A student wants to maximize the amount of a nonpolar gas dissolved in water for an experiment. Which combination is most logically favorable, assuming the gas remains chemically unchanged?

A.Low gas pressure, vigorous stirring, and immediate exposure to air
B.High gas pressure, a closed system, and sufficient time to reach equilibrium ✅
C.Low gas pressure, an open container, and rapid warming
D.High gas pressure followed by opening the container immediately
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: High gas pressure increases the equilibrium amount dissolved, while a closed system prevents rapid loss of the gas. Allowing sufficient time ensures the solution approaches equilibrium rather than merely increasing the dissolution rate.

Q15. Two students make different claims. Student 1 says, 'Nonpolar gases are insoluble because water is polar.' Student 2 says 'Nonpolar gases are usually poorly soluble because interactions with water are relatively unfavorable.' Which evaluation is most accurate?

A.Only Student 1 is correct because polarity makes dissolution impossible
B.Only Student 2 is correct because poor solubility does not mean absolute insolubility ✅
C.Both are equally correct in every circumstance
D.Neither is correct because all gases are highly soluble in water
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Student 2 gives the more scientifically precise explanation. Polarity mismatch contributes to weak solubility, but nonpolar gases can still dissolve to limited extents. Describing them as completely insoluble is an overgeneralization.

Q16. Consider a hypothetical nonpolar gas whose interaction with water is unchanged while the gas pressure is increased from PPP to 2P2P2P. Under an approximately proportional dilute-solution model, what qualitative change should occur in its dissolved concentration?

A.It should approximately double ✅
B.It should become exactly zero
C.It should necessarily quadruple
D.It should remain unchanged because the gas is nonpolar
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Under a simple proportional model, dissolved concentration increases approximately in proportion to gas pressure. Therefore, increasing pressure from PPP to 2P2P2P predicts an approximately doubled concentration, while recognizing that real systems can deviate.

Q17. A hypothetical nonpolar gas has an unusually strong attractive interaction with water while remaining nonpolar overall. Compared with an otherwise similar nonpolar gas having weaker interactions with water, what outcome is most plausible?

A.The strongly interacting gas could show substantially greater water solubility despite remaining nonpolar ✅
B.It must become an ion before dissolving
C.Its solubility must be lower because stronger interactions always prevent dissolution
D.Its nonpolar character guarantees identical solubility to every other nonpolar gas
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Polarity provides useful guidance but does not completely determine solubility. If a nominally nonpolar gas has stronger favorable interactions with water, the energetic balance can become more favorable, increasing its dissolved concentration.
