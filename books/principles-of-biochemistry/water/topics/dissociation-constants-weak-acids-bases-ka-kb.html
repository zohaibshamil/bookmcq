What is Dissociation constants weak acids bases Ka Kb?

Definition:
The dissociation constants \( K_a \) and \( K_b \) are equilibrium constants that measure the strength of weak acids and weak bases, respectively, where \( K_a \) is the acid dissociation constant (\( HA \rightleftharpoons H^+ + A^- \)), and \( K_b \) is the base dissociation constant (\( B + H_2O \rightleftharpoons BH^+ + OH^- \)); larger \( K_a \) indicates a stronger acid (more dissociation), and larger \( K_b \) indicates a stronger base; these constants are essential for predicting the ionization behavior of biomolecules in biological systems.

Working:
For a weak acid HA, \( K_a = \frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]} \), and for a weak base B, \( K_b = \frac{[BH^+][OH^-]}{[B]} \), and they are related by \( K_w = K_a \times K_b = 1.0 \times 10^{-14} \) at 25°C; the \( pK_a \) is defined as \( pK_a = -\log K_a \), and the \( pK_b = -\log K_b \); weaker acids have larger \( pK_a \) (e.g., acetic acid \( pK_a = 4.76 \)), and these values determine the ionization state at a given pH, which is critical for protein structure, enzyme catalysis, and drug action, as many molecules are only active in a specific ionization state.

Example:
A simple example is acetic acid (CH₃COOH), with \( K_a = 1.8 \times 10^{-5} \) (\( pK_a = 4.76 \)), meaning it is a weak acid that partially dissociates; another example is ammonia (NH₃), with \( K_b = 1.8 \times 10^{-5} \) (\( pK_b = 4.74 \)), illustrating how \( K_a \) and \( K_b \) quantify the strength of weak acids and bases.

Reason:
Understanding \( K_a \) and \( K_b \) is essential for biochemistry, as they determine the ionization state of functional groups in amino acids, nucleotides, and drugs, affecting their solubility, reactivity, and biological activity, and they are crucial for understanding buffer systems and acid-base balance.

Q1. A weak acid \(HA\) establishes the equilibrium \(HA \rightleftharpoons H^+ + A^-\). Which quantity most directly characterizes the acid's intrinsic tendency to dissociate under specified conditions?

A.The initial concentration of \(HA\) only
B.The acid dissociation constant \(K_a\) ✅
C.The final concentration of \(H^+\) only
D.The volume of the solution only
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The acid dissociation constant \(K_a\) describes the equilibrium tendency of a weak acid to donate protons. Unlike initial concentration, it is characteristic of the acid under a specified temperature and does not simply represent how much acid was initially added.

Q2. For a weak base \(B\), the equilibrium is \(B + H_2O \rightleftharpoons BH^+ + OH^-\). If two weak bases are measured under identical conditions and base A has the larger \(K_b\), what can be concluded?

A.Base A has a greater equilibrium tendency to form \(BH^+\) and \(OH^-\) ✅
B.Base A must have the lower initial concentration
C.Base A necessarily has the higher molar mass
D.Base A produces no conjugate acid
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A larger \(K_b\) means the equilibrium favors ionization of the weak base more strongly under comparable conditions. Therefore, base A has a greater tendency to react with water to produce \(BH^+\) and \(OH^-\), regardless of its initial concentration.

Q3. Two solutions contain the same weak acid. Solution X is \(0.10\,M\), while solution Y is \(0.010\,M\). A student concludes that X must have a \(K_a\) ten times larger because its acid concentration is ten times greater. What is the best evaluation?

A.Correct, because \(K_a\) increases directly with concentration
B.Correct, because more molecules automatically mean a stronger acid
C.Incorrect, because \(K_a\) is an equilibrium constant characteristic of the acid at a given temperature ✅
D.Incorrect, because weak acids have no measurable equilibrium constant
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The student's reasoning confuses acid concentration with acid strength. For the same weak acid at the same temperature, \(K_a\) remains characteristic of that acid, although the equilibrium concentrations of all species change when the starting concentration changes.

Q4. An acid has \(K_a=1.0\times10^{-5}\), while another has \(K_a=1.0\times10^{-3}\). Both are prepared at equal concentrations. Which prediction is most defensible?

A.The first acid is stronger because its \(K_a\) is numerically smaller
B.The second acid has a greater equilibrium tendency to ionize ✅
C.Both acids must ionize by exactly the same fraction
D.The first acid must have the greater hydrogen-ion concentration
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A larger \(K_a\) indicates that the ionization equilibrium lies farther toward products under comparable conditions. Thus, the acid with \(K_a=1.0\times10^{-3}\) is intrinsically stronger, although actual hydrogen-ion concentrations also depend on starting concentration.

Q5. A weak acid is diluted substantially with water. Which statement best distinguishes what happens to its \(K_a\) from what happens to its equilibrium concentrations?

A.\(K_a\) necessarily decreases, while all concentrations increase
B.\(K_a\) remains characteristic of the acid, while equilibrium concentrations and percent ionization can change ✅
C.Both \(K_a\) and percent ionization remain exactly unchanged
D.\(K_a\) increases because dilution creates more water molecules
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Dilution changes the concentrations of the reacting species and can alter the degree of ionization, often increasing the fraction ionized for a weak acid. However, the acid's \(K_a\) remains characteristic at constant temperature.

Q6. A researcher compares two weak acids at the same temperature and concentration. Acid P has \(K_a=2\times10^{-6}\), and acid Q has \(K_a=2\times10^{-4}\). Which experimental observation would best support the conclusion that Q is the stronger acid?

A.Q reaches equilibrium with a larger fraction of its molecules ionized ✅
B.P contains more total acid molecules
C.Q has a larger container volume
D.P and Q have identical equilibrium compositions
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A stronger weak acid has a greater equilibrium tendency to transfer a proton to water. Under the same initial concentration and temperature, the acid with the larger \(K_a\) should generally have a greater fraction of molecules present as ions at equilibrium.

Q7. A laboratory sample contains a weak acid at concentration \(C\). A second sample contains the same acid at \(4C\). Without calculating exact values, which comparison is most reasonable for the fraction of acid molecules ionized?

A.The more concentrated solution must have four times the ionized fraction
B.The less concentrated solution generally has a larger fraction ionized ✅
C.Both solutions necessarily have identical fractions ionized
D.The more concentrated solution must be completely ionized
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a weak acid, dilution generally increases the fraction of molecules that ionize because the equilibrium responds to changing species concentrations. Increasing the initial concentration does not increase acid strength and commonly decreases the percentage ionized.

Q8. A weak acid has \(K_a=4.0\times10^{-6}\). A student calculates its \(K_a\) from measured equilibrium concentrations but accidentally uses the initial acid concentration in the numerator instead of the equilibrium concentration. What conceptual error has occurred?

A.The student treated an equilibrium expression as though initial concentrations define equilibrium ✅
B.The student assumed weak acids cannot ionize
C.The student correctly used concentration because \(K_a\) is concentration-independent
D.The student confused \(K_a\) with molar mass
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An equilibrium constant must be constructed from equilibrium concentrations, because it describes the composition of the system after equilibrium is established. Substituting the initial acid concentration changes the expression and generally produces an incorrect \(K_a\).

Q9. For a weak acid, a graph of equilibrium fraction ionized versus initial acid concentration slopes downward as concentration increases. Which interpretation best explains the graph?

A.The acid becomes chemically stronger at lower concentration because its \(K_a\) increases
B.The fraction ionized decreases with increasing concentration even though the acid's characteristic \(K_a\) remains essentially constant ✅
C.The acid stops obeying equilibrium at high concentration
D.The graph proves that \(K_a\) is directly proportional to initial concentration
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The downward trend is consistent with the behavior of weak electrolytes: dilution generally increases the fraction ionized. The intrinsic \(K_a\) does not need to change for the fraction ionized to vary because equilibrium concentrations depend on the starting concentration.

Q10. A student compares a weak acid with \(K_a=10^{-5}\) to a weak base with \(K_b=10^{-5}\) and claims they must produce identical equilibrium concentrations of \(H^+\) and \(OH^-\) when placed in equal-concentration solutions. Why is this conclusion unjustified?

A.Because \(K_a\) and \(K_b\) describe different proton-transfer equilibria and their solutions must be analyzed separately ✅
B.Because equilibrium constants are never useful for weak electrolytes
C.Because equal constants always imply equal concentrations of every species
D.Because weak bases cannot react with water
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Although the numerical values happen to be equal, \(K_a\) measures an acid's proton-donation equilibrium while \(K_b\) measures a base's proton-accepting reaction with water. Equal numerical constants do not imply identical solution compositions or identical ion concentrations.

Q11. A student states: 'If acid A has a larger \(K_a\) than acid B, then every solution of A must have a higher \(H^+\) concentration than every solution of B.' Which counterexample most directly exposes the flaw?

A.Use A at extremely low concentration and B at much higher concentration ✅
B.Use A and B at equal concentrations
C.Use the same temperature for both
D.Compare their \(K_a\) values again
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Acid strength and solution acidity are related but not identical. A larger \(K_a\) indicates stronger intrinsic ionization, while actual \(H^+\) concentration also depends strongly on the amount of acid present, so concentration can reverse the simple comparison.

Q12. A weak acid is described by \(K_a=1.0\times10^{-8}\). Another acid has \(K_a=1.0\times10^{-2}\). A student says the first acid must produce more \(H^+\) because its smaller \(K_a\) means the equilibrium 'needs more products' to compensate. What is the most precise correction?

A.A smaller \(K_a\) indicates a greater tendency toward products
B.A smaller \(K_a\) indicates a weaker tendency toward ionization; actual \(H^+\) also depends on concentration ✅
C.Both acids must produce identical \(H^+\) concentrations
D.The smaller \(K_a\) means the first acid is completely ionized
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The student's interpretation reverses the meaning of the equilibrium constant. A smaller \(K_a\) indicates that the ionized products are less favored relative to the undissociated acid. The resulting hydrogen-ion concentration still depends on initial acid concentration.

Q13. Two weak acids have \(K_a\) values \(9.0\times10^{-6}\) and \(1.0\times10^{-7}\). If both are placed separately in solutions with the same initial concentration and temperature, which ranking is most consistent with equilibrium reasoning?

A.The second acid has the greater intrinsic ionization tendency
B.The first acid has the greater intrinsic ionization tendency ✅
C.They have identical intrinsic ionization tendencies because both are weak
D.Their \(K_a\) values cannot be compared because they use different powers of ten
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The first acid has a \(K_a\) about 90 times larger than the second, indicating substantially greater equilibrium favoring ionization. Calling both acids weak does not mean they have equal strength; weak acids can differ greatly in their dissociation constants.

Q14. For a hypothetical weak acid \(HA\), the equilibrium expression is \(K_a=\frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}\). Suppose an experiment reports \([H^+]=1.0\times10^{-3}\,M\), \([A^-]=1.0\times10^{-3}\,M\), and \([HA]=0.10\,M\). What value of \(K_a\) is implied, and what does it indicate?

A.\(1.0\times10^{-8}\), indicating extremely weak ionization
B.\(1.0\times10^{-5}\), indicating a weak acid with measurable ionization ✅
C.\(1.0\times10^{-2}\), indicating a relatively stronger weak acid
D.\(1.0\times10^{-1}\), indicating complete ionization
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Substituting the equilibrium concentrations gives \(K_a=(10^{-3})(10^{-3})/(0.10)=10^{-5}\). This value indicates that the acid is weak but has a measurable tendency to ionize, leaving most of the acid in its undissociated form.
