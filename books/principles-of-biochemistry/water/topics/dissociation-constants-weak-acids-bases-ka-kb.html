What is Dissociation constants weak acids bases Ka Kb?

Definition:
The dissociation constants KaK_aKa​ and KbK_bKb​ are equilibrium constants that measure the strength of weak acids and weak bases, respectively, where KaK_aKa​ is the acid dissociation constant (HA⇌H++A−HA \rightleftharpoons H^+ + A^-HA⇌H++A−), and KbK_bKb​ is the base dissociation constant (B+H2O⇌BH++OH−B + H_2O \rightleftharpoons BH^+ + OH^-B+H2​O⇌BH++OH−); larger KaK_aKa​ indicates a stronger acid (more dissociation), and larger KbK_bKb​ indicates a stronger base; these constants are essential for predicting the ionization behavior of biomolecules in biological systems.

Working:
For a weak acid HA, Ka=[H+][A−][HA]K_a = \frac{[H^+][A^-]}{[HA]}Ka​=[HA][H+][A−]​, and for a weak base B, Kb=[BH+][OH−][B]K_b = \frac{[BH^+][OH^-]}{[B]}Kb​=[B][BH+][OH−]​, and they are related by Kw=Ka×Kb=1.0×10−14K_w = K_a \times K_b = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}Kw​=Ka​×Kb​=1.0×10−14 at 25°C; the pKapK_apKa​ is defined as pKa=−log⁡KapK_a = -\log K_apKa​=−logKa​, and the pKb=−log⁡KbpK_b = -\log K_bpKb​=−logKb​; weaker acids have larger pKapK_apKa​ (e.g., acetic acid pKa=4.76pK_a = 4.76pKa​=4.76), and these values determine the ionization state at a given pH, which is critical for protein structure, enzyme catalysis, and drug action, as many molecules are only active in a specific ionization state.

Example:
A simple example is acetic acid (CH₃COOH), with Ka=1.8×10−5K_a = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}Ka​=1.8×10−5 (pKa=4.76pK_a = 4.76pKa​=4.76), meaning it is a weak acid that partially dissociates; another example is ammonia (NH₃), with Kb=1.8×10−5K_b = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}Kb​=1.8×10−5 (pKb=4.74pK_b = 4.74pKb​=4.74), illustrating how KaK_aKa​ and KbK_bKb​ quantify the strength of weak acids and bases.

Reason:
Understanding KaK_aKa​ and KbK_bKb​ is essential for biochemistry, as they determine the ionization state of functional groups in amino acids, nucleotides, and drugs, affecting their solubility, reactivity, and biological activity, and they are crucial for understanding buffer systems and acid-base balance.

Q1. A weak acid HAHAHA establishes the equilibrium HA⇌H++A−HA \rightleftharpoons H^+ + A^-HA⇌H++A−. Which quantity most directly characterizes the acid's intrinsic tendency to dissociate under specified conditions?

A.The initial concentration of HAHAHA only
B.The acid dissociation constant KaK_aKa​ ✅
C.The final concentration of H+H^+H+ only
D.The volume of the solution only
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The acid dissociation constant KaK_aKa​ describes the equilibrium tendency of a weak acid to donate protons. Unlike initial concentration, it is characteristic of the acid under a specified temperature and does not simply represent how much acid was initially added.

Q2. For a weak base BBB, the equilibrium is B+H2O⇌BH++OH−B + H_2O \rightleftharpoons BH^+ + OH^-B+H2​O⇌BH++OH−. If two weak bases are measured under identical conditions and base A has the larger KbK_bKb​, what can be concluded?

A.Base A has a greater equilibrium tendency to form BH+BH^+BH+ and OH−OH^-OH− ✅
B.Base A must have the lower initial concentration
C.Base A necessarily has the higher molar mass
D.Base A produces no conjugate acid
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A larger KbK_bKb​ means the equilibrium favors ionization of the weak base more strongly under comparable conditions. Therefore, base A has a greater tendency to react with water to produce BH+BH^+BH+ and OH−OH^-OH−, regardless of its initial concentration.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The student's reasoning confuses acid concentration with acid strength. For the same weak acid at the same temperature, KaK_aKa​ remains characteristic of that acid, although the equilibrium concentrations of all species change when the starting concentration changes.

Thus, the acid with Ka=1.0×10−3K_a=1.0\times10^{-3}Ka​=1.0×10−3 is intrinsically stronger, although actual hydrogen-ion concentrations also depend on starting concentration.

Q5. A weak acid is diluted substantially with water. Which statement best distinguishes what happens to its KaK_aKa​ from what happens to its equilibrium concentrations?

A.KaK_aKa​ necessarily decreases, while all concentrations increase

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Dilution changes the concentrations of the reacting species and can alter the degree of ionization, often increasing the fraction ionized for a weak acid. However, the acid's KaK_aKa​ remains characteristic at constant temperature.

📖 Explanation: A stronger weak acid has a greater equilibrium tendency to transfer a proton to water. Under the same initial concentration and temperature, the acid with the larger KaK_aKa​ should generally have a greater fraction of molecules present as ions at equilibrium.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Although the numerical values happen to be equal, KaK_aKa​ measures an acid's proton-donation equilibrium while KbK_bKb​ measures a base's proton-accepting reaction with water. Equal numerical constants do not imply identical solution compositions or identical ion concentrations.

📖 Explanation: Acid strength and solution acidity are related but not identical. A larger KaK_aKa​ indicates stronger intrinsic ionization, while actual H+H^+H+ concentration also depends strongly on the amount of acid present, so concentration can reverse the simple comparison.
