What is Ionization of pure water H+ and OH-?

Definition:
The ionization of pure water is the reversible process in which water molecules dissociate into hydronium ions (H3O+H_3O^+H3​O+) and hydroxide ions (OH−OH^-OH−), represented by the equation 2H2O⇌H3O++OH−2H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+ + OH^-2H2​O⇌H3​O++OH−, and at 25°C, the concentration of H+H^+H+ and OH−OH^-OH− are both 1.0×10−71.0 \times 10^{-7}1.0×10−7 M, resulting in a neutral pH of 7, and this ionization is the basis for acid-base chemistry in aqueous systems.

Example:
A simple example is pure water at 25°C, where the concentration of H+H^+H+ is 10−710^{-7}10−7 M, giving a pH of 7; another example is the addition of an acid, which increases [H+][H^+][H+], shifting the equilibrium and lowering the pH, illustrating the ionization of water as the basis of pH changes.

Reason:
Understanding the ionization of water is fundamental to biochemistry and physiology, as it forms the basis for pH, acid-base balance, and enzyme activity, and it is essential for diagnosing and treating metabolic disorders.

📝 All Ionization of pure water H+ and OH- MCQs

Q1. A sample of pure water at 25∘C25^\circ\mathrm{C}25∘C contains equal concentrations of which two ions because water molecules occasionally transfer protons between one another?

A.H+H^+H+ and OH−OH^-OH− ✅
B.H2+H_2^+H2+​ and O2−O^{2-}O2−
C.H3O+H_3O^+H3​O+ and O2−O^{2-}O2−
D.H−H^-H− and OH+OH^+OH+
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Pure water undergoes a very small extent of self-ionization, producing hydronium and hydroxide ions in equal amounts. The equal concentrations result because each ionization event produces one positively charged species and one negatively charged species.

Q2. Why does a beaker of pure water remain electrically neutral even though a small fraction of its molecules exists as ions?

A.The ions have no charges at equilibrium
B.Positive and negative ions are produced in equal amounts ✅
C.Water immediately converts every ion back to a molecule
D.The ions become electrically neutral after formation
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Water remains electrically neutral because ionization produces positively and negatively charged ions in equal numbers. Although individual ions carry charge, their total positive and negative charges balance throughout an otherwise pure sample.

Q3. A student claims that because pure water is mostly molecular, it contains absolutely no ions. Which observation most directly contradicts this reasoning?

A.Pure water can conduct a small electrical current ✅
B.Pure water has a relatively high boiling point
C.Pure water molecules can form hydrogen bonds
D.Pure water occupies a measurable volume
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Pure water is a weak electrical conductor because a small population of ions is always present. Its poor conductivity reflects the low ion concentration, not the complete absence of charged particles.

Q4. Two identical samples of pure water are maintained at the same temperature. Sample X contains twice as many water molecules as sample Y. Which comparison is most reasonable for their ion concentrations at equilibrium?

A.X must have twice the ion concentration of Y
B.Y must have twice the ion concentration of X
C.Their ion concentrations can be the same despite different sample sizes ✅
D.Neither sample can contain ions because both are pure
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Ion concentration describes the amount of ions per unit volume rather than the total number of ions. Therefore, equal equilibrium conditions can produce the same ion concentrations even when the samples have different volumes.

Q5. A researcher removes half the water from a sealed container of pure water without changing temperature. The remaining water is allowed to re-equilibrate. What is the best prediction for ion concentration?

A.It must remain permanently twice as large
B.It should return toward the same equilibrium concentration ✅
C.It must become exactly half as concentrated
D.All ions must disappear because the volume decreased
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Removing water changes the total number of molecules but does not automatically double the equilibrium ion concentration. After disturbance, ionization and recombination continue until the characteristic equilibrium concentration at that temperature is restored.

Q6. A conductivity experiment compares pure water with a solution containing many dissolved ions. The pure water gives a much smaller conductivity reading. What conclusion is best supported?

A.Pure water contains no charged particles
B.Pure water contains fewer mobile ions ✅
C.Water molecules cannot move through the solution
D.Ionization occurs only after a salt is added
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Electrical conductivity depends strongly on the concentration and mobility of charged particles. Pure water contains only a very small concentration of ions, so it conducts electricity much less effectively than an ionic solution.

Q7. A sealed flask initially contains only water molecules. After sufficient time, a tiny amount of H3O+H_3O^+H3​O+ and OH−OH^-OH− is detected. Which model best explains the result?

A.Ionization is impossible without an external acid
B.Some water molecules transfer protons to other water molecules ✅
C.Oxygen atoms spontaneously become negatively charged
D.Hydrogen atoms permanently leave the flask
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Water molecules can interact so that one molecule donates a proton while another accepts it. This produces hydronium and hydroxide ions, while the reverse process continuously occurs, establishing a dynamic equilibrium.

Q8. A student argues, 'If water ionizes, every water molecule must eventually become an ion.' What is the main flaw in this argument?

A.Ionization creates only gases
B.Ionization occurs to a very small fraction of molecules at any instant ✅
C.Water molecules cannot interact with one another
D.Ions cannot exist in liquid water
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Self-ionization is limited, so only a very small fraction of water molecules exists in ionic form at equilibrium. Most molecules remain un-ionized, while ion formation and recombination continue simultaneously.

Q9. At constant temperature, a conductivity sensor is connected to increasingly pure water samples. The readings approach a small nonzero value rather than exactly zero. Which explanation best fits the trend?

A.Purification creates additional permanent charges
B.A small equilibrium population of ions remains in pure water ✅
C.The sensor generates all ions detected
D.Neutral molecules conduct electricity exactly like ions
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Even highly pure water contains a small equilibrium population of hydronium and hydroxide ions. These mobile charged species account for the small residual conductivity observed under controlled conditions.

Q10. A graph plots ion concentration on the vertical axis against time for freshly purified water. The curve starts near zero, rises rapidly, and then approaches a horizontal plateau. What does the plateau most likely represent?

A.Complete conversion of water into ions
B.A stable equilibrium ion concentration ✅
C.Permanent removal of water molecules
D.Continuous unlimited ion production
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The rising portion represents net ion formation as the system approaches equilibrium. The plateau indicates that ionization and recombination occur at balanced rates, so the concentrations remain approximately constant rather than continuing to increase.

Q11. Two students analyze the same pure-water sample. Student A says ionization stops once equilibrium is reached. Student B says ionization and recombination continue while their rates balance. Who is correct?

A.Only A
B.Only B ✅
C.Both equally
D.Neither, because equilibrium means no molecular motion
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Dynamic equilibrium does not mean molecular processes stop. Water molecules continue forming and recombining into ions, but at equilibrium the forward and reverse processes occur at equal rates, maintaining nearly constant concentrations.

Q12. A sample of pure water is warmed, and measurements show that the equilibrium concentration of ions increases. A student concludes that heating simply creates more water molecules. Which reasoning error is present?

A.The student ignores that temperature can alter ionization equilibrium ✅
B.The student assumes ions have no charge
C.The student correctly connects heating only with molecular number
D.The student assumes water is a solid
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The observation concerns equilibrium ion concentration, not the number of water molecules. Temperature can change the balance between ion formation and recombination, so increased ion concentration cannot be explained simply by creating more molecules.

Q14. In an idealized model, pure water contains NNN water molecules and a tiny equilibrium fraction fff becomes ionized. If the number of molecules is doubled while temperature and volume concentration conditions remain comparable, what is the most important distinction between total ions and ion concentration?

A.Both must double under every condition
B.Total ions may increase while ion concentration can remain unchanged ✅

📖 Explanation: Doubling the amount of solution can increase the total number of ions present while leaving concentration unchanged if the volume increases proportionally. Concentration is an intensive quantity, whereas total particle number depends on sample size.
