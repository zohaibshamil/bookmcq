What is Radioactive decay differential equation?

Definition:
Radioactive decay follows dNdt=−λN\frac{dN}{dt} = -\lambda NdtdN​=−λN where λ\lambdaλ is decay constant, yielding N(t)=N0e−λtN(t) = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}N(t)=N0​e−λt describing exponential decrease in unstable nuclei count.

Example:
Carbon-14 with λ=0.000121\lambda = 0.000121λ=0.000121/year: from 1000 atoms, after 1000 years N(1000)=1000e−0.121≈886N(1000) = 1000e^{-0.121} \approx 886N(1000)=1000e−0.121≈886 atoms remain.

Reason:
This model enables radiometric dating and nuclear physics calculations, crucial for archaeology and understanding atomic stability.
