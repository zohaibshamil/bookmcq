What is Inhibited / Logistic population growth model?

Definition:
Logistic growth accounts for limited resources by introducing carrying capacity \( K \), modeled as \( \frac{dP}{dt} = kP(1 - \frac{P}{K}) \) with S-shaped curve approaching \( K \).

Example:
With \( K=1000 \), \( k=0.1 \), \( P_0=100 \), population grows rapidly then slows, approaching 1000 as \( t \to \infty \).

Reason:
Real populations face resource limits; this model captures saturation effects, providing more realistic long-term predictions than exponential growth.

Q1. A population follows the logistic model \( \frac{dy}{dt} = k(1 - \frac{y}{L})y \). If the current population is \( y = \frac{L}{4} \), which of the following best describes the instantaneous growth rate relative to the maximum possible growth rate for this system?

A.The growth rate is exactly 25% of the maximum.
B.The growth rate is exactly 50% of the maximum.
C.The growth rate is exactly 75% of the maximum. ✅
D.The growth rate is equal to the maximum.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The maximum growth rate in a logistic model occurs at \( y = L/2 \), where \( dy/dt = kL/4 \). At \( y = L/4 \), substituting into the differential equation yields \( k(1 - 1/4)(L/4) = k(3/4)(L/4) = 3kL/16 \). Comparing this to the maximum \( kL/4 \) (which is \( 4kL/16 \)), the ratio is \( 3/4 \) or 75%. Students often mistakenly assume linearity and select 25%, confusing the population fraction with the growth rate fraction.

Q2. In a logistic growth scenario, a researcher observes that the population growth rate \( dy/dt \) is decreasing even though the population size \( y \) is still increasing and is well below the carrying capacity \( L \). Which of the following must be true about the current population size?

A.\( y < L/2 \)
B.\( y > L/2 \) ✅
C.\( y = L/2 \)
D.\( y > L \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The logistic growth rate function \( f(y) = ky(1 - y/L) \) is a downward-opening parabola with a peak at \( y = L/2 \). For \( y < L/2 \), the growth rate increases as population increases. For \( y > L/2 \), the growth rate decreases as population increases toward \( L \). Therefore, if the population is increasing but the *rate* of that increase is slowing down, the population must have already passed the inflection point at \( L/2 \). This tests understanding of the derivative of the growth rate function.

Q4. Consider the logistic differential equation \( \frac{dy}{dt} = 0.5y(1 - \frac{y}{100}) \). A student solves this and obtains the solution \( y(t) = \frac{100}{1 + Ce^{-0.5t}} \). They claim that regardless of the initial population \( y_0 > 0 \), the population will reach exactly 50 individuals at \( t = \ln(C)/0.5 \). What is the fundamental error in this reasoning?

A.The student confused the inflection point with the time to reach half-capacity.
B.The formula for \( t \) is algebraically incorrect; it should involve \( \ln(1/C) \).
C.The value \( C \) depends on \( y_0 \), so the time to reach 50 varies with initial conditions. ✅
D.There is no error; all logistic curves pass through \( L/2 \) at the same time.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The constant \( C \) is determined by the initial condition: \( C = (L - y_0)/y_0 \). The time at which \( y(t) = L/2 \) corresponds to when the denominator equals 2, i.e., \( 1 + Ce^{-kt} = 2 \Rightarrow e^{-kt} = 1/C \Rightarrow t = \frac{\ln C}{k} \). Since \( C \) changes with \( y_0 \), the specific calendar time at which the population hits 50 shifts left or right. Only the *shape* of the curve is invariant; the horizontal translation depends entirely on the starting value. This tests the link between integration constants and physical initial conditions.

Q5. Which of the following modifications to the standard logistic model \( y' = ky(1-y/L) \) would best represent a population subject to a minimum viable threshold \( T \) below which the population cannot sustain itself and declines to extinction?

A.\( y' = ky(1 - y/L)(y/T - 1) \) ✅
B.\( y' = ky(1 - y/L) - T \)
C.\( y' = ky(1 - (y-T)/L) \)
D.\( y' = ky(T/y)(1 - y/L) \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This requires synthesizing logistic growth with an Allee effect or threshold model. The term \( (y/T - 1) \) introduces a new zero at \( y=T \). When \( y < T \), this term is negative, making the entire derivative negative (assuming \( y < L \)), causing decline. When \( T < y < L \), all terms are positive, allowing growth. Option B creates a constant subtraction which doesn't guarantee extinction dynamics correctly near zero. Option C merely shifts the carrying capacity. Option D alters the scaling but not the sign change required for a threshold. This tests modeling adaptation skills.

Q6. B.The population will grow indefinitely.
C.The population will stabilize at 500. ✅
D.The population will initially decrease before growing to 500.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The graph of \( dy/dt \) vs \( y \) directly reveals the equilibrium points and stability. The x-intercepts (where \( dy/dt=0 \)) are the equilibrium solutions. Here, they are \( y=0 \) and \( y=500 \). Since the parabola opens downward (implied by logistic context and vertex being a maximum), \( dy/dt > 0 \) for \( 0 < y < 500 \). Thus, starting at 100, the population increases. As \( y \to 500 \), \( dy/dt \to 0 \). The vertex at 250 represents the point of maximum growth rate, not the carrying capacity. Q7. Suppose a population obeys logistic growth with \( L=1000 \) and \( k=0.2 \). Due to a sudden environmental disaster at time \( t^* \), the carrying capacity instantly drops to \( L_{new}=400 \). If the population at \( t^* \) was 800, describe the immediate mathematical consequence for the differential equation governing the system after \( t^* \).

A.The growth rate becomes negative, initiating exponential-like decay toward 400. ✅
B.The growth rate remains positive but decreases until reaching 400.
C.The population instantly drops to 400 to match the new capacity.
D.The differential equation becomes undefined due to the discontinuity in L.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Before the disaster, \( y=800 < 1000 \), so \( dy/dt > 0 \). Immediately after, with \( L_{new}=400 \) and \( y=800 \), the term \( (1 - y/L_{new}) = (1 - 800/400) = -1 \). Thus \( dy/dt = k(-1)(800) < 0 \). The population is now above carrying capacity and must decline. The decline is not purely exponential because the \( y \) term also changes, but the sign flip is the critical immediate consequence. Option C violates continuity of biological populations. Option B ignores that \( y > L \) implies negative growth. This tests dynamic response to parameter shifts.

Q8. When deriving the solution to the logistic equation via separation of variables, one arrives at the integral \( \int \frac{L}{y(L-y)} dy \). A student performs partial fraction decomposition incorrectly as \( \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{L-y} \) instead of \( \frac{1}{y} + \frac{1}{L-y} \) multiplied by \( 1/L \). How does this specific error manifest in the final explicit solution?

A.The exponent in the solution becomes \( kt \) instead of \( kLt \) or similar scaling.
B.The carrying capacity \( L \) disappears from the numerator of the final fraction.
C.The integration constant \( C \) is scaled incorrectly, shifting the curve horizontally.
D.The resulting function fails to satisfy the original differential equation entirely. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: If the partial fraction coefficient \( 1/L \) is omitted, the integration yields \( \ln|y| - \ln|L-y| = kt + C \) (missing the factor of \( L \) on the RHS or adjusting the LHS). Exponentiating gives \( \frac{y}{L-y} = Ae^{kt} \). Solving for \( y \) yields \( y = \frac{LAe^{kt}}{1+Ae^{kt}} \). While this looks structurally similar, differentiating this result produces \( y' = ky(1-y/L) \times (\text{missing factor}) \). Specifically, the chain rule on the incorrect solution will not reproduce the original ODE unless the scaling matches. More fundamentally, dimensional analysis fails: \( kt \) is dimensionless, but without proper scaling during integration, the relationship between time units and growth parameters breaks. The most rigorous check is always back-substitution.

Q9. In a fishery management scenario, the harvest rate \( H \) is modeled as a constant removal. The modified logistic equation is \( \frac{dy}{dt} = ky(1 - \frac{y}{L}) - H \). If \( H > \frac{kL}{4} \), what is the biological and mathematical implication for the fish population?

A.The population stabilizes at a lower equilibrium than \( L \).
B.The population exhibits oscillatory behavior around a new mean.
C.The quadratic growth-minus-harvest function has no real roots, leading to inevitable extinction. ✅
D.The population stabilizes at \( L/2 \) but with reduced resilience.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The equilibria occur where \( ky(1-y/L) - H = 0 \), or \( -\frac{k}{L}y^2 + ky - H = 0 \). The discriminant of this quadratic is \( k^2 - 4(-k/L)(-H) = k^2 - \frac{4kH}{L} \). For real roots to exist, we need \( k^2 \geq \frac{4kH}{L} \Rightarrow H \leq \frac{kL}{4} \). If \( H > kL/4 \), the discriminant is negative, meaning the harvest parabola lies entirely above the growth parabola. The net rate \( dy/dt \) is always negative for all \( y \). Consequently, no stable equilibrium exists, and the population declines monotonically to zero. This connects algebraic properties of quadratics to ecological collapse thresholds.

Q10. Compare the uninhibited exponential model \( y' = ky \) and the logistic model \( y' = ky(1-y/L) \) over the interval \( 0 < y < L/2 \). Which statement accurately characterizes the divergence between these two models?

A.The logistic model predicts faster growth because environmental resistance aids reproduction at low densities.
B.The difference between the predicted growth rates increases linearly with \( y \).
C.The relative error between the two growth rates is proportional to \( y/L \). ✅
D.The models are indistinguishable in this interval because \( y/L \approx 0 \).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Exponential rate: \( R_E = ky \). Logistic rate: \( R_L = ky - \frac{k}{L}y^2 \). The absolute difference is \( \frac{k}{L}y^2 \), which is quadratic, not linear (eliminating B). The relative difference is \( \frac{R_E - R_L}{R_E} = \frac{(ky - ky + ky^2/L)}{ky} = \frac{y}{L} \). Thus, the fractional deviation from exponential growth scales directly with the fraction of carrying capacity utilized. While they are *similar* at very low \( y \), they are not indistinguishable; the deviation is systematic and predictable. This quantifies the 'inhibition' effect precisely.

Q12. Given the logistic solution \( y(t) = \frac{L}{1 + Ae^{-kt}} \), suppose you are given three equally spaced data points \( (t_1, y_1), (t_2, y_2), (t_3, y_3) \) where \( t_2 - t_1 = t_3 - t_2 = \Delta t \). Which relationship allows estimation of \( L \) without knowing \( k \) or \( A \)?

A.\( L = \frac{y_1 y_3 - y_2^2}{y_1 + y_3 - 2y_2} \) ✅
B.\( L = \frac{y_2(y_1 + y_3)}{2y_2 - y_1 - y_3} \)
C.\( L = \sqrt{y_1 y_3} \)
D.\( L = \frac{y_1 y_2 y_3}{y_1 y_3 - y_2^2} \)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This exploits the structure of the logistic function. Rearranging the solution gives \( \frac{L-y}{y} = Ae^{-kt} \). Taking logs: \( \ln(\frac{L-y}{y}) = \ln A - kt \). This is linear in \( t \). For equally spaced points, the values \( z_i = \ln(\frac{L-y_i}{y_i}) \) form an arithmetic progression, so \( z_1 + z_3 = 2z_2 \). Substituting back: \( \ln(\frac{L-y_1}{y_1}) + \ln(\frac{L-y_3}{y_3}) = 2\ln(\frac{L-y_2}{y_2}) \). Exponentiating and solving algebraically for \( L \) yields the formula in Option A. This is a classic parameter estimation technique that bypasses nonlinear regression, testing deep algebraic manipulation of the model's implicit linearizability.

Q13. A population model uses \( \frac{dy}{dt} = ky(1 - \frac{y}{L}) \). If the environment changes such that \( L \) becomes a function of time \( L(t) \), why can we no longer use the standard analytical solution \( y = \frac{L}{1+Ae^{-kt}} \)?

A.Because \( k \) also becomes time-dependent implicitly.
B.Because the separation of variables step \( \int \frac{dy}{y(1-y/L)} \) is invalid when \( L \) varies with \( t \). ✅
C.Because the population will definitely go extinct.
D.Because the differential equation becomes second-order.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The standard solution relies on integrating \( \frac{1}{y(1-y/L)} \) with respect to \( y \) while treating \( L \) as a constant. If \( L=L(t) \), the integrand becomes a function of both \( y \) and \( t \) in a non-separable way: \( \frac{dy}{dt} = f(y,t) \) where the variables cannot be isolated to opposite sides. The partial fraction decomposition assumes constant coefficients. This tests understanding of *why* solution methods work, not just how to apply them. It highlights the distinction between autonomous and non-autonomous ODEs.

D.The critic, because logistic growth should appear linear in semi-log plots.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Semi-log plots (\( \ln y \) vs \( t \)) show exponential growth as linear. Any growth that slows relative to exponential (including both Logistic and Gompertz) appears concave down. Visual inspection of concavity alone cannot discriminate between different inhibition mechanisms. The logistic model specifically linearizes under the logit transform \( \ln(\frac{y}{L-y}) = kt + C \). Gompertz linearizes under \( \ln(-\ln(y/L)) \). Confirming the *type* of inhibition requires testing the specific transformation that yields linearity. This addresses model selection and diagnostic plotting.

Q15. Consider the discrete logistic map \( x_{n+1} = rx_n(1-x_n) \) as an approximation of the continuous logistic ODE. For \( r > 3 \), the discrete system exhibits period-doubling bifurcations and chaos. Why does the continuous ODE \( y' = ky(1-y/L) \) never exhibit chaotic behavior regardless of parameter values?

A.Continuous first-order autonomous ODEs cannot oscillate or be chaotic due to the uniqueness theorem preventing trajectory crossing.
B.The continuous model lacks the time-delay feedback present in the discrete map.
C.Chaos requires at least three dimensions in continuous systems; the logistic ODE is one-dimensional.
D.All of the above reasons contribute to the absence of chaos. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: This connects calculus to dynamical systems theory. In 1D continuous autonomous systems \( y'=f(y) \), trajectories on the phase line can only move toward fixed points; they cannot cross (uniqueness) or reverse direction without hitting a fixed point. Oscillations require at least 2D (Poincaré-Bendixson), and chaos typically requires 3D (Lorenz). The discrete map allows 'jumping' over fixed points, enabling complex dynamics impossible in the smooth flow of the ODE. Option D captures the multifaceted mathematical justification, testing synthesis of ODE theory and discrete dynamics.

Q16. A student calculates the time to reach 90% of carrying capacity starting from \( y_0 = L/2 \) and gets \( t = \frac{\ln 9}{k} \). Another student starts from \( y_0 = L/10 \) and calculates the time to reach 90% of \( L \). How do these times compare?

From \( L/10 \) to \( 0.9L \), it includes the acceleration phase, the peak rate phase, AND the deceleration phase. Even though early growth is faster per capita, the total distance (in terms of population magnitude) is much greater. Calculating explicitly: \( t = \frac{1}{k} \ln|\frac{y(L-y_0)}{y_0(L-y)}| \). Q18. Which of the following statements about the second derivative \( y'' \) of the logistic function is FALSE?

A.\( y'' = 0 \) when \( y = L/2 \).
B.\( y'' > 0 \) for \( 0 < y < L/2 \).
C.\( y'' \) is maximized when \( y = L/2 \). ✅
D.\( y'' \) changes sign exactly once for \( y_0 \in (0, L) \).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Differentiating \( y' = ky(1-y/L) \) with respect to \( t \): \( y'' = k y' (1 - 2y/L) \). At \( y=L/2 \), \( y''=0 \) (inflection point), confirming A. For \( y<L/2 \), \( y'>0 \) and \( (1-2y/L)>0 \), so \( y''>0 \), confirming B. Sign change occurs at \( y=L/2 \), confirming D. However, \( y'' \) is ZERO at \( y=L/2 \), not maximized. The *first* derivative \( y' \) is maximized there. The second derivative actually has extrema elsewhere (found by setting \( y'''=0 \)). This tests careful distinction between properties of \( y \), \( y' \), and \( y'' \).

Q22. A numerical simulation of \( y' = ky(1-y/L) \) using Euler's method with a very large step size \( \Delta t \) produces oscillations or negative populations, even though the true solution is smooth and positive. What causes this artifact?

A.Euler's method assumes linearity over \( \Delta t \); if \( \Delta t \) exceeds the system's characteristic timescale, the discrete update overshoots the equilibrium. ✅
B.The logistic equation is inherently unstable numerically.
C.Round-off error accumulates exponentially.
D.The carrying capacity \( L \) must be adjusted for discrete steps.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Euler's update is \( y_{n+1} = y_n + h f(y_n) \). Near equilibrium \( y \approx L \), \( f(y) \) is small but negative if \( y>L \). If \( h \) is too large, the correction \( h f(y_n) \) can be so negative that \( y_{n+1} \ll L \) or even \( <0 \). On the next step, \( f(y) \) becomes large positive, causing a massive upward jump. This numerical instability arises when the step size violates the stability region of the method relative to the eigenvalue of the ODE at equilibrium. It's a crucial warning about blind numerical application.

Q23. Interpret the integral \( \int_{y_0}^{y} \frac{du}{u(1-u/L)} = kt \) physically. What does the integrand \( \frac{1}{u(1-u/L)} \) represent?

A.The instantaneous growth rate at population \( u \).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Since \( dt = \frac{dy}{y'} = \frac{dy}{ky(1-y/L)} \), the integrand (ignoring \( k \)) is \( dt/dy \). This reframes the solution process from abstract calculus to accumulating temporal costs, deepening conceptual grasp.

Q30. In a laboratory culture, bacteria follow logistic growth. At \( t=0 \), \( y=100 \). At \( t=2 \), \( y=200 \). At \( t=4 \), \( y=350 \). Estimate \( L \) using the three-point method logic conceptually.

Q36. If the logistic equation is rewritten as \( \frac{d}{dt}(\ln \frac{y}{L-y}) = k \), what does this reveal about the 'per-capita growth rate adjusted for crowding'?
