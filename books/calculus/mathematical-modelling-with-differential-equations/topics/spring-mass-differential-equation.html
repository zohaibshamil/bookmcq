What is Spring mass differential equation?

The spring-mass system is governed by Newton's second law leading to the second-order equation \( m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + c\frac{dx}{dt} + kx = F(t) \), which models oscillatory motion by balancing inertial, damping, restoring, and external forces acting on the mass.

Q1. A mass-spring system is modeled by \( m\frac{d^2x}{dt^2} + kx = 0 \). If the mass \( m \) is doubled while the spring constant \( k \) remains unchanged, which of the following best describes the effect on the system's natural frequency and period?

A. The frequency doubles and the period halves.
B. The frequency decreases by a factor of \( \sqrt{2} \) and the period increases by \( \sqrt{2} \). ✅
C. Both frequency and period double.
D. The frequency remains constant but the amplitude decreases.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The natural angular frequency is given by \( \omega = \sqrt{k/m} \). Doubling the mass changes this to \( \sqrt{k/2m} = \omega/\sqrt{2} \), reducing frequency by \( \sqrt{2} \). Since period \( T = 2\pi/\omega \), it increases by \( \sqrt{2} \). This tests understanding of parameter dependence rather than rote memorization of formulas.

This error analysis question targets deep conceptual understanding of how mathematical signs encode physical stability.

Q11. If the solution to \( mx''+kx=0 \) is written as \( x(t)=A\cos(\omega t - \delta) \), and initial conditions give \( x(0)=a \), \( x'(0)=b \), which expression correctly gives the phase angle \( \delta \)?

A. \( \tan \delta = b/(a\omega) \)
B. \( \tan \delta = -b/(a\omega) \) ✅
C. \( \tan \delta = a\omega/b \)
D. \( \tan \delta = -a\omega/b \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Expanding: \( x(t)=A\cos\delta\cos(\omega t)+A\sin\delta\sin(\omega t) \). So \( x(0)=A\cos\delta=a \), \( x'(0)=A\omega\sin\delta=b \). Thus \( \tan\delta = (A\sin\delta)/(A\cos\delta) = b/(a\omega) \). But wait—derivative of \( \cos(\omega t - \delta) \) is \( \omega\sin(\omega t - \delta) \), so \( x'(0)=A\omega\sin(-\delta)=-A\omega\sin\delta=b \). Hence \( \sin\delta = -b/(A\omega) \), and \( \tan\delta = -b/(a\omega) \). This error-prone derivation makes it ideal for HOTS, as sign mistakes are common.
