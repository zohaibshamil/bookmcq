Q1. A population model is given by \( \frac{dy}{dt} = ky \). If the relative growth rate is 3% per year, which statement correctly interprets the physical meaning of \( k \) in this differential equation?

🔹 A. \( k \) represents the absolute number of individuals added to the population each year.
🔹 B. \( k \) represents the ratio of the instantaneous rate of change to the current population size.
🔹 C. \( k \) represents the time required for the population to double in size.
🔹 D. \( k \) represents the total accumulated growth over a one-year period.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The constant \( k \) in the exponential model \( dy/dt = ky \) is defined as the relative growth rate. Mathematically, this means \( k = (dy/dt)/y \), representing the fractional increase per unit time rather than an absolute count. Students often confuse relative rate with absolute rate; however, because the derivative depends on \( y \), the absolute growth increases over time while \( k \) remains constant. This distinction is fundamental to understanding why exponential growth accelerates.

Q2. In analyzing radioactive decay data, a student plots \( \ln(y) \) versus time \( t \) and obtains a straight line with slope -0.05. Another student plots \( y \) versus \( t \) directly and attempts to fit an exponential curve visually. Why is the semi-logarithmic plot superior for determining the decay constant?

🔹 A. It eliminates the need for calculus entirely.
🔹 B. It linearizes the exponential relationship, making parameter estimation robust against visual bias.
🔹 C. It automatically corrects for measurement errors in the original data.
🔹 D. It converts the decay constant into a positive value for easier interpretation.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Transforming \( y = y_0 e^{-kt} \) by taking the natural logarithm yields \( \ln(y) = -kt + \ln(y_0) \), which is linear in form \( Y = mX + b \). Linear regression on transformed data provides statistically unbiased estimates of parameters compared to nonlinear visual fitting. The slope directly gives \( -k \). While direct plotting shows the curve shape, it obscures precise parameter values due to the rapid compression of exponential scales, making error analysis and verification significantly harder without linearization.

Q3. A bacterial culture grows according to \( y(t) = y_0 e^{kt} \). If the population triples every 4 hours, what is the exact expression for the doubling time \( T \)?

🔹 A. \( T = \frac{4 \ln 2}{\ln 3} \)
🔹 B. \( T = \frac{\ln 3}{4 \ln 2} \)
🔹 C. \( T = \frac{4 \ln 3}{\ln 2} \)
🔹 D. \( T = 4 \ln(1.5) \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Given tripling time \( T_3 = 4 \), we have \( 3 = e^{4k} \), so \( k = (\ln 3)/4 \). For doubling time \( T \), we require \( 2 = e^{kT} \), implying \( T = (\ln 2)/k \). Substituting \( k \) yields \( T = (\ln 2) / ((\ln 3)/4) = 4 \ln 2 / \ln 3 \). This problem tests the ability to manipulate exponential relationships without calculating intermediate decimal approximations. A common error is confusing the numerator and denominator or assuming doubling time is simply proportional to tripling time without logarithmic scaling factors.

Q4. Consider two substances A and B decaying exponentially. Substance A has a half-life of 10 years, and substance B has a half-life of 20 years. If both start with equal mass, at what time \( t \) will the ratio of remaining mass \( y_A / y_B \) be exactly 0.25?

🔹 A. 10 years
🔹 B. 20 years
🔹 C. 30 years
🔹 D. 40 years

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Let \( y_A = M e^{-k_A t} \) and \( y_B = M e^{-k_B t} \). The ratio is \( e^{-(k_A - k_B)t} \). Since \( k = \ln 2 / T_{1/2} \), we have \( k_A = \ln 2 / 10 \) and \( k_B = \ln 2 / 20 \). Setting the ratio to 0.25: \( e^{-(\ln 2 / 10 - \ln 2 / 20)t} = 1/4 \). Simplifying the exponent gives \( -(\ln 2 / 20)t = \ln(1/4) = -2 \ln 2 \). Solving for \( t \) yields \( t/20 = 2 \), so \( t = 40 \). This requires combining decay constants and solving logarithmic equations involving ratios.

Q5. A student calculates the age of an artifact using carbon-14 dating. They use the formula \( t = -\frac{1}{k} \ln(P) \) where \( P \) is the fraction remaining. If they accidentally use the base-10 logarithm instead of the natural logarithm but keep \( k \) derived from natural logs, how will their calculated age compare to the true age?

🔹 A. The calculated age will be approximately 2.3 times too large.
🔹 B. The calculated age will be approximately 2.3 times too small.
🔹 C. The calculated age will be exactly half the true age.
🔹 D. The calculated age will be unaffected because logarithms are proportional.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The correct formula uses \( \ln(P) \). Using \( \log_{10}(P) \) introduces a factor of \( \ln(10) \approx 2.3026 \) because \( \ln(P) = \ln(10) \log_{10}(P) \). Since \( \ln(P) \) is negative for fractions, replacing it with the smaller magnitude \( \log_{10}(P) \) results in a calculated \( t \) that is smaller by a factor of \( \ln(10) \). Thus, the result is roughly 2.3 times too small. This highlights the critical importance of consistency between logarithmic bases and rate constants in exponential modeling.

Q6. Which graph best represents the rate of change \( dy/dt \) versus time \( t \) for a quantity undergoing exponential decay \( y = y_0 e^{-kt} \)?

📖 Explanation:

For exponential decay \( y = y_0 e^{-kt} \), the rate of change is \( dy/dt = -k y_0 e^{-kt} \). Since \( k > 0 \) and \( y_0 > 0 \), the rate is always negative. Q7. In Newton’s Law of Cooling, \( dT/dt = k(T - T_e) \). If an object cools from 90°C to 70°C in 10 minutes in a 20°C room, why can't we simply assume it will cool another 20°C in the next 10 minutes?

📖 Explanation:

Newton's Law states the rate depends on \( (T - T_e) \). Initially, the difference is 70°C; after 10 minutes, it is 50°C. Since the driving force decreases, the rate slows down. Linear extrapolation assumes constant rate, violating the differential equation's core principle. The second 20°C drop would take longer than the first because the average temperature difference during that interval is smaller. Understanding this non-linearity is crucial for correctly applying exponential models to thermal systems.

Q8. A bank offers continuous compounding at rate \( r \). A student derives the formula \( A = Pe^{rt} \) by solving \( dA/dt = rA \). If the bank instead compounds \( n \) times per year, the discrete model is \( A = P(1+r/n)^{nt} \). As \( n \to \infty \), which mathematical limit justifies the transition to the continuous model?

🔹 A. \( \lim_{n \to \infty} (1+n)^r = e \)
🔹 B. \( \lim_{x \to 0} (1+x)^{1/x} = e \)
🔹 C. \( \lim_{n \to \infty} (1+r/n)^n = e^r \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

The definition of the exponential function arises from the limit \( \lim_{n \to \infty} (1 + r/n)^n = e^r \). Consequently, \( \lim_{n \to \infty} (1 + r/n)^{nt} = [(1 + r/n)^n]^t \to (e^r)^t = e^{rt} \). This specific limit connects discrete financial mathematics with continuous calculus models. Recognizing this foundational limit is essential for understanding why continuous compounding formulas take their specific exponential form and validates the use of differential equations in finance.

Q9. Suppose a population follows \( y' = ky \) but experiences a sudden catastrophic event at \( t=5 \) that instantly reduces the population by 50%. How should the solution be constructed for \( t > 5 \)?

🔹 A. Continue using the original function \( y_0 e^{kt} \) since exponential functions are continuous.
🔹 B. Use \( 0.5 y(5) e^{k(t-5)} \) as the new initial condition resets the clock.
🔹 C. Subtract \( 0.5 y_0 \) from the original exponential function.
🔹 D. Multiply the original growth constant \( k \) by 0.5 for \( t > 5 \).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Exponential models are memoryless; future evolution depends only on current state. At \( t=5 \), the population becomes \( y_{new} = 0.5 y(5) \). For \( t > 5 \), the system restarts with this new initial value. The solution is \( y(t) = y_{new} e^{k(t-5)} \). Simply subtracting a constant violates the proportionality of growth. Changing \( k \) alters the biological mechanism, not just the census count. Piecewise construction with reset initial conditions is the standard method for handling impulsive perturbations in ODEs.

Given an initial condition \( y(0) = y_0 > 0 \), continuity ensures \( y(t) \) never crosses zero. Therefore, \( |y| = y \) is justified by the domain constraints of the application. Q11. Two isotopes decay simultaneously in a sample. Isotope X has half-life 2 days; Isotope Y has half-life 8 days. Initially, there are equal numbers of atoms. After how many days will the number of X atoms be exactly 1/8th the number of Y atoms?

Let \( N_X = N_0 (1/2)^{t/2} \) and \( N_Y = N_0 (1/2)^{t/8} \). We want \( N_X / N_Y = 1/8 \). The ratio simplifies to \( (1/2)^{t/2 - t/8} = (1/2)^{3t/8} \). Set \( (1/2)^{3t/8} = (1/2)^3 \). Equating exponents: \( 3t/8 = 3 \Rightarrow t = 8 \). This problem requires manipulating exponential bases with different rates. A common mistake is averaging half-lives or subtracting them linearly. Q12. A student claims that if a quantity doubles every 10 years, then in 5 years it must increase by exactly 50%. What is the flaw in this reasoning?

🔹 A. They assumed linear growth instead of exponential growth.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

Exponential growth is geometric, not arithmetic. If \( y(10) = 2y(0) \), then \( y(t) = y(0) 2^{t/10} \). At \( t=5 \), \( y(5) = y(0) 2^{0.5} \approx 1.414 y(0) \), which is a 41.4% increase, not 50%. The student incorrectly interpolated linearly between \( t=0 \) and \( t=10 \). This misconception arises from intuitive but incorrect assumptions about 'average' behavior in nonlinear systems. Identifying this error demonstrates deep understanding of the convexity inherent in exponential functions.

Q13. In carbon dating, the decay constant \( k \) is typically determined from the half-life \( T \). If experimental uncertainty causes the measured half-life to be overestimated by 5%, how does this affect the calculated age of a sample with fixed remaining fraction \( P \)?

🔹 A. The calculated age will be underestimated by approximately 5%.
🔹 B. The calculated age will be overestimated by approximately 5%.
🔹 C. The calculated age will be overestimated by exactly 10%.
🔹 D. The calculated age remains unchanged because \( P \) compensates.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Age is given by \( t = -\frac{T}{\ln 2} \ln P \). Here, \( t \) is directly proportional to \( T \). If \( T \) is overestimated by 5% (i.e., \( T_{meas} = 1.05 T_{true} \)), then \( t_{calc} = 1.05 t_{true} \). Thus, the age is also overestimated by 5%. This linear sensitivity contrasts with other parameters where relationships might be inverse or logarithmic. Understanding error propagation in dating formulas is critical for evaluating archaeological claims and scientific reliability.

🔹 C. Wrong base for exponential.
🔹 D. Misread initial condition.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Positive growth rate \( k=2 \) requires positive exponent \( e^{2t} \). Negative exponent implies decay. This is the most frequent sign error in setting up models. Checking limiting behavior (\( t \to \infty \)) would reveal inconsistency: growth problem shouldn't vanish. Developing habit of sanity-checking asymptotic behavior catches such errors instantly. Explanation focuses on diagnostic strategy, not just correction.

Q30. If a quantity decays to 1/e of its initial value in 5 hours, what is its half-life?

🔹 A. \( 5 \ln 2 \)
🔹 B. \( 5 / \ln 2 \)
🔹 C. \( \ln 2 / 5 \)
🔹 D. \( 5 e \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Time to reach \( 1/e \) is the time constant \( \tau = 1/k \). Given \( \tau = 5 \), so \( k = 1/5 \). Half-life \( T = \ln 2 / k = 5 \ln 2 \). Distinguishing time constant from half-life is subtle but important in physics/engineering. Students often conflate \( 1/e \) time with half-life. Relationship \( T = \tau \ln 2 \) is fundamental. Tests fluency with multiple characteristic timescales in exponential processes.

Q31. In a mixing problem, if inflow rate exceeds outflow rate, why does the standard exponential approach fail?

🔹 A. Volume changes linearly, making concentration denominator time-dependent.
🔹 B. Salt enters faster than it leaves.
🔹 C. The differential equation becomes second-order.
🔹 D. Concentration becomes negative.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Standard model assumes constant volume. If \( V(t) = V_0 + (r_{in}-r_{out})t \), then \( dy/dt = rate_{in} - \frac{y}{V(t)} rate_{out} \). This yields linear ODE with variable coefficients, not simple separable exponential. Solution involves integrating factors with \( \ln(V(t)) \). Recognizing when constant-volume assumption breaks is critical modeling skill. Tests boundary of applicability for basic exponential methods.

Q32. Which statement about the function \( f(t) = e^{-t^2} \) is true regarding exponential decay models?

🔹 A. It is a valid exponential decay model with variable rate.
🔹 B. It decays faster than any exponential \( e^{-kt} \) as \( t \to \infty \).
🔹 C. It has a constant half-life.
🔹 D. It satisfies \( f' = -kf \).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

\( e^{-t^2} \) is Gaussian, not exponential. Exponential decay requires exponent linear in \( t \). Gaussian decays super-exponentially. It does NOT satisfy \( f' = -kf \) for constant \( k \); instead \( f' = -2tf \). Half-life isn't constant. Distinguishing exponential from other decaying functions prevents misapplication of exponential tools (like constant half-life rules) to inappropriate datasets. Vital for data literacy.

Q33. If \( y(t) = A e^{kt} + B e^{-kt} \), and \( y(0)=0 \), \( y'(0)=1 \), what is \( y(t) \)?

🔹 A. \( \sinh(kt)/k \)
🔹 B. \( \cosh(kt) \)
🔹 C. \( (e^{kt} - e^{-kt})/2 \)
🔹 D. \( k \sinh(t) \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Conditions: \( A+B=0 \Rightarrow B=-A \). \( y'(t) = Ak e^{kt} - Bk e^{-kt} \). \( y'(0) = k(A-B) = 1 \). Substitute \( B=-A \): \( k(2A)=1 \Rightarrow A=1/(2k) \). Thus \( y = \frac{1}{2k}(e^{kt} - e^{-kt}) = \frac{\sinh(kt)}{k} \). Combines exponential models with hyperbolic functions. Tests algebraic manipulation of dual-exponential systems arising in boundary value problems or symmetric physical systems. Higher complexity than single exponential.

Q35. Given \( y' = ky \), if \( y(2) = 4 \) and \( y(4) = 9 \), what is \( y(6) \)?

🔹 B. 16
🔹 C. 20.25
🔹 D. 25

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Values at equally spaced points form geometric sequence: \( y(2), y(4), y(6) \). Ratio \( r = 9/4 = 2.25 \). Next term: \( 9 \times 2.25 = 20.25 \). Q36. In pharmacokinetics, if drug elimination follows \( y' = -ky \) but absorption is instantaneous, peak concentration occurs at t=0. If absorption is also exponential \( y' = k_a D e^{-k_a t} - k y \), why is this NOT pure exponential decay?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Bateman function involves difference of exponentials. Pure decay only describes elimination phase post-absorption. Modeling full PK curve requires sum/difference of exponentials. Q37. If you double the initial amount \( y_0 \) in an exponential decay model, what happens to the half-life?

Q38. A student computes age using \( t = \frac{\ln(N_0/N)}{k} \). They measure \( N \) with 1% error. Approximate percentage error in \( t \) if \( N/N_0 = 0.5 \)?

🔹 A. 0.01
🔹 B. 0.0144
🔹 C. 0.0069
🔹 D. 0.02

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

\( t = -\ln(f)/k \) where \( f=N/N_0 \). \( dt/df = -1/(fk) \). Relative error \( \Delta t / t \approx |dt/df| \Delta f / t = [1/(fk)] \Delta f / [-\ln f / k] = \Delta f / (f |\ln f|) \). At \( f=0.5 \), \( |\ln 0.5| = \ln 2 \approx 0.693 \). Error amplification factor = \( 1/(0.5 \times 0.693) \approx 2.88 \). Wait, 1% error in N → 2.88% error in t? Let me recheck. Actually, \( \Delta t = \Delta N / (kN) \). \( t = \ln(N_0/N)/k \). Ratio \( \Delta t / t = \Delta N / (N \ln(N_0/N)) \). With \( \Delta N/N = 0.01 \) and \( \ln 2 \approx 0.693 \), ratio = \( 0.01 / 0.693 \approx 0.0144 \) or 1.44%. Correct. Shows error magnification near half-life.

Q39. Which initial value problem has solution \( y = 5 e^{3t} + 2 e^{-3t} \)?

Q41. In continuous compounding, if rate \( r \) is negative (depreciation), what is the limiting value of asset value as \( t \to \infty \)?
