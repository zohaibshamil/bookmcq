What is Integrating factor method differential equations?

Definition:
The integrating factor method solves linear first-order ODEs y′+P(x)y=Q(x)y' + P(x)y = Q(x)y′+P(x)y=Q(x) by multiplying by μ(x)=e∫P(x)dx\mu(x) = e^{\int P(x)dx}μ(x)=e∫P(x)dx, converting left side to derivative of product ddx[μy]\frac{d}{dx}[\mu y]dxd​[μy].

Example:
For y′−yx=xy' - \frac{y}{x} = xy′−xy​=x, μ=e−∫1xdx=1x\mu = e^{-\int \frac{1}{x}dx} = \frac{1}{x}μ=e−∫x1​dx=x1​. Then ddx[yx]=1\frac{d}{dx}[\frac{y}{x}] = 1dxd​[xy​]=1, so y=x2+Cxy = x^2 + Cxy=x2+Cx.

Reason:
This powerful technique systematically solves all first-order linear equations, making it essential tool for differential equations coursework.
