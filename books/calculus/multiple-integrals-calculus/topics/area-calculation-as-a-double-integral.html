What is Area Calculation as a Double Integral?

Definition:
The area of a region RRR in the xy-plane can be calculated by integrating the constant function f(x,y)=1f(x, y) = 1f(x,y)=1 over RRR: Area=∬R1 dA\text{Area} = \iint_R 1 \, dAArea=∬R​1dA.

Example:
The area of the triangle with vertices (0,0), (1,0), (1,1) is ∫01∫0x1 dy dx=∫01x dx=12\int_0^1 \int_0^x 1 \, dy \, dx = \int_0^1 x \, dx = \frac{1}{2}∫01​∫0x​1dydx=∫01​xdx=21​.

Reason:
This unifies the concept of area with volume calculation, showing that area is simply the volume under the flat surface z=1z=1z=1, providing a consistent framework for geometric measurements.

Q2. Why does the double integral ∬R1 dA\iint_R 1\,dA∬R​1dA measure the area of a region RRR, even though no explicit geometric area formula appears in the expression?

A.Because every small element dAdAdA contributes exactly one unit of area density ✅
B.Because dAdAdA represents the boundary length of RRR
C.Because the integrand 111 automatically finds the perimeter
D.Because double integration always produces area regardless of the integrand
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The differential dAdAdA represents a small area element, while the integrand 111 assigns unit density to every point in the region. Summing these infinitesimal contributions over RRR therefore gives the total area. A different integrand would generally represent a weighted quantity rather than ordinary area.
