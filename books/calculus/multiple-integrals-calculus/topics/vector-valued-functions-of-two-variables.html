What is Vector valued functions of two variables?

Definition:
A vector-valued function of two variables maps a pair \( (u,v) \) to a vector in 3D space: \( \mathbf{r}(u,v) = \langle x(u,v), y(u,v), z(u,v) \rangle \).

Example:
The function \( \mathbf{r}(u,v) = \langle u+v, u-v, uv \rangle \) defines a hyperbolic paraboloid surface.

Reason:
These functions provide a flexible way to define surfaces, enabling the use of vector calculus tools like partial derivatives and cross products for geometric analysis.

Q1. A vector field is defined as \( \mathbf{F}(x,y) = (P(x,y), Q(x,y)) \) where \( P(x,y) = x^2 y \) and \( Q(x,y) = y^2 x \). Which of the following correctly describes the divergence of \( \mathbf{F} \) at the point (1,2)?

A.10
B.6 ✅
C.4
D.8
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The divergence is computed as \( \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial Q}{\partial y} = 2xy + 2yx = 4xy \). At (1,2), this equals 8. Option B is correct. Common errors include computing the gradient instead of divergence, or evaluating partial derivatives incorrectly as \( x^2 + y^2 \) leading to 5, which is not listed. This tests the definition and application of divergence.

Q3. A fluid flows according to \( \mathbf{F}(x,y) = (-y, x) \). A student claims the flow is irrotational because the vectors rotate around the origin. Which error analysis best describes this misconception?

A.The student confused rotation of vectors with curl; curl is zero here, so the claim is actually correct.
B.The student confused rotation of vectors with curl; curl is not zero, so the claim is incorrect.
C.The student miscomputed partial derivatives; curl is \( -2 \).
D.The student miscomputed partial derivatives; curl is \( +2 \). ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: For \( \mathbf{F} = (-y, x) \), \( P = -y \), \( Q = x \). Curl = \( \partial Q/\partial x - \partial P/\partial y = 1 - (-1) = 2 \). So the flow has non-zero curl, meaning it is rotational. The student's error is thinking that the visual circular motion implies irrotationality. In reality, curl measures local rotation, not global path shape. Option D correctly identifies the computed value. This tests the distinction between global and local rotation.

Q4. Consider the vector-valued function \( \mathbf{r}(u,v) = (u^2 - v^2, 2uv) \) mapping a region in the \( uv \)-plane to the \( xy \)-plane. If \( u \) and \( v \) are polar-like coordinates, what is the Jacobian determinant of this transformation?

A.\( 4(u^2 + v^2) \) ✅
B.\( 4(u^2 - v^2) \)
C.\( 2(u^2 + v^2) \)
D.\( -4uv \)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Jacobian matrix is \( \begin{pmatrix} 2u & -2v \\ 2v & 2u \end{pmatrix} \). Its determinant is \( (2u)(2u) - (-2v)(2v) = 4u^2 + 4v^2 = 4(u^2+v^2) \). This is positive for all non-zero \( u,v \), indicating orientation preservation. This transformation is essentially the complex square function. Option A is correct. Common mistakes include forgetting the cross terms or sign errors.

Q5. A parametric surface is given by \( \mathbf{r}(u,v) = (u, v, u^2 + v^2) \). Which of the following integrals correctly computes the surface area over the rectangle \( 0 \le u \le 1, 0 \le v \le 1 \)?

A.\( \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \sqrt{1 + 4u^2 + 4v^2} \, du\,dv \) ✅
B.\( \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \sqrt{4u^2 + 4v^2} \, du\,dv \)
C.\( \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \sqrt{1 + u^2 + v^2} \, du\,dv \)
D.\( \int_0^1 \int_0^1 (1 + 2u + 2v) \, du\,dv \)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The surface area element is \( |\mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v| \). Here \( \mathbf{r}_u = (1,0,2u) \), \( \mathbf{r}_v = (0,1,2v) \). Cross product = \( (-2u, -2v, 1) \), magnitude = \( \sqrt{4u^2+4v^2+1} \). So option A is correct. Option B misses the '1' from the vertical component. Option C has wrong coefficients. This requires multi-step reasoning: partial derivatives, cross product, norm, then setting up the double integral.

Q6. The vector field \( \mathbf{F}(x,y) = (2x, 2y) \) represents a radial expansion. A student computes the flux through the unit circle using the divergence theorem and gets \( 4\pi \). Another student computes directly and gets \( 2\pi \). Who is correct and why?

A.First student is correct; divergence is 4, area is \( \pi \), so flux = \( 4\pi \). ✅
B.Second student is correct; direct computation gives \( 2\pi \).
C.Both are incorrect; correct flux is \( 8\pi \).
D.Both are correct due to different parameterizations.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Divergence of \( \mathbf{F} = 2+2 = 4 \). By divergence theorem, flux = \( \int \int \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F} \, dA = 4 \times \text{area} = 4\pi \). Direct computation with outward normal also yields \( 4\pi \). The second student likely forgot the Jacobian or used wrong normal. So option A is correct. This tests understanding of the divergence theorem and common computational errors in flux integrals.

Q8. The transformation \( T(u,v) = (u+v, u-v) \) maps a square in the \( uv \)-plane to a rotated square in the \( xy \)-plane. What is the area scaling factor at any point?

A.2 ✅
B.1
C.\( \sqrt{2} \)
D.4
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Jacobian determinant is \( \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = -2 \). Absolute value is 2. So area scales by 2 everywhere. This is constant because the transformation is linear. Option A is correct. Students often compute the determinant incorrectly as 0 or forget absolute value. This tests the geometric meaning of the Jacobian as a local area scaling factor.

Q9. A vector field is given by \( \mathbf{F}(x,y) = (x^2 + y^2, x^2 - y^2) \). At which point is the divergence equal to the curl (scalar curl)?

Q10. Consider the vector-valued function \( \mathbf{r}(u,v) = (u \cos v, u \sin v, v) \) which represents a helicoid. What is the magnitude of the cross product \( \mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v \) at \( u=1, v=0 \)?

A.\( \sqrt{2} \) ✅
B.1
C.2
D.\( \sqrt{3} \)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Compute \( \mathbf{r}_u = (\cos v, \sin v, 0) \), \( \mathbf{r}_v = (-u \sin v, u \cos v, 1) \). Cross product = \( (\sin v \cdot 1 - 0, 0 - \cos v \cdot 1, \cos v \cdot u \cos v - \sin v \cdot (-u \sin v)) \) = \( (\sin v, -\cos v, u) \). Magnitude = \( \sqrt{\sin^2 v + \cos^2 v + u^2} = \sqrt{1+u^2} \). At u=1, v=0, magnitude = \( \sqrt{2} \). So option A. This tests surface parameterization and cross product computation.

Q11. A student attempts to find the potential function for \( \mathbf{F}(x,y) = (2xy, x^2 + 2y) \) and writes \( f(x,y) = x^2 y + y^2 \). Which of the following is the correct error analysis?

D.The student is incorrect; the field is not conservative.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Check \( \partial f/\partial x = 2xy \) matches P, and \( \partial f/\partial y = x^2 + 2y \) matches Q. So the student's expression is correct up to an additive constant. Option B correctly notes that any constant can be added. Option A is technically correct but incomplete; the presence of '+C' is important. The field is conservative (curl = \( 2x - 2x = 0 \)). So the best answer is B, as it highlights the arbitrary constant. This tests the concept of potential functions and the constant of integration.

Q13. A transformation \( T(u,v) = (u^2 - v^2, 2uv) \) is applied to the rectangle \( 0 \le u \le 1, 0 \le v \le 1 \). What is the area of the image region in the \( xy \)-plane?

A.\( \int_0^1 \int_0^1 4(u^2+v^2) \, du\,dv = 8/3 \) ✅
B.\( \int_0^1 \int_0^1 2(u^2+v^2) \, du\,dv = 4/3 \)
C.\( \int_0^1 \int_0^1 \sqrt{4u^2+4v^2} \, du\,dv \)
D.\( \int_0^1 \int_0^1 4uv \, du\,dv = 1 \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The Jacobian determinant is \( 4(u^2+v^2) \) as computed earlier. Area = \( \int_0^1 \int_0^1 4(u^2+v^2) \, du\,dv = 4[\int_0^1 u^2 du \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot \int_0^1 v^2 dv] = 4(1/3+1/3)=8/3 \). Option A is correct. This requires computing the Jacobian, setting up the integral, and evaluating. Option B misses factor 2, C is wrong norm, D is wrong cross term. This is multi-step and Olympiad-style due to exact integration and transformation.

Q14. Given \( \mathbf{F}(x,y) = (y, x) \), which of the following is the correct line integral from (0,0) to (1,1) along the straight line \( y=x \)?

A.1 ✅
B.0
C.2
D.\( \sqrt{2} \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Parameterize \( r(t) = (t,t), t:0\to1 \). Then \( \mathbf{F}(r(t)) = (t, t) \), \( r'(t) = (1,1) \). Dot product = \( t+t = 2t \). Integral = \( \int_0^1 2t dt = 1 \). Since the field is conservative with potential \( xy \), the integral equals \( f(1,1)-f(0,0)=1-0=1 \). Option A is correct. Common error: using wrong parameterization or forgetting dot product. This tests both direct computation and use of potential function.
