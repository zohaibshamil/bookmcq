What is Triple Integrals in Spherical Coordinates?

Definition:
In spherical coordinates, x=ρsin⁡ϕcos⁡θ,y=ρsin⁡ϕsin⁡θ,z=ρcos⁡ϕx=\rho\sin\phi\cos\theta, y=\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta, z=\rho\cos\phix=ρsinϕcosθ,y=ρsinϕsinθ,z=ρcosϕ, and dV=ρ2sin⁡ϕ dρ dϕ dθdV = \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\thetadV=ρ2sinϕdρdϕdθ.

Example:
Integrating over a sphere of radius RRR: ∫02π∫0π∫0Rf(ρ,ϕ,θ) ρ2sin⁡ϕ dρ dϕ dθ\int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^\pi \int_0^R f(\rho,\phi,\theta) \, \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta∫02π​∫0π​∫0R​f(ρ,ϕ,θ)ρ2sinϕdρdϕdθ.

Reason:
The factor ρ2sin⁡ϕ\rho^2\sin\phiρ2sinϕ is the Jacobian, and these coordinates are ideal for spheres, cones, and other radially symmetric objects.
