What is Triple Integrals in Calculus?

Definition:
A triple integral extends the concept of double integrals to three dimensions, integrating a function \( f(x,y,z) \) over a 3D region \( E \): \( \iiint_E f(x,y,z) \, dV \).

Example:
The mass of a solid with density \( \rho(x,y,z) \) is \( M = \iiint_E \rho(x,y,z) \, dV \).

Reason:
Triple integrals allow us to calculate physical quantities like mass, charge, and average value over 3D volumes, essential for physics and engineering applications.

Q1. A cylindrical region has density \( \rho(r,\theta,z)=z \). If the total mass is computed as \( M=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{h} z \, r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta \), a student claims mass is independent of \( h \) because \( z \) cancels. Which error analysis is correct?

A. The student integrated \( z \) incorrectly; the inner integral is \( h^2/2 \), so mass depends on \( h^2 \). ✅
B. The student forgot the Jacobian \( r \); without it mass is independent of \( h \).
C. The student used wrong limits; \( z \) should go from 0 to \( 2\pi \).
D. The student is correct; the \( r \, dz \) makes mass independent of \( h \).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The inner integral \( \int_0^h z \, dz = h^2/2 \), so \( M = \pi h^2 \). The misconception arises from treating \( z \) as a constant. The Jacobian \( r \) is correctly included; the error is purely in evaluating the \( z \)-integral. Thus mass strongly depends on \( h^2 \).

Q2. A solid occupies the region bounded by \( z=4-x^2-y^2 \) and \( z=0 \). A student sets up \( \int_{-2}^{2}\int_{-\sqrt{4-x^2}}^{\sqrt{4-x^2}}\int_{0}^{4-x^2-y^2} dz \, dy \, dx \). What is the most efficient method to verify the volume?

A. Convert to cylindrical: \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^2\int_0^{4-r^2} r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta \) ✅
B. Convert to spherical: \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}\int_0^2 \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta \)
C. Keep Cartesian but swap \( dz \) and \( dx \)
D. Use divergence theorem on the surface

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The region is a paraboloid of revolution. Cylindrical symmetry makes the \( r \)-integral \( \int_0^2 r(4-r^2)dr = 4 \), times \( 2\pi \) gives \( 8\pi \). Spherical is messy because the surface is not a sphere. Cartesian works but is more tedious. The question tests recognition of coordinate system efficiency.

Q3. A tetrahedron has vertices at \( (0,0,0), (1,0,0), (0,1,0), (0,0,1) \). The integral \( I=\int_0^1\int_0^{1-x}\int_0^{1-x-y} f(x,y,z) \, dz \, dy \, dx \) is given. If the order is changed to \( dz \, dx \, dy \), what are the new limits for \( x \)?

A. \( x \) from 0 to \( 1-y \) ✅
B. \( x \) from 0 to \( 1-y-z \)
C. \( x \) from 0 to \( 1-y \) and \( z \) from 0 to \( 1-x-y \)
D. \( x \) from 0 to \( 1-y \) and \( z \) from 0 to \( 1-x-y \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The tetrahedron is defined by \( x\ge0,y\ge0,z\ge0,x+y+z\le1 \). For order \( dz \, dx \, dy \), outer \( y \) from 0 to 1, middle \( x \) from 0 to \( 1-y \), inner \( z \) from 0 to \( 1-x-y \). So \( x \) limits are \( 0 \) to \( 1-y \). Option C is same as A but duplicates; A is correct. This requires reconstructing the region from limits.

Q4. Consider the integral \( \int_0^1 \int_0^{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \int_0^{\sqrt{1-x^2-y^2}} dz \, dy \, dx \). Which graph correctly describes the region?

A. A quarter of a sphere of radius 1 in the first octant ✅
B. A full sphere of radius 1
C. A cylinder of radius 1 and height 1
D. A cone with vertex at origin

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The upper limit on \( z \) is \( \sqrt{1-x^2-y^2} \), and \( x,y \) are in the first quadrant quarter circle. This is exactly the portion of the unit sphere where \( x\ge0, y\ge0, z\ge0 \). The graph would show a spherical octant. This tests visualization of 3D regions from integral limits.

Q5. A student evaluates \( \int_0^1 \int_0^x \int_0^{x+y} dz \, dy \, dx \) and gets \( 1/6 \). Another student changes order to \( dz \, dx \, dy \) and gets \( 1/3 \). Which is correct and why?

A. First is correct; \( 1/6 \) is correct because region is \( 0\le y\le x\le1, 0\le z\le x+y \)
B. Second is correct; \( 1/3 \) is correct because limits for \( x \) should be \( y \) to 1
C. Both are incorrect; correct value is \( 1/4 \) ✅
D. Both are correct because Fubini's theorem allows any order

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The direct computation gives \( \int_0^1 \int_0^x (x+y) dy dx = \int_0^1 (3x^2/2) dx = 1/2 \). The second student's order also gives 1/2 if limits are correctly transformed. The first student's 1/6 and second's 1/3 are arithmetic errors. This question tests the ability to detect and correct errors in order switching.

Q6. The volume of a solid is given by \( V=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/4}\int_0^{2} \rho^2\sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta \). What is the shape of the solid?

A. A spherical cone with radius 2 and angle \( \pi/4 \) ✅
B. A hemisphere of radius 2
C. A cylinder of radius 2 and height 2
D. A cone of height 2 and base radius 2

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Spherical coordinates: \( \rho \) from 0 to 2, \( \phi \) from 0 to \( \pi/4 \), \( \theta \) full circle. This is a spherical sector (a cone-shaped region) with apex at origin, radius 2, and half-angle \( \pi/4 \). Not a full hemisphere because \( \phi \) stops at \( \pi/4 \). This tests recognition of spherical coordinate limits.

Q7. A solid has density \( \delta(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2 \). The moment of inertia about z-axis is \( I_z=\int\int\int (x^2+y^2)\delta \, dV \). Which integral in cylindrical coordinates is correct?

A. \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^R\int_0^H r^2 \cdot r \cdot r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta \)
B. \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^R\int_0^H r^2 \cdot r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta \)
C. \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^R\int_0^H r^2 \cdot r^2 \, dz \, dr \, d\theta \)
D. \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^R\int_0^H r^2 \cdot r^3 \, dz \, dr \, d\theta \) ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Moment of inertia about z-axis requires \( (x^2+y^2) \) times density \( \delta \). In cylindrical, \( x^2+y^2=r^2 \), \( \delta=r^2 \), and \( dV=r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta \). So total integrand is \( r^2 \cdot r^2 \cdot r = r^5 \). Option D correctly represents this as \( r^2 \cdot r^3 \).

Q8. The integral \( \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-z} \int_0^{1-y-z} dx \, dy \, dz \) represents the volume of a tetrahedron. What is the volume?

A. \( 1/6 \) ✅
B. \( 1/3 \)
C. \( 1/2 \)
D. \( 1 \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The region is \( x\ge0,y\ge0,z\ge0, x+y+z\le1 \). The volume of this standard tetrahedron is \( 1/6 \). This is a classic result. The integral evaluates to \( \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-z} (1-y-z) dy dz = \int_0^1 (1-z)^2/2 dz = 1/6 \).

Q9. A student sets up a triple integral for the volume of a sphere of radius R using spherical coordinates as \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi}\int_0^R \rho^2 \sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta \). They then claim the volume is \( \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 \). Another student uses \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi}\int_0^R \rho^2 \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta \) and gets \( 2\pi R^3/3 \). Which is correct and what is the error?

A. First is correct; second forgot \( \sin\phi \) Jacobian ✅
B. Second is correct; first has extra \( \sin\phi \)
C. Both are correct because they use different coordinate systems
D. Neither; volume of sphere is \( \pi R^3 \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Spherical volume element is \( \rho^2 \sin\phi \, d\rho \, d\phi \, d\theta \). Omitting \( \sin\phi \) is a common mistake. The first integral correctly includes it and yields \( 4/3\pi R^3 \). The second yields \( 2\pi R^3/3 \), which is the volume of a cylinder in spherical coordinates without the angular correction. This tests the crucial Jacobian factor.

Q10. A solid is bounded by \( z=0 \), \( z=4-y^2 \), and \( x^2+y^2=4 \). Which of the following correctly sets up the volume integral in cylindrical coordinates?

A. \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^2\int_0^{4-r^2\sin^2\theta} r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta \) ✅
B. \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^2\int_0^{4-r^2} r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta \)
C. \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^4\int_0^{4-r^2} r \, dz \, dr \, d\theta \)
D. \( \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^2\int_0^{4-y^2} dz \, dy \, dx \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The cylinder \( x^2+y^2=4 \) becomes \( r=2 \). The paraboloid \( z=4-y^2 \) becomes \( z=4-r^2\sin^2\theta \). So the integrand is \( r \), limits: \( r:0\to2 \), \( \theta:0\to2\pi \), \( z:0\to4-r^2\sin^2\theta \). Option A is correct. Option B incorrectly uses \( 4-r^2 \) which would be a paraboloid of revolution, but here it's parabolic in y only.

Q11. The average value of \( f(x,y,z)=x \) over the unit sphere is 0. A student argues that because the sphere is symmetric, the average of \( x^2 \) is also 0. Which is correct?

A. The student is wrong; \( x^2 \) is always positive so average is positive ✅
B. The student is correct by symmetry
C. The average of \( x^2 \) is 0 only if the sphere is centered at origin
D. The average of \( x^2 \) is 0 because \( x^2 \) is odd

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The average of \( x \) is 0 because \( x \) is an odd function over a symmetric domain. However \( x^2 \) is even and non-negative, so its average is \( 1/3 \) (by symmetry, \( \bar{x^2}=\bar{y^2}=\bar{z^2} \) and sum = 1). The student confuses oddness with evenness. This tests symmetry arguments and expectation values.

Q12. A solid is formed by intersecting the cylinder \( x^2+y^2=1 \) and the planes \( z=0 \) and \( z=x+2 \). A student computes volume as \( \int_{-1}^{1}\int_{-\sqrt{1-x^2}}^{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\int_0^{x+2} dz \, dy \, dx \). Is this correct? If not, what is the error?

A. Correct ✅
B. Incorrect: \( z \) lower limit should be \( x+2 \) and upper 0
C. Incorrect: the cylinder radius is 2, not 1
D. Incorrect: the order of integration should be \( dz \, dx \, dy \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The cylinder is \( x^2+y^2=1 \), so \( x \) from -1 to 1, \( y \) from \( -\sqrt{1-x^2} \) to \( \sqrt{1-x^2} \), and \( z \) from 0 to \( x+2 \). Since \( x+2 \ge 1 \) for all \( x\in[-1,1] \), the lower limit 0 is always below the upper, so the setup is correct. The integral gives volume \( \int_{-1}^{1}\int_{-\sqrt{1-x^2}}^{\sqrt{1-x^2}} (x+2) dy dx = \int_{-1}^{1} 2\sqrt{1-x^2}(x+2) dx = 2\int_{-1}^{1} x\sqrt{1-x^2} dx + 4\int_{-1}^{1}\sqrt{1-x^2} dx = 0 + 4\cdot(\pi/2) = 2\pi \). So correct. This is a multi-step reasoning problem combining geometry and integration.

Q13. The integral \( \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_0^{1-x-y} f(x,y,z) \, dz \, dy \, dx \) is given. If \( f(x,y,z)=1 \), the value is 1/6. If \( f(x,y,z)=x \), what is the value?

A. \( 1/24 \) ✅
B. \( 1/12 \)
C. \( 1/6 \)
D. \( 1/8 \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The tetrahedron region is \( x+y+z\le1 \). We need \( \int\int\int x \, dV \). By symmetry, \( \int x = \int y = \int z \), and sum \( \int (x+y+z) = \int 1 = 1/6 \). Since each is equal, each is \( (1/6)/3 = 1/18 \). Wait, that gives 1/18, not in options. Let's compute directly: \( \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_0^{1-x-y} x \, dz \, dy \, dx = \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} x(1-x-y) dy dx = \int_0^1 x[(1-x)^2/2] dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^1 x(1-2x+x^2) dx = \frac{1}{2}[\frac{1}{2} - \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4}] = \frac{1}{2}[\frac{6-8+3}{12}] = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{12} = \frac{1}{24} \). So correct is 1/24. This tests expectation values and symmetry.

Q14. A student attempts to find the volume of the region inside the sphere \( x^2+y^2+z^2=4 \) and above the cone \( z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2} \) using spherical coordinates. They set \( \phi \) from 0 to \( \pi/4 \) and \( \rho \) from 0 to 2. What is the error?

A. They should set \( \phi \) from 0 to \( \pi/4 \) and \( \rho \) from 0 to \( 2/\cos\phi \)
B. They should set \( \phi \) from \( \pi/4 \) to \( \pi/2 \)
C. They should set \( \phi \) from 0 to \( \pi/4 \) and \( \rho \) from 0 to 2 is correct ✅
D. They should set \( \phi \) from 0 to \( \pi/2 \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The cone \( z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2} \) has spherical equation \( \phi=\pi/4 \). The region inside the sphere and above the cone corresponds to \( 0\le\phi\le\pi/4 \) and \( 0\le\rho\le2 \). So the student's limits are correct. No error. This tests the correct interpretation of 'above' in spherical coordinates.
