What is Fubini's theorem for double integrals?

Definition:
Fubini's Theorem states that if f(x,y) f(x, y) f(x,y) is continuous on a rectangular region R=[a,b]×[c,d] R = [a, b] \times [c, d] R=[a,b]×[c,d], then ∬Rf(x,y) dA=∫ab∫cdf(x,y) dy dx=∫cd∫abf(x,y) dx dy \iint_R f(x, y) \, dA = \int_a^b \int_c^d f(x, y) \, dy \, dx = \int_c^d \int_a^b f(x, y) \, dx \, dy ∬R​f(x,y)dA=∫ab​∫cd​f(x,y)dydx=∫cd​∫ab​f(x,y)dxdy.

Example:
For f(x,y)=x+y f(x,y) = x+y f(x,y)=x+y on [0,1]×[0,1] [0,1]\times[0,1] [0,1]×[0,1], integrating first with respect to y y y or first with respect to x x x yields the same result: 1 1 1.

Reason:
This theorem guarantees that the order of integration does not affect the final value for continuous functions on rectangles, giving us flexibility to choose the easier order of calculation.
