What is Surface Area: Parametric Surfaces?

Definition:
The surface area of a parametric surface r(u,v)=⟨x(u,v),y(u,v),z(u,v)⟩ \mathbf{r}(u,v) = \langle x(u,v), y(u,v), z(u,v) \rangle r(u,v)=⟨x(u,v),y(u,v),z(u,v)⟩ is given by A=∬D∥ru×rv∥ dA A = \iint_D \| \mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v \| \, dA A=∬D​∥ru​×rv​∥dA, where D D D is the parameter domain.

Example:
For a sphere of radius R R R parametrized by spherical angles, the cross product magnitude is R2sin⁡ϕ R^2\sin\phi R2sinϕ, leading to area 4πR2 4\pi R^2 4πR2.

Reason:
This generalizes the concept of arc length to surfaces, allowing us to calculate the area of curved objects like spheres, cylinders, and complex engineering surfaces.
