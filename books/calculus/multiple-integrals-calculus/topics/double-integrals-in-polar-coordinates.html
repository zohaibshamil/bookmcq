What is Double Integrals in Polar Coordinates?

Definition:
In polar coordinates, x=rcos⁡θx=rcosθ, y=rsin⁡θy=rsinθ, and the area element becomes dA=r dr dθdA=rdrdθ. The double integral transforms to ∬Rf(rcos⁡θ,rsin⁡θ) r dr dθ∬R​f(rcosθ,rsinθ)rdrdθ.

Example:
To integrate f(x,y)=x2+y2f(x,y)=x2+y2 over the unit disk, we use ∫02π∫01(r2) r dr dθ=∫02πdθ∫01r3 dr=2π⋅14=π2∫02π​∫01​(r2)rdrdθ=∫02π​dθ∫01​r3dr=2π⋅41​=2π​.

Reason:
Polar coordinates simplify integrals over circular or radially symmetric regions, avoiding complex square root boundaries that appear in Cartesian coordinates.
