What is Properties of Double Integrals?

Definition:
Double integrals satisfy linearity and additivity properties: ∬R[af(x,y)+bg(x,y)] dA=a∬Rf(x,y) dA+b∬Rg(x,y) dA\iint_R [af(x,y) + bg(x,y)] \, dA = a\iint_R f(x,y) \, dA + b\iint_R g(x,y) \, dA∬R​[af(x,y)+bg(x,y)]dA=a∬R​f(x,y)dA+b∬R​g(x,y)dA and ∬R1∪R2f dA=∬R1f dA+∬R2f dA\iint_{R_1 \cup R_2} f \, dA = \iint_{R_1} f \, dA + \iint_{R_2} f \, dA∬R1​∪R2​​fdA=∬R1​​fdA+∬R2​​fdA if regions do not overlap.

Example:
If ∬Rx2 dA=5\iint_R x^2 \, dA = 5∬R​x2dA=5 and ∬Ry2 dA=3\iint_R y^2 \, dA = 3∬R​y2dA=3, then ∬R(2x2−y2) dA=2(5)−3=7\iint_R (2x^2 - y^2) \, dA = 2(5) - 3 = 7∬R​(2x2−y2)dA=2(5)−3=7.

Reason:
These properties allow us to simplify complex integrals by breaking them into smaller, manageable parts or combining known results, significantly reducing computational effort.

Q1. Suppose f(x,y)≥0f(x,y)\geq 0f(x,y)≥0 on a region RRR, and RRR is divided into two non-overlapping subregions R1R_1R1​ and R2R_2R2​ whose union is RRR. Which relationship must hold when the required integrals exist?

A.∬Rf dA=∬R1f dA−∬R2f dA\iint_R f\,dA=\iint_{R_1}f\,dA-\iint_{R_2}f\,dA∬R​fdA=∬R1​​fdA−∬R2​​fdA
B.∬Rf dA=∬R1f dA+∬R2f dA\iint_R f\,dA=\iint_{R_1}f\,dA+\iint_{R_2}f\,dA∬R​fdA=∬R1​​fdA+∬R2​​fdA ✅
C.∬Rf dA=∬R1∩R2f dA\iint_R f\,dA=\iint_{R_1\cap R_2}f\,dA∬R​fdA=∬R1​∩R2​​fdA
D.∬Rf dA=∬R1f dA∬R2f dA\iint_R f\,dA=\iint_{R_1}f\,dA\iint_{R_2}f\,dA∬R​fdA=∬R1​​fdA∬R2​​fdA
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The additive property states that an integral over a region can be separated into the sum of integrals over non-overlapping pieces whose union forms the original region. The nonnegative assumption is not actually required for this property, but it makes the interpretation especially intuitive as accumulated volume or mass.

Q2. For a constant ccc and an integrable function fff, which expression correctly describes the effect of multiplying the integrand by ccc?

A.The double integral is multiplied by ccc ✅
B.The double integral is multiplied by c2c^2c2
C.The constant disappears because integration only depends on the region
D.The double integral is divided by ccc
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Double integration is linear with respect to constant scaling. Therefore, multiplying every value of the integrand by ccc multiplies the accumulated integral by exactly ccc. The factor does not become c2c^2c2, because the scaling applies to function values rather than independently scaling the two coordinate directions.

Q3. A rectangular region RRR is split into R1R_1R1​ and R2R_2R2​. The same function fff is integrated over both pieces. A student claims that the larger subregion must always produce the larger integral. Which statement best evaluates the claim?

A.It is always true for every integrable function
B.It is true only when fff is positive and constant
C.It can be false because the values of fff may be much larger on the smaller region ✅
D.It is false only when fff changes sign
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Region size alone does not determine the value of a double integral. A smaller region can produce a larger integral if the function has substantially larger values there. The claim becomes reliable under additional conditions such as a nonnegative constant integrand, but not for an arbitrary integrable function.

Q4. Let RRR be a region and suppose f(x,y)≤g(x,y)f(x,y)\leq g(x,y)f(x,y)≤g(x,y) at every point of RRR. What conclusion follows most directly when both functions are integrable?

A.∬Rf dA≥∬Rg dA\iint_R f\,dA\geq\iint_R g\,dA∬R​fdA≥∬R​gdA
B.∬Rf dA≤∬Rg dA\iint_R f\,dA\leq\iint_R g\,dA∬R​fdA≤∬R​gdA ✅
C.The two integrals must be equal
D.Their difference must equal the area of RRR
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: If f≤gf\leq gf≤g pointwise throughout the same region, then the accumulated contribution from fff cannot exceed that from ggg. Integrating the inequality preserves its direction. Equality occurs only under additional conditions, such as when the functions agree except possibly on a set that does not affect the integral.

Q6. A rectangular plate has uniform surface density 555 units of mass per unit area and occupies a region of area 181818. Without evaluating any iterated integral, what is its total mass?

📖 Explanation: For uniform surface density, the mass is the integral of the density over the plate. No detailed integration is necessary.

Q7. A model predicts a quantity using I=∬R(f+g) dAI=\iint_R(f+g)\,dAI=∬R​(f+g)dA. The analyst already knows F=∬Rf dAF=\iint_R f\,dAF=∬R​fdA and G=∬Rg dAG=\iint_R g\,dAG=∬R​gdA. Which approach gives III without repeating the integration?

A.Compute I=FGI=FGI=FG
B.Compute I=F−GI=F-GI=F−G
C.Compute I=F+GI=F+GI=F+G ✅
D.Compute I=F/GI=F/GI=F/G
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The linearity property allows a sum inside the integrand to be separated into a sum of integrals over the same region. Therefore I=∬Rf dA+∬Rg dA=F+GI=\iint_Rf\,dA+\iint_Rg\,dA=F+GI=∬R​fdA+∬R​gdA=F+G. This is useful in modelling because independently computed contributions can be combined without integrating their sum again.

Q8. A student writes ∬R3f(x,y) dA=3∬Rf(x,y) dA\iint_R 3f(x,y)\,dA=3\iint_R f(x,y)\,dA∬R​3f(x,y)dA=3∬R​f(x,y)dA, but another student argues that the factor should become 999 because there are two variables. Who is correct, and why?

A.The second student, because both xxx and yyy contribute a factor of 333
B.The first student, because the constant multiplies the integrand only once ✅
C.Both are correct depending on the shape of RRR
D.Neither is correct because constants cannot be moved outside a double integral
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The factor 333 multiplies the function value at each point, so linearity pulls out exactly one factor of 333. The presence of two integration variables does not create another factor. A factor of 999 would arise only if the original integrand itself contained a factor 999, not merely because two integrations are performed.

Q9. A student argues: If f(x,y)f(x,y)f(x,y) is positive on R1R_1R1​ and negative on R2R_2R2​, then ∬R1∪R2f dA\iint_{R_1\cup R_2}f\,dA∬R1​∪R2​​fdA must be positive because R1R_1R1​ and R2R_2R2​ have equal areas. What is the flaw?

A.Equal areas guarantee equal integral magnitudes
B.The sign of the total depends on the magnitudes and distributions of fff, not only on region areas ✅
C.Negative values cannot occur inside a double integral
D.The integral over the union is always zero
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Equal areas do not imply equal contributions to the integral. If the positive values on R1R_1R1​ have small magnitude while the negative values on R2R_2R2​ have large magnitude, the total can be negative. Additivity permits the two contributions to be combined, but their signs and magnitudes determine the result.

Q10. Consider a graph of a function over two adjacent regions of equal area. Over R1R_1R1​, the surface lies mostly near height 222, while over R2R_2R2​, it lies mostly near height 666. Which comparison is most reasonable if the function remains nonnegative?

C.The two integrals must be equal because the areas are equal
D.Both integrals must be zero
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A double integral measures accumulated height over area. Since the two regions have equal area but the surface is substantially higher over R2R_2R2​, its accumulated value is expected to be larger. Equal areas alone would guarantee equal integrals only under stronger conditions, such as matching function behavior across the regions.

Q12. Two teams calculate the same integral over RRR. Team A obtains 121212. Team B divides RRR into three non-overlapping regions and obtains contributions 555, 444, and 333. Which conclusion is justified?

A.Team B must be wrong because subdivision changes the integral

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The additive property provides an independent consistency check. Since 5+4+3=125+4+3=125+4+3=12, Team B's calculation is consistent with Team A's result.

Q14. A function fff is nonnegative on a region RRR, which is split into two parts of equal area. The average value of fff on the first part is 333, while on the second part it is 777. What is the average value of fff over all of RRR?

A.-3
B.-5 ✅
C.-7
D.-10
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Because the two subregions have equal area, their contributions to the overall average are weighted equally. The total integral is the sum of the two regional integrals, each equal to its average multiplied by the common area. Thus the overall average is the weighted mean (3+7)/2=5(3+7)/2=5(3+7)/2=5, not the sum or either individual average.
