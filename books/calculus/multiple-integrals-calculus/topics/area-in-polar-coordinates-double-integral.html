What is Area in polar coordinates double integral?

Definition:
The area enclosed by a polar curve r=f(θ) r = f(\theta) r=f(θ) from θ=α \theta=\alpha θ=α to θ=β \theta=\beta θ=β is A=12∫αβ[f(θ)]2 dθ A = \frac{1}{2} \int_\alpha^\beta [f(\theta)]^2 \, d\theta A=21​∫αβ​[f(θ)]2dθ, derived from ∬Rr dr dθ \iint_R r \, dr \, d\theta ∬R​rdrdθ.

Example:
The area of one petal of r=cos⁡(2θ) r = \cos(2\theta) r=cos(2θ) is 12∫−π/4π/4cos⁡2(2θ) dθ=π8 \frac{1}{2} \int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} \cos^2(2\theta) \, d\theta = \frac{\pi}{8} 21​∫−π/4π/4​cos2(2θ)dθ=8π​.

Reason:
This formula provides a direct method for calculating areas of polar shapes without converting back to Cartesian coordinates, leveraging the simplicity of polar descriptions.
