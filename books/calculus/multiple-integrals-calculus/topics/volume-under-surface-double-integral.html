What is Volume under surface double integral?

Definition:
The volume of a solid region bounded above by a continuous surface z=f(x,y) z = f(x, y) z=f(x,y) and below by a region R R R in the xy-plane is given by the double integral V=∬Rf(x,y) dA V = \iint_R f(x, y) \, dA V=∬R​f(x,y)dA.

Example:
To find the volume under the plane z=4−x−y z = 4 - x - y z=4−x−y over the rectangle R=[0,1]×[0,2] R = [0, 1] \times [0, 2] R=[0,1]×[0,2], we evaluate ∫02∫01(4−x−y) dx dy \int_0^2 \int_0^1 (4 - x - y) \, dx \, dy ∫02​∫01​(4−x−y)dxdy.

Reason:
This geometric interpretation connects the abstract concept of integration to physical space, allowing us to calculate quantities like mass or charge when density varies.
