What is Change of Variables Formula for Double Integrals?

Definition:
∬R​f(x,y)dA=∬S​f(x(u,v),y(u,v))​∂(u,v)∂(x,y)​​dudv, where S is the pre-image of R.

Example:
Using u=x+y,v=x−y to simplify an integral over a parallelogram.

Reason:
This formula allows us to integrate in a coordinate system where the region or function is simpler, expanding the toolkit for solving complex integrals.

Q1. A transformation is given by x=u+v and y=u−v. At a point where u=2 and v=1, what is the absolute value of the Jacobian determinant needed in a double-integral transformation?

A.0
B.1 ✅
C.2
D.4
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Jacobian matrix is (11​amp;1amp;−1​). Its determinant is 1(−1)−1(1)=−2, so the absolute value is 2. The absolute value is required because area scaling must be nonnegative. A common mistake is to use the signed determinant directly or confuse the determinant with its reciprocal.

Q2. Why is the absolute value of the Jacobian determinant used when transforming a double integral from (x,y) to (u,v)?

A.It guarantees that orientation is preserved
B.It represents the local area-scaling factor ✅
C.It makes every transformation one-to-one
D.It eliminates the need to transform the region
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Jacobian determinant measures signed local area scaling, while area itself cannot be negative. Therefore, the absolute value gives the actual factor by which a small region in the uv-plane changes area in the xy-plane. It does not guarantee one-to-one behavior or eliminate the need to transform boundaries.

Q3. Suppose x=2u and y=3v. A rectangular region in the uv-plane has area 5. What is the corresponding area in the xy-plane, assuming the transformation is one-to-one?

A.5
B.6
C.15 ✅
D.30
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Jacobian matrix is (20​amp;0amp;3​), whose determinant is 6. Thus every small area is multiplied by 6. Since the original region has area 5, the transformed area is 6(5)=30. The result follows from local area scaling and does not require evaluating a double integral.

Q4. A student transforms x=u+v, y=u−v, obtains dxdy=−2dudv, and substitutes the negative factor into an integral over a positive region. What is the main error?

A.The transformation cannot be used
B.The Jacobian must always equal 1
C.The area element requires the absolute value of the Jacobian ✅
D.The variables must be changed back before integration
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The determinant is −2, but the transformed area element is dxdy=∣−2∣dudv=2dudv. The negative sign indicates orientation reversal rather than negative physical area. Failing to take the absolute value can produce an incorrect sign for an otherwise positive area or integral.

Q5. A region bounded by x+y=1, x+y=4, x−y=2, and x−y=5 is difficult to integrate directly. Which substitution most naturally converts the boundary curves into coordinate lines?

A.u=x2+y2, v=xy
B.u=x+y, v=x−y ✅
C.u=x−y, v=x2+y2
D.u=x, v=y2
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Each boundary is already expressed using either x+y or x−y. Setting u=x+y and v=x−y converts the four boundaries into u=1, u=4, v=2, and v=5, producing a rectangle in the uv-plane. This simplifies both the region and the integration limits.

Q6. A transformation maps a small uv-rectangle into a parallelogram in the xy-plane. If the transformation has Jacobian determinant −3, which statement best describes the mapping locally?

A.Area is multiplied by −3
B.Area is divided by 3 and orientation is preserved
C.Area is multiplied by 3 and orientation is reversed ✅
D.Area remains unchanged because the determinant is negative
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The magnitude of the determinant gives the area scaling factor, so the local area is multiplied by 3. The negative sign indicates that orientation is reversed. Therefore, the correct interpretation combines both pieces of information: magnitude 3 for area scaling and a sign change for orientation.

Q7. A lamina occupies a region that becomes a rectangle 1≤u≤3, 0≤v≤2 under a one-to-one transformation. Its density in transformed coordinates is proportional to u+v, and the Jacobian magnitude is 4. Which integral represents its total mass?

A.∫13​∫02​(u+v)dvdu
B.4∫13​∫02​(u+v)dvdu ✅
C.41​∫13​∫02​(u+v)dvdu
D.∫04​∫13​(u+v)dvdu
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The change of variables requires the area element to be multiplied by the absolute Jacobian. Since the Jacobian magnitude is 4, the mass becomes 4∫13​∫02​(u+v)dvdu. The bounds describe the transformed rectangular region, so changing them or using the reciprocal Jacobian would produce an incorrect mass.

Q8. A student uses the substitution u=x+y, v=x−y for a region whose boundaries are x+y=2, x+y=6, x−y=−1, and x−y=3. The student correctly obtains a rectangle but uses dxdy=2dudv. What should be concluded?

A.The setup is correct
B.The region is wrong but the Jacobian is correct
C.The Jacobian factor should be 1/2 ✅
D.The transformation is invalid because the boundaries are straight lines
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Solving for the inverse gives x=(u+v)/2 and y=(u−v)/2. Therefore, the inverse Jacobian has determinant −1/2, whose magnitude is 1/2. Hence dxdy=21​dudv. The student likely computed the forward Jacobian, 2, but used it in the wrong direction.

Q9. Consider the transformation x=u2−v2, y=2uv. A small square near a point where u2+v2=5 is mapped into a curved quadrilateral. Which quantity determines the approximate ratio of the image area to the original area?

A.u+v
B.u2+v2
C.The absolute value of the Jacobian determinant ✅
D.The perimeter of the image
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: For a sufficiently small region, a differentiable transformation behaves approximately like its linearization. The determinant of its derivative therefore controls local area scaling. Here the relevant quantity is the absolute Jacobian determinant. The curved appearance of the image does not change this local principle; higher-order curvature effects become negligible for sufficiently small regions.

Q10. A graph shows a parallelogram in the xy-plane bounded by x+y=1, x+y=3, x−y=0, and x−y=2. Which transformed description matches the same region under u=x+y, v=x−y?

A.1≤u≤3, 0≤v≤2 ✅
B.0≤u≤2, 1≤v≤3
C.1≤u≤2, 0≤v≤3
D.0≤u≤1, 2≤v≤3
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Each family of parallel boundary lines becomes a constant-coordinate boundary. The equations involving x+y become u=1 and u=3, while those involving x−y become v=0 and v=2. Thus the parallelogram becomes the rectangle 1≤u≤3, 0≤v≤2.

Q11. Two methods are proposed for evaluating an integral over a region bounded by several curves. Method A keeps x,y and uses complicated intersecting limits. Method B introduces variables that make every boundary a constant-coordinate curve and produces a constant nonzero Jacobian. Why is Method B generally preferable?

A.It always gives a smaller numerical answer
B.It removes the need to transform the integrand
C.It simplifies the region and gives a systematic area factor ✅
D.It guarantees that the transformed integral is one-dimensional
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A useful transformation simplifies both the geometry and the differential area element. If the boundaries become coordinate lines and the Jacobian is constant and nonzero, the transformed integral often has much simpler limits and scaling. However, the integrand still must be transformed, and the resulting problem remains a double integral.

Q12. Suppose a transformation has Jacobian determinant J(u,v)=u−v, and the transformed region is 0≤u≤2, 0≤v≤3. A student replaces J by u−v without absolute values. Why can this be problematic?

A.The determinant is never allowed to depend on u,v
B.The sign of u−v changes within the region, so the area factor cannot remain signed ✅
C.The transformation becomes automatically one-to-one
D.The integral must be evaluated before the substitution
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Within the region, u−v is positive in some parts and negative in others. A signed Jacobian would therefore introduce artificial cancellation between areas. The correct area factor is ∣u−v∣. In addition, a zero Jacobian occurs along u=v, so the transformation's local invertibility must be considered carefully.

Q13. For a one-to-one transformation, the Jacobian magnitude is 5. An integrand f(x,y) becomes g(u,v), and the original region becomes R&#039;. Which expression correctly represents the transformed double integral?

A.\iint_{R&#039;}g(u,v)\,du\,dv
B.5\iint_{R&#039;}g(u,v)\,du\,dv ✅
C.\frac15\iint_{R&#039;}g(u,v)\,du\,dv
D.\iint_{R&#039;}5f(x,y)\,du\,dv
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The change-of-variables formula replaces dxdy by the absolute Jacobian multiplied by dudv. Since the Jacobian magnitude is 5, the transformed integral is 5\iint_{R&#039;}g(u,v)\,du\,dv. The integrand must already be expressed in terms of u,v, so retaining f(x,y) without substitution is incomplete.

Q14. A transformation is proposed for evaluating an integral over a region. At one interior point, its Jacobian determinant is zero. Which conclusion is most justified without additional information?

A.The transformation must be one-to-one everywhere
B.The local area scaling vanishes at that point, so the standard local inverse behavior may fail there ✅
C.The original integral is automatically zero
D.The transformed region must be rectangular
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A zero Jacobian means that the derivative matrix loses rank at that point, so local two-dimensional area can collapse in one direction. This prevents the usual local inverse interpretation there. It does not by itself prove that the entire transformation is invalid or that the original integral equals zero; additional global information is required.

Q15. Let x=u+v and y=u−v. A region in the uv-plane is the rectangle 0≤u≤1, 0≤v≤1. A student claims its image has area 1 because the original rectangle has area 1. Another claims its image has area 2. Which claim is correct, and why?

A.The first, because linear transformations preserve area
B.The second, because the Jacobian magnitude is 2 ✅
C.Neither, because the Jacobian is zero
D.The first, because the negative determinant cancels the scaling
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The Jacobian matrix is (11​amp;1amp;−1​), with determinant −2, so the area-scaling magnitude is 2. The uv-rectangle has area 1, hence its image has area 2. The negative sign indicates orientation reversal and does not make the physical area negative. Thus the second claim is correct.
