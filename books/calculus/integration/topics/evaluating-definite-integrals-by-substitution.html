What is Evaluating Definite Integrals by Substitution?

Definition:
To evaluate ∫abf(g(x))g′(x) dx \int_a^b f(g(x))g'(x) \, dx ∫ab​f(g(x))g′(x)dx by substitution, let u=g(x) u=g(x) u=g(x). Change limits: u(a) u(a) u(a) and u(b) u(b) u(b). Then compute ∫u(a)u(b)f(u) du \int_{u(a)}^{u(b)} f(u) \, du ∫u(a)u(b)​f(u)du. This avoids converting back to x.

Example:
∫012xex2 dx \int_0^1 2x e^{x^2} \, dx ∫01​2xex2dx. Let u=x2 u=x^2 u=x2. Limits: 0→0,1→1 0 \to 0, 1 \to 1 0→0,1→1. du=2xdx du=2x dx du=2xdx. Integral: ∫01eudu=e1−e0=e−1 \int_0^1 e^u du = e^1 - e^0 = e - 1 ∫01​eudu=e1−e0=e−1.

Reason:
Changing limits simplifies the process by keeping the calculation in the u-domain, reducing algebraic errors associated with substituting back to the original variable.
