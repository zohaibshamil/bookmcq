Q1. A student solves the initial-value problem \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 2x \) with \( y(0)=0 \) and obtains \( y = x^2 + 1 \). What Medium best describes this mistake?

🔹 A. The student incorrectly integrated \(2x\) as \(x^2\)
🔹 B. The student evaluated the constant of integration incorrectly
🔹 C. The student used the wrong initial condition
🔹 D. The student differentiated instead of integrated

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The student correctly integrated \(2x\) to get \(x^2 + C\). Using the initial condition \(y(0)=0\), we get \(0 = 0^2 + C\) so \(C=0\), yielding \(y=x^2\). The student likely misapplied the initial condition by setting \(C=1\). This is a common error where students forget to substitute the initial condition correctly into the integrated function.

Q2. Given the slope field for \( \frac{dy}{dx} = y - x \), which of the following statements about the solution curves is true?

🔹 A. All solution curves are parallel
🔹 B. Solution curves approach the line \(y = x + 1\) asymptotically
🔹 C. Solution curves are circular
🔹 D. Solution curves are exponential growth functions

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The differential equation \( \frac{dy}{dx} = y - x \) can be rewritten by letting \(u = y - x\). Then \( \frac{du}{dx} = \frac{dy}{dx} - 1 = (y - x) - 1 = u - 1\). The equilibrium solution occurs when \(u=1\), i.e., \(y = x+1\). For large \(x\), solution curves approach this line asymptotically. Easy analysis of slope fields is essential for understanding qualitative behavior of solutions to differential equations.

Q3. The temperature of a cup of coffee changes at a rate proportional to the difference between its temperature and room temperature (20°C). If the coffee initially is 90°C and cools to 70°C in 5 minutes, what is the correct differential equation model?

🔹 A. \( \frac{dT}{dt} = k(T-20) \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T-20) \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{dT}{dt} = k(90-T) \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T-90) \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Newton's Law of Cooling states that the rate of change of temperature is proportional to the temperature difference between the object and its surroundings. Since the coffee is cooling, the rate of change \(dT/dt\) is negative when \(T > 20\). Thus \(dT/dt = -k(T-20)\), where \(k>0\) is the cooling constant. Option A lacks the negative sign, which would actually describe warming behavior. This Easy of differential equations to real-world scenarios is crucial for modeling physical processes.

Q4. Which of the following functions is a solution to the initial-value problem \( \frac{dy}{dx} = y^2 \) with \( y(0)=1 \) over its interval of existence?

🔹 A. \( y = \frac{1}{1-x} \)
🔹 B. \( y = e^x \)
🔹 C. \( y = \tan x \)
🔹 D. \( y = \frac{1}{1+x} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To verify, we differentiate \( y = \frac{1}{1-x} \) to get \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{(1-x)^2} = y^2 \), so it satisfies the differential equation. Also, \( y(0) = 1 \) satisfies the initial condition. This solution exists on \( (-\infty, 1) \) since it blows up at \( x=1 \). Option D also satisfies the differential equation but would require \( y(0)=1 \) which gives \(1=1\), wait actually D gives \(y(0)=1\) as well, but D differentiates to \(-\frac{1}{(1+x)^2} = -y^2\), not \(y^2\). So A is correct. This demonstrates the importance of verifying solutions and understanding domains of existence.

Q5. A particle's velocity satisfies \( \frac{dv}{dt} = 9.8 - 0.5v \) with \( v(0)=0 \). What does the limit \( \lim_{t\to\infty} v(t) \) represent physically?

🔹 A. The initial velocity
🔹 B. The terminal velocity due to air resistance
🔹 C. The acceleration due to gravity
🔹 D. The time when velocity is zero

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The differential equation models a particle falling under gravity with air resistance proportional to velocity. As \( t \to \infty \), the solution approaches the equilibrium value where \( dv/dt = 0 \), i.e., \( 9.8 - 0.5v = 0 \) so \( v = 19.6 \). This is the terminal velocity, the maximum velocity the particle attains when gravitational force balances air resistance. Understanding this physical interpretation connects differential equations to real-world physics and requires comprehension of equilibrium solutions.

Q6. Which of the following initial-value problems would have a unique solution guaranteed by the Existence and Uniqueness Theorem?

🔹 A. \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \sqrt{y}, y(0) = 0 \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{y}, y(0) = 0 \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{dy}{dx} = y^2, y(0) = 0 \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x}{y}, y(0) = 0 \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Existence and Uniqueness Theorem requires that \(f(x,y)\) and \(\partial f/\partial y\) be continuous near the initial point. For option C, \(f(x,y) = y^2\) is continuous everywhere and \(\partial f/\partial y = 2y\) is also continuous, so uniqueness is guaranteed. Option A has \(f(x,y) = \sqrt{y}\) where \(\partial f/\partial y = 1/(2\sqrt{y})\) is not continuous at \(y=0\), potentially violating uniqueness. Option B has \(f(x,y)=1/y\) not defined at \(y=0\). Option D has \(f(x,y)=x/y\) not defined at \(y=0\). This Olympiad-style question tests deep understanding of theoretical conditions for existence and uniqueness.

Q7. A solution to \( \frac{dy}{dx} = f(x) \) can be written as \( y(x) = y_0 + \int_{x_0}^x f(t)\,dt \). If \( f(x) = \cos(x^2) \), what is the best description of this solution?

🔹 A. It is an elementary function
🔹 B. It is expressed as a definite integral and cannot be simplified to elementary functions
🔹 C. It has no closed form and cannot be computed
🔹 D. It is simply \( \sin(x^2) \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The solution is \( y(x) = y_0 + \int_{0}^x \cos(t^2) dt \). The Fresnel cosine integral \( \int \cos(t^2) dt \) is a well-known non-elementary function; it cannot be expressed in terms of polynomial, exponential, trigonometric, or logarithmic functions. This is an excellent example of how integral representations can define new functions (special functions). Option D would be the derivative of \( \sin(x^2) \) which is \(2x\cos(x^2)\), not the integral. This connects integration to function definition and the limitations of elementary functions.

Q8. What mistake is made in solving \( \frac{dy}{dx} = y \) with \( y(0)=1 \) as follows: \( \int \frac{dy}{y} = \int dx \) gives \( \ln y = x \), so \( y = e^x + C \)?

🔹 A. The constant \(C\) is missing in the first integration step
🔹 B. The constant of integration should be added before exponentiating
🔹 C. The solution should be \( y = C e^x \), but the student wrote \( y = e^x + C \)
🔹 D. The natural logarithm should be \( \ln|y| \) not \( \ln y \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The student made an error when solving for \(y\). From \( \ln y = x + C\), exponentiating gives \( y = e^{x+C} = e^x e^C = C_1 e^x\), not \( y = e^x + C\). The mistake is failing to apply the exponential to the entire expression. The correct solution with \(y(0)=1\) is \(y = e^x\). This is a common integration error where students incorrectly separate the constant of integration. Proper understanding of the properties of logarithms and exponentials is essential for solving separable differential equations.

Q9. The population of a city is modeled by \( \frac{dP}{dt} = kP \) with \( P(0) = P_0 \). If the population doubles in 10 years, what is the correct expression for \(k\)?

🔹 A. \( k = \frac{\ln 2}{10} \)
🔹 B. \( k = \frac{2}{10} \)
🔹 C. \( k = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln 10} \)
🔹 D. \( k = \frac{10}{\ln 2} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Solving \( \frac{dP}{dt} = kP \) gives \( P(t) = P_0 e^{kt} \). If the population doubles, \( P(10) = P_0 e^{10k} = 2P_0 \), so \( e^{10k} = 2 \), \( 10k = \ln 2 \), and \( k = \frac{\ln 2}{10} \). This is a standard Easy of exponential growth models. Option B would be appropriate for linear growth, not exponential. Option C confuses bases of logarithms. Understanding how to derive and use growth rates from given data is a key Easy of differential equations.

Q10. A slope field for \( \frac{dy}{dx} = x + y \) is given. If the solution curve passes through (0,1), what is the slope of the tangent line at that point?

🔹 A. 0

🔹 C. -1

A slope field shows the slope \( dy/dx \) at various points. At the point (0,1), the slope is simply \( x + y = 0 + 1 = 1 \). This is a direct reading from the differential equation, not a complicated computation. However, interpreting slope fields requires understanding that each point (x,y) has an associated slope equal to the value of \( f(x,y) \). Q11. Given the initial-value problem \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2x}{y} \) with \( y(0) = -3 \), what is the solution?

🔹 A. \( y = \sqrt{2x^2 + 9} \)
🔹 B. \( y = -\sqrt{2x^2 + 9} \)
🔹 C. \( y = x^2 - 3 \)
🔹 D. \( y = x^2 + 3 \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

This is a separable equation: \( y\,dy = 2x\,dx\). Integrating gives \( \frac{y^2}{2} = x^2 + C\), so \( y^2 = 2x^2 + 2C\). Using \( y(0) = -3 \), we get \( 9 = 2C \), so \( 2C = 9 \). Thus \( y^2 = 2x^2 + 9 \), and \( y = \pm\sqrt{2x^2 + 9} \). Since \( y(0) = -3 < 0 \), we choose the negative root: \( y = -\sqrt{2x^2 + 9} \). Option A gives the positive root, which would not satisfy the initial condition. This problem requires careful attention to signs when taking square roots, a common source of errors.

Q12. In solving \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x}{y} \) with \( y(0) = 2 \), which of the following is the correct step?

🔹 A. Separate variables: \( y\,dy = x\,dx \)
🔹 B. Separate variables: \( \frac{dy}{y} = \frac{dx}{x} \)
🔹 C. Separate variables: \( dy = \frac{x}{y}dx \)
🔹 D. This equation cannot be solved by separation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The equation \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x}{y} \) can be rewritten as \( y\,dy = x\,dx \) by multiplying both sides by \( y\,dx \). This is a correct separation of variables. Option B would be incorrect because the variables are not separated as \(\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{x}{y}\), not \(\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y}{x}\). Option C is just the original equation in differential form, not a separated form. Option D is false since the equation is separable. Recognizing correct separation technique is fundamental to solving first-order ODEs.

Q13. A model for the spread of a rumor is \( \frac{dR}{dt} = kR(1-R) \). If \( R(0) = 0.01 \) and \( R(1) = 0.1 \), what is the approximate value of \(k\)?

🔹 A. \( k \approx 0.1 \)
🔹 B. \( k \approx 0.5 \)
🔹 C. \( k \approx 1.0 \)
🔹 D. \( k \approx 2.3 \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The logistic equation \( \frac{dR}{dt} = kR(1-R) \) has solution \( R(t) = \frac{1}{1 + \left(\frac{1}{R_0} - 1\right)e^{-kt}} \). With \( R(0) = 0.01 \), we have \( R(t) = \frac{1}{1 + 99e^{-kt}} \). Using \( R(1) = 0.1 \), we get \( 0.1 = \frac{1}{1 + 99e^{-k}} \), so \( 1 + 99e^{-k} = 10 \), \( 99e^{-k} = 9 \), \( e^{-k} = \frac{1}{11} \), and \( k = \ln 11 \approx 2.4 \). This problem combines separation of variables, logistic growth modeling, and logarithmic manipulation, testing multiple concepts simultaneously.

Q14. A student solving \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 3y^{2/3} \) with \( y(0) = 0 \) obtains \( y = x^3 \). What is the flaw in this solution?

🔹 A. The integration of \( y^{-2/3}dy = 3dx \) gives \( 3y^{1/3} = x + C \), not \( y = x^3 \)
🔹 B. The differential equation has multiple solutions through (0,0), so \( y=x^3 \) is not unique
🔹 C. The initial condition \( y(0)=0 \) cannot be used with this solution
🔹 D. The function \( y=x^3 \) does not satisfy the differential equation

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Separating variables: \( y^{-2/3}dy = 3dx \), integrating gives \( 3y^{1/3} = 3x + C \), so \( y^{1/3} = x + C/3 \), \( y = (x + C/3)^3 \). With \( y(0) = 0 \), we get \( C=0 \), so \( y=x^3 \) is a solution. However, \( y=0 \) is also a solution through (0,0). The Existence and Uniqueness Theorem fails because \( f(x,y) = 3y^{2/3} \) has partial derivative \( \partial f/\partial y = 2y^{-1/3} \) not continuous at \( y=0 \), so uniqueness is not guaranteed. The flaw is not the algebra but assuming uniqueness when it doesn't exist. This is a subtle but important Medium.

Q15. Which interpretation correctly describes the solution of \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 2\sqrt{y} \) with \( y(0) = 0 \)?

🔹 A. The solution is unique and is \( y = x^2 \)
🔹 B. The solution is unique and is \( y = 0 \)
🔹 C. There are infinitely many solutions
🔹 D. There is no solution

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

This is a classic example of non-uniqueness. \( y=0 \) is a solution. Also, for any \( a \geq 0 \), the function defined by \( y(x) = 0 \) for \( x \leq a \) and \( y(x) = (x-a)^2 \) for \( x > a \) is a solution. This is because \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 2(x-a) = 2\sqrt{(x-a)^2} = 2\sqrt{y} \). There are infinitely many such solutions, parameterized by \( a \). This occurs because \( f(x,y)=2\sqrt{y} \) is not Lipschitz at \( y=0 \). This Olympiad-style question tests deep understanding of solution existence and uniqueness conditions, going beyond standard textbook problems.

Q18. Given \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y}{x} \) with \( y(0) = 1 \), what is the correct analysis?

🔹 A. The solution is \( y = x \), defined for all \( x \)
🔹 B. The solution is \( y = |x| \), defined for all \( x \neq 0 \)
🔹 C. The solution does not exist because \( f(x,y) = y/x \) is not defined at \( x=0 \)
🔹 D. The solution is \( y = x \), defined for \( x \neq 0 \) and can be extended by continuity

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The differential equation is \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y}{x} \). The function \( f(x,y) = y/x \) is not defined at \( x=0 \), so the initial condition \( y(0)=1 \) is invalid because the differential equation itself is not defined at \( x=0 \). The Existence and Uniqueness Theorem requires \( f \) to be continuous in a neighborhood of the initial point; here it's not even defined. Option B confuses the solution of \( dy/dx = y/x \) (which is \( y = Cx \)) with an absolute value. Q20. In the differential equation \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 3y \) with \( y(0) = 2 \), if the student uses separation of variables and gets \( y = 2e^{3x} \), which step confirms the solution?

Q21. A chemical reaction rate is modeled by \( \frac{dC}{dt} = -kC^2 \). If the initial concentration is \( C_0 \), what is the time required for the concentration to halve?

🔹 A. \( t = \frac{1}{kC_0} \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

With \( C(0) = C_0 \), \( 1/C_0 = C_2 \), so \( 1/C = kt + 1/C_0 \), \( C = \frac{C_0}{1 + kC_0t} \). Option B would be the half-life for first-order kinetics \( \frac{dC}{dt} = -kC \). Option C is the time for concentration to decrease by half in this model, wait actually C is half of A. Q22. Given the slope field for \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \sin x \), what can be said about the solution curves?
