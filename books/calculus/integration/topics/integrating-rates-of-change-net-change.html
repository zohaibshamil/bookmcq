What is Integrating rates of change net change?

If \( F'(t) \) represents the rate of change of a quantity \( F(t) \), then \( \int_a^b F'(t) \, dt = F(b) - F(a) \) gives the net change in \( F \) over \( [a,b] \), illustrating how integration accumulates instantaneous rates to determine total change regardless of intermediate fluctuations.

Q1. A population of bacteria grows at a rate of \(r(t) = 500e^{0.2t}\) cells per hour. What is the net change in the population from \(t=0\) to \(t=5\) hours?

A. \(\int_0^5 500e^{0.2t} dt\) ✅
B. \(\int_0^5 500e^{0.2t} dt + C\)
C. \(500e^{0.2(5)} - 500e^{0.2(0)}\)
D. \(500e^{0.2(5)} + C\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The net change in a quantity over an interval is given by the definite integral of its rate of change over that interval. Options C and D represent the antiderivative evaluated at a single point, not the change over the interval. Option B incorrectly includes a constant of integration, which cancels out in a definite integral. The correct expression is the definite integral from 0 to 5, as stated in option A.

Q2. A particle moves along a line with velocity \(v(t) = 4t - 12\) m/s. The displacement from \(t=1\) to \(t=5\) is 0, but the distance traveled is 16 m. What is the most plausible explanation?

A. The particle’s velocity changes sign, so displacement and distance differ. ✅
B. The velocity is constant, so displacement and distance are equal.
C. The particle’s position at \(t=1\) and \(t=5\) is the same, so the distance must also be 0.
D. The acceleration is zero, so displacement and distance are the same.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Displacement is the integral of velocity (net signed area), while distance traveled is the integral of speed (total area). If the velocity changes sign, these two values can differ. Option C is a common misconception: if displacement is zero, the distance traveled can still be positive. Options B and D ignore the possibility of sign changes in velocity.

Q3. Water flows into a tank at a rate of \(r(t) = 10 + 2t\) L/min. At \(t=0\), the tank contains 100 L. How much water is in the tank at \(t=10\) minutes?

A. \(100 + \int_0^{10} (10 + 2t) dt\) ✅
B. \(\int_0^{10} (10 + 2t) dt\)
C. \(100 + 10 + 2(10)\)
D. \(100 + \int_0^{10} (10 + 2t) dt + C\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The total amount at time \(T\) is the initial amount plus the integral of the rate of change from 0 to \(T\). Option B gives only the net change, not the final amount. Option C is an incorrect calculation. Option D includes a constant of integration, which is unnecessary since the initial condition is specified. Option A correctly adds the initial amount to the definite integral.

Q4. The rate of change of the volume of a balloon is \(V'(t) = 8\pi t\) cm³/s. Which statement is true?

A. The volume function is \(V(t) = 4\pi t^2 + C\). ✅
B. The volume function is \(V(t) = 4\pi t^2\).
C. The volume function is \(V(t) = 8\pi\).
D. The volume function is \(V(t) = 8\pi t^2 + C\).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since \(V'(t) = 8\pi t\), the volume function is the antiderivative: \(V(t) = 4\pi t^2 + C\). Option B omits the constant of integration, which is necessary unless an initial condition is given. Option C is the derivative of the volume function, and option D incorrectly integrates the constant factor. Option A correctly represents the general antiderivative.

Q5. A student claims that if the rate of change of a function is always positive on an interval, then the function itself must be increasing on that interval. Is this always true, and why?

A. Yes, because a positive derivative implies an increasing function. ✅
B. No, because the function could be negative but increasing.
C. Yes, because the function’s values will be positive.
D. No, because the integral of a positive function is always zero.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

If the derivative \(f'(x) > 0\) for all \(x\) in an interval, then by the Mean Value Theorem, \(f\) is strictly increasing on that interval. Option B is a misconception: a function can be increasing even if its values are negative. Option C confuses the sign of the function with its rate of change. Option D is false, as the integral of a positive function is positive, not zero. Option A correctly applies the relationship between the sign of the derivative and the monotonicity of the function.

Q6. Given the graph of \(f'(x)\) below, which integral correctly represents the change in \(f(x)\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=4\)? (Assume the graph shows a positive area of 10 units² and a negative area of 6 units².)

A. \(\int_0^4 f'(x) dx = 4\) ✅
B. \(\int_0^4 f'(x) dx = 16\)
C. \(\int_0^4 f'(x) dx = 10\)
D. \(\int_0^4 f'(x) dx = 6\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The change in \(f(x)\) is given by the net signed area under \(f'(x)\). The net signed area is the positive area minus the negative area: \(10 - 6 = 4\). Option B incorrectly adds the areas. Option C ignores the negative area. Option D ignores the positive area. Option A correctly computes the net change.

Q7. A car’s velocity is given by \(v(t) = 20 - 0.5t\) m/s for \(0 \le t \le 40\). The displacement is 400 m, but the distance traveled is 400 m as well. What does this imply?

A. The car never changes direction. ✅
B. The car changes direction exactly once.
C. The car’s acceleration is zero.
D. The car’s speed is always positive.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

If displacement equals distance traveled, the velocity must not change sign; the car moves in one direction. Options B and D are incorrect because if the velocity changes sign, displacement and distance would differ. Option C is false because the acceleration is constant and non-zero. Option A correctly identifies that the car never changes direction.

Q8. The rate of growth of a tree is \(r(t) = 2t + 1\) cm/year. The tree’s height at \(t=0\) is 5 cm. What is the height at \(t=3\) years?

A. \(5 + \int_0^3 (2t + 1) dt = 17\) cm ✅
B. \(\int_0^3 (2t + 1) dt = 12\) cm
C. \(5 + 2(3) + 1 = 12\) cm
D. \(5 + \int_0^3 (2t + 1) dt = 12\) cm

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The height is \(h(3) = h(0) + \int_0^3 r(t) dt = 5 + [t^2 + t]_0^3 = 5 + (9 + 3) = 17\) cm. Option B gives only the net change. Option C misapplies the rate. Option D miscalculates the integral. Option A correctly computes the final height.

Q9. The marginal cost of producing \(x\) units is \(C'(x) = 3x^2 - 2x + 5\) dollars per unit. If the fixed cost is \(C(0) = 100\), what is the total cost to produce 10 units?

B. \(\int_0^{10} (3x^2 - 2x + 5) dx\)
C. \(100 + 3(10)^2 - 2(10) + 5 = 385\)
D. \(100 + \int_0^{10} (3x^2 - 2x + 5) dx + C\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The total cost is \(C(10) = C(0) + \int_0^{10} C'(x) dx\). Option B omits the fixed cost. Option C incorrectly applies the rate. Option D includes an unnecessary constant. Option A correctly incorporates the fixed cost and the integral.

Q10. A function \(F\) has \(F'(x) = x^2 - 4\). If \(F(0) = 3\), what is the change in \(F\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=3\)?

A. 3
B. -3 ✅
C. 6
D. 0

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The change is \(F(3) - F(0) = \int_0^3 (x^2 - 4) dx = [x^3/3 - 4x]_0^3 = 9 - 12 = -3\). Option A is \(F(0)\). Option C is the integral of the positive part. Option D is a common error. Option B is the correct net change.

Q14. If \(f'(x) = 2x\) and \(f(0) = 1\), what is \(f(2)\)?

A. 5 ✅
B. 4
C. 3
D. 6

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

\(f(x) = \int 2x dx = x^2 + C\). Using \(f(0) = 1\), \(C = 1\). Thus, \(f(2) = 4 + 1 = 5\). Option B is \(x^2\) without the constant. Option C is a common error. Option D is incorrect. Option A correctly applies the initial condition.

Q19. A student argues that since the integral of a rate of change gives the net change, the constant of integration is always needed. Is the student correct?

A. No, the constant cancels when evaluating a definite integral. ✅
B. Yes, the constant is always part of the general antiderivative.
C. Yes, because every function has infinitely many antiderivatives.
D. No, because the definite integral does not exist.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

For a definite integral, the constant of integration cancels out: \(F(b) + C - (F(a) + C) = F(b) - F(a)\). Option B is true for indefinite integrals. Option C is a misconception. Option D is false. Option A correctly explains why the constant is unnecessary for definite integrals.

Q20. The rate of change of a function \(f\) is given by \(f'(x) = 2x - 3\). If \(f(2) = 4\), which of the following is the correct expression for \(f(5)\)?

Q23. The velocity of a particle is \(v(t) = 6t - 2t^2\). The particle’s displacement from \(t=0\) to \(t=3\) is 9. What is the distance traveled?

A. 9
B. More than 9 ✅
C. Less than 9
D. Cannot be determined

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

If velocity changes sign, displacement and distance differ. Here, \(v(t) = 2t(3 - t)\), which is positive for \(0 < t < 3\). Since velocity is non-negative, displacement equals distance, so distance is also 9. Option B is incorrect because the distance is exactly 9. Options C and D are incorrect. Option A correctly states that displacement equals distance when velocity does not change sign.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

If \(r'(t) = 2\), then \(r(t) = 2t + C\). Using \(r(0) = 5\), \(C = 5\), so \(r(3) = 11\). Option A is correct but lacks the integral notation. Option B is incomplete. Option D omits the initial radius. Option C correctly expresses the solution with the integral.
