What is U substitution for nonlinear functions?

Definition:
Nonlinear u-substitution handles cases where u u u is a quadratic or higher-degree polynomial. Success depends on the derivative du du du being proportional to the non-u part of the integrand, often requiring algebraic manipulation or factoring constants.

Example:
Integrate ∫xx2+4 dx \int x\sqrt{x^2+4} \, dx ∫xx2+4​dx. Let u=x2+4 u = x^2+4 u=x2+4, du=2x dx⇒x dx=du/2 du = 2x \, dx \Rightarrow x \, dx = du/2 du=2xdx⇒xdx=du/2. Integral: ∫udu2=12⋅23u3/2+C=13(x2+4)3/2+C \int \sqrt{u} \frac{du}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3}u^{3/2} + C = \frac{1}{3}(x^2+4)^{3/2} + C ∫u​2du​=21​⋅32​u3/2+C=31​(x2+4)3/2+C.

Reason:
This extends substitution utility to radical and rational functions, demonstrating how variable changes can simplify seemingly difficult integrals into standard power rule applications.
