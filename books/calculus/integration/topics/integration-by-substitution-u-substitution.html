What is Integration by Substitution u-Substitution?

Definition:
U-substitution reverses the chain rule for integration by changing variables. If u=g(x) u = g(x) u=g(x), then du=g′(x)dx du = g'(x)dx du=g′(x)dx, transforming ∫f(g(x))g′(x) dx \int f(g(x))g'(x) \, dx ∫f(g(x))g′(x)dx into ∫f(u) du \int f(u) \, du ∫f(u)du, simplifying composite functions into basic forms.

Example:
Integrate ∫2x(x2+1)3 dx \int 2x(x^2+1)^3 \, dx ∫2x(x2+1)3dx. Let u=x2+1 u = x^2+1 u=x2+1, du=2x dx du = 2x \, dx du=2xdx. Integral becomes ∫u3 du=u44+C=(x2+1)44+C \int u^3 \, du = \frac{u^4}{4} + C = \frac{(x^2+1)^4}{4} + C ∫u3du=4u4​+C=4(x2+1)4​+C.

Reason:
This technique is essential for integrating composite functions where the inner function's derivative is present, expanding the range of integrable functions beyond simple polynomials.
