What is Displacement vs distance traveled integration?

Definition:
Displacement is the net change in position \( \int_a^b v(t) \, dt \), while distance traveled is the total path length \( \int_a^b |v(t)| \, dt \). Distance requires splitting the integral at points where velocity changes sign.

Example:
For \( v(t) = t-1 \) on [0,2]. Displacement: \( \int_0^2 (t-1) dt = 0 \). Distance: Split at t=1. \( \int_0^1 -(t-1) dt + \int_1^2 (t-1) dt = 0.5 + 0.5 = 1 \).

Reason:
Distinguishing these prevents errors in navigation and logistics, where total fuel consumption (distance) differs from final location relative to start (displacement).

Q1. A particle moves along a line with velocity \( v(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 \) m/s. What is the displacement of the particle from \( t=0 \) to \( t=4 \) seconds?

A. 0 m
B. 4 m ✅
C. 8 m
D. 12 m

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Displacement is the integral of velocity: \( \int_0^4 (3t^2 - 12t + 9) dt = [t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t]_0^4 = (64 - 96 + 36) - 0 = 4 \) m. The particle ends 4 meters from its starting position. It is important to remember that displacement is the net change in position and can be positive, negative, or zero.

Q2. For the same particle with \( v(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 \) m/s, what is the total distance traveled from \( t=0 \) to \( t=4 \) seconds?

A. 4 m
B. 8 m
C. 12 m ✅
D. 16 m

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Distance traveled requires integrating the absolute value of velocity: \( \int_0^4 |3t^2 - 12t + 9| dt \). The velocity is zero at \( t=1 \) and \( t=3 \). On [0,1], v≥0; on [1,3], v≤0; on [3,4], v≥0. Distance = \( \int_0^1 v dt - \int_1^3 v dt + \int_3^4 v dt = 4 - (-4) + 4 = 12 \) m. The particle changes direction, so distance traveled is greater than displacement.

Q3. A student computes the distance traveled by a particle with velocity \( v(t) = \cos t \) from \( t=0 \) to \( t=\pi \) as \( \int_0^\pi \cos t \, dt = 0 \). What error did the student make?

A. They integrated the wrong function.
B. They forgot to take the absolute value of velocity. ✅
C. They used the wrong limits of integration.
D. They misapplied the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The student computed displacement (net change in position), not distance traveled. Distance traveled is \( \int_0^\pi |\cos t| dt \). Since \( \cos t \) is positive on [0, π/2] and negative on [π/2, π], the correct distance is \( \int_0^{π/2} \cos t dt - \int_{π/2}^π \cos t dt = 1 - (-1) = 2 \). The student's error is a classic mistake: confusing net displacement with total path length.

Q6. The velocity of a particle is graphed as a straight line from (0,2) to (4,6). What is the displacement of the particle from \( t=0 \) to \( t=4 \)?

A. 8 units
B. 12 units
C. 16 units ✅
D. 20 units

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The velocity function is \( v(t) = t + 2 \). Displacement = \( \int_0^4 (t+2) dt = [t^2/2 + 2t]_0^4 = 8 + 8 = 16 \). This can also be found as the area under the velocity curve, which is a trapezoid with bases 2 and 6 and height 4: area = \( (1/2)(2+6)(4) = 16 \). Since the velocity is always positive, displacement equals distance traveled.

Q7. A particle's acceleration is \( a(t) = 6t \). If \( v(0) = 2 \) and \( s(0) = 1 \), what is the distance traveled from \( t=0 \) to \( t=2 \)?

A. 10
B. 12 ✅
C. 14
D. 16

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

\( v(t) = \int 6t dt = 3t^2 + C \). With \( v(0) = 2 \), \( C = 2 \), so \( v(t) = 3t^2 + 2 \), which is always positive. Distance = displacement = \( \int_0^2 (3t^2 + 2) dt = [t^3 + 2t]_0^2 = 8 + 4 = 12 \). The acceleration is always positive, so velocity increases from 2 to 14, never changing sign, so distance equals displacement.

Q13. The graph of velocity \( v(t) \) is a semicircle of radius 2 centered at (2,0) above the t-axis. What is the displacement from \( t=0 \) to \( t=4 \)?

A. \( 2\pi \) ✅
B. \( 4\pi \)
C. \( \pi \)
D. \( 8\pi \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The area under the velocity curve is the area of a semicircle with radius 2: area = \( \frac{1}{2}\pi(2)^2 = 2\pi \). Since the velocity is always nonnegative, displacement equals the area under the curve. The graph is a semicircle, so the integral is simply the geometric area.

Q15. A particle moves with velocity \( v(t) = 2t - 3 \). What is the average velocity from \( t=1 \) to \( t=4 \)?

A. 1
B. 2 ✅
C. 3
D. 4

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Average velocity = \( \frac{1}{4-1} \int_1^4 (2t - 3) dt = \frac{1}{3} [t^2 - 3t]_1^4 = \frac{1}{3} [(16 - 12) - (1 - 3)] = \frac{1}{3} [4 - (-2)] = \frac{1}{3} [6] = 2 \). The average velocity is the displacement divided by the time interval, which is also the average value of the velocity function.

Q16. A particle's position is given by \( s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t + 2 \). What is the total distance traveled from \( t=0 \) to \( t=4 \)?

A. 8
B. 10
C. 12 ✅
D. 14

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

\( v(t) = s'(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 9 = 3(t-1)(t-3) \). Velocity is positive on (0,1), negative on (1,3), positive on (3,4). Distance = \( \int_0^1 v(t) dt - \int_1^3 v(t) dt + \int_3^4 v(t) dt \). \( s(1) = 1 - 6 + 9 + 2 = 6 \). \( s(3) = 27 - 54 + 27 + 2 = 2 \). \( s(4) = 64 - 96 + 36 + 2 = 6 \). Displacement from 0 to 1 is \( s(1)-s(0) = 6-2 = 4 \). From 1 to 3 is \( s(3)-s(1) = 2-6 = -4 \). From 3 to 4 is \( s(4)-s(3) = 6-2 = 4 \). Total distance = \( 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 \). This method uses the position function directly to find distances between turning points.

Q17. A particle moves with velocity \( v(t) = \cos(2t) \). What is the displacement from \( t=0 \) to \( t=\pi \)?

A. 0 ✅
B. 1
C. -1
D. 2

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

\( \int_0^\pi \cos(2t) dt = [\sin(2t)/2]_0^\pi = 0 \). The integral of a cosine over a full period is zero. The particle returns to its starting position after one full period of the velocity function.

Q18. For the same particle with \( v(t) = \cos(2t) \), what is the total distance traveled from \( t=0 \) to \( t=\pi \)?

A. 1
B. 2 ✅
C. 3
D. 4

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

\( \cos(2t) \) changes sign at \( t = \pi/4, 3\pi/4 \). Distance = \( \int_0^{\pi/4} \cos(2t) dt - \int_{\pi/4}^{3\pi/4} \cos(2t) dt + \int_{3\pi/4}^{\pi} \cos(2t) dt \). Each of the three integrals has magnitude \( 1/2 \). Actually, \( \int_0^{\pi/4} \cos(2t) dt = [\sin(2t)/2]_0^{\pi/4} = 1/2 \). The integral from \( \pi/4 \) to \( 3\pi/4 \) is \( -1 \), so its absolute value is 1. The integral from \( 3\pi/4 \) to \( \pi \) is \( 1/2 \). Total distance = \( 1/2 + 1 + 1/2 = 2 \). The particle moves back and forth, so distance is greater than displacement.

Q21. A student claims that for any velocity function, the distance traveled is always greater than or equal to the displacement. Is the student correct?

A. Yes, because distance is the integral of the absolute value. ✅
B. Yes, because displacement is always less than distance.
C. No, because if velocity is always positive, they are equal.
D. No, because displacement can be greater than distance.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The student is correct. Distance is \( \int |v| dt \), and displacement is \( \int v dt \). Since \( |v| \ge v \), the integral of \( |v| \) is always greater than or equal to the integral of \( v \). They are equal only when \( v \) does not change sign (i.e., the particle never reverses direction). This is a fundamental inequality in calculus.

Q23. A particle moves with velocity \( v(t) = 3t^2 - 6t + 3 = 3(t-1)^2 \). What is the total distance traveled from \( t=0 \) to \( t=2 \)?

Q26. A particle's velocity is \( v(t) = \sin t + \cos t \). What is the displacement from \( t=0 \) to \( t=\pi \)?

A. 0
B. 1
C. 2 ✅
D. -1

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

\( \int_0^\pi (\sin t + \cos t) dt = [-\cos t + \sin t]_0^\pi = (1 + 0) - (-1 + 0) = 1 - (-1) = 2 \). The displacement is 2. This requires integrating a sum of trigonometric functions and evaluating at the limits.
