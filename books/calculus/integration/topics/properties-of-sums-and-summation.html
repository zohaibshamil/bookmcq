What is Properties of sums and summation?

Definition:
Summation properties allow splitting sums and factoring constants: \( \sum (a_i + b_i) = \sum a_i + \sum b_i \) and \( \sum c \cdot a_i = c \sum a_i \). These linear properties simplify complex sums by breaking them into simpler, known components.

Example:
Evaluate \( \sum_{i=1}^{5} (2i + 3) \). Split: \( 2\sum_{i=1}^5 i + \sum_{i=1}^5 3 \). Calculate: \( 2(15) + 3(5) = 30 + 15 = 45 \).

Reason:
These properties reduce computational effort in Riemann sums, allowing students to apply standard formulas for \( \sum i \), \( \sum i^2 \), etc., rather than calculating each term manually.

Q1. A student claims that \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (a_k + b_k)^2 = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k^2 + \sum_{k=1}^{n} b_k^2 \). Which property of summation does this statement violate, and why?

A.It violates the constant multiple rule because it ignores the cross terms.
B.It violates the distributive property because the square of a sum is not the sum of squares. ✅
C.It is correct because summation is a linear operator.
D.It violates the commutative property because the order of terms matters.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: This is a common error. The student incorrectly applies linearity to a non-linear operation. While summation is linear over addition (\( \sum (a_k+b_k) = \sum a_k + \sum b_k \)), it does not distribute over multiplication or powers. The correct expansion includes the cross term \(2a_kb_k\). Recognizing the domain of linearity is crucial in higher mathematics.

Q2. Given the sum \( \sum_{k=1}^{5} k^2 = 55 \), what is the value of \( \sum_{k=1}^{5} (k^2 - 10) \) without recalculating each term?

A.45
B.-5
C.5 ✅
D.55
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: This question tests the Easy of the subtraction property of sums: \( \sum (a_k - b_k) = \sum a_k - \sum b_k \). Here, \( \sum_{k=1}^{5} k^2 = 55 \) and \( \sum_{k=1}^{5} 10 = 10 \times 5 = 50 \). Therefore, the result is \(55 - 50 = 5\). This demonstrates how to manipulate sums to simplify calculations.

Q3. Evaluate the sum: \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{3k}{n} \).

A.\( \frac{3}{n} \cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \)
B.\( \frac{3(n+1)}{2} \)
C.Both A and B are equivalent. ✅
D.\( \frac{3n+1}{2} \)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: This problem tests Medium of the constant multiple rule. The constant \( \frac{3}{n} \) is factored out: \( \frac{3}{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} k \). Since \( \sum k = \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \), the result is \( \frac{3}{n} \cdot \frac{n(n+1)}{2} = \frac{3(n+1)}{2} \). Option C correctly identifies that A and B are the same expression, highlighting that simplification can take different but equivalent forms.

The sum of an arithmetic series is \( \frac{n}{2}(2a_1 + (n-1)d) = \frac{10}{2}(4 + 27) = 5 \times 31 = 155 \). It requires recognizing the sequence type, identifying the correct formula, and applying it accurately.

Q5. If \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = A \) and \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} b_k = B \), which of the following must be true?

A.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (2a_k - b_k) = 2A - B \) ✅
B.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (a_k b_k) = AB \)
C.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{a_k}{b_k} = \frac{A}{B} \)
D.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k^2 = A^2 \)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This question isolates the property of linearity of sums. Options B, C, and D are common misconceptions where students incorrectly assume that summation distributes over multiplication, division, or powers. Linearity only applies to addition and subtraction of terms multiplied by constants. Only Option A correctly applies the constant multiple and difference rules.

Q6. In the context of Riemann sums, why is it important that the summation operator is linear (i.e., \( \sum c f(x_i) \Delta x = c \sum f(x_i) \Delta x \))?

A.It allows us to interpret the sum as an area.
B.It allows us to evaluate sums without limits.
C.It allows us to break complex functions into simpler parts for integration. ✅
D.It proves that all functions are integrable.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: This is a conceptual question linking algebra to calculus. The linearity of the sum is fundamental to the definition of the definite integral. Because we can factor constants and split sums, the integral of a sum is the sum of integrals, and constants factor out. This allows us to compute areas under complex curves by integrating simpler component functions.

Q7. A student evaluates \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} c \) as \( c \). What is the error?

A.The student forgot to multiply by the number of terms, \( n \).
B.The student incorrectly applied the constant multiple rule.
C.The student thinks the sum of a constant is the constant itself.
D.All of the above. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: This is an Medium question. \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} c = c + c + ... + c \) (n times) = \( nc \). The student's error could stem from several misconceptions: forgetting that the constant is repeated for each term, misapplying the constant multiple rule in reverse, or simply misunderstanding what the sum represents. Option D correctly identifies that all listed are potential underlying errors.

Q8. If \( \sum_{k=1}^{10} a_k = 20 \) and \( a_3 = 5 \), find \( \sum_{k=1, k \neq 3}^{10} a_k \).

A.5
B.15 ✅
C.20
D.25
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: This problem applies the concept of decomposition of sums. We know \( \sum_{k=1}^{10} a_k = a_3 + \sum_{k=1, k \neq 3}^{10} a_k \). Therefore, \( 20 = 5 + \sum_{k=1, k \neq 3}^{10} a_k \), which gives \( \sum_{k=1, k \neq 3}^{10} a_k = 15 \). This illustrates how to manipulate the limits of summation by isolating specific terms.

Q9. Which of the following is a valid Easy of the summation property \( \sum (a_k - b_k) = \sum a_k - \sum b_k \)?

A.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (k^2 - 1) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^2 - n \) ✅
B.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (k^2 - 1) = (\sum_{k=1}^{n} k)^2 - n \)
C.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (k^2 - 1) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^2 - 1 \)
D.Both A and C are correct.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Option A is correct because \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} 1 = n \), so \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (k^2 - 1) = \sum k^2 - n \). Option B incorrectly assumes the sum of squares is the square of the sum. Option C is incorrect because it subtracts 1 only once instead of for every term. This question forces students to carefully apply the property and recognize common mistakes.

Q10. Given \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = S \), what is \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (a_k - a_{k-1}) \) in terms of \( a_0, a_n \)?

A.\( S \)
B.\( a_n - a_0 \) ✅
C.\( a_n + a_0 \)
D.\( S - a_0 - a_n \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: This is a classic telescoping sum problem. Expanding the sum: \( (a_1-a_0)+(a_2-a_1)+...+(a_n-a_{n-1}) \). All intermediate terms cancel, leaving \( a_n - a_0 \). This is a powerful technique for evaluating sums. It tests the student's ability to recognize patterns and simplify sums using the properties of summation, moving beyond simple computational skills.

Q11. If \( \sum_{k=1}^{6} (x_k - 2)^2 = 30 \) and \( \sum_{k=1}^{6} x_k^2 = 60 \), find \( \sum_{k=1}^{6} x_k \).

A.\( 15 \) ✅
B.\( 10 \)
C.\( 5 \)
D.Cannot be determined.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This is an Olympiad-style problem combining algebra and summation properties. Expand \( \sum (x_k - 2)^2 = \sum (x_k^2 - 4x_k + 4) = \sum x_k^2 - 4\sum x_k + \sum 4 = 60 - 4\sum x_k + 24 = 30 \). Thus, \( 84 - 4\sum x_k = 30 \), so \( 4\sum x_k = 54 \), and \( \sum x_k = 13.5 \). This requires careful expansion, applying the distributive property of sums, and solving a linear equation.

Q12. Which of the following is NOT a property of sums?

A.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (a_k + b_k) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k + \sum_{k=1}^{n} b_k \)
B.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} c = c n \)
C.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k b_k = (\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k)(\sum_{k=1}^{n} b_k) \) ✅
D.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} c a_k = c \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k \)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: This is a Easy question. Options A, B, and D are standard, valid properties of summation. Option C is a common and critical misconception. The sum of a product is NOT the product of the sums. This property only holds in very specific, trivial cases. Recognizing this is essential to avoid errors in more complex problems involving series and sequences.

Q13. A programmer writes a loop to calculate \( S = \sum_{k=1}^{100} (k^2 + 5) \). Their code calculates \( \sum k^2 \) and then adds 5. What is wrong with this logic?

A.Nothing; the logic is correct.
B.It should add 5 for each k, so it must add \( 100 \times 5 = 500 \). ✅
C.It should multiply the sum of k by 5.
D.It should calculate \( (\sum k)^2 + 5 \).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: This is a scenario-based Easy question. The programmer made the common error of treating the '+5' as a constant added once, rather than a constant added in each of the 100 iterations. Since \( \sum_{k=1}^{100} (k^2 + 5) = \sum k^2 + \sum 5 = \sum k^2 + 500 \), the code is incorrect. This highlights the practical importance of the constant multiple rule.

Q15. If \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = 0 \), can we conclude \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k^2 = 0 \)?

A.Yes, because if a sum is zero, every term must be zero.
B.No, because if a sum is zero, the terms could be positive and negative. ✅
C.Yes, because \( a_k^2 = a_k \).
D.No, because the sum of squares is always positive.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: This is a Hard question combining algebra and logic. A sum equal to zero does not imply each term is zero. For example, \( \sum_{k=1}^{2} (1, -1) = 0 \), but \( \sum_{k=1}^{2} (1^2 + (-1)^2) = 2 \). The student must understand the difference between the sum of terms and the sum of their squares. Option B correctly identifies that cancellations can occur.

Q19. If \( \sum_{k=1}^{10} a_k = 100 \), what is \( \sum_{k=1}^{10} (a_k - 5) \)?

A.50 ✅
B.95
C.90
D.105
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This problem tests Easy of the subtraction property. \( \sum (a_k - 5) = \sum a_k - \sum 5 = 100 - 10(5) = 100 - 50 = 50 \). A common error is to subtract 5 only once, leading to 95. This highlights the importance of understanding that the constant is subtracted for each term in the sum.

Q21. If \( m \) and \( n \) are positive integers with \( m < n \), what is the value of \( \sum_{k=m}^{n} 1 \)?

B.\( n-m+1 \) ✅
C.\( n+m \)
D.\( n+m-1 \)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: This is a common point of confusion. The number of terms from \( m \) to \( n \) inclusive is \( n-m+1 \). For example, from \( k=2 \) to \( k=5 \), there are 4 terms: 2, 3, 4, 5. The formula \( 5-2+1 = 4 \). Students often forget to add 1. This demonstrates the importance of carefully considering the limits of summation.

Q23. Given \( \sum_{k=1}^{5} a_k = 15 \) and \( \sum_{k=1}^{5} a_k^2 = 55 \), find \( \sum_{k=1}^{5} (a_k - 3)^2 \).

A.10 ✅
B.25
C.40
D.55
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This is an Olympiad-style problem requiring expansion and Easy of the distributive and constant multiple rules. \( \sum (a_k-3)^2 = \sum (a_k^2 - 6a_k + 9) = \sum a_k^2 - 6\sum a_k + \sum 9 = 55 - 6(15) + 45 = 55 - 90 + 45 = 10 \). It requires careful expansion and the ability to combine multiple properties in a single problem.

Q26. If \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k = 0 \), which of the following must be true?

A.All \( a_k = 0 \) for all k.
B.The sum of the positive \( a_k \) equals the absolute sum of the negative \( a_k \). ✅
C.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} |a_k| = 0 \)
D.\( \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k^2 = 0 \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: This is a conceptual question testing the meaning of a sum. A zero sum means the total positive contribution equals the total negative contribution. It does not mean all terms are zero (A), or that their absolute values sum to zero (C), which is only true if all terms are zero. Similarly, the sum of squares is not necessarily zero (D). Option B correctly identifies the condition for cancellation.
