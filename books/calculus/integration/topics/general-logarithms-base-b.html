What is General logarithms base b?

Definition:
Logarithms with base b b b are defined as log⁡b(x)=ln⁡(x)ln⁡(b) \log_b(x) = \frac{\ln(x)}{\ln(b)} logb​(x)=ln(b)ln(x)​. Their derivative is ddxlog⁡b(x)=1xln⁡(b) \frac{d}{dx}\log_b(x) = \frac{1}{x \ln(b)} dxd​logb​(x)=xln(b)1​. This change-of-base formula links all logs to the natural log.

Example:
Derivative of log⁡10(x) \log_{10}(x) log10​(x). Using formula: 1xln⁡(10) \frac{1}{x \ln(10)} xln(10)1​. Since ln⁡(10)≈2.3 \ln(10) \approx 2.3 ln(10)≈2.3, slope is smaller than for ln⁡(x) \ln(x) ln(x).

Reason:
General logs are used in decibels, pH, and other scales; understanding their calculus allows analysis of these logarithmic scales in scientific contexts.
