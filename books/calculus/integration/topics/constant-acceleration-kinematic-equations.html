What is Constant acceleration kinematic equations?

Definition:
For constant acceleration aaa, integration yields v(t)=at+v0v(t) = at + v_0v(t)=at+v0​ and s(t)=12at2+v0t+s0s(t) = \frac{1}{2}at^2 + v_0t + s_0s(t)=21​at2+v0​t+s0​. These are the standard kinematic equations derived directly from calculus principles.

Example:
Car accelerates at 2m/s22 m/s^22m/s2 from rest. v(t)=2tv(t) = 2tv(t)=2t. s(t)=t2s(t) = t^2s(t)=t2. At t=5s, v=10m/sv=10 m/sv=10m/s, s=25ms=25 ms=25m. Matches algebraic formulas.

Reason:
Deriving these from integration reinforces the connection between calculus and classical mechanics, showing that algebraic physics formulas are special cases of general calculus laws.
