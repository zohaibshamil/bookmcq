What is The Indefinite Integral?

Definition:
The indefinite integral represents the family of all antiderivatives of a function f(x)f(x). It is denoted as ∫f(x) dx=F(x)+C∫f(x)dx=F(x)+C, where CC is an arbitrary constant, indicating that there are infinitely many solutions differing by a constant value.

Example:
Evaluate ∫4x3 dx∫4x3dx. Using the power rule, increase exponent by 1 and divide by new exponent: 4x44+C=x4+C44x4​+C=x4+C. Check: derivative of x4+Cx4+C is 4x34x3.

Reason:
It generalizes the process of reversing differentiation, allowing us to find original functions from their rates of change without specific boundary conditions.
