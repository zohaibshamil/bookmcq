Q1. A student simplifies \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (k^2 - 3k + 1) \) as \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} - \frac{3n(n+1)}{2} + n \). This expression is correct but not fully simplified. Which of the following is the fully simplified closed form for this sum?

🔹 A. \( \frac{2n^3 - 3n^2 + 11n}{6} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} - 3n \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n-5)}{6} + n \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n-8)}{6} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This problem requires applying the standard formulas for sums of \( k \) and \( k^2 \), combining them, and simplifying the resulting polynomial. The correct simplification is \( \frac{2n^3 - 3n^2 + 11n}{6} \). Students often correctly apply the formulas but then fail to combine like terms or distribute correctly, leading to common errors. The formula \( \sum_{k=1}^n (a_k + b_k) = \sum a_k + \sum b_k \) is crucial here.

Q2. A student is given the sum \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} k(2k+1) \). They use the formulas \( \sum k = n(n+1)/2 \) and \( \sum k^2 = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 \) to get \( 2 \sum k^2 + \sum k \). Which of the following correctly evaluates this?

🔹 A. \( \frac{n(n+1)(4n+5)}{6} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{3} + \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{n(n+1)(4n+1)}{6} \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+5)}{6} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is a straightforward Easy of the summation properties and standard formulas. The student must correctly identify that \( k(2k+1) = 2k^2 + k \), then apply the formulas. The correct result is \( \frac{2n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} + \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \), which simplifies to \( \frac{n(n+1)(4n+5)}{6} \). Common errors come from misapplying the \( 2 \) factor or making an error in polynomial addition.

Q3. Given the identity \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^3 = [ \sum_{k=1}^{n} k ]^2 \). Which statement about this identity is true?

🔹 A. It can be used to prove that \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^3 \) has a lower growth rate than \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^2 \).
🔹 B. It shows that the sum of cubes is a perfect square for all positive integers \( n \).
🔹 C. It is a consequence of the formula for the sum of \( k^2 \).
🔹 D. The identity is only true for even values of \( n \).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

This fundamental identity states that the sum of the first \( n \) cubes is equal to the square of the sum of the first \( n \) natural numbers. This is a deep and powerful result. Option B is the correct Medium. The other options represent common misconceptions: that it relates to growth rate (incorrect, both are quartic), that it arises from the \( k^2 \) formula, or that it's conditionally true.

Q4. Evaluate the sum \( \sum_{k=1}^{20} (3k^2 - 2k) \).

🔹 A. 8610
🔹 B. 86100
🔹 C. 2870
🔹 D. 28700

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This problem requires direct Easy of the summation formulas. The sum is calculated as \( 3 \sum_{k=1}^{20} k^2 - 2 \sum_{k=1}^{20} k \). Using the formulas, \( 3 \times \frac{20(21)(41)}{6} - 2 \times \frac{20(21)}{2} = 8610 - 420 = 8610 \). Students often make arithmetic errors when evaluating the fractions or misunderstand the distributive property of summation, leading to options that reflect mistakes in calculation.

Q5. Which of the following is NOT a valid property of sums?

🔹 A. \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} c a_k = c \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k \)
🔹 B. \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (a_k + b_k) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k + \sum_{k=1}^{n} b_k \)
🔹 C. \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (a_k b_k) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k \sum_{k=1}^{n} b_k \)
🔹 D. \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} (a_k - b_k) = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k - \sum_{k=1}^{n} b_k \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A common misconception is that summation distributes over multiplication, which is false. The correct property for sums is that it distributes over addition and subtraction, and constant factors can be pulled out. The product of sums is not the sum of products. Students who try to treat the sum as a linear operator in all cases will make this mistake. A counterexample quickly disproves this option, e.g., for \( n=2, a_1=1, a_2=2, b_1=3, b_2=4 \).

Q6. A sequence of approximations to the area under a curve is given by \( A_n = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{k^2}{n^2} \). What is the value of this limit as \( n \to \infty \)?

🔹 A. 01-Mar
🔹 B. 0
🔹 C. 01-Feb
🔹 D. 01-Apr

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This problem connects summation to Riemann sums and area approximation. The expression represents a Riemann sum for the function \( f(x) = x^2 \) on the interval [0,1]. Its limit is the definite integral \( \int_0^1 x^2 dx = 1/3 \). The concept is often tested in calculus. Students might try to compute the sum using formulas, leading to \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6n^3} \), whose limit is \( 1/3 \). The key is recognizing the sum as a Riemann sum.

The correct formula for the sum of squares is \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} \). A student making this error is confusing the formulas for \( \sum k^2 \) and \( \sum k^3 \). The other options are not valid critiques of this specific mistake.

Q8. Simplify \( \sum_{k=3}^{n+2} (k-1)^2 \).

🔹 A. \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{(n+1)(n+2)(2n+3)}{6} - 1 \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} + 1 \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{(n+1)(n+2)(2n+3)}{6} + 2 \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This problem tests the concept of shifting indices. By letting \( j = k-1 \), the sum becomes \( \sum_{j=2}^{n+1} j^2 \). This can be separated as \( \sum_{j=1}^{n+1} j^2 - 1 \). Using the formula for \( \sum_{j=1}^{n+1} j^2 \) gives \( \frac{(n+1)(n+2)(2n+3)}{6} - 1 \). The correct answer simplifies to the standard formula for \( \sum_{k=1}^n k^2 \), which is \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} \). A common mistake is failing to adjust the limits correctly.

Q10. A car accelerates such that its speed at the end of the \( k \)-th second is \( k^2 \) m/s. What is the total distance traveled from \( t=1 \) to \( t=n \) seconds if distance is speed times time?

📖 Explanation:

This is a modeling problem. Distance is speed × time. Since time is 1 second for each interval, the distance is the sum of speeds: \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^2 \). The correct closed form is \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} \). The student must recognize which sum is being modeled. The scenario helps to apply the formula in a real-world setting, moving beyond abstract computation.

Q11. A student reasons that since \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} k = \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \), the sum of the first n odd numbers must be \( \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \). What is wrong with this reasoning?

🔹 A. Odd numbers are not the same as the first n natural numbers.
🔹 B. The sum of odd numbers is \( n^2 \).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

This question tests the ability to identify a common logical error. The student incorrectly assumes that a formula for one set of numbers applies to another. The sum of the first \( n \) odd numbers is a classic result: \( 1+3+5+\dots+(2n-1) = n^2 \). A simple counterexample for \( n=2 \) is \( 1+3=4 \), while \( 2(3)/2 = 3 \), proving the student's reasoning is flawed. Option B is the correct formula for the sum of odd numbers.

Q13. What is the limit of \( \frac{1}{n^4} \sum_{k=1}^{n} k^3 \) as \( n \) approaches infinity?

The student is likely applying a formula from an expansion of \( k^2 + k \) but forgetting to subtract the \( \sum k \) part. This is a high-level Medium question.

Q23. The formula for \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} k(k-1) \) is:

🔹 A. \( \frac{n(n+1)(n-1)}{3} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{n(n+1)(n-1)}{6} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} - n \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{n(n+1)}{2} - n \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is a more advanced derivation. \( \sum k(k-1) = \sum k^2 - \sum k \). Using the standard formulas, we get \( \frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6} - \frac{n(n+1)}{2} \). Simplifying this, we get \( \frac{n(n+1)}{6}(2n+1 - 3) = \frac{n(n+1)(2n-2)}{6} = \frac{n(n+1)(n-1)}{3} \). This problem requires a manipulation and simplification of the summation formulas, testing the ability to derive new formulas from existing ones. It's a classic Olympiad-style Easy.

Errors often arise from incorrectly substituting or not changing the limits.

Q25. Given the graph of \( y=x^2 \) and the area under it from \( x=0 \) to \( x=1 \), which Riemann sum expression corresponds to the left endpoint approximation with \( n \) rectangles?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \left( \frac{k}{n} \right)^2 \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} \left( \frac{k}{n} \right)^2 \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \left( \frac{k+1}{n} \right)^2 \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{k=1}^{n} \left( \frac{k-1}{n} \right)^2 \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This problem connects the geometric concept of area under a curve to algebraic summation notation. The left endpoint of the \( k \)-th subinterval is \( (k-1)/n \). The sum is \( \sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{n} ((k-1)/n)^2 \). By shifting the index \( j = k-1 \), it becomes \( \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} (j/n)^2 \). This tests the connection between Riemann sums and summations, an important bridge to calculus.
