What is Work energy theorem calculus?

Definition:
The Work-Energy Theorem states that the net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy. Wnet​=ΔKE=21​mvf2​−21​mvi2​. In calculus, this connects the integral of force over distance to the change in velocity squared.

Example:
A 2kg object accelerates from 3 m/s to 5 m/s. Solution: W=21​(2)(52−32)=25−9=16 Joules.

Reason:
This theorem provides a powerful alternative to Newton's laws for solving motion problems, especially when forces are complex or time-dependent, by focusing on energy states rather than instantaneous acceleration.

Q19. A student states: 'If an object's kinetic energy is zero, the net force acting on it must be zero.' Evaluate this statement.

A.True, because zero kinetic energy means zero motion
B.False, because kinetic energy depends on speed, not force ✅
C.True, because energy conservation requires it
D.False, because forces could be doing no work while object is at rest
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The statement is false. An object can have zero kinetic energy (i.e., be at rest) while forces are acting on it, as long as the forces are balanced (net force is zero) or the object is at a turning point where forces do no work. More fundamentally, kinetic energy is a function of speed, not force. A net force can be zero while kinetic energy is zero (object at rest), but zero kinetic energy doesn't require zero net force - consider an object thrown upward at the highest point: kinetic energy is zero but net force (gravity) is not zero. Distractor A incorrectly links energy to motion; C misapplies conservation laws; D has the right answer but for the wrong reason.

Q21. A graph of kinetic energy versus position shows the kinetic energy increasing then decreasing. What can you conclude about the force?
