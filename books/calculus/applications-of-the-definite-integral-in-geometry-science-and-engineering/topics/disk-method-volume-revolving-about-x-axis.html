Q1. Which of the following is the correct formula for the volume of a solid generated by revolving the region bounded by \(y = f(x)\), the x-axis, and the lines \(x = a\) and \(x = b\) about the x-axis, assuming \(f(x) \ge 0\)?

🔹 A. \(V = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2 \, dx\)
🔹 B. \(V = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)] \, dx\)
🔹 C. \(V = \pi \int_a^b [f'(x)]^2 \, dx\)
🔹 D. \(V = \int_a^b [f(x)]^2 \, dx\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is a Easy question. The method of disks states that when a region under \(y=f(x)\) is revolved around the x-axis, the cross-sectional area at any point x is a circle with radius \(f(x)\), giving area \(\pi[f(x)]^2\). The volume is the integral of this area from a to b. Therefore, the correct formula includes the \(\pi\) outside the integral and squares the function inside.

Q14. Find the volume of the solid generated by revolving the region bounded by \(y = \sqrt{4 - x^2}\) and the x-axis about the x-axis.

🔹 C. \(\frac{8\pi}{3}\)
🔹 D. \(\frac{64\pi}{3}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The curve \(y = \sqrt{4 - x^2}\) is the upper half of a circle of radius 2. Revolving this region about the x-axis generates a sphere. The volume should be \(\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi (2)^3 = \frac{32\pi}{3}\). Using the disk method: \(V = \pi \int_{-2}^{2} (\sqrt{4-x^2})^2 dx = \pi \int_{-2}^{2} (4-x^2) dx = \pi [4x - x^3/3]_{-2}^{2} = \pi [(8 - 8/3) - (-8 + 8/3)] = \pi [(16/3) - (-16/3)] = 32\pi/3\).

Q17. Consider the region bounded by \(y = x^2\) and the x-axis from \(x=0\) to \(x=1\). If this region is revolved about the x-axis, the volume is \(V = \frac{\pi}{5}\). Now, consider the region bounded by \(y = x^4\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=1\). Without integrating, what is the volume of the solid formed by revolving this new region about the x-axis?

🔹 A. \(\frac{\pi}{9}\)
🔹 B. \(\frac{\pi}{7}\)
🔹 C. \(\frac{\pi}{5}\)
🔹 D. \(\frac{\pi}{3}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is an Easy question. For \(y=x^n\), the volume from 0 to 1 is \(V = \pi \int_0^1 (x^n)^2 dx = \pi \int_0^1 x^{2n} dx = \frac{\pi}{2n+1}\). For \(n=2\), this is \(\frac{\pi}{5}\). For \(n=4\), this is \(\frac{\pi}{2(4)+1} = \frac{\pi}{9}\). A common mistake is to assume the volume scales with the function, but the squaring inside the integral changes the exponent.

Q20. The volume of the solid generated by rotating the region under \(y = \sqrt{x}\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=k\) about the x-axis is \(8\pi\). What is the value of k?

🔹 A. 4
🔹 B. 2
🔹 C. \(\sqrt{8}\)
🔹 D. 8

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is a Hard question involving working backwards. The volume is \(V = \pi \int_0^k (\sqrt{x})^2 dx = \pi \int_0^k x dx = \pi [x^2/2]_0^k = \frac{\pi k^2}{2}\). We are given that this equals \(8\pi\), so \(\frac{\pi k^2}{2} = 8\pi\). Dividing by \(\pi\), \(\frac{k^2}{2} = 8\), so \(k^2 = 16\), and \(k = 4\) (since \(k>0\)).

A common mistake is to forget the factor of 1/3 when integrating \(x^2\), leading to \(\pi \frac{r^2}{h^2} [\frac{x^3}{2}]_0^h = \frac{1}{2}\pi r^2 h\).

This tests the interpretation of the geometry. The radius of the base of the cone is the y-value at the end of the interval. The function is \(y=2x\). At \(x=3\), the y-value is \(y = 2(3) = 6\). This is the radius of the largest disk and hence the radius of the base of the cone. The height of the cone is 3.

Q30. If the volume of a solid formed by rotating the region under \(y = f(x)\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=2\) about the x-axis is \(V\), what is the volume if the same region is rotated about the x-axis but the function is scaled by a factor of 2, i.e., \(y = 2f(x)\)?

🔹 A. \(4V\)
🔹 B. \(2V\)
🔹 C. \(8V\)
🔹 D. \(V\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is a scaling reasoning question. If \(f(x)\) is replaced by \(2f(x)\), the new volume is \(V_{new} = \pi \int_0^2 [2f(x)]^2 dx = \pi \int_0^2 4[f(x)]^2 dx = 4\pi \int_0^2 [f(x)]^2 dx = 4V\). The volume scales with the square of the scaling factor because the radius is squared in the integrand. A common mistake is to think it scales linearly.

Q31. The region bounded by \(y = x^2\) and \(y = 4\) is revolved about the x-axis. To find the volume, one could use the disk method if the region is considered as the area under which curve?

🔹 A. The disk method cannot be used because the region is not bounded by the x-axis.
🔹 B. \(y = 4\) from \(x=-2\) to \(x=2\)
🔹 C. \(y = x^2\) from \(x=-2\) to \(x=2\)
🔹 D. The region can be split into two parts, each under a different curve.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is an analysis of method applicability. The region bounded by \(y=x^2\) and \(y=4\) is between two curves. The x-axis is not a boundary. To use the disk method, the region would need to be bounded by the x-axis. One could use the washer method directly. Alternatively, one could use the disk method for two separate regions if the region is split. For example, the area between \(y=x^2\) and the x-axis from -2 to 2, and the area between \(y=4\) and the x-axis from -2 to 2. The volume of the solid is the difference of the volumes of these two solids.

Q32. A student evaluates the integral for the volume of a solid of revolution as \(\int_0^1 \pi x^2 dx = \frac{\pi}{3}\). The student claims the radius of the disk at x=0.5 is 0.5. Is this correct based on the integral?

🔹 A. Yes, because the integrand is \(\pi x^2\), so the radius is \(x\).
🔹 B. No, the radius is \(\sqrt{x^2} = x\), but the radius function must be identified correctly.
🔹 C. Yes, because the volume is \(\frac{\pi}{3}\).
🔹 D. No, the radius is \(\pi x^2\).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This tests the ability to interpret an integral. The integrand is \(\pi [f(x)]^2\). If the integrand is \(\pi x^2\), then \([f(x)]^2 = x^2\), so \(f(x) = x\) (assuming positive radius). Therefore, the radius at x=0.5 is indeed 0.5. The student is correct. The question tests if the student can correctly extract the radius function from the integrand.

Q33. If the region under \(y = \sqrt{4 - x^2}\) from \(x=-2\) to \(x=2\) is revolved about the x-axis, the volume is \( \frac{32\pi}{3}\). If the region is revolved about the line \(y=-2\), what would be the new radius function?

🔹 A. \(f(x) + 2 = \sqrt{4 - x^2} + 2\)
🔹 B. \(f(x) - 2 = \sqrt{4 - x^2} - 2\)
🔹 C. \(2 - f(x) = 2 - \sqrt{4 - x^2}\)
🔹 D. \(f(x) = \sqrt{4 - x^2}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is a Hard extension question. The disk method requires the radius to be the distance from the axis of revolution to the curve. If the axis is \(y=-2\), the distance from the axis to a point on the curve \(y=f(x)\) is \(f(x) - (-2) = f(x) + 2\). So the radius function becomes \(\sqrt{4 - x^2} + 2\). This involves modifying the radius function for a shifted axis and is a more complex Medium of the disk method concept.

Q34. A solid of revolution is formed by rotating the region bounded by \(y = x^2\) and \(y = 0\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=1\) about the x-axis. What is the approximate volume if the region is approximated by 4 disks of equal width using right endpoints?

🔹 A. \(\pi \sum_{i=1}^4 (\frac{i}{4})^2 \cdot \frac{1}{4}\)
🔹 B. \(\pi \sum_{i=1}^4 (\frac{i-1}{4})^2 \cdot \frac{1}{4}\)
🔹 C. \(\pi \sum_{i=1}^4 (\frac{i}{4})^2\)
🔹 D. \(\sum_{i=1}^4 (\frac{i}{4})^2 \cdot \frac{1}{4}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This tests the understanding of the Riemann sum approximation. For 4 disks from 0 to 1, the width of each disk is \(\Delta x = 1/4\). Using right endpoints, the x-values are \(x_i = i/4\) for \(i=1,2,3,4\). The radius of each disk is \(f(x_i) = (x_i)^2 = (i/4)^2\). The volume of each disk is \(\pi [f(x_i)]^2 \Delta x = \pi (i/4)^2 (1/4)\). The total approximate volume is the sum of these. Option A is correct. This question connects the integral to its Riemann sum representation.
