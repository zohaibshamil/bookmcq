📝 Volume by slicing method

What is Volume by slicing method?

Definition:
The slicing method calculates volume by integrating the cross-sectional area \( A(x) \) perpendicular to an axis. If slices are perpendicular to the x-axis, the volume is \( V = \int_{a}^{b} A(x) \, dx \). This generalizes volume calculation beyond simple geometric shapes to complex solids with known cross-sections.

Example:
Solid with square base \( [0,2] \times [0,2] \) and triangular cross-sections of height equal to base. Area \( A(x) = \frac{1}{2}x^2 \). Solution: \( V = \int_{0}^{2} \frac{1}{2}x^2 \, dx = [\frac{x^3}{6}]_0^2 = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3} \).

Reason:
This method is fundamental because it applies to any solid where the cross-sectional area can be defined as a function, allowing for the calculation of volumes of irregular objects in engineering design.

Q1. A student claims that the volume of any solid can be found by simply multiplying the area of its base by its height. Is this statement always correct, and why?

A. Yes, because all solids have a base and a constant height.
B. Yes, but only if the solid is a prism or a cylinder.
C. No, because the cross-sectional area must be constant along the height for this formula to work. ✅
D. No, because the base area must be a circle for this to be valid.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

This statement reflects a common misconception. The formula \( V = \text{base area} \times \text{height} \) only applies to right cylinders (solids with uniform cross-sections). For solids with varying cross-sections, like a pyramid or a cone, this formula fails. The correct approach is to integrate the cross-sectional area function \( A(x) \) along the axis, which accounts for changes in the shape and size of the slices. The student's claim is a misinterpretation of the slicing method, which requires an integral, not a simple product.

Q2. A solid's base is the region between \( y = x^2 \) and \( y = 4 \) for \( 0 \le x \le 2 \). Cross-sections perpendicular to the x-axis are squares. A student sets up the integral for the volume as \( \int_{0}^{2} (4 - x^2)^2 \, dx \). Is this setup correct?

A. Yes, the integrand correctly represents the area of the square cross-section. ✅
B. No, the integrand should be \( \sqrt{4 - x^2} \) to find the side length.
C. No, the limits of integration should be from 0 to 4.
D. Yes, but the integral should be multiplied by 2.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The student's setup is correct. At any \( x \) in [0, 2], the vertical distance between the curves \( y = x^2 \) and \( y = 4 \) is \( 4 - x^2 \). Since the cross-sections are squares, the side length is this distance, and the area of each square is \( (4 - x^2)^2 \). The solid is bounded by \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 2 \), so the limits of integration are correct. The student has successfully applied the slicing method by integrating the area of the square cross-sections over the interval. This is a direct Medium of the volume formula \( V = \int A(x) \, dx \).

Q4. A solid is formed such that its cross-sections perpendicular to the y-axis are rectangles with height equal to twice the width. If the solid is bounded by \( x = 0 \), \( x = y^2 \), \( y = 0 \), and \( y = 2 \), what is the volume?

A. \( \frac{32}{5} \)
B. \( \frac{16}{5} \)
C. \( 8 \)
D. \( \frac{64}{5} \) ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

This problem requires careful setup. The region is bounded by \( x = 0 \) and \( x = y^2 \) for \( y \in [0, 2] \). The width of the cross-section perpendicular to the y-axis is the horizontal distance between the curves, which is \( y^2 \). Since the height is twice the width, the height is \( 2y^2 \). The area of the rectangular cross-section is \( A(y) = \text{width} \times \text{height} = y^2 \cdot 2y^2 = 2y^4 \). The volume is \( V = \int_{0}^{2} 2y^4 \, dy = 2 \left[ \frac{y^5}{5} \right]_{0}^{2} = \frac{2 \cdot 32}{5} = \frac{64}{5} \). This is a multi-step problem involving interpreting the geometry and setting up the integral correctly. The distractors come from common errors like not squaring the width or mixing up the axes.

Q5. Which of the following integrals represents the volume of a solid whose base is the region bounded by \( y = \sin x \), \( y = 0 \), \( x = 0 \), and \( x = \pi \), and whose cross-sections perpendicular to the x-axis are semicircles with diameters on the base?

A. \( \frac{\pi}{8} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin^2 x \, dx \) ✅
B. \( \frac{\pi}{2} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin x \, dx \)
C. \( \frac{\pi}{4} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin^2 x \, dx \)
D. \( \frac{\pi}{8} \int_{0}^{\pi} \sin x \, dx \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The diameter of each semicircle is the vertical distance between the curves, which is \( \sin x \). The radius is \( \frac{\sin x}{2} \). The area of a semicircle is \( \frac{1}{2} \pi r^2 = \frac{1}{2} \pi \left( \frac{\sin x}{2} \right)^2 = \frac{\pi}{8} \sin^2 x \). Thus, the volume is \( V = \int_{0}^{\pi} \frac{\pi}{8} \sin^2 x \, dx \). This matches option A. The other options arise from incorrectly calculating the area of the semicircle (e.g., forgetting the factor of \( 1/2 \) or using the diameter instead of the radius). This question tests the ability to translate a geometric description into a correct integral expression.

Q7. A solid has a circular base of radius 2. Cross-sections perpendicular to a diameter are squares. What is the volume of the solid?

A. \( \frac{32}{3} \)
B. \( \frac{64}{3} \)
C. \( \frac{128}{3} \) ✅
D. \( 64 \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

This is a classic problem. Place the circle with center at the origin and diameter along the x-axis. The equation of the circle is \( x^2 + y^2 = 4 \). For a given \( x \), the vertical distance across the circle is \( 2y = 2\sqrt{4 - x^2} \). This is the side length of the square cross-section. The area is \( A(x) = (2\sqrt{4 - x^2})^2 = 4(4 - x^2) \). The volume is \( V = \int_{-2}^{2} 4(4 - x^2) \, dx = 4 \left[ 4x - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-2}^{2} = 4 \left( (8 - \frac{8}{3}) - (-8 + \frac{8}{3}) \right) = 4 \left( \frac{16}{3} - (-\frac{16}{3}) \right) = 4 \cdot \frac{32}{3} = \frac{128}{3} \). The correct answer is \( \frac{128}{3} \). This problem requires setting up the integral from the geometry of the circle and correctly identifying the side length of the square.

Option A is incorrect because it uses \( 4 - y \) as the side length instead of \( 2\sqrt{4 - y} \). Option C is the setup for cross-sections perpendicular to the x-axis. Q16. Which of the following integrals represents the volume of a solid whose base is the region bounded by \( y = x^2 \) and \( y = 4x - x^2 \), and whose cross-sections perpendicular to the x-axis are semicircles with diameters on the base?

A. \( \frac{\pi}{8} \int_{0}^{2} (4x - 2x^2)^2 \, dx \) ✅
B. \( \frac{\pi}{4} \int_{0}^{2} (4x - 2x^2)^2 \, dx \)
C. \( \frac{\pi}{2} \int_{0}^{2} (4x - 2x^2) \, dx \)
D. \( \frac{\pi}{8} \int_{0}^{2} (4x - 2x^2) \, dx \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

First, find the points of intersection: \( x^2 = 4x - x^2 \) => \( 2x^2 - 4x = 0 \) => \( 2x(x - 2) = 0 \), so \( x = 0 \) and \( x = 2 \). The vertical distance between the curves is \( (4x - x^2) - x^2 = 4x - 2x^2 \). This is the diameter of the semicircle. The radius is \( \frac{4x - 2x^2}{2} = 2x - x^2 \). The area of a semicircle is \( \frac{1}{2} \pi r^2 = \frac{1}{2} \pi (2x - x^2)^2 = \frac{\pi}{2} (2x - x^2)^2 = \frac{\pi}{8} (4x - 2x^2)^2 \). So the volume is \( V = \int_{0}^{2} \frac{\pi}{8} (4x - 2x^2)^2 \, dx \). This matches option A. The other options have incorrect factors of \( \pi \) or use the diameter instead of the radius in the area formula.

Q28. The student writes the integral as \( \int_{0}^{1} 2(x^2 - x) \, dx \). Is this correct?

A. Yes, the setup is correct.
B. No, the limits should be from 1 to 0.
C. No, the integrand should be \( 2(x - x^2) \). ✅
D. No, the integrand should be \( (x - x^2)^2 \).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The student's setup is incorrect. The region is bounded above by \( y = x \) and below by \( y = x^2 \) on the interval \( [0, 1] \). The vertical distance between the curves is \( x - x^2 \), not \( x^2 - x \). The height of the rectangle is 2, so the area is \( A(x) = 2(x - x^2) \). The correct integral is \( \int_{0}^{1} 2(x - x^2) \, dx \). Option C correctly identifies the error. Option A is incorrect because the sign in the integrand is wrong. Option B is incorrect because the limits are in the correct order. Option D is incorrect because it squares the distance, which would be for square cross-sections.

Q29. A solid has a base that is a circular disk of radius 3. Cross-sections perpendicular to the x-axis are semicircles with diameters in the base. What is the volume?

A. \( 18\pi \) ✅
B. \( 36\pi \)
C. \( 9\pi \)
D. \( 27\pi \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The base is the circle \( x^2 + y^2 = 9 \). The vertical distance across the circle is \( 2\sqrt{9 - x^2} \). This is the diameter of the semicircle. The radius of the semicircle is \( \sqrt{9 - x^2} \). The area of the semicircle is \( \frac{1}{2} \pi (\sqrt{9 - x^2})^2 = \frac{\pi}{2} (9 - x^2) \). The volume is \( V = \int_{-3}^{3} \frac{\pi}{2} (9 - x^2) \, dx = \frac{\pi}{2} \left[ 9x - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_{-3}^{3} = \frac{\pi}{2} \left( (27 - 9) - (-27 + 9) \right) = \frac{\pi}{2} (18 - (-18)) = \frac{\pi}{2} \cdot 36 = 18\pi \). The correct answer is \( 18\pi \). This problem is a straightforward Medium of the slicing method with a circular base.

Q30. A solid has a base that is the region bounded by the parabola \( y = 4 - x^2 \) and the x-axis. Cross-sections perpendicular to the x-axis are squares. What is the volume?

A. \( \frac{512}{15} \) ✅
B. \( \frac{256}{15} \)
C. \( \frac{128}{15} \)
D. \( \frac{64}{15} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The base is bounded by \( y = 4 - x^2 \) and the x-axis. The x-intercepts are \( x = \pm 2 \). The vertical distance from the x-axis to the parabola is \( 4 - x^2 \). This is the side length of the square. The area is \( A(x) = (4 - x^2)^2 \). The volume is \( V = \int_{-2}^{2} (4 - x^2)^2 \, dx = \int_{-2}^{2} (16 - 8x^2 + x^4) \, dx = \left[ 16x - \frac{8x^3}{3} + \frac{x^5}{5} \right]_{-2}^{2} = (32 - \frac{64}{3} + \frac{32}{5}) - (-32 + \frac{64}{3} - \frac{32}{5}) = 64 - \frac{128}{3} + \frac{64}{5} = \frac{960 - 640 + 192}{15} = \frac{512}{15} \). The correct answer is \( \frac{512}{15} \). This problem is a standard example of the slicing method.
