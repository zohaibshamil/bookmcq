What is Derivatives integrals inverse hyperbolic functions?

The derivative of the inverse hyperbolic sine is \( \frac{d}{dx}\sinh^{-1} x = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+1}} \), and corresponding integral formulas like \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} = \sinh^{-1}(\frac{x}{a}) + C \) allow evaluation of integrals containing radical expressions that resist standard trigonometric substitution.

Q1. If \(y = \sinh^{-1}(3x^2)\), then \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) is:

A. \(\frac{6x}{\sqrt{1+9x^4}}\) ✅
B. \(\frac{2x}{\sqrt{1+9x^4}}\)
C. \(\frac{6x}{\sqrt{1-9x^4}}\)
D. \(\frac{6x}{\sqrt{9x^4-1}}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To find the derivative, use the chain rule. The derivative of \(\sinh^{-1}(u)\) with respect to x is \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^2}} \frac{du}{dx}\). Here, \(u = 3x^2\), so \(du/dx = 6x\). Substituting gives \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(3x^2)^2}} \cdot 6x = \frac{6x}{\sqrt{1+9x^4}}\). Option B misses the factor from the derivative of the inside function, option C uses the derivative formula for \(\cosh^{-1}\), and option D is for the derivative of \(\cosh^{-1}\).

The domain of \(\cosh^{-1}(u)\) requires \(u>1\), which is satisfied here for \(x>0\). Option B is the derivative of \(\sinh^{-1}(e^x)\), and option C misses the chain rule factor for the derivative of \(e^x\).

Q5. A student writes \(\frac{d}{dx}[\sinh^{-1}(\tan x)] = \frac{\sec^2 x}{\sqrt{1-\tan^2 x}}\). Is this correct?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The student's error lies in using the derivative formula for \(\sin^{-1}\) instead of \(\sinh^{-1}\). Here, \(u=\tan x\), so the correct derivative is \(\frac{\sec^2 x}{\sqrt{1+\tan^2 x}}\). This can be simplified to \(|\cos x| \sec^2 x\) or \(\sec x |\sec x|\), but the key is the plus sign in the denominator. Understanding the distinction between trigonometric and hyperbolic inverse derivatives is essential.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The integrand \(\frac{1}{1-x^2}\) has a vertical asymptote at \(x=1\) and \(x=-1\), so the integral is improper. The integral \(\int_{-1}^{1} \frac{dx}{1-x^2}\) diverges to infinity. This is a common trap. The antiderivative is \(\tanh^{-1}(x)\), but it is only valid for \(|x|<1\). At the endpoints, the function is undefined. Option A is the antiderivative evaluated at the endpoints, but it's not a finite value. Option C is incorrect. Option D is also infinite. The correct answer is that the integral is divergent.

Q31. If \(y = \sinh^{-1}(\frac{1-x}{1+x})\), find \(dy/dx\).

A. \(-\frac{2}{(1+x)\sqrt{(1-x)^2+(1+x)^2}}\) ✅
B. \(-\frac{2}{(1+x)^2\sqrt{1+(\frac{1-x}{1+x})^2}}\)
C. \(\frac{2}{(1+x)\sqrt{1+(\frac{1-x}{1+x})^2}}\)
D. \(-\frac{2}{(1+x)^2}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Let \(u = \frac{1-x}{1+x}\). Then \(du/dx = \frac{-(1+x)-(1-x)}{(1+x)^2} = \frac{-2}{(1+x)^2}\). The derivative is \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^2}} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\frac{1-x}{1+x})^2}} \cdot \frac{-2}{(1+x)^2}\). Simplifying: \(\sqrt{1+(\frac{1-x}{1+x})^2} = \sqrt{\frac{(1+x)^2+(1-x)^2}{(1+x)^2}} = \frac{\sqrt{(1-x)^2+(1+x)^2}}{|1+x|}\). So the derivative is \(-\frac{2}{(1+x)^2} \cdot \frac{|1+x|}{\sqrt{(1-x)^2+(1+x)^2}} = -\frac{2}{(1+x)\sqrt{(1-x)^2+(1+x)^2}}\) for \(1+x>0\). Option A is this simplified form. Option B is unsimplified with an extra factor of \(1/(1+x)\). Option C has the wrong sign. Option D misses the square root term.
