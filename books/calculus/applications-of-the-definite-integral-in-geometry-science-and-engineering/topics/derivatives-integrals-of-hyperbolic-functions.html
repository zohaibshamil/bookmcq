What is Derivatives integrals of hyperbolic functions?

Definition:
Derivatives: \( \frac{d}{dx}\sinh x = \cosh x \), \( \frac{d}{dx}\cosh x = \sinh x \). Integrals: \( \int \sinh x \, dx = \cosh x + C \), \( \int \cosh x \, dx = \sinh x + C \). Note the sign change in derivative of cosh compared to trig cos.

Example:
Find \( \int \sinh(3x) \, dx \). Solution: Let \( u=3x \), \( du=3dx \). \( \frac{1}{3} \int \sinh u \, du = \frac{1}{3} \cosh(3x) + C \).

Reason:
Knowing these derivatives and integrals allows for the efficient solution of differential equations modeling heat transfer, wave propagation, and other physical processes where hyperbolic functions naturally arise.

Q1. What is the derivative of \( \sinh(x^2) \) with respect to \( x \)?

A. \( 2x \cosh(x^2) \) ✅
B. \( 2x \sinh(x^2) \)
C. \( \cosh(x^2) \)
D. \( 2x \cosh(x^2) + \sinh(x^2) \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of \( \sinh(u) \) is \( \cosh(u) \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \). Here, \( u = x^2 \), so \( \frac{du}{dx} = 2x \). Thus, the derivative is \( 2x \cosh(x^2) \). Options B, C, and D incorrectly apply the chain rule or the derivative formula.

Q2. Evaluate \( \int \sinh(3x) \, dx \).

A. \( \frac{1}{3} \cosh(3x) + C \) ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{3} \sinh(3x) + C \)
C. \( 3 \cosh(3x) + C \)
D. \( -\frac{1}{3} \cosh(3x) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The integral of \( \sinh(ax) \) is \( \frac{1}{a} \cosh(ax) + C \). For \( a = 3 \), the answer is \( \frac{1}{3} \cosh(3x) + C \). Option B is the derivative of \( \sinh(3x) \), and Option D would be the integral of \( -\sinh(3x) \). Option C is missing the division by 3.

Q3. Given that \( \frac{d}{dx}[\tanh x] = \operatorname{sech}^2 x \), what is \( \int \operatorname{sech}^2(2x) \, dx \)?

A. \( \frac{1}{2} \tanh(2x) + C \) ✅
B. \( 2 \tanh(2x) + C \)
C. \( \tanh(2x) + C \)
D. \( \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{sech}^2(2x) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The integral of \( \operatorname{sech}^2(u) \) is \( \tanh(u) + C \). However, due to the chain rule in reverse, we must account for the derivative of \( u = 2x \). Since \( \frac{d}{dx}[\tanh(2x)] = 2 \operatorname{sech}^2(2x) \), the integral is \( \frac{1}{2} \tanh(2x) + C \). Options B and C ignore the need to divide by the derivative of the inner function, and Option D is not a valid integration result.

Q4. If \( y = \ln(\tanh x) \), what is \( \frac{dy}{dx} \)?

A. \( \frac{\operatorname{sech}^2 x}{\tanh x} \) ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{\tanh x} \)
C. \( \operatorname{sech}^2 x \)
D. \( \frac{\tanh x}{\operatorname{sech}^2 x} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using the chain rule, \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\tanh x} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}[\tanh x] = \frac{\operatorname{sech}^2 x}{\tanh x} \). This can also be simplified to \( \frac{1}{\sinh x \cosh x} \). Option B is the derivative of \( \ln(\tanh x) \) without applying the chain rule, and Option C is the derivative of \( \tanh x \). Option D is the reciprocal of the correct expression.

Q5. What is the derivative of \( \cosh^{-1}(x) \) for \( x > 1 \)?

A. \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 - 1}} \) ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^2}} \)
C. \( \frac{1}{x^2 - 1} \)
D. \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of the inverse hyperbolic cosine is a standard formula: \( \frac{d}{dx}[\cosh^{-1} x] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 - 1}} \). Option B is the derivative of \( \sin^{-1} x \). Option D is the derivative of \( \sinh^{-1} x \). Option C is missing the square root. This formula is valid for \( x > 1 \), as \( \cosh^{-1} x \) is only defined for \( x \ge 1 \).

Q6. Evaluate the integral: \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{9x^2 + 16}} \).

A. \( \frac{1}{3} \sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{3x}{4}\right) + C \) ✅
B. \( \sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{3x}{4}\right) + C \)
C. \( \frac{1}{3} \sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{4x}{3}\right) + C \)
D. \( \ln(3x + \sqrt{9x^2 + 16}) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The integral is of the form \( \int \frac{du}{\sqrt{u^2 + a^2}} \). Here, \( u = 3x \), so \( du = 3 dx \). Thus, \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{(3x)^2 + 4^2}} = \frac{1}{3} \sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{3x}{4}\right) + C \). Option B misses the \( \frac{1}{3} \) factor. Option C incorrectly identifies the coefficients. While Option D is a logarithmic form, it lacks the correct multiplicative constant and is equivalent to \( \sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{3x}{4}\right) + \ln(4) \), not \( \frac{1}{3} \sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{3x}{4}\right) \).

Q7. A student claims that \( \int \frac{dx}{4 - x^2} = \frac{1}{2} \tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) + C \). What is the flaw in this reasoning?

A. The formula is only valid for \( |x| < 2 \). For \( |x| > 2 \), the answer involves \( \coth^{-1} \). ✅
B. The derivative of \( \tanh^{-1}(x/2) \) is \( \frac{1}{4 - x^2} \), not \( \frac{2}{4 - x^2} \).
C. The constant should be \( \frac{1}{4} \) instead of \( \frac{1}{2} \).
D. The integral is always \( \frac{1}{2} \coth^{-1}(x/2) + C \).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The formula \( \int \frac{du}{a^2 - u^2} = \frac{1}{a} \tanh^{-1}(u/a) \) is only valid when \( |u| < a \). Since the student did not specify the domain of \( x \), the answer is incomplete. For \( |x| > 2 \), the correct antiderivative is \( \frac{1}{2} \coth^{-1}(x/2) + C \). Option B has the derivative wrong. Option C has the incorrect constant. Option D is only true for \( |x| > 2 \), so it is not universally correct.

Q8. If \( y = \operatorname{sech}^{-1}(e^x) \), what is \( \frac{dy}{dx} \) for \( x < 0 \)?

A. \( -\frac{e^x}{e^x \sqrt{1 - e^{2x}}} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - e^{2x}}} \)
B. \( -\frac{e^x}{\sqrt{1 - e^{2x}}} \)
C. \( -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - e^{2x}}} \) ✅
D. \( -\frac{e^x}{\sqrt{1 - e^{2x}}} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of \( \operatorname{sech}^{-1}(u) \) is \( -\frac{1}{u \sqrt{1 - u^2}} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \). Here, \( u = e^x \) and \( \frac{du}{dx} = e^x \). This gives \( -\frac{1}{e^x \sqrt{1 - e^{2x}}} \cdot e^x = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - e^{2x}}} \). Option A is the final simplification. Option B misses the \( 1/u \) term. Option D has the correct format but is missing the negative sign.

Q9. Find \( \frac{d}{dx} [\sinh^3(2x)] \).

A. \( 6 \sinh^2(2x) \cosh(2x) \) ✅
B. \( 3 \sinh^2(2x) \cosh(2x) \)
C. \( 6 \sinh^3(2x) \)
D. \( \sinh^2(2x) \cosh(2x) \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This requires the chain rule and the power rule. First, the derivative of \( u^3 \) is \( 3u^2 \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \). Here, \( u = \sinh(2x) \), so \( \frac{du}{dx} = 2 \cosh(2x) \). Multiplying gives \( 3 \sinh^2(2x) \cdot 2 \cosh(2x) = 6 \sinh^2(2x) \cosh(2x) \). Option B is missing the factor of 2 from the derivative of \( 2x \). Option C incorrectly applies the derivative of \( e^x \).

Q10. Which of the following integrals correctly represents the length of the catenary \( y = 3 \cosh(x/3) \) from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = 3 \)?

A. \( \int_0^3 \cosh(x/3) \, dx \) ✅
B. \( \int_0^3 \sqrt{1 + \sinh^2(x/3)} \, dx \)
C. \( \int_0^3 \sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{9} \sinh^2(x/3)} \, dx \)
D. \( \int_0^3 \sqrt{1 + 9 \sinh^2(x/3)} \, dx \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The arc length formula is \( L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + (dy/dx)^2} \, dx \). For \( y = 3 \cosh(x/3) \), \( dy/dx = \sinh(x/3) \). Thus, the integrand is \( \sqrt{1 + \sinh^2(x/3)} = \cosh(x/3) \). Option A is the simplified correct integral. Option B is the same integrand before simplification. Option C has the wrong derivative (missing the factor from the chain rule). Option D has an extra factor of 3.

Q11. What is the derivative of \( \tanh^{-1}(\sqrt{x}) \)?

A. \( \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}(1 - x)} \) ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}(1 - x)} \)
C. \( \frac{1}{1 - x} \)
D. \( \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}(1 + x)} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of \( \tanh^{-1}(u) \) is \( \frac{1}{1 - u^2} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \). Here, \( u = \sqrt{x} \), so \( \frac{du}{dx} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} \). The final expression is \( \frac{1}{1 - x} \cdot \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}(1 - x)} \). Note that Option A and B are identical (a typo in the options list). Option C misses the chain rule term. Option D has a \( +x \) instead of \( -x \).

Q12. Evaluate \( \int_0^{\ln 2} \tanh x \, dx \).

A. \( \ln(\frac{5}{4}) \) ✅
B. \( \ln(\frac{4}{5}) \)
C. \( \ln 2 \)
D. \( \frac{3}{4} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The integral of \( \tanh x \) is \( \ln(\cosh x) + C \). Evaluating from 0 to \( \ln 2 \): \( \ln(\cosh(\ln 2)) - \ln(\cosh 0) \). \( \cosh(\ln 2) = \frac{e^{\ln 2} + e^{-\ln 2}}{2} = \frac{2 + 1/2}{2} = \frac{5}{4} \). \( \cosh 0 = 1 \). Thus, the result is \( \ln(5/4) \). Option B is the negative of the correct answer. Option C ignores the integral formula. Option D is a numeric approximation that is not exact.

Q13. Find the derivative of \( y = \ln(\cosh x) \).

A. \( \tanh x \) ✅
B. \( \cosh x \)
C. \( \operatorname{sech} x \)
D. \( \sinh x \cosh x \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is a classic Medium of the chain rule. \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\cosh x} \cdot \sinh x = \tanh x \). Option B is the inner function. Option C is not a derivative of \( \ln(\cosh x) \). Option D would be the derivative of \( \sinh^2 x \) or \( \cosh^2 x \).

Q14. If \( y = x \sinh x \), what is \( \frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} \)?

A. \( 2 \cosh x + x \sinh x \) ✅
B. \( 2 \sinh x + x \cosh x \)
C. \( x \sinh x + \cosh x \)
D. \( 2 \cosh x + x \sinh x \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

First derivative: \( y' = \sinh x + x \cosh x \). Second derivative: \( y'' = \cosh x + (\cosh x + x \sinh x) = 2 \cosh x + x \sinh x \). Option B is the first derivative. Option C is a misMedium of the product rule. Option D is an incorrect coefficient for the hyperbolic term.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of \( \operatorname{csch}^{-1} x \) is a standard formula: \( -\frac{1}{|x| \sqrt{1 + x^2}} \). The absolute value is crucial because the domain of \( \operatorname{csch}^{-1} x \) includes negative values, and the derivative must be negative for all \( x \neq 0 \). Option A is missing the absolute value. Option C is the derivative of \( \sec^{-1} x \). Option D is the correct formula. Option B is the derivative of \( \operatorname{sech}^{-1} x \).

Q17. The area enclosed by \( y = \sinh x \), \( y = 0 \), and \( x = \ln 2 \) is given by what expression?

A. \( \cosh(\ln 2) - 1 \) ✅
B. \( \sinh(\ln 2) \)
C. \( \int_0^{\ln 2} \cosh x \, dx \)
D. \( \ln 2 \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The area under the curve is \( \int_0^{\ln 2} \sinh x \, dx = [\cosh x]_0^{\ln 2} = \cosh(\ln 2) - \cosh 0 = \cosh(\ln 2) - 1 \). Option B is the integral of \( \cosh x \). Option C is the integrand for a different area. Option D is irrelevant. Calculating \( \cosh(\ln 2) = (2 + 0.5)/2 = 1.25 \), so the area is 0.25.

Q19. A student attempted to find \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 - 4}} \) and got \( \operatorname{sech}^{-1}(x/2) \). What was their mistake?

A. They used the formula for \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{a^2 - u^2}} \) instead of \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{u^2 - a^2}} \). ✅
B. They used the formula for \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} \).
C. They forgot the chain rule.
D. They used the formula for \( \int \frac{dx}{x \sqrt{x^2 - a^2}} \).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The integral \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}} \) is a standard form that results in \( \cosh^{-1}(x/a) \). The derivative of \( \operatorname{sech}^{-1}(x/2) \) is \( -\frac{1}{x \sqrt{1 - x^2/4}} \), which is not the integrand. The student confused this with the integral \( \int \frac{dx}{x \sqrt{a^2 - x^2}} \), which yields \( -\frac{1}{a} \operatorname{sech}^{-1}(x/a) \). This is a common misconception. The correct answer for \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 - 4}} \) is \( \cosh^{-1}(x/2) \).

Q20. What is the derivative of \( \operatorname{sech}^2 x \) with respect to \( x \)?

A. \( -2 \operatorname{sech}^2 x \tanh x \) ✅
B. \( -2 \operatorname{sech} x \tanh x \)
C. \( 2 \operatorname{sech}^2 x \tanh x \)
D. \( -2 \operatorname{sech} x \tanh^2 x \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Let \( u = \operatorname{sech} x \). Then \( y = u^2 \), so \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 2u \cdot \frac{du}{dx} = 2 \operatorname{sech} x \cdot (-\operatorname{sech} x \tanh x) = -2 \operatorname{sech}^2 x \tanh x \). Option B is missing one \( \operatorname{sech} x \). Option C has the wrong sign. Option D has the wrong power on \( \tanh x \).

B. \( \sinh(e^x) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

Let \( u = e^x \), so \( du = e^x dx \). The integral becomes \( \int \sinh(u) \, du = \cosh(u) + C = \cosh(e^x) + C \). Option B is the integral of \( \cosh(e^x) \). Option C is the same as B. Option D has the wrong sign for the integral of \( \sinh(u) \).

Q22. The graph of \( y = \sinh x \) has a derivative that is always positive. Which of the following statements about the inverse function \( y = \sinh^{-1} x \) is true?

A. The inverse function is always increasing and has a vertical asymptote.
B. The inverse function is always increasing and has a horizontal asymptote.
C. The inverse function is always increasing and has no asymptotes. ✅
D. The inverse function is always decreasing and has no asymptotes.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Since \( \sinh x \) is strictly increasing, its inverse \( \sinh^{-1} x \) is also strictly increasing. The range of \( \sinh x \) is \( (-\infty, \infty) \), so the domain of its inverse is all real numbers. It does not have vertical or horizontal asymptotes; it continues to increase linearly as \( x \to \pm\infty \). Option A is incorrect because there are no vertical asymptotes. Option B is incorrect because there are no horizontal asymptotes. Option D is wrong because the function is increasing, not decreasing.

Q24. What is the result of \( \int \operatorname{csch}^2 x \coth x \, dx \)?

A. \( -\frac{1}{2} \coth^2 x + C \) ✅
B. \( -\frac{1}{2} \operatorname{csch}^2 x + C \)
C. \( \frac{1}{2} \operatorname{csch}^2 x + C \)
D. \( -\operatorname{csch}^2 x + C \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Let \( u = \coth x \). Then \( du = -\operatorname{csch}^2 x \, dx \). The integral becomes \( \int u \cdot (-\operatorname{csch}^2 x) \, dx = -\int u \, du = -\frac{u^2}{2} + C = -\frac{1}{2} \coth^2 x + C \). Option B is the integral of \( \operatorname{csch}^2 x \coth x \) if one mistakenly uses \( u = \operatorname{csch} x \). Option C has the wrong sign. Option D is missing the constant of integration.
