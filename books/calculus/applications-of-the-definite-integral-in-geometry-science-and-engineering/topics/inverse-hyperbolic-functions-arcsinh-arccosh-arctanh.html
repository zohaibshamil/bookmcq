What is Inverse hyperbolic functions arcsinh arccosh arctanh?

Definition:
Inverse hyperbolic functions are defined via logarithms. \( \sinh^{-1} x = \ln(x + \sqrt{x^2+1}) \), \( \cosh^{-1} x = \ln(x + \sqrt{x^2-1}) \) for \( x \geq 1 \), and \( \tanh^{-1} x = \frac{1}{2} \ln(\frac{1+x}{1-x}) \) for \( |x| < 1 \).

Example:
Calculate \( \sinh^{-1}(0) \). Solution: \( \ln(0 + \sqrt{0+1}) = \ln(1) = 0 \).

Reason:
These functions appear in solutions to integrals involving square roots of quadratic expressions, providing closed-form solutions for problems in geometry and physics that would otherwise require numerical approximation.

Q2. Which of the following is the correct derivative of \( \sinh^{-1}(3x) \)?

D. \( \frac{3x}{\sqrt{1+9x^2}} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using the chain rule, \( \frac{d}{dx}[\sinh^{-1}(u)] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}\frac{du}{dx} \). Here \( u=3x \), so \( \frac{du}{dx}=3 \), giving \( \frac{3}{\sqrt{1+9x^2}} \). Option B incorrectly uses a minus sign (confusing it with \( \sin^{-1} \)), while C and D miss the chain rule factor.

The inverse \( \cosh^{-1}(x) \) therefore has domain \( [1, \infty) \) and range \( [0, \infty) \).

Q30. A student states that \( \cosh^{-1}(x) \) is always positive. Is this true?

A. Yes, because the range of \( \cosh^{-1}(x) \) is \( [0, \infty) \). ✅
B. Yes, but only for \( x > 1 \).
C. No, because \( \cosh^{-1}(x) \) can be negative for \( x < 1 \).
D. No, because \( \cosh^{-1}(x) \) is undefined for \( x < 1 \).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The domain of \( \cosh^{-1}(x) \) is \( [1, \infty) \), and its range is \( [0, \infty) \). Therefore, \( \cosh^{-1}(x) \) is always non-negative, and for \( x > 1 \), it is strictly positive. At \( x=1 \), it is 0. Option B is partially correct but misses the point that it's also non-negative for all x in the domain. C is incorrect because the range is non-negative, and D is true but doesn't address the student's statement.
