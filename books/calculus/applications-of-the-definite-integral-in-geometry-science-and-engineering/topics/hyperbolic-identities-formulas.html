Q1. Which of the following is the correct identity relating \( \cosh^2 x \) and \( \sinh^2 x \)?

🔹 A. \( \cosh^2 x + \sinh^2 x = 1 \)
🔹 B. \( \cosh^2 x - \sinh^2 x = 1 \)
🔹 C. \( \sinh^2 x - \cosh^2 x = 1 \)
🔹 D. \( \cosh^2 x + \sinh^2 x = 0 \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The fundamental hyperbolic identity is \( \cosh^2 x - \sinh^2 x = 1 \). This is analogous to the trigonometric identity \( \cos^2 x + \sin^2 x = 1 \), but with a minus sign. This identity can be derived directly from the definitions of \( \cosh x = \frac{e^x + e^{-x}}{2} \) and \( \sinh x = \frac{e^x - e^{-x}}{2} \). It is a cornerstone for deriving other hyperbolic identities and transformations.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

\( \cosh x = \sqrt{1 + \sinh^2 x} = \sqrt{1 + 9} = \sqrt{10} \). Then \( \tanh x = \frac{\sinh x}{\cosh x} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{10}} \). This combines the fundamental identity with the definition of \( \tanh \).
