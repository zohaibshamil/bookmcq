What is Moments and centers of gravity calculus?

Moments measure the tendency of a distributed mass to rotate about an axis, with the moment about the y-axis given by \( M_y = \int x \, dm \) and center of gravity coordinates found by dividing these moments by the total mass \( m = \int dm \) to locate the balance point.

C. (-10, -25) ✅
D. (8, 20)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The combined center of gravity is the weighted average of the individual centers. For the x-coordinate: 0 = (12*2 + 3*x)/15 -> 0 = 24 + 3x -> x = -8. For the y-coordinate: 0 = (12*5 + 3*y)/15 -> 0 = 60 + 3y -> y = -20. Therefore, the mass must be placed at (-8, -20).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

A lamina with uniform density has its center of gravity at its centroid, and the mass is proportional to area. Q14. The center of gravity of a system of two masses is located closer to the larger mass. This is always true.

The first moment of a lamina about the x-axis is defined as \( M_x = \iint_R y \rho(x,y) \, dA \). Since \( \rho(x,y) = y \), the integral becomes \( \iint_R y^2 \, dA \), which is the second moment about the x-axis. The integral \( \iint_R y \rho(x,y) \, dA \) is the moment about the x-axis. If \( \rho(x,y) = y \), then the integral is \( \iint_R y^2 \, dA \), which is the second moment, not the first moment. The correct interpretation is the second moment.

Q34. A thin wire is bent into the shape of a semicircle of radius R. Where is its center of gravity?

A. At the center of the semicircle
B. At a distance \( 2R/\pi \) from the diameter along the axis of symmetry ✅
C. At a distance \( R/\pi \) from the diameter along the axis of symmetry
D. At the midpoint of the diameter

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

For a uniform semicircular wire of radius R, the center of gravity lies on the axis of symmetry at a distance \( 2R/\pi \) from the straight edge (diameter). This is found by integrating the position of each infinitesimal length element along the wire and dividing by the total length.

Q35. Two identical masses are placed at (0,0) and (2,0). Where is the center of gravity of the system?

A. At (0,0)
B. At (2,0)
C. At (1,0) ✅
D. At (1,1)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

For two identical masses, the center of gravity is the midpoint of the line segment joining them. The midpoint of (0,0) and (2,0) is (1,0).

Q36. If the origin is chosen at the center of gravity of a rigid body, what is the sum of the moments of all the masses about the origin?

C. Maximum
D. Minimum

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The center of gravity is defined as the point about which the sum of the moments of the weights of all particles is zero. If the origin is chosen at this point, the condition for equilibrium is satisfied, and the sum of the moments about the origin is zero.

Q37. A lamina is described by the region bounded by \( y = e^x \), \( y=0 \), \( x=0 \), and \( x=1 \). What is the x-coordinate of its centroid?

A. \( (e-1)/(e+1) \) ✅
B. \( (e+1)/(e-1) \)
C. \( (e-1)^2/(e^2-1) \)
D. \( 1/(e-1) \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The area A is \( \int_0^1 e^x dx = e - 1 \). The first moment about the y-axis is \( M_y = \int_0^1 x e^x dx \). Using integration by parts, \( M_y = [x e^x]_0^1 - \int_0^1 e^x dx = e - (e-1) = 1 \). The x-coordinate of the centroid is \( \bar{x} = M_y / A = 1 / (e-1) \).
