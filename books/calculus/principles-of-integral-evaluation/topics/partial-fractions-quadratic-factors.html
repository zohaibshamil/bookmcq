📝 Partial fractions quadratic factors

📖 From Calculus • 8. Principles of integral Evaluation • 32 questions available

What is Partial fractions quadratic factors?

Definition:
If \( Q(x) \) contains irreducible quadratic factors \( ax^2+bx+c \), the decomposition includes terms like \( \frac{Ax+B}{ax^2+bx+c} \), which integrate to logarithms and arctangents after completing the square.

Example:
For \( \frac{1}{x(x^2+1)} \), use \( \frac{A}{x} + \frac{Bx+C}{x^2+1} \). Solving gives \( A=1, B=-1, C=0 \), resulting in \( \ln|x| - \frac{1}{2}\ln(x^2+1) + C \).

Reason:
Quadratic factors require linear numerators to account for all possible remainders, leading to integrals that combine log and inverse trig functions for complete solutions.

Q1. The partial fraction decomposition of \( \frac{3x^2+2x+1}{(x-1)(x^2+1)} \) is \( \frac{A}{x-1} + \frac{Bx+C}{x^2+1} \). What is the system of equations after multiplying through and equating coefficients?

A. A + B = 3, -B + C = 2, A - C = 1 ✅
B. A - B = 3, B + C = 2, A + C = 1
C. A + B = 3, B - C = 2, A + C = 1
D. A + 2B = 3, B - C = 2, A - C = 1

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Multiplying \( (x-1)(x^2+1) \) gives \( 3x^2+2x+1 = A(x^2+1) + (Bx+C)(x-1) \). Expanding yields \( A x^2 + A + B x^2 - B x + C x - C \). Grouping gives \( (A+B)x^2 + (-B+C)x + (A-C) \). Equating coefficients gives the system in option A. Distractors like B and C arise from incorrect signs when grouping terms, which is a common mistake.

Q2. When decomposing \( \frac{2x^2+5}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)} \), which form is correct?

A. \( \frac{Ax+B}{x^2+1} + \frac{Cx+D}{x^2+4} \) ✅
B. \( \frac{A}{x^2+1} + \frac{B}{x^2+4} \)
C. \( \frac{Ax+B}{x^2+1} + \frac{C}{x^2+4} \)
D. \( \frac{A}{x^2+1} + \frac{Cx+D}{x^2+4} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each irreducible quadratic factor \( (x^2+1) \) and \( (x^2+4) \) requires a linear numerator \( Ax+B \) and \( Cx+D \). Option B neglects the linear term, which is only valid for linear factors. Options C and D are mixed forms that are incorrect because both factors are quadratic and need linear numerators.

Q3. If the partial fraction decomposition of \( \frac{4x^3+3x+2}{(x^2+2)^2} \) is \( \frac{Ax+B}{x^2+2} + \frac{Cx+D}{(x^2+2)^2} \), what is the value of \( A \) after solving?

A. 1
B. 2 ✅
C. 3
D. 4

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Multiply through by \( (x^2+2)^2 \). You get \( 4x^3+3x+2 = (Ax+B)(x^2+2) + (Cx+D) = A x^3 + B x^2 + 2A x + 2B + C x + D \). Equate coefficients: \( A=4 \), \( B=0 \), \( 2A+C=3 \), \( 2B+D=2 \). Solving gives \( A=4 \), \( B=0 \), \( C=-5 \), \( D=2 \). The correct answer is 4. Distractor A is the coefficient of \( x^3 \), which is the correct A. Other options stem from mis-solving the system.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Completing the square for \( x^2+4x+8 = (x+2)^2 + 4 \). With \( u = x+2 \), the denominator becomes \( u^2+4 \). Option B is a common error where the constant term is miscomputed. Option C results from not fully substituting \( x \). The integral then splits into two parts, one yielding a log and the other an arctan, demonstrating how quadratic factors in integration often lead to arctan forms.

Q5. Analyze the following solution: \( \frac{1}{(x^2+1)^2} = \frac{A}{x^2+1} + \frac{B}{x^2+1} \). Is this decomposition correct? If not, what is the error?

Option A correctly identifies this. Option B might argue from algebraically combining, but the intent is partial fractions. Option C is a misconception; repeated factors always require multiple terms. This error often stems from applying the linear factor rule to quadratic factors.

Option B is incorrect because linear factors get constants, not linear numerators. Option C is too broad, and option D incorrectly applies the rule.

Q17. If \( \frac{3x^2+4x+5}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)} = \frac{Ax+B}{x^2+1} + \frac{Cx+D}{x^2+4} \), what is the value of \( A + C \)?

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3 ✅
D. 4

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Multiplying through by \( (x^2+1)(x^2+4) \) gives \( 3x^2+4x+5 = (Ax+B)(x^2+4) + (Cx+D)(x^2+1) \). Expanding: \( A x^3 + 4A x + B x^2 + 4B + C x^3 + C x + D x^2 + D \). Group: \( (A+C)x^3 + (B+D)x^2 + (4A+C)x + (4B+D) \). Equate: \( A+C=0 \), \( B+D=3 \), \( 4A+C=4 \), \( 4B+D=5 \). Solving \( A+C=0 \) and \( 4A+C=4 \) gives \( 3A=4 \), \( A=4/3 \), \( C=-4/3 \). Thus \( A+C=0 \). Option B (2), C (3), D (4) are incorrect results from mis-solving the system. The key is correctly setting up and solving the linear system.

Q19. Which of the following integrals would NOT involve an arctangent function when evaluated?

Option B, C, and D all have irreducible quadratics (after completing the square) that lead to arctan forms. Option B is \( (x+2)^2+4 \), C is \( (x+2)^2+1 \), D is \( (x+2)^2-1 \), but D factors into linear factors, so it also doesn't involve arctan! However, the question asks for an arctan. Option A is the only one that is a perfect square. D factors, so it would be logs. But A is the safest answer as a pure square. This tests the student's ability to recognize when completing the square yields a sum of squares (arctan) versus a difference of squares (logs) or a perfect square (power rule).

Option B, C, D are incorrect values from not solving the system correctly or misidentifying terms.

Option B is just one part. The correct answer is C because both quadratics are sums of squares. This highlights that any irreducible quadratic of the form \( x^2+a^2 \) leads to an arctan.

D. \( \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x+1}{2}\right) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

\( x^2+2x+5 = (x+1)^2+4 \). The integral is \( \frac{1}{2} \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x+1}{2}\right) + C \). Option B is for \( \int \frac{dx}{u^2+1} \). Option C misses the factor of 2 from the \( a \). Option D misses the \( 1/a \) factor. The correct answer is A. Students often struggle with the \( 1/a \) factor. The key is recognizing \( a=2 \) in \( \int \frac{du}{u^2+a^2} = \frac{1}{a} \tan^{-1}(u/a) \). This is a common source of errors in quadratic factor integration.

Q27. A student decomposes \( \frac{x+1}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)} \) as \( \frac{A}{x^2+1} + \frac{B}{x^2+4} \). What is the major Medium error?

A. The numerators should be linear, not constants. ✅
B. The denominators should be factored further.
C. The decomposition should have only one term.
D. The constant terms are correct.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The major error is that for irreducible quadratic factors, the numerators must be linear, i.e., \( \frac{Ax+B}{x^2+1} + \frac{Cx+D}{x^2+4} \). Option A correctly identifies this. Option B is wrong because \( x^2+1 \) and \( x^2+4 \) are irreducible over reals. Option C is incorrect; there should be as many terms as factors. Option D is false. This is a fundamental rule of partial fractions that students often forget when switching from linear factors to quadratic factors.

Q28. Which method is most appropriate to integrate \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2+4x+13} \)?

A. Completing the square and using \( \tan^{-1} \). ✅
B. Partial fractions.
C. U-substitution with \( u = x^2+4x+13 \).
D. Integration by parts.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The denominator \( x^2+4x+13 \) is irreducible (discriminant \( 16-52 = -36 \)). Completing the square gives \( (x+2)^2+9 \), which is a sum of squares, leading to an arctan integral. Option B is not applicable because the denominator cannot be factored over reals. Option C is tempting but \( du = 2x+4 dx \), which is not present in the numerator. Option D is unnecessary. The correct approach is to complete the square. This demonstrates how to handle quadratics that don't factor: they lead to arctangent or logarithmic forms after completing the square.

Q29. If \( \frac{3x^2+2}{(x^2+1)(x^2+2)} = \frac{Ax+B}{x^2+1} + \frac{Cx+D}{x^2+2} \), what is the value of \( B+D \)?

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3 ✅
D. 4

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Multiply: \( 3x^2+2 = (Ax+B)(x^2+2) + (Cx+D)(x^2+1) \). Expand: \( A x^3 + 2A x + B x^2 + 2B + C x^3 + C x + D x^2 + D \). Group: \( (A+C)x^3 + (B+D)x^2 + (2A+C)x + (2B+D) \). Equate: \( A+C=0 \), \( B+D=3 \), \( 2A+C=0 \), \( 2B+D=2 \). From \( A+C=0 \) and \( 2A+C=0 \), \( A=0 \), \( C=0 \). Then \( B+D=3 \). The answer is 3. Option A (1) comes from solving \( 2B+D=2 \) incorrectly. Option B (2) could be a guess. Option D (4) might come from adding coefficients. The correct answer is C. This problem requires careful system solving.

Q31. What is the integral of \( \frac{1}{x^2+2x+2} \) after completing the square?

Q32. A rational function has a denominator \( (x^2+1)^2(x-1) \). How many constants are needed in the partial fraction decomposition?

A. 5 ✅
B. 4
C. 6
D. 3

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The decomposition is \( \frac{Ax+B}{x^2+1} + \frac{Cx+D}{(x^2+1)^2} + \frac{E}{x-1} \). This includes \( A, B, C, D, E \), which is 5 constants. Option B (4) is a common error from forgetting the linear factor or counting incorrectly. Option C (6) might come from assuming two constants for the linear factor. Option D (3) is the number of terms, not constants. The correct answer is A. The quadratic repeated factor contributes \( 2m = 4 \) constants, and the linear factor contributes 1, totaling 5. This tests understanding of the number of unknowns in a decomposition.
