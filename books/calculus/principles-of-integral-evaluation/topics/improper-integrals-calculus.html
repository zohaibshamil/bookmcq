What is Improper integrals calculus?

Definition:
Improper integrals involve infinite intervals or discontinuous integrands, defined as limits of definite integrals. They converge if the limit exists and diverge otherwise. Form: \( \int_a^\infty f(x) dx = \lim_{t \to \infty} \int_a^t f(x) dx \).

Example:
\( \int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^2} dx = \lim_{t \to \infty} [-\frac{1}{x}]_1^t = \lim_{t \to \infty} (1 - \frac{1}{t}) = 1 \), so it converges.

Reason:
They extend integration to unbounded domains or functions with vertical asymptotes, crucial for probability distributions and physics applications involving infinite ranges.

Q1. Which of the following integrals is classified as improper due to an infinite interval of integration?

A.\( \int_{0}^{2} \frac{1}{x-1} \, dx \)
B.\( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^2} \, dx \) ✅
C.\( \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} \, dx \)
D.\( \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{1}{x} \, dx \)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An improper integral can have an infinite limit of integration or an infinite discontinuity within the interval. Options A, C, and D have finite intervals but contain discontinuities. Option B has an infinite upper limit, making it improper due to the interval's infinite extent.

Q2. What is the value of \( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^2} \, dx \)?

A.0
B.1 ✅
C.2
D.Diverges
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: This is a standard \( p \)-integral with \( p=2 > 1 \). The antiderivative is \( -1/x \), and evaluating from 1 to \( b \) and taking the limit as \( b \to \infty \) gives 1. This is a foundational result for understanding convergence.

Q3. Explain why \( \int_{0}^{2} \frac{1}{(x-1)^2} \, dx \) is divergent.

A.The integrand is undefined at \( x=1 \), but the improper integral can still converge.
B.The function has an infinite discontinuity at \( x=1 \), and both resulting improper integrals diverge. ✅
C.The function is not continuous on the interval, but the fundamental theorem still applies.
D.The limit of the antiderivative at \( x=1 \) exists.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The integrand has a vertical asymptote at \( x=1 \). To evaluate, we split the integral at 1. Evaluating each improper integral, both tend to infinity. A common misconception is that symmetry around the asymptote implies convergence, which is not true for this integrand.

Q4. Analyze the convergence of \( \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} \, dx \).

A.Converges to 1
B.Converges to 2 ✅
C.Diverges to infinity
D.Converges to 0
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The integrand has an infinite discontinuity at \( x=0 \). We evaluate the limit as \( t \to 0^+ \) of \( \int_{t}^{1} x^{-1/2} \, dx = 2(1 - \sqrt{t}) \). The limit is 2, so the improper integral converges.

Q5. A student evaluates \( \int_{-1}^{1} \frac{1}{x} \, dx \) as \( \ln|1| - \ln|-1| = 0 \). What is the error?

A.The antiderivative is incorrect.
B.The integral is improper at \( x=0 \), and it diverges. ✅
C.The limits of integration are incorrect.
D.The fundamental theorem applies directly.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The function \( 1/x \) has an infinite discontinuity at \( x=0 \), which lies within the interval of integration. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus cannot be applied directly. Splitting the integral shows that both parts diverge, so the integral does not converge to 0.

Q6. Which of the following integrals converges?

A.\( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x} \, dx \)
B.\( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} \, dx \)
C.\( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^{1.1}} \, dx \) ✅
D.\( \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{x} \, dx \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A \( p \)-integral of the form \( \int_{1}^{\infty} x^{-p} \, dx \) converges for \( p>1 \). Options A and B have \( p=1 \) and \( p=1/2 \), so they diverge. Option D diverges as \( p=1 \) on a finite interval with a discontinuity.

Q7. For what value of \( p \) does \( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^p} \, dx \) converge to 2?

A.\( p=1 \)
B.\( p=2 \)
C.\( p=1.5 \) ✅
D.No such \( p \) exists
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The integral converges to \( 1/(p-1) \) for \( p>1 \). Setting \( 1/(p-1) = 2 \) gives \( p-1 = 0.5 \), so \( p=1.5 \). This requires solving a simple equation after applying the convergence formula.

Q8. A region is bounded by \( y = 1/x^2 \), the x-axis, and \( x=1 \). The area is \( A \). If the same region is revolved around the x-axis, the volume is \( V \). Which statement is true?

A.Both \( A \) and \( V \) are finite. ✅
B.\( A \) is finite, but \( V \) is infinite.
C.\( A \) is infinite, but \( V \) is finite.
D.Both \( A \) and \( V \) are infinite.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The area is \( \int_{1}^{\infty} x^{-2} \, dx = 1 \), which is finite. The volume of revolution is \( \pi \int_{1}^{\infty} x^{-4} \, dx = \pi/3 \), which is also finite. This connects two different Easys of improper integrals.

Q9. Given the graph of \( y = 1/\sqrt{x} \) on \( (0,1] \), what does the area under the curve represent?

A.A finite area of 2 ✅
B.An infinite area
C.A finite area of 1
D.A finite area of 1/2
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The area is \( \int_{0}^{1} x^{-1/2} \, dx \), which converges to 2. The graph has a vertical asymptote at \( x=0 \), but the area under the curve is finite. This demonstrates that a function can be unbounded but still have a finite integral.

Q10. Analyze the convergence of \( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{\ln x}{x^2} \, dx \).

A.Converges to 1 ✅
B.Converges to 0
C.Diverges
D.Converges to 2
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Using integration by parts with \( u = \ln x \) and \( dv = x^{-2} dx \), the integral becomes \( \left[ -\frac{\ln x}{x} \right]_{1}^{b} + \int_{1}^{b} x^{-2} dx \). The limit as \( b \to \infty \) is \( 0 + 1 = 1 \). This combines integration techniques with improper limits.

Q11. A common misconception is that \( \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x} \, dx = 0 \) because \( e^{-x} \) approaches 0. Is this true?

A.True, the integral is 0.
B.False, the integral is 1. ✅
C.False, the integral diverges.
D.True, because the limit is 0.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The integral evaluates to \( \lim_{b \to \infty} (1 - e^{-b}) = 1 \). The misconception is confusing the limit of the integrand with the value of the integral. Even though the function tends to 0, the area under the curve is finite and non-zero.

Q12. Which Hard would correctly determine the convergence of \( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^3 + x}} \, dx \)?

A.Compare with \( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^{3/2}} \, dx \) ✅
B.Compare with \( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} \, dx \)
C.Compare with \( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x} \, dx \)
D.Compare with \( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^{1/3}} \, dx \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For large \( x \), \( \sqrt{x^3 + x} \approx x^{3/2} \). Thus, the integrand behaves like \( x^{-3/2} \), which is a convergent \( p \)-integral since \( p=1.5 > 1 \). This is a direct Easy of the Limit Hard Test.

Q13. Find the volume of the solid formed by rotating the region under \( y = e^{-x} \) from \( x=0 \) to \( \infty \) about the x-axis.

A.\( \pi/2 \) ✅
B.\( \pi \)
C.\( 2\pi \)
D.Diverges
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The volume is \( \pi \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-2x} \, dx = \pi \cdot (1/2) = \pi/2 \). This tests the ability to set up and evaluate a volume integral with an infinite limit.

Q14. A student claims that \( \int_{0}^{\infty} \sin x \, dx = 0 \) because the areas above and below the axis cancel. What is the correct assessment?

A.The student is correct.
B.The integral converges to 1.
C.The integral diverges because the limit does not exist. ✅
D.The integral converges to 0.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The limit \( \lim_{b \to \infty} \int_{0}^{b} \sin x \, dx = \lim (1 - \cos b) \) does not exist. The function oscillates, and the area does not settle to a finite value. Improper integrals of oscillating functions require careful analysis.

Q15. Given the graph showing the curve \( y = 1/x \) and the region from \( x=1 \) to \( \infty \), which statement is true?

A.The area is infinite. ✅
B.The area is 1.
C.The area is finite but less than 1.
D.The area is finite but greater than 1.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The area is \( \int_{1}^{\infty} x^{-1} \, dx \), which diverges to infinity. The curve \( y = 1/x \) approaches the x-axis slowly, accumulating infinite area. This is a classic example of a function that tends to zero but whose integral diverges.

Q16. Which of the following is an improper integral with an infinite discontinuity?

A.\( \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-x} \, dx \)

D.\( \int_{1}^{2} \ln x \, dx \)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An infinite discontinuity occurs when the function becomes unbounded at a point within the interval. Option B has a vertical asymptote at \( x=1 \). Option A and C have infinite intervals, while D is continuous on [1,2].

Q17. What is the value of \( \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-2x} \, dx \)?

A.0
B.01-Feb ✅
C.1
D.Diverges
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The integral evaluates to \( \lim_{b \to \infty} \left[ -\frac{1}{2} e^{-2x} \right]_{0}^{b} = \lim_{b \to \infty} \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2} e^{-2b} \right) = \frac{1}{2} \).

Q18. Analyze the convergence of \( \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{x}{x^2+1} \, dx \).

A.Converges to 1
B.Converges to 0
C.Diverges ✅
D.Converges to 1/2
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The antiderivative is \( \frac{1}{2} \ln(x^2+1) \). Evaluating from 0 to \( b \) and taking the limit gives \( \frac{1}{2} \ln(b^2+1) \), which tends to infinity. Thus, the integral diverges.

Q19. Solve for \( k \) if \( \int_{0}^{\infty} k e^{-x} \, dx = 3 \).

Q20. A student evaluates \( \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} \, dx = 2 \) but forgets to take a limit. What is the implication of this omission?

A.The answer is still correct, so no issue.

D.The integral converges to a different value.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The integral is improper at \( x=0 \). The correct evaluation requires the limit \( \lim_{t \to 0^+} \int_{t}^{1} x^{-1/2} dx \). Without this step, the process is not rigorous, though the final answer happens to be 2 in this case.

Q21. Which Hard would correctly determine the convergence of \( \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} \, dx \)?

A.Compare with \( \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{x} \, dx \)

D.Compare with \( \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{x^2} \, dx \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Near \( x=0 \), \( 1/\sqrt{x} \) is less than \( 1/x^{0.9} \) for any exponent less than 1. A direct Hard with the convergent \( p \)-integral \( \int_{0}^{1} x^{-1/2} dx \) is not helpful. Comparing to a constant is not standard; the Hard test usually uses a known convergent/divergent function with similar behavior near the singularity.

Q22. What is the arc length of \( y = x^{3/2} \) from \( x=0 \) to \( x=1 \)? (This involves an improper integral)

A.\( \frac{2}{27}(10^{3/2} - 1) \) ✅
B.\( \frac{2}{27}(10^{3/2} + 1) \)
C.\( \frac{1}{27}(10^{3/2} - 1) \)
D.\( \frac{2}{9}(10^{3/2} - 1) \)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The arc length formula gives \( \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{3}{2}x^{1/2}\right)^2} dx = \int_{0}^{1} \sqrt{1 + \frac{9}{4}x} dx \). This is a proper integral. A similar problem can be constructed where the derivative is unbounded, requiring improper integral techniques.

Q23. Compare the convergence of \( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^2} \, dx \) and \( \int_{1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x} \, dx \).

A.Both converge.
B.Both diverge.
C.The first converges, the second diverges. ✅
D.The first diverges, the second converges.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: \( \int_{1}^{\infty} x^{-2} dx \) converges to 1. \( \int_{1}^{\infty} x^{-1} dx \) diverges. The boundary between convergence and divergence for \( p \)-integrals is at \( p=1 \).

Q24. If \( \int_{1}^{\infty} f(x) \, dx \) converges and \( 0 \le f(x) \le g(x) \), what can be said about \( \int_{1}^{\infty} g(x) \, dx \)?

A.It also converges.
B.It diverges.
C.It may converge or diverge. ✅
D.It converges to the same value.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The Hard test states that if \( f(x) \le g(x) \) and \( \int f \) diverges, then \( \int g \) diverges. If \( \int f \) converges, \( \int g \) is not guaranteed to converge, as \( g \) could be larger. This is a common point of confusion.

Q25. Given the graph of \( y = 1/x^2 \) on \( [1, \infty) \), the area is finite. What does the region look like when revolved around the x-axis?

Q29. Analyze the error in the following evaluation: \( \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x} dx = \left[ \ln x \right]_{0}^{\infty} = \infty - (-\infty) \), which is indeterminate.

A.The evaluation is correct.
B.The antiderivative is wrong.
C.The limits should be considered separately, and the integral diverges. ✅
D.The integral converges to 0.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The expression \( \infty - (-\infty) \) is indeterminate. The correct approach is to evaluate \( \lim_{b \to \infty} \ln b - \lim_{a \to 0^+} \ln a \), both of which are infinite, so the integral diverges.

Q30. Which of the following integrals represents a finite area?

📖 Explanation: \( \int_{0}^{1} x^{-1/2} dx \) converges to 2. Options A and D diverge, and B diverges because \( p=1/2 < 1 \) on an infinite interval.

Q31. Suppose \( \int_{0}^{\infty} f(x) dx \) converges and \( \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = L \). What must be true about \( L \)?

Q34. Explain why \( \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{x} dx \) diverges, but \( \int_{0}^{1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}} dx \) converges.

Q39. A region is bounded by \( y = \frac{1}{x} \), the x-axis, and \( x=1 \). The area is infinite. If this region is rotated around the x-axis, the volume is finite. Is this possible?

📖 Explanation: The area \( \int_{1}^{\infty} x^{-1} dx \) diverges. The volume \( \pi \int_{1}^{\infty} x^{-2} dx \) converges to \( \pi \). This is a famous paradox (Gabriel's Horn) that demonstrates the difference between area and volume convergence.
