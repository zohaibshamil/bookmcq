What is Trigonometric substitution integration?

Definition:
Trigonometric substitution uses trigonometric identities to simplify integrals containing square roots of quadratic expressions, substituting \( x \) with a trig function to eliminate the radical via Pythagorean identities.

Example:
For \( \int \sqrt{9-x^2} \, dx \), let \( x = 3\sin \theta \). Then \( \sqrt{9-9\sin^2 \theta} = 3\cos \theta \), transforming the integral into \( \int 9\cos^2 \theta \, d\theta \).

Reason:
It converts algebraic radicals into trigonometric functions that are easier to integrate, leveraging identities like \( 1-\sin^2 \theta = \cos^2 \theta \) to remove square roots.

Q1. Which substitution is most appropriate for evaluating the integral \( \int \sqrt{9-25x^2} \, dx \)?

A. \( x = \frac{3}{5} \sin \theta \) ✅
B. \( x = \frac{5}{3} \sin \theta \)
C. \( x = 3 \sin \theta \)
D. \( x = 5 \sin \theta \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The integrand contains \( \sqrt{a^2 - (kx)^2} \) with \( a=3 \) and \( k=5 \). To eliminate the radical, we set \( kx = a \sin \theta \), so \( 5x = 3 \sin \theta \), giving \( x = \frac{3}{5} \sin \theta \). This substitution simplifies the radical to \( 3 \cos \theta \), making the integral tractable. Other choices either fail to match the required form or lead to more complicated expressions.

Q2. For the integral \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+16}} \), which trigonometric substitution correctly transforms the integrand into a form involving \( \sec \theta \) and \( \tan \theta \)?

A. \( x = 4 \sin \theta \)
B. \( x = 4 \tan \theta \) ✅
C. \( x = 4 \sec \theta \)
D. \( x = 16 \tan \theta \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The expression \( \sqrt{x^2+16} \) matches the form \( \sqrt{x^2+a^2} \) with \( a=4 \). The standard substitution for this form is \( x = a \tan \theta \), which gives \( dx = 4 \sec^2 \theta d\theta \) and simplifies the radical to \( 4 \sec \theta \). Substituting \( x = 4 \sin \theta \) is suitable for \( \sqrt{a^2 - x^2} \), while \( x = 4 \sec \theta \) is used for \( \sqrt{x^2 - a^2} \). Thus, \( x=4\tan\theta \) is the correct choice.

Q3. A student incorrectly uses the substitution \( x = 2 \sec \theta \) to evaluate \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{4-x^2}} \). What is the most likely consequence of this error?

A. The integral becomes \( \int \sec \theta d\theta \), which is more difficult.
B. The substitution is invalid because \( \sec \theta \ge 1 \), but \( x \) must be in \( [-2,2] \). ✅
C. The radical simplifies to \( 2 \tan \theta \), which is incorrect.
D. The substitution is valid and produces the correct result.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The expression \( \sqrt{4-x^2} \) requires the substitution \( x = a \sin \theta \) because the domain of \( x \) is limited to \( [-2,2] \). Using \( x = 2 \sec \theta \) restricts \( x \) to \( (-\infty, -2] \cup [2, \infty) \), which is outside the domain of the original integrand. This is a critical error because the substitution must preserve the domain of the original variable. Using \( x=2\sec\theta \) would lead to an incorrect simplification of the radical and an invalid integration.

Q4. Given the integral \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2 \sqrt{9-x^2}} \), after the substitution \( x = 3 \sin \theta \), the integral becomes:

A. \( \frac{1}{9} \int \csc^2 \theta \, d\theta \) ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{9} \int \sec^2 \theta \, d\theta \)
C. \( \frac{1}{9} \int \csc \theta \cot \theta \, d\theta \)
D. \( \frac{1}{3} \int \csc^2 \theta \, d\theta \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With \( x = 3 \sin \theta \), \( dx = 3 \cos \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{9-x^2} = 3 \cos \theta \). Substituting these into the integral gives \( \int \frac{3 \cos \theta d\theta}{(9 \sin^2 \theta)(3 \cos \theta)} = \frac{1}{9} \int \frac{d\theta}{\sin^2 \theta} = \frac{1}{9} \int \csc^2 \theta d\theta \). This is a direct Easy of the substitution and simplification rules, requiring careful handling of the constants and trigonometric simplifications.

Q5. Evaluate \( \int_0^1 x^2 \sqrt{1-x^2} \, dx \) using a trigonometric substitution. What is the value of the integral?

A. \( \frac{\pi}{16} \) ✅
B. \( \frac{\pi}{8} \)
C. \( \frac{\pi}{32} \)
D. \( \frac{\pi}{4} \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using \( x = \sin \theta \), \( dx = \cos \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{1-x^2} = \cos \theta \). The limits change to \( 0 \) and \( \pi/2 \). The integral becomes \( \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^2 \theta \cos^2 \theta d\theta = \frac{1}{4} \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^2 2\theta d\theta \). Using the identity \( \sin^2 2\theta = \frac{1-\cos 4\theta}{2} \), the integral evaluates to \( \frac{1}{4} [\frac{\theta}{2} - \frac{\sin 4\theta}{8}]_0^{\pi/2} = \frac{\pi}{16} \). This problem combines substitution, trigonometric identities, and definite integral evaluation.

Q6. The area of the ellipse \( \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1 \) is \( \pi ab \). Which of the following integrals correctly represents the area of the ellipse using a trigonometric substitution?

A. \( 4ab \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos^2 \theta \, d\theta \) ✅
B. \( 4ab \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^2 \theta \, d\theta \)
C. \( 4ab \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos \theta \, d\theta \)
D. \( 4ab \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin \theta \, d\theta \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The area is \( 4 \int_0^a \frac{b}{a} \sqrt{a^2-x^2} dx \). Substituting \( x = a \sin \theta \), \( dx = a \cos \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{a^2-x^2} = a \cos \theta \), the integral becomes \( 4 \frac{b}{a} \int_0^{\pi/2} a \cos \theta \cdot a \cos \theta d\theta = 4ab \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos^2 \theta d\theta \). The other options either have the wrong power of sine/cosine or are missing the constant factor. This question tests the Easy of trigonometric substitution to a geometric formula.

Q7. A student evaluating \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2 \sqrt{x^2-9}} \) chooses \( x = 3 \sec \theta \). What is the simplified form of the integral after substitution?

A. \( \frac{1}{9} \int \cos \theta \, d\theta \) ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{9} \int \sec \theta \, d\theta \)
C. \( \frac{1}{9} \int \sin \theta \, d\theta \)
D. \( \frac{1}{9} \int \tan \theta \, d\theta \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

For \( x = 3 \sec \theta \), \( dx = 3 \sec \theta \tan \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{x^2-9} = 3 \tan \theta \). Substituting gives \( \int \frac{3 \sec \theta \tan \theta d\theta}{9 \sec^2 \theta \cdot 3 \tan \theta} = \frac{1}{9} \int \frac{\sec \theta \tan \theta}{\sec^2 \theta \tan \theta} d\theta = \frac{1}{9} \int \frac{1}{\sec \theta} d\theta = \frac{1}{9} \int \cos \theta d\theta \). This simplification requires careful cancellation of trigonometric factors and recognizing the relationship between secant and cosine.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The expression \( x^2+4x+5 \) is not in the standard form \( x^2 \pm a^2 \). Completing the square transforms it to \( (x+2)^2+1 \), which matches \( u^2 + a^2 \) with \( u = x+2 \) and \( a=1 \). This allows the use of the substitution \( u = \tan \theta \). The other options are either incorrect algebraically or do not lead to a form suitable for trigonometric substitution.

Q9. Evaluate \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{4x^2+4x+2}} \) using an appropriate substitution. What is the result?

A. \( \frac{1}{2} \sinh^{-1}(2x+1) + C \) ✅
B. \( \sinh^{-1}(2x+1) + C \)
C. \( \frac{1}{2} \tan^{-1}(2x+1) + C \)
D. \( \ln|2x+1 + \sqrt{4x^2+4x+2}| + C \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Completing the square: \( 4x^2+4x+2 = (2x+1)^2+1 \). Let \( u = 2x+1 \), then \( du = 2dx \), so the integral becomes \( \frac{1}{2} \int \frac{du}{\sqrt{u^2+1}} = \frac{1}{2} \sinh^{-1} u + C = \frac{1}{2} \sinh^{-1}(2x+1) + C \). The logarithmic form is also valid but not simplified to match the options. This tests the ability to handle non-standard quadratics and choose the correct integration formula.

Q10. Which of the following integrals requires a trigonometric substitution to evaluate?

A. \( \int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}} dx \)
B. \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2-1}} \) ✅
C. \( \int \frac{x}{x^2+1} dx \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The integral \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2-1}} \) contains \( \sqrt{x^2-a^2} \), which is a classic case for trigonometric substitution (specifically \( x = \sec \theta \)). The other integrals can be evaluated using simple \( u \)-substitution: \( u = x^2+1 \) for (a), \( u = x^2+1 \) for (c), and \( u = x + \sqrt{x^2+1} \) or a direct formula for (d). This question tests the recognition of when trigonometric substitution is the most appropriate method.

Q11. A student evaluates \( \int \sqrt{4-x^2} dx \) using \( x = 2 \sin \theta \) and obtains \( 2 \cos \theta + C \). What is the correct expression in terms of \( x \)?

A. \( \frac{x}{2} \sqrt{4-x^2} + 2 \sin^{-1}(x/2) + C \) ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{2} x \sqrt{4-x^2} + \sin^{-1}(x/2) + C \)
C. \( \frac{x}{2} \sqrt{4-x^2} + \sin^{-1}(x/2) + C \)
D. \( x \sqrt{4-x^2} + 2 \sin^{-1}(x/2) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The integral evaluates to \( 2 \int \cos^2 \theta d\theta = 2(\frac{\theta}{2} + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{4}) = \theta + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{2} = \theta + \sin \theta \cos \theta \). Since \( \sin \theta = x/2 \) and \( \cos \theta = \sqrt{4-x^2}/2 \), the result is \( \sin^{-1}(x/2) + \frac{x}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2} = \sin^{-1}(x/2) + \frac{x\sqrt{4-x^2}}{4} \). Multiplying by 2 gives \( 2 \sin^{-1}(x/2) + \frac{x\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2} \). The student's answer missed the inverse sine term and the correct coefficient.

The area is \( \int_0^2 \sqrt{4-x^2} dx \). With \( x = 2 \sin \theta \), \( dx = 2 \cos \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{4-x^2} = 2 \cos \theta \). The limits become \( 0 \) and \( \pi/2 \). The integral becomes \( \int_0^{\pi/2} (2 \cos \theta)(2 \cos \theta) d\theta = 4 \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos^2 \theta d\theta \). This is a direct Easy of the substitution to a geometric area problem, requiring the student to visualize the graph and set up the integral correctly.

Q14. To evaluate \( \int \sqrt{x^2-4} dx \) for \( x \ge 2 \), a student uses \( x = 2 \sec \theta \). What is the correct antiderivative?

A. \( \frac{x}{2} \sqrt{x^2-4} - 2 \ln|x + \sqrt{x^2-4}| + C \) ✅
B. \( \frac{x}{2} \sqrt{x^2-4} + 2 \ln|x + \sqrt{x^2-4}| + C \)
C. \( x \sqrt{x^2-4} - 2 \ln|x + \sqrt{x^2-4}| + C \)
D. \( x \sqrt{x^2-4} + 2 \ln|x + \sqrt{x^2-4}| + C \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With \( x = 2 \sec \theta \), \( dx = 2 \sec \theta \tan \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{x^2-4} = 2 \tan \theta \). The integral becomes \( \int 4 \sec \theta \tan^2 \theta d\theta = 4 \int \sec \theta (\sec^2 \theta - 1) d\theta = 4(\frac{1}{2} \sec \theta \tan \theta - \frac{1}{2} \ln|\sec \theta + \tan \theta|) = 2 \sec \theta \tan \theta - 2 \ln|\sec \theta + \tan \theta| + C \). Substituting back gives \( \frac{x}{2} \sqrt{x^2-4} - 2 \ln|\frac{x}{2} + \frac{\sqrt{x^2-4}}{2}| + C = \frac{x}{2} \sqrt{x^2-4} - 2 \ln|x + \sqrt{x^2-4}| + C \).

Q16. In evaluating \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2 \sqrt{x^2-25}} \), a student uses \( x = 5 \sec \theta \). After substitution, they incorrectly simplify \( \sqrt{x^2-25} \) to \( 5 \cos \theta \). What is the correct simplification?

A. \( 5 \tan \theta \) ✅
B. \( 5 \sec \theta \)
C. \( 5 \sin \theta \)
D. \( 5 \cos \theta \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

For \( x = 5 \sec \theta \), \( x^2-25 = 25 \sec^2 \theta - 25 = 25(\sec^2 \theta - 1) = 25 \tan^2 \theta \). Therefore, \( \sqrt{x^2-25} = 5 |\tan \theta| \). Since \( x \ge 5 \) (or \( x \le -5 \)), we can take \( \tan \theta \ge 0 \) in the appropriate branch, so \( \sqrt{x^2-25} = 5 \tan \theta \). The student confused the simplification for the \( \sqrt{a^2-x^2} \) case, which gives \( a \cos \theta \). This is a common error that highlights the importance of knowing which substitution corresponds to which radical form.

A. \( \int_0^1 \sqrt{1+x^2} dx \); \( x = \tan \theta \) ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The arc length formula is \( L = \int_0^1 \sqrt{1+(dy/dx)^2} dx \). For \( y = x^2/2 \), \( dy/dx = x \), so the integrand is \( \sqrt{1+x^2} \). This matches the form \( \sqrt{a^2+x^2} \) with \( a=1 \), so the substitution is \( x = \tan \theta \). The other options either use the wrong derivative or the wrong substitution for the radical. This question connects the arc length formula with trigonometric substitution.

Q18. Which of the following is the correct form of the partial fraction decomposition after a trigonometric substitution?

Q19. The integral \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2 \sqrt{x^2-4}} \) can be evaluated using \( x = 2 \sec \theta \). What is the value of the integral?

A. \( \frac{1}{4} \sec^{-1}(x/2) + C \) ✅
B. \( \frac{1}{4} \cos^{-1}(2/x) + C \)
C. \( \frac{1}{4} \sin^{-1}(x/2) + C \)
D. \( \frac{1}{2} \sec^{-1}(x/2) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With \( x = 2 \sec \theta \), \( dx = 2 \sec \theta \tan \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{x^2-4} = 2 \tan \theta \). The integral becomes \( \int \frac{2 \sec \theta \tan \theta}{4 \sec^2 \theta \cdot 2 \tan \theta} d\theta = \frac{1}{4} \int \frac{1}{\sec \theta} d\theta = \frac{1}{4} \int \cos \theta d\theta = \frac{1}{4} \sin \theta + C \). Since \( \sin \theta = \frac{\sqrt{x^2-4}}{x} \) (from the right triangle), the result is \( \frac{\sqrt{x^2-4}}{4x} + C \). The inverse secant form is equivalent, but not directly obtained. The correct choice is the simplified form.

The substitution \( u = a \sin \theta \) is used for \( \sqrt{a^2 - u^2} \). The student's claim is incorrect because it uses the wrong substitution for the given radical form, which would not simplify the expression correctly.

Q21. Evaluate \( \int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\cos \theta}{\sqrt{1+\sin^2 \theta}} d\theta \) using a substitution. What is the value?

A. \( \ln(1+\sqrt{2}) \)
B. \( \sinh^{-1}(1) \)
C. Both A and B ✅
D. \( \tan^{-1}(1) \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Let \( u = \sin \theta \), then \( du = \cos \theta d\theta \). The limits change to \( 0 \) and \( 1 \). The integral becomes \( \int_0^1 \frac{du}{\sqrt{1+u^2}} = \sinh^{-1}(u) \big|_0^1 = \sinh^{-1}(1) \). This is equal to \( \ln(1+\sqrt{2}) \). This problem tests the ability to recognize that a trigonometric substitution can be simplified by an additional substitution, and it verifies the equivalence of inverse hyperbolic and logarithmic forms.

Q22. For the integral \( \int \frac{x^3}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} dx \), which of the following is the correct substitution and resulting integral?

A. \( x = \sin \theta \); \( \int \sin^3 \theta d\theta \) ✅
B. \( x = \tan \theta \); \( \int \tan^3 \theta \sec \theta d\theta \)
C. \( x = \sec \theta \); \( \int \sec^3 \theta \tan \theta d\theta \)
D. \( x = \cos \theta \); \( \int -\cos^3 \theta d\theta \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The radical \( \sqrt{1-x^2} \) suggests the substitution \( x = \sin \theta \). Then \( dx = \cos \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{1-x^2} = \cos \theta \). The integral becomes \( \int \frac{\sin^3 \theta \cos \theta}{\cos \theta} d\theta = \int \sin^3 \theta d\theta \). This is a direct Easy. The other options use substitutions that either do not match the radical (like \( \tan \theta \) for \( \sqrt{1+x^2} \)) or are for a different radical form (like \( \sec \theta \) for \( \sqrt{x^2-1} \)).

Q23. Which of the following integrals would NOT benefit from a trigonometric substitution?

A. \( \int \frac{x}{(x^2+1)^{3/2}} dx \)
B. \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+2x+5}} \)
C. \( \int \frac{x^2}{x^2+1} dx \) ✅
D. \( \int \sqrt{4-x^2} dx \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The integral \( \int \frac{x^2}{x^2+1} dx \) can be solved by algebraic manipulation: \( \frac{x^2}{x^2+1} = 1 - \frac{1}{x^2+1} \), leading to \( x - \tan^{-1} x + C \). This is much simpler than a trigonometric substitution. The other integrals all involve radicals or quadratic forms that are typically handled with trigonometric substitutions. This question tests the ability to identify when a simpler method (like algebraic division) is more appropriate, rather than automatically applying a trigonometric substitution.

Q24. The integral \( \int \sqrt{2x-x^2} dx \) is often solved by completing the square and then using a trigonometric substitution. What is the correct form after completing the square?

A. \( \sqrt{1-(x-1)^2} \) ✅
B. \( \sqrt{1+(x-1)^2} \)
C. \( \sqrt{(x-1)^2-1} \)
D. \( \sqrt{x^2-2x} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Completing the square: \( 2x-x^2 = -(x^2-2x) = -(x^2-2x+1-1) = -((x-1)^2-1) = 1-(x-1)^2 \). This is of the form \( \sqrt{a^2 - u^2} \) with \( a=1 \) and \( u=x-1 \). This shows that the quadratic can be transformed into a standard form suitable for a sine or cosine substitution. The other options are algebraically incorrect.

Q25. Evaluate \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2-6x+13}} \) by completing the square and using a trigonometric substitution. What is the antiderivative?

A. \( \sinh^{-1}(x-3) + C \)
B. \( \ln|x-3 + \sqrt{x^2-6x+13}| + C \)
C. Both A and B ✅
D. \( \tan^{-1}(x-3) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Completing the square: \( x^2-6x+13 = (x-3)^2+4 \). Let \( u = x-3 \), then the integral is \( \int \frac{du}{\sqrt{u^2+4}} = \sinh^{-1}(u/2) + C = \sinh^{-1}(\frac{x-3}{2}) + C \). This is also equal to \( \ln|u+\sqrt{u^2+4}| + C = \ln|x-3 + \sqrt{x^2-6x+13}| + C \). Both forms are equivalent due to the identity \( \sinh^{-1} z = \ln(z + \sqrt{z^2+1}) \). The inverse tangent would be for an integral of the form \( \int \frac{du}{u^2+a^2} \). This problem tests the understanding of the equivalence of inverse hyperbolic and logarithmic forms.

Q26. A student evaluates \( \int \frac{dx}{x\sqrt{x^2-1}} \) using \( x = \sec \theta \) and obtains \( \sec^{-1} x + C \). What is the correct antiderivative?

A. \( \sec^{-1} x + C \) ✅
B. \( \tan^{-1} x + C \)
C. \( \sin^{-1} x + C \)
D. \( \ln|\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2-1}}| + C \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With \( x = \sec \theta \), \( dx = \sec \theta \tan \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{x^2-1} = \tan \theta \). The integral becomes \( \int \frac{\sec \theta \tan \theta}{\sec \theta \tan \theta} d\theta = \int d\theta = \theta + C = \sec^{-1} x + C \). The student's answer is correct. This problem is a straightforward Easy of the substitution and simplification, but it tests the recognition of the derivative of \( \sec^{-1} x \). It can also be solved using a different substitution, but the result is the same.

Q27. Given the integral \( \int \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{4-x^2}} dx \), which of the following is the correct antiderivative?

A. \( 2 \sin^{-1}(x/2) - \frac{x}{2}\sqrt{4-x^2} + C \) ✅
B. \( 2 \sin^{-1}(x/2) + \frac{x}{2}\sqrt{4-x^2} + C \)
C. \( 2 \sin^{-1}(x/2) - x\sqrt{4-x^2} + C \)
D. \( 2 \sin^{-1}(x/2) + x\sqrt{4-x^2} + C \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using \( x = 2 \sin \theta \), \( dx = 2 \cos \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{4-x^2} = 2 \cos \theta \). The integral becomes \( \int 4 \sin^2 \theta d\theta = 4(\frac{\theta}{2} - \frac{\sin 2\theta}{4}) = 2\theta - 2 \sin \theta \cos \theta \). Substituting back: \( 2 \sin^{-1}(x/2) - 2 \cdot \frac{x}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2} = 2 \sin^{-1}(x/2) - \frac{x\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2} \). The correct answer has the term \( -\frac{x}{2}\sqrt{4-x^2} \). This problem requires careful substitution and algebraic back-substitution.

Q28. Consider the integral \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2+4x+8} \). To evaluate it, a student completes the square and gets \( (x+2)^2+4 \). What substitution should they use, and what is the result?

A. \( x+2 = 2 \tan \theta \); \( \frac{1}{2} \tan^{-1}(\frac{x+2}{2}) + C \) ✅
B. \( x+2 = 2 \sin \theta \); \( \frac{1}{2} \sin^{-1}(\frac{x+2}{2}) + C \)
C. \( x+2 = 2 \sec \theta \); \( \frac{1}{2} \sec^{-1}(\frac{x+2}{2}) + C \)
D. \( x = 2 \tan \theta \); \( \frac{1}{2} \tan^{-1}(x/2) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Completing the square gives \( (x+2)^2+4 \), which is of the form \( u^2+a^2 \) with \( u = x+2 \) and \( a=2 \). The substitution \( u = a \tan \theta \) (i.e., \( x+2 = 2 \tan \theta \)) transforms the integral into \( \int \frac{2 \sec^2 \theta d\theta}{4 \tan^2 \theta + 4} = \frac{1}{2} \int d\theta = \frac{1}{2} \theta + C = \frac{1}{2} \tan^{-1}((x+2)/2) + C \). The other options use the wrong trigonometric function for this form.

Q29. Which of the following substitutions would be valid for evaluating \( \int \sqrt{1-x^2} \, dx \) and \( \int \sqrt{x^2-1} \, dx \) respectively?

A. \( x = \sin \theta \); \( x = \sec \theta \) ✅
B. \( x = \cos \theta \); \( x = \tan \theta \)
C. \( x = \tan \theta \); \( x = \sin \theta \)
D. \( x = \sec \theta \); \( x = \cos \theta \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The radical \( \sqrt{1-x^2} \) requires a substitution where \( 1 - x^2 = \cos^2 \theta \), which is achieved by \( x = \sin \theta \) (or \( x = \cos \theta \)). The radical \( \sqrt{x^2-1} \) requires \( x^2-1 = \tan^2 \theta \), which is achieved by \( x = \sec \theta \). This is a fundamental pair of substitutions that students must know for these standard forms. The other options mix up the substitutions for different radical types.

Q30. A student evaluates \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2 \sqrt{4-x^2}} \) and obtains \( -\frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{4x} + C \). Was the student's answer correct?

A. Yes ✅
B. No; the correct answer is \( -\frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{4x} + C \)
C. No; the correct answer is \( -\frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{4x} + C \)
D. No; the correct answer is \( \frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{4x} + C \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using \( x = 2 \sin \theta \), \( dx = 2 \cos \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{4-x^2} = 2 \cos \theta \), the integral becomes \( \int \frac{2 \cos \theta}{4 \sin^2 \theta \cdot 2 \cos \theta} d\theta = \frac{1}{4} \int \csc^2 \theta d\theta = -\frac{1}{4} \cot \theta + C \). Since \( \cot \theta = \frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{x} \), the result is \( -\frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}{4x} + C \). The student's answer is correct. This question tests the ability to verify a given solution by working through the substitution and checking the simplification.

Q31. What is the value of the improper integral \( \int_0^1 \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \)?

A. \( \frac{\pi}{2} \) ✅
B. \( \frac{\pi}{4} \)
C. \( \pi \)
D. The integral diverges

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is a classic improper integral with a vertical asymptote at \( x=1 \). Using the substitution \( x = \sin \theta \), \( dx = \cos \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{1-x^2} = \cos \theta \), the limits become \( 0 \) and \( \pi/2 \). The integral becomes \( \int_0^{\pi/2} d\theta = \pi/2 \). The integral converges to a finite value. This problem tests the ability to handle an improper integral using a trigonometric substitution and evaluating the limit correctly.

Q32. For \( \int \frac{dx}{(x^2+1)^2} \), the substitution \( x = \tan \theta \) leads to:

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With \( x = \tan \theta \), \( dx = \sec^2 \theta d\theta \), and \( x^2+1 = \sec^2 \theta \). The integral becomes \( \int \frac{\sec^2 \theta}{\sec^4 \theta} d\theta = \int \cos^2 \theta d\theta \). This is a straightforward Easy. Q33. Evaluate the integral \( \int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+4x+8}} dx \). What is the correct antiderivative?

A. \( \sqrt{x^2+4x+8} - 2 \sinh^{-1}((x+2)/2) + C \) ✅
B. \( \sqrt{x^2+4x+8} + 2 \sinh^{-1}((x+2)/2) + C \)
C. \( \sqrt{x^2+4x+8} - 2 \tan^{-1}((x+2)/2) + C \)
D. \( \sqrt{x^2+4x+8} + 2 \tan^{-1}((x+2)/2) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Completing the square: \( x^2+4x+8 = (x+2)^2+4 \). Let \( u = x+2 \), then the integral becomes \( \int \frac{u-2}{\sqrt{u^2+4}} du = \int \frac{u}{\sqrt{u^2+4}} du - 2 \int \frac{du}{\sqrt{u^2+4}} = \sqrt{u^2+4} - 2 \sinh^{-1}(u/2) + C \). Substituting back gives \( \sqrt{x^2+4x+8} - 2 \sinh^{-1}((x+2)/2) + C \). This problem combines completing the square, splitting the numerator, and recognizing the derivative of a square root.

Q34. A student solves \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2 \sqrt{x^2+1}} \) using \( x = \tan \theta \) and gets \( -\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x} + C \). Is this correct?

A. No; the correct answer is \( -\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x} + C \) ✅
B. Yes
C. No; the correct answer is \( \frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x} + C \)
D. No; the correct answer is \( -\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+1}} + C \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With \( x = \tan \theta \), \( dx = \sec^2 \theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{x^2+1} = \sec \theta \). The integral becomes \( \int \frac{\sec^2 \theta}{\tan^2 \theta \sec \theta} d\theta = \int \frac{\sec \theta}{\tan^2 \theta} d\theta = \int \frac{1}{\sin \theta} \cdot \frac{\cos \theta}{\sin \theta} d\theta = \int \csc \theta \cot \theta d\theta = -\csc \theta + C \). Since \( \csc \theta = \frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x} \), the result is \( -\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{x} + C \). The student's answer is correct. This problem tests the ability to perform the substitution and correctly simplify the trigonometric expression.
