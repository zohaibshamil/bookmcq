Q1. Which of the following is the correct trigonometric substitution to evaluate \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} \) ?

🔹 A. \( x = 9\sin\theta \)
🔹 B. \( x = 3\tan\theta \)
🔹 C. \( x = 3\sin\theta \)
🔹 D. \( x = 9\sec\theta \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The integrand contains \( \sqrt{x^2 + 9} \) which matches the form \( \sqrt{x^2 + a^2} \). The correct substitution for this form is \( x = a\tan\theta \), where \( a = 3 \). Therefore \( x = 3\tan\theta \) is the appropriate substitution. Options A and C are used for \( \sqrt{a^2 - x^2} \), while option D is used for \( \sqrt{x^2 - a^2} \).

Q2. Evaluate \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + 16}} \) using the substitution \( x = 4\tan\theta \). What is the resulting integral in terms of \( \theta \)?

🔹 A. \( \int \sec\theta\,d\theta \)
🔹 B. \( \int \csc\theta\,d\theta \)
🔹 C. \( \int \sec^2\theta\,d\theta \)
🔹 D. \( \int \tan\theta\,d\theta \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using \( x = 4\tan\theta \), we get \( dx = 4\sec^2\theta\,d\theta \) and \( \sqrt{x^2 + 16} = \sqrt{16\tan^2\theta + 16} = 4\sec\theta \). Substituting: \( \int \frac{4\sec^2\theta}{4\sec\theta}d\theta = \int \sec\theta\,d\theta \). Option B would arise from using \( \csc\theta \) incorrectly, option C is the derivative of \( \tan\theta \), and option D would appear if the simplification was done incorrectly.

Q3. A student makes the substitution \( x = 5\tan\theta \) to evaluate \( \int \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + 25}} dx \). After simplification, they obtain \( 25\int \sec^3\theta\,d\theta - 25\int \sec\theta\,d\theta \). Which step in their solution is incorrect?

🔹 A. They used the identity \( \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta - 1 \)
🔹 B. They substituted \( dx = 5\sec^2\theta\,d\theta \)
🔹 C. They simplified \( \sqrt{x^2 + 25} \) to \( 5\sec\theta \)
🔹 D. They canceled terms incorrectly in the fraction

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Let's trace the correct simplification: \( dx = 5\sec^2\theta\,d\theta \), \( \sqrt{x^2+25} = 5\sec\theta \), and \( x^2 = 25\tan^2\theta \). Substituting: \( \int \frac{25\tan^2\theta}{5\sec\theta}(5\sec^2\theta\,d\theta) = 25\int \tan^2\theta\sec\theta\,d\theta \). Using \( \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta - 1 \), this becomes \( 25\int \sec^3\theta\,d\theta - 25\int \sec\theta\,d\theta \). The student's work is actually correct. No error was made in the listed steps, so the question itself contains a flaw in its premise.

The form \( \sqrt{x^2 + a^2} \) is specifically designed for \( x = a\tan\theta \) because it transforms to \( \sqrt{a^2\tan^2\theta + a^2} = a\sqrt{\tan^2\theta + 1} = a\sec\theta \), eliminating the radical completely. While \( x = 2\sin\theta \) could be used with more complex transformations, it's not the standard or preferred method. Option A is incorrect because \( \sin\theta \) is valid but not appropriate for this form. Option C is incorrect because it doesn't produce a negative under the radical. Q5. What is the value of \( \int_0^3 \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} \)?

🔹 A. \( \ln(1 + \sqrt{2}) \)
🔹 B. \( \sinh^{-1}(1) \)
🔹 C. \( \ln(1 + \sqrt{2}) \) and \( \sinh^{-1}(1) \) are equal
🔹 D. \( \ln(2 + \sqrt{5}) \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

\( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} = \sinh^{-1}(x/a) + C = \ln(x + \sqrt{x^2 + a^2}) + C \) (up to constant). Evaluating from 0 to 3 with a=3 gives \( \sinh^{-1}(1) = \ln(1 + \sqrt{2}) \). These are indeed equal due to the identity \( \sinh^{-1}(u) = \ln(u + \sqrt{u^2 + 1}) \). Option D would be correct if the upper limit was different. Options A and B are the same value expressed differently.

Q6. A particle moves along a path described by \( y = \sqrt{x^2 + 9} \). Which integral represents the arc length from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = 4 \)?

🔹 A. \( \int_0^4 \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} dx \)
🔹 B. \( \int_0^4 \sqrt{1 + \frac{x^2}{x^2 + 9}} dx \)
🔹 C. \( \int_0^4 \frac{\sqrt{2x^2 + 9}}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} dx \)
🔹 D. \( \int_0^4 \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} dx \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Arc length formula: \( L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + (dy/dx)^2}dx \). With \( y = \sqrt{x^2 + 9} \), \( dy/dx = x/\sqrt{x^2 + 9} \). So \( 1 + (dy/dx)^2 = 1 + \frac{x^2}{x^2 + 9} = \frac{2x^2 + 9}{x^2 + 9} \). Taking square root gives \( \frac{\sqrt{2x^2 + 9}}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} \). Option A is just the derivative. Option B is missing the square root simplification. Option D is incomplete.

While the resulting integral can be transformed, it's not the standard method. Option B is incorrect because \( \sqrt{a^2\sec^2\theta + a^2} \) doesn't simplify to \( a\tan\theta \). Q8. The graph of \( y = \sqrt{9 + x^2} \) is shown. If we approximate the integral \( \int_0^3 \sqrt{9 + x^2}dx \) using the midpoint rule with 3 subintervals, which geometric interpretation is correct?

The midpoint rule approximates the definite integral by partitioning the interval [0,3] into 3 equal subintervals of length 1, then evaluating the function at the midpoint of each subinterval: \( x = 0.5, 1.5, 2.5 \). The approximation is \( 1[f(0.5) + f(1.5) + f(2.5)] \), which geometrically is the sum of rectangles whose heights are the function values at the midpoints. Options B, C, and D describe different approximation methods (trapezoidal, right endpoint, left endpoint).

Q9. Which substitution would be most efficient to evaluate \( \int \frac{x^3}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4}} dx \)?

🔹 A. \( x = 2\tan\theta \)
🔹 B. \( u = x^2 + 4 \)
🔹 C. \( x = 2\sin\theta \)
🔹 D. Integration by parts with \( u = x^2 \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While \( x = 2\tan\theta \) would work, the substitution \( u = x^2 + 4 \) is more efficient here. Let \( u = x^2 + 4 \), then \( du = 2x\,dx \), and \( x^2 = u - 4 \). The integral becomes \( \int \frac{x^3}{\sqrt{x^2+4}}dx = \int \frac{x^2 \cdot x}{\sqrt{x^2+4}}dx = \frac{1}{2}\int \frac{u-4}{\sqrt{u}}du = \frac{1}{2}\int (u^{1/2} - 4u^{-1/2})du \). This avoids trigonometric substitutions entirely. Option A would work but is longer. Option C is inappropriate since it's for \( \sqrt{a^2-x^2} \). Option D doesn't address the radical.

Q11. An engineer needs to find the length of a parabolic cable \( y = \frac{x^2}{4} \) from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = 4 \). Which integral correctly represents this arc length?

🔹 A. \( \int_0^4 \sqrt{1 + x^2} dx \)
🔹 B. \( \int_0^4 \sqrt{1 + \frac{x^2}{4}} dx \)
🔹 C. \( \int_0^4 \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + x^2}} dx \)
🔹 D. \( \int_0^4 \sqrt{1 + \frac{x^2}{16}} dx \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Arc length formula: \( L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + (dy/dx)^2}dx \). For \( y = x^2/4 \), \( dy/dx = x/2 \). Then \( 1 + (dy/dx)^2 = 1 + x^2/4 \). So the integral is \( \int_0^4 \sqrt{1 + x^2/4} dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^4 \sqrt{4 + x^2} dx \). This matches option A after factoring out 1/2 from the square root: \( \frac{1}{2}\int_0^4 \sqrt{4+x^2}dx = \int_0^4 \sqrt{1+x^2/4}dx \). Option B has \( x^2/4 \), option C is incorrect, and option D has \( x^2/16 \).

Q12. Given that \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} = \ln(x + \sqrt{x^2 + a^2}) + C \), what is the value of \( \int_0^a \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} \)?

🔹 A. \( \ln(1 + \sqrt{2}) \)
🔹 B. \( \ln(a + a\sqrt{2}) - \ln a \)
🔹 C. \( \sinh^{-1}(1) \)
🔹 D. All of the above are equal

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Evaluating: \( \int_0^a \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} = [\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2})]_0^a = \ln(a + \sqrt{2a^2}) - \ln(0 + \sqrt{a^2}) = \ln(a(1+\sqrt{2})) - \ln a = \ln(1+\sqrt{2}) = \sinh^{-1}(1) \). All three expressions are equivalent. Option A is correct, option B is the same expression before simplification, and option C is the same value using inverse hyperbolic notation. This demonstrates the connection between logarithmic and hyperbolic forms.

Q14. What is the correct back-substitution for \( \theta \) in \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} \) after integrating to get \( \ln|\sec\theta + \tan\theta| + C \)?

🔹 A. \( \ln\left|\frac{\sqrt{x^2+9} + x}{3}\right| + C \)
🔹 B. \( \ln|\sqrt{x^2+9} + x| + C \)
🔹 C. \( \sinh^{-1}(x/3) + C \)
🔹 D. All of the above are equivalent up to a constant

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

With \( x = 3\tan\theta \), we have \( \tan\theta = x/3 \) and \( \sec\theta = \sqrt{x^2+9}/3 \). So \( \sec\theta + \tan\theta = \frac{\sqrt{x^2+9}+x}{3} \). Thus \( \ln|\sec\theta + \tan\theta| = \ln\left|\frac{\sqrt{x^2+9}+x}{3}\right| = \ln|\sqrt{x^2+9}+x| - \ln 3 \). The constant \( -\ln 3 \) can be absorbed into the constant of integration, so this is equivalent to \( \ln|\sqrt{x^2+9}+x| + C \). Also \( \sinh^{-1}(x/3) = \ln(x/3 + \sqrt{x^2/9+1}) = \ln\left(\frac{x+\sqrt{x^2+9}}{3}\right) \). All three are equivalent up to constants.

Q15. Which of the following is a correct first step to evaluate \( \int \frac{x^2}{(x^2+4)^{3/2}} dx \)?

🔹 A. Let \( u = x^2 + 4 \)
🔹 B. Let \( x = 2\tan\theta \), then \( x^2 + 4 = 4\sec^2\theta \)

📖 Explanation:

The form \( (x^2+4)^{3/2} \) with \( x^2+4 \) suggests \( x = 2\tan\theta \). Then \( x^2+4 = 4\tan^2\theta+4 = 4\sec^2\theta \), so \( (x^2+4)^{3/2} = 8\sec^3\theta \). Also \( dx = 2\sec^2\theta d\theta \) and \( x^2 = 4\tan^2\theta \). The integral becomes \( \int \frac{4\tan^2\theta}{8\sec^3\theta} 2\sec^2\theta d\theta = \int \tan^2\theta \sec^{-1}\theta d\theta = \int \sin^2\theta \cos^{-3}\theta d\theta \). Option A with \( u = x^2+4 \) gives \( du = 2x dx \), but the numerator has \( x^2 \), not \( x \), so it doesn't fully simplify. Option D uses the wrong substitution type (\( x = 2\sin\theta \) is for \( \sqrt{4-x^2} \)).

This interval choice is important because it ensures \( \sec\theta > 0 \), avoiding absolute value complications.

Q22. Evaluate \( \int_0^4 \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + 16}} \). Which expression correctly represents the answer?

🔹 A. \( \sinh^{-1}(1) \)
🔹 B. \( \ln(1 + \sqrt{2}) \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

Since the question asks for which expression correctly represents the answer, D is the more complex equivalent form.

Q23. When evaluating \( \int \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} dx \), which substitution would be most efficient?

🔹 A. \( u = x^2 + 9 \)
🔹 B. \( x = 3\tan\theta \)
🔹 C. \( x = 3\sec\theta \)
🔹 D. Integration by parts with \( u = x^2 \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

While \( u = x^2 + 9 \) would work (since \( x^2 = u-9 \), \( dx = du/(2x) \), but the x in the denominator complicates things), the trigonometric substitution \( x = 3\tan\theta \) is more straightforward. Then \( \sqrt{x^2+9} = 3\sec\theta \), \( dx = 3\sec^2\theta d\theta \), \( x^2 = 9\tan^2\theta \). The integral becomes \( \int \frac{9\tan^2\theta}{3\sec\theta} 3\sec^2\theta d\theta = 9\int \tan^2\theta\sec\theta d\theta \). Option C uses the wrong substitution type. Option D doesn't directly handle the radical. Option A leads to a more complex integral.

Q24. A student incorrectly states that \( \frac{d}{dx}\left[\sinh^{-1}(x/2)\right] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4-x^2}} \). What is their error?

🔹 A. They forgot the chain rule
🔹 B. They used the wrong inverse hyperbolic function
🔹 C. They used the derivative of \( \sin^{-1}(x/2) \)
🔹 D. They used the derivative of \( \cosh^{-1}(x/2) \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

\( \frac{d}{dx}[\sinh^{-1}(x/2)] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+4}} \). The student's result \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{4-x^2}} \) is actually the derivative of \( \sin^{-1}(x/2) \). The student confused inverse hyperbolic sine with inverse sine. Option A is incorrect because the chain rule is correctly applied (the derivative of \( x/2 \) is 1/2, which cancels). Option B is partially correct but option C is more precise. Option D would give \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2-4}} \) for \( x > 2 \).

Q25. What is the value of \( \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} dx \) in terms of inverse trigonometric functions?

🔹 A. \( \sin^{-1}(x/a) + C \)
🔹 B. \( \cos^{-1}(x/a) + C \)
🔹 C. \( \tan^{-1}(x/a) + C \)
🔹 D. It cannot be expressed in terms of inverse trigonometric functions

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

\( \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} dx \) is \( \sinh^{-1}(x/a) + C = \ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}) + C \). It cannot be expressed in terms of inverse trigonometric functions like \( \sin^{-1} \), \( \cos^{-1} \), or \( \tan^{-1} \). The inverse trigonometric functions appear in integrals with \( \sqrt{a^2-x^2} \) (\( \sin^{-1} \) or \( \cos^{-1} \)) or \( a^2+x^2 \) (\( \tan^{-1} \)). Option D is correct. Options A, B, and C are common misconceptions from confusing different radical forms.

Q26. Which of the following integrals requires a trigonometric substitution of the form \( x = a\tan\theta \)?

🔹 A. \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{9-x^2}} \)
🔹 B. \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+9}} \)
🔹 C. \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2-9}} \)
🔹 D. \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2+9} \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The form \( \sqrt{x^2 + a^2} \) specifically requires \( x = a\tan\theta \). Option A has \( \sqrt{9-x^2} \), requiring \( x = 3\sin\theta \). Option C has \( \sqrt{x^2-9} \), requiring \( x = 3\sec\theta \). Option D has \( x^2+9 \) without a square root, which can be integrated directly as \( \frac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}(x/3) \). Option B is the only one with \( \sqrt{x^2 + a^2} \) form.

Q27. In evaluating \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} \), why do we prefer \( x = a\tan\theta \) over \( x = a\sinh u \)?

🔹 A. \( a\tan\theta \) is always simpler
🔹 B. \( a\sinh u \) is not valid
🔹 C. Neither is preferred; it depends on the context
🔹 D. \( a\tan\theta \) works for all \( x \), while \( a\sinh u \) has domain restrictions

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Both substitutions are valid and lead to the same result. \( x = a\tan\theta \) with \( \theta \in (-\pi/2, \pi/2) \) gives \( \sqrt{x^2+a^2} = a\sec\theta \), leading to \( \int \sec\theta\,d\theta \). \( x = a\sinh u \) gives \( \sqrt{x^2+a^2} = a\cosh u \), leading to \( \int du = u + C = \sinh^{-1}(x/a) \). The choice depends on whether the student is more comfortable with trigonometric or hyperbolic functions. Option A is subjective. Option B is false. Option D is incorrect because \( \sinh u \) maps all real u to all real x.

Q28. A student evaluating \( \int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 4}} dx \) uses the substitution \( u = x^2 + 4 \). What do they obtain?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{2}\int \frac{du}{\sqrt{u}} \)
🔹 B. \( \int \frac{du}{\sqrt{u}} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{1}{2}\int \frac{du}{u^{3/2}} \)
🔹 D. \( \int \frac{du}{u^{3/2}} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Let \( u = x^2 + 4 \). Then \( du = 2x\,dx \), so \( x\,dx = \frac{1}{2}du \). The integral becomes \( \int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+4}}dx = \frac{1}{2}\int \frac{du}{\sqrt{u}} \). Option B is missing the \( 1/2 \) factor. Options C and D would result from differentiating incorrectly (like \( du = x\,dx \) instead of \( 2x\,dx \)). This is a good example where a simple u-substitution is more efficient than trigonometric substitution.

Q29. Which of the following is the correct antiderivative of \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} \)?

🔹 A. \( \sin^{-1}(x/a) + C \)
🔹 B. \( \sinh^{-1}(x/a) + C \)
🔹 C. \( \tan^{-1}(x/a) + C \)
🔹 D. \( \cosh^{-1}(x/a) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

\( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} = \sinh^{-1}(x/a) + C = \ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}) + C \). This is the standard result. Option A is for \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}} \). Option C is for \( \int \frac{dx}{a^2+x^2} \). Option D is for \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2-a^2}} \) with \( x > a \). It's important to remember the four key forms and their corresponding antiderivatives.

Q30. A particle travels along the curve \( y = \frac{1}{2}(e^x - e^{-x}) \). The arc length from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = \ln 2 \) is given by \( \int_0^{\ln 2} \cosh x dx \). What is this value?

🔹 A. \( \frac{3}{4} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{1}{2} \)
🔹 C. \( \ln 2 \)
🔹 D. \( 1 \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{2}(e^x + e^{-x}) = \cosh x \), the arc length integral is \( \int_0^{\ln 2} \sqrt{1 + \cosh^2 x} dx \). But \( 1 + \cosh^2 x \neq \cosh^2 x \). Wait, the question states the arc length is \( \int_0^{\ln 2} \cosh x dx \), which implies \( \sqrt{1 + (dy/dx)^2} = \cosh x \), so \( 1 + (dy/dx)^2 = \cosh^2 x \), which would mean \( dy/dx = \sinh x \). This is inconsistent. The correct arc length for \( y = \sinh x \) is \( \int \cosh x dx \). So if \( y = \sinh x \), then \( \int_0^{\ln 2} \cosh x dx = [\sinh x]_0^{\ln 2} = \sinh(\ln 2) - 0 = \frac{2 - 1/2}{2} = \frac{3}{4} \). Option A is correct. This question tests the connection between derivatives of hyperbolic functions and arc length.

Q31. When using \( x = 3\tan\theta \) to evaluate \( \int_0^3 \frac{dx}{x^2+9} \), what is the correct transformed integral?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{3}\int_0^{\pi/4} d\theta \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{1}{3}\int_0^{1} d\theta \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{1}{9}\int_0^{\pi/4} d\theta \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{1}{3}\int_0^{1} d\theta \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

With \( x = 3\tan\theta \), \( dx = 3\sec^2\theta d\theta \), and \( x^2+9 = 9\sec^2\theta \). The integral becomes \( \int \frac{3\sec^2\theta}{9\sec^2\theta} d\theta = \frac{1}{3}\int d\theta \). The limits: when \( x = 0 \), \( \theta = 0 \); when \( x = 3 \), \( \theta = \tan^{-1}(1) = \pi/4 \). So it's \( \frac{1}{3}\int_0^{\pi/4} d\theta \). Option A is correct. Option B has upper limit 1 instead of \( \pi/4 \), confusing radians with tangent values. Option C has \( 1/9 \) incorrectly. Option D combines both errors.

Q32. A container is shaped like the solid formed by rotating the region under \( y = \sqrt{x^2 + 1} \) from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = 1 \) about the x-axis. Which integral represents its volume?

🔹 A. \( \pi\int_0^1 (x^2+1) dx \)
🔹 B. \( \pi\int_0^1 \sqrt{x^2+1} dx \)
🔹 C. \( 2\pi\int_0^1 (x^2+1) dx \)
🔹 D. \( \pi\int_0^1 (x^2+1)^{1/2} dx \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Disk method: \( V = \pi\int_a^b [f(x)]^2 dx \). Here \( f(x) = \sqrt{x^2+1} \), so \( [f(x)]^2 = x^2+1 \). Thus \( V = \pi\int_0^1 (x^2+1) dx \). Option B is missing the square. Option C has an extra factor of 2 (shell method). Option D has the wrong exponent. This is a straightforward Easy of the disk method with a function involving \( \sqrt{x^2+a^2} \).

Q33. Evaluate \( \int \frac{x^3}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} dx \) using the substitution \( u = \sqrt{x^2 + a^2} \). What is the resulting integral in terms of \( u \)?

🔹 A. \( \int (u^2 - a^2) du \)
🔹 B. \( \int \frac{u^2 - a^2}{u^2} du \)
🔹 C. \( \int (u^2 - a^2) u du \)
🔹 D. \( \int \frac{u^2 - a^2}{u} du \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Let \( u = \sqrt{x^2 + a^2} \). Then \( u^2 = x^2 + a^2 \), so \( 2u\,du = 2x\,dx \), giving \( x\,dx = u\,du \). Also \( x^2 = u^2 - a^2 \), so \( x^3\,dx = x^2 \cdot x\,dx = (u^2 - a^2)u\,du \). Thus \( \int \frac{x^3}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} dx = \int \frac{(u^2-a^2)u}{u} du = \int (u^2 - a^2) du \). Option A is correct. Option B has \( u^2 \) in denominator. Option C has an extra u. Option D has division by u.

Q34. The graph of \( y = \sqrt{x^2 + 4} \) is shown. If we use the substitution \( x = 2\tan\theta \), which of the following correctly describes the transformation of the area from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = 2 \)?

🔹 A. The area becomes \( \int_0^{\pi/4} 4\sec^3\theta\,d\theta \)
🔹 B. The area becomes \( \int_0^{\pi/4} 4\sec^2\theta\,d\theta \)
🔹 C. The area becomes \( \int_0^{\pi/4} 2\sec^2\theta\,d\theta \)
🔹 D. The area becomes \( \int_0^{\pi/4} 4\sec\theta\,d\theta \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Area under \( y = \sqrt{x^2+4} \) from 0 to 2 is \( \int_0^2 \sqrt{x^2+4} dx \). With \( x = 2\tan\theta \), \( dx = 2\sec^2\theta d\theta \), \( \sqrt{x^2+4} = \sqrt{4\tan^2\theta+4} = 2\sec\theta \). The integrand becomes \( 2\sec\theta \cdot 2\sec^2\theta d\theta = 4\sec^3\theta d\theta \). Limits: \( x = 0 \to \theta = 0 \), \( x = 2 \to \theta = \pi/4 \). So the integral is \( \int_0^{\pi/4} 4\sec^3\theta\,d\theta \). Option A is correct. Option B and C have the wrong power of sec. Option D is the substitution for a different radical form.

Q35. What is the value of \( \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + 9}} dx \) for \( x \) in terms of inverse trigonometric functions?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}(x/3) + C \)
🔹 B. \( \sin^{-1}(x/3) + C \)
🔹 C. \( \cos^{-1}(x/3) + C \)
🔹 D. Cannot be expressed in terms of inverse trigonometric functions

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

As established earlier, \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} = \sinh^{-1}(x/a) + C = \ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}) + C \). It cannot be expressed using inverse trigonometric functions like \( \sin^{-1} \), \( \cos^{-1} \), or \( \tan^{-1} \). Option A is for \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2+9} \). Options B and C are for \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{9-x^2}} \). This is a common point of confusion that requires careful attention to the form under the radical.

Q36. Evaluate \( \int_0^{2} \sqrt{x^2 + 4} dx \) using the substitution \( x = 2\tan\theta \). What is the exact value?

🔹 A. \( 2\sqrt{2} + 2\ln(1+\sqrt{2}) \)
🔹 B. \( 2\sqrt{2} + 4\ln(1+\sqrt{2}) \)
🔹 C. \( \sqrt{2} + 2\ln(1+\sqrt{2}) \)
🔹 D. \( 2\sqrt{2} + \ln(1+\sqrt{2}) \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using \( x = 2\tan\theta \), \( dx = 2\sec^2\theta d\theta \), \( \sqrt{x^2+4} = 2\sec\theta \). The integral becomes \( \int_0^{\pi/4} 4\sec^3\theta d\theta \). Using the reduction formula \( \int \sec^3\theta d\theta = \frac{1}{2}\sec\theta\tan\theta + \frac{1}{2}\ln|\sec\theta+\tan\theta| + C \), we get \( 4[\frac{1}{2}\sec\theta\tan\theta + \frac{1}{2}\ln(\sec\theta+\tan\theta)]_0^{\pi/4} = 2[\sec\theta\tan\theta + \ln(\sec\theta+\tan\theta)]_0^{\pi/4} \). At \( \theta = \pi/4 \), \( \sec\theta = \sqrt{2} \), \( \tan\theta = 1 \), so \( \sec\theta\tan\theta = \sqrt{2} \), and \( \sec\theta+\tan\theta = \sqrt{2}+1 \). At \( \theta = 0 \), \( \sec\theta = 1 \), \( \tan\theta = 0 \), so \( \sec\theta\tan\theta = 0 \), and \( \sec\theta+\tan\theta = 1 \), whose log is 0. Thus the integral is \( 2[\sqrt{2} + \ln(1+\sqrt{2})] = 2\sqrt{2} + 2\ln(1+\sqrt{2}) \). Option A is correct.

Q37. A student claims that \( \int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} dx = \sqrt{x^2 + a^2} + C \). Which of the following best analyzes this claim?

🔹 A. The claim is correct
🔹 B. The claim is incorrect; the antiderivative is \( \ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}) + C \)
🔹 C. The claim is incorrect; the antiderivative is \( \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{x^2+a^2} + C \)
🔹 D. The claim is incorrect; the antiderivative is \( \frac{x^2}{2\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} + C \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Let's differentiate \( \sqrt{x^2+a^2} \). Using the chain rule, \( \frac{d}{dx}\sqrt{x^2+a^2} = \frac{1}{2}(x^2+a^2)^{-1/2} \cdot 2x = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} \). This matches the integrand exactly. So the student's claim is correct. Option B is the antiderivative of \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} \). Option C is missing the factor of 2 in the chain rule. Option D is incorrect. This is a good example of why checking by differentiation is important.

Q38. Which of the following is the correct antiderivative of \( \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} \)?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{2}(x\sqrt{x^2+a^2} - a^2\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2})) + C \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{1}{2}(x\sqrt{x^2+a^2} + a^2\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2})) + C \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{1}{2}(x\sqrt{x^2+a^2} - a^2\sinh^{-1}(x/a)) + C \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{1}{2}(x\sqrt{x^2+a^2} + a^2\sinh^{-1}(x/a)) + C \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using integration by parts with \( u = x \), \( dv = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} dx \), we get \( du = dx \), \( v = \sqrt{x^2+a^2} \). Then \( \int \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} dx = x\sqrt{x^2+a^2} - \int \sqrt{x^2+a^2} dx \). We know \( \int \sqrt{x^2+a^2} dx = \frac{1}{2}x\sqrt{x^2+a^2} + \frac{a^2}{2}\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}) + C \). Substituting: \( x\sqrt{x^2+a^2} - \frac{1}{2}x\sqrt{x^2+a^2} - \frac{a^2}{2}\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}) = \frac{1}{2}x\sqrt{x^2+a^2} - \frac{a^2}{2}\ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}) + C \). Option A is correct. Option B has plus instead of minus. Options C and D use \( \sinh^{-1} \) but \( \ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}) = \sinh^{-1}(x/a) + \ln a \), and the constant could be absorbed, so C would be equivalent to A if the sign of \( a^2 \) is correct.

Q39. What is the value of \( \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} \)?

🔹 A. \( \infty \)
🔹 B. \( 2\sinh^{-1}(\infty) = \infty \)
🔹 C. \( \pi/a \)
🔹 D. \( \pi \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The integral is improper: \( \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} = \lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{-R}^{R} \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} = \lim_{R\to\infty} 2\sinh^{-1}(R/a) = \infty \). The integrand does not decay fast enough for the integral to converge (it behaves like \( 1/|x| \) at infinity). This is similar to \( \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{1}{x^2+1} dx = \pi \), but the \( \sqrt{x^2+a^2} \) in the denominator makes the decay slower. Option C would be the value for \( \int \frac{dx}{x^2+a^2} \). Option D would be for \( \int \frac{dx}{a^2-x^2} \) with appropriate limits.

Q40. Which of the following is a correct first step to evaluate \( \int \sqrt{x^2 + 9} dx \)?

🔹 A. \( \int \sqrt{x^2+9} dx = \frac{1}{3}\int \sqrt{\tan^2\theta+1} d\theta \)
🔹 B. \( \int \sqrt{x^2+9} dx = 9\int \sec^3\theta d\theta \)
🔹 C. \( \int \sqrt{x^2+9} dx = 9\int \tan^2\theta\sec\theta d\theta \)
🔹 D. \( \int \sqrt{x^2+9} dx = 9\int \sec^3\theta d\theta \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

With \( x = 3\tan\theta \), \( dx = 3\sec^2\theta d\theta \), \( \sqrt{x^2+9} = 3\sec\theta \). So \( \int \sqrt{x^2+9} dx = \int 3\sec\theta \cdot 3\sec^2\theta d\theta = 9\int \sec^3\theta d\theta \). Option B is correct. Option A has \( \frac{1}{3} \) and \( \sqrt{\tan^2\theta+1} \) incorrectly. Option C has \( \tan^2\theta\sec\theta \) which would come from \( \int \frac{x^2}{\sqrt{x^2+9}} dx \). Option D has 9 instead of 9? Actually D is the same as B, so there's a duplicate. The correct is B (or D if they're identical).

Q41. A rectangular plot of land has a curved boundary given by \( y = \sqrt{x^2 + 25} \) from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = 5 \). What is the length of this curved boundary?

🔹 A. \( \int_0^5 \sqrt{1 + \frac{x^2}{x^2+25}} dx \)
🔹 B. \( \int_0^5 \frac{\sqrt{2x^2+25}}{\sqrt{x^2+25}} dx \)
🔹 C. \( \int_0^5 \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+25}} dx \)
🔹 D. \( \int_0^5 \sqrt{\frac{2x^2+25}{x^2+25}} dx \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Arc length formula: \( L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1+(dy/dx)^2} dx \). For \( y = \sqrt{x^2+25} \), \( dy/dx = x/\sqrt{x^2+25} \). Then \( 1+(dy/dx)^2 = 1 + \frac{x^2}{x^2+25} = \frac{x^2+25+x^2}{x^2+25} = \frac{2x^2+25}{x^2+25} \). Taking square root gives \( \frac{\sqrt{2x^2+25}}{\sqrt{x^2+25}} \). Option B is correct. Option A is missing the square root of the sum. Option C is just the derivative. Option D has the same expression as B but written differently. Actually B and D are the same. This question tests the Easy of arc length formula to functions involving \( \sqrt{x^2+a^2} \).

Q42. When evaluating \( \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + a^2}} dx \), if a student uses \( x = a\cot\theta \) instead of \( x = a\tan\theta \), what happens?

🔹 A. They get the same result after simplification
🔹 B. They get a different but valid result
🔹 C. The substitution is invalid
🔹 D. They get the integral of \( -\csc\theta d\theta \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

If a student uses \( x = a\cot\theta \), then \( dx = -a\csc^2\theta d\theta \), and \( \sqrt{x^2+a^2} = \sqrt{a^2\cot^2\theta+a^2} = a\sqrt{\cot^2\theta+1} = a\csc\theta \) (assuming \( \theta \in (0,\pi) \), where \( \csc\theta > 0 \)). Then \( \int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^2+a^2}} = \int \frac{-a\csc^2\theta}{a\csc\theta} d\theta = -\int \csc\theta d\theta = \ln|\csc\theta+\cot\theta| + C \). This is equal to \( \ln(x+\sqrt{x^2+a^2}) + C \) up to a constant. Option D is correct for the intermediate step. Option A is correct in the sense that it yields the same final result. The question asks what happens, so D is the more immediate result.
