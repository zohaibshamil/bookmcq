What is Special substitutions for integrals?

Definition:
Special substitutions like u=ax+bn u = \sqrt[n]{ax+b} u=nax+b​ or hyperbolic substitutions are used for specific algebraic forms that do not fit standard trigonometric or partial fraction methods.

Example:
For ∫dx1+x \int \frac{dx}{1+\sqrt{x}} ∫1+x​dx​, let u=x u=\sqrt{x} u=x​, so x=u2,dx=2udu x=u^2, dx=2u du x=u2,dx=2udu. The integral becomes ∫2u1+udu=2(u−ln⁡∣1+u∣)+C \int \frac{2u}{1+u} du = 2(u - \ln|1+u|) + C ∫1+u2u​du=2(u−ln∣1+u∣)+C.

Reason:
These targeted substitutions simplify radicals or complex algebraic structures into rational functions, making them amenable to standard integration techniques like partial fractions.
