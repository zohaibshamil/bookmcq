💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The primary goal of reduction formulas like \( \int \sin^n x \, dx = -\frac{1}{n}\sin^{n-1}x\cos x + \frac{n-1}{n}\int \sin^{n-2}x \, dx \) is to systematically lower the exponent by 2. 📖 Explanation:

The reduction formula \( \int \sin^n x \, dx = -\frac{1}{n}\sin^{n-1} x \cos x + \frac{n-1}{n} \int \sin^{n-2} x \, dx \) reduces the exponent by 2 each time. For \( n=12 \) (even), it takes 6 Easys to reach \( \int \sin^0 x \, dx = \int 1 \, dx = x \). For \( n=11 \) (odd), it takes 5 Easys to reach \( \int \sin^1 x \, dx = \int \sin x \, dx = -\cos x \). This understanding is crucial for efficiently evaluating higher powers and planning the integration path. The number of steps is directly determined by the parity of the exponent.

Q25. Which statement correctly describes the graph of \( \sin^n x \) for increasing even integer \( n \) over \( [0, \pi] \)?

🔹 A. The peaks become taller and the curves become more rounded.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

For any positive even integer \( n \), \( \sin^n x \) has a maximum value of 1 at \( x = \pi/2 \) because \( \sin(\pi/2) = 1 \). As \( n \) increases, the function becomes 'flatter' near the peaks and drops off more steeply near the edges of the interval, so the area under the curve decreases. This is because values of \( \sin x \) less than 1 become smaller when raised to a higher power. This has significant implications for probability and physics, as it describes the shape of distributions. The integral \( \int_0^\pi \sin^n x \, dx \) is often used to compute volumes and probabilities.

Q26. What is the correct expression for \( \int \sin^4 x \cos^2 x \, dx \) after applying the half-angle identities \( \sin^2 x = \frac{1}{2}(1-\cos 2x) \) and \( \cos^2 x = \frac{1}{2}(1+\cos 2x) \)?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{8} \int (1 - \cos 2x)^2 (1 + \cos 2x) \, dx \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{1}{4} \int (1 - \cos 2x)(1 + \cos 2x) \, dx \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{1}{8} \int (1 - \cos 2x) (1 + \cos 2x)^2 \, dx \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Applying the half-angle identities gives \( \sin^4 x = \left( \frac{1}{2}(1-\cos 2x) \right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}(1-\cos 2x)^2 \) and \( \cos^2 x = \frac{1}{2}(1+\cos 2x) \). Multiplying these gives \( \frac{1}{8} (1-\cos 2x)^2 (1+\cos 2x) \). Expanding this expression will produce a sum of powers of \( \cos 2x \), which can then be further reduced using the half-angle identity again. This question tests the student's ability to correctly apply the identities and combine constants, a common source of errors.

Q27. Why is the integral \( \int \sin^4 x \cos^3 x \, dx \) easier to solve than \( \int \sin^4 x \cos^2 x \, dx \)?

🔹 A. Because the exponent of cosine is odd, allowing for a \( u \)-substitution.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The integral \( \int \sin^4 x \cos^3 x \, dx \) has an odd exponent on cosine, so we can factor out one cosine, convert the rest to sine, and use \( u \)-substitution. The integral \( \int \sin^4 x \cos^2 x \, dx \) has both exponents even, requiring the more complex half-angle identities. This demonstrates a critical point in integration strategy: recognizing the parity of the exponents is the key to choosing the most efficient method. The odd exponent makes the integral a straightforward polynomial after substitution, whereas the all-even case is more involved.

Q28. A student attempts to evaluate \( \int \sin^4 x \cos^4 x \, dx \) by taking the fourth root, rewriting it as \( \int (\sin x \cos x)^4 \, dx \), and then using the identity \( \sin 2x = 2\sin x \cos x \). What is the resulting integral in terms of \( \sin 2x \)?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{16} \int \sin^4 2x \, dx \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{1}{8} \int \sin^4 2x \, dx \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{1}{4} \int \sin^4 2x \, dx \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{1}{2} \int \sin^4 2x \, dx \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since \( \sin x \cos x = \frac{1}{2} \sin 2x \), we have \( (\sin x \cos x)^4 = \left( \frac{1}{2} \sin 2x \right)^4 = \frac{1}{16} \sin^4 2x \). This is a clever simplification that turns a product of even powers of sine and cosine into a single power of a sine function. This is a very useful trick for this specific form. The student must be careful with the constant factor, as \( (1/2)^4 = 1/16 \), not \( 1/8 \) or \( 1/4 \). This approach is far more efficient than expanding using half-angle identities multiple times.

Q29. If the substitution \( u = \sin x \) is used to evaluate \( \int \sin^5 x \cos^2 x \, dx \), what is the resulting polynomial integrand in terms of \( u \)?

🔹 A. \( \int (1-u^2)^2 u^2 \, du \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Since the exponent of sine is odd, we save a factor of \( \sin x \) for \( du \), so \( du = \cos x \, dx \). With \( u = \cos x \), the integrand becomes \( -(1-u^2)^2 u^2 \, du \). Option D correctly represents the integrand with the negative sign that comes from \( \sin x \, dx = -du \). Q30. What is the maximum value of \( \sin^n x \cos^m x \) for even \( n \) and \( m \)?

Q31. A student evaluates \( \int \sin^2 x \cos^2 x \, dx \) and gets \( \frac{x}{8} - \frac{\sin 4x}{32} + C \). Is this correct?

Q33. What is the value of \( \int_0^{\pi} \sin^4 x \cos^2 x \, dx \)?

🔹 A. \( \frac{\pi}{16} \)

🔹 D. \( \frac{\pi}{2} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using the half-angle identities and the properties of definite integrals, \( \int_0^\pi \sin^4 x \cos^2 x \, dx = \frac{1}{8} \int_0^\pi (1-\cos 2x)^2(1+\cos 2x) \, dx \). Expanding and integrating term by term using the fact that \( \int_0^\pi \cos kx \, dx = 0 \) for integer \( k \), the constant term becomes \( \frac{1}{8} \int_0^\pi 1 \, dx = \frac{\pi}{8} \), but the average value of the sine and cosine powers over a full period is \( \frac{1}{4} \), leading to \( \frac{\pi}{16} \). This problem requires careful algebraic manipulation and knowledge of definite integrals of even functions. It also tests the ability to integrate powers of cosine. A full solution would involve expanding the integrand and using the reduction formula or half-angle identities.
