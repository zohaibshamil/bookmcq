📝 Trapezoidal rule integration

What is Trapezoidal rule integration?

Definition:
The Trapezoidal Rule approximates the area under a curve by dividing the interval into subintervals and summing the areas of trapezoids formed by function values at endpoints. Formula: \( T_n = \frac{\Delta x}{2}[f(x_0) + 2\sum f(x_i) + f(x_n)] \).

Example:
For \( \int_0^2 x^2 dx \) with \( n=2 \), \( \Delta x=1 \), \( T_2 = \frac{1}{2}[0 + 2(1) + 4] = 3 \), close to exact 8/3.

Reason:
It provides a simple linear approximation that improves with more subintervals, offering a balance between computational ease and accuracy for smooth functions.

Q1. A function \( f(x) \) is strictly concave up on the interval \([a, b]\). Without performing any calculations, which inequality correctly describes the relationship between the Trapezoidal approximation \( T_n \), the Midpoint approximation \( M_n \), and the exact integral \( I = \int_a^b f(x) \, dx \)?

A. \( T_n < I < M_n \)
B. \( M_n < I < T_n \) ✅
C. \( I < M_n < T_n \)
D. \( M_n < T_n < I \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When a function is strictly concave up on an interval, the trapezoids used in the Trapezoidal rule lie above the curve, resulting in an overestimate of the area. Conversely, the tangent lines at the midpoints used in the Midpoint rule lie below the curve, resulting in an underestimate. Therefore, for a concave up function, the Midpoint approximation is less than the true integral, which is less than the Trapezoidal approximation, yielding \( M_n < I < T_n \).

Q2. You are approximating \( \int_0^1 \sqrt{x} \, dx \) using the Trapezoidal Rule. What is the primary mathematical reason for this degraded performance?

B. The Trapezoidal Rule only works accurately for polynomials of degree 2 or less.

D. The function is concave down, causing systematic cancellation errors.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The standard error bound for the Trapezoidal Rule depends on the maximum value of the second derivative \( |f''(x)| \) on the interval. Because the derivative is unbounded, the constant \( K_2 \) in the error formula does not exist, invalidating the standard \( O(1/n^2) \) convergence rate. This requires modified analysis or variable transformations to restore optimal convergence.

Q5. An engineer computes \( \int_0^1 e^{-x^2} \, dx \) using the Trapezoidal Rule. They observe that doubling \( n \) from 10 to 20 reduces the absolute error by a factor of approximately 4. If they double \( n \) again to 40, but the error only reduces by a factor of 2 instead of 4, what is the most likely explanation?

A. The function changed concavity within the new subintervals.

C. The error bound formula only applies to even values of \( n \).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Numerical integration involves two competing error sources: truncation error (which decreases as \( n \) increases) and round-off error (which increases as \( n \) increases due to more arithmetic operations). Initially, truncation error dominates, showing the expected \( O(1/n^2) \) behavior where doubling \( n \) quarters the error. However, once \( n \) becomes sufficiently large, the accumulated floating-point round-off errors begin to dominate. At this stage, further increasing \( n \) no longer improves accuracy and may degrade it, representing a fundamental computational limit distinct from theoretical calculus.

Q6. Given the graph of a continuous function \( f(x) \) on \([a,b]\) that is positive, decreasing, and concave down, arrange the following quantities in strictly increasing order: Left Riemann Sum (\( L_n \)), Right Riemann Sum (\( R_n \)), Trapezoidal Approximation (\( T_n \)), and Exact Integral (\( I \)).

A. \( R_n < T_n < I < L_n \) ✅
B. \( L_n < T_n < I < R_n \)
C. \( R_n < I < T_n < L_n \)
D. \( T_n < R_n < I < L_n \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

For a decreasing function, the Left Sum overestimates and the Right Sum underestimates the integral, so \( R_n < I < L_n \). Since the function is concave down, the trapezoids lie below the curve, meaning \( T_n < I \). Additionally, the Trapezoidal Rule is the average of Left and Right sums: \( T_n = (L_n + R_n)/2 \). Combining these facts for a decreasing, concave down function yields the ordering \( R_n < T_n < I < L_n \). Understanding this requires synthesizing monotonicity and concavity properties geometrically.

Q7. A student attempts to approximate \( \int_{-1}^1 |x| \, dx \) using the Trapezoidal Rule with \( n=2 \) subintervals. They obtain an exact answer of 1. 💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

With \( n=3 \), the nodes are \(-1, -1/3, 1/3, 1\); the middle subinterval \([-1/3, 1/3]\) straddles the corner, so the linear interpolant differs from \( |x| \), introducing error. Q9. Suppose \( f(x) \) satisfies \( f(a) = f(b) \) and \( f'(a) = f'(b) \). When applying the Trapezoidal Rule to \( \int_a^b f(x) \, dx \), the observed error decays much faster than \( O(1/n^2) \). What explains this superconvergence?

A. The boundary terms in the Euler-Maclaurin expansion vanish due to periodicity conditions. ✅
B. The function must be a trigonometric polynomial.
C. The Trapezoidal Rule automatically detects endpoint matching and adjusts weights.
D. Second-order error terms cancel only when \( n \) is a power of 2.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Euler-Maclaurin formula expresses the Trapezoidal Rule error as an asymptotic series involving derivatives at endpoints. The leading error term is proportional to \( f'(b) - f'(a) \). When both function values and first derivatives match at endpoints (periodic boundary conditions), this leading term vanishes. Subsequent terms involve higher odd derivatives, which also vanish if sufficient smoothness and periodicity hold. This causes the error to decay exponentially or as a very high power of \( 1/n \), far exceeding the standard algebraic convergence rate. This connects numerical analysis to Fourier analysis concepts.

Applying the uniform-spacing formula to non-uniform data produces systematically incorrect results. Recognizing when standard shortcuts fail and reverting to first principles is essential for real-world data analysis where controlled experimental conditions rarely produce perfectly uniform sampling.

Q11. When approximating \( \int_0^\pi \sin(x) \, dx \) using the Trapezoidal Rule, the approximation consistently underestimates the true value of 2 for any finite \( n \). Which property of \( \sin(x) \) on this interval guarantees this directional bias?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The geometric interpretation of the Trapezoidal Rule connects directly to concavity. Geometrically, chords connecting points on a concave-down curve lie below the curve itself. Since trapezoids are formed by these chords, their combined area must be less than the area under the curve. This fundamental relationship between second derivative sign and approximation bias is central to understanding numerical integration error behavior.

Q13. You need to estimate the area under a curve known to be highly oscillatory with frequency much higher than your sampling rate allows. Before applying the Trapezoidal Rule, what preprocessing step is mathematically necessary to avoid catastrophic error?

📖 Explanation:

The Trapezoidal Rule samples the function at discrete points. No amount of sophisticated integration can recover information lost to undersampling. The only valid solutions are increasing sampling density to satisfy Nyquist or pre-filtering to band-limit the signal. This connects numerical integration to signal processing fundamentals, emphasizing that discretization assumptions matter as much as the integration formula itself.

Q17. A student derives the Trapezoidal Rule error bound and obtains \( E_T = -\frac{(b-a)^3}{12n^2} f''(\xi) \). They notice the negative sign and conclude that the Trapezoidal Rule ALWAYS underestimates the integral. What is the flaw in this reasoning?

A. The error bound formula is incorrect; there should be no negative sign.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The error formula contains \( f''(\xi) \) for some unknown \( \xi \in [a,b] \). The negative sign in front does not determine the error’s sign alone; the sign of \( f''(\xi) \) matters equally. If \( f'' > 0 \) (concave up), the product is negative, indicating underestimation. If \( f'' < 0 \) (concave down), the product is positive, indicating overestimation. The student incorrectly treated the formula’s constant factor as determining sign independently of the function’s curvature. Proper interpretation requires considering both factors together, reinforcing that error formulas encode conditional behavior, not universal directionality.

Q18. In computational finance, option pricing often requires integrating payoff functions with kinks (discontinuous derivatives). Why might practitioners prefer the Trapezoidal Rule over higher-order Newton-Cotes formulas for such problems?

A. Higher-order methods require continuous derivatives for their error bounds to hold, and may oscillate near discontinuities. ✅
B. The Trapezoidal Rule is computationally faster for all financial Mediums.
C. Financial regulations mandate the use of the Trapezoidal Rule for compliance.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Higher-order quadrature methods achieve their superior convergence rates by assuming sufficient smoothness. At points where derivatives are discontinuous (kinks), the theoretical error expansions break down, and higher-order polynomial interpolation can introduce spurious oscillations (Runge-type phenomena) near the singularity. The Trapezoidal Rule, relying only on linear interpolation, is insensitive to derivative discontinuities and maintains stable, predictable \( O(1/n^2) \) convergence even for non-smooth integrands. In applied contexts where integrand regularity cannot be guaranteed, robustness often trumps theoretical optimality, making simpler methods practically superior.

Q19. Consider approximating \( \int_0^1 f(x) \, dx \) where \( f(x) \) is unknown but you know \( |f''(x)| \leq 10 \) for all \( x \in [0,1] \). What is the minimum \( n \) required to guarantee the Trapezoidal approximation error is less than \( 0.001 \)?

A. \( n \geq 29 \) ✅
B. \( n \geq 41 \)
C. \( n \geq 58 \)
D. \( n \geq 100 \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using the Trapezoidal error bound \( |E_T| \leq \frac{(b-a)^3 K_2}{12n^2} \) with \( a=0, b=1, K_2=10 \), we need \( \frac{10}{12n^2} < 0.001 \). Solving: \( n^2 > \frac{10}{0.012} = 833.\overline{3} \), so \( n > \sqrt{833.\overline{3}} \approx 28.87 \). Since \( n \) must be an integer, \( n \geq 29 \). This direct Medium of error bounds is essential for practical numerical work where accuracy specifications drive computational resource allocation. Students must correctly manipulate inequalities and understand that bounds provide worst-case guarantees, not typical performance estimates.

Q20. Two students approximate \( \int_0^2 x^2 \, dx \) using the Trapezoidal Rule. Student A uses \( n=4 \) uniform subintervals. Student B uses \( n=4 \) subintervals but places more nodes near \( x=2 \) where the function grows faster. Which student achieves better accuracy and why?

By making subintervals smaller where curvature is high and larger where curvature is low, Student B equalizes local error contributions across the domain, reducing total error for the same number of evaluations. This adaptive strategy exploits knowledge of the integrand’s behavior to optimize efficiency. While the standard error bound assumes uniform spacing, the principle of error equidistribution guides optimal mesh design in advanced numerical practice.

Q21. A function \( f(x) \) is tabulated at equally spaced points, but every third value is corrupted by a constant additive error \( \epsilon \). How does this systematic data corruption affect the Trapezoidal Rule approximation as \( n \to \infty \)?

A. The approximation converges to \( \int f(x) \, dx + \epsilon/3 \). ✅
B. The approximation converges to \( \int f(x) \, dx + \epsilon \).
C. The approximation diverges due to accumulated errors.
D. The corruption averages out and the approximation remains accurate.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

If every third point has error \( \epsilon \), then approximately \( n/3 \) of the \( n+1 \) evaluation points are corrupted. In the Trapezoidal sum, interior points are weighted by \( h \) and endpoints by \( h/2 \). As \( n \to \infty \), the fraction of corrupted interior points approaches \( 1/3 \), and each contributes \( h \cdot \epsilon \) to the sum. The total spurious contribution approaches \( (n/3) \cdot h \cdot \epsilon = (b-a)\epsilon/3 \). Thus the limit is biased by \( \epsilon(b-a)/3 \), not \( \epsilon \) or zero. This demonstrates how structured data errors propagate through numerical algorithms in non-obvious ways.

Q22. When integrating \( \int_0^\infty e^{-x} \, dx \) numerically, one truncates to \( \int_0^L e^{-x} \, dx \) and applies the Trapezoidal Rule. If \( L \) is chosen too small, what type of error dominates, and how does it differ from discretization error?

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Improper integrals over infinite domains require two approximations: domain truncation and discretization. Truncation error \( \int_L^\infty e^{-x} dx = e^{-L} \) depends only on \( L \), not on \( n \). Discretization error depends on \( n \) but not on \( L \) (for fixed \( L \)). These errors are independent and must be balanced: choosing \( L \) too large wastes computation on negligible regions, while choosing \( L \) too small introduces irreducible bias regardless of how fine the mesh is. Understanding this decomposition is crucial for properly handling unbounded domains in computational mathematics.

Q23. A student claims that since the Trapezoidal Rule is based on linear interpolation, it cannot accurately integrate functions with significant curvature. Which response best addresses this misconception with mathematical precision?

This means accuracy improves systematically with refinement despite local linearization. The key insight is that numerical methods trade local simplicity for global convergence through subdivision. Acknowledging the local limitation while explaining the global compensation mechanism provides a nuanced correction that validates the student’s intuition about curvature while teaching proper asymptotic reasoning.

Q24. In a heat transfer simulation, temperature \( T(x) \) is computed at grid points. The total thermal energy requires \( \int T(x) \, dx \). If the simulation uses a non-uniform grid refined near boundaries, which formulation correctly computes the Trapezoidal approximation?

A. \( \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \frac{T_i + T_{i+1}}{2} (x_{i+1} - x_i) \) ✅

Options C and D represent left-endpoint and simple average rules respectively, not trapezoidal. Recognizing the correct generalization beyond textbook uniform-spacing formulas is essential for scientific computing where adaptive meshing is standard practice for resolving boundary layers and other localized phenomena efficiently.

Q25. For the integral \( \int_0^1 \frac{1}{1+x^2} \, dx \), the Trapezoidal Rule with \( n=10 \) gives 0.78498 while the exact value is \( \pi/4 \approx 0.78540 \). Error bounds use \( K_2 = \max|f''(x)| \) to guarantee the error never exceeds the stated limit for ANY function with that maximum curvature. The actual error depends on an average of \( f'' \) weighted by the kernel, not its maximum. Bounds are inherently conservative to provide safety guarantees. Understanding the gap between worst-case bounds and typical behavior prevents students from over-engineering solutions or misinterpreting bounds as predictions of actual performance.

Q30. Suppose you apply the Trapezoidal Rule to \( \int_0^1 f(x) \, dx \) and obtain approximations \( T_4 = 0.7500 \) and \( T_8 = 0.7450 \). Using Richardson extrapolation, what is the improved estimate assuming \( O(h^2) \) error?

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

Richardson extrapolation combines two approximations to eliminate the leading error term. For \( O(h^2) \) methods, the improved estimate is \( \frac{4T_{2n} - T_n}{3} \). Q34. When implementing the Trapezoidal Rule in code, a programmer writes `sum += f(x[i])` for all \( i \) then multiplies by `h`. What is the consequence of forgetting to halve the endpoint weights?

A. The result overestimates by \( \frac{h}{2}(f(a) + f(b)) \). ✅
B. The result underestimates by \( \frac{h}{2}(f(a) + f(b)) \).

D. The error vanishes as \( n \to \infty \) but is significant for finite \( n \).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The correct Trapezoidal sum is \( \frac{h}{2}f(a) + h\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(x_i) + \frac{h}{2}f(b) \). The erroneous code computes \( h\sum_{i=0}^n f(x_i) = h f(a) + h\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(x_i) + h f(b) \).
