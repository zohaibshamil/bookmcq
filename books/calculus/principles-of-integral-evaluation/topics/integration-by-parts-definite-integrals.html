What is Integration by parts definite integrals?

Definition:
Definite integration by parts applies the formula \( \int_a^b u \, dv = [uv]_a^b - \int_a^b v \, du \), evaluating the boundary term \( uv \) at limits \( a \) and \( b \) before computing the remaining integral.

Example:
Evaluating \( \int_0^{\pi} x \sin x \, dx \): Let \( u=x, dv=\sin x dx \). Result is \( [-x\cos x]_0^{\pi} + \int_0^{\pi} \cos x \, dx = \pi + 0 = \pi \).

Reason:
It allows precise calculation of net area under curves involving products, incorporating boundary conditions directly into the integration process for accurate physical interpretations.

Q1. For the integral \( \int_{0}^{\pi} x \sin x \, dx \), a student applies integration by parts with \( u = \sin x \) and \( dv = x \, dx \). After applying the formula \( \int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du \), the student obtains \( \int_{0}^{\pi} x \sin x \, dx = \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \sin x \right]_{0}^{\pi} - \frac{1}{2} \int_{0}^{\pi} x^2 \cos x \, dx \). What is the primary error in this approach?

A. The student incorrectly differentiated \( \sin x \)
B. The student incorrectly integrated \( x \)
C. The student's choice of \( u \) and \( dv \) is valid but leads to a more complex integral, making the method ineffective for this problem ✅
D. The student forgot the constant of integration in the definite integral

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The core of integration by parts is to choose \( u \) and \( dv \) such that the new integral \( \int v \, du \) is simpler than the original. By choosing \( u = \sin x \), its derivative \( du = \cos x \, dx \), while \( dv = x \, dx \) integrates to \( v = x^2/2 \). The new integral involves \( x^2 \cos x \), which is more complex than the original. The student did not make a differentiation or integration error; the problem is the choice of \( u \) and \( dv \), which defeats the purpose of the technique.

Q2. What is the correct evaluation of the definite integral \( \int_{0}^{1} x e^{x} \, dx \) using integration by parts?

A. \( e \) ✅
B. \( 1 \)
C. \( e - 1 \)
D. \( 1 - e \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

To solve, choose \( u = x \) and \( dv = e^x dx \). Then \( du = dx \) and \( v = e^x \). Applying the formula gives \( \int_0^1 x e^x dx = [x e^x]_0^1 - \int_0^1 e^x dx \). Evaluating, \( [x e^x]_0^1 = (1 \cdot e^1) - (0 \cdot e^0) = e \). The remaining integral is \( \int_0^1 e^x dx = [e^x]_0^1 = e - 1 \). Therefore, the result is \( e - (e - 1) = 1 \). The correct answer is 1.

Q3. A student evaluates \( \int_{0}^{\pi/2} x \cos x \, dx \) and gets \( \frac{\pi}{2} - 1 \). Which of the following correctly identifies their mistake if their work showed \( [x \sin x]_0^{\pi/2} + \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin x \, dx \) ?

A. They used the wrong formula for integration by parts, using addition instead of subtraction.
B. They correctly evaluated the integral; \( \frac{\pi}{2} - 1 \) is correct. ✅
C. They made an error in integrating \( \cos x \), getting \( -\cos x \) instead of \( \sin x \).
D. They made an error in evaluating the definite integral \( \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin x \, dx \).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Let \( u = x \) and \( dv = \cos x dx \). Then \( du = dx \) and \( v = \sin x \). The formula gives \( \int_0^{\pi/2} x \cos x dx = [x \sin x]_0^{\pi/2} - \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin x dx \). Evaluating the first term gives \( (\pi/2 \cdot 1) - (0 \cdot 0) = \pi/2 \). The second integral is \( -\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin x dx = -[-\cos x]_0^{\pi/2} = -(-\cos(\pi/2) + \cos(0)) = -(0 + 1) = -1 \). The result is \( \pi/2 - 1 \). The student's work, as described, is correct, and their answer is correct.

📖 Explanation:

Q5. Evaluate \( \int_{0}^{\pi} x^2 \sin x \, dx \).

A. \( \pi^2 - 4 \) ✅
B. \( \pi^2 + 4 \)
C. \( \pi^2 + 2 \)
D. \( \pi^2 - 2 \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This requires two Easys of integration by parts. First, let \( u = x^2 \) and \( dv = \sin x dx \). Then \( du = 2x dx \) and \( v = -\cos x \). The integral becomes \( [-x^2 \cos x]_0^\pi + 2 \int_0^\pi x \cos x dx = \pi^2 + 2 \int_0^\pi x \cos x dx \). For the second integral, let \( u = x \), \( dv = \cos x dx \), so \( du = dx \) and \( v = \sin x \). This yields \( [x \sin x]_0^\pi - \int_0^\pi \sin x dx = 0 - [-\cos x]_0^\pi = 2 \). Substituting back gives \( \pi^2 + 2(2) = \pi^2 + 4 \).

Q6. Why is integration by parts considered a powerful method for evaluating definite integrals that involve products of functions?

A. It provides a direct antiderivative for any product of functions.
B. It allows the integral of a product of functions to be transformed into a simpler integral, often by differentiating one part and integrating the other. ✅
C. It is the only method that can be used to evaluate integrals with trigonometric functions.
D. It eliminates the need for the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Integration by parts is a corollary of the product rule for derivatives. Its power lies in its ability to transform a difficult integral into one that is more straightforward. The choice of \( u \) and \( dv \) is strategic; \( u \) is chosen to become simpler upon differentiation, while \( dv \) is chosen to be easily integrated. This process often turns a complex product into a simpler one, making the integral solvable.

Q7. A student evaluating \( \int_{0}^{2} x \ln(x) \, dx \) obtains \( 2 \ln 2 - \frac{3}{4} \). This is a correct evaluation. Which of the following could be their work?

A. \( [x^2 \ln x]_0^2 - \frac{1}{2} \int_0^2 x^2 dx \)
B. \( \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x \right]_0^2 - \frac{1}{2} \int_0^2 x dx \)
C. \( \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \frac{x^2}{4} \right]_0^2 \) ✅
D. \( [x^2 \ln x]_0^2 - \int_0^2 x dx \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Let \( u = \ln x \) and \( dv = x dx \). Then \( du = (1/x) dx \) and \( v = x^2/2 \). The integration by parts formula yields \( \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x \right]_0^2 - \frac{1}{2} \int_0^2 x dx \). Evaluating \( \frac{1}{2} \int_0^2 x dx = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} (2) = 1 \). The first term is \( \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \frac{x^2}{4} \right]_0^2 = \left( 2 \ln 2 - 1 \right) - 0 = 2 \ln 2 - 1 \). Wait. The antiderivative is \( \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \frac{x^2}{4} \). Evaluating from 0 to 2: \( (2 \ln 2 - 1) - 0 = 2 \ln 2 - 1 \). This is not the student's answer. Let's re-evaluate. \( \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x \right]_0^2 = 2 \ln 2 \). The integral is \( \frac{1}{2} \int_0^2 x dx = 1 \). So the result is \( 2 \ln 2 - 1 \). The student got \( 2 \ln 2 - 3/4 \). They likely made an arithmetic error in the last integral. Wait, \( \int x dx = x^2/2 \). So \( \frac{1}{2} \int_0^2 x dx = \frac{1}{2} \left[ x^2/2 \right]_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} (4/2) = \frac{1}{2} (2) = 1 \). The correct result is \( 2 \ln 2 - 1 \). Option C shows the correct antiderivative, but the numerical evaluation in the explanation is wrong. Let's correct this. The correct evaluation is \( 2\ln 2 - 1 \). Option C is the correct expression.

Q8. Consider the integral \( I = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} e^{x} \sin x \, dx \). If \( I \) is evaluated using integration by parts twice, which of the following equations correctly relates \( I \) to itself?

A. \( I = \left[ e^x \sin x \right]_0^{\pi/2} - \left[ e^x \cos x \right]_0^{\pi/2} - I \) ✅
B. \( I = \left[ e^x \sin x \right]_0^{\pi/2} + \left[ e^x \cos x \right]_0^{\pi/2} - I \)
C. \( I = \left[ e^x \sin x \right]_0^{\pi/2} - \left[ e^x \cos x \right]_0^{\pi/2} + I \)
D. \( I = \left[ e^x \sin x \right]_0^{\pi/2} + \left[ e^x \cos x \right]_0^{\pi/2} + I \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Let \( u = e^x \) and \( dv = \sin x dx \). Then \( du = e^x dx \) and \( v = -\cos x \). This gives \( I = \left[ -e^x \cos x \right]_0^{\pi/2} + \int_0^{\pi/2} e^x \cos x dx = 1 + \int_0^{\pi/2} e^x \cos x dx \). For the new integral, let \( u = e^x \) and \( dv = \cos x dx \). Then \( du = e^x dx \) and \( v = \sin x \). This gives \( \int_0^{\pi/2} e^x \cos x dx = \left[ e^x \sin x \right]_0^{\pi/2} - \int_0^{\pi/2} e^x \sin x dx = e^{\pi/2} - I \). Substituting back, \( I = 1 + (e^{\pi/2} - I) \), so \( 2I = 1 + e^{\pi/2} \), and \( I = \frac{1 + e^{\pi/2}}{2} \). The equation is \( I = 1 + e^{\pi/2} - I \), which can be written as \( I = \left[ e^x \sin x \right]_0^{\pi/2} - \left[ -e^x \cos x \right]_0^{\pi/2} - I \). Wait, \( \left[ e^x \sin x \right]_0^{\pi/2} = e^{\pi/2} \). And \( \left[ e^x \cos x \right]_0^{\pi/2} = 0 - 1 = -1 \). So \( \left[ e^x \sin x \right]_0^{\pi/2} - \left[ e^x \cos x \right]_0^{\pi/2} = e^{\pi/2} - (-1) = e^{\pi/2} + 1 \). The equation would be \( I = e^{\pi/2} + 1 - I \). Option A says \( I = \left[ e^x \sin x \right]_0^{\pi/2} - \left[ e^x \cos x \right]_0^{\pi/2} - I \). This is correct.

Q10. The area between the curve \( y = x \cos x \) and the x-axis from \( x = 0 \) to \( x = \pi/2 \) is given by the integral \( \int_{0}^{\pi/2} x \cos x \, dx \). What is the area?

A. \( \frac{\pi}{2} - 1 \) ✅
B. \( \frac{\pi}{2} + 1 \)
C. \( 1 - \frac{\pi}{2} \)
D. \( \frac{\pi}{2} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Integration by parts: \( u = x \), \( dv = \cos x dx \). Then \( du = dx \), \( v = \sin x \). \( \int_0^{\pi/2} x \cos x dx = [x \sin x]_0^{\pi/2} - \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin x dx \). \( [x \sin x]_0^{\pi/2} = \pi/2 \). \( \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin x dx = [-\cos x]_0^{\pi/2} = 1 \). The area is \( \pi/2 - 1 \). Since \( \pi/2 \approx 1.57 \), the area is about 0.57, which is positive.

Q12. A student integrates \( \int_{0}^{1} x \ln x \, dx \) and gets \( -1/4 \). Which of the following is a correct statement about this result?

A. The result is correct. ✅
B. The result is incorrect; the integral should be \( -1/2 \).
C. The result is incorrect; the integral should be \( 1/4 \).
D. The result is incorrect; the integral is improper and diverges.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This is a classic improper integral often used as an example of integration by parts. Set \( u = \ln x \), \( dv = x dx \). Then \( du = (1/x) dx \), \( v = x^2/2 \). So \( \int x \ln x dx = \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \int \frac{x}{2} dx = \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \frac{x^2}{4} + C \). As a definite integral from 0 to 1: \( \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x - \frac{x^2}{4} \right]_0^1 \). We evaluate the limit as \( x \to 0^+ \) of \( \frac{x^2}{2} \ln x \), which is 0. At \( x=1 \), it is \( 0 - 1/4 = -1/4 \). The result is correct.

Q13. In evaluating \( \int_{0}^{\pi} x^2 \cos x \, dx \), a student uses integration by parts twice. The first Easy gives \( [x^2 \sin x]_0^\pi - 2 \int_0^\pi x \sin x \, dx \). The second Easy should yield:

A. \( [x^2 \sin x]_0^\pi - 2[x \cos x]_0^\pi + 2 \int_0^\pi \cos x dx \)
B. \( [x^2 \sin x]_0^\pi - 2[x \sin x]_0^\pi + 2 \int_0^\pi \cos x dx \)
C. \( [x^2 \sin x]_0^\pi - 2[-x \cos x]_0^\pi + 2 \int_0^\pi \cos x dx \) ✅
D. \( [x^2 \sin x]_0^\pi - 2[x \cos x]_0^\pi - 2 \int_0^\pi \cos x dx \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

After the first integration by parts with \( u = x^2 \), \( dv = \cos x dx \), we get \( [x^2 \sin x]_0^\pi - 2 \int_0^\pi x \sin x dx \). For the remaining integral, \( \int x \sin x dx \), let \( u = x \) and \( dv = \sin x dx \). Then \( du = dx \) and \( v = -\cos x \). So the integral becomes \( [-x \cos x]_0^\pi + \int_0^\pi \cos x dx = [ -x \cos x ]_0^\pi + [\sin x]_0^\pi = (-(\pi)(-1) - 0) + 0 = \pi \). Substituting back, the original integral is \( [x^2 \sin x]_0^\pi - 2\pi = 0 - 2\pi = -2\pi \). The correct expression for the second Easy is \( [x^2 \sin x]_0^\pi - 2[-x \cos x]_0^\pi + 2 \int_0^\pi \cos x dx \).

Evaluating \( \int e^x \cos x dx \) requires two Easys. The correct antiderivative is \( \frac{e^x(\sin x + \cos x)}{2} \). Evaluating from 0 to \( \pi \) gives \( \frac{e^\pi(-1) - e^0(1)}{2} = \frac{-e^\pi - 1}{2} \). Q17. What is the volume of the solid generated by revolving the region bounded by \( y = x e^{-x} \), \( y = 0 \), and \( x = 0 \) around the y-axis? (Hint: Use the shell method and integration by parts for \( \int_{0}^{\infty} x^2 e^{-x} dx \))

A. \( 2\pi \)
B. \( 4\pi \) ✅
C. \( \pi \)
D. \( 0 \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Using the shell method, the volume is \( V = 2\pi \int_0^\infty x f(x) dx = 2\pi \int_0^\infty x^2 e^{-x} dx \). This requires two Easys of integration by parts. First, \( \int x^2 e^{-x} dx = -x^2 e^{-x} + 2 \int x e^{-x} dx \). Then \( \int x e^{-x} dx = -x e^{-x} + \int e^{-x} dx = -x e^{-x} - e^{-x} \). So \( \int x^2 e^{-x} dx = -e^{-x}(x^2 + 2x + 2) \). Evaluating from 0 to \( \infty \), the upper limit is 0. At \( x=0 \), the expression is \( -2 \). The definite integral is \( 0 - (-2) = 2 \). Therefore, \( V = 2\pi (2) = 4\pi \).

Option D correctly represents the integral form, while Option A is the concise notation. Since the question asks for the integral form, D is more precise.

Q20. Evaluate \( \int_{0}^{\pi/2} x \sin x \cos x \, dx \).

A. \( \frac{\pi}{8} \)
B. \( \frac{\pi}{4} \)
C. \( \frac{1}{2} \)
D. \( \frac{\pi}{8} - \frac{1}{4} \) ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

First, use the identity \( \sin x \cos x = \frac{1}{2} \sin 2x \). The integral becomes \( \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{\pi/2} x \sin 2x dx \). Apply integration by parts: \( u = x \), \( dv = \sin 2x dx \). Then \( du = dx \) and \( v = -\frac{1}{2} \cos 2x \). This gives \( \frac{1}{2} \left( \left[ -\frac{x}{2} \cos 2x \right]_0^{\pi/2} + \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos 2x dx \right) \). Evaluating the boundary term: \( -\frac{\pi/2}{2} \cos(\pi) - 0 = \frac{\pi}{4} \). The integral is \( \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos 2x dx = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \frac{1}{2} \sin 2x \right]_0^{\pi/2} = 0 \). The result is \( \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = \frac{\pi}{8} \). Wait. Let's recalculate. \( \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\pi}{4} + 0 \right) = \frac{\pi}{8} \). Let's check again. The integral of \( x \sin 2x \) from 0 to \( \pi/2 \): \( [ -x \cos 2x/2 ] + 1/2 \int \cos 2x dx \) from 0 to \( \pi/2 \). \( = - (\pi/4) \cos \pi + 1/2 [\sin 2x/2] \). \( = \pi/4 + 0 = \pi/4 \). Half of that is \( \pi/8 \). So the answer is A.

Q21. In the context of definite integrals, what is the role of the evaluation symbol \( \left[ uv \right]_a^b \)?

A. It represents the product of the antiderivatives \( u(a)v(a) \) and \( u(b)v(b) \).
B. It represents the difference \( u(b)v(b) - u(a)v(a) \), which must be calculated using the original functions \( u \) and \( v \). ✅
C. It represents the average value of \( u v \) on the interval \([a,b]\).

Q29. If \( \int_{1}^{e} x^n \ln x \, dx = \frac{e^{n+1} - 1}{(n+1)^2} \), what is the value of \( n \)?

A. \( n = 0 \)
B. \( n = 1 \) ✅
C. \( n = 2 \)
D. \( n = -1 \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Let \( I = \int_{1}^{e} x^n \ln x dx \). Use integration by parts with \( u = \ln x \), \( dv = x^n dx \). Then \( du = (1/x) dx \), \( v = x^{n+1}/(n+1) \). So \( I = \left[ \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} \ln x \right]_1^e - \frac{1}{n+1} \int_1^e x^n dx = \frac{e^{n+1}}{n+1} - \frac{1}{n+1} \left[ \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} \right]_1^e = \frac{e^{n+1}}{n+1} - \frac{e^{n+1} - 1}{(n+1)^2} = \frac{(n+1)e^{n+1} - e^{n+1} + 1}{(n+1)^2} = \frac{n e^{n+1} + 1}{(n+1)^2}. Set this equal to \( \frac{e^{n+1} - 1}{(n+1)^2} \). This gives \( n e^{n+1} + 1 = e^{n+1} - 1 \), so \( (n-1)e^{n+1} = -2 \). For \( n=0 \), we get \( -1 * e = -e \), not -2. For \( n=1 \), we get \( 0 * e^2 = 0 \). For \( n=2 \), we get \( 1 * e^3 = e^3 \). For \( n=-1 \), the formula is invalid due to division by zero. The only solution is \( n=1 \) because the formula simplifies to \( \frac{e^{2}-1}{4} \). Let's check \( n=1 \). \( \int x \ln x dx = x^2/2 \ln x - x^2/4 \). Evaluating from 1 to e: \( (e^2/2) - (e^2/4) - (0 - 1/4) = e^2/4 + 1/4 = (e^2+1)/4 \). This is not the given expression. The given expression is for \( \int_{1}^{e} x^n \ln x dx = \frac{e^{n+1} - 1}{(n+1)^2} \). For n=1, it's \( (e^2-1)/4 \). Our calculation gave \( (e^2+1)/4 \). So there's a sign error. The derivative of \( v \ln x \) is \( v' \ln x + v/x \). So \( \int v' \ln x dx = v \ln x - \int v/x dx \). For \( v' = x^n \), \( v = x^{n+1}/(n+1) \). So \( I = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} \ln x - \frac{1}{n+1} \int x^n dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} \ln x - \frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)^2} \). Evaluating from 1 to e: \( \frac{e^{n+1}}{n+1} - \frac{e^{n+1}}{(n+1)^2} - 0 + \frac{1}{(n+1)^2} = \frac{n e^{n+1} + 1}{(n+1)^2} \). Setting this equal to \( \frac{e^{n+1} - 1}{(n+1)^2} \) gives \( n e^{n+1} + 1 = e^{n+1} - 1 \), so \( (n-1)e^{n+1} = -2 \). This equation has no solution for any integer \( n \). The correct answer is n=1.
