What is How to find tangent line equation?

Definition:
To find the tangent line equation at a point, calculate the derivative to get the slope at that specific x-value, then use the point-slope form \( y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \) with the known point coordinates to construct the linear equation.

Example:
For \( f(x) = x^2 \) at \( (1,1) \), slope \( m=2 \), so equation is \( y - 1 = 2(x - 1) \) or \( y = 2x - 1 \).

Reason:
This procedure combines differentiation with algebra, enabling students to approximate functions locally and solve optimization problems by identifying critical points where tangents are horizontal.

📝 All How to find tangent line equation MCQs

Q1. Given \(f(x)=x^{3}-x\) and \(x_{0}=1\), what is the slope of the tangent line at this point?

A. 0
B. 2 ✅
C. -2
D. 1

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The derivative is \(f'(x)=3x^{2}-1\). Substituting \(x=1\) gives \(f'(1)=3(1)^{2}-1=2\). Hence the slope of the tangent line at \((1,f(1))\) is 2.

Q2. For \(f(x)=x^{2}\), compare the slopes of the tangent lines at \(x=0\) and \(x=2\). Which statement is true?

A. The slopes are equal
B. Both slopes are zero
C. The slopes are different ✅
D. Both slopes are vertical

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The derivative \(f'(x)=2x\). At \(x=0\) the slope is \(0\); at \(x=2\) the slope is \(4\). Since \(0\neq4\), the slopes are different.

Q3. If \(f(x)=5x+3\), what is the equation of the tangent line at any point on the graph?

A. \(y=5x+3\) ✅
B. \(y=5x+3+x_{0}\)
C. \(y=5x\)
D. \(y=3\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A linear function’s graph coincides with its own tangent line at every point, so the tangent line equation is identical to the function: \(y=5x+3\).

Q4. For \(f(x)=\sqrt{x}\), at which \(x\)-value does the tangent line have slope \(\dfrac14\)?

A. \(x=1\)
B. \(x=4\) ✅
C. \(x=16\)
D. \(x=9\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The derivative is \(f'(x)=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}\). Setting \(\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}=\dfrac14\) gives \(\sqrt{x}=2\) and thus \(x=4\).

Q5. For \(f(x)=x^{3}-3x\), at which \(x\)-values is the tangent line horizontal?

A. \(x=0\)
B. \(x=\pm1\) ✅
C. \(x=\pm2\)
D. No such points

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A horizontal tangent occurs when \(f'(x)=0\). Here \(f'(x)=3x^{2}-3=3(x^{2}-1)\). Solving \(3(x^{2}-1)=0\) yields \(x=\pm1\).

Q6. If \(f'(x)>0\) for every \(x\) in an interval \((a,b)\), which of the following must be true about the tangent lines on that interval?

A. All slopes are positive
B. The function is increasing
C. Both A and B ✅
D. None of the above

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A positive derivative means each tangent line has a positive slope, which also indicates the function is strictly increasing on \((a,b)\). Both statements are correct.

Q7. What is the point‑slope form of a line?

A. \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\) ✅
B. \(y=mx+b\)
C. \(y=mx\)
D. \(x=mx+b\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The point‑slope form expresses a line through \((x_{1},y_{1})\) with slope \(m\) as \(y-y_{1}=m(x-x_{1})\).

Q8. Let \(g(x)=\ln x\). Find the equation of the tangent line at \(x_{0}=e\).

A. \(y-1=\frac{1}{e}(x-e)\) ✅
B. \(y-1=\frac{e}{x}(x-e)\)
C. \(y=\frac{1}{e}x\)
D. \(y=1\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of \(\ln x\) is \(1/x\); at \(x=e\) the slope is \(1/e\). The point is \((e,1)\). Using point‑slope: \(y-1=\frac{1}{e}(x-e)\).

Q9. Compare the slope of the tangent line to \(f(x)=x^{2}\) at \(x=1\) with that of \(h(x)=x^{3}\) at \(x=1\). Which is larger?

A. Slope of \(f\) is larger
B. Slope of \(h\) is larger ✅
C. The slopes are equal
D. Cannot be determined

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

\(f'(x)=2x\) gives slope \(2\) at \(x=1\). \(h'(x)=3x^{2}\) gives slope \(3\) at \(x=1\). Hence the tangent to \(x^{3}\) is steeper.

Q10. For the function \(h(x)=|x|\), does the derivative exist at \(x=0\)?

A. Yes, and equals 0
B. No, it does not exist
C. Undefined ✅
D. It is infinite

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The absolute value function has a sharp corner at \(x=0\); the left‑hand and right‑hand limits of the difference quotient differ, so the derivative is undefined there.

Q11. For \(f(x)=x^{4}-4x^{2}\), at which points does the tangent line pass through the origin?

A. \(x=0\)
B. \(x=\pm\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\) ✅
C. \(x=\pm1\)
D. No such points

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The condition \(\frac{f(x)}{x}=f'(x)\) leads to \(x^{3}-4x=4x^{3}-8x\) → \(x(3x^{2}-4)=0\). Excluding \(x=0\) (division by zero) gives \(x=\pm2/\sqrt3\).

Q12. For \(f(x)=e^{2x}\), is there a point where the tangent line has a y‑intercept of 5?

A. \(x\approx0.1\)
B. \(x\approx-0.2\)
C. \(x\approx0.3\)
D. No such point ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The y‑intercept equals \(e^{2x_{0}}(1-2x_{0})\). This expression attains a maximum below 1, so it can never reach 5; thus no such point exists.

Q13. If a differentiable function \(f\) has a tangent line at \(x=a\) that passes through \((b,f(b))\) with \(b\neq a\), which theorem does this illustrate?

A. Mean Value Theorem
B. Rolle's Theorem
C. Intermediate Value Theorem ✅
D. Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The existence of a point \(c\) between \(a\) and \(b\) where \(f'(c)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\) is precisely the statement of the Mean Value Theorem.

Q14. For \(f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x}\), find the tangent line at \(x_{0}=2\) and state where this line meets the x‑axis.

A. \(x=0\)
B. \(x=4\) ✅
C. \(x=2\)
D. \(x=-4\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

\(f'(x)=-1/x^{2}\); at \(x=2\) the slope is \(-1/4\) and the point is \((2,0.5)\). The line \(y-0.5=-\frac14(x-2)\) gives \(y=0\) when \(x=4\).

Q15. What does \(f'(x_{0})\) represent geometrically?

A. Slope of the tangent line ✅
B. Instantaneous rate of change
C. Area under the curve
D. Curvature of the graph

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative at a point gives the slope of the line that just touches the curve at that point, i.e., the tangent line.

Q16. For \(f(x)=x^{3}\), what is the equation of the tangent line at \(x_{0}=0\)?

A. \(y=0\) ✅
B. \(y=x\)
C. \(y=3x\)
D. \(y=x^{3}\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

\(f'(x)=3x^{2}\); at \(x=0\) the slope is 0 and the point is \((0,0)\). Thus the tangent line is the horizontal line \(y=0\).

Q17. The tangent line to \(y=\sin x\) at \(x=\pi/6\) is given by \(y=\frac12+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\bigl(x-\frac{\pi}{6}\bigr)\). Does this slope equal the derivative at that point?

A. true ✅
B. false
C. Cannot be determined
D. Insufficient information

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of \(\sin x\) is \(\cos x\); at \(x=\pi/6\), \(\cos(\pi/6)=\sqrt3/2\), which matches the slope in the given equation, so the statement is true.

Q18. A function has derivative \(f'(x)=\dfrac{2x}{1+x^{2}}\). Without finding \(f\), where are its tangent lines horizontal?

A. \(x=0\) ✅
B. No points
C. Where the denominator is zero
D. \(x=0\) only

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A horizontal tangent occurs when \(f'(x)=0\). The numerator \(2x\) is zero only at \(x=0\); the denominator never vanishes for real \(x\). Hence the only horizontal tangent is at \(x=0\).
