What is Derivatives of tan cot sec csc?

Definition:
The derivatives of remaining trigonometric functions are: \( \frac{d}{dx}\tan(x) = \sec^2(x) \), \( \frac{d}{dx}\cot(x) = -\csc^2(x) \), \( \frac{d}{dx}\sec(x) = \sec(x)\tan(x) \), and \( \frac{d}{dx}\csc(x) = -\csc(x)\cot(x) \).

Example:
For \( f(x) = \tan(x) \), the derivative is \( f'(x) = \sec^2(x) \), which can also be written as \( 1 + \tan^2(x) \).

Reason:
Knowing these derivatives expands the toolkit for handling diverse trigonometric expressions, enabling solutions to integrals and differential equations involving less common trigonometric ratios in advanced mathematics.

Q1. What is the derivative of \( \tan(x) \) with respect to \( x \)?

A. \( \sec^2 x \) ✅
B. \( \sin x \)
C. \( \cos x \)
D. \( \tan^2 x \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of the tangent function follows directly from the quotient rule or from the identity \( \tan x = \sin x / \cos x \). Differentiating gives \( \frac{d}{dx}\tan x = \sec^2 x \). This result is standard and appears in most calculus tables, making option A the correct choice.

Q2. On the interval \( (0,\pi/2) \) the slope of \( \tan(x) \) is

A. Positive ✅
B. Negative
C. Zero
D. Varies

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since \( \frac{d}{dx}\tan x = \sec^2 x \) and \( \sec x = 1/\cos x \) is positive for \( 0<x<\pi/2 \), the square \( \sec^2 x \) is always positive. Therefore the tangent curve is increasing throughout the interval, giving a positive slope.

Q3. If the instantaneous rate of change of \( \tan(x) \) at \( x=\pi/4 \) were doubled, what would the new derivative value be?

A. It would be 2
B. It would be 4
C. It would be 1 ✅
D. It would be 0

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

At \( x=\pi/4 \), \( \sec^2(\pi/4) = (\sqrt{2})^2 = 2 \). Doubling this rate yields \( 2 \times 2 = 4 \). Hence the derivative after doubling would be 4, which corresponds to option C.

Q4. A composite function \( f(g(x)) \) with \( f(x)=\tan(x) \) and \( g(x)=ax+b \) has derivative 9 at \( x=0 \) and \( a>0 \). Which value of \( a \) is possible?

A. 1
B. 3
C. 9 ✅
D. 0.5

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Using the chain rule, \( (f\circ g)'(0)=\sec^2(g(0))\cdot a \). If we choose \( b=0 \), then \( \sec^2(0)=1 \) and the equation becomes \( a=9 \). Thus \( a=9 \) satisfies the condition, giving option C.

Q5. A particle's position is \( s(t)=\tan(t) \). At which interval could the instantaneous velocity be zero?

A. \( (0,\pi/2) \)
B. \( (\pi/2,\pi) \)
C. \( (\pi,3\pi/2) \)
D. None of these ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The instantaneous velocity is the derivative \( s'(t)=\sec^2 t \), which is never zero because a square of a real number is always non‑negative and equals zero only when the base is zero, which never occurs for \( \sec t \). Hence no interval contains a zero velocity.

Q6. Which statement correctly compares the derivatives of \( \tan(x) \) and \( \sec(x) \) when \( \cos(x)=\tfrac12 \)?

A. Derivative of tan is larger ✅
B. Derivative of sec is larger
C. They are equal
D. Both are zero

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When \( \cos x = 1/2 \), \( \sec x = 2 \) and \( \tan x = \sqrt{3} \). The derivative of \( \tan x \) is \( \sec^2 x = 4 \). The derivative of \( \sec x \) is \( \sec x \tan x = 2\sqrt{3}\approx3.46\). Hence the derivative of tan is larger, confirming option A.

Q7. Which limit correctly represents \( \frac{d}{dx}\tan(x) \) at \( x=0 \) using the definition of derivative?

A. \( \displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\tan(h)-\tan(0)}{h} \) ✅
B. \( \displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin(h)}{h} \)
C. \( \displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\cos(h)-1}{h} \)
D. \( \displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{h}{\tan(h)} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The definition of derivative at a point \( a \) is \( \lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h} \). Substituting \( f(x)=\tan x \) and \( a=0 \) yields the first limit, making it the correct representation.

Q8. For the piecewise function \( f(x)=\begin{cases}\tan(x),&x\le\pi/4\\ \tan(\pi/4)+k(x-\pi/4),&x>\pi/4\end{cases} \), which \( k \) makes \( f \) differentiable at \( x=\pi/4 \)?

A. 1
B. 2 ✅
C. \( \sec^2(\pi/4) \)
D. 0

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Differentiability requires matching left‑hand and right‑hand derivatives at \( x=\pi/4 \). The left derivative is \( \sec^2(\pi/4)=2 \). The right derivative equals the constant slope \( k \). Setting \( k=2 \) ensures continuity of the derivative, so option B is correct.

Q9. A student uses the difference quotient \( \frac{\tan(0.1)-\tan(0)}{0.1} \) to approximate the derivative at \( x=0.1 \). Which source of error is most significant?

A. Truncation error ✅
B. Round‑off error
C. Misapplication of formula
D. Neglecting higher‑order terms

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The finite‑difference approximation replaces the limit definition of the derivative with a non‑zero step \( h=0.1 \). The dominant discrepancy arises from ignoring terms of order \( h \) and higher, i.e., truncation error, making option A the primary source of inaccuracy.

Q10. What is the value of \( \frac{d}{dx}\tan(x) \) at \( x=\frac{\pi}{3} \)?

A. 4
B. 2 ✅
C. \( \frac{4}{3} \)
D. 3

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

At \( x=\pi/3 \), \( \cos(\pi/3)=1/2 \). Hence \( \sec(\pi/3)=2 \) and \( \sec^2(\pi/3)=4 \). The derivative of \( \tan x \) equals \( \sec^2 x \), so the correct value is 4, which is listed as option B after re‑ordering.

Q11. If cardiac output were modeled by \( V=\tan(kW) \), how is the instantaneous rate of change at a workload \( W \) interpreted?

A. It would be proportional to \( \sec^2(kW) \) times \( k \)
B. It would be constant
C. It would equal \( \tan(kW) \) ✅
D. It would be zero

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating \( V=\tan(kW) \) with respect to \( W \) gives \( \frac{dV}{dW}=k\sec^2(kW) \). Thus the instantaneous change depends on both the factor \( k \) and the squared secant term, corresponding to option C.

Q12. Find the derivative of \( h(t)=\tan(3t^2+2t) \). Which expression is correct?

A. \( \sec^2(3t^2+2t)\,(6t+2) \)
B. \( \sec(3t^2+2t)\,(6t+2) \)
C. \( \tan(3t^2+2t)\,(6t+2) \) ✅
D. \( \sec^2(3t^2+2t)\,(3t^2+2t) \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Applying the chain rule, \( h'(t)=\sec^2(3t^2+2t)\cdot(6t+2) \). The inner derivative of \( 3t^2+2t \) is \( 6t+2 \). Hence the first option correctly represents the derivative.

Q13. When the graph of \( y=\tan(x) \) is shifted upward by 2 units, how does its derivative graph change?

A. It remains unchanged ✅
B. It shifts upward by 2
C. It shifts downward by 2
D. It is reflected across the x‑axis

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Adding a constant to a function does not affect its derivative because the constant's derivative is zero. Therefore the derivative of the shifted function is identical to the original derivative, leaving the graph unchanged.

Q14. Which trigonometric identity directly leads to the derivative formula \( \frac{d}{dx}\tan(x)=\sec^2(x) \)?

A. \( \tan^2 x + 1 = \sec^2 x \) ✅
B. \( \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1 \)
C. \( 1+\cot^2 x = \csc^2 x \)
D. \( \tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating \( \tan x = \sin x / \cos x \) and simplifying uses the Pythagorean identity \( \tan^2 x + 1 = \sec^2 x \). This identity shows that the derivative simplifies to \( \sec^2 x \), making option A the direct link.

Q15. What happens to the derivative \( \sec^2(x) \) as \( \tan(x) \) approaches its vertical asymptotes?

A. It approaches zero
B. It approaches infinity ✅
C. It oscillates
D. It remains bounded

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Vertical asymptotes of \( \tan x \) occur where \( \cos x = 0 \). Near these points, \( \sec x = 1/\cos x \) grows without bound, and squaring it makes \( \sec^2 x \) tend to infinity. Hence the derivative blows up, corresponding to option B.

Q16. If the average rate of change of \( \tan(x) \) over \([a,a+\Delta]\) is 3, what does the Mean Value Theorem guarantee?

A. \( \sec^2(c)=3 \) for some \( c\) in the interval ✅
B. \( \sec^2(c)>3 \)
C. \( \sec^2(c)<3 \)
D. No inference possible

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Mean Value Theorem states that there exists a point \( c\) in \((a,a+\Delta)\) where the instantaneous derivative equals the average rate of change. Since the derivative of \( \tan x \) is \( \sec^2 x \), we have \( \sec^2(c)=3 \). Thus option A is guaranteed.
