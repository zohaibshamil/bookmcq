What is Derivative definition first principle calculus?

Definition:
The derivative defined by first principles uses the limit definition \( f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} \) to calculate the instantaneous rate of change, establishing the rigorous mathematical foundation for differential calculus operations.

Example:
For \( f(x) = x^2 \), \( f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(x+h)^2 - x^2}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} (2x+h) = 2x \).

Reason:
This method ensures conceptual clarity by deriving rules from basics rather than memorization, helping students understand why derivative formulas work and building strong analytical reasoning skills.

Q1. According to the definition, how is the derivative \(f'(x)\) expressed as a limit?

A.\(f'(x)=\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\) ✅
B.\(f'(x)=\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x)-f(x-h)}{h}\)
C.\(f'(x)=\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x)-f(x+h)}{h}\)
D.\(f'(x)=\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)+f(x)}{h}\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The definition of the derivative states that the instantaneous rate of change at a point is the limit of the difference quotient as \(h\) approaches zero. Option A matches this definition exactly, while the other options alter the numerator or sign, resulting in incorrect expressions. Hence, A is the correct choice.

Q2. What is the name given to the expression \(\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\) in the definition of the derivative?

A.Difference quotient ✅
B.Derivative limit
C.Instantaneous slope
D.Tangent ratio
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The term \(\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\) is traditionally called the difference quotient because it measures the average rate of change over an interval of width \(h\). It is the central building block for forming the derivative via a limit. The other names are not standard terminology for this expression.

Q3. If a function \(f\) is differentiable at \(x=1\) and \(f'(1)=0\), which of the following must be true about the graph of \(f\) near \(x=1\)?

A.The tangent line is horizontal at \(x=1\). ✅
B.The function attains a maximum at \(x=1\).
C.The function is constant on an interval containing \(x=1\).
D.The graph must be symmetric about \(x=1\).
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A zero derivative indicates that the slope of the tangent line at that point is zero, meaning the tangent line is horizontal. This does not guarantee a maximum, constancy, or symmetry; those require additional information about the second derivative or the function’s behavior. Therefore, only statement A is guaranteed.

Q4. Suppose \(g(x)=\sqrt{x}\). Using the definition of derivative, which of the following statements correctly explains why \(g'(4)=\frac{1}{4}\)?

A.The limit \(\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sqrt{4+h}-2}{h}\) simplifies to \(\frac{1}{4}\).
B.The limit \(\lim_{h\to0}\frac{4+h-4}{h}\) gives \(\frac{1}{4}\). ✅
C.The derivative of \(\sqrt{x}\) is always \(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}\), evaluated at \(x=4\).
D.The function \(\sqrt{x}\) is linear near \(x=4\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Evaluating the limit directly shows \(\frac{\sqrt{4+h}-2}{h}=\frac{h}{(\sqrt{4+h}+2)h}\) which approaches \(\frac{1}{4}\) as \(h\to0\). Option B misapplies the definition, C uses a known formula without justification, and D incorrectly claims linearity. Hence, A provides the correct logical reasoning.

Q5. If a function \(f\) is differentiable on an interval and its derivative \(f'(x)\) is always positive, what can be inferred about the original function \(f\) on that interval?

A.\(f\) is strictly increasing. ✅
B.\(f\) has a global maximum.
C.\(f\) is constant.
D.\(f\) is concave upward.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A positive derivative at every point means the slope of the tangent line never falls below zero, implying the function rises as \(x\) increases. This guarantees strict monotonic increase. It does not imply a maximum, constancy, or concavity, which depend on the sign of the second derivative or other conditions. Therefore, statement A is correct.

Q6. Consider two functions \(f\) and \(g\) with \(f'(x)=g'(x)\) for all \(x\) in their common domain. Which conclusion is logically valid?

A.\(f(x)=g(x)+C\) for some constant \(C\). ✅
B.\(f\) and \(g\) are identical functions.
C.\(f\) and \(g\) have the same curvature.
D.\(f\) and \(g\) intersect at exactly one point.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: If the derivatives of two functions are identical everywhere, their difference \(h(x)=f(x)-g(x)\) has derivative zero, meaning \(h\) is constant. Hence \(f(x)=g(x)+C\). Equality of functions, curvature, or intersection count cannot be deduced without additional information. Option A captures the valid inference.

Q7. A function \(p\) satisfies \(p'(x)=2p(x)\) for every \(x\). Which of the following must be true about the shape of its graph?

A.Its graph is an exponential curve. ✅
B.Its graph is a straight line through the origin.
C.Its graph is a parabola.
D.Its graph is periodic.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The differential equation \(p'(x)=2p(x)\) solves to \(p(x)=Ce^{2x}\), an exponential function whose rate of change is proportional to its current value. This produces a characteristic exponential growth shape, not a line, parabola, or periodic pattern. Therefore, the graph must be exponential.

📖 Explanation: The definition requires evaluating the limit of the difference quotient and setting it to zero to locate points with zero slope. While the simplified derivative \(3x^2-3\) yields the same result, the question explicitly asks for a method using the limit definition, making option A the correct logical approach.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The definition approach requires forming \(\frac{\ln((x+h)^2+1)-\ln(x^2+1)}{h}\), using logarithmic properties to combine terms, rationalizing, and then evaluating the limit, which yields \(\frac{2x}{x^2+1}\). Options B, C, and D invoke rules without the limit justification required by the question.

📖 Explanation: The term “slope-producing” emphasizes that the value of \(f'(x)\) at a particular \(x\) is precisely the slope of the tangent line to the curve \(y=f(x)\) at that abscissa. While the derivative also reflects growth rate, the specific geometric interpretation highlighted by the phrase is the tangent slope, making option A correct.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

Hence differentiability guarantees continuity, validating the student's claim. The other options misstate the relationship.

Q14. Consider the function \(k(x)=|x|\). Which of the following correctly uses the definition of derivative to explain why \(k\) is not differentiable at \(x=0\)?

A.The left-hand limit of the difference quotient equals \(-1\) while the right-hand limit equals \(1\). ✅
B.The derivative of \(|x|\) is \(\frac{x}{|x|}\) everywhere.
C.The function is not continuous at \(0\).
D.The graph has a cusp at \(0\).
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Applying the definition, the limit from the left yields \(\lim_{h\to0^-}\frac{|h|-0}{h}=-1\) and from the right yields \(\lim_{h\to0^+}\frac{|h|-0}{h}=1\). Since the two one-sided limits differ, the overall limit does not exist, so \(k\) is not differentiable at \(0\). The other statements either misapply the derivative or address unrelated properties.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The condition \(m'(x)=m(x)\) is a first‑order linear differential equation whose unique solution, given the initial value \(m(0)=3\), is the exponential function \(m(x)=3e^{x}\). This conceptual insight bypasses direct limit computation. The other options misuse calculus rules or ignore the exponential nature.

While the other statements are true, option A directly addresses the failure of the limit definition, providing the correct logical explanation.

Q19. If a function \(s\) satisfies \(s'(x)=\cos(x)\) and \(s(0)=5\), which logical deduction correctly determines \(s\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\)?

A.\(s\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=5+\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos(t)\,dt=5+1\). ✅
B.\(s\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=5+\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\).
C.\(s\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=5+\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\).
D.\(s\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=5\).
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Since \(s'(x)=\cos(x)\), integrating from \(0\) to \(\pi/2\) gives the change in \(s\): \(\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos(t)dt = \sin(\pi/2)-\sin(0)=1\). Adding this to the initial value \(5\) yields \(s(\pi/2)=6\). Option A correctly applies the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, while the others misuse the antiderivative or neglect the integral.

Q20. A function \(r\) is defined by \(r(x)=x^4\). Which of the following correctly uses the definition of derivative to show that the tangent line at \(x=1\) has slope \(4\)?

A.Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{(1+h)^4-1}{h}=4\). ✅
B.Apply the power rule to obtain \(r'(x)=4x^3\) and substitute \(x=1\).
C.Differentiate \(x^4\) using the product rule.
D.Assume the slope is the coefficient of the highest-degree term.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Using the limit definition, \(\frac{(1+h)^4-1}{h} = \frac{4h+6h^2+4h^3+h^4}{h}=4+6h+4h^2+h^3\). As \(h\to0\), the expression approaches \(4\), confirming the tangent slope at \(x=1\). The other options rely on rules rather than the explicit limit process requested.
