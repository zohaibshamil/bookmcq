Q1. Which principle allows moving a constant through a derivative sign?

🔹 A. Constant Multiple Rule
🔹 B. Power Rule
🔹 C. Product Rule
🔹 D. Quotient Rule

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The rule that permits moving a constant through the derivative sign is precisely the Constant Multiple Rule. It states that for any constant \(c\) and differentiable function \(f\), the derivative of \(c\,f(x)\) equals \(c\,f'(x)\). This principle is the foundation for all subsequent examples.

Q2. Compare the derivative of \(5x^{2}\) with the derivative of \(x^{2}\).

🔹 A. \(5x\)
🔹 B. \(10x\)
🔹 C. \(2x\)
🔹 D. \(5(2x)\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

For \(f(x)=x^{2}\) the derivative is \(2x\). Multiplying the original function by 5 yields \(5x^{2}\); applying the Constant Multiple Rule gives derivative \(5\cdot2x =10x\). Hence the derivative of \(5x^{2}\) is exactly five times the derivative of \(x^{2}\).

Q3. If \(g(x)= -2h(x)\) and \(h'(2)=4\), what is \(g'(2)\)?

🔹 A. -4
🔹 B. 8
🔹 C. -8
🔹 D. 4

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Given \(g(x) = -2h(x)\) and \(h'(2)=4\), the Constant Multiple Rule tells us to multiply the known derivative by the constant factor: \(g'(2)= -2 \times 4 = -8\). The sign and magnitude are directly inherited from the constant multiplier.

Q4. What is the derivative of a constant function \(c\)?

🔹 A. 0
🔹 B. \(c\)
🔹 C. Undefined
🔹 D. \(c'\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A constant function \(c\) does not change with \(x\); its rate of change is zero. Therefore \(\frac{d}{dx}[c]=0\) regardless of the value of \(c\). This follows directly from the definition of derivative as a limit of a zero difference.

Q5. Given \(f(x)=\sin x\), what is \(\frac{d}{dx}[7f(x)]\)?

🔹 A. \(7\sin x\)
🔹 B. \(\cos x\)
🔹 C. \(7\)
🔹 D. \(\displaystyle 7\cos x\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Since \(\sin x\) differentiates to \(\cos x\), the function \(7\sin x\) has derivative \(7\cos x\) by the Constant Multiple Rule. The constant 7 remains outside the differentiation, and only the inner function \(\sin x\) contributes its derivative.

Q6. If the derivative of \(4p(x)\) at \(x=2\) is 96, what is \(p'(2)\)?

🔹 A. 12
🔹 B. 24
🔹 C. 48
🔹 D. 96

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of \(4p(x)\) equals \(4p'(x)\). If at \(x=2\) this derivative equals 96, then \(4p'(2)=96\) implying \(p'(2)=96/4=24\). This logical step isolates the unknown \(p'(2)\) using the constant factor.

Q7. Which function's derivative cannot be obtained solely by the Constant Multiple Rule?

🔹 A. \(3x^{2}\)
🔹 B. \(5\sin x\)
🔹 C. \(\displaystyle e^{2x}\)
🔹 D. \(4\ln x\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Constant Multiple Rule applies only when the constant multiplies the entire function. For \(e^{2x}\) the exponent contains the variable, so differentiating requires the Chain Rule, not just the constant factor rule. Hence this function cannot be handled solely by the constant multiple rule.

Q8. Compare the derivative of \(c\ln x\) with that of \(\ln(x^{c})\).

🔹 A. They are identical
🔹 B. First is larger
🔹 C. Second is larger
🔹 D. Cannot compare

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using the rule, the derivative of \(c\ln x\) is \(c\cdot\frac{1}{x}= \frac{c}{x}\). For \(\ln(x^{c})\), rewrite as \(c\ln x\) before differentiating, which yields the same result \(\frac{c}{x}\). Thus both derivatives are identical.

Q9. If \(q(x)=k\,f(x)\) with \(q'(a)=12\) and \(f'(a)=3\), what is \(k\)?

🔹 A. 2
🔹 B. 3
🔹 C. 6
🔹 D. 4

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

From \(q(x)=k f(x)\) we have \(q'(a)=k f'(a)\). Substituting the given numbers gives \(12 = k\cdot3\), so solving for \(k\) yields \(k=4\). The constant factor is directly determined by dividing the known derivative values.

Q10. Why does the Constant Multiple Rule hold for negative or irrational constants?

🔹 A. Because limits are linear
🔹 B. Because a constant can be factored out of any limit (correct)
🔹 C. Because derivative of constant is zero
🔹 D. Because of the product rule

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The rule holds for any real constant, positive, negative, or irrational, because the limit definition treats the constant as a scalar that can be factored out of the difference quotient. Whether the constant is \(-\pi\) or \(\sqrt{2}\) does not affect the algebraic step of moving it through the limit.

Q11. What is \(\frac{d}{dx}\bigl[\frac{1}{5}x^{5}\bigr]\)?

🔹 A. \(x^{4}\)
🔹 B. \(\frac{1}{5}5x^{4}\)
🔹 C. \(x^{5}\)
🔹 D. \(\frac{1}{5}x^{4}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

First rewrite \(\frac{1}{5}x^{5}\) as \(\frac{1}{5}\cdot x^{5}\). Applying the Constant Multiple Rule gives derivative \(\frac{1}{5}\cdot5x^{4}=x^{4}\). The intermediate option \(\frac{1}{5}5x^{4}\) simplifies to the same result, confirming the correct choice.

Q12. Given \(f'(x)=2f(x)\), what is \(\frac{d}{dx}[3f(x)]\)?

🔹 A. \(6f(x)\)
🔹 B. \(3f(x)\)
🔹 C. \(2f(x)\)
🔹 D. \(9f(x)\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Given \(f'(x)=2f(x)\), the derivative of \(3f(x)\) is \(3f'(x)=3\cdot2f(x)=6f(x)\) by the Constant Multiple Rule. This shows that the derivative of the scaled function is six times the original function value, preserving the proportional relationship.

Q13. For \(u(x)=\sqrt{2}\,(x^{3}-4x)\), what is \(u''(x)\)?

🔹 A. \(12\sqrt{2}x\)
🔹 B. \(3\sqrt{2}x\)
🔹 C. \(\sqrt{2}x\)
🔹 D. \displaystyle 6\sqrt{2}x

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

For \(u(x)=\sqrt{2}\,(x^{3}-4x)\), the first derivative is \(\sqrt{2}(3x^{2}-4)\). Differentiating again, the constant \(\sqrt{2}\) stays outside, and the derivative of \(3x^{2}-4\) is \(6x\). Hence \(u''(x)=\sqrt{2}\cdot6x=6\sqrt{2}x\).

Q14. A student claims the derivative of \(2\sin x\) is the constant alone. Which statement identifies the error?

🔹 A. They omitted the derivative of \(f\)
🔹 B. They treated the constant as the derivative (correct)
🔹 C. They applied the product rule
🔹 D. They used the chain rule unnecessarily

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The student's claim ignores the derivative of the inner function; the constant multiple rule multiplies the constant by the derivative of \(f(x)\), not by the function itself. For \(2\sin x\), the correct derivative is \(2\cos x\). The error lies in assuming the derivative equals the constant alone.
