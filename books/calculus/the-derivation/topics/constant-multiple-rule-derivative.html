What is Constant multiple rule derivative?

Definition:
The constant multiple rule states that the derivative of a constant times a function equals the constant times the derivative of the function, expressed as ddx[cf(x)]=cf′(x) \frac{d}{dx}[cf(x)] = c f'(x) dxd​[cf(x)]=cf′(x), allowing constants to be factored out during differentiation.

Example:
For f(x)=4x3 f(x) = 4x^3 f(x)=4x3, the derivative is f′(x)=4⋅3x2=12x2 f'(x) = 4 \cdot 3x^2 = 12x^2 f′(x)=4⋅3x2=12x2, keeping the constant multiplier intact.

Reason:
This property simplifies calculations by separating scalar coefficients from variable parts, reducing complexity when differentiating scaled functions and maintaining accuracy in multi-term expressions.
