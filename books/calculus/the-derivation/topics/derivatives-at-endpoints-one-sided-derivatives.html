What is Derivatives at endpoints one sided derivatives?

Definition:
At endpoints of a closed interval, derivatives are defined using one-sided limits, where the right-hand derivative applies at the left endpoint and the left-hand derivative applies at the right endpoint, since the function is not defined beyond these boundaries.

Example:
For f(x)=xf(x) = \sqrt{x}f(x)=x​ on [0,1][0,1][0,1], the right-hand derivative at x=0x=0x=0 is lim⁡h→0+hh=∞\lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{\sqrt{h}}{h} = \inftylimh→0+​hh​​=∞, so it is not differentiable at zero.

Reason:
Understanding one-sided derivatives is essential for analyzing functions on restricted domains, ensuring correct application of theorems like Rolle's Theorem and Mean Value Theorem which require differentiability on open intervals.
