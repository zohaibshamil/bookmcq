What is Chain rule for derivatives?

Definition:
The chain rule states that the derivative of a composite function f(g(x))f(g(x))f(g(x)) is the derivative of the outer function evaluated at the inner function times the derivative of the inner function, expressed as ddxf(g(x))=f′(g(x))g′(x)\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))g'(x)dxd​f(g(x))=f′(g(x))g′(x).

Example:
For f(x)=(x2+1)3f(x) = (x^2 + 1)^3f(x)=(x2+1)3, let u=x2+1u = x^2 + 1u=x2+1, then f′(x)=3u2⋅2x=6x(x2+1)2f'(x) = 3u^2 \cdot 2x = 6x(x^2 + 1)^2f′(x)=3u2⋅2x=6x(x2+1)2.

Reason:
The chain rule is indispensable for differentiating nested functions, which are ubiquitous in real-world models, allowing breakdown of complex compositions into manageable differentiation steps.
