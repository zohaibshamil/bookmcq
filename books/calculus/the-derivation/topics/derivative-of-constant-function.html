What is Derivative of constant function?

Definition:
The derivative of a constant function is always zero because a constant does not change with respect to the variable, meaning its rate of change is nonexistent, resulting in a horizontal line with zero slope across the entire domain.

Example:
For \( f(x) = 7 \), the derivative is \( f'(x) = 0 \) since there is no variation in output regardless of input changes.

Reason:
This basic rule simplifies differentiation of polynomial terms and serves as a foundation for understanding that only varying components contribute to rates of change in composite functions.

Q1. What is \(\frac{d}{dx}[7]\)?

A. 0 ✅
B. 7
C. 1
D. undefined

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of any constant is zero because the definition \(\frac{d}{dx}[c]=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{c-c}{h}\) gives \(\lim_{h\to0}0=0\). Thus \(\frac{d}{dx}[7]=0\), reflecting that a horizontal line has no steepness.

Q3. Suppose \(g(x)=c\) and \(h(x)=3g(x)+5\). What is \(h'(x)\)?

A. 0 ✅
B. 3
C. 5
D. 15

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

First compute \(h(x)=3c+5\), which is still a constant because \(c\) does not depend on \(x\). The derivative of any constant is zero, so \(h'(x)=0\). Options involving 3,5, or 15 would arise only if the expression contained \(x\).

Q4. Which statement correctly compares the derivative of \(f(x)=c\) and the derivative of \(p(x)=mx+b\) (with \(m\neq0\))?

A. Both are zero
B. The derivative of \(f\) is zero while that of \(p\) equals \(m\) ✅
C. Both equal \(m\)
D. The derivative of \(f\) equals \(m\) while that of \(p\) is zero

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

For a constant function \(f(x)=c\), the derivative is always zero. For a linear function \(p(x)=mx+b\), the derivative is the constant coefficient \(m\). Hence the correct comparison is that \(f'\) is zero and \(p'\) equals \(m\).

Q7. Which principle explains why the derivative of any constant function is zero?

A. Mean Value Theorem
B. Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
C. Definition of derivative as limit of difference quotient ✅
D. Chain Rule

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The definition of the derivative as \(\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\) directly yields zero when \(f(x)=c\) because the numerator is always \(c-c=0\). This limit‑based reasoning is the fundamental principle behind the result.
