What is Power rule for derivatives?

Definition:
The power rule states that for any real number n n n, the derivative of xn x^n xn is nxn−1 nx^{n-1} nxn−1, providing a straightforward method to differentiate polynomial terms by multiplying by the exponent and reducing the power by one.

Example:
For f(x)=x5 f(x) = x^5 f(x)=x5, applying the power rule gives f′(x)=5x5−1=5x4 f'(x) = 5x^{5-1} = 5x^4 f′(x)=5x5−1=5x4 directly without using limit definitions.

Reason:
This rule dramatically speeds up differentiation of polynomials, making it efficient to handle higher-degree terms and forming the basis for differentiating more complex algebraic expressions in calculus.
