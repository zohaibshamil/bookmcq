Q1. Which of the following symbols correctly denotes the fourth derivative of a function \(f\) with respect to \(x\)?

🔹 A. \(f^{(4)}(x)\)
🔹 B. \(f^{(3)}(x)\)
🔹 C. \(\frac{d^{4}}{dx^{4}}f(x)\)
🔹 D. Both A and C

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The fourth derivative can be written either as the superscript notation \(f^{(4)}(x)\) or using the differential operator \(\frac{d^{4}}{dx^{4}}f(x)\). Both expressions represent the same mathematical object, so the answer that acknowledges both forms (option D) is correct. The other choices either refer to a different order of derivative or omit the differential operator entirely.

Q2. If a function satisfies \(f''(x)=0\) for every real \(x\), what can be concluded about the general form of \(f(x)\)?

🔹 A. \(f(x)\) is a linear function
🔹 B. \(f(x)\) is a constant function
🔹 C. \(f(x)\) is a quadratic function
🔹 D. \(f(x)\) is a cubic function

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When the second derivative is identically zero, the first derivative must be a constant, because differentiating a constant yields zero. Integrating that constant once gives a linear function, and a second integration adds another constant term. Hence the original function must be of the form \(f(x)=mx+b\), a linear function.

Q3. Given \(f(x)=3x^{4}-2x^{3}+x^{2}-4x+2\), what is the second derivative \(f''(x)\)?

🔹 A. \(12x^{2}-6x+2\)
🔹 B. \(24x^{2}-8x+2\)
🔹 C. \(36x^{2}-12x+2\)
🔹 D. \(48x^{2}-16x+2\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

First differentiate: \(f'(x)=12x^{3}-6x^{2}+2x-4\). Differentiating again gives \(f''(x)=36x^{2}-12x+2\). The other options either have incorrect coefficients or miss the factor of three that arises from differentiating a fourth‑degree term twice. Thus option C matches the correct computation.

Q4. Which statement accurately describes the relationship between the order of a derivative and the number of differentiations performed?

🔹 A. The order is always one less than the number of differentiations
🔹 B. The order equals the number of differentiations performed
🔹 C. The order is twice the number of differentiations
🔹 D. The order is unrelated to the number of differentiations

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

By definition, taking the first derivative yields a first‑order derivative, the second derivative is second‑order, and so on. Each differentiation increments the order by exactly one, so the order of the derivative is identical to the count of differentiations applied. This direct correspondence makes option B the correct description.

Q5. What is meant by the term “higher‑order derivative’’ in calculus?

🔹 A. The derivative of a derivative taken more than once
🔹 B. Any derivative after the first derivative
🔹 C. The derivative evaluated at a higher point
🔹 D. A derivative that involves higher powers of \(x\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A higher‑order derivative refers to the derivative obtained after differentiating the original function multiple times, beyond the first derivative. It specifically denotes the nth derivative where \(n\ge 2\). Option C captures this idea by stating it is the derivative evaluated at a higher point, which aligns with the standard definition of higher‑order derivatives.

Q6. For \(g(x)=e^{kx}\) with constant \(k\), which expression correctly gives the \(n\)th derivative \(g^{(n)}(x)\)?

🔹 A. \(g^{(n)}(x)=k^{n}e^{kx}\)
🔹 B. \(g^{(n)}(x)=ke^{kx}\)
🔹 C. \(g^{(n)}(x)=e^{kx}\)
🔹 D. \(g^{(n)}(x)=nke^{kx}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating \(e^{kx}\) repeatedly multiplies by the constant factor \(k\) each time. After the first derivative we have \(k e^{kx}\); after the second derivative \(k^{2} e^{kx}\); continuing this pattern yields \(g^{(n)}(x)=k^{n}e^{kx}\). The other options either omit the exponentiation of \(k\) or introduce extraneous factors, making option A the accurate representation.

Q7. Compare the second derivatives of \(f(x)=x^{3}\) and \(h(x)=\sin x\) at \(x=0\). Which statement is true?

🔹 A. \(f''(0)=0\) and \(h''(0)=1\)
🔹 B. \(f''(0)=1\) and \(h''(0)=0\)
🔹 C. Both second derivatives are non‑zero
🔹 D. Both second derivatives are zero

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The second derivative of \(f(x)=x^{3}\) is \(f''(x)=6x\), which evaluates to \(0\) at \(x=0\). For \(h(x)=\sin x\), the second derivative is \(h''(x)=-\sin x\), also giving \(0\) at \(x=0\). Hence both second derivatives vanish at the origin, confirming option D as the correct comparison.

Q8. Consider \(p(x)=x^{4}-4x^{3}+6x^{2}\). Does this function have any inflection points?

🔹 A. \(x=0\) is an inflection point
🔹 B. \(x=1\) is an inflection point
🔹 C. No inflection points exist
🔹 D. Both \(x=0\) and \(x=1\) are inflection points

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

An inflection point requires a sign change in the second derivative. For \(p(x)\) we find \(p''(x)=12(x-1)^{2}\), which is always non‑negative and equals zero only at \(x=1\). However, the sign does not change around \(x=1\) because the expression is a perfect square. Therefore the curve has no inflection points, making option C correct.

Q9. If the fifth derivative of a function is identically zero, what is the highest possible degree of its polynomial representation?

🔹 A. 4
🔹 B. 5
🔹 C. 6
🔹 D. 7

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Each differentiation reduces the degree of a polynomial by one. If the fifth derivative vanishes everywhere, the original polynomial must have degree at most four; a degree‑four polynomial becomes zero after five differentiations. Degrees higher than four would produce a non‑zero fifth derivative. Hence the maximum possible degree is four, corresponding to option A.

Q10. Which expression correctly represents the third derivative of \(f(x)=\ln x\)?

🔹 A. \(-\frac{2}{x^{3}}\)
🔹 B. \(\frac{2}{x^{3}}\)
🔹 C. \(-\frac{1}{x^{3}}\)
🔹 D. \(\frac{1}{x^{3}}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Starting with \(f'(x)=1/x\), differentiate to obtain \(f''(x)=-1/x^{2}\). Differentiating once more yields \(f'''(x)=2/x^{3}\). The sign is positive because the derivative of \(-1/x^{2}\) introduces a factor of \(-2\) that cancels the existing negative sign. Thus the correct third derivative is \(2/x^{3}\), which matches option B.

Q11. Suppose a function satisfies \(f''(x)=-f(x)\) for all real \(x\). Which of the following could be a valid form of \(f(x)\)?

🔹 A. \(f(x)=\sin x\)
🔹 B. \(f(x)=e^{x}\)
🔹 C. \(f(x)=\cosh x\)
🔹 D. \(f(x)=e^{-x}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The differential equation \(f''=-f\) has characteristic roots \(\pm i\), leading to solutions that are linear combinations of \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\). Among the options, \(\sin x\) directly satisfies the equation because its second derivative is \(-\sin x\). Exponential and hyperbolic functions do not fulfill the required relationship, so option A is correct.

Q12. For \(h(x)=x^{2}e^{x}\), what is the third derivative \(h^{(3)}(x)\)?

🔹 A. \(e^{x}(x^{2}+4x+2)\)
🔹 B. \(e^{x}(x^{2}+3x+1)\)
🔹 C. \(e^{x}(x^{2}+5x+3)\)
🔹 D. \(e^{x}(x^{2}+6x+6)\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Applying the product rule repeatedly: \(h' = e^{x}(x^{2}+2x)\); \(h'' = e^{x}(x^{2}+4x+2)\); \(h''' = e^{x}(x^{2}+6x+6)\). Each differentiation adds a term from the derivative of the exponential and from differentiating the polynomial factor. The final expression matches option D.

Q13. In the Taylor series of a function \(f\) about a point \(a\), the coefficient of \((x-a)^{n}\) involves which derivative of \(f\)?

🔹 A. \(f^{(n)}(a)\)
🔹 B. \(f^{(n-1)}(a)\)
🔹 C. \(f^{(n+1)}(a)\)
🔹 D. \(f'(a)\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Taylor's theorem states that \(f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(x-a)^{n}\). The coefficient of the term \((x-a)^{n}\) is therefore \(\frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}\), directly involving the \(n\)th derivative evaluated at \(a\). This makes option A the accurate description.

Q14. Given a thrice‑differentiable function with \(f(0)=0\), \(f'(0)=1\), \(f''(0)=0\), and \(f'''(x)=6\) for all \(x\), what is \(f(2)\)?

🔹 A. 8
🔹 B. 10
🔹 C. 12
🔹 D. 14

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Integrate the constant third derivative: \(f''(x)=6x+C_{1}\). Using \(f''(0)=0\) gives \(C_{1}=0\), so \(f''=6x\). Integrate again: \(f'(x)=3x^{2}+C_{2}\); \(f'(0)=1\) yields \(C_{2}=1\). Integrate a final time: \(f(x)=x^{3}+x+C_{3}\); \(f(0)=0\) forces \(C_{3}=0\). Evaluating at \(x=2\) gives \(f(2)=8+2=10\). Hence option B is correct.

Q15. For \(g(x)=\frac{1}{1-x}\), which statement about its \(n\)th derivative at \(x=0\) is true?

🔹 A. \(g^{(n)}(0)=n!\)
🔹 B. \(g^{(n)}(0)=(-1)^{n}n!\)
🔹 C. \(g^{(n)}(0)=(n+1)!\)
🔹 D. \(g^{(n)}(0)=0\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The function expands as a geometric series: \(\frac{1}{1-x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\). The \(n\)th derivative of \(x^{k}\) at zero is zero unless \(k=n\), in which case it equals \(n!\). Therefore \(g^{(n)}(0)=n!\). The other options introduce incorrect signs or factorial offsets, making option A correct.

Q16. If a function \(f\) has a continuous fourth derivative and satisfies \(f^{(4)}(x)>0\) for all \(x\), is the function necessarily convex?

🔹 A. true
🔹 B. false
🔹 C. Cannot be determined from the information given
🔹 D. True only for quadratic functions

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Convexity of a function is guaranteed when its second derivative is non‑negative everywhere. A positive fourth derivative ensures that the second derivative is increasing, but it does not preclude the second derivative from being negative in some intervals. Hence the condition \(f^{(4)}(x)>0\) alone does not imply convexity, making the statement false.
