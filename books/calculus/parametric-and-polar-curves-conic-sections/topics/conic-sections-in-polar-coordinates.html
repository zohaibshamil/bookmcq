What is Conic sections in polar coordinates?

Definition: In polar coordinates, all conics with a focus at the origin are expressed as r=ep1±ecos⁡θr = \frac{ep}{1 \pm e\cos\theta}r=1±ecosθep​ or with sine, where p is the distance from focus to directrix. e=1 parabola, e<1 ellipse, e>1 hyperbola.
Example: r=62−3cos⁡θr = \frac{6}{2 - 3\cos\theta}r=2−3cosθ6​ → divide by 2: r=31−1.5cos⁡θr = \frac{3}{1 - 1.5\cos\theta}r=1−1.5cosθ3​ → e=1.5>1 hyperbola. r=21+cos⁡θr = \frac{2}{1+\cos\theta}r=1+cosθ2​ → e=1 parabola.
Reason: This unified polar representation is standard in celestial mechanics and orbital dynamics.
