What is Translated conics shifted conics?

Definition: A conic centered at \( (h,k) \) has its equation by replacing \( x \) with \( (x-h) \) and \( y \) with \( (y-k) \) in the standard form. Example: circle: \( (x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2 \); ellipse: \( (x-h)^2/a^2 + (y-k)^2/b^2 = 1 \); parabola: \( (y-k)^2 = 4p(x-h) \).
Example: Parabola \( (y-2)^2 = 8(x-3) \) has vertex (3,2), opens right, p=2. Ellipse \( (x+1)^2/9 + (y-4)^2/4 = 1 \) center (-1,4).
Reason: Shifting allows placement anywhere in plane, reflecting real-world positioning of objects.

Q1. A parabolic arch is modeled by \( y = -0.02(x-30)^2 + 18 \). Engineers need to install a horizontal support beam at a height where the width of the arch is exactly 40 units. What is the required vertical distance from the ground to this beam, and why does solving \( -0.02(x-30)^2 + 18 = h \) yield two solutions for \( x \) but only one valid \( h \)?

B.16 units; the quadratic yields two heights but only the lower one is structurally feasible.
C.10 units; the equation has two x-solutions because the beam intersects the parabola twice, confirming the width. ✅
D.8 units; the discriminant must be positive for real intersections, and h=8 satisfies the width constraint.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The problem requires translating the geometric condition (width = 40) into an algebraic constraint. Setting \( y = h \), we solve \( -0.02(x-30)^2 + 18 = h \), giving \( (x-30)^2 = (18-h)/0.02 \). The width is \( 2\sqrt{(18-h)/0.02} = 40 \), so \( \sqrt{(18-h)/0.02} = 20 \). Squaring gives \( (18-h)/0.02 = 400 \), hence \( 18-h = 8 \) and \( h = 10 \). The two x-values are symmetric about the axis \( x=30 \), which is expected for a horizontal chord. Option A miscomputes the square root step; B confuses multiple x with multiple h; D uses incorrect arithmetic. This tests application of translated conics in modeling with multi-step reasoning.

Q18. When completing the square for \( 3x^2 - 2y^2 + 12x + 8y - 6 = 0 \), a student factors as 3(x²+4x) - 2(y²-4y) = 6, then adds 3(4) and -2(4) to both sides. What critical error occurs in balancing the equation?

D.The sign for y-term is wrong; should be -2(y²+4y), leading to different completion.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Correct completion: 3(x²+4x) = 3[(x+2)²-4] = 3(x+2)²-12. -2(y²-4y) = -2[(y-2)²-4] = -2(y-2)²+8. So left side becomes 3(x+2)² - 2(y-2)² -12 +8 = 3(x+2)² - 2(y-2)² -4. For y: y²-4y needs +4 inside, but multiplied by -2, so -2*4=-8 added to left, meaning subtract 8 from left, so add 8 to right to balance. Total right: 6+12+8=26? Contradiction. Saying “add -2(4)” is ambiguous; it should be “add 8”. Option A captures this nuance. Thus, despite numerical correctness, the reasoning has a balancing misconception.
