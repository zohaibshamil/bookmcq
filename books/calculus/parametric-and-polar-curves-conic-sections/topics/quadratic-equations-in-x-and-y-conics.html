What is Quadratic equations in x and y conics?

Definition: A general second-degree equation \( Ax^2+Bxy+Cy^2+Dx+Ey+F=0 \) represents a conic (or degenerate). The discriminant \( \Delta = B^2 - 4AC \) determines type: \( \Delta<0 \) ellipse/circle, \( \Delta=0 \) parabola, \( \Delta>0 \) hyperbola.
Example: \( x^2+4xy+4y^2 -1=0 \): \( B^2-4AC = 16-16=0 \) → parabola. \( x^2+xy+y^2=1 \): Δ=1-4=-3<0 → ellipse. \( x^2 - y^2=1 \): Δ=0-4(-1)=4>0 → hyperbola.
Reason: The discriminant quickly classifies conics without completing squares, useful for larger systems.

Q1. A student attempts to eliminate the parameter from \( x = t^2 + 1 \) and \( y = t^3 - t \). They solve for \( t = \sqrt{x-1} \) and substitute into \( y \), obtaining \( y = (x-1)^{3/2} - \sqrt{x-1} \). Which statement best analyzes this derivation?

A.The result is correct and represents the entire curve.
B.The result is algebraically valid but only represents the portion where \( t \geq 0 \), missing the branch where \( t < 0 \). ✅
C.The student should have solved for \( t \) using the cubic equation instead.
D.The substitution is invalid because \( x-1 \) can be negative.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: This error analysis question targets a common misconception in parametric elimination. When solving \( x = t^2 + 1 \) for \( t \), taking only the positive square root restricts the domain to non-negative parameters. Since the original parametric equations allow \( t \) to be any real number, the Cartesian form must account for both branches, typically requiring \( y^2 = (x-1)(x-2)^2 \) or similar implicit forms to capture the full geometry without artificial restrictions.

Q3. A satellite dish is modeled by the parabola \( y = ax^2 \). Engineers require the focal length to be exactly one-fourth of the latus rectum length. If the current design has \( a = 0.5 \), how must the coefficient be adjusted to meet this specific geometric constraint while maintaining the vertex at the origin?

A.No adjustment is needed; all parabolas satisfy this ratio. ✅
B.Change \( a \) to 1.
C.Change \( a \) to 0.25.
D.It is impossible for a parabola to have this ratio.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This conceptual understanding question tests knowledge of intrinsic parabolic properties. For any parabola \( y = ax^2 \), the focal length is \( 1/(4a) \) and the latus rectum length is \( 1/a \). The ratio of focal length to latus rectum is always \( (1/(4a)) / (1/a) = 1/4 \). Therefore, the condition is universally true regardless of the value of \( a \). Distractors prey on students who memorize formulas without understanding their proportional relationships, leading them to unnecessarily manipulate coefficients when the geometric definition already satisfies the requirement inherently.

Option C correctly identifies the hyperbola and uses valid polar representations for the vertices on the transverse axis, testing deep understanding of polar coordinate conventions for conics.

This tests whether students check the feasibility of conic models after parameter changes, a critical engineering validation step often overlooked when focusing solely on algebraic manipulation.

Students must distinguish between the static Cartesian shape and the dynamic parametric tracing behavior, recognizing that the full parameter interval causes double traversal unlike simpler Lissajous figures.

This distinguishes between free and constrained optimization, a crucial higher-order distinction in multivariable calculus applied to conic sections.
