📝 Arc length of polar curve formula

📖 From Calculus • 11. Parametric and Polar curves: Conic Sections • 25 questions available

What is Arc length of polar curve formula?

Definition: The arc length of a polar curve \( r=f(\theta) \) from \( \theta=\alpha \) to \( \theta=\beta \) is \( L = \int_\alpha^\beta \sqrt{ r^2 + (dr/d\theta)^2 } \, d\theta \). Example: For \( r=e^\theta \) from \( \theta=0 \) to \( 1 \), \( dr/d\theta=e^\theta \), so \( L = \int_0^1 \sqrt{e^{2\theta} + e^{2\theta}} \, d\theta = \int_0^1 e^\theta\sqrt{2} \, d\theta = \sqrt{2}(e-1) \). Reason: Derived from parametric arc length using \( x=r\cos\theta, y=r\sin\theta \), it measures curve length directly in polar coordinates.

Q1. A student calculates the arc length of \( r = \sin(3\theta) \) over \( [0, 2\pi] \) by integrating \( \sqrt{r^2 + (dr/d\theta)^2} \) from 0 to \( 2\pi \). They obtain a value exactly double the expected geometric length. What is the most likely conceptual error in their setup?

B. The curve traces each petal twice over \( [0, 2\pi] \), so the integral computes traversed distance rather than unique geometric length. ✅
C. They used \( r \) instead of \( r^2 \) under the square root.
D. The formula requires \( |dr/d\theta| \) instead of \( dr/d\theta \), causing sign cancellation.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The polar rose \( r = \sin(3\theta) \) completes its entire geometric shape as \( \theta \) ranges from 0 to \( \pi \). Over \( [0, 2\pi] \), each petal is traced exactly twice because \( \sin(3(\theta+\pi)) = -\sin(3\theta) \), which corresponds to the same points in polar coordinates. The arc length integral measures path traversal, not unique geometry, so integrating over the full period doubles the result. Recognizing domain redundancy is essential for correct geometric interpretation.

This multi-step analysis reveals non-intuitive scaling behavior critical in engineering design where parameter sensitivity matters.

Q13. Suppose \( r(\theta) \) is differentiable and positive on \( [a,b] \). If \( \int_a^b \sqrt{r^2 + (dr/d\theta)^2} d\theta = \int_a^b r d\theta \), what must be true about \( r(\theta) \)?

Q21. In a mixed-concepts problem, suppose \( r(\theta) \) satisfies the differential equation \( (dr/d\theta)^2 + r^2 = 4r^2 \). What is the arc length from \( \theta = 0 \) to \( \theta = \pi \)?

Unless… if we assume the curve passes through a standard point? The problem doesn’t specify. However, notice that \( ds/d\theta = 2r \), but without \( r(\theta) \), L isn’t determined. So L = ∫2r dθ, and r = r₀e^{±√3 θ}, so L = 2r₀ ∫e^{±√3 θ}dθ from 0 to π, which indeed depends on r₀ and sign. So D is correct. But why would A be tempting? If someone mistakenly thinks ds/dθ = 2 (constant), they’d get 2π. The explanation must emphasize that differential equations define families, not unique curves, and arc length inherits this dependency. This blends ODEs with polar arc length, testing synthesis skills.

Q24. Which of the following best justifies using \( \theta \) as the parameter in polar arc length instead of arc length itself?
