What is Polar coordinates system explained?

Definition: The polar coordinate system uses a fixed point O (pole) and a ray (polar axis). A point P is located by distance r=OP r = OP r=OP and angle θ \theta θ from the axis, measured counterclockwise. r r r can be negative, meaning opposite direction.

Example: (5,30∘) (5, 30^\circ) (5,30∘) is 5 units at 30° above axis; (−5,30∘) (-5, 30^\circ) (−5,30∘) is 5 units at 210° (opposite). The pole is (0,θ) (0, \theta) (0,θ) for any θ \theta θ.

Reason: This system is natural for circular and rotational motion, and many physical phenomena (like waves, orbits) are easier to express radially.
