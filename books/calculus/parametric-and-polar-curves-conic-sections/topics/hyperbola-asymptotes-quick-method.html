What is Hyperbola asymptotes quick method?

Definition: For horizontal hyperbola x2/a2−y2/b2=1x^2/a^2 - y^2/b^2 = 1x2/a2−y2/b2=1, asymptotes are y=±(b/a)xy = \pm (b/a)xy=±(b/a)x. For vertical y2/a2−x2/b2=1y^2/a^2 - x^2/b^2 = 1y2/a2−x2/b2=1, asymptotes are y=±(a/b)xy = \pm (a/b)xy=±(a/b)x. They pass through the center.
Example: For x2/16−y2/9=1x^2/16 - y^2/9 = 1x2/16−y2/9=1, a=4,b=3 → asymptotes y=±(3/4)xy = \pm (3/4)xy=±(3/4)x. For y2/25−x2/4=1y^2/25 - x^2/4 = 1y2/25−x2/4=1, a=5,b=2 → y=±(5/2)xy = \pm (5/2)xy=±(5/2)x.
Reason: Asymptotes guide the hyperbola's shape; the branches approach these lines as x,y → ∞, making sketching accurate.
