What is Graphing inverse functions reflection?

Definition:
The graph of an inverse function f−1f^{-1}f−1 is the reflection of the graph of fff across the line y=xy = xy=x, meaning that if (a,b)(a,b)(a,b) lies on fff, then (b,a)(b,a)(b,a) lies on f−1f^{-1}f−1, swapping coordinates.

Example:
For f(x)=x3f(x)=x^3f(x)=x3, graph passes through (2,8); its inverse f−1(x)=x3f^{-1}(x)=\sqrt[3]{x}f−1(x)=3x​ passes through (8,2), and the two curves are symmetric about y=xy=xy=x.

Reason:
This geometric relationship provides a visual check and aids in sketching inverse graphs without algebraic computation.
