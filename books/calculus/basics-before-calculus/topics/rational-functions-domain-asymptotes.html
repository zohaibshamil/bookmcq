Q1. Which of the following best defines a rational function?

🔹 A. A function that can be written as a sum of two polynomials.
🔹 B. A function that can be expressed as the ratio of two polynomials.
🔹 C. A function whose graph is always continuous.
🔹 D. A function that has only horizontal asymptotes.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A rational function is precisely a quotient of two polynomial expressions, written as \(P(x)/Q(x)\) where \(Q(x)\neq0\). This definition matches option B, whereas the other options describe unrelated properties such as continuity or asymptotic behavior.

Q2. Consider \(f(x)=\frac{x^{2}-4}{x-2}\). After simplifying, what type of discontinuity does the function have at \(x=2\)?

🔹 A. Vertical asymptote
🔹 B. No discontinuity
🔹 C. Removable hole
🔹 D. Jump discontinuity

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Factoring the numerator gives \((x-2)(x+2)\). Cancelling the common factor with the denominator leaves \(f(x)=x+2\) for all \(x\neq2\). The point \(x=2\) is omitted, creating a removable hole rather than an infinite break, so option C is correct.

Q3. What is the domain of \(g(x)=\frac{x+3}{x^{2}-9}\)?

🔹 A. All real numbers except \(x=-3\) and \(x=3\)
🔹 B. All real numbers
🔹 C. All real numbers except \(x=0\)
🔹 D. All real numbers except \(x=9\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The denominator \(x^{2}-9\) factors to \((x-3)(x+3)\). The function is undefined wherever this product equals zero, i.e., at \(x=3\) and \(x=-3\). All other real numbers are allowed, making option A the correct description of the domain.

Q4. If \(h(x)=\frac{x+1}{x-2}\), what is the sign of \(h(x)\) just to the right of its vertical asymptote at \(x=2\)?

🔹 A. Positive
🔹 B. Negative
🔹 C. Zero
🔹 D. Undefined

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Near the vertical asymptote \(x=2\), evaluate the sign of the numerator and denominator. For \(x\) just larger than 2, the numerator \(x+1\) is positive, and the denominator \(x-2\) is a small positive number, giving a positive quotient. Hence the function is positive to the right of the asymptote, matching option A.

Q5. Given \(k(x)=\frac{3x^{2}+5}{2x^{2}-7}\), which of the following statements about its horizontal asymptote is true?

🔹 A. The horizontal asymptote is \(y=\frac{3}{2}\).
🔹 B. The function has no horizontal asymptote.
🔹 C. The horizontal asymptote is \(y=0\).
🔹 D. The horizontal asymptote is \(y=1\).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The degrees of numerator and denominator are both 2, so the horizontal asymptote is the ratio of leading coefficients: \(\frac{3}{2}\). Therefore the statement that the horizontal asymptote is \(y=\frac{3}{2}\) is true, which corresponds to option A.

Q6. Which of the following rational functions shares the same horizontal asymptote as \(f(x)=\frac{2x^{2}+3}{x^{2}-5}\)?

🔹 A. \(\frac{2x^{2}+1}{x^{2}+4}\)
🔹 B. \(\frac{x^{2}+1}{x^{2}+4}\)
🔹 C. \(\frac{2x^{2}+3}{2x^{2}+5}\)
🔹 D. \(\frac{x^{2}+2}{2x^{2}+3}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Horizontal asymptotes depend on the leading terms. The original function has leading coefficient ratio \(2/1 = 2\). Among the choices, only \(\frac{2x^{2}+1}{x^{2}+4}\) retains that ratio, giving the same horizontal asymptote \(y=2\). Thus option A is correct.

Q7. For the rational function \(p(x)=\frac{x^{2}-9}{x-3}\), which statement correctly describes its discontinuity at \(x=3\)?

🔹 A. There is a vertical asymptote at \(x=3\).
🔹 B. The function is continuous at \(x=3\).
🔹 C. There is a removable hole at \(x=3\).
🔹 D. The function has a jump discontinuity at \(x=3\).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The numerator \(x^{2}-9\) factors as \((x-3)(x+3)\). Cancelling the factor \((x-3)\) with the denominator leaves \(p(x)=x+3\) for all \(x\neq3\). The point at \(x=3\) is removed, producing a hole rather than a vertical asymptote, so option C is correct.

Q8. What is \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x^{3}-x}{2x^{3}+5x^{2}}\)?

🔹 A. \(\frac{3}{2}\)
🔹 B. 0
🔹 C. \(\infty\)
🔹 D. -\(\frac{3}{2}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When \(x\) grows without bound, the highest-degree terms dominate. The ratio of the leading coefficients \(\frac{3}{2}\) determines the limit, because lower-degree terms become negligible. Hence \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x^{3}-x}{2x^{3}+5x^{2}}=\frac{3}{2}\), which matches option A.

Q9. Find all real numbers \(a\) such that the rational function \(r(x)=\frac{x^{2}-a}{x-a}\) has a removable discontinuity (hole) at \(x=a\).

🔹 A. All real numbers
🔹 B. No real numbers
🔹 C. Only \(a=0\) or \(a=1\)
🔹 D. Only \(a=1\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A removable discontinuity occurs when both numerator and denominator vanish at the same point, allowing cancellation. Setting the numerator zero at \(x=a\) gives \(a^{2}-a=0\), factoring to \(a(a-1)=0\). Thus \(a\) must be 0 or 1. Option C lists exactly these values, making it the correct choice.

Q10. For \(s(x)=\frac{x^{2}-4}{x^{2}-9}\), on which interval is the function increasing?

🔹 A. \((-\infty,-3)\)
🔹 B. \((-3,-2)\)
🔹 C. \((-2,2)\)
🔹 D. \((2,\infty)\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating \(s(x)=\frac{x^{2}-4}{x^{2}-9}\) yields \(s'(x)=\frac{-10x}{(x^{2}-9)^{2}}\). The denominator is always positive except at the undefined points \(x=\pm3\). Therefore the sign of \(s'(x)\) is opposite to the sign of \(x\). The function is increasing where \(x<0\); the interval \((-\infty,-3)\) satisfies this, so option A is correct.

Q11. Why can a rational function have a horizontal asymptote even though it is not a polynomial?

🔹 A. Because the numerator eventually dominates the denominator.
🔹 B. Because the degrees of numerator and denominator determine the end behavior.
🔹 C. Because rational functions are always bounded.
🔹 D. Because they have vertical asymptotes.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The end behavior of a rational function is governed by the degrees of its numerator and denominator. When these degrees are equal, the ratio of the leading coefficients determines a constant value approached as \(x\) tends to \(\pm\infty\). This explains why such functions have a horizontal asymptote, matching option B.

Q12. When sketching the graph of \(f(x)=\frac{x^{2}-1}{x-1}\), which of the following steps is essential?

🔹 A. Identify the hole at \(x=1\) and the vertical asymptote at \(x=0\).
🔹 B. Cancel the common factor to find the simplified function and locate the hole.
🔹 C. Find the horizontal asymptote \(y=0\).
🔹 D. Determine the intercepts without simplifying.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The original expression simplifies by factoring \(x^{2}-1=(x-1)(x+1)\) and cancelling the common factor \((x-1)\). This reveals a hole at \(x=1\) and reduces the function to \(f(x)=x+1\) elsewhere. Recognizing and removing the factor is essential for accurate graphing, so option B is correct.

Q13. What is the vertical asymptote of the rational function \(f(x)=\frac{2}{x-5}\)?

🔹 A. x = 0
🔹 B. x = 5
🔹 C. x = -5
🔹 D. No vertical asymptote

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A vertical asymptote occurs where the denominator equals zero while the numerator remains non‑zero. For \(f(x)=\frac{2}{x-5}\), the denominator vanishes at \(x=5\), producing an infinite blow‑up there. Hence the vertical asymptote is the line \(x=5\), which is option B.
