What is Irrational exponents and real powers?

Definition:
Irrational exponents define real powers \( a^r \) for any real \( r \), extending rational exponent rules via limits of rational approximations, ensuring continuity and consistency for exponential functions with real exponents.

Example:
\( 2^{\sqrt{2}} \) is defined as the limit of \( 2^{1.414}, 2^{1.4142}, \ldots \), approximately equal to \( 2.665 \), using rational approximations of \( \sqrt{2} \).

Reason:
This extension allows exponential functions to be defined on all real numbers, enabling continuous growth models (like compound interest continuously) and natural logarithms.

📝 All Irrational exponents and real powers MCQs

Q1. Which of the following best describes an irrational exponent?

A.A rational number that can be expressed as a fraction
B.An exponent that cannot be written as a ratio of two integers ✅
C.An exponent that is negative
D.An exponent that is zero
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An irrational exponent is a number that cannot be expressed as a fraction of two integers, i.e., it is not rational. Because it cannot be written in the form \(p/q\) with integers \(p,q\), its decimal expansion is non‑terminating and non‑repeating, which is the defining property of irrational numbers.

Q2. Given the function \(f(x)=x^{\sqrt{2}}\) defined for \(x>0\), which statement is true?

A.\(f\) is decreasing on \((0,1)\) and increasing on \((1,\infty)\) ✅
B.\(f\) is increasing on \((0,1)\) and decreasing on \((1,\infty)\)
C.\(f\) is decreasing on its entire domain
D.\(f\) is constant
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Since the exponent \(\sqrt{2}>0\), the derivative \(f'(x)=\sqrt{2}\,x^{\sqrt{2}-1}\) is negative when \(0<x<1\) (because the power \(\sqrt{2}-1\) is positive but \(x^{\text{positive}}\) is less than 1) and positive when \(x>1\). Thus the function falls on \((0,1)\) and rises after \(x=1\).

Q3. Compare the graphs of \(y=x^{\sqrt{2}}\) and \(y=x^{2}\) for \(x>0\). Which statement is correct?

A.\(x^{\sqrt{2}}\) is larger than \(x^{2}\) for all \(x>0\)
B.\(x^{\sqrt{2}}\) is smaller than \(x^{2}\) for \(x>1\) and larger for \(0<x<1\) ✅
C.Both functions are identical
D.\(x^{\sqrt{2}}\) is always smaller than \(x^{2}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Because \(\sqrt{2}\approx1.414<2\), raising a number greater than 1 to a larger exponent yields a larger value, so for \(x>1\) we have \(x^{\sqrt{2}}<x^{2}\). Conversely, for numbers between 0 and 1, a larger exponent makes the result smaller, reversing the inequality. At \(x=1\) the two functions coincide.

Q4. Simplify \((\sqrt[3]{x})^{\sqrt{2}}\).

A.\(x^{\sqrt{2}/3}\) ✅
B.\(x^{3\sqrt{2}}\)
C.\(\sqrt[3]{x^{\sqrt{2}}}\)
D.\(x^{\sqrt{2}}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The cube root \(\sqrt[3]{x}\) is \(x^{1/3}\). Raising this to the power \(\sqrt{2}\) multiplies the exponents: \((x^{1/3})^{\sqrt{2}}=x^{\sqrt{2}/3}\). This follows directly from the law \((a^{m})^{n}=a^{mn}\).

Q5. If \(a>0\) and \(b>0\) satisfy \(a^{\sqrt{2}}=b\) and \(b^{\sqrt{2}}=a\), what is \(a\)?

A.1 ✅
B.0
C.\(e\)
D.Undefined
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Substituting the first equation into the second gives \((a^{\sqrt{2}})^{\sqrt{2}}=a\), which simplifies to \(a^{2}=a\). Solving \(a^{2}-a=0\) yields \(a=0\) or \(a=1\). Since \(a>0\), the admissible solution is \(a=1\).

Q6. Determine the domain of \(f(x)=\sqrt{x^{\sqrt{2}}-1}\).

A.\((-\infty,0]\)
B.\([0,1]\)
C.\([1,\infty)\) ✅
D.\((0,\infty)\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The radicand must be non‑negative: \(x^{\sqrt{2}}-1\ge0\Rightarrow x^{\sqrt{2}}\ge1\). Because the base \(x\) is required to be positive for the irrational exponent to be real, this inequality reduces to \(x\ge1\). Hence the domain is the interval \([1,\infty)\).

Q7. What is \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^{+}} x^{\sqrt{2}}\)?

A.0 ✅
B.1
C.\(\infty\)
D.Undefined
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For any positive exponent, the function \(x^{c}\) approaches 0 as \(x\) approaches 0 from the right. Since \(\sqrt{2}>0\), the limit \(\lim_{x\to0^{+}}x^{\sqrt{2}}=0\). This follows from the continuity of the power function on \((0,\infty)\).

Q8. Consider \(f(x)=x^{\sqrt{2}}\) for negative \(x\). Which statement is correct?

A.Real values exist for all negative \(x\)
B.Real values exist only when the exponent has an odd denominator
C.No real values exist for any negative \(x\) ✅
D.Real values exist only when \(x=-1\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: When the base is negative and the exponent is irrational, the result cannot be expressed as a real number because the exponent cannot be written as a fraction with an odd denominator that would allow a real root. Consequently, \(x^{\sqrt{2}}\) is undefined in the real number system for every negative \(x\).

Q9. When graphing \(f(x)=x^{2/3}\), which modification ensures the graph includes negative \(x\)?

A.Graph \(g(x)=|x|^{2/3}\) ✅
B.Graph \(g(x)=\frac{|x|}{x}\,|x|^{2/3}\)
C.Graph \(g(x)=\sqrt[3]{x^{2}}\)
D.Graph \(g(x)=x^{2/3}\) for \(x\ge0\) only
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Here the numerator 2 is even and the denominator 3 is odd. According to the rule, we replace the original function with \(g(x)=|x|^{p/q}\); thus \(g(x)=|x|^{2/3}\) correctly produces real values for both positive and negative inputs.

Q10. What is the status of \(f(x)=x^{\sqrt{2}}\) at \(x=0\)?

A.Continuous from the right
B.Continuous from the left
C.Discontinuous
D.Not defined at 0 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The expression \(x^{\sqrt{2}}\) is only defined for \(x>0\) when we restrict ourselves to real numbers, because a negative or zero base with an irrational exponent leads to an undefined or complex value. Therefore the function does not exist at \(x=0\), and continuity cannot be assessed there.

Q11. As \(x\to\infty\), how does the growth of \(f(x)=x^{\sqrt{2}}\) compare to \(g(x)=\ln(x)\)?

A.\(f\) grows faster than \(g\) ✅
B.\(g\) grows faster than \(f\)
C.Both grow at the same rate
D.Neither grows
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Power functions with positive exponents dominate logarithmic functions for large arguments. Since \(\sqrt{2}>0\), \(x^{\sqrt{2}}\) increases without bound much more rapidly than \(\ln x\), which grows only logarithmically. Hence \(f(x)\) outpaces \(g(x)\) as \(x\) becomes large.

Q12. Evaluate \((16)^{\sqrt{2}/2}\).

A.\(2^{2\sqrt{2}}\) ✅
B.\(4^{\sqrt{2}}\)
C.\(8^{\sqrt{2}}\)
D.\(16^{\sqrt{2}}\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Rewrite \(16\) as \(2^{4}\). Then \((2^{4})^{\sqrt{2}/2}=2^{4\cdot(\sqrt{2}/2)}=2^{2\sqrt{2}}\). This simplification uses the exponent rule \((a^{m})^{n}=a^{mn}\), yielding the exact expression \(2^{2\sqrt{2}}\).
