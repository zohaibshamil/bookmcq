What is One to one functions and invertibility?

Definition:
A one-to-one function (injective) maps distinct inputs to distinct outputs, i.e., f(a)=f(b) f(a) = f(b) f(a)=f(b) implies a=b a = b a=b, making it invertible over its range, with a unique inverse function defined everywhere on that range.

Example:
f(x)=3x−1 f(x)=3x-1 f(x)=3x−1 is one-to-one because if 3a−1=3b−1 3a-1=3b-1 3a−1=3b−1, then a=b a=b a=b; hence it is invertible, with inverse f−1(x)=(x+1)/3 f^{-1}(x) = (x+1)/3 f−1(x)=(x+1)/3.

Reason:
One-to-one property is the key to invertibility, allowing for reversible processes in mathematics, cryptography, and data encoding.
