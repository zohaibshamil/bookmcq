What is Logarithmic scales pH decibel Richter?

Definition:
Logarithmic scales, such as pH (pH=−log⁡10[H+] \mathrm{pH} = -\log_{10}[\mathrm{H}^+] pH=−log10​[H+]), decibels (dB=10log⁡10(I/I0) \mathrm{dB} = 10 \log_{10}(I/I_0) dB=10log10​(I/I0​)), and Richter magnitude (M=log⁡10(A/A0) M = \log_{10}(A/A_0) M=log10​(A/A0​)), express wide-ranging quantities in compact, human-readable numbers.

Example:
A solution with [H+]=10−5 [\mathrm{H}^+] = 10^{-5} [H+]=10−5 has pH = 5; a sound with intensity 1000 times reference has dB=10log⁡10(1000)=30 \mathrm{dB} = 10 \log_{10}(1000) = 30 dB=10log10​(1000)=30.

Reason:
Log scales compress exponential ranges (e.g., acidity, loudness, earthquake energy) into linear intervals, making comparisons and interpretations practical.
