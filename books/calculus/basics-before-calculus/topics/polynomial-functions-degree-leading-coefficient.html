📝 Polynomial functions degree leading coefficient (12 MCQs)

Polynomial Structure, Degree, and Leading Term

A polynomial function is a finite sum of terms containing nonnegative integer powers of a variable, with real coefficients. Its degree and leading coefficient determine important features of its graph and long-term behaviour. Polynomial functions form a major family in precalculus and differential calculus because they are continuous and differentiable at every real input and have straightforward algebraic derivative rules.

How Polynomial Terms Determine Algebraic Form

Every nonzero polynomial can be arranged in descending powers of its variable. The highest exponent with a nonzero coefficient determines the degree, while the coefficient of that term is the leading coefficient. These quantities help predict end behaviour, possible turning points, and the number of real zeros without requiring a complete graph.

Standard Polynomial Form and Degree Identification

A polynomial has the form \(P(x)=a_nx^n+\cdots+a_1x+a_0\), where \(a_n\neq0\) and \(n\) is a nonnegative integer. Its degree is \(n\), and its leading coefficient is \(a_n\). The zero polynomial is treated separately because it has no highest nonzero-degree term under the usual convention.

Determining Degree and Leading Coefficient from an Expression

Consider \(P(x)=4x^5-3x^2+7x-9\). The highest power is five, so the degree is 5, and its leading coefficient is 4. Since the degree is odd and the leading coefficient positive, the graph falls toward negative infinity on the left and rises toward positive infinity on the right.

Polynomial Behaviour in Derivatives and Applications

Polynomials model trajectories, approximations, costs, and many smooth relationships. Their derivatives are obtained term by term using the power rule, and polynomial integrals are also straightforward. A degree-\(n\) nonzero polynomial has at most \(n\) distinct real zeros and at most \(n-1\) turning points, although its actual graph may have fewer.

Errors in Polynomial Degree and Leading-Term Analysis

'The degree is the number of terms' is false because degree is the highest exponent after simplification. 'The leading coefficient is always the largest coefficient' ignores its position on the highest-degree term. 'A degree-five polynomial must have five real roots' confuses the maximum number of complex roots, counted with multiplicity, with the number of real roots.

Polynomial Properties in Examination Questions

Identify polynomial degree, leading coefficient, zeros, and end behaviour from an expression. Practise factoring, determining maximum possible turning points, and applying the power rule. Be alert to terms that cancel before the degree is identified. Graph questions commonly combine the leading term with degree parity to determine the direction of each end.

Key Terms of Polynomial Functions

  • Polynomial - Finite sum of nonnegative integer powers.
  • Degree - Highest exponent with nonzero coefficient.
  • Leading coefficient - Coefficient of the highest-degree term.
  • Constant term - Term independent of the variable.
  • Zero - Input producing an output of zero.
  • End behaviour - Graph behaviour as inputs approach infinity.

📝 All Polynomial functions degree leading coefficient MCQs

Q1. What is the degree of the polynomial \(3x^4 - 5x^2 + 7\)?

A.2
B.4 ✅
C.3
D.5
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The degree of a polynomial is the highest exponent of the variable with a non‑zero coefficient. In the expression \(3x^4 - 5x^2 + 7\) the term with the largest exponent is \(3x^4\), whose exponent is 4. Hence the polynomial’s degree is 4, which matches option B.

Q2. If a polynomial \(p(x)\) has zeros at \(x=1\) and \(x=-2\), which of the following must be a factor of \(p(x)\)?

A.(x+1)(x-2)
B.(x-1)^2
C.(x+2)^2
D.(x-1)(x+2) ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Because a zero \(r\) of a polynomial implies \((x-r)\) is a factor, the zeros \(1\) and \(-2\) give the linear factors \((x-1)\) and \((x+2)\). Their product \((x-1)(x+2)\) must therefore divide \(p(x)\). This corresponds to option D.

Q3. Given \(p(x)=x^3-4x^2+5x-2\), what is \(p(2)\)?

A.2
B.-2
C.0 ✅
D.4
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Evaluating the polynomial at \(x=2\) gives \(p(2)=2^3-4\cdot2^2+5\cdot2-2=8-16+10-2=0\). Since the result is zero, the remainder upon division by \(x-2\) is zero, and option C correctly states this value.

Q4. Which statement correctly describes the end behavior of the polynomial \(f(x)= -2x^5 + 3x^3 - x\) compared to \(g(x)= x^5 - 4x^2 + 1\)?

A.f(x) falls while g(x) rises as \(x\to\infty\) ✅
B.Both rise to \(+\infty\) as \(x\to\infty\)
C.Both fall to \(-\infty\) as \(x\to\infty\)
D.f(x) rises while g(x) falls as \(x\to\infty\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The leading term dictates end behavior. For \(f(x)\) the dominant term is \(-2x^5\), which tends to \(-\infty\) as \(x\to\infty\). For \(g(x)\) the dominant term is \(x^5\), which tends to \(+\infty\). Thus \(f(x)\) falls and \(g(x)\) rises, matching option A.

Q5. If the product \(h(x)=p(x)q(x)\) has a double root at \(x=3\) and \(p(x)\) is known to have a simple root at \(x=3\), what must be true about \(q(x)\) at \(x=3\)?

A.\(q(3)=0\) and \(q'(3)=0\)
B.\(q(3)=0\) and \(q'(3)\neq0\) ✅
C.\(q(3)\neq0\)
D.\(q(3)=0\) only
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A double root means the total multiplicity at \(x=3\) is two. Since \(p(x)\) contributes a simple (multiplicity 1) root, \(q(x)\) must contribute the remaining multiplicity 1. A simple root requires the function to vanish while its derivative does not, giving \(q(3)=0\) and \(q'(3)\neq0\), which is option B.

Q6. When \(p(x)=x^3-6x^2+11x-6\) is divided by \(x-1\), what is the remainder?

A.1
B.2
C.3
D.0 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The Remainder Theorem states that the remainder of dividing \(p(x)\) by \(x-a\) equals \(p(a)\). Substituting \(a=1\) gives \(p(1)=1-6+11-6=0\). Hence the remainder is 0, which appears as option D.

Q7. Which polynomial grows faster as \(x\to\infty\): \(f(x)=3x^4-2x^2\) or \(g(x)=5x^3+7x\)?

A.g(x) grows faster
B.f(x) grows faster ✅
C.Both grow at the same rate
D.Growth cannot be determined
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Growth rate is governed by the highest degree term. \(f(x)\) has degree 4 while \(g(x)\) has degree 3, so as \(x\) becomes large, the \(x^4\) term dominates and \(f(x)\) outpaces \(g(x)\). Therefore option B is correct.

Q8. Which of the following polynomials has zeros at \(x= -1, 2\) and a leading coefficient of \(4\)?

A.\(4(x-1)(x-2)\)
B.\(4(x+1)(x+2)\)
C.\(4(x+1)(x-2)\) ✅
D.\(4(x-1)(x+2)\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A polynomial with specified zeros can be written as the product of linear factors \((x - r_i)\). For zeros \(-1\) and \(2\) the factors are \((x+1)\) and \((x-2)\). Multiplying and then applying the leading coefficient \(4\) yields \(4(x+1)(x-2)\), which is option C.

Q9. If a polynomial \(p(x)\) is an odd function, which of the following must be true about its coefficients?

A.All odd-degree coefficients are zero
B.Constant term is non‑zero
C.Leading coefficient must be negative
D.All even-degree coefficients are zero ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: An odd function satisfies \(p(-x) = -p(x)\). This condition forces every term with an even power of \(x\) to vanish, because such terms would remain unchanged under the sign change. Consequently all even-degree coefficients must be zero, making option D correct.

Q10. Let \(q(x)=x^4+ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\) be divided by \(x-1\) with remainder \(5\). Which of the following equations must hold?

A.\(1+a+b+c+d=5\) ✅
B.\(1-a+b-c+d=5\)
C.\(1+a-b+c-d=5\)
D.\(a+b+c+d=5\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: By the Remainder Theorem, the remainder equals \(q(1)\). Substituting \(x=1\) gives \(q(1)=1+a+b+c+d\). Setting this equal to the given remainder 5 yields the equation \(1+a+b+c+d=5\), which matches option A.

Q11. For a monic cubic polynomial \(p(x)=x^3+px^2+qx+r\), which expression gives the sum of its three roots?

A.\(-q\)
B.\(-r\)
C.\(-p\) ✅
D.\(p\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Vieta’s formulas relate coefficients to sums of roots. For a monic cubic \(x^3+px^2+qx+r\), the sum of the roots equals \(-p\) (the negative of the coefficient of \(x^2\)). Thus the correct expression is \(-p\), which is option C.

Q12. If a polynomial \(f(x)\) has a root at \(x=2\) of multiplicity 2, what can be said about \(f'(2)\) and \(f''(2)\)?

A.\(f'(2)=0\) and \(f''(2)=0\)
B.\(f'(2)=0\) but \(f''(2)\neq0\) ✅
C.\(f'(2)\neq0\) and \(f''(2)=0\)
D.\(f'(2)\neq0\) and \(f''(2)\neq0\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A root of multiplicity m forces the function and its first \(m-1\) derivatives to vanish at that point. With multiplicity 2, both the function and its first derivative are zero at \(x=2\), while the second derivative is generally non‑zero. Hence \(f'(2)=0\) and \(f''(2)\neq0\), which corresponds to option B.
