Q1. What is the degree of the polynomial \(3x^4 - 5x^2 + 7\)?

🔹 A. 2
🔹 B. 4
🔹 C. 3
🔹 D. 5

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The degree of a polynomial is the highest exponent of the variable with a non‑zero coefficient. In the expression \(3x^4 - 5x^2 + 7\) the term with the largest exponent is \(3x^4\), whose exponent is 4. Hence the polynomial’s degree is 4, which matches option B.

Q2. If a polynomial \(p(x)\) has zeros at \(x=1\) and \(x=-2\), which of the following must be a factor of \(p(x)\)?

🔹 A. (x+1)(x-2)
🔹 B. (x-1)^2
🔹 C. (x+2)^2
🔹 D. (x-1)(x+2)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Because a zero \(r\) of a polynomial implies \((x-r)\) is a factor, the zeros \(1\) and \(-2\) give the linear factors \((x-1)\) and \((x+2)\). Their product \((x-1)(x+2)\) must therefore divide \(p(x)\). This corresponds to option D.

Q3. Given \(p(x)=x^3-4x^2+5x-2\), what is \(p(2)\)?

🔹 A. 2
🔹 B. -2
🔹 C. 0
🔹 D. 4

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Evaluating the polynomial at \(x=2\) gives \(p(2)=2^3-4\cdot2^2+5\cdot2-2=8-16+10-2=0\). Since the result is zero, the remainder upon division by \(x-2\) is zero, and option C correctly states this value.

Q4. Which statement correctly describes the end behavior of the polynomial \(f(x)= -2x^5 + 3x^3 - x\) compared to \(g(x)= x^5 - 4x^2 + 1\)?

🔹 A. f(x) falls while g(x) rises as \(x\to\infty\)
🔹 B. Both rise to \(+\infty\) as \(x\to\infty\)
🔹 C. Both fall to \(-\infty\) as \(x\to\infty\)
🔹 D. f(x) rises while g(x) falls as \(x\to\infty\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The leading term dictates end behavior. For \(f(x)\) the dominant term is \(-2x^5\), which tends to \(-\infty\) as \(x\to\infty\). For \(g(x)\) the dominant term is \(x^5\), which tends to \(+\infty\). Thus \(f(x)\) falls and \(g(x)\) rises, matching option A.

Q5. If the product \(h(x)=p(x)q(x)\) has a double root at \(x=3\) and \(p(x)\) is known to have a simple root at \(x=3\), what must be true about \(q(x)\) at \(x=3\)?

🔹 A. \(q(3)=0\) and \(q'(3)=0\)
🔹 B. \(q(3)=0\) and \(q'(3)\neq0\)
🔹 C. \(q(3)\neq0\)
🔹 D. \(q(3)=0\) only

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A double root means the total multiplicity at \(x=3\) is two. Since \(p(x)\) contributes a simple (multiplicity 1) root, \(q(x)\) must contribute the remaining multiplicity 1. A simple root requires the function to vanish while its derivative does not, giving \(q(3)=0\) and \(q'(3)\neq0\), which is option B.

Q6. When \(p(x)=x^3-6x^2+11x-6\) is divided by \(x-1\), what is the remainder?

🔹 A. 1
🔹 B. 2
🔹 C. 3
🔹 D. 0

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The Remainder Theorem states that the remainder of dividing \(p(x)\) by \(x-a\) equals \(p(a)\). Substituting \(a=1\) gives \(p(1)=1-6+11-6=0\). Hence the remainder is 0, which appears as option D.

Q7. Which polynomial grows faster as \(x\to\infty\): \(f(x)=3x^4-2x^2\) or \(g(x)=5x^3+7x\)?

🔹 A. g(x) grows faster
🔹 B. f(x) grows faster
🔹 C. Both grow at the same rate
🔹 D. Growth cannot be determined

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Growth rate is governed by the highest degree term. \(f(x)\) has degree 4 while \(g(x)\) has degree 3, so as \(x\) becomes large, the \(x^4\) term dominates and \(f(x)\) outpaces \(g(x)\). Therefore option B is correct.

Q8. Which of the following polynomials has zeros at \(x= -1, 2\) and a leading coefficient of \(4\)?

🔹 A. \(4(x-1)(x-2)\)
🔹 B. \(4(x+1)(x+2)\)
🔹 C. \(4(x+1)(x-2)\)
🔹 D. \(4(x-1)(x+2)\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A polynomial with specified zeros can be written as the product of linear factors \((x - r_i)\). For zeros \(-1\) and \(2\) the factors are \((x+1)\) and \((x-2)\). Multiplying and then applying the leading coefficient \(4\) yields \(4(x+1)(x-2)\), which is option C.

Q9. If a polynomial \(p(x)\) is an odd function, which of the following must be true about its coefficients?

🔹 A. All odd-degree coefficients are zero
🔹 B. Constant term is non‑zero
🔹 C. Leading coefficient must be negative
🔹 D. All even-degree coefficients are zero

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

An odd function satisfies \(p(-x) = -p(x)\). This condition forces every term with an even power of \(x\) to vanish, because such terms would remain unchanged under the sign change. Consequently all even-degree coefficients must be zero, making option D correct.

Q10. Let \(q(x)=x^4+ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\) be divided by \(x-1\) with remainder \(5\). Which of the following equations must hold?

🔹 A. \(1+a+b+c+d=5\)
🔹 B. \(1-a+b-c+d=5\)
🔹 C. \(1+a-b+c-d=5\)
🔹 D. \(a+b+c+d=5\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

By the Remainder Theorem, the remainder equals \(q(1)\). Substituting \(x=1\) gives \(q(1)=1+a+b+c+d\). Setting this equal to the given remainder 5 yields the equation \(1+a+b+c+d=5\), which matches option A.

Q11. For a monic cubic polynomial \(p(x)=x^3+px^2+qx+r\), which expression gives the sum of its three roots?

🔹 A. \(-q\)
🔹 B. \(-r\)
🔹 C. \(-p\)
🔹 D. \(p\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Vieta’s formulas relate coefficients to sums of roots. For a monic cubic \(x^3+px^2+qx+r\), the sum of the roots equals \(-p\) (the negative of the coefficient of \(x^2\)). Thus the correct expression is \(-p\), which is option C.

Q12. If a polynomial \(f(x)\) has a root at \(x=2\) of multiplicity 2, what can be said about \(f'(2)\) and \(f''(2)\)?

🔹 A. \(f'(2)=0\) and \(f''(2)=0\)
🔹 B. \(f'(2)=0\) but \(f''(2)\neq0\)
🔹 C. \(f'(2)\neq0\) and \(f''(2)=0\)
🔹 D. \(f'(2)\neq0\) and \(f''(2)\neq0\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A root of multiplicity m forces the function and its first \(m-1\) derivatives to vanish at that point. With multiplicity 2, both the function and its first derivative are zero at \(x=2\), while the second derivative is generally non‑zero. Hence \(f'(2)=0\) and \(f''(2)\neq0\), which corresponds to option B.
