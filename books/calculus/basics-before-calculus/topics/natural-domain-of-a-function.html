What is Natural domain of a function?

Definition:
The natural domain of a function is the largest set of real numbers for which the algebraic expression is defined, without any external restrictions, determined solely by the mathematical operations involved.

Example:
For \( f(x) = \frac{1}{x-3} \), the natural domain excludes \( x = 3 \) because division by zero is undefined, so domain is \( (-\infty,3) \cup (3,\infty) \).

Reason:
The natural domain ensures that all computations are valid within real numbers, which is fundamental before applying any contextual or applied restrictions.

📝 All Natural domain of a function MCQs

Q1. What is the natural domain of the function \( f(x)=\dfrac{1}{(x-2)(x+5)} \)?

A.All real numbers except \(x=2\) and \(x=-5\) ✅
B.All real numbers except \(x=2\)
C.All real numbers except \(x=-5\)
D.All real numbers
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The denominator vanishes when \(x-2=0\) or \(x+5=0\), i.e., at \(x=2\) and \(x=-5\). Since division by zero is undefined, those two points must be excluded while every other real number is allowed, giving the domain \((-\infty, -5)\cup(-5,2)\cup(2,\infty)\).

Q2. For \( f(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}-4x+3} \), which intervals belong to its natural domain?

A.\((-\infty,1]\cup[3,\infty)\) ✅
B.\((-\infty,1)\cup(3,\infty)\)
C.\([1,3]\)
D.\((1,3)\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Factor the radican\(x^{2}-4x+3=(x-1)(x-3)\). The product is non‑negative when \(x\le1\) or \(x\ge3\). Including the zeros gives the closed intervals at 1 and 3, so the domain is \((-\infty,1]\cup[3,\infty)\).

Q3. Compare the natural domains of \( f(x)=\tan x \) and \( g(x)=\sec x \). Which statement is correct?

A.tan domain excludes odd multiples of \( \pi/2 \); sec excludes even multiples
B.tan excludes odd multiples of \( \pi/2 \); sec excludes same set
C.Both have the same domain, namely all real numbers except odd multiples of \( \pi/2 \) ✅
D.The domains are different
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Both \(\tan x\) and \(\sec x\) are undefined wherever \(\cos x=0\). This occurs at odd integer multiples of \(\pi/2\). Consequently, each function shares the exact same set of permissible \(x\)-values, namely \(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{(2k+1)\pi/2\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\}\).

Q4. Which of the following best defines the natural domain of a function?

A.The set of y‑values the function can produce
B.The set of x‑values for which the function yields real numbers ✅
C.All real numbers from \(-\infty\) to \(+\infty\)
D.Only the non‑negative x‑values
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The natural domain consists of all input values \(x\) for which the expression defining the function is mathematically meaningful and returns a real number. It is determined by the restrictions imposed by denominators, radicals, logarithms, and other operations that limit admissible inputs.

Q5. Given \( h(x)=\dfrac{x^{2}-9}{x-3} \), what is its natural domain?

A.All real numbers
B.All real numbers except \(x=2\)
C.All real numbers except \(x=3\) ✅
D.All real numbers except \(x=9\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The denominator becomes zero when \(x-3=0\), i.e., at \(x=3\). No other restriction exists because the numerator can take any real value. Hence the function is defined for every real number except \(x=3\), giving the domain \((-\infty,3)\cup(3,\infty)\).

Q6. For \( f(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{x-1}}{x-2} \), which interval correctly describes its natural domain?

A.\([1,2)\cup(2,\infty)\) ✅
B.\((1,2)\cup(2,\infty)\)
C.\([1,\infty)\setminus\{2\}\)
D.\((-\infty,2)\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The radicand requires \(x-1\ge0\) → \(x\ge1\). The denominator cannot be zero, so \(x\neq2\). Combining these conditions yields the set \([1,2)\cup(2,\infty)\). The endpoint \(x=1\) is permissible because the numerator becomes zero while the denominator stays non‑zero.

Q7. Which interval correctly represents the natural domain of \( p(x)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2}-4}} \)?

A.\((-\infty,-2)\cup(2,\infty)\) ✅
B.\((-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty)\)
C.\((-\infty,-2)\cup[2,\infty)\)
D.\([ -2,2 ]\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The expression under the square root must be strictly positive because it appears in the denominator. Solving \(x^{2}-4>0\) gives \(x<-2\) or \(x>2\). Therefore the domain is the union of the two open intervals \((-\infty,-2)\) and \((2,\infty)\).

Q8. Determine the natural domain of \( f(x)=\ln\bigl(x^{2}-4x+3\bigr) \).

A.\((-\infty,1)\cup(3,\infty)\) ✅
B.\((-\infty,1]\cup[3,\infty)\)
C.\([1,3]\)
D.\((1,3)\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The argument of the logarithm must be positive. Factoring gives \((x-1)(x-3)>0\), which holds for \(x<1\) or \(x>3\). The endpoints where the product equals zero are excluded because \(\ln 0\) is undefined. Hence the domain is \((-\infty,1)\cup(3,\infty)\).

Q9. If \( g(x)=\dfrac{x^{2}-4}{x-2} \) is simplified to \( h(x)=x+2 \), which statement about their natural domains is true?

A.Both functions have the same domain
B.\( g \) is undefined at \(x=2\) while \( h \) is defined for all real \(x\) ✅
C.\( h \) is undefined at \(x=2\)
D.Both are undefined at \(x=2\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The original fraction has a denominator that vanishes at \(x=2\), creating a hole in its graph. After canceling the common factor, the simplified expression \(x+2\) no longer shows that restriction, so it appears defined at \(x=2\). Consequently, the domains differ: \(g\) excludes \(2\) whereas \(h\) includes it.

Q10. Find the natural domain of \( f(x)=\sqrt{\dfrac{x-1}{x+2}} \).

A.\([1,\infty)\) ✅
B.\((1,\infty)\)
C.\((-\infty,-2]\cup[1,\infty)\)
D.\((-\infty,-2)\cup[1,\infty)\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The fraction must be non‑negative and the denominator cannot be zero. Solving \(\frac{x-1}{x+2}\ge0\) yields \(x\ge1\) (the zero at \(x=1\) is allowed) and automatically excludes \(x=-2\) because it lies outside the solution set. Hence the domain is \([1,\infty)\).

Q11. What is the natural domain of \( f(x)=\bigl(x^{2}-5x+6\bigr)^{1/3} \)?

A.All real numbers ✅
B.Values of \(x\) where the radicand is non‑negative
C.All real numbers except \(x=2\) and \(x=3\)
D.\(x>0\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A cube root is defined for any real radicand, unlike even roots that require non‑negative arguments. Since \(x^{2}-5x+6\) can take any real value, there is no restriction on \(x\). Therefore the natural domain consists of all real numbers.

Q12. Determine the natural domain of \( f(x)=\sqrt{\dfrac{x^{2}-4}{x-3}} \).

A.\([ -2,2]\cup(3,\infty)\) ✅
B.\(( -2,2)\cup(3,\infty)\)
C.\((-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty)\setminus\{3\}\)
D.\([ -2,2]\cup[3,\infty)\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Both numerator and denominator must keep the quotient non‑negative and the denominator cannot be zero. Solving \(\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-3}\ge0\) gives intervals \([-2,2]\) and \((3,\infty)\). The point \(x=3\) is excluded because it makes the denominator zero, while the zeros at \(-2\) and \(2\) are allowed, yielding the domain \([ -2,2]\cup(3,\infty)\).

Q13. For \( f(x)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{x-1}} \), what is its natural domain and does the expression have any removable discontinuities?

A.\([1,\infty)\); no holes ✅
B.\((0,\infty)\) excluding \(x=1\); hole at \(x=1\)
C.\((0,\infty)\); hole at \(x=1\)
D.\((0,\infty)\) excluding \(x=0\); hole at \(x=0\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Both square‑root terms require non‑negative arguments, giving \(x\ge1\). The denominator \(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{x-1}\) never vanishes for \(x\ge1\) because \(\sqrt{x}>\sqrt{x-1}\). Hence the function is defined for all \(x\ge1\) with no removable discontinuities, i.e., the domain is \([1,\infty)\).

Q14. Consider \( f(x)=\sqrt{\dfrac{x-4}{x^{2}-9}} \). After multiplying numerator and denominator by \(x+3\), how does the natural domain change?

A.Original domain: \((-3,3)\cup[4,\infty)\); after multiplication domain becomes all real numbers except \(x=\pm3\)
B.Original domain: \((-3,3)\cup[4,\infty)\); after multiplication domain remains unchanged ✅
C.Original domain: \((-3,3)\cup[4,\infty)\); after multiplication domain loses \(x=4\)
D.Original domain: \((-3,3)\cup[4,\infty)\); after multiplication domain adds \(x=4\) as a removable hole
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The original radicand \(\frac{x-4}{(x-3)(x+3)}\) is non‑negative for \((-3,3)\) and for \(x\ge4\); \(x=\pm3\) are excluded because they zero the denominator. Multiplying numerator and denominator by \(x+3\) introduces an extra factor \((x+3)^2\) in the denominator, which still only forbids \(x=-3\). No new restrictions appear, so the domain stays \((-3,3)\cup[4,\infty)\).
