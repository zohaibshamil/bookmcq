What is Algebraic functions definition and examples?

Definition:
Algebraic functions are functions that can be expressed using polynomial equations with integer exponents, roots, and arithmetic operations, such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and taking roots, but not involving transcendental operations like exponentials or logs.

Example:
\( f(x) = \sqrt{x^2+1} \) and \( g(x) = \frac{1}{x-2} \) are algebraic; \( h(x) = e^x \) is not algebraic.

Reason:
Algebraic functions cover a wide range of elementary functions, and their properties (like differentiability) are well-studied, making them foundational in calculus.

Q1. Which of the following expressions is an algebraic function?

A.\(f(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}-4}\) ✅
B.\(g(x)=\sin x\)
C.\(h(x)=e^{x}\)
D.\(k(x)=\ln x\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An algebraic function is built from polynomials using a finite number of algebraic operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and root extraction. The expression \(f(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}-4}\) uses only polynomial terms and a square‑root, which is an allowed root extraction, so it qualifies. The other choices involve transcendental functions (sine, exponential, logarithm) and therefore are not algebraic.

Q2. Given \(f(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}-4}\) and \(g(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{3}(x+2)^{2}\), which statement about their domains is correct?

A.Both have the same domain.
B.Domain of \(f\) is all real numbers, domain of \(g\) is all real numbers.
C.Domain of \(f\) is \((-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty)\) and \(g\) is all real numbers. ✅
D.Domain of \(g\) is \((-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty)\) and \(f\) is all real numbers.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The square‑root in \(f(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}-4}\) requires the radicand to be non‑negative, giving \(x^{2}-4\ge0\) or \(|x|\ge2\), so its domain is \((-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty)\). The polynomial expression for \(g(x)\) is defined for every real \(x\). Therefore the correct description is option C.

Q3. If a function \(h(x)=A\sin(Bx-C)\) has zeros at \(x=0\) and \(x=\pi/4\) and the amplitude is 3, what are the possible values of \(B\)?

A.\(B=4\) ✅
B.\(B=2\)
C.\(B=8\)
D.\(B=1\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Zeros of a sine function occur when its argument equals an integer multiple of \(\pi\): \(Bx-C=n\pi\). Setting \(x=0\) gives \(-C=n_1\pi\) and \(x=\pi/4\) gives \(B(\pi/4)-C=n_2\pi\). Subtracting yields \(B(\pi/4)= (n_2-n_1)\pi\), so \(B=4(n_2-n_1)\). The smallest positive integer difference gives \(B=4\), which matches option A.

Q4. Suppose \(p(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}-4}\) and \(q(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2}-4}}\). If a new function \(r(x)=p(x)^{\,q(x)}\) is defined, which of the following statements about the continuity of \(r\) at \(x=3\) is true?

A.\(r\) is continuous at \(x=3\). ✅
B.\(r\) has a removable discontinuity at \(x=3\).
C.\(r\) is discontinuous because \(q(x)\) is undefined at \(x=3\).
D.\(r\) is continuous for all \(x\) where \(p\) is defined.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: At \(x=3\) the radicand \(3^{2}-4=5\) is positive, so both \(p(3)=\sqrt5\) and \(q(3)=1/\sqrt5\) exist and are finite. Each component function is continuous at \(x=3\); the composition \(p^{\,q}\) of continuous functions is also continuous there. Hence \(r\) is continuous at \(x=3\).

Q5. Which of the following correctly describes the amplitude and period of \(y=2\sin 4x\)?

A.amplitude 2, period \(2\pi\)
B.amplitude 2, period \(\pi/2\) ✅
C.amplitude 4, period \(\pi/2\)
D.amplitude 4, period \(2\pi\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a sinusoid \(y=A\sin(Bx)\), the amplitude equals \(|A|\) and the period equals \(2\pi/|B|\). Here \(A=2\) gives an amplitude of 2, and \(B=4\) gives a period of \(2\pi/4=\pi/2\). Therefore the correct description is option B.

Q6. Consider the algebraic function \(f(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{3}(x+2)^{2}\). Which of the following describes its behavior as \(x\to -\infty\)?

A.\(f(x)\to -\infty\)
B.\(f(x)\to 0\)
C.\(f(x)\to +\infty\) ✅
D.\(f(x)\) oscillates without bound
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Expanding the expression yields \(f(x)=\frac{1}{3}x^{4}+ \frac{4}{3}x^{3}+ \frac{4}{3}x^{2}\). The leading term \(\frac{1}{3}x^{4}\) dominates for large \(|x|\) and is positive, so as \(x\to -\infty\) the function grows without bound in the positive direction, i.e., \(f(x)\to +\infty\).

Q7. For the function \(g(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}-4}\), determine the limit \(\lim_{x\to 2^{+}} g(x)\).

A.0 ✅
B.2
C.\(\sqrt{0}\)
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When \(x\) approaches 2 from the right, the radicand \(x^{2}-4\) approaches \(0^{+}\). The square‑root of a quantity tending to zero from above approaches zero. Hence \(\lim_{x\to 2^{+}}\sqrt{x^{2}-4}=0\), which corresponds to option A.

Q8. The amplitude of a sinusoidal function \(y=A\sin(Bx)\) is defined as:

A.|A| ✅
B.\(A^{2}\)
C.\(1/|A|\)
D.|B|
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Amplitude measures the maximum vertical displacement from the midline and is given by the absolute value of the coefficient multiplying the sine (or cosine) term. Therefore for \(y=A\sin(Bx)\) the amplitude equals \(|A|\), matching option A.

Q9. If the constant \(B\) in \(y=A\cos(Bx)\) is negative, how does it affect the period of the function?

A.Period becomes \(2\pi/B\) (negative value).
B.Period is unchanged, still \(2\pi/|B|\). ✅
C.Period doubles.
D.No periodic behavior.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The period of a sinusoid depends on the absolute value of the angular frequency: \(\text{period}=2\pi/|B|\). Changing the sign of \(B\) reflects the graph horizontally but does not alter the distance required for one full cycle. Hence the period remains \(2\pi/|B|\), which is option B.

Q10. A function is defined as \(h(x)=A\sin(Bx-C)\) with amplitude 5, period \(\pi\), and a phase shift of \(\pi/6\) to the right. Which ordered triple \((A,B,C)\) satisfies these conditions?

A.\((5,2,\pi/3)\) ✅
B.\((5,2,-\pi/3)\)
C.\((5,4,\pi/6)\)
D.\((5,4,-\pi/6)\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Amplitude gives \(|A|=5\). Period \(\pi\) implies \(2\pi/|B|=\pi\) so \(|B|=2\). A rightward shift of \(\pi/6\) means \(C/B=\pi/6\); with \(B=2\) this yields \(C=2\cdot\pi/6=\pi/3\). Thus the triple \((5,2,\pi/3)\) meets all requirements, which is option A.

Q11. Consider the composition \(F(x)=\sqrt{\,\bigl(\frac{x^{2}}{3}(x+2)^{2}\bigr)^{2}-4}\). Which of the following statements about the domain of \(F\) is true?

A.Domain is all real numbers.
B.Domain consists of \(x\) such that \(\frac{x^{2}}{3}(x+2)^{2}\ge 2\) or \(\le -2\). ✅
C.Domain is \((-\infty,-2]\cup[2,\infty)\).
D.Domain is empty.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Inside the outer square‑root we have \(\bigl(\frac{x^{2}}{3}(x+2)^{2}\bigr)^{2}-4\). For the expression to be non‑negative we need \(|\frac{x^{2}}{3}(x+2)^{2}|\ge2\). Since \(\frac{x^{2}}{3}(x+2)^{2}\ge0\) for all real \(x\), the condition reduces to \(\frac{x^{2}}{3}(x+2)^{2}\ge2\). Hence the domain is precisely the set of \(x\) satisfying that inequality, which is described by option B.

Q12. If \(f(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}-4}\) and \(g(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2}-4}}\), which of the following statements about the product \(h(x)=f(x)g(x)\) is correct?

A.\(h(x)=1\) for all \(x\) in the domain. ✅
B.\(h(x)=0\) for all \(x\) in the domain.
C.\(h(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}-4}\).
D.Undefined everywhere.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Within the common domain \(|x|\ge2\), the product simplifies algebraically: \(f(x)g(x)=\sqrt{x^{2}-4}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2}-4}}=1\). The result is constant 1 wherever the original functions are defined, so option A accurately describes the product.
