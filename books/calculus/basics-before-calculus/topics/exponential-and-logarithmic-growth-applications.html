Q1. If \(y = e^{x}\) exceeds 1000, which inequality must hold for \(x\)?

🔹 A. \(x > \ln 1000\)
🔹 B. \(x < \ln 1000\)
🔹 C. \(x > 1000\)
🔹 D. \(x < 1000\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since \(e^{x}\) is strictly increasing, the inequality \(e^{x}>1000\) is equivalent to \(x>\ln 1000\). Any smaller \(x\) would give a value of \(e^{x}\) less than 1000, so the only valid statement is \(x>\ln 1000\).

Q2. Given that \(\ln x > 5\), which statement is always true?

🔹 A. \(x > 5\)
🔹 B. \(x > e^{5}\)
🔹 C. \(x < e^{5}\)
🔹 D. \(x < 5\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The natural logarithm function is monotonic increasing, so \(\ln x > 5\) implies \(x\) must be greater than the number whose logarithm is 5, namely \(e^{5}\). Any value less than \(e^{5}\) would produce a logarithm at most 5, contradicting the premise.

Q3. Suppose \(f(x)=e^{x}\) and \(g(x)=\ln x\). If for some \(a>0\) we have \(f(a)=g(a^{2})\), which must be true about \(a\)?

🔹 A. \(a>1\)
🔹 B. \(a=1\)
🔹 C. \(0<a<1\)
🔹 D. No solution

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Setting \(e^{a}=\ln(a^{2})\) leads to \(e^{a}=2\ln a\). For \(a>0\), the left side grows exponentially while the right side grows only logarithmically, and at \(a=1\) the equality fails. Consequently the equation has no real solution, making “No solution” the correct choice.

Q4. If the function \(h(x)=\ln(e^{x}+1)\) is increasing for all real \(x\), what can be inferred about its derivative \(h'(x)\)?

🔹 A. \(h'(x)=e^{x}+1\)
🔹 B. \(h'(x)=\frac{1}{e^{x}+1}\)
🔹 C. \(h'(x)=\frac{e^{x}}{e^{x}+1}>0\)
🔹 D. \(h'(x)=\ln(e^{x}+1)\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating gives \(h'(x)=\frac{e^{x}}{e^{x}+1}\). Because both numerator and denominator are positive for all real \(x\), the fraction is positive, confirming that \(h(x)\) is indeed increasing everywhere. The other options either misrepresent the derivative or give a non‑positive expression.

Q5. Which function grows faster as \(x\to\infty\): \(e^{x}\) or \(x^{5}\)?

🔹 A. \(x^{5}\) grows faster
🔹 B. \(e^{x}\) grows faster
🔹 C. Both grow at the same rate
🔹 D. Cannot be compared

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Exponential growth outpaces any polynomial. As \(x\) becomes large, the ratio \(\frac{e^{x}}{x^{5}}\) tends to infinity, showing that \(e^{x}\) dominates \(x^{5}\). Hence \(e^{x}\) grows faster, which is captured by option B.

Q6. Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\ln x}{e^{x}}\).

🔹 A. \(\infty\)
🔹 B. 1
🔹 C. Does not exist
🔹 D. 0

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The exponential function grows much more rapidly than the logarithm. Applying L'Hôpital's Rule once gives \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1/x}{e^{x}}=0\) because the numerator approaches zero while the denominator grows without bound. Therefore the original limit equals 0.

Q7. For which value of \(k\) does the function \(f(x)=e^{x}-k\ln x\) have exactly one critical point on \((0,\infty)\)?

🔹 A. k = 0
🔹 B. k < 0
🔹 C. Any positive real number
🔹 D. No such k

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Setting the derivative to zero yields \(e^{x}=k/x\) or \(k=x e^{x}\). The function \(x e^{x}\) is strictly increasing from 0 to \(\infty\), so for every positive \(k\) there is exactly one solution, giving a single critical point. Hence any positive \(k\) works.

Q8. Consider \(p(x)=e^{2x}\) and \(q(x)=\ln(x^{2})\). Which statement correctly describes their relative growth for large \(x\)?

🔹 A. \(q(x)\) eventually dominates \(p(x)\)
🔹 B. Neither dominates the other
🔹 C. They grow at comparable rates
🔹 D. \(p(x)\) eventually dominates \(q(x)\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Because \(e^{2x}\) grows exponentially while \(\ln(x^{2})=2\ln x\) grows only logarithmically, the exponential term outpaces the logarithmic term for sufficiently large \(x\). Thus \(p(x)\) dominates \(q(x)\) as \(x\to\infty\).

Q9. Solve for \(x\): \(e^{2x}=7\).

🔹 A. \(x = \ln 7\)
🔹 B. \(x = \frac{1}{2}\ln 7\)
🔹 C. \(x = \frac{1}{2}\ln 14\)
🔹 D. \(x = \ln \sqrt{7}\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Taking natural logs of both sides gives \(2x=\ln 7\). Dividing by 2 yields \(x=\frac{1}{2}\ln 7\). The other options either miss the factor of 2 or misapply logarithmic identities.

Q10. If \(y=\ln(x)\) is shifted 3 units up, what is the new equation?

🔹 A. \(y = \ln(x+3)\)
🔹 B. \(y = \ln(x-3)+3\)
🔹 C. \(y = e^{x}+3\)
🔹 D. \(y = \ln(x)+3\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A vertical shift adds a constant to the entire function. Raising the graph by 3 units changes the equation to \(y=\ln(x)+3\). The other choices represent horizontal shifts or unrelated transformations, which do not match a pure upward translation.

Q11. A population follows \(P(t)=P_0 e^{kt}\). If it doubles in 5 years, what is the expression for \(k\) using natural logarithms?

🔹 A. \(k = 5\ln 2\)
🔹 B. \(k = \frac{2}{\ln 5}\)
🔹 C. \(k = \frac{\ln 2}{5}\)
🔹 D. \(k = \ln(2/5)\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Doubling means \(P(t)=2P_0\). Substituting gives \(2P_0=P_0 e^{k\cdot5}\) → \(e^{5k}=2\). Taking natural logs: \(5k=\ln 2\) → \(k=\frac{\ln 2}{5}\). This isolates \(k\) in terms of \(\ln\).

Q12. Which expression represents the inverse of \(f(x)=e^{x}\)?

🔹 A. \(f^{-1}(x)=e^{x}\)
🔹 B. \(f^{-1}(x)=\ln x\)
🔹 C. \(f^{-1}(x)=\frac{1}{e^{x}}\)
🔹 D. \(f^{-1}(x)=\log_{10} x\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The inverse function reverses the effect of \(e^{x}\). Solving \(y=e^{x}\) for \(x\) yields \(x=\ln y\). Hence the inverse is \(f^{-1}(x)=\ln x\). The other options either repeat the original function or use unrelated operations.

Q13. What is the domain of the natural logarithm function \(\ln x\)?

🔹 A. \(x\ge 0\)
🔹 B. All real numbers
🔹 C. \(x<0\)
🔹 D. \(x>0\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The natural logarithm is defined only for positive arguments because \(\ln x\) represents the exponent to which \(e\) must be raised to obtain \(x\). Therefore the domain is the set of all real numbers greater than zero, i.e., \(x>0\).
