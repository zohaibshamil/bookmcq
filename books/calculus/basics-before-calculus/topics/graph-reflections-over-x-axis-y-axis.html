What is Graph reflections over x axis y axis?

Definition:
Graph reflections flip the graph over an axis: y=−f(x) y = -f(x) y=−f(x) reflects over the x-axis (changing signs of outputs), while y=f(−x) y = f(-x) y=f(−x) reflects over the y-axis (changing signs of inputs), mirroring the original graph.

Example:
For f(x)=x3 f(x) = x^3 f(x)=x3, y=−x3 y = -x^3 y=−x3 is a reflection over x-axis, and y=(−x)3=−x3 y = (-x)^3 = -x^3 y=(−x)3=−x3 is same here, but for f(x)=x2 f(x)=x^2 f(x)=x2, y=(−x)2=x2 y = (-x)^2 = x^2 y=(−x)2=x2 (no change due to evenness).

Reason:
Reflections are used in optics, signal processing, and symmetry analysis, helping to visualize inverse relationships or transformed data.
