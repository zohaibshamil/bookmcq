What is Natural exponential function e^x?

Definition:
The natural exponential function \( f(x) = e^x \) uses Euler's number \( e \approx 2.71828 \), which is the unique base where the derivative equals the function itself, making it the most important exponential function in calculus and continuous growth.

Example:
\( e^2 \approx 7.389 \), and its graph passes through (0,1), with slope 1 at \( x=0 \), and increases faster than any polynomial.

Reason:
\( e^x \) is central in natural processes like population growth, radioactive decay, and compound interest, due to its self-derivative property simplifying differential equations.
