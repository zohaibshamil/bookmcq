📝 Scale and units in graphing functions (12 MCQs)

Numerical Scale and Measurement on Coordinate Axes

Scale and units determine how numerical values are represented along the horizontal and vertical axes of a function graph. Scale specifies the distance corresponding to each numerical increment, while units identify the quantities being measured. These choices support accurate interpretation of mathematical models and become essential in calculus when graph slopes, rates of change, and accumulated areas carry physical dimensions.

Why Equal Spacing and Consistent Units Matter

A graph can be visually misleading when its numerical intervals are uneven or its axes use unclear measurements. Equal distances on a linear axis must represent equal numerical changes. Different scales may be used on the two axes, but each must remain consistent along its own axis so that plotted relationships are not distorted.

Selecting Axis Scales and Labelling Quantities

Identify the range of input and output values, choose convenient numerical intervals, and assign a consistent scale to each axis. Label the horizontal and vertical quantities with their units. Plot ordered pairs using these scales, and retain the distinction between numerical values and physical measurements when interpreting slopes or changes.

Plotting Distance Against Time with Correct Units

Suppose a vehicle travels according to \(s(t)=60t\), where \(s\) is measured in kilometres and \(t\) in hours. Plot \((0,0)\), \((1,60)\), and \((2,120)\). A horizontal scale of one hour per division and a vertical scale of 30 kilometres per division produce a consistent straight-line graph with slope 60 kilometres per hour.

Scale Choices in Rates of Change and Applications

Correct units make graphical slopes physically meaningful: distance divided by time gives speed, while output divided by input may describe marginal quantities. Scale selection also affects how clearly small changes and turning points appear. Logarithmic scales can represent very large ranges, but ordinary geometric interpretations of slope and area require additional care on transformed axes.

Graphing Errors Caused by Scale and Unit Confusion

'Both axes must use the same numerical scale' is false because different quantities may need different intervals. 'A graph has no units' ignores measured variables and dimensional interpretation. 'A steeper appearance always means a larger physical rate' is incorrect because visual slope depends on the chosen axis scales as well as the underlying numerical relationship.

Scale and Units in Graph-Based Examination Questions

Practise selecting sensible intervals, plotting accurately, and labelling quantities with units. Read slopes using the numerical values represented by each division rather than measuring visual steepness alone. For applied calculus questions, state derivative units explicitly and distinguish a graph's coordinate scale from the units of the quantities shown on its axes.

Key Terms for Graph Scales and Units

  • Scale - Numerical value represented by a division.
  • Axis - Reference line for graph coordinates.
  • Unit - Standard used to measure a quantity.
  • Gradient - Change in vertical value per horizontal change.
  • Linear scale - Scale with equal numerical increments.
  • Dimensional analysis - Checking consistency of physical units.

📝 All Scale and units in graphing functions MCQs

Q1. When graphing a circle, what is the visual effect of using a longer unit length on the x‑axis than on the y‑axis?

A.The circle appears stretched horizontally.
B.The circle appears stretched vertically.
C.The circle becomes an ellipse squashed vertically. ✅
D.The circle remains a perfect circle.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Because the horizontal units are longer, each unit on the x‑axis covers more distance than a unit on the y‑axis. Points that should be equally distant from the center in both directions are plotted farther apart horizontally, turning the true circle into an ellipse that is compressed vertically.

Q2. A graph of \(y = x^{2}\) for \(-10 \le x \le 10\) is drawn with equal unit lengths on both axes, but the y‑axis is then compressed uniformly by a factor \(k\) so that the entire curve fits on the same paper size. Which value of \(k\) is required?

A.k = 0.1
B.k = 0.5
C.k = 0.05
D.k = 0.2 ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The y‑values range from 0 to 100, while the x‑range spans 20 units. To make the vertical extent match the horizontal extent (20 units), the y‑axis must be scaled down by 20/100 = 0.2. Thus a compression factor of 0.2 allows the whole parabola to occupy the same paper height as its width.

Q3. If the unit length on the x‑axis is doubled while the y‑axis unit remains unchanged, how does the slope of a line originally having slope 2 appear on the new graph?

A.It appears as slope 1 ✅
B.It appears as slope 4
C.It appears unchanged
D.It appears as slope 0.5
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Doubling the horizontal unit length stretches the x‑axis, so a given horizontal change now covers twice as many units. The visual rise‑over‑run ratio therefore halves, turning the original slope of 2 into an apparent slope of 1 on the resized graph.

Q4. On a plot where 1 cm represents 2 seconds on the x‑axis and 1 cm represents 1 meter on the y‑axis, a point moving at constant speed 3 m/s is drawn. What is the visual slope (rise over run) of its trajectory?

A.6 ✅
B.3
C.1.5
D.0.5
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: In graph units, a 1 cm horizontal step corresponds to 2 seconds, and a 1 cm vertical step corresponds to 1 meter. At 3 m/s, in 2 seconds the point moves 6 meters, which is 6 cm vertically. Thus the rise/run ratio is 6 cm / 1 cm = 6, giving a visual slope of 6.

Q5. Two graphs display the same quadratic function \(y = x^{2}\) over \([-5,5]\). Graph A uses equal unit lengths on both axes; Graph B compresses the y‑axis by a factor of 0.5. Which statement about the area under the curve from \(-5\) to \(5\) is true?

A.Both graphs show the same numerical area.
B.Graph A shows twice the area of Graph B.
C.Graph B shows half the area of Graph A.
D.The areas cannot be compared because scaling changes units. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Area under a curve is measured in square units that depend on the scaling of each axis. When the y‑axis is compressed, the vertical dimension of each infinitesimal rectangle is halved, so the numerical area value changes. Therefore the two numerical areas are not directly comparable.

Q6. When plotting \(y = \sin(x)\) for \(0 \le x \le 4\pi\), which scaling choice best preserves the wave shape while keeping the graph readable?

A.Equal units, 1 cm per radian on x and 1 cm per unit on y.
B.Stretch x‑axis by factor 2, keep y‑axis equal. ✅
C.Compress y‑axis by factor 0.5, keep x‑axis equal.
D.Compress both axes by factor 0.5.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The sine wave spans roughly 12.6 cm horizontally if 1 cm per radian is used, making the plot too wide. Stretching the x‑axis by a factor of 2 reduces the horizontal length to about 6.3 cm, preserving the wave’s proportions while keeping the vertical amplitude unchanged, so the shape remains clear.

Q7. A parabola \(y = -x^{2} + 4\) is plotted with equal axis scales. If the y‑axis is stretched by factor 3, how does the visual prominence of the maximum point change compared to the minimum point of \(y = x^{2} - 4\) plotted with the same stretch?

A.The maximum appears three times taller than the minimum appears deep. ✅
B.Both appear equally prominent.
C.The maximum appears less prominent than the minimum.
D.The stretch affects only the maximum, not the minimum.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Vertical stretching multiplies every vertical distance from the x‑axis by the same factor. The peak of the first parabola is 4 units above the axis; after stretching, it rises 12 units, making it appear three times taller. The depth of the second parabola is also multiplied by 3, so both features are amplified proportionally.

Q8. Why is it important to use equal unit lengths on both axes when comparing geometric shapes such as circles or squares?

A.It ensures the numerical values are identical.
B.It preserves the true geometric proportions of the shapes. ✅
C.It makes the graph easier to print.
D.It eliminates the need for a legend.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Equal axis scales mean that a unit of length represents the same physical distance in both directions. Consequently, shapes are drawn with their authentic proportions; a circle remains a circle and a square retains equal sides. Any distortion would misrepresent the geometry and lead to incorrect visual conclusions.

Q9. You need to graph the function \(y = e^{x}\) for \(-2 \le x \le 2\). Which strategy best selects axis scales to display the rapid growth while keeping the graph interpretable?

A.Use equal unit lengths on both axes.
B.Compress the y‑axis by a factor of 0.2.
C.Choose a logarithmic scale for the y‑axis. ✅
D.Extend the x‑axis length twice as much as the y‑axis.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The exponential function grows dramatically, so a linear y‑scale would force most of the curve into a tiny region. A logarithmic y‑scale linearizes the growth, spreading the points more evenly across the plot while preserving the shape’s essential features, making the graph far more readable.

Q10. If a graph is vertically stretched by a factor \(k\), how does the derivative \(dy/dx\) at any point change?

A.It is multiplied by \(k\). ✅
B.It is divided by \(k\).
C.It remains unchanged.
D.It is multiplied by \(k^{2}\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The derivative represents the instantaneous rate of change, which is the ratio of a small vertical change to a small horizontal change. Stretching the vertical direction multiplies the numerator of this ratio by \(k\) while leaving the denominator unchanged, so the derivative itself is multiplied by \(k\).

Q11. Applying a scaling transformation that multiplies the x‑coordinate by \(a\) and the y‑coordinate by \(b\) to the circle \(x^{2}+y^{2}=r^{2}\) yields which equation, and under what condition does the image remain a circle?

A.\( (x/a)^{2}+(y/b)^{2}=r^{2}\); remains a circle if \(a=b\). ✅
B.\( (x a)^{2}+(y b)^{2}=r^{2}\); remains a circle if \(a=b\).
C.\( (x/a)^{2}+(y/b)^{2}=r^{2}\); remains a circle for any \(a,b\).
D.\( (x a)^{2}+(y b)^{2}=r^{2}\); remains a circle if \(ab=1\).
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Replacing \(x\) with \(x/a\) and \(y\) with \(y/b\) reflects the scaling: the new coordinates satisfy \((x/a)^{2}+(y/b)^{2}=r^{2}\). This describes an ellipse unless the scaling factors are equal, i.e., \(a=b\); only then does the equation reduce to a circle of radius \(r\) in the transformed coordinates.

Q12. Two plots show the same data set: Plot 1 uses equal axis scales; Plot 2 uses unequal scales that preserve the area under the curve. Which plot gives a more accurate visual comparison of instantaneous rates of change?

A.Plot 1, because equal scales preserve slopes. ✅
B.Plot 2, because area preservation maintains integrals.
C.Both are equally accurate.
D.Neither; a third method is required.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Instantaneous rates of change correspond to the slope of the curve at a point, which depends directly on the relative scaling of the axes. Equal scales keep the geometric slope proportional to the actual derivative, whereas unequal scales distort this ratio, making Plot 1 the more reliable visual tool for comparing rates.
