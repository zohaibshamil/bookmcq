What is Derivative of ln x and log base b?

Definition:
The derivative of the natural logarithm function $\ln(x)$ is uniquely simple, given by $\frac{1}{x}$ for $x > 0$. For a logarithm with base $b$, denoted $\log_b(x)$, the derivative is $\frac{1}{x \ln(b)}$. This relationship arises because $\log_b(x)$ can be rewritten as $\frac{\ln(x)}{\ln(b)}$, making $\frac{1}{\ln(b)}$ a constant multiplier in the differentiation process.

Example:
Differentiate $y = \ln(5x)$. Using the chain rule, $y' = \frac{1}{5x} \cdot 5 = \frac{1}{x}$. Alternatively, for $y = \log_{10}(x)$, the derivative is $y' = \frac{1}{x \ln(10)}$, which is approximately $\frac{0.434}{x}$.

Reason:
Knowing these specific forms allows for quick calculation of rates of change in scientific formulas. The simplicity of $\frac{1}{x}$ for natural logs makes them preferred in calculus, while the base conversion factor handles other logarithmic bases efficiently.

Q1. What is the derivative of \\(\\ln x\\) for \\(x>0\\)?

A.\\(1/x\\) ✅
B.\\(x\\)
C.\\(\\ln x\\)
D.\\(0\\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The derivative of the natural logarithm follows directly from the definition of the logarithmic function. Differentiating \\(\\ln x\\) yields \\(\\frac{d}{dx}\\ln x = \\frac{1}{x}\\). This result holds for every positive \\(x\\), confirming option A as the correct choice.

📖 Explanation: Using the change‑of‑base formula \\(\\log_{b}x=\\frac{\\ln x}{\\ln b}\\) and differentiating, we obtain \\(\\frac{d}{dx}\\log_{b}x = \\frac{1}{\\ln b}\\cdot\\frac{1}{x}=\\frac{1}{x\\ln b}\\). This matches option C, while the other choices do not reflect the correct application of the chain rule.
