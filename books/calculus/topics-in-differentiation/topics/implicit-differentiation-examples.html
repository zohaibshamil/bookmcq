What is Implicit differentiation examples?

Definition:
Implicit differentiation is a technique used to find the derivative dydx\frac{dy}{dx}dxdy​ when a function is defined implicitly by an equation involving both xxx and yyy. Instead of solving for yyy first, we differentiate both sides with respect to xxx, treating yyy as a function of xxx and applying the chain rule to terms containing yyy.

Example:
For x2+y2=25x^2 + y^2 = 25x2+y2=25, differentiate both sides: ddx(x2)+ddx(y2)=ddx(25)\frac{d}{dx}(x^2) + \frac{d}{dx}(y^2) = \frac{d}{dx}(25)dxd​(x2)+dxd​(y2)=dxd​(25). This yields 2x+2ydydx=02x + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 02x+2ydxdy​=0. Solving for dydx\frac{dy}{dx}dxdy​ gives dydx=−xy\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y}dxdy​=−yx​.

Reason:
This method simplifies finding derivatives for equations where isolating yyy is difficult or impossible, allowing us to compute slopes and tangent lines directly from the original implicit equation without algebraic manipulation.
