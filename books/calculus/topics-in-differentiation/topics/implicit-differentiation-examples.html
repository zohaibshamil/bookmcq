What is Implicit differentiation examples?

Implicit differentiation is a technique used to find \( \frac{dy}{dx} \) when \( y \) is not isolated, by differentiating both sides of an equation like \( x^2 + y^2 = r^2 \) with respect to \( x \) and applying the chain rule to terms containing \( y \).

Q1. Given the equation \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\), if at a point the slope \(dy/dx\) equals 0, what is the y‑coordinate of that point?

A. 0
B. 5
C. -5
D. Cannot be determined ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating implicitly gives \(2x + 2y\,dy/dx = 0\) so \(dy/dx = -x/y\). Setting \(dy/dx = 0\) forces \(x = 0\). Substituting into the original equation yields \(y = \pm5\). Since both satisfy the condition, the y‑coordinate is not uniquely determined, hence option D.

Q2. For the curve defined by \(xy = 6\), if \(x\) increases, what can be said about the behavior of \(dy/dx\)?

A. It becomes more negative
B. It becomes more positive
C. It stays zero
D. Cannot be determined ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Implicit differentiation gives \(y + x\,dy/dx = 0\) so \(dy/dx = -y/x\). As \(x\) grows, the magnitude of \(-y/x\) depends on the sign of \(y\), which is not fixed by the information provided. Therefore the effect on \(dy/dx\) cannot be determined from the given data.

Q3. Suppose a curve satisfies \(e^{xy} + y = 3\). If at a point \(x = 0\), what is the value of \(dy/dx\)?

A. -2 ✅
B. -1
C. 0
D. 2

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating implicitly: \(e^{xy}(y + x\,dy/dx) + dy/dx = 0\). At \(x=0\) we have \(e^{0}=1\) and the original equation gives \(y = 2\). Substituting, \(1\cdot (2 + 0) + dy/dx = 0\) leads to \(dy/dx = -2\). Hence option A is correct.

Q4. For the implicit function defined by \(\sin(x+y) = x\), determine the value of \(dy/dx\) at the point \((0,0)\).

A. Positive
B. Negative
C. Zero ✅
D. Undefined

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating gives \(\cos(x+y)(1 + dy/dx) = 1\). At \((0,0)\) we have \(\cos0 = 1\), so \(1 + dy/dx = 1\) and thus \(dy/dx = 0\). The derivative is zero, making option C correct.

Q5. Consider the curve \(x^3 + y^3 = 6xy\). Show that at any point where \(x = y \neq 0\), the second derivative \(d^2y/dx^2\) equals 1. Which statement is correct?

A. true ✅
B. false
C. Cannot determine
D. Depends on the point

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

First differentiate: \(3x^2 + 3y^2\,dy/dx = 6(y + x\,dy/dx)\). Setting \(x=y\) yields \(dy/dx = -1\). Differentiating again and simplifying using the original relation gives \(d^2y/dx^2 = 1\) for all non‑zero points where \(x=y\). Hence the statement is true.

Q6. For the curve defined by \(x^2 y + \ln y = 4\), if at a point \(y = 1\), determine the sign of the second derivative \(d^2y/dx^2\).

A. Positive
B. Negative
C. Zero
D. Cannot determine ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

With \(y=1\) the first derivative simplifies to \(dy/dx = -2x/(x^2+1)\). Computing the second derivative yields a rational expression whose sign depends on the value of \(x\). Since no specific \(x\) is given, the sign cannot be conclusively determined, so option D is appropriate.

Q7. Compare the implicit differentiation of \(x^2 + y^2 = r^2\) with the explicit differentiation of \(y = \sqrt{r^2 - x^2}\). Which statement is correct?

A. Implicit gives an extra negative sign
B. Both give the same derivative ✅
C. Explicit derivative is undefined at \(x=0\)
D. Implicit derivative is simpler

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating implicitly gives \(2x + 2y\,dy/dx = 0\) leading to \(dy/dx = -x/y\). Explicitly differentiating \(y = \sqrt{r^2 - x^2}\) also yields \(dy/dx = -x/\sqrt{r^2 - x^2}\), which is the same as \(-x/y\). Thus both methods give the same derivative.

Q8. Evaluate which method is more efficient for finding \(dy/dx\) for the curve \(x e^{y} + y e^{x} = 1\).

A. Implicit differentiation ✅
B. Solving for \(y\) first
C. Numerical approximation
D. Graphical method

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The equation mixes \(x\) and \(y\) in both exponential and linear terms, making it impractical to solve explicitly for \(y\). Directly applying implicit differentiation to each term avoids solving for \(y\) and yields a straightforward expression for \(dy/dx\), so the implicit method is the most efficient.

Q9. Analyze the effect of differentiating the equation \(x^2 y + y^3 = 7\) twice implicitly. Which term will involve \((dy/dx)^2\) in the second‑derivative expression?

A. The \(x^2\) term
B. The \(y^3\) term
C. Both terms ✅
D. Neither term

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When the first derivative is taken, the term \(x^2 y\) contributes a factor of \(dy/dx\). Differentiating again produces a \((dy/dx)^2\) piece from the derivative of the \(y\) factor in both the \(x^2 y\) term and the \(y^3\) term. Hence both terms generate a squared derivative component.

Q10. Given the implicit curve \(\cos(xy) = x + y\), determine which statement about \(dy/dx\) at the point \((0,0)\) is true.

A. Undefined
B. Zero
C. One
D. -1 ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating yields \(-\sin(xy)(y + x\,dy/dx) = 1 + dy/dx\). At \((0,0)\) the sine term vanishes, leaving \(0 = 1 + dy/dx\). Solving gives \(dy/dx = -1\); therefore the derivative exists and equals \(-1\).

Q11. For the curve \(x^4 + y^4 = 16\), which expression correctly represents the second derivative \(d^2y/dx^2\) in terms of \(x\) and \(y\)?

A. \(-\frac{48 x^2}{y^7}\) ✅
B. \(\frac{48 x^2}{y^7}\)
C. \(-\frac{12 x^2}{y^5}\)
D. \(\frac{12 x^2}{y^5}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

First differentiate: \(4x^3 + 4y^3\,dy/dx = 0\) giving \(dy/dx = -x^3/y^3\). Differentiating again and substituting the original relation \(x^4 + y^4 = 16\) simplifies the result to \(-48x^2/y^7\). Thus option A is the correct formula.

Q12. Consider the implicit function defined by \(\ln(x^2 + y^2) = xy\). Which statement about the curvature at the point \((1,1)\) is correct?

A. Curvature is zero
B. Curvature is positive
C. Curvature is negative
D. Cannot be determined without higher derivatives ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating once yields an identity at \((1,1)\), leaving \(dy/dx\) undetermined. Because the first derivative is not uniquely defined, the second derivative—and hence the curvature—cannot be computed from the given information alone. Therefore the curvature cannot be determined.

Q13. Apply implicit differentiation to find \(dy/dx\) for the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 9\).

A. \(-x/y\) ✅
B. \(-y/x\)
C. \(x/y\)
D. \(y/x\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating implicitly gives \(2x + 2y\,dy/dx = 0\). Solving for \(dy/dx\) yields \(-x/y\). This is the standard derivative for a circle centered at the origin.

Q14. Explain why implicit differentiation is necessary for the curve \(x^{y}=2\).

A. Because solving for \(y\) is impossible
B. Because the derivative of \(x^{y}\) is not standard
C. Because \(y\) appears both as exponent and base
D. All of the above ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The relation \(x^{y}=2\) mixes \(x\) and \(y\) in a way that prevents solving for \(y\) explicitly. Additionally, the derivative of a variable exponent requires the chain rule and logarithmic differentiation. Thus all listed reasons make implicit differentiation essential.

Q15. For the relation \(x^2 - y^2 = 4\), which option best describes the geometric nature of the curve?

A. Circle
B. Hyperbola ✅
C. Parabola
D. Ellipse

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The equation \(x^2 - y^2 = 4\) matches the standard form of a hyperbola \(\frac{x^2}{4} - \frac{y^2}{4} = 1\). Hence the curve is a hyperbola.

Q16. Synthesize the steps required to find \(\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}\) for an implicitly defined function.

A. Differentiate once, solve for dy/dx, differentiate again, substitute ✅
B. Differentiate twice directly
C. Solve for y first then differentiate twice
D. No need to differentiate twice

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The systematic approach is: (1) differentiate the original relation once, (2) solve the resulting equation for \(dy/dx\), (3) differentiate that expression again with respect to \(x\), and (4) substitute any previously found expressions to simplify. This yields the second derivative.

Q17. Given the implicit equation \(x^3 + y^3 - 3xy = 0\) (the folium of Descartes), determine the behavior of \(dy/dx\) as the curve approaches the origin along the line \(y = kx\). Which statement is correct?

A. \(dy/dx\) approaches \(-1\) for any \(k\)
B. \(dy/dx\) approaches \(-k^2\)
C. \(dy/dx\) approaches \(-k\) ✅
D. \(dy/dx\) is undefined

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating gives \((y^2 - x)dy/dx = y - x^2\). Substituting \(y = kx\) leads to \((k^2x^2 - x)dy/dx = kx - x^2\). As \(x \to 0\), the dominant terms give \( -x\,dy/dx \approx kx\), so \(dy/dx \to -k\). Hence the derivative tends to \(-k\).

Q18. For the implicit function defined by \(x^2 y + y^3 = 6\), evaluate the limit of \(dy/dx\) as \(x \to 0^+\) assuming \(y>0\).

A. 0 ✅
B. #NAME?
C. \(\infty\)
D. Does not exist

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

From implicit differentiation, \(dy/dx = -2xy/(x^2 + 3y^2)\). With \(y>0\) fixed, as \(x\) approaches zero from the positive side the numerator tends to zero while the denominator approaches \(3y^2>0\). Consequently the fraction tends to 0, so the limit is 0.

Q19. What is the general formula for differentiating an implicit function \(F(x,y)=0\) with respect to \(x\)?

A. \(-F_x/F_y\) ✅
B. \(-F_y/F_x\)
C. \(F_x/F_y\)
D. \(F_y/F_x\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Treating \(y\) as a function of \(x\), differentiate \(F(x,y)=0\) to obtain \(F_x + F_y\,dy/dx = 0\). Solving for \(dy/dx\) gives \(dy/dx = -F_x/F_y\). This compact formula is the cornerstone of implicit differentiation.

Q20. In implicit differentiation, which rule is applied to differentiate a product of functions of \(x\) and \(y\)?

A. Product rule ✅
B. Chain rule
C. Quotient rule
D. L'Hôpital's rule

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When a term involves a product such as \(x\,y\) or \(x^2 y\), the product rule \(d(uv) = u\,dv + v\,du\) is used, remembering that \(y\) depends on \(x\). This rule is essential for correctly handling mixed terms in implicit equations.
