📝 All Derivatives of Logarithmic Functions MCQs

Q1. What is the derivative of \( \ln x \) for \( x>0 \)?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{x} \)
🔹 B. \( x \)
🔹 C. \( \ln x \)
🔹 D. \( e^{x} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using the definition of derivative for the natural logarithm, one obtains \( \frac{d}{dx}\ln x = \lim_{h\to0}\frac{\ln(x+h)-\ln x}{h}= \frac{1}{x} \) for all \( x>0 \). Hence option A is correct.

Q2. Given \( f(x)=\log_{2}(x^{3}) \), which expression correctly represents \( f'(x) \)?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{3x\ln 2} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{3\ln 2}{x} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{3}{x\ln 2} \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{1}{x\ln 2^{3}} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Rewrite \( f(x)=\log_{2}(x^{3}) = \frac{\ln(x^{3})}{\ln 2}= \frac{3\ln x}{\ln 2}\). Differentiating gives \( f'(x)=\frac{3}{x\ln 2}\). This matches option C, confirming the logical deduction from the properties of logarithms and the constant‑multiple rule.

Q3. How does the derivative of \( \log_{10} x \) compare to the derivative of \( \ln x \)?

🔹 A. Both are \( \frac{1}{x} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{1}{x\ln 10} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{\ln 10}{x} \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{10}{x} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The general formula \( \frac{d}{dx}\log_{b}x = \frac{1}{x\ln b}\) applies. For base 10, \( \ln 10\) is a constant, so the derivative becomes \( \frac{1}{x\ln 10}\). This differs from \( \frac{1}{x}\) for the natural log, making option B correct.

Q4. Apply the chain rule to find \( \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[\ln(\sqrt{x^{2}+1})\bigr] \).

🔹 A. \( \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+1}} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x^{2}+1}} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{2x}{x^{2}+1} \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{x}{x^{2}+1} \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Let \( u=\sqrt{x^{2}+1}\). Then \( \ln u\)' = \( \frac{1}{u}u'\). Since \( u = (x^{2}+1)^{1/2}\), \( u' = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+1}}\). Multiplying gives \( \frac{x}{x^{2}+1}\). This matches option D, demonstrating careful chain‑rule application.

Q5. Using the limit definition, which limit evaluates to the derivative of \( \ln x \) at a point \( x>0 \)?

🔹 A. \( \displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\ln(x+h)-\ln x}{h} \)
🔹 B. \( \displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{e^{x+h}-e^{x}}{h} \)
🔹 C. \( \displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{x^{h}-1}{h} \)
🔹 D. \( \displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\ln h}{h} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of a function \( f \) at \( x \) is defined as \( \lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\). Substituting \( f(x)=\ln x\) yields the expression in option A, which directly computes \( \frac{1}{x}\). The other limits correspond to different functions.

Q6. What is \( \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[\ln(5x^{2}+3)\bigr] \)?

🔹 A. \( \frac{5x}{5x^{2}+3} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{10x}{5x^{2}+3} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{2x}{5x^{2}+3} \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{10x}{x^{2}+3} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Apply the chain rule: derivative of \( \ln u\) is \( u'/u\). Here \( u=5x^{2}+3\) and \( u'=10x\). Hence \( \frac{d}{dx}\ln(5x^{2}+3)=\frac{10x}{5x^{2}+3}\). This corresponds to option B.

Q7. Why is the natural logarithm base \( e \) preferred when differentiating logarithmic functions?

🔹 A. It yields integer results
🔹 B. It simplifies integration only
🔹 C. It makes logarithms periodic
🔹 D. Its derivative formula is \( \frac{1}{x} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

When the base is \( e\), the derivative of \( \log_{e}x = \ln x\) reduces to the simple form \( \frac{1}{x}\). This eliminates the extra constant \( \ln b\) that appears for other bases, streamlining calculations in calculus, which justifies option D.

Q8. If \( g'(x)=\frac{2}{x\ln 3} \), which function could represent \( g(x) \) (up to an additive constant)?

🔹 A. \( 2\log_{3}x \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{2}{\ln x} \)
🔹 C. \( \ln(3x^{2}) \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{2}{x^{2}\ln 3} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Integrating \( g'(x)=\frac{2}{x\ln 3}\) gives \( g(x)=\frac{2}{\ln 3}\int\frac{1}{x}dx =\frac{2}{\ln 3}\ln x +C =2\log_{3}x +C\). Therefore the antiderivative matches option A, showing the connection between derivative and logarithm base conversion.

Q9. Find the derivative of \( h(x)=\frac{\ln x}{\ln (x^{2})} \).

🔹 A. 0
🔹 B. \( \frac{1}{x\ln x} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{1}{2x\ln x} \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{2}{x} \)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since \( \ln (x^{2}) = 2\ln x\), the function simplifies to \( h(x)=\frac{\ln x}{2\ln x}= \frac{1}{2}\) for \( x>0, x\neq1\). The derivative of a constant is zero, so the correct answer is option A. Recognizing the simplification is essential for this higher‑order problem.

Q10. For which base \( b \) does the derivative of \( \log_{b}x \) equal \( \frac{1}{x} \)?

🔹 A. b=10
🔹 B. b=2
🔹 C. b=e
🔹 D. Any \( b>0 \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The general derivative formula is \( \frac{d}{dx}\log_{b}x = \frac{1}{x\ln b}\). To equal \( \frac{1}{x}\), the denominator \( \ln b\) must be 1, which occurs only when \( b=e\). Thus option C correctly identifies the natural base.

Q11. Differentiate \( f(x)=\ln (x)^{2} \) (i.e., \( (\ln x)^{2} \)). Which expression is correct?

🔹 A. \( \frac{2}{x\ln x} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{2\ln x}{x} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{\ln x}{2x} \)
🔹 D. \( \frac{2}{x} \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Treat \( f(x) = (\ln x)^{2}\) as a composition. Using the chain rule, \( f'(x)=2\ln x \cdot \frac{1}{x}= \frac{2\ln x}{x}\). This matches option B, demonstrating proper application of differentiation rules for composite functions.
