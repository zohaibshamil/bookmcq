What is Derivatives of Logarithmic Functions?

Definition:
The derivative of a logarithmic function measures the rate of change of the logarithm with respect to its argument. For the natural logarithm ln⁡(x)\ln(x)ln(x), the derivative is 1x\frac{1}{x}x1​. For a general base bbb, the derivative of log⁡b(x)\log_b(x)logb​(x) involves dividing by the natural logarithm of the base, reflecting how the scaling factor of the logarithm affects its slope at any given positive input value.

Example:
Find the derivative of f(x)=log⁡2(x)f(x) = \log_2(x)f(x)=log2​(x). Using the formula ddxlog⁡b(x)=1xln⁡(b)\frac{d}{dx}\log_b(x) = \frac{1}{x \ln(b)}dxd​logb​(x)=xln(b)1​, we get f′(x)=1xln⁡(2)f'(x) = \frac{1}{x \ln(2)}f′(x)=xln(2)1​. If x=1x=1x=1, the slope is 1ln⁡(2)≈1.44\frac{1}{\ln(2)} \approx 1.44ln(2)1​≈1.44.

Reason:
These derivatives are fundamental in modeling phenomena involving exponential decay or growth, such as pH levels in chemistry or sound intensity in decibels, where logarithmic scales are used to compress wide ranges of values.
