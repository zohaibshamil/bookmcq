What is Derivative of x^n for real powers?

Definition:
The power rule for differentiation extends to any real number exponent $n$, stating that the derivative of $x^n$ is $nx^{n-1}$. This holds true not just for integers but also for rational and irrational exponents, provided $x$ is in the domain where the function is defined. This generalization is proven using logarithmic differentiation or the definition of the derivative.

Example:
Find the derivative of $f(x) = x^{\sqrt{2}}$. Applying the power rule, $f'(x) = \sqrt{2}x^{\sqrt{2}-1}$. Similarly, for $g(x) = x^{-3/4}$, the derivative is $g'(x) = -\frac{3}{4}x^{-7/4}$.

Reason:
This universal power rule simplifies the differentiation of root functions (like $\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$) and reciprocal powers, unifying the treatment of polynomial-like terms and allowing consistent application of calculus rules across all real-valued power functions.

Q1. What is the derivative of \(f(x)=x^{p}\) where \(p\) is a real constant?

A. \(p x^{p-1}\) ✅
B. \(x^{p}\)
C. \(p x^{p}\)
D. \(x^{p-1}\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The power rule states that when a constant exponent \(p\) multiplies the variable \(x\), the derivative is obtained by bringing the exponent down as a coefficient and decreasing the exponent by one, giving \(p x^{p-1}\). This directly follows from the limit definition of the derivative.

Q2. If \(g(x)=x^{3.5}\) and \(g'(a)=0\) for some \(a>0\), what must be true about \(a\)?

A. \(a\) must be 0
B. \(a\) must be 1
C. No such \(a\) exists ✅
D. \(a\) can be any positive number

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Computing the derivative yields \(g'(x)=3.5x^{2.5}\). Since the factor 3.5 is never zero, the only way the product can be zero is if \(x^{2.5}=0\), which occurs at \(x=0\). Because the premise requires \(a>0\), there is no possible \(a\) satisfying the condition.

Q3. Which statement about the growth rates of \(f(x)=x^{2}\) and \(h(x)=x^{5}\) for large \(x\) is correct?

A. \(f'(x)\) grows faster than \(h'(x)\)
B. \(h'(x)\) grows faster than \(f'(x)\) ✅
C. Both derivatives grow at the same rate
D. Both derivatives decrease as \(x\) increases

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The derivatives are \(f'(x)=2x\) and \(h'(x)=5x^{4}\). As \(x\) becomes large, the term \(x^{4}\) dominates, making \(5x^{4}\) increase far more rapidly than the linear term \(2x\). Thus the derivative of the higher‑power function outpaces the lower‑power one.

Q4. Let \(y = (3x^{2}+2)^{4.2}\). What is \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\)?

A. \(4.2(3x^{2}+2)^{3.2}\cdot6x\)
B. \(4.2(3x^{2}+2)^{4.2}\cdot6x\)
C. \(25.2x(3x^{2}+2)^{3.2}\) ✅
D. \(25.2x(3x^{2}+2)^{4.2}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Apply the chain rule: differentiate the outer function \((u)^{4.2}\) to get \(4.2u^{3.2}\) and multiply by the derivative of the inner function \(u=3x^{2}+2\), which is \(6x\). Multiplying gives \(4.2\cdot6x\,(3x^{2}+2)^{3.2}=25.2x(3x^{2}+2)^{3.2}\).

Q5. For \(f(x)=x^{n}\) with real \(n\), which condition guarantees that \(f'(x)\) is negative for all \(x<0\)?

A. \(n\) is an even integer ✅
B. \(n\) is an odd integer
C. \(n>0\)
D. \(n\) is a negative odd integer

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative is \(f'(x)=n x^{\,n-1}\). When \(n\) is even, \(n-1\) is odd, so \(x^{\,n-1}\) is negative for negative \(x\). Multiplying by the positive coefficient \(n\) yields a negative product, ensuring \(f'(x)<0\) for every \(x<0\).

Q6. Using the definition of derivative, compute \(\displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{(5+h)^{2.3}-5^{2.3}}{h}\).

A. \(2.3\cdot5^{1.3}\)
B. Approximately \(18.63\) ✅
C. \(2.3\cdot5^{2.3}\)
D. \(5^{2.3}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The limit equals the derivative of \(x^{2.3}\) at \(x=5\): \(f'(5)=2.3\cdot5^{1.3}\). Numerically, \(5^{1.3}\approx8.10\); multiplying by 2.3 gives about \(18.63\). Hence the limit evaluates to roughly \(18.63\).

Q7. Consider \(f(x)=x^{x}\) for \(x>0\). Which expression correctly represents \(f'(x)\)?

A. \(x^{x}(\ln x+1)\) ✅
B. \(x^{x}\ln x\)
C. \(x^{x}(1-\ln x)\)
D. \(x^{x}(\ln x-1)\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Write \(f(x)=e^{x\ln x}\). Differentiating using the product rule inside the exponent gives \(\frac{d}{dx}(x\ln x)=\ln x+1\). Multiplying by the original function yields \(f'(x)=e^{x\ln x}(\ln x+1)=x^{x}(\ln x+1)\).

Q8. If \(f(x)=x^{0.5}\) for \(x\ge0\), what is the sign of \(f''(x)\) on \((0,\infty)\)?

A. Positive
B. Negative ✅
C. Zero
D. Undefined

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

First derivative: \(f'(x)=\tfrac12x^{-0.5}\). Differentiating again gives \(f''(x)=-\tfrac14x^{-1.5}\). Since \(x^{-1.5}>0\) for \(x>0\) and the coefficient \(-\tfrac14\) is negative, the second derivative is negative throughout the interval.

Q9. At \(x=2\), which derivative is larger: that of \(x^{3}\) or of \(x^{2.5}\)?

A. Derivative of \(x^{3}\) is larger ✅
B. Derivative of \(x^{2.5}\) is larger
C. They are equal
D. Cannot be determined without more information

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Compute each derivative at \(x=2\): \((x^{3})' =3x^{2}=12\); \((x^{2.5})' =2.5x^{1.5}\approx7.07\). Since \(12>7.07\), the derivative of the cubic function exceeds that of the \(2.5\) power at the given point.

Q10. Given \(x^{2}+y^{3}=7\), find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) in terms of \(x\) and \(y\).

A. \(-\dfrac{2x}{3y^{2}}\) ✅
B. \(-\dfrac{3y^{2}}{2x}\)
C. \(\dfrac{2x}{3y^{2}}\)
D. \(\dfrac{3y^{2}}{2x}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Differentiate implicitly: \(2x+3y^{2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0\). Solving for \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) yields \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac{2x}{3y^{2}}\). This expression relates the slope of the curve to the current coordinates.

Q11. For the function \(f(x)=x^{p}\) with \(0<p<1\), which statement about its monotonicity on \((0,\infty)\) is true?

A. Increasing and concave up
B. Increasing and concave down ✅
C. Decreasing
D. Constant

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The first derivative \(f'(x)=p x^{p-1}\) is positive because \(p>0\) and \(x^{p-1}>0\) for \(x>0\). The second derivative \(f''(x)=p(p-1)x^{p-2}\) is negative since \(p-1<0\). Thus the function rises while bending downward, i.e., it is increasing and concave down.

Q12. If \(g(x)=k x^{p}\) where \(k>0\) and \(p\) is a real constant, how does \(g'(x)\) compare to the derivative of \(f(x)=x^{p}\)?

A. \(g'(x)=k f'(x)\) ✅
B. \(g'(x)=\dfrac{f'(x)}{k}\)
C. \(g'(x)=k^{p} f'(x)\)
D. \(g'(x)=p k x^{p-1}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating \(g(x)\) gives \(g'(x)=k\cdot p x^{p-1}=k\big(p x^{p-1}\big)=k f'(x)\). The constant factor \(k\) simply scales the original derivative, leaving the functional form unchanged.

Q13. The volume of a sphere is \(V=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}\). If the radius is increasing at \(0.2\text{ cm/s}\) when \(r=5\text{ cm}\), what is \(\dfrac{dV}{dt}\) at that instant?

A. \(4\pi\) cm\(^3\)/s
B. \(10\pi\) cm\(^3\)/s
C. \(15\pi\) cm\(^3\)/s
D. \(20\pi\) cm\(^3\)/s ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Differentiate implicitly: \(\dfrac{dV}{dt}=4\pi r^{2}\dfrac{dr}{dt}\). Substituting \(r=5\) cm and \(\dfrac{dr}{dt}=0.2\) cm/s gives \(4\pi(25)(0.2)=20\pi\) cm\(^3\)/s.

Q14. Suppose \(h(x)=x^{p}\) with \(p\neq0\). If \(h'(c)=0\) for some \(c\neq0\), which conclusion follows?

A. \(p\) must be zero
B. \(c\) must be zero
C. Such a \(c\) cannot exist ✅
D. \(h\) is a constant function

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The derivative is \(h'(x)=p x^{p-1}\). Setting this equal to zero gives \(p x^{p-1}=0\). Since \(p\neq0\) by hypothesis, the only way the product can be zero is if \(x^{p-1}=0\), which occurs at \(x=0\). Hence no non‑zero \(c\) can satisfy the condition.

Q15. Find the second derivative of \(f(x)=x^{\alpha}\) where \(\alpha\) is a real constant, and state for which \(\alpha\) the second derivative is positive for all \(x>0\).

A. \(f''(x)=\alpha(\alpha-1)x^{\alpha-2}\); positive when \(\alpha<0\) or \(\alpha>1\)
B. \(f''(x)=\alpha(\alpha+1)x^{\alpha-2}\); positive when \(\alpha>0\)
C. \(f''(x)=\alpha(\alpha-1)x^{\alpha-2}\); positive when \(0<\alpha<1\)
D. \(f''(x)=\alpha(\alpha-2)x^{\alpha-2}\); positive when \(\alpha>2\) ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating twice yields \(f''(x)=\alpha(\alpha-1)x^{\alpha-2}\). For \(x>0\), the sign of the second derivative matches the sign of the coefficient \(\alpha(\alpha-1)\). This product is positive when either \(\alpha<0\) or \(\alpha>1\), giving the required condition.
