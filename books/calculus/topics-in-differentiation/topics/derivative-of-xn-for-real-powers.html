What is Derivative of x^n for real powers?

Definition:
The power rule for differentiation extends to any real number exponent nnn, stating that the derivative of xnx^nxn is nxn−1nx^{n-1}nxn−1. This holds true not just for integers but also for rational and irrational exponents, provided xxx is in the domain where the function is defined. This generalization is proven using logarithmic differentiation or the definition of the derivative.

Example:
Find the derivative of f(x)=x2f(x) = x^{\sqrt{2}}f(x)=x2​. Applying the power rule, f′(x)=2x2−1f'(x) = \sqrt{2}x^{\sqrt{2}-1}f′(x)=2​x2​−1. Similarly, for g(x)=x−3/4g(x) = x^{-3/4}g(x)=x−3/4, the derivative is g′(x)=−34x−7/4g'(x) = -\frac{3}{4}x^{-7/4}g′(x)=−43​x−7/4.

Reason:
This universal power rule simplifies the differentiation of root functions (like x=x1/2\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}x​=x1/2) and reciprocal powers, unifying the treatment of polynomial-like terms and allowing consistent application of calculus rules across all real-valued power functions.
