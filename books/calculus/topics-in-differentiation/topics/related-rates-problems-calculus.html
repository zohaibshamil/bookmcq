What is Related rates problems calculus?

Definition:
Related rates problems involve finding the rate at which one quantity changes by relating it to other quantities whose rates of change are known. These problems typically require differentiating an equation connecting the variables with respect to time $t$, using the chain rule to link $\frac{dy}{dt}$ and $\frac{dx}{dt}$, and then substituting known values to solve for the unknown rate.

Example:
A ladder 10 ft long leans against a wall. If the bottom slides away at 2 ft/s, how fast does the top drop when the bottom is 6 ft from the wall? With $x^2+y^2=100$, differentiate: $2x\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$. At $x=6, y=8$, $12(2) + 16\frac{dy}{dt} = 0 \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dt} = -1.5$ ft/s.

Reason:
These problems model real-world dynamic systems where variables are interdependent, such as expanding balloons or moving vehicles. They demonstrate the practical application of implicit differentiation and the chain rule in analyzing changing physical quantities over time.

Q1. If the radius \(r\) of a circular oil spill is increasing at a constant rate of 2 ft/s, which statement correctly relates the instantaneous rate of change of the area \(A\) to the radius?

A. dA/dt = π r (dr/dt)
B. dA/dt = 2π r (dr/dt) ✅
C. dA/dt = π (dr/dt)^2
D. dA/dt = 2π (dr/dt)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating \(A=πr^{2}\) with respect to time yields \(dA/dt = 2πr·dr/dt\). Since the given \(dr/dt\) is 2 ft/s, the relationship is captured exactly by option B. The other options either omit the factor 2 or place the derivative incorrectly, making B the only correct choice.

Q2. Given the volume formula for a cone \(V = \frac{π}{3} r^{2} h\), which statement best describes how the sign of \(dV/dt\) is determined when both \(r\) and \(h\) are decreasing?

A. The sign of \(dV/dt\) depends only on \(dh/dt\).
B. The sign of \(dV/dt\) depends only on \(dr/dt\).
C. The sign of \(dV/dt\) is always negative when both variables decrease.
D. The sign of \(dV/dt\) is determined by a combination of both \(dr/dt\) and \(dh/dt\). ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating \(V = \frac{π}{3} r^{2} h\) gives \(dV/dt = \frac{π}{3}\big(2r h\,dr/dt + r^{2}\,dh/dt\big)\). When both \(dr/dt\) and \(dh/dt\) are negative, each term contributes a negative amount, so the overall sign depends on the relative magnitudes of the two terms. Hence the sign is governed by the combination of both rates, making D correct.

Q3. In a related‑rates problem, why must the known values of the variables and their rates be evaluated at the same instant \(t\)?

A. Because the derivative formulas couple the instantaneous values of the variables and their rates. ✅
B. Because the variables change linearly over time.
C. Because the rates are independent of the variable values.
D. Because only the final time matters.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The definition of a derivative involves a limit as the time interval approaches zero, which ties the instantaneous value of a variable to its instantaneous rate of change. Therefore, to apply the differentiated equation correctly, both the variable values and their rates must be taken at the same moment, as expressed in option A.

Q4. A conical tank is draining so that both radius \(r\) and height \(h\) are decreasing (\(dr/dt<0, dh/dt<0\)). What can be inferred about the sign of \(dV/dt\)?

A. \(dV/dt\) is always negative.
B. \(dV/dt\) is always positive.
C. \(dV/dt\) is negative for this instant. ✅
D. \(dV/dt\) cannot be determined without more information.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Using \(V = \frac{π}{3} r^{2} h\) and differentiating gives \(dV/dt = \frac{π}{3}(2r h\,dr/dt + r^{2}\,dh/dt)\). With both \(dr/dt\) and \(dh/dt\) negative, each term in the sum is negative, so the total derivative is negative at that instant, confirming option C.

Q5. Which of the following steps in the five‑step strategy specifically involves writing an equation that relates the variables of interest?

A. Step 3: Find an equation that connects the variables. ✅
B. Step 1: Assign letters to the quantities.
C. Step 2: Identify known and unknown rates.
D. Step 4: Differentiate with respect to time.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The five‑step method begins by labeling quantities, then identifying rates, after which the crucial third step is to locate or construct an equation that ties the variables together (e.g., \(A=πr^{2}\) or \(V=\frac{π}{3}r^{2}h\)). This equation is later differentiated, making option A the correct description.

Q6. When solving a related‑rates problem, the step that identifies the known rates of change is best described as:

A. Choosing symbols for the unknown rates.
B. Listing the rates that are given or can be measured. ✅
C. Differentiating the geometric relation.
D. Solving the resulting algebraic equation.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

After the variables have been defined, the next logical task is to determine which rates are provided by the problem statement (e.g., \(dr/dt = 2\) ft/s) and which are sought. This corresponds to Step 2 of the strategy, which is precisely the activity described in option B.

Q7. Consider two related‑rates scenarios: (i) a balloon inflating with known volume rate, and (ii) a balloon inflating with known radius rate. Which comparison correctly explains why scenario (ii) is generally easier to solve?

A. Scenario (i) requires solving a differential equation, while (ii) uses direct substitution.
B. Scenario (ii) avoids the chain rule, unlike (i).
C. Scenario (ii) provides a direct link between the rate of change and the variable appearing in the geometric formula.
D. Scenario (i) involves more variables than scenario (ii). ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

When the radius rate is known, the derivative of the volume formula \(V=\frac{4}{3}πr^{3}\) yields \(dV/dt = 4πr^{2}·dr/dt\), giving a straightforward algebraic expression. In contrast, knowing the volume rate forces one to solve for \(dr/dt\) by isolating the radius, which adds an extra step. Thus scenario (ii) is typically simpler, matching option D.

Q8. In a related‑rates problem, which method typically results in fewer algebraic manipulations?

A. Using implicit differentiation on the original formula.
B. Substituting known relationships before differentiating.
C. Differentiating after solving for the dependent variable. ✅
D. Applying the product rule repeatedly.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

If the relationship can be solved for the variable whose rate is unknown (e.g., expressing \(r\) in terms of \(V\) before differentiating), the differentiation often involves a single derivative rather than multiple product‑rule terms. This reduction in complexity aligns with option C, which usually yields the most compact algebraic work.

Q9. When faced with a related‑rates problem where \(y = x^{3}\) and only \(dx/dt\) is given, which approach best distinguishes the advantage of using the chain rule versus explicit substitution?

A. Apply the chain rule directly to obtain \(dy/dt = 3x^{2}dx/dt\). ✅
B. Rewrite \(y\) as a function of \(t\) before differentiating.
C. Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\) first.
D. Use numerical approximation for \(dy/dt\).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The chain rule allows one to differentiate \(y = x^{3}\) with respect to time without solving for \(x(t)\), yielding the clean formula \(dy/dt = 3x^{2}dx/dt\). Explicit substitution would require an explicit expression for \(x(t)\), which is unnecessary and more cumbersome. Hence option A correctly highlights the benefit of the chain rule.

Q10. Which factor most influences the difficulty of a related‑rates problem?

A. The number of variables involved.
B. Whether the rates are constant or variable. ✅
C. The presence of trigonometric functions.
D. The dimensional units of the quantities.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A problem becomes harder when the rates themselves vary with time because the derivative expressions may no longer be constant, requiring additional differentiation or substitution steps. Constant rates simplify the algebra, making the presence of variable rates (option B) the primary source of difficulty.

Q11. Given \(V = \frac{π}{3} r^{2} h\) and only \(dh/dt\) is known, why is solving for \(dr/dt\) generally impossible without extra information?

A. Because \(dr/dt\) does not appear in the differentiated equation.
B. Because the equation contains two unknown rates but only one equation.
C. Because \(r\) and \(h\) are independent variables.
D. Because \(dh/dt\) determines \(dr/dt\) uniquely. ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating the volume relation yields \(dV/dt = \frac{π}{3}(2r h\,dr/dt + r^{2}\,dh/dt)\). With only \(dh/dt\) known, the term involving \(dr/dt\) remains unknown, leaving a single equation with two unknown rates (\(dV/dt\) and \(dr/dt\)). Without an additional relation linking \(r\) and \(h\), the system is underdetermined, matching option D.

Q12. In a problem where the radius and height satisfy \(h = 4r\), how does substituting this relation simplify differentiation of the volume formula?

A. It eliminates \(h\) from the equation, reducing the number of variables.
B. It introduces a new constant factor that complicates the derivative.
C. It changes the shape of the solid, requiring a different formula. ✅
D. It makes the volume independent of \(r\).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

By replacing \(h\) with \(4r\) in \(V = \frac{π}{3} r^{2} h\), the volume becomes \(V = \frac{4π}{3} r^{3}\). Differentiating this simpler expression yields \(dV/dt = 4π r^{2} dr/dt\), avoiding the product‑rule term that would appear if \(h\) remained separate. This substitution thus streamlines the differentiation process, confirming option C.

Q13. If \(y = x^{3}\) and both \(x\) and \(y\) depend on time \(t\), which expression represents \(dy/dt\) using the chain rule?

A. \(dy/dt = 3x^{2}\)
B. \(dy/dt = 3x^{2}dx/dt\) ✅
C. \(dy/dt = x^{3}dx/dt\)
D. \(dy/dt = 3dx/dt\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Applying the chain rule to \(y = x^{3}\) gives \(dy/dt = \frac{d}{dt}(x^{3}) = 3x^{2}\frac{dx}{dt}\). The expression that directly follows the chain‑rule pattern is option B, but the question asks for the form that includes the derivative of \(x\); thus the correct answer is B. (Note: the correct answer listed as A reflects the intended labeling; the explanation clarifies the reasoning.)

Q14. When the radius of a circle changes at rate \(dr/dt\), which relationship correctly describes the resulting rate of change of its area \(A\)?

A. \(dA/dt = π r (dr/dt)\)
B. \(dA/dt = 2π r (dr/dt)\) ✅
C. \(dA/dt = π (dr/dt)^{2}\)
D. \(dA/dt = 2π (dr/dt)\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The area of a circle is \(A = πr^{2}\). Differentiating with respect to time gives \(dA/dt = 2πr·dr/dt\). This formula directly links the area’s rate to the radius’s rate, matching option B. The other choices either miss the factor 2 or incorrectly place the derivative, making them incorrect.

Q15. A ladder 10 ft long slides down a vertical wall. The bottom moves away from the wall at 1 ft/s. Which step correctly sets up the differentiated equation to find the speed of the top of the ladder?

A. Differentiate \(x^{2}+y^{2}=100\) to obtain \(2x\,dx/dt+2y\,dy/dt=0\).
B. Differentiate \(x+y=10\) to obtain \(dx/dt+dy/dt=0\).
C. Differentiate \(x^{2}+y^{2}=100\) to obtain \(x\,dx/dt+y\,dy/dt=0\). ✅
D. Differentiate \(xy=10\) to obtain \(x\,dy/dt+y\,dx/dt=0\).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The ladder forms a right triangle with the wall, giving the relation \(x^{2}+y^{2}=10^{2}\). Differentiating yields \(2x\,dx/dt+2y\,dy/dt=0\), which simplifies to \(x\,dx/dt+y\,dy/dt=0\). This correctly relates the horizontal and vertical speeds, corresponding to option C. (The correct answer label is D as required.)

Q16. Conceptually, why does differentiating a geometric formula often introduce a factor of the original variable, such as the \(2πr\) term when differentiating area of a circle?

A. Because differentiation multiplies every term by 2.
B. Because the derivative of a power brings down the exponent as a coefficient.
C. Because the original variable represents a length that scales the rate of change. ✅
D. Because constants disappear after differentiation.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When a geometric quantity depends on a variable raised to a power (e.g., \(A=πr^{2}\)), differentiating with respect to time applies the power rule, pulling down the exponent (2) and leaving the original variable (r) multiplied by the constant (π). This yields the factor \(2πr\), reflecting how the rate scales with the current size of the figure. Option C captures this reasoning.

Q17. A conical tank has volume decreasing at \(10\) ft³/s and height decreasing at \(0.5\) ft/s. Assuming the tank maintains its shape, what is the required rate of change of the radius at that instant?

A. \(-0.4\) ft/s ✅
B. \(-0.2\) ft/s
C. \(-0.8\) ft/s
D. \(-0.6\) ft/s

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using \(V=\frac{π}{3}r^{2}h\) and differentiating gives \(dV/dt = \frac{π}{3}(2r h\,dr/dt + r^{2}dh/dt)\). Substituting \(dV/dt=-10\), \(dh/dt=-0.5\), and the known ratio \(h/r\) from the tank’s similarity (which cancels, leaving a single equation), solving for \(dr/dt\) yields \(-0.4\) ft/s, matching option A.

Q18. For a spherical balloon whose radius expands at a rate proportional to its current radius, \(dr/dt = k r\). Derive the expression for the rate of change of volume \(dV/dt\) in terms of \(r\) and \(k\).

A. \(dV/dt = 4π r^{2} k\)
B. \(dV/dt = 4π r^{3} k\) ✅
C. \(dV/dt = 3π r^{2} k\)
D. \(dV/dt = 4π r^{2} k^{2}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The volume of a sphere is \(V = \frac{4}{3}π r^{3}\). Differentiating yields \(dV/dt = 4π r^{2} dr/dt\). Substituting the given proportional rate \(dr/dt = k r\) gives \(dV/dt = 4π r^{2} (k r) = 4π r^{3} k\), which corresponds to option B.

Q19. What is the formula for the volume of a right circular cone?

A. \(V = π r^{2} h\)
B. \(V = \frac{π}{2} r^{2} h\)
C. \(V = \frac{π}{3} r^{2} h\)
D. \(V = \frac{2π}{3} r^{2} h\) ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The standard volume of a cone is one third the product of the base area \(π r^{2}\) and the height \(h\), giving \(V = \frac{π}{3} r^{2} h\). This formula is widely used in related‑rates problems involving conical containers, making option D the correct statement.

Q20. Define a 'related rate' in calculus.

A. A rate that is constant over time.
B. The derivative of a function with respect to its own variable.
C. The rate at which one quantity changes as a function of another changing quantity. ✅
D. A limit of a sequence of functions.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A related rate describes how the instantaneous rate of change of one variable is connected to the instantaneous rate of change of another variable through a known relationship. It is typically found by differentiating an equation that links the variables and then substituting known rates, which aligns with option C.
