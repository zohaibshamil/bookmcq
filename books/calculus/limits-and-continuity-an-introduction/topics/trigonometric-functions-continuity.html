What is Trigonometric functions continuity?

Definition:
All basic trigonometric functions—\( \sin x \), \( \cos x \), \( \tan x \), \( \cot x \), \( \sec x \), and \( \csc x \)—are continuous on their respective domains. Specifically, \( \sin x \) and \( \cos x \) are continuous everywhere on \( \mathbb{R} \), while \( \tan x \) and \( \sec x \) have discontinuities where \( \cos x = 0 \), and \( \cot x \) and \( \csc x \) where \( \sin x = 0 \), due to vertical asymptotes.

Example:
Check continuity of \( f(x) = \tan x \) at \( x = \pi/4 \).
Solution: \( \cos(\pi/4) \neq 0 \), so \( \tan x \) is continuous; \( \lim_{x \to \pi/4} \tan x = 1 = f(\pi/4) \).

Reason:
Continuity of trig functions ensures they can be used reliably in modeling periodic phenomena like oscillations and waves, and allows for the application of limit laws and derivative rules in calculus.

📝 All Trigonometric functions continuity MCQs

Q1. If \( \lim_{x\to c}\sin x = \sin c\) and \( \lim_{x\to c}\cos x = \cos c\), what can we infer about \( \lim_{x\to c}\tan x\) provided \( \cos c\neq 0\)?

A. \( \frac{\sin c}{\cos c}\) ✅
B. \( \sin c\)
C. \( \cos c\)
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because both sine and cosine are continuous, the limit of their quotient exists when the denominator is non‑zero. Hence \( \lim_{x\to c}\tan x = \lim_{x\to c}\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}= \frac{\sin c}{\cos c}\). This matches option A.

Q2. Compare the continuity of \( \sin x\) and \( \sec x\) at \( x = \pi/2\).

A. Both continuous
B. Only sin continuous ✅
C. Only sec continuous
D. Neither continuous

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The sine function is defined and continuous for every real number, while \( \sec x = 1/\cos x\) is undefined wherever \( \cos x = 0\). At \( x = \pi/2\) the cosine equals zero, so \( \sec x\) is not defined and thus not continuous. Only \( \sin x\) remains continuous, giving option B.

Q3. Determine \( \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin(5x)}{x}\).

A. 0
B. 1
C. 5 ✅
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Using the standard limit \( \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin(kx)}{x}=k\) for any constant \(k\), we substitute \(k=5\). The limit evaluates to 5, which corresponds to option C.

Q4. Suppose \( f(x)=\sin x\) for \( x\neq\pi\) and \( f(\pi)=2\). Is \( f\) continuous at \( x=\pi\)?

A. Yes
B. No, because limit differs ✅
C. No, because function undefined
D. Cannot determine

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The function equals \( \sin x\) everywhere except at \( x=\pi\) where it is artificially set to 2. Since \( \lim_{x\to \pi}\sin x = 0\neq 2\), the limit and the function value differ, so the function is not continuous at \( x=\pi\). Option B states this reason.

Q5. Evaluate the continuity of \( g(x)=\tan x\) at \( x=\pi/2\).

A. Continuous because limit exists
B. Discontinuous because limit infinite ✅
C. Continuous because tan defined
D. Not defined domain

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

At \( x = \pi/2\) the cosine term in \( \tan x = \sin x / \cos x\) approaches zero, causing the quotient to blow up. The limit does not exist (it tends to \( \pm\infty\)), so \( \tan x\) is discontinuous there. Option B captures this.

Q6. Which theorem justifies \( \lim_{x\to c}\cos(g(x)) = \cos(\lim_{x\to c}g(x))\) when the limit exists?

A. Intermediate Value Theorem
B. Squeeze Theorem
C. Continuity of cosine ✅
D. Mean Value Theorem

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The step \( \lim_{x\to c}\cos(g(x)) = \cos(\lim_{x\to c}g(x))\) relies on the fact that cosine is a continuous function on its entire domain. This is precisely the continuity property of cosine, making option C correct.

Q7. Given that \( \sin x\) is continuous on \( \mathbb{R}\), which statement must be true?

A. \( \sin x\) attains a maximum on any closed interval
B. \( \sin x\) is differentiable everywhere
C. \( \sin x\) is bounded on \( \mathbb{R}\) ✅
D. \( \sin x\) has an inverse on \( \mathbb{R}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Continuity alone guarantees that \( \sin x\) does not exceed its known bounds of \([-1,1]\) on the whole real line, so it is bounded. The other statements require additional properties such as differentiability or invertibility, which are not implied by continuity. Hence option C is correct.

Q8. Consider \( h(x)=\frac{\sin x}{x}\) for \( x\neq 0\) and \( h(0)=1\). Determine continuity at \( x=0\).

A. Continuous because limit equals 1 ✅
B. Discontinuous because denominator zero
C. Continuous only from right
D. Not defined

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The limit \( \lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\) is a classic result. Defining \( h(0)=1\) makes the function value match the limit, ensuring continuity at \( x=0\). Therefore the function is continuous, corresponding to option A.

Q9. If a function is continuous at a point and its limit equals the function value, which must hold for the composition \( f\circ g\) at a point \( c\), given \( g\) is continuous at \( c\) and \( f\) is continuous at \( g(c)\)?

A. \( \lim_{x\to c}f(g(x)) = f(g(c))\) ✅
B. Limit does not exist
C. Equality holds only if \( f\) is linear
D. Equality holds only if \( g\) is monotonic

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

If \( g\) is continuous at \( c\) then \( \lim_{x\to c}g(x)=g(c)\). If \( f\) is continuous at \( g(c)\) then \( \lim_{y\to g(c)}f(y)=f(g(c))\). Combining the two gives \( \lim_{x\to c}f(g(x)) = f(g(c))\). This is exactly option A.

Q10. What is the natural domain of \( \sec x\)?

A. All real numbers
B. \( \mathbb{R}\\{(2k+1)\\pi/2\\}\) ✅
C. \( \mathbb{R}\\{k\\pi\\}\)
D. \( \mathbb{R}\\{k\\pi/2\\}\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The secant function is defined as \( \sec x = 1/\cos x\). It is undefined wherever \( \cos x = 0\), which occurs at odd multiples of \( \pi/2\). Hence its natural domain is \( \mathbb{R}\\{(2k+1)\\pi/2 : k\\in\\mathbb{Z}\\}\), matching option B.

Q11. If \( \cos x\) is continuous and \( x_n\\to c\), what can be said about the sequence \( \\cos x_n\\)?

A. It converges to \\(\\cos c\\) ✅
B. It diverges
C. It oscillates
D. No information

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because cosine is continuous, the image of any sequence converging to \( c\) must converge to \\(\\cos c\\). Therefore the sequence \\(\\cos x_n\\) converges to \\(\\cos c\\). Option A expresses this conclusion.

Q12. Compare the continuity of \\(\\tan x\\) and \\(\\cot x\\) at \\(x = \\pi\\).

A. Both continuous
B. Both discontinuous
C. Only tan continuous ✅
D. Only cot continuous

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

At \\(x = \\pi\\), \\(\\tan \\pi = 0\\) because \\(\\sin \\pi = 0\\) and \\(\\cos \\pi = -1\\neq 0\\); thus \\(\\tan x\\) is continuous there. Conversely, \\(\\cot x = \\cos x / \\sin x\\) is undefined because \\(\\sin \\pi = 0\\). Hence only \\(\\tan x\\) remains continuous, which is option C.

Q13. Which of the following limits demonstrates the continuity of \\(\\sin x\\) at \\(c = \\pi/4\\)?

A. \\(\\lim_{x\\to \\pi/4}\\sin x = \\sin(\\pi/4)\\) ✅
B. \\(\\lim_{x\\to \\pi/4}\\cos x = \\sin(\\pi/4)\\)
C. \\(\\lim_{x\\to \\pi/4}\\tan x = \\sin(\\pi/4)\\)
D. \\(\\lim_{x\\to \\pi/4}\\csc x = \\sin(\\pi/4)\\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The statement \\(\\lim_{x\\to \\pi/4}\\sin x = \\sin(\\pi/4)\\) directly reflects the definition of continuity for \\(\\sin x\\) at the point \\(c = \\pi/4\\). This is the correct illustration, corresponding to option A.

Q14. Evaluate \\(\\lim_{x\\to 0}\\frac{1-\\cos(2x)}{x^2}\\).

A. 0
B. 1
C. 2 ✅
D. 4

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Rewrite \\(1-\\cos(2x)=2\\sin^2 x\\). Then \\(\\frac{1-\\cos(2x)}{x^2}=2\\left(\\frac{\\sin x}{x}\\right)^2\\). As \\(x\\to 0\\), \\(\\frac{\\sin x}{x}\\to 1\\), so the limit equals \\(2\\cdot1^2 = 2\\). Option C provides this value.

Q15. Let \\(f(x)=\\sec x\\) on its domain. Is \\(f\\) uniformly continuous on \\([0,\\pi/4]\\)?

A. Yes, because continuous on closed interval ✅
B. No, because derivative unbounded
C. Yes, because sec is bounded
D. No, because interval includes discontinuity

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

On the closed interval \\([0,\\pi/4]\\) the secant function is continuous and its domain contains no points where \\(\\cos x = 0\\). By the Heine–Cantor theorem, any continuous function on a closed, bounded interval is uniformly continuous. Hence option A is correct.
