What is Trigonometric functions continuity?

Definition:
All basic trigonometric functions—sin⁡x\sin xsinx, cos⁡x\cos xcosx, tan⁡x\tan xtanx, cot⁡x\cot xcotx, sec⁡x\sec xsecx, and csc⁡x\csc xcscx—are continuous on their respective domains. Specifically, sin⁡x\sin xsinx and cos⁡x\cos xcosx are continuous everywhere on R\mathbb{R}R, while tan⁡x\tan xtanx and sec⁡x\sec xsecx have discontinuities where cos⁡x=0\cos x = 0cosx=0, and cot⁡x\cot xcotx and csc⁡x\csc xcscx where sin⁡x=0\sin x = 0sinx=0, due to vertical asymptotes.

Example:
Check continuity of f(x)=tan⁡xf(x) = \tan xf(x)=tanx at x=π/4x = \pi/4x=π/4.
Solution: cos⁡(π/4)≠0\cos(\pi/4) \neq 0cos(π/4)=0, so tan⁡x\tan xtanx is continuous; lim⁡x→π/4tan⁡x=1=f(π/4)\lim_{x \to \pi/4} \tan x = 1 = f(\pi/4)limx→π/4​tanx=1=f(π/4).

Reason:
Continuity of trig functions ensures they can be used reliably in modeling periodic phenomena like oscillations and waves, and allows for the application of limit laws and derivative rules in calculus.
