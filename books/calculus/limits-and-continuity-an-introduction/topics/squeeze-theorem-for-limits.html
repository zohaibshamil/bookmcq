What is Squeeze theorem for limits?

Definition:
The Squeeze Theorem states that if \( g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x) \) for all \( x \) near \( a \) (except possibly at \( a \)) and \( \lim_{x \to a} g(x) = \lim_{x \to a} h(x) = L \), then \( \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \). This theorem is used to evaluate limits of functions that are difficult to compute directly by squeezing" them between two simpler functions with known limits.

Example:
Find \( \lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin(1/x) \).
Solution: \( -x^2 \le x^2 \sin(1/x) \le x^2 \), and \( \lim x^2 = 0 \), so by Squeeze Theorem, limit is \( 0 \).

Reason:
The Squeeze Theorem is invaluable for proving limits of oscillating functions and for establishing fundamental limits like \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} \), which are central to calculus and analysis."

📝 All Squeeze theorem for limits MCQs

Q1. What does the Squeezing Theorem assert about the limit of a function f when it is bounded between two functions g and h that share the same limit L as x approaches c?

A.If g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x) and lim g = lim h = L, then lim f = L. ✅
B.If g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x) and lim g = lim h = L, then lim f may differ from L.
C.If g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x), then lim f exists regardless of limits of g and h.
D.If g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x) and lim g = lim h = L, then lim f does not exist.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The theorem requires three conditions: g and h bound f, g and h have the same limit L at c, and the inequalities hold on a punctured neighborhood of c. When these are satisfied, any function f squeezed between them must share the same limit L, guaranteeing lim f = L.

Q2. Using the Squeezing Theorem, evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}\).

A.0
B.∞
C.does not exist
D.1 ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: By the inequality \(\cos x \le \frac{\sin x}{x} \le 1\) for \(x\) near 0, and knowing \(\lim_{x\to0}\cos x=1\), the squeeze forces \(\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\). The other options contradict this established bound.

Q3. Compare the limits \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x^2}\) and \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x}\).

A.Both limits are 0
B.Both limits are \(\tfrac12\)
C.First limit is \(\tfrac12\) and second limit is 0 ✅
D.First limit is 0 and second limit is \(\tfrac12\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Using \(1-\cos x=2\sin^2(x/2)\) and \(|\sin(x/2)|\le|x|/2\), we obtain \(0\le1-\cos x\le x^2/2\). Dividing by \(x^2\) gives a squeeze to \(\tfrac12\); dividing by \(x\) yields a squeeze to 0. Hence the first limit equals \(\tfrac12\) and the second equals 0.

Q4. If the inequalities \(g(x)\le f(x)\le h(x)\) hold only for \(x>c\), what can be concluded about the right‑hand limit of f at c?

A.The limit does not exist
B.The limit equals L ✅
C.The limit equals the left‑hand limit
D.No conclusion can be drawn
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When the bounding functions satisfy the squeeze on a right‑hand neighborhood of c and both have the same right‑hand limit L, the squeeze theorem guarantees \(\lim_{x\to c^+}f(x)=L\). The left‑hand behavior is irrelevant for this conclusion.

Q5. Which condition is NOT required for the Squeezing Theorem to hold?

A.The functions are defined on an interval containing c
B.The inequalities must hold at c ✅
C.Both bounding functions have the same limit at c
D.The function f is bounded on the interval
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The theorem explicitly allows the inequalities to fail at the point c itself; it only requires them on a punctured neighborhood. Therefore, demanding the inequalities at c is unnecessary, while the other three conditions are essential.

Q6. Determine \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0} x\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\) using the Squeeze Theorem.

A.1
B.-1
C.does not exist
D.0 ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Since \(-1\le\sin(1/x)\le1\) for all non‑zero x, multiplying by \(|x|\) yields \(-|x|\le x\sin(1/x)\le|x|\). As \(x\to0\), both bounds approach 0, so by squeezing the limit of the product is 0.

Q7. Which inequality is essential to prove \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\) via the Squeeze Theorem?

A.\(-|x|\le\sin x\le|x|\) leading to limit 1
B.\(\cos x\le\frac{\sin x}{x}\le1\) ✅
C.\(-1\le\sin x\le1\) leading to limit 0
D.No inequality works for this limit
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The classic proof uses the geometric inequality \(\cos x\le\frac{\sin x}{x}\le1\) for \(0<x<\pi/2\). Since \(\lim_{x\to0}\cos x=1\), the squeeze forces \(\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\). The other options either give weaker bounds or incorrect conclusions.

Q8. Suppose \(-|x|\le f(x)\le|x|\) for all x. What can be inferred about \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}f(x)\)?

A.0
B.1
C.does not exist ✅
D.cannot be determined
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Both bounding functions \(-|x|\) and \(|x|\) have limit 0 as \(x\to0\). By the squeeze theorem, any function trapped between them must also tend to 0, guaranteeing \(\lim_{x\to0}f(x)=0\).

Q9. Which of the following functions can be shown to have limit 0 at 0 by the Squeeze Theorem?

A.\(x^{2}\sin\!\left(\tfrac{1}{x}\right)\) ✅
B.\(\sin x\)
C.\(\tfrac{1}{x}\)
D.\(e^{x}\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For \(f(x)=x^{2}\sin(1/x)\), we have \(-x^{2}\le f(x)\le x^{2}\). Since \(\lim_{x\to0}(\pm x^{2})=0\), the squeeze forces \(\lim_{x\to0}f(x)=0\). The other functions either lack suitable bounds or have non‑zero limits.

Q10. Find \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+1}}\) using appropriate inequalities.

A.0 ✅
B.1
C.-1
D.does not exist
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Because \(\sqrt{x^{2}+1}\ge1\) for all x, we have \(0\le\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+1}}\le x^{2}\). Both bounding expressions tend to 0 as \(x\to0\); thus, by squeezing, the limit of the given fraction is 0.

Q11. In the one‑sided version of the Squeeze Theorem for \(x\to c^{+}\), which hypothesis must be ensured?

A.The interval becomes \((c,c+\delta)\)
B.The inequality may fail at c
C.Both g and h need the same right‑hand limit ✅
D.None of the above
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: For a right‑hand limit, the bounding functions must agree on their right‑hand limits at c. The inequalities need only hold on a punctured right‑hand neighborhood, and the interval is naturally \((c,c+\delta)\). Hence the essential condition is the equality of the right‑hand limits.

Q12. Which pair of functions can be used to squeeze \(\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{\sin x}{x}\) as \(x\to\infty\)?

A.\(-\tfrac{1}{x}\le f(x)\le \tfrac{1}{x}\) ✅
B.\(-1\le f(x)\le 1\)
C.\(0\le f(x)\le \tfrac{1}{x}\)
D.\(\tfrac{1}{x}\le f(x)\le 1\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Since \(|\sin x|\le1\), dividing by \(|x|\) gives \(-1/|x|\le\sin x/x\le1/|x|\). As \(x\to\infty\), both bounds approach 0, so the squeeze theorem shows \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\sin x/x=0\).

Q13. Using the Squeeze Theorem, what is \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x^{2}}\)?

A.0 ✅
B.does not exist
C.1
D.-1
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Because \(-1\le\sin x\le1\), dividing by \(x^{2}>0\) yields \(-1/x^{2}\le\sin x/x^{2}\le1/x^{2}\). Both outer terms tend to 0 as \(x\to\infty\); thus the squeezed function also tends to 0.

Q14. If \(g(x)\le f(x)\le h(x)\) holds for all \(x\neq c\) near c, but \(\lim_{x\to c^{-}}g(x)=L_{1}\) and \(\lim_{x\to c^{+}}h(x)=L_{2}\) with \(L_{1}\neq L_{2}\), what can be said about \(\lim_{x\to c}f(x)\)?

A.The limit does not exist ✅
B.The limit equals the average of \(L_{1}\) and \(L_{2}\)
C.The limit may exist if extra conditions are met
D.The limit equals \(L_{1}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When the left‑ and right‑hand limits of the bounding functions differ, the squeeze theorem cannot guarantee a common limit for f at c. Without a single shared limit for both sides, we cannot conclude the existence or value of \(\lim_{x\to c}f(x)\).

Q15. To prove \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x^{2}}=\tfrac12\) via the Squeeze Theorem, which inequality is pivotal?

A.\(0\le 1-\cos x\le x^{2}\)
B.\(\tfrac{x^{2}}{2}\le 1-\cos x\le x^{2}\)
C.\(0\le 1-\cos x\le \tfrac{x^{2}}{2}\) ✅
D.\(x^{2}\le 1-\cos x\le 2x^{2}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Using the identity \(1-\cos x=2\sin^{2}(x/2)\) and the fact that \(|\sin(u)|\le|u|\), we obtain \(0\le1-\cos x\le x^{2}/2\). Dividing by \(x^{2}\) yields \(0\le\frac{1-\cos x}{x^{2}}\le\frac12\); the lower bound approaches \(\frac12\) as well, forcing the limit to be \(\frac12\).

Q16. Given \(x^{3}\le f(x)\le x\) for \(x>0\) and both bounding limits as \(x\to0^{+}\) equal 0, what is \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^{+}}f(x)\)?

A.0
B.May be non‑zero
C.Does not exist
D.0 ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Both \(x^{3}\) and \(x\) approach 0 as \(x\to0^{+}\). Since \(f(x)\) is trapped between them on a right‑hand neighborhood, the squeeze theorem forces \(\lim_{x\to0^{+}}f(x)=0\). The statement holds regardless of the specific form of f within the bounds.

Q17. Which statement correctly generalizes the Squeeze Theorem for limits at \(+\infty\)?

A.If \(g(x)\le f(x)\le h(x)\) for large x and \(\lim_{x\to\infty}g=\lim_{x\to\infty}h=L\), then \(\lim_{x\to\infty}f=L\). ✅
B.If \(g(x)\le f(x)\le h(x)\) for large x and \(\lim_{x\to\infty}g\) exists, then \(\lim_{x\to\infty}f\) exists.
C.If \(g(x)\le f(x)\le h(x)\) for all x, then \(\lim_{x\to\infty}f\) equals the average of the limits of g and h.
D.If \(g(x)\le f(x)\le h(x)\) and both limits are finite, then f is bounded.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The theorem extends to infinite limits: when two functions bound a third for all sufficiently large x and share the same finite limit L as \(x\to\infty\), the squeezed function must also converge to L. The other statements either omit necessary conditions or assert incorrect conclusions.
