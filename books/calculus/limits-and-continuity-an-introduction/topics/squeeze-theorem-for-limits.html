What is Squeeze theorem for limits?

Definition:
The Squeeze Theorem states that if g(x)≤f(x)≤h(x) g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x) g(x)≤f(x)≤h(x) for all x x x near a a a (except possibly at a a a) and lim⁡x→ag(x)=lim⁡x→ah(x)=L \lim_{x \to a} g(x) = \lim_{x \to a} h(x) = L limx→a​g(x)=limx→a​h(x)=L, then lim⁡x→af(x)=L \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L limx→a​f(x)=L. This theorem is used to evaluate limits of functions that are difficult to compute directly by squeezing" them between two simpler functions with known limits.

Example:
Find lim⁡x→0x2sin⁡(1/x) \lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin(1/x) limx→0​x2sin(1/x).
Solution: −x2≤x2sin⁡(1/x)≤x2 -x^2 \le x^2 \sin(1/x) \le x^2 −x2≤x2sin(1/x)≤x2, and lim⁡x2=0 \lim x^2 = 0 limx2=0, so by Squeeze Theorem, limit is 0 0 0.

Reason:
The Squeeze Theorem is invaluable for proving limits of oscillating functions and for establishing fundamental limits like lim⁡x→0sin⁡xx \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} limx→0​xsinx​, which are central to calculus and analysis."

Q1. What does the Squeezing Theorem assert about the limit of a function f when it is bounded between two functions g and h that share the same limit L as x approaches c?

A.If g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x) and lim g = lim h = L, then lim f = L. ✅
B.If g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x) and lim g = lim h = L, then lim f may differ from L.
C.If g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x), then lim f exists regardless of limits of g and h.
D.If g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x) and lim g = lim h = L, then lim f does not exist.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The theorem requires three conditions: g and h bound f, g and h have the same limit L at c, and the inequalities hold on a punctured neighborhood of c. When these are satisfied, any function f squeezed between them must share the same limit L, guaranteeing lim f = L.
