What is Limits at infinity as x approaches infinity?

Limits at infinity describe the end behavior of a function as \( x \) increases or decreases without bound, written as \( \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = L \) or \( \lim_{x \to -\infty} f(x) = L \), focusing on long-term trends rather than local behavior.

Q1. What is the definition of a horizontal asymptote for a function f(x) as x→+∞?

A. A line y = L such that lim_{x→+∞} f(x) = L ✅
B. A line x = L such that lim_{x→+∞} f(x) = L
C. A point where f(x) is undefined
D. A line y = L such that lim_{x→-∞} f(x) = L

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A horizontal asymptote is a horizontal line that the graph of the function approaches as x grows without bound. Formally, if the limit of f(x) as x→+∞ equals a constant L, then y = L is a horizontal asymptote.

Q2. Evaluate lim_{x→+∞} 1/x.

A. 1
B. ∞
C. 0 ✅
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

As x becomes larger and larger, the denominator grows without bound while the numerator stays at 1. The fraction therefore gets arbitrarily close to zero, so the limit is 0. The answer choice representing zero is the correct one.

Q3. Suppose lim_{x→+∞} f(x)=0. Which statement must be true about lim_{x→+∞} 1/(f(x)+1)?

A. 0
B. ∞
C. does not exist
D. 1 ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

If f(x) approaches 0, then f(x)+1 approaches 1. The reciprocal of a quantity that approaches 1 also approaches 1. Therefore the limit of 1/(f(x)+1) is 1, making the fourth option the correct choice.

Q4. If lim_{x→-∞} g(x)=5 and h(x)=2g(x)-3, what is lim_{x→-∞} h(x)?

A. 7
B. 10 ✅
C. 2
D. 5

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Substituting the limiting value of g(x) into h(x) gives h(x)≈2·5−3=10−3=7. Hence the limit of h(x) as x→-∞ is 7, which appears as the second option.

Q5. Given that lim_{x→+∞} p(x)=L exists and is finite, which statement about lim_{x→+∞} p(x)/x is necessarily true?

A. 0 ✅
B. L
C. ∞
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Since p(x) approaches a finite number L, the numerator stays bounded while the denominator grows without bound. A bounded quantity divided by an unbounded one tends to 0, so the limit must be 0.

Q6. For the function f(x)=(x^2-1)/(x^2+2x+1), determine lim_{x→-∞} f(x).

A. 1
B. -1
C. 0 ✅
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Both numerator and denominator are quadratic with leading coefficient 1. As |x| becomes large, lower‑order terms become negligible, so the ratio approaches 1. The third option correctly states this limit.

Q7. Compare the limits lim_{x→+∞}(2x+3)/x and lim_{x→+∞} x/(2x+3). Which statement is correct?

A. Both limits equal 2
B. First limit equals 2, second equals 0.5 ✅
C. First limit equals 2, second equals 0
D. Both limits equal 1

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The first expression simplifies to 2+3/x, whose limit is 2. The second simplifies to 1/(2+3/x), whose limit is 0 because the denominator tends to 2, making the fraction approach 0. Hence the second option is correct.

Q8. Which rational function has a horizontal asymptote at y=3 as x→+∞?

A. (3x^2+1)/(x^2-4)
B. (3x+5)/(2x-1)
C. (6x^2-3)/(2x^2+7)
D. x^3/(3x^3+1) ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The horizontal asymptote is given by the ratio of leading coefficients. For (3x^2+1)/(x^2-4) the ratio is 3/1=3, but the correct answer is placed in option D after rearranging the list, so D is the right choice.

Q9. Consider f(x)=(x^3+2x)/(x^3-5). Which description best fits its end behavior?

A. Approaches y=1 from above
B. Approaches y=1 from below
C. No horizontal asymptote ✅
D. Approaches y=0

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Both numerator and denominator have the same highest degree with leading coefficient 1, so the limit is 1. For large positive x the extra +2x makes the numerator slightly larger, so the graph approaches y=1 from above, matching the third option.

Q10. The function f(x)=tan^{-1}x has horizontal asymptotes at which y-values?

A. y=±π/2
B. y=0
C. y=π ✅
D. none

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

As x→+∞, arctan x approaches π/2, and as x→-∞ it approaches -π/2. Thus the graph has two horizontal asymptotes at y=π/2 and y=-π/2, which is the first answer choice.

Q11. If g(x)=1/x and h(x)=sin(g(x)), what is lim_{x→+∞} h(x)?

A. 0
B. 1
C. does not exist
D. 0 (since sin(0)=0) ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

When x grows, g(x)=1/x tends to 0. The sine of a quantity that approaches 0 also approaches 0. Therefore the limit of h(x) is 0, which appears as the fourth option.

Q12. For the composite function F(x)=1/atan(x), determine lim_{x→+∞} F(x).

A. 0
B. 2/π
C. π/2 ✅
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

As x→+∞, atan x approaches π/2. Taking the reciprocal gives 1/(π/2)=2/π. Hence the second option correctly gives the limit.

Q13. Let f(x)=x^2/√(x^4+1). Evaluate lim_{x→-∞} f(x).

A. -1
B. 0
C. 1 ✅
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

For large |x|, √(x^4+1)=|x^2|·√(1+1/x^4)≈|x^2|. Since |x^2|=x^2, the fraction simplifies to x^2/x^2=1. Thus the limit is 1, which is the third option.

Q14. If lim_{x→-∞} k(x)=L and L≠0, what is lim_{x→-∞} 1/k(x)?

A. 1/L
B. 0
C. ∞ ✅
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When k(x) approaches a non‑zero constant L, its reciprocal approaches 1/L. This follows directly from the continuity of the reciprocal function at non‑zero points, making the first option correct.

Q15. For which values of a does f(x)=(x^2+ax+1)/(x^2+1) have a horizontal asymptote y=1 as x→±∞?

A. all real a
B. a=0
C. a=2
D. no such a ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The horizontal asymptote depends on the ratio of leading coefficients, which are both 1 regardless of a. Therefore the function always approaches y=1 for any real a, so the correct answer is the first option, placed as D after shuffling.

Q16. Evaluate lim_{x→+∞} (e^{x}+x)/e^{x}.

A. 0
B. 1 ✅
C. ∞
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The term e^{x} dominates both the numerator and denominator. Dividing numerator and denominator by e^{x} yields 1 + x/e^{x}, and x/e^{x}→0. Hence the limit equals 1, which is the second option.

Q17. Let f(x)=ln x / x. What is lim_{x→+∞} f(x)?

A. 0
B. 1
C. ∞ ✅
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The logarithm grows much more slowly than the linear term x, so the ratio ln x / x tends to 0 as x→∞. The fourth option correctly states this limit.

Q18. Suppose lim_{x→+∞} m(x)=5 and lim_{x→+∞} n(x)=∞. What is lim_{x→+∞} m(x)/n(x)?

A. 0
B. 5
C. ∞ ✅
D. does not exist

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A finite constant divided by a quantity that grows without bound approaches 0. Therefore the limit of the quotient is 0, which corresponds to the first answer choice.
