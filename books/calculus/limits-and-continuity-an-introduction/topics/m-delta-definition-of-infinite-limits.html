What is M delta definition of infinite limits?

Definition:
The M-delta definition for an infinite limit \( \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = \infty \) states that for every positive number \( M \), there exists a \( \delta > 0 \) such that if \( 0 < |x-a| < \delta \), then \( f(x) > M \). This formalizes the idea that \( f(x) \) can be made arbitrarily large by taking \( x \) sufficiently close to \( a \), providing a rigorous basis for vertical asymptotes and unbounded behavior.

Example:
Prove \( \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{1}{(x-1)^2} = \infty \).
Solution: For \( M > 0 \), choose \( \delta = 1/\sqrt{M} \). If \( 0 < |x-1| < \delta \), then \( 1/(x-1)^2 > 1/\delta^2 = M \).

Reason:
This definition is essential for rigorously proving infinite limits, which is necessary for understanding singularities in physical models and for advanced calculus topics like improper integrals.
