Q1. Which of the following statements correctly describes continuity of a function \\(f\\) on a closed interval \\([a,b]\\)?

🔹 A. \\(f\\) is continuous on \\((a,b)\\) only.
🔹 B. \\(f\\) is continuous on \\((a,b)\\), continuous from the right at \\(a\\), and continuous from the left at \\(b\\).
🔹 C. \\(f\\) must be differentiable on \\([a,b]\\).
🔹 D. \\(f\\) is continuous at \\(a\\) and \\(b\\) but not necessarily on \\((a,b)\\).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The definition of continuity on a closed interval requires three conditions: continuity on the open interval, continuity from the right at the left endpoint, and continuity from the left at the right endpoint. Option B lists exactly those conditions, making it the correct description.

Q2. What does it mean for a function \\(f\\) to be continuous from the right at a point \\(c\\)?

🔹 A. \\(\\lim_{x\\to c^{-}} f(x)=f(c)\\)
🔹 B. \\(\\lim_{x\\to c^{+}} f(x)=f(c)\\)
🔹 C. Both one‑sided limits equal \\(f(c)\\)
🔹 D. No limit is required for continuity from the right

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Continuity from the right uses the right‑hand limit: the function value at \\(c\\) must equal the limit as \\(x\\) approaches \\(c\\) from values greater than \\(c\\). This is precisely statement B.

Q3. Consider \\(f(x)=\\sqrt{9-x^{2}}\\) and \\(g(x)=|x|\\) on the interval \\([-3,3]\\). Which statement is true?

🔹 A. Both are continuous on \\([-3,3]\\).
🔹 B. \\(f\\) is continuous, \\(g\\) is not.
🔹 C. \\(g\\) is continuous, \\(f\\) is not.
🔹 D. Neither is continuous.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Both functions are compositions of continuous elementary functions on their domains, and their domains include the entire interval \\([-3,3]\\). Therefore each is continuous everywhere on that interval, making option A correct.

Q4. If \\(f\\) and \\(g\\) are continuous at a point \\(c\\), which of the following is guaranteed to be continuous at \\(c\\)?

🔹 A. \\(f+g\\)
🔹 B. \\(f-g\\)
🔹 C. \\(f\\cdot g\\)
🔹 D. All of the above

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Theorem 1.5.3 states that the sum, difference, product, and quotient (when the denominator is non‑zero) of continuous functions are continuous at the same point. Hence all listed combinations are continuous, so option D is correct.

Q5. Let \\(h(x)=\\begin{cases}x^{2}, & 0<x\\le1\\\\2x-1, & 1<x<2\\end{cases}\\). Is \\(h\\) continuous on the open interval \\((0,2)\\)?

🔹 A. Yes, because each piece is continuous and they match at \\(x=1\\).
🔹 B. No, because there is a jump at \\(x=1\\).
🔹 C. Yes, but only on \\((0,1)\\).
🔹 D. No, because the domain excludes 0 and 2.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Both pieces are polynomial functions, which are continuous on their domains. At \\(x=1\\) the left‑hand value \\(1^{2}=1\\) equals the right‑hand value \\(2(1)-1=1\\), so the function matches there. Hence \\(h\\) is continuous throughout \\((0,2)\\).

Q6. Which interval(s) guarantee that the function \\(f(x)=1/x\\) is continuous?

🔹 A. \\((-1,1)\\)
🔹 B. \\((0,\\infty)\\)
🔹 C. \\(( -\\infty,0)\\)
🔹 D. Both B and C

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The function \\(1/x\\) is continuous on any interval that does not contain the point where the denominator is zero. Both \\((0,\\infty)\\) and \\(( -\\infty,0)\\) avoid zero, so the union of those intervals (option D) correctly describes where \\(f\\) is continuous.

Q7. Let \\(p(x)=\\sin x\\) and \\(q(x)=\\sqrt{x}\\) defined on \\([0,\\pi]\\). Which statement about the product \\(r(x)=p(x)q(x)\\) is correct?

🔹 A. \\(r\\) is continuous on \\([0,\\pi]\\) because both factors are continuous on that interval.
🔹 B. \\(r\\) is discontinuous at \\(x=0\\).
🔹 C. \\(r\\) is continuous only on \\((0,\\pi]\\).
🔹 D. Continuity cannot be determined without derivatives.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Both \\(\\sin x\\) and \\(\\sqrt{x}\\) are continuous on \\([0,\\pi]\\). The product of continuous functions is continuous at every point where both are defined, so \\(r(x)\\) is continuous on the entire closed interval.

Q8. If \\(\\lim_{x\\to a^{+}} f(x)=f(a)=3\\), which of the following must be true?

🔹 A. \\(f\\) is continuous from the left at \\(a\\).
🔹 B. \\(f\\) is continuous from the right at \\(a\\).
🔹 C. \\(f\\) is discontinuous at \\(a\\).
🔹 D. No conclusion can be drawn.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Continuity from the right at a point requires the right‑hand limit to equal the function value. The given equality satisfies this condition, so \\(f\\) is indeed continuous from the right at \\(a\\).

Q9. Define \\(k(x)=\\begin{cases}\\frac{\\sin x}{x},&x\\neq0\\\\1,&x=0\\end{cases}\\). Is \\(k\\) continuous at \\(x=0\\)?

🔹 A. Yes, because the limit equals 1.
🔹 B. No, because the limit does not exist.
🔹 C. Yes, because the function is defined piecewise.
🔹 D. No, because \\(\\frac{\\sin x}{x}\\) is undefined at 0.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Using the standard limit \\(\\lim_{x\\to0}\\frac{\\sin x}{x}=1\\), the piecewise definition matches the limit value, so the function is continuous at \\(0\\).

Q10. The function \\(m(x)=e^{-x}\\) is continuous on which of the following intervals?

🔹 A. \\(( -\\infty,\\infty)\\)
🔹 B. \\([0,\\infty)\\)
🔹 C. \\(( -\\infty,0]\\)
🔹 D. All of the above

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The exponential function is continuous for every real argument, so it remains continuous on any subinterval, including all options listed. Hence option D, stating all of the above, is correct.

Q11. Which of the following statements is always true for any function \\(f\\)?

🔹 A. If \\(f\\) is continuous on \\([a,b]\\) then it is also continuous on \\((a,b)\\).
🔹 B. If \\(f\\) is continuous on \\((a,b)\\) then it must be continuous at \\(a\\) and \\(b\\).
🔹 C. Continuity on \\([a,b]\\) implies differentiability on \\((a,b)\\).
🔹 D. None of the above.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Continuity on a closed interval includes continuity on the interior open interval by definition. The other statements are false in general, making option A the universally correct choice.

Q12. Let \\(f\\) be continuous on \\([2,5]\\). Which reasoning correctly justifies that \\(f\\) attains a maximum value on this interval?

🔹 A. Because a continuous function on a closed interval is bounded and the supremum is achieved.
🔹 B. Because the Intermediate Value Theorem guarantees a maximum.
🔹 C. Because differentiability forces a critical point.
🔹 D. Because the function can be extended to a larger interval.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The Extreme Value Theorem states that a continuous function on a closed, bounded interval achieves both its supremum and infimum within that interval. This reasoning explains why a maximum must exist, making option A correct.

Q13. The function \\(g(x)=\\frac{x^{2}-4}{x-2}\\) has a discontinuity at \\(x=2\\). Which modification makes \\(g\\) continuous on \\(\\mathbb{R}\\)?

🔹 A. Define \\(g(2)=0\\).
🔹 B. Define \\(g(2)=4\\).
🔹 C. Define \\(g(2)=2\\).
🔹 D. No modification can make it continuous.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Factoring the numerator gives \\((x-2)(x+2)\\); cancelling the \\((x-2)\\) factor yields \\(x+2\\) for \\(x\\neq2\\). The limit as \\(x\\to2\\) is \\(4\\), so defining \\(g(2)=4\\) removes the removable discontinuity.

Q14. Suppose \\(h\\) is continuous on \\((0,1)\\) and \\(\\lim_{x\\to0^{+}}h(x)=5\\). Which statement is true regarding continuity of \\(h\\) on \\([0,1)\\)?

🔹 A. \\(h\\) is continuous on \\([0,1)\\) after defining \\(h(0)=5\\).
🔹 B. \\(h\\) cannot be continuous on \\([0,1)\\) because it is undefined at 0.
🔹 C. Continuity on \\([0,1)\\) is independent of the limit at 0.
🔹 D. \\(h\\) is automatically continuous on \\([0,1)\\).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Continuity at the left endpoint requires the function value to equal the right‑hand limit. By assigning \\(h(0)=5\\), the definition matches the limit, satisfying the endpoint continuity condition.

Q15. Let \\(f(x)=x^{3}-x-2\\). Given that \\(f\\) is continuous on \\([1,2]\\) and \\(f(1)=-2\\), \\(f(2)=4\\), which conclusion follows from the Intermediate Value Theorem?

🔹 A. There exists a \\(c\\in(1,2)\\) with \\(f(c)=0\\).
🔹 B. No root exists in \\([1,2]\\).
🔹 C. \\(f\\) is monotonic on \\([1,2]\\).
🔹 D. The theorem does not apply because \\(f\\) is not differentiable.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The Intermediate Value Theorem guarantees that a continuous function takes every value between its endpoints. Since 0 lies between \\(-2\\) and \\(4\\), there must be some \\(c\\) in \\((1,2)\\) where \\(f(c)=0\\).

Q16. The function \\(s(x)=\\sum_{n=1}^{\\infty}\\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\\) equals \\(e^{x}-1\\). Which statement best describes its continuity?

🔹 A. \\(s\\) is continuous only on \\((-\\infty,\\infty)\\).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The series for \\(e^{x}\\) converges uniformly on every bounded interval, and uniform convergence preserves continuity. Since the sum equals \\(e^{x}-1\\), a known continuous function, \\(s\\) is continuous for all real \\(x\\).
