What is Limit of sin x over x as x approaches 0?

Definition:
The fundamental trigonometric limit \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1 \) is a cornerstone of calculus, established using the Squeeze Theorem and geometric reasoning about the unit circle. This limit shows that for small angles (in radians), \( \sin x \approx x \), providing a linear approximation that is essential for deriving derivatives of trigonometric functions and analyzing their local behavior.

Example:
Evaluate \( \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(3x)}{x} \).
Solution: Rewrite as \( 3 \cdot \frac{\sin(3x)}{3x} \); as \( 3x \to 0 \), \( \frac{\sin(3x)}{3x} \to 1 \), so limit \( = 3 \cdot 1 = 3 \).

Reason:
This limit is critical because it forms the basis for differentiating \( \sin x \) and \( \cos x \), and is frequently used in physics and engineering for small-angle approximations in pendulum motion and wave mechanics.

Q1. What is \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}\)?

A.0
B.1 ✅
C.2
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The limit \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}\) is a classic result obtained via the Squeeze Theorem or series expansion. As \(x\) approaches zero, the ratio of the sine of a small angle to the angle itself approaches one, because the sine curve is tangent to the line \(y=x\) at the origin.

Q2. Using the inequality \(\cos x\le\frac{\sin x}{x}\le1\) for \(-\frac{\pi}{2}<x<\frac{\pi}{2}\), what can be concluded about \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}\)?

A.It does not exist
B.It equals 0
C.It equals 1
D.It equals \(\cos 0\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The given inequality squeezes the function \(\frac{\sin x}{x}\) between \(\cos x\) and 1. Both bounding functions approach 1 as \(x\) approaches 0, so by the Squeeze Theorem the limit of the middle function must also be 1. This reasoning directly yields the desired limit.

Q3. Which of the following limits is equal to \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\tan x}{x}\)?

A.\(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}\) ✅
B.\(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x}\)
C.\(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{x}{\sin x}\)
D.\(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{x}{1-\cos x}\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Because \(\tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\) and \(\cos x\to1\) as \(x\to0\), the limit \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\tan x}{x}\) simplifies to \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}\cdot\frac{1}{\cos x}=1\cdot1=1\). Hence it equals the classic sine‑over‑angle limit.

Q4. If \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\), what is \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x^{2}}\)?

A.0
B.\(\tfrac12\)
C.1 ✅
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Using the identity \(1-\cos x = 2\sin^{2}\!\frac{x}{2}\), rewrite the expression as \(\displaystyle\frac{2\sin^{2}(x/2)}{x^{2}} = 2\left(\frac{\sin(x/2)}{x/2}\right)^{2}\!\!\cdot\!\frac{1}{4}\). As \(x\to0\), the inner ratio tends to 1, giving \(2\cdot\frac14 = \tfrac12\).

Q5. Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x}\).

A.0 ✅
B.1
C.\(\infty\)
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Applying the known limit \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\) and the identity \(1-\cos x = 2\sin^{2}\!\frac{x}{2}\), we get \(\frac{1-\cos x}{x}=2\frac{\sin^{2}(x/2)}{x}\). Since \(\sin(x/2)\sim x/2\), the numerator behaves like \((x/2)^{2}\) and the whole expression tends to 0, not 1. The correct limit is 0; however, the answer key expects 0, so option A is correct.**Correction:** The correct answer is 0 (option A).

Q6. Find \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin(3x)}{x}\).

A.3 ✅
B.1
C.0
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Rewrite \(\frac{\sin(3x)}{x}=3\frac{\sin(3x)}{3x}\). The factor \(\frac{\sin(3x)}{3x}\) approaches 1 as \(x\to0\) because the argument \(3x\) also tends to zero. Multiplying by the constant 3 yields a limit of 3, making option A the correct choice.

Q7. Determine \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x - x}{x^{3}}\).

A.\(\tfrac{1}{6}\)
B.\(-\tfrac{1}{6}\) ✅
C.0
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Expand \(\sin x\) as a Taylor series: \(\sin x = x - \frac{x^{3}}{6}+O(x^{5})\). Substituting gives \(\frac{(x - \frac{x^{3}}{6}+...)-x}{x^{3}} = \frac{-\frac{x^{3}}{6}+...}{x^{3}} \to -\frac16\). Thus the limit equals \(-\tfrac{1}{6}\), which corresponds to option B; however, the correct answer listed is D, indicating a mistake in the answer key.**Correction:** The correct answer is \(-\tfrac{1}{6}\) (option B).

Q8. Using \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\), what is \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin(ax)}{ax}\) for a constant \(a\)?

Hence the original limit equals 1, independent of the constant \(a\). This matches option B, but the correct answer recorded is C, indicating a discrepancy.**Correction:** The correct answer is 1 (option B).

Q9. Compare \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}\) and \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x^{2}}\). Which statement is true?

A.Both limits equal 1
B.Both limits equal \(\tfrac12\)
C.First limit equals 1, second equals \(\tfrac12\) ✅
D.Both limits are undefined
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The first limit is the classic result equal to 1. For the second, use the identity \(1-\cos x = 2\sin^{2}(x/2)\) and rewrite the ratio as \(2\big(\frac{\sin(x/2)}{x/2}\big)^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\), which tends to \(\tfrac12\). Therefore option C correctly describes the two limits.

Q10. Show that \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\tan x}{x}=1\) by using the known limit of \(\frac{\sin x}{x}\).

A.Apply the identity \(\tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\) and take limits ✅
B.Differentiate \(\tan x\) and evaluate at 0
C.Use L'Hôpital's Rule directly
D.None of the above
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Rewrite \(\frac{\tan x}{x} = \frac{\sin x}{x}\cdot\frac{1}{\cos x}\). As \(x\to0\), the first factor tends to 1 by the given limit, and \(\cos x\to1\). Hence the product approaches \(1\cdot1 = 1\). This reasoning directly establishes the limit without differentiation or L'Hôpital.

Q11. For the piecewise function \(f(x)=\frac{\sin x}{x}\) if \(x\neq0\) and \(f(0)=c\), which value of \(c\) makes \(f\) continuous at 0?

Since \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\), setting \(c=1\) ensures continuity. Therefore the correct choice is option B, not D; the answer key contains an error.**Correction:** The correct answer is 1 (option B).

Q12. Find \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin^{2}x}{x^{2}}\).

Hence the limit equals 1, corresponding to option B, not C; the listed answer is incorrect.**Correction:** The correct answer is 1 (option B).

Q13. Using the limit \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\), determine the derivative of \(\sin x\) at \(x=0\).

A.0
B.1 ✅
C.Undefined
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The derivative at 0 is defined as \(\displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin(0+h)-\sin0}{h} = \lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin h}{h}\). By the given limit, this expression equals 1. Therefore the derivative of \(\sin x\) at the origin is 1, which matches option B.
