What is Limit laws sum product quotient?

Definition:
Limit laws provide algebraic rules for evaluating limits of combinations of functions: the sum law \( \lim (f+g) = \lim f + \lim g \), the product law \( \lim (fg) = (\lim f)(\lim g) \), and the quotient law \( \lim (f/g) = (\lim f)/(\lim g) \) provided \( \lim g \neq 0 \). These laws allow us to break limits of sums, products, and quotients into simpler limits, provided each individual limit exists.

Example:
Given \( \lim_{x \to 2} f(x)=3 \) and \( \lim_{x \to 2} g(x)=4 \), find \( \lim_{x \to 2} (2f(x) + g(x)/f(x)) \).
Solution: \( 2(3) + 4/3 = 6 + 4/3 = 22/3 \).

Reason:
These laws simplify limit evaluation, making it systematic and reliable, which is crucial for finding limits of polynomial, rational, and other composite functions without tedious direct substitution in complex expressions.

Q1. If \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} f(x)=3\) and \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} g(x)=-2\), what is \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} (2f(x)-g(x))\)?

A.8 ✅
B.4
C.10
D.5
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Applying the constant multiple law gives \(\lim 2f(x)=2\cdot3=6\). The difference law yields \(\lim (2f(x)-g(x))=6-(-2)=8\). Therefore the limit equals 8, making option A correct.

Q2. Given that \(\lim_{x\to-\infty} h(x)=L\) exists, which of the following must be true about \(\lim_{x\to-\infty} (h(x))^{3}\)?

A.\(\displaystyle L^{3}\)
B.L ✅
C.|L|
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The power law for limits at infinity states that the limit of a power equals the power of the limit, provided the original limit exists. Hence \(\lim (h(x))^{3}=L^{3}\), which corresponds to option B.

Q3. Suppose \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} f(x)=0\) and \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} \frac{1}{f(x)}=\infty\). Which statement follows?

A.\(f(x)\) approaches 0 from the positive side
B.\(f(x)\) approaches 0 from the negative side
C.\(f(x)\) oscillates near zero
D.Insufficient information to determine sign ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The given information only tells us that the magnitude of \(f(x)\) shrinks to zero while its reciprocal grows without bound. It provides no insight into the sign of \(f(x)\) as \(x\to+\infty\); thus we cannot conclude which side it approaches from, making D correct.

Q4. Using the constant multiple law, compute \(\lim_{x\to-\infty} 5\cdot\frac{1}{x}\).

A.0 ✅
B.-5
C.5
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The factor 5 can be pulled out of the limit, giving \(5\cdot\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{1}{x}\). Since \(\frac{1}{x}\) tends to 0 as \(x\to-\infty\), the product is \(5\cdot0=0\). Hence option A is correct.

Q5. If \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} f(x)=4\) and \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} g(x)=\infty\), what can be said about \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\)?

A.0
B.4 ✅
C.\(\infty\)
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When the numerator approaches a finite number (4) and the denominator grows without bound, the quotient approaches 0. This follows from the quotient law for limits at infinity, so the correct answer is 0, listed as option B.

Q6. Consider \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{x^{2}-x}{x^{2}}\). Using limit laws, what is the limit?

A.1
B.0
C.-1
D.Does not exist ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Divide numerator and denominator by \(x^{2}\) to obtain \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}(1-\frac{1}{x})\). The term \(\frac{1}{x}\) tends to 0, leaving a limit of 1. Since option D corresponds to 1, it is the correct choice.

Q7. Compare \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{x}\) and \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{x^{2}}\). Which statement is true?

A.Both limits equal 0 ✅
B.First limit is 0, second is 1
C.Both limits diverge to \(\infty\)
D.First limit diverges, second equals 0
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Both \(\frac{1}{x}\) and \(\frac{1}{x^{2}}\) shrink to zero as \(x\) grows without bound. The limit laws for powers of \(x\) confirm that each limit is 0, making option A correct.

Q8. It follows from the limit laws that \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\left(1+\frac{1}{2x}\right)^{2x}=e\). Which law is primarily used?

A.Product law
B.Power law
C.Continuity of the exponential function
D.Limit \((1+\frac{1}{n})^{n}\) as \(n\to\infty\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Rewrite the expression as \(\left[\left(1+\frac{1}{2x}\right)^{2x}\right]\) and recognize the familiar form \((1+\frac{1}{n})^{n}\) with \(n=2x\). The known limit \(\lim_{n\to\infty}(1+\frac{1}{n})^{n}=e\) directly yields the result, so option D is correct.

Q9. Given \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} f(x)=L\neq0\) and a constant \(k\), determine \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{k}{f(x)}\).

A.\(\displaystyle \frac{k}{L}\)
B.\(\displaystyle \frac{L}{k}\)
C.0 ✅
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Apply the constant multiple law to write \(\frac{k}{f(x)}=k\cdot\frac{1}{f(x)}\). The reciprocal limit law gives \(\lim \frac{1}{f(x)}=\frac{1}{L}\). Multiplying by \(k\) yields \(\frac{k}{L}\). Since this matches option C, it is correct.

Q10. Which of the following is NOT a limit law that holds for limits at infinity?

A.Sum law
B.Product law
C.Quotient law
D.Intermediate value law ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The intermediate value property is a theorem about continuous functions on closed intervals, not a limit law. All other listed laws (sum, product, quotient) extend to limits at infinity, so option D is the correct answer.

Q11. For a positive integer \(n\), compare \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{1}{x^{n}}\) with \(\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{1}{x^{n}}\). Which statement holds?

A.Both limits equal 0
B.First limit is 0, second does not exist ✅
C.Both limits diverge
D.First limit diverges, second equals 0
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When \(n\) is even, \(\frac{1}{x^{n}}\) is positive for both large positive and negative \(x\), and each tends to 0. When \(n\) is odd, the sign changes but the magnitude still approaches 0. Thus both limits are 0, making option B the correct choice.

Q12. Suppose \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} f(x)=a\) and \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} g(x)=b\) with both finite. Using limit laws, which expression equals \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}(f(x)g(x)+3)\)?

A.\(ab+3\) ✅
B.\(a+b+3\)
C.\(a\cdot b+3\)
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The product law gives \(\lim f(x)g(x)=ab\). Adding a constant uses the sum law, so \(\lim (f(x)g(x)+3)=ab+3\). This matches option A.

Q13. Apply limit laws to find \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{3x^{2}-2x}{x^{2}}\).

A.3 ✅
B.1
C.0
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Separate the fraction: \(\frac{3x^{2}}{x^{2}}-\frac{2x}{x^{2}}=3-\frac{2}{x}\). The term \(\frac{2}{x}\) tends to 0 as \(x\to+\infty\), leaving a limit of 3. Hence option A is correct.

Q14. Explain why \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\left(1+\frac{1}{x}\right)^{x}=e\) follows from limit laws.

A.Because of continuity of the exponential function
B.Because the base approaches 1 while the exponent grows
C.Because of the power law
D.Because it matches the definition of \(e\) via \((1+\frac{1}{n})^{n}\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The expression is precisely the classic sequence defining \(e\). By recognizing \((1+\frac{1}{x})^{x}\) as \((1+\frac{1}{n})^{n}\) with \(n=x\) and applying the known limit \(\lim_{n\to\infty}(1+\frac{1}{n})^{n}=e\), we justify the result, so option D is correct.

Q15. Given \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} f(x)=0\) and \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} g(x)=\infty\), evaluate \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} f(x)^{g(x)}\).

A.0
B.1
C.\(\infty\)
D.Indeterminate form ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The expression \(0^{\infty}\) is an indeterminate form; the limit can depend on the rates at which \(f(x)\) approaches zero and \(g(x)\) diverges. Limit laws do not resolve this case, so the appropriate answer is that the form is indeterminate, option D.

Q16. Using the constant function limit law, what is \(\lim_{x\to-\infty}7\)?

A.7
B.0 ✅
C.Does not exist
D.\(\infty\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A constant function does not change with \(x\). The limit law for constants at infinity states that the limit equals the constant itself, so \(\lim_{x\to-\infty}7=7\). This corresponds to option B.

Q17. If \(\lim_{x\to+\infty} f(x)=L\) with \(L>0\), which law allows us to assert \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\sqrt{f(x)}=\sqrt{L}\)?

A.Power law (exponent \(1/2\)) ✅
B.Continuity of the square‑root function
C.Product law
D.Sum law
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The power law extends to fractional exponents: \(\lim (f(x))^{1/2}=(\lim f(x))^{1/2}\) when the limit exists and is non‑negative. Since \(L>0\), we may apply this law directly, giving option A.

Q18. Consider \(f_n(x)=\frac{x}{n}\). Determine \(\lim_{n\to\infty}\lim_{x\to+\infty}f_n(x)\) and compare with \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}f_n(x)\).

A.Both limits equal 0
B.First limit 0, second does not exist
C.First does not exist, second equals 0 ✅
D.Both limits do not exist
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: For each fixed \(n\), \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{x}{n}=+\infty\); then \(\lim_{n\to\infty}(+\infty)\) is undefined. Conversely, for each fixed \(x\), \(\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{x}{n}=0\); then \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}0=0\). Thus the first limit does not exist while the second equals 0, matching option C.

📖 Explanation: The passage explicitly mentions that the limit laws in Theorem 1.2.2 carry over unchanged to limits at \(+\infty\) and \(-\infty\). Therefore Theorem 1.2.2 is the correct reference, option A.

Q20. According to the limit laws, what is \(\lim_{x\to+\infty}k\) for a constant \(k\)?

A.\(k\)
B.0
C.\(k\) ✅
