What is Continuity of composite functions?

Definition:
If \( g \) is continuous at \( a \) and \( f \) is continuous at \( g(a) \), then the composite function \( f \circ g \) (i.e., \( f(g(x)) \)) is continuous at \( a \). This means that the limit of a composite can be found by applying the outer function to the limit of the inner, \( \lim_{x \to a} f(g(x)) = f(\lim_{x \to a} g(x)) \), provided the inner limit exists and the outer is continuous at that value.

Example:
Show \( h(x) = \sin(x^2) \) is continuous at \( x=0 \).
Solution: \( g(x)=x^2 \) continuous, \( f(u)=\sin u \) continuous, so \( h \) continuous at \( 0 \); limit \( = \sin(0)=0 \).

Reason:
This theorem allows us to extend continuity to complex functions, which is essential for evaluating limits of nested functions and for ensuring that compositions of well-behaved functions remain well-behaved in applications.

📝 All Continuity of composite functions MCQs

Q1. What does it mean for a function \(f\) to be continuous at a point \(a\)?

A. The limit \(\lim_{x\to a} f(x)\) exists and equals \(f(a)\). ✅
B. The function \(f\) is differentiable at \(a\).
C. Both the limit exists and equals \(f(a)\).
D. Only that \(f(a)\) is defined.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

A function is continuous at \(a\) precisely when the limit as \(x\) approaches \(a\) exists and matches the function’s value at that point. This captures the intuitive idea that there is no jump or break in the graph at \(a\).

Q2. According to Theorem 1.5.5, if \(\lim_{x\to c} g(x)=L\) and \(f\) is continuous at \(L\), then which of the following equalities holds?

A. \(\lim_{x\to c} f(g(x)) = L\).
B. \(\lim_{x\to c} f(g(x)) = f(L)\).
C. \(\lim_{x\to c} g(f(x)) = f(L)\). ✅
D. \(\lim_{x\to c} f(g(x))\) does not exist.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The theorem explicitly allows the limit operator to pass through a continuous outer function, yielding \(\lim_{x\to c} f(g(x)) = f\big(\lim_{x\to c} g(x)\big)=f(L)\). The other options either misuse composition order or ignore the continuity condition.

Q3. If \(g\) is continuous at \(c\) but \(f\) is discontinuous at \(g(c)\), what can be concluded about the continuity of the composition \(f\circ g\) at \(c\)?

A. It must be continuous because \(g\) is continuous.
B. It is discontinuous because the discontinuity of \(f\) is transferred through the composition.
C. Continuity cannot be determined without more information about \(f\). ✅
D. It is continuous only from the left.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Continuity of a composition requires the outer function to be continuous at the image point. Since \(f\) fails that condition at \(g(c)\), the composition inherits the discontinuity, making \(f\circ g\) discontinuous at \(c\).

Q4. Suppose \(\lim_{x\to c^-} g(x)=L\) and \(f\) is continuous from the right at \(L\). Which of the following statements is true about \(\lim_{x\to c^-} f(g(x))\)?

A. The limit equals \(f(L)\). ✅
B. The limit does not exist.
C. The limit equals \(L\).
D. Continuity of \(f\) from the right is irrelevant.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When the inner limit approaches \(L\) from the left and the outer function is continuous from the right at that same point, the composition’s left‑hand limit equals the outer function evaluated at \(L\), i.e., \(f(L)\). The directional continuity of \(f\) is sufficient.

Q5. Let \(g(x)=\begin{cases} x^2 & x\le 0\\ 2x & x>0 \end{cases}\) and \(f(y)=\sqrt{y+1}\). Determine whether \(\lim_{x\to 0} f(g(x))\) exists and, if so, its value.

A. The limit exists and equals \(1\).
B. The limit exists and equals \(\sqrt{1}\).
C. The limit does not exist because \(g\) is not continuous at 0.
D. The limit exists and equals \(\sqrt{0+1}=1\). ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Both one‑sided definitions of \(g\) approach 0 as \(x\to0\), so \(\lim_{x\to0}g(x)=0\). Since \(f(y)=\sqrt{y+1}\) is continuous at \(y=0\), the composition limit equals \(f(0)=\sqrt{1}=1\).

Q6. Compare the continuity of the composition \(|g(x)|\) when \(g\) is continuous at \(c\) versus when \(g\) has a removable discontinuity at \(c\).

A. Both are continuous at \(c\) because absolute value preserves continuity.
B. If \(g\) has a removable discontinuity, \(|g(x)|\) may become continuous after taking absolute value. ✅
C. If \(g\) is continuous at \(c\), \(|g(x)|\) is always discontinuous at \(c\).
D. The continuity of \(|g(x)|\) is unrelated to the continuity of \(g\).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A removable jump where \(g\) changes sign can be eliminated by the absolute value, turning a pointwise break into a continuous value. When \(g\) is already continuous, \(|g|\) remains continuous, but the absolute value can also repair certain sign‑related gaps.

Q7. Given two functions \(f\) and \(g\) where \(f\) is continuous everywhere and \(g\) is continuous at \(c\), which of the following statements best describes the continuity of \(f\circ g\) at \(c\) compared to \(g\) alone?

A. The composition inherits any discontinuities of \(g\) at \(c\).
B. The composition is always continuous at \(c\) regardless of \(g\).
C. The composition is continuous at \(c\) only if \(g(c)\) lies in a region where \(f\) is differentiable.
D. The composition is continuous at \(c\) if and only if \(g\) is continuous at \(c\). ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Since \(f\) is continuous everywhere, the only obstacle to continuity of the composition is the continuity of the inner function. Thus \(f\circ g\) is continuous at \(c\) exactly when \(g\) is continuous there, matching the theorem’s condition.

Q8. Let \(f(x)=\begin{cases}x^2 & x\le 1\\ 2x-1 & x>1\end{cases}\) and \(g(x)=\sqrt{x}\). Analyze the continuity of the composition \(h(x)=f(g(x))\) at \(x=1\).

A. h is continuous at 1 because both f and g are continuous at the relevant points. ✅
B. h is discontinuous at 1 because f is not continuous at g(1).
C. h is continuous at 1 but not differentiable there.
D. h is undefined at 1.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

At \(x=1\), \(g(1)=1\). Both pieces of \(f\) meet at \(x=1\) with the same value (1) and matching limits, so \(f\) is continuous there. Since \(g\) is continuous everywhere, the composition inherits continuity, giving \(h\) continuity at \(x=1\).

Q9. If \(g\) is continuous at \(c\) and \(f\) is the absolute value function, what can be said about the limit \(\lim_{x\to c}|g(x)|\)?

A. It equals \(|g(c)|\).
B. It does not exist unless \(g(c)=0\).
C. It equals \(\lim_{x\to c} g(x)\). ✅
D. It equals \(|\lim_{x\to c} g(x)|\) only if the limit exists.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The absolute value function is continuous everywhere, so the limit of \(|g(x)|\) as \(x\) approaches \(c\) is simply the absolute value of the limit of \(g(x)\), which equals \(|g(c)|\) because \(g\) itself is continuous at \(c\).

Q10. Consider the composition \(h(x)=\ln(\sqrt{x^2+1})\). Using the continuity of the inner functions, determine whether \(h\) is continuous for all real \(x\).

A. Yes, because both \(\sqrt{x^2+1}\) and \(\ln\) are continuous on their domains, and the inner expression is always positive. ✅
B. No, because \(\ln\) is not defined for non‑positive arguments.
C. It is continuous only for \(x\neq 0\).
D. Continuity cannot be concluded without evaluating limits.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The inner function \(\sqrt{x^2+1}\) yields values \(\ge 1\) for every real \(x\), keeping the argument of \(\ln\) strictly positive. Both the square‑root and natural‑log functions are continuous on their respective domains, so their composition is continuous for all real numbers.

Q11. Let \(g(x)=\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}\) for \(x\neq 2\) and define \(g(2)=4\). Let \(f(y)=|y|\). Determine whether the composition \(f\circ g\) is continuous at \(x=2\).

A. It is continuous because the limit of \(g(x)\) as \(x\to2\) exists and equals 4, and \(|y|\) is continuous. ✅
B. It is discontinuous because \(g\) has a removable discontinuity at 2.
C. It is continuous only from the left.
D. It is discontinuous because the absolute value function introduces a cusp.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The simplified form of \(g(x)\) is \(x+2\) for \(x\neq2\), whose limit as \(x\to2\) is 4. By defining \(g(2)=4\), \(g\) becomes continuous at 2. Since the absolute value function is continuous everywhere, the composition inherits this continuity, making it continuous at \(x=2\).

Q12. Suppose \(g\) is continuous at \(c\) and \(f\) is continuous everywhere except at a single point \(p\neq g(c)\). What can be said about the continuity of \(f\circ g\) at \(c\)?

A. It is continuous at \(c\) because the discontinuity of \(f\) does not affect the composition.
B. It is discontinuous at \(c\) because any discontinuity in \(f\) propagates through the composition.
C. Continuity at \(c\) depends on whether \(g(c)=p\). ✅
D. The composition is continuous only if \(g\) is differentiable at \(c\).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Since \(f\) is continuous at every point except \(p\) and \(g(c)\neq p\), the outer function is continuous at the specific argument \(g(c)\). The inner function’s continuity guarantees that the composition meets the theorem’s conditions, ensuring continuity at \(c\).

Q13. If a function \(g\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and \(f\) is continuous on the range of \(g\), which theorem guarantees that \(f\circ g\) attains its maximum on \([a,b]\)?

A. Intermediate Value Theorem.
B. Extreme Value Theorem applied to the composition. ✅
C. Mean Value Theorem.
D. Bolzano's Theorem.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The Extreme Value Theorem states that a continuous function on a closed interval attains both a maximum and a minimum. Since \(g\) maps \([a,b]\) continuously into a compact range and \(f\) is continuous on that range, the composition \(f\circ g\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and thus also satisfies the Extreme Value Theorem.

Q14. Given that \(\lim_{x\to c^+} g(x)=L\) and \(f\) is continuous at \(L\) but not defined for arguments less than \(L\), what can be said about \(\lim_{x\to c^+} f(g(x))\)?

A. The limit exists and equals \(f(L)\). ✅
B. The limit does not exist because \(f\) is undefined for some values.
C. The limit equals \(L\).
D. Continuity of \(f\) from the right is insufficient to determine the limit.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Even though \(f\) lacks a definition for inputs below \(L\), the right‑hand limit of \(g\) approaches \(L\) from above, staying within the domain where \(f\) is defined. The continuity of \(f\) at \(L\) then ensures the composition’s right‑hand limit equals \(f(L)\).

Q15. Let \(g(x)=\sin\frac{1}{x}\) for \(x\neq 0\) and \(g(0)=0\). Let \(f(y)=y^2\). Analyze the continuity of \(f\circ g\) at \(x=0\).

A. Continuous because \(f\) smooths out the oscillations of \(g\).
B. Discontinuous because \(g\) is not continuous at 0. ✅
C. Continuous only from the right.
D. Undefined at \(x=0\).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The inner function \(g\) oscillates without settling as \(x\to0\), so its limit does not exist. Squaring does not eliminate this oscillation; \((\sin(1/x))^2\) still varies between 0 and 1. Consequently, the composition fails to have a limit at 0 and is discontinuous there.
