What is Rational functions limits at infinity?

Definition:
For a rational function f(x)=P(x)Q(x) f(x) = \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} f(x)=Q(x)P(x)​, the limit as x→∞ x \to \infty x→∞ is determined by the degrees of P P P and Q Q Q: if deg⁡P<deg⁡Q \deg P < \deg Q degP<degQ, the limit is 0 0 0; if deg⁡P=deg⁡Q \deg P = \deg Q degP=degQ, the limit is the ratio of leading coefficients; if deg⁡P>deg⁡Q \deg P > \deg Q degP>degQ, the limit is ∞ \infty ∞ or −∞ -\infty −∞. This behavior describes the horizontal or oblique asymptotes and overall end behavior.

Example:
Evaluate lim⁡x→∞3x2+2x2−5 \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x^2+2}{x^2-5} limx→∞​x2−53x2+2​.
Solution: Degrees equal, leading coefficients 3/1=3 3/1 = 3 3/1=3, so limit is 3 3 3.

Reason:
This rule is crucial for analyzing rational models in engineering and science, such as damping ratios in control systems or concentration of chemicals in reaction kinetics, where long-term behavior is often of primary interest.
