What is Vectors in calculus?

Definition:
A vector is a mathematical object possessing both magnitude and direction, denoted v⃗\vec{v}v or v\mathbf{v}v, used to represent quantities like velocity or force that cannot be described by scalars alone.

Example:
A wind velocity of v⃗=⟨3,4⟩\vec{v} = \langle 3, 4 \ranglev=⟨3,4⟩ m/s indicates motion 3 m/s east and 4 m/s north, with speed ∣v⃗∣=5|\vec{v}| = 5∣v∣=5 m/s.

Reason:
Vectors encode directional information essential for multivariable calculus, enabling differentiation and integration of fields that vary in both magnitude and orientation across space.
