What is Angle between two vectors formula?

Definition:
The angle \( \theta \) between nonzero vectors \( \vec{u} \) and \( \vec{v} \) satisfies \( \cos\theta = \frac{\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v}}{\|\vec{u}\|\|\vec{v}\|} \), with \( \theta \in [0, \pi] \) determined uniquely by the inverse cosine.

Example:
For \( \vec{u} = \langle 1,0 \rangle \) and \( \vec{v} = \langle 1,1 \rangle \), \( \cos\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \) so \( \theta = 45^\circ \).

Reason:
This formula translates algebraic dot product into measurable geometric angle, crucial for determining molecular bond angles, antenna alignment, or surface incidence in optics.

Q1. A student calculates the angle between \( \vec{u} = \langle 1, 2, 3 \rangle \) and \( \vec{v} = \langle -1, -2, -3 \rangle \) using \( \cos \theta = \frac{\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v}}{\|\vec{u}\| \|\vec{v}\|} \) and obtains \( \theta = 0^\circ \). What is the fundamental error in this reasoning?

A. The student forgot to take the absolute value of the dot product.
B. The student used the wrong magnitude formula for 3D vectors.
C. The student failed to recognize that antiparallel vectors yield \( \cos \theta = -1 \), not \( +1 \). ✅
D. The calculator was set to radians instead of degrees.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The dot product of antiparallel vectors is negative, specifically \( \vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = -\|\vec{u}\|^2 \). Dividing by the product of magnitudes yields exactly \(-1\), corresponding to \( \theta = 180^\circ \). Claiming \( 0^\circ \) indicates a sign error or misinterpretation of the cosine inverse function's range, which is a common conceptual misconception when students focus only on magnitude alignment.

Q2. In a molecular modeling simulation, two bond vectors are \( \vec{a} = \langle 2, 0, 0 \rangle \) and \( \vec{b} = \langle 1, 1, k \rangle \). If the bond angle must be exactly \( 60^\circ \) for structural stability, what value of \( k \) satisfies this condition?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Setting \( \cos 60^\circ = 0.5 = \frac{2}{2\sqrt{2+k^2}} \) leads to \( \sqrt{2+k^2} = 2 \), so \( k^2 = 2 \). Both positive and negative roots are valid because the angle depends on \( k^2 \). Students often discard the negative root assuming physical length constraints, but \( k \) is a coordinate component, not a magnitude, making both solutions mathematically and physically permissible in vector space.
