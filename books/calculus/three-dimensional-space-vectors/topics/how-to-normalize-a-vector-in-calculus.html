What is How to normalize a vector in calculus?

Definition:
Normalization converts nonzero vector \( \vec{v} \) to unit vector \( \hat{v} = \frac{\vec{v}}{\|\vec{v}\|} \), preserving direction while scaling magnitude to 1 through division by its norm.

Example:
Normalizing \( \vec{w} = \langle 1, -2, 2 \rangle \) gives \( \hat{w} = \frac{\langle 1,-2,2 \rangle}{\sqrt{1+4+4}} = \langle \frac{1}{3}, -\frac{2}{3}, \frac{2}{3} \rangle \).

Reason:
Normalized vectors are required for computing direction cosines, unit normals to surfaces, and ensuring numerical stability in gradient-based algorithms.
