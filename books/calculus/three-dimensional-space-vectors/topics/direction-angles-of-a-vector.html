What is Direction angles of a vector?

Definition:
Direction angles α,β,γ\alpha, \beta, \gammaα,β,γ are angles between vector v⃗\vec{v}v and positive x,y,zx,y,zx,y,z axes, with direction cosines cos⁡α=vx∥v⃗∥\cos\alpha = \frac{v_x}{\|\vec{v}\|}cosα=∥v∥vx​​, cos⁡β=vy∥v⃗∥\cos\beta = \frac{v_y}{\|\vec{v}\|}cosβ=∥v∥vy​​, cos⁡γ=vz∥v⃗∥\cos\gamma = \frac{v_z}{\|\vec{v}\|}cosγ=∥v∥vz​​ satisfying cos⁡2α+cos⁡2β+cos⁡2γ=1\cos^2\alpha + \cos^2\beta + \cos^2\gamma = 1cos2α+cos2β+cos2γ=1.

Example:
For v⃗=⟨2,2,1⟩\vec{v} = \langle 2,2,1 \ranglev=⟨2,2,1⟩, ∥v⃗∥=3\|\vec{v}\| = 3∥v∥=3, so cos⁡α=23\cos\alpha = \frac{2}{3}cosα=32​, cos⁡β=23\cos\beta = \frac{2}{3}cosβ=32​, cos⁡γ=13\cos\gamma = \frac{1}{3}cosγ=31​, and α=β≈48.2∘\alpha = \beta \approx 48.2^\circα=β≈48.2∘, γ≈70.5∘\gamma \approx 70.5^\circγ≈70.5∘.

Reason:
Direction cosines provide normalized directional data independent of magnitude, used in crystallography, navigation, and defining orientation tensors in continuum mechanics.
