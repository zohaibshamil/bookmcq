What is Vector addition subtraction scalar multiplication?

Definition:
Vector addition u⃗+v⃗=⟨ux+vx,uy+vy,uz+vz⟩\vec{u}+\vec{v} = \langle u_x+v_x, u_y+v_y, u_z+v_z \rangleu+v=⟨ux​+vx​,uy​+vy​,uz​+vz​⟩, subtraction u⃗−v⃗=⟨ux−vx,uy−vy,uz−vz⟩\vec{u}-\vec{v} = \langle u_x-v_x, u_y-v_y, u_z-v_z \rangleu−v=⟨ux​−vx​,uy​−vy​,uz​−vz​⟩, and scalar multiplication cv⃗=⟨cvx,cvy,cvz⟩c\vec{v} = \langle cv_x, cv_y, cv_z \ranglecv=⟨cvx​,cvy​,cvz​⟩ follow component-wise rules preserving vector space structure.

Example:
If u⃗=⟨1,2,3⟩\vec{u} = \langle 1,2,3 \rangleu=⟨1,2,3⟩ and v⃗=⟨4,−1,0⟩\vec{v} = \langle 4,-1,0 \ranglev=⟨4,−1,0⟩, then 2u⃗−v⃗=⟨2−4,4+1,6−0⟩=⟨−2,5,6⟩2\vec{u} - \vec{v} = \langle 2-4, 4+1, 6-0 \rangle = \langle -2, 5, 6 \rangle2u−v=⟨2−4,4+1,6−0⟩=⟨−2,5,6⟩.

Reason:
These linear operations model superposition of forces or velocities and form the foundation for defining vector spaces critical in differential equations and linear transformations.
