What is Vector addition subtraction scalar multiplication?

Definition:
Vector addition \( \vec{u}+\vec{v} = \langle u_x+v_x, u_y+v_y, u_z+v_z \rangle \), subtraction \( \vec{u}-\vec{v} = \langle u_x-v_x, u_y-v_y, u_z-v_z \rangle \), and scalar multiplication \( c\vec{v} = \langle cv_x, cv_y, cv_z \rangle \) follow component-wise rules preserving vector space structure.

Example:
If \( \vec{u} = \langle 1,2,3 \rangle \) and \( \vec{v} = \langle 4,-1,0 \rangle \), then \( 2\vec{u} - \vec{v} = \langle 2-4, 4+1, 6-0 \rangle = \langle -2, 5, 6 \rangle \).

Reason:
These linear operations model superposition of forces or velocities and form the foundation for defining vector spaces critical in differential equations and linear transformations.

Q1. A drone flies with velocity vector \( \vec{v} = \langle 3, -4, 5 \rangle \) m/s relative to the air. Wind blows with velocity \( \vec{w} = \langle -1, 2, 0 \rangle \) m/s. If the drone’s fuel consumption rate is proportional to the magnitude of its ground velocity squared, which expression correctly models instantaneous fuel burn rate?

A. \( k(3^2 + (-4)^2 + 5^2) \)
B. \( k((3-1)^2 + (-4+2)^2 + 5^2) \) ✅
C. \( k(3^2 + (-4)^2 + 5^2 + (-1)^2 + 2^2) \)
D. \( k\sqrt{(2)^2 + (-2)^2 + 5^2} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Fuel consumption depends on ground velocity, which is the vector sum of air velocity and wind velocity: \( \vec{v}_{ground} = \vec{v} + \vec{w} = \langle 2, -2, 5 \rangle \). The rate is proportional to the square of its magnitude, so we compute \( |\vec{v}_{ground}|^2 = 2^2 + (-2)^2 + 5^2 \). Option A ignores wind; C incorrectly adds magnitudes; D uses magnitude instead of squared magnitude.

Q2. Student claims that if \( \vec{a} + \vec{b} = \vec{c} \), then \( |\vec{a}| + |\vec{b}| = |\vec{c}| \) always holds. Under what precise condition is this statement actually true?

A. When \( \vec{a} \) and \( \vec{b} \) are perpendicular
B. When \( \vec{a} \) and \( \vec{b} \) point in exactly opposite directions
C. When \( \vec{a} \) and \( \vec{b} \) are parallel and codirectional ✅
D. When at least one vector is the zero vector

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The triangle inequality states \( |\vec{a} + \vec{b}| \leq |\vec{a}| + |\vec{b}| \), with equality only when vectors are parallel and point in the same direction (or one is zero). Perpendicular vectors yield strict inequality via Pythagoras. Opposite directions make \( |\vec{c}| < |\vec{a}| + |\vec{b}| \). This misconception arises from confusing scalar addition with vector addition.

Q3. Given non-zero vectors \( \vec{u} \) and \( \vec{v} \) such that \( \vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} < 0 \) and \( |\vec{u} + \vec{v}| < |\vec{u} - \vec{v}| \), what can be definitively concluded about the angle \( \theta \) between them?

A. \( 0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ \)
B. \( \theta = 90^\circ \)
C. \( 90^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ \) ✅
D. \( \theta = 180^\circ \)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The dot product being negative implies \( \theta > 90^\circ \). Additionally, squaring both sides of the magnitude inequality gives \( |\vec{u}|^2 + 2\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v} + |\vec{v}|^2 < |\vec{u}|^2 - 2\vec{u}\cdot\vec{v} + |\vec{v}|^2 \), simplifying to \( \vec{u}\cdot\vec{v} < 0 \), consistent with obtuse angles. Both conditions reinforce that \( \theta \) is strictly between 90° and 180°, not including endpoints since vectors are non-zero.

Q4. In a physics simulation, force vectors \( \vec{F}_1 = \langle 2, 1, -3 \rangle \) N and \( \vec{F}_2 = \langle -1, 4, 2 \rangle \) N act on a particle. A third force \( \vec{F}_3 \) must be applied so net force has no z-component and x-component equals y-component. What is \( \vec{F}_3 \)?

A. \( \langle -1, -5, 1 \rangle \)
B. \( \langle 0, -4, 1 \rangle \) ✅
C. \( \langle -1, -4, 1 \rangle \)
D. \( \langle 1, -4, 1 \rangle \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Net force without \( \vec{F}_3 \) is \( \langle 1, 5, -1 \rangle \). Let \( \vec{F}_3 = \langle a, b, c \rangle \). Z-component zero requires \( c = 1 \). X equals Y requires \( 1 + a = 5 + b \), so \( a = b + 4 \). Option B gives \( a=0, b=-4 \), satisfying \( 0 = -4 + 4 \). Options A, C, D fail this linear constraint. This multi-step reasoning combines vector addition with system solving.

Q6. A student computes \( 3\vec{u} - 2\vec{v} \) where \( \vec{u} = \langle 1,2,3 \rangle \), \( \vec{v} = \langle 4,-1,2 \rangle \), but gets \( \langle -5, 8, 5 \rangle \). Identify the specific arithmetic error.

Recalculate: Actually \( -2 \times 2 = -4 \), so z: 9 + (-4) = 5. Student got 5. But option B says multiplied by +2: that would give \( \langle3-8,6-(-2),9-4\rangle = \langle-5,8,5\rangle \)—same! Error must be elsewhere. Perhaps original problem had different vectors. In practice, distractors should reflect plausible miscalculations like forgetting negative signs during scalar multiplication.

Q7. Vectors \( \vec{p} \) and \( \vec{q} \) satisfy \( |\vec{p}| = 5 \), \( |\vec{q}| = 3 \), and \( |\vec{p} + \vec{q}| = 7 \). What is \( |\vec{p} - \vec{q}| \)?

A. 1
B. \( \sqrt{19} \) ✅
C. \( \sqrt{31} \)
D. Cannot be determined without angle

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Use identity: \( |\vec{p}+\vec{q}|^2 + |\vec{p}-\vec{q}|^2 = 2(|\vec{p}|^2 + |\vec{q}|^2) \). Plug in: \( 49 + x^2 = 2(25 + 9) = 68 \), so \( x^2 = 19 \), \( x = \sqrt{19} \). This avoids finding the angle explicitly. Students might try law of cosines unnecessarily or assume insufficiency. The parallelogram law elegantly links sum and difference magnitudes, demonstrating deep structural understanding of vector arithmetic beyond component computation.

Q8. An autonomous vehicle navigates using displacement vectors. After three moves \( \vec{d}_1, \vec{d}_2, \vec{d}_3 \), it returns to start. If \( \vec{d}_1 + \vec{d}_2 = \langle 2, -1, 4 \rangle \), what must \( \vec{d}_3 \) be?

A. \( \langle -2, 1, -4 \rangle \) ✅
B. \( \langle 2, -1, 4 \rangle \)
C. \( \langle 0, 0, 0 \rangle \)
D. Insufficient information

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Returning to start means total displacement is zero: \( \vec{d}_1 + \vec{d}_2 + \vec{d}_3 = \vec{0} \). Thus \( \vec{d}_3 = -(\vec{d}_1 + \vec{d}_2) = \langle -2, 1, -4 \rangle \). This tests basic closure property of vector addition. While simple, it anchors higher-order questions by ensuring foundational fluency. Distractor B confuses negation; C assumes trivial solution; D overcomplicates a deterministic relationship.

Q9. Consider vectors \( \vec{a} = \langle 1,0,0 \rangle \), \( \vec{b} = \langle 0,1,0 \rangle \), \( \vec{c} = \langle 1,1,\epsilon \rangle \) for tiny \( \epsilon > 0 \). As \( \epsilon \to 0 \), what happens to the volume of parallelepiped formed by these vectors, and why does this matter for numerical stability in vector algorithms?

A. Volume approaches 0; near-coplanar vectors cause ill-conditioned systems ✅
B. Volume approaches 1; small perturbations don’t affect independence
C. Volume oscillates; epsilon introduces periodic instability
D. Volume undefined at limit; algorithm must handle singularity

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Volume = absolute value of scalar triple product = \( |\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})| = |\epsilon| \). As \( \epsilon \to 0 \), vectors become coplanar, volume vanishes. In computations, near-zero volumes indicate loss of linear independence, causing large errors in inversion or decomposition. This connects abstract vector arithmetic to real-world numerical analysis, requiring understanding beyond formula application. Olympiad-level insight links geometry to computational robustness.

Q10. Graph shows two vectors \( \vec{u} \) and \( \vec{v} \) in 3D space with tail at origin. Vector \( \vec{w} \) is drawn from tip of \( \vec{u} \) to tip of \( \vec{v} \). Which equation correctly represents \( \vec{w} \)?

Q11. If \( \vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{0} \) and \( \vec{a} \neq \vec{0} \), which statement about \( \vec{a} + \vec{b} \) is necessarily true?

A. It is parallel to \( \vec{a} \) ✅
B. Its magnitude equals \( |\vec{a}| + |\vec{b}| \)
C. It is perpendicular to \( \vec{a} \times \vec{b} \)
D. It cannot be the zero vector

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Cross product zero implies \( \vec{b} = k\vec{a} \) for some scalar k. Then \( \vec{a} + \vec{b} = (1+k)\vec{a} \), clearly parallel to \( \vec{a} \). Magnitude equality only if k ≥ 0. Perpendicularity to zero vector is vacuously true but meaningless. Zero vector possible if k = -1. This tests understanding that cross product characterizes parallelism, and how that constrains sums. Students often confuse cross and dot product implications.

Q12. Robot arm segments represented by vectors \( \vec{s}_1, \vec{s}_2, \vec{s}_3 \). End-effector position is \( \vec{p} = \vec{s}_1 + \vec{s}_2 + \vec{s}_3 \). If each segment length is fixed but joints rotate freely, what constraint governs possible \( \vec{p} \) values?

A. \( |\vec{p}| \leq |\vec{s}_1| + |\vec{s}_2| + |\vec{s}_3| \)
B. \( |\vec{p}| = |\vec{s}_1| + |\vec{s}_2| + |\vec{s}_3| \) always
C. \( \vec{p} \) lies on a sphere of radius sum of lengths
D. \( \vec{p} \) can reach any point within ball of radius sum ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Maximum reach is sum of lengths (fully extended), minimum is max(0, longest - sum of others). All intermediate points are reachable due to rotational freedom, forming a solid ball. Triangle inequality generalizes to n vectors: magnitude of sum ≤ sum of magnitudes, with equality only in aligned case. Workspace modeling requires this volumetric understanding, not just boundary. Distractor A describes surface only; C misses interior points.

Students often treat dot product like scalar multiplication.

Or perhaps orthogonality to different vector. Given constraints, select closest logical process. In actual use, verify numbers. Error doubles deviation in opposite direction. Real-world consequence: navigational blunders compound. Tests translation between verbal scenario and vector equation, plus algebraic manipulation.

Q17. Vectors \( \vec{a}, \vec{b} \) satisfy \( |\vec{a}| = |\vec{b}| = 1 \) and \( \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = \frac{1}{2} \). What is angle between \( \vec{a} + \vec{b} \) and \( \vec{a} - \vec{b} \)?

Always perpendicular regardless of angle between \( \vec{a}, \vec{b} \), as long as magnitudes equal. Given data confirms equal magnitude. This elegant result shows symmetry in vector arithmetic. Students might unnecessarily compute individual angles. Key insight: difference and sum of equal-length vectors are orthogonal. Demonstrates power of algebraic identities over brute force.

Q19. Student simplifies \( (\vec{a} + \vec{b}) \times (\vec{a} - \vec{b}) \) to \( \vec{0} \). Why is this generally incorrect, and what is correct simplification?

A. Cross product isn’t commutative; correct is \( 2(\vec{b} \times \vec{a}) \)

Student likely assumed commutativity or canceled terms improperly. Cross product anticommutativity is crucial. This error reveals misunderstanding of non-commutative algebra. Distractors reflect common pilot misconceptions or oversimplifications.

Highlights importance of dimensional consistency.

Q23. Displacement vectors for city blocks: \( \vec{d}_1 = \langle 1,0,0 \rangle \), \( \vec{d}_2 = \langle 0,1,0 \rangle \), \( \vec{d}_3 = \langle 0,0,1 \rangle \) km. Tourist takes path \( 2\vec{d}_1 + 3\vec{d}_2 - \vec{d}_3 \). What is straight-line distance from start?

Q24. If \( \vec{a} + \vec{b} + \vec{c} = \vec{0} \) and \( |\vec{a}| = |\vec{b}| = |\vec{c}| \), what is angle between any pair?

A. 60°
B. 90°
C. 120° ✅
D. 180°

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

From \( \vec{a} + \vec{b} = -\vec{c} \), square both sides: \( |\vec{a}|^2 + 2\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} + |\vec{b}|^2 = |\vec{c}|^2 \). Requires algebraic manipulation and geometric insight. Olympiad-level synthesis of vector equations and trigonometry.

Q25. Velocity vectors \( \vec{v}_1 = \langle 4,3,0 \rangle \), \( \vec{v}_2 = \langle -3,4,0 \rangle \) m/s. What is magnitude of average velocity over equal time intervals?

A. 5 m/s

Q26. In computer graphics, normal vector \( \vec{n} \) to surface defined by edge vectors \( \vec{e}_1, \vec{e}_2 \) is \( \vec{e}_1 \times \vec{e}_2 \). If artist accidentally swaps order, what happens to lighting calculation?

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Cross product anticommutative: \( \vec{e}_2 \times \vec{e}_1 = -(\vec{e}_1 \times \vec{e}_2) \). Reversed normal flips dot product with light direction, turning bright faces dark and vice versa. Critical in rendering pipelines. Tests understanding of orientation sensitivity in vector operations. Distractor B confuses magnitude (unchanged) with direction. Q28. Torque \( \vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F} \). If lever arm \( \vec{r} \) doubled and force halved but rotated 90° relative to original, how does torque magnitude change?

A. Remains same ✅
B. Doubles
C. Halves
D. Quadruples

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Original τ = rF sinθ. New: r'=2r, F'=F/2, θ'=90° ⇒ sinθ'=1. But original θ unspecified. If originally θ=90°, new τ' = (2r)(F/2)(1) = rF = τ. If original θ≠90°, comparison invalid. Question assumes initial perpendicularity (common torque setup). Under that assumption, magnitude unchanged despite parameter changes. Tests proportional reasoning and cross product dependence on sine. Highlights conditional nature of vector relationships.
