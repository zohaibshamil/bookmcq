📝 Traces of quadric surfaces

📖 From Calculus • 12. Three Dimensional Space: Vectors • 26 questions available

What is Traces of quadric surfaces?

Definition:
Traces are intersections of a quadric surface with coordinate planes (\( x=0 \), \( y=0 \), \( z=0 \)) or parallel planes, yielding 2D conic sections that reveal surface shape.

Example:
For hyperboloid \( x^2 + y^2 - z^2 = 1 \), trace at \( z=0 \) is circle \( x^2+y^2=1 \); trace at \( x=0 \) is hyperbola \( y^2 - z^2 = 1 \).

Reason:
Analyzing traces builds mental model of 3D surface from familiar 2D curves, essential for sketching, verifying equations, and understanding cross-sectional properties in engineering design.

Q1. A student claims that the trace of the surface \( z = x^2 + y^2 \) in the plane \( y = 0 \) is a straight line because setting \( y = 0 \) eliminates one variable. Which statement best identifies the flaw in this reasoning?

📖 Explanation:

The error lies in misunderstanding how substitution affects functional form. Setting \( y = 0 \) in \( z = x^2 + y^2 \) yields \( z = x^2 \), which remains quadratic. Eliminating a variable does not guarantee linearity; the algebraic structure of the remaining terms determines the curve type. This misconception arises from overgeneralizing simplification rules without considering residual dependencies.
