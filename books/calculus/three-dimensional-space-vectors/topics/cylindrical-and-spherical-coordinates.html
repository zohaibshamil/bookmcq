What is Cylindrical and Spherical Coordinates?

Definition:
Cylindrical coordinates (r,θ,z) (r,\theta,z) (r,θ,z) extend polar coordinates with height z z z; spherical coordinates (ρ,θ,ϕ) (\rho,\theta,\phi) (ρ,θ,ϕ) use radial distance ρ \rho ρ, azimuthal angle θ \theta θ, and polar angle ϕ \phi ϕ from positive z z z-axis.

Example:
Point (x,y,z)=(1,1,1) (x,y,z)=(1,1,1) (x,y,z)=(1,1,1) in cylindrical is (r=2,θ=π/4,z=1) (r=\sqrt{2}, \theta=\pi/4, z=1) (r=2​,θ=π/4,z=1); in spherical is (ρ=3,θ=π/4,ϕ=arccos⁡(1/3)) (\rho=\sqrt{3}, \theta=\pi/4, \phi=\arccos(1/\sqrt{3})) (ρ=3​,θ=π/4,ϕ=arccos(1/3​)).

Reason:
Alternative coordinates exploit symmetry in problems with cylindrical or spherical geometry, simplifying integrals, PDEs, and physical laws like Gauss’s law or wave equations.
