What is Cylindrical and Spherical Coordinate Systems?

Definition:
Cylindrical: x=rcos⁡θ,y=rsin⁡θ,z=zx=r\cos\theta, y=r\sin\theta, z=zx=rcosθ,y=rsinθ,z=z with r≥0,θ∈[0,2π)r\geq0, \theta\in[0,2\pi)r≥0,θ∈[0,2π); Spherical: x=ρsin⁡ϕcos⁡θ,y=ρsin⁡ϕsin⁡θ,z=ρcos⁡ϕx=\rho\sin\phi\cos\theta, y=\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta, z=\rho\cos\phix=ρsinϕcosθ,y=ρsinϕsinθ,z=ρcosϕ with ρ≥0,θ∈[0,2π),ϕ∈[0,π]\rho\geq0, \theta\in[0,2\pi), \phi\in[0,\pi]ρ≥0,θ∈[0,2π),ϕ∈[0,π].

Example:
Cylinder x2+y2=4x^2+y^2=4x2+y2=4 becomes r=2r=2r=2 in cylindrical; sphere x2+y2+z2=9x^2+y^2+z^2=9x2+y2+z2=9 becomes ρ=3\rho=3ρ=3 in spherical.

Reason:
Coordinate transformations reduce complex Cartesian equations to simple constants, dramatically easing integration and revealing inherent symmetries in physical systems.
