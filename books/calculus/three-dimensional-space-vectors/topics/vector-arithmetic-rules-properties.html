What is Vector arithmetic rules properties?

Definition:
Vector arithmetic obeys commutative \( \vec{u}+\vec{v}=\vec{v}+\vec{u} \), associative \( (\vec{u}+\vec{v})+\vec{w}=\vec{u}+(\vec{v}+\vec{w}) \), distributive \( c(\vec{u}+\vec{v})=c\vec{u}+c\vec{v} \), and identity/inverse properties forming a vector space over real numbers.

Example:
Simplifying \( 3(\vec{a} + 2\vec{b}) - \vec{a} \) uses distributivity and associativity to yield \( 2\vec{a} + 6\vec{b} \), regardless of vector dimensions.

Reason:
Algebraic consistency permits symbolic manipulation of vectors analogous to scalars, enabling rigorous proofs and efficient computation in higher-dimensional analysis.

Q1. A drone navigates using displacement vectors. If it flies \(\vec{a}\) then \(\vec{b}\), but wind adds an unknown drift \(\vec{w}\) such that the actual path is \(\vec{a} + \vec{w} = \vec{b}\), which expression correctly isolates the wind vector?

A. \(\vec{w} = \vec{a} - \vec{b}\)
B. \(\vec{w} = \vec{b} - \vec{a}\) ✅
C. \(\vec{w} = -\vec{a} - \vec{b}\)
D. \(\vec{w} = \vec{a} + \vec{b}\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Students often confuse the order of subtraction when rearranging vector equations. Since \(\vec{a} + \vec{w} = \vec{b}\), subtracting \(\vec{a}\) from both sides yields \(\vec{w} = \vec{b} - \vec{a}\). This models real-world navigation where environmental factors must be deduced from intended versus actual displacement, requiring algebraic manipulation of vector relationships rather than simple computation.

A. Both properties arise from the geometric definition of vectors as directed line segments.

📖 Explanation:

This question targets conceptual understanding by distinguishing between geometric intuition and algebraic structure. Commutativity means the resultant displacement is independent of traversal order, while distributivity guarantees that scaling a combined motion equals combining scaled motions. Recognizing these as fundamental structural properties, not mere computational rules, deepens understanding of vector spaces beyond rote memorization of formulas.

Understanding this boundary condition is crucial for analyzing when triangle inequality becomes equality in physical systems like force composition.

Q6. If \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = 0\) and \(\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \vec{0}\) simultaneously for non-zero vectors, what must be true?

D. One vector must be the zero vector despite the non-zero stipulation.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This Olympiad-level problem tests deep understanding of dot and cross product conditions. Non-zero vectors cannot satisfy both simultaneously since they'd need to be both perpendicular and parallel. Only the zero vector trivially satisfies both, contradicting the premise. This reveals the mutual exclusivity of these fundamental vector relationships in Euclidean space.

Q26. If \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{c}\) and \(\vec{b} \times \vec{c} = \vec{a}\) for unit vectors, what constraint must \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}\) satisfy?

B. They are all parallel.
C. They lie in a single plane with 120-degree separation.
D. No such unit vectors exist satisfying both equations.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

From \(\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \vec{c}\), vectors are orthogonal and right-handed. Substituting into second equation: \(\vec{b} \times (\vec{a} \times \vec{b}) = \vec{a}\). Using vector triple product identity yields \((\vec{b} \cdot \vec{b})\vec{a} - (\vec{b} \cdot \vec{a})\vec{b} = \vec{a}\). Since \(\vec{b} \cdot \vec{b} = 1\) and orthogonality gives \(\vec{b} \cdot \vec{a} = 0\), this simplifies to \(\vec{a} = \vec{a}\), confirming consistency. Thus they form a right-handed orthonormal triad. This challenging problem synthesizes cross product identities with basis properties, testing deep structural understanding beyond routine computation.

Correct visualization reinforces that subtraction involves negation before addition, ensuring accurate representation of relative quantities like displacement differences and velocity changes in kinematic diagrams.
