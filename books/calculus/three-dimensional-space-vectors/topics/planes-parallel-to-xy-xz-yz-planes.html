What is Planes parallel to xy xz yz planes?

Definition:
Planes parallel to coordinate planes have one variable constant: \( z=k \) (parallel to \( xy \)), \( y=k \) (parallel to \( xz \)), \( x=k \) (parallel to \( yz \)), with normal vectors aligned to respective axes.

Example:
The plane \( z=5 \) is horizontal, 5 units above \( xy \)-plane, containing all points \( (x,y,5) \); its normal is \( \langle 0,0,1 \rangle \).

Reason:
These special cases simplify integration bounds, boundary conditions in PDEs, and serve as reference frames for slicing 3D objects in tomography and volumetric rendering.

Q1. A drone is programmed to maintain a constant altitude of \( z = 5 \) while surveying terrain. If the drone’s path is restricted to the region where \( x^2 + y^2 \leq 25 \), which statement best describes the geometric nature of its flight domain?

A. The drone flies on a plane parallel to the xy-plane, but its actual path is a disk of radius 5 centered at (0,0,5). ✅
B. The drone flies on the entire infinite plane \( z = 5 \) since altitude defines an unbounded surface.
C. The drone’s path is a sphere of radius 5 because the inequality involves three variables.
D. The drone flies on a plane parallel to the xz-plane due to the circular constraint in x and y.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This question tests conceptual understanding by combining the definition of a plane parallel to the xy-plane with a bounded region. Students must recognize that while \( z = 5 \) defines an infinite plane, the inequality restricts the domain to a finite disk, distinguishing between the supporting plane and the actual trajectory set.

D. The error lies in assuming y-planes can be parallel; only z-planes can be truly parallel in standard orientation.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

This error analysis question requires students to algebraically manipulate plane equations before applying geometric definitions. Many learners mistakenly equate syntactic difference with geometric distinction. Recognizing that scalar multiples represent the same locus is crucial for avoiding false conclusions about spatial relationships in vector geometry problems.

Q6. If a plane is described parametrically as \( \vec{r}(s,t) = \langle s, 4, t \rangle \) where \( s,t \in \mathbb{R} \), what is the shortest distance from the point \( P(1, -2, 3) \) to this plane?

A. \( \sqrt{(1-s)^2 + 36 + (3-t)^2} \) minimized over s and t.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

This application question links parametric representation to geometric distance. The parameterization shows y is constantly 4, identifying the plane \( y = 4 \). Distance from \( P \) is simply \( |4 - (-2)| = 6 \). Students who attempt calculus minimization miss the structural insight that constant parameters define coordinate-parallel planes.

Q7. A student argues that the planes \( x = a \) and \( x = b \) are parallel only if \( a \neq b \), but if \( a = b \), they cease to be planes altogether. Evaluate this reasoning.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

This error analysis probes deep conceptual understanding of mathematical definitions. Coincident planes satisfy the definition of parallelism (normals are scalar multiples, including equality). Dismissing them as 'not planes' reflects confusion between uniqueness and existence. Clarifying this prevents foundational misunderstandings in linear algebra and analytic geometry.

Q8. In a 3D printing calibration test, layers are deposited on planes \( z = k \) for integer \( k \). If a defect occurs whenever \( z \equiv 2 \pmod{5} \), how many defective layers exist between \( z = 0 \) and \( z = 48 \) inclusive?

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

This challenging problem merges modular arithmetic with spatial layering. Defective layers occur at \( z = 2, 7, 12, ..., 47 \). This arithmetic sequence has first term 2, common difference 5, last term ≤48. Solving \( 2 + 5(n-1) \leq 48 \) gives \( n = 10 \). Multi-step reasoning connects number theory to physical manufacturing constraints.

Q11. A tetrahedron has vertices at (0,0,0), (4,0,0), (0,3,0), and (0,0,h). If the face opposite the origin lies on a plane parallel to the xy-plane, what must h equal?

A. 0
B. Any positive real number
C. Undefined; no such tetrahedron exists with that vertex configuration ✅
D. 12

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

This Olympiad-style question tests logical consistency. The face opposite (0,0,0) connects (4,0,0), (0,3,0), and (0,0,h). For this triangle to lie on a plane parallel to xy-plane, all three points must share the same z-coordinate. But (4,0,0) and (0,3,0) have z=0 while (0,0,h) has z=h≠0 unless h=0, collapsing the tetrahedron. Thus, no valid non-degenerate tetrahedron satisfies the condition.

Q26. Olympiad challenge: Find the minimum number of planes parallel to coordinate planes needed to partition \( \mathbb{R}^3 \) into exactly 27 congruent rectangular boxes.

A. 6 planes ✅
B. 9 planes
C. 12 planes
D. Impossible with coordinate-parallel planes alone

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

This Olympiad-style question demands combinatorial spatial reasoning. Each pair of parallel planes divides space into slabs; n planes create n+1 regions. For 3 regions per axis, need 2 planes per axis. Total 6 planes yield 3×3×3=27 congruent boxes. Misconceptions arise from confusing planes per region or forgetting axis independence, testing deep structural understanding of grid formation via coordinate-aligned partitions.
