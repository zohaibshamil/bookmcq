What is Parametric equations of a line in 3D?

Definition:
A line through point P0(x0,y0,z0) P_0(x_0,y_0,z_0) P0​(x0​,y0​,z0​) with direction vector v⃗=⟨a,b,c⟩ \vec{v} = \langle a,b,c \rangle v=⟨a,b,c⟩ has parametric equations x=x0+at x = x_0 + at x=x0​+at, y=y0+bt y = y_0 + bt y=y0​+bt, z=z0+ct z = z_0 + ct z=z0​+ct, where t∈R t \in \mathbb{R} t∈R is parameter.

Example:
Line through (1,2,3) (1,2,3) (1,2,3) parallel to ⟨2,−1,4⟩ \langle 2,-1,4 \rangle ⟨2,−1,4⟩ is x=1+2t x=1+2t x=1+2t, y=2−t y=2-t y=2−t, z=3+4t z=3+4t z=3+4t; at t=1 t=1 t=1, point is (3,1,7) (3,1,7) (3,1,7).

Reason:
Parameterization describes motion along straight path with uniform speed, essential for ray tracing, trajectory planning, and solving intersection problems in 3D graphics and robotics.
