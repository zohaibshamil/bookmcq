What is Plane equation in 3D?

Definition:
A plane with normal vector \( \vec{n} = \langle a,b,c \rangle \) passing through \( (x_0,y_0,z_0) \) satisfies \( a(x-x_0) + b(y-y_0) + c(z-z_0) = 0 \), or generally \( ax+by+cz+d=0 \) where \( d = -(ax_0+by_0+cz_0) \).

Example:
Plane through \( (1,2,3) \) with normal \( \langle 2,-1,4 \rangle \) has equation \( 2(x-1) - (y-2) + 4(z-3) = 0 \), simplifying to \( 2x - y + 4z = 12 \).

Reason:
Normal vector form encodes orientation intrinsically, enabling distance calculations, reflection laws, and defining tangent planes to surfaces in multivariable optimization.

Q1. A student claims that the plane \(2x - y + 3z = 6\) is parallel to the vector \(\langle 4, -2, 6 \rangle\). Which statement best evaluates this claim?

A.The claim is correct because the vector is a scalar multiple of the normal vector.
B.The claim is incorrect; the vector is actually perpendicular to the plane, not parallel. ✅
C.The claim is correct because substituting the vector components satisfies the plane equation.
D.The claim is incorrect; parallelism requires the dot product with the normal to equal one.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Students often confuse normal vectors with direction vectors lying in the plane. The vector \(\langle 4, -2, 6 \rangle\) is exactly twice the normal vector \(\langle 2, -1, 3 \rangle\), meaning it is orthogonal to the plane. A vector parallel to a plane must have a zero dot product with the normal, indicating it lies within or is parallel to the surface.

Q2. Given two intersecting planes \(x + 2y - z = 4\) and \(3x - y + 2z = 1\), which method most efficiently finds a direction vector for their line of intersection without solving the full system parametrically?

A.Set \(z = 0\) and solve the resulting \(2 \times 2\) linear system for \(x\) and \(y\).
B.Compute the cross product of the two normal vectors \(\langle 1,2,-1 \rangle\) and \(\langle 3,-1,2 \rangle\). ✅
C.Add the two normal vectors to get a resultant direction vector.
D.Take the dot product of the normals and use the magnitude as the direction component.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The line of intersection lies in both planes, so its direction vector must be perpendicular to both normal vectors. The cross product of the normals directly yields such a vector. Setting \(z=0\) works only if the line actually crosses the \(xy\)-plane, which is not guaranteed, making the cross product method universally reliable and computationally efficient.

Q3. An engineer models a solar panel as the plane \(x + y + z = 10\). Sunlight arrives along direction \(\langle 0, 0, -1 \rangle\). What is the sine of the angle between the sunlight and the panel’s surface, which determines energy absorption efficiency?

A.\(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\) ✅
B.\(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}}\)
C.\(\frac{1}{3}\)
D.\(\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The angle between sunlight and the panel’s surface is complementary to the angle between sunlight and the normal. The cosine of the angle with the normal is \(|\vec{s} \cdot \vec{n}| / (|\vec{s}||\vec{n}|) = 1/\sqrt{3}\). Thus, the sine of the angle with the surface equals this cosine value, since \(\sin(\theta_{\text{surface}}) = \cos(90^\circ - \theta_{\text{surface}}) = \cos(\theta_{\text{normal}})\).

Q4. A student computes the distance from point \(P(1,2,3)\) to the plane \(2x - y + z = 5\) as \(\frac{|2(1) - (2) + (3) - 5|}{\sqrt{2^2 + (-1)^2 + 1^2}} = \frac{0}{\sqrt{6}} = 0\). They conclude the point lies on the plane. What is the flaw in this reasoning?

A.The arithmetic is correct and the conclusion is valid.
B.The numerator should include absolute value after evaluation, but the result is still zero.
C.The formula was applied correctly, but the student misread the plane equation.
D.There is no flaw; however, the student failed to verify by direct substitution into the original equation. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: While the distance formula computation is arithmetically correct and yields zero, best practice requires verifying by plugging \(P\) into the plane equation: \(2(1) - 2 + 3 = 3 \neq 5\). The error lies in miscalculating the numerator: \(2 - 2 + 3 - 5 = -2\), not 0. This highlights the need to double-check arithmetic before drawing geometric conclusions.

Q5. Consider the family of planes \(ax + by + cz = d\) where \(a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = 1\) and \(d > 0\). If all such planes are tangent to a fixed sphere centered at the origin, what must be true about \(d\)?

A.\(d\) equals the radius of the sphere. ✅
B.\(d\) is arbitrary as long as positive.
C.\(d\) must equal \(1/\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}\).
D.\(d\) depends on the orientation of the normal vector.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: For a plane with unit normal vector (since \(a^2+b^2+c^2=1\)) to be tangent to a sphere centered at origin with radius \(r\), the perpendicular distance from origin to plane must equal \(r\). That distance is \(|d|/1 = d\). Hence \(d = r\). This connects normalization of plane equations directly to geometric tangency conditions in a non-obvious way.

Q6. Which graph best represents the set of points satisfying both \(z = 2\) and \(x + y = 3\) simultaneously in three-dimensional space?

A.A horizontal plane at height 2.
B.A vertical plane slicing diagonally through the x-y axes.
C.A straight line parallel to neither axis but lying in a horizontal plane. ✅
D.A single point at the intersection of two planes.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The equation \(z = 2\) defines a horizontal plane, while \(x + y = 3\) defines a vertical plane (independent of z). Their intersection is a line that lies entirely within \(z = 2\) and satisfies \(x + y = 3\). This line is neither horizontal nor vertical in the xy-projection but is constrained to constant z, matching option C’s description precisely.

Q7. Two planes are given: \(P_1: x - 2y + 3z = 6\) and \(P_2: 2x - 4y + 6z = k\). For what value(s) of \(k\) do these planes represent the same geometric object rather than distinct parallel planes?

A.Any real number \(k\)
B.Only \(k = 12\) ✅
C.Only \(k = 6\)
D.No value of \(k\) makes them identical
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Plane \(P_2\) has coefficients exactly double those of \(P_1\), so they are either identical or parallel. They are identical only if the right-hand side also scales by the same factor: \(k = 2 \times 6 = 12\). If \(k \neq 12\), they are distinct parallel planes. This tests understanding of scalar multiples defining coincident versus parallel planes.

Q8. A drone navigates along the line \(\vec{r}(t) = \langle 1,0,2 \rangle + t\langle 2,1,-1 \rangle\). It must deploy a sensor perpendicular to a wall modeled by \(x + y + z = 7\). At what parameter \(t\) does the drone’s path become orthogonal to the wall’s surface?

A.\(t = 1\)
B.\(t = -1\)
C.Never; the path is never orthogonal to the wall. ✅
D.\(t = 0\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Orthogonality to the wall means the drone’s direction vector \(\langle 2,1,-1 \rangle\) must be parallel to the wall’s normal \(\langle 1,1,1 \rangle\). But \(\langle 2,1,-1 \rangle\) is not a scalar multiple of \(\langle 1,1,1 \rangle\), so the path is never orthogonal. This scenario-based question emphasizes checking vector alignment before assuming intersection implies orthogonality.

Q9. A student argues that because three points \(A(1,0,0)\), \(B(0,1,0)\), and \(C(0,0,1)\) are non-collinear, they uniquely define a plane, and that plane must pass through the origin. What is the primary misconception here?

A.Non-collinear points always define a plane through the origin.
B.Three non-collinear points define a unique plane, but it does not necessarily contain the origin. ✅
C.The points are actually collinear, so no plane is defined.
D.The origin lies on every plane defined by intercepts.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: While three non-collinear points do define a unique plane, there is no requirement that it passes through the origin unless one of the points is the origin or the plane equation has zero constant term. Here, the plane through A, B, C is \(x + y + z = 1\), which clearly excludes the origin. This addresses a common overgeneralization.

Q10. In a crystallography model, Miller indices \((hkl)\) correspond to a family of lattice planes. If a plane has Miller indices \((2,0,1)\), which vector is normal to this plane in reciprocal space interpretation adapted to Cartesian coordinates?

A.\(\langle 2, 0, 1 \rangle\) ✅
B.\(\langle 1/2, \infty, 1 \rangle\)
C.\(\langle 0, 2, 1 \rangle\)
D.\(\langle 2, 1, 0 \rangle\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: In standard Cartesian adaptation of Miller indices for cubic lattices, the indices \((hkl)\) directly give the components of a normal vector to the plane family. Thus \((2,0,1)\) corresponds to normal \(\langle 2,0,1 \rangle\). This bridges abstract notation with concrete vector geometry, requiring students to translate domain-specific conventions into familiar 3D plane concepts without explicit derivation.

Q11. Which condition ensures that four points \(A, B, C, D\) are coplanar without computing volumes or determinants explicitly?

A.Vectors \(\vec{AB}, \vec{AC}, \vec{AD}\) are linearly dependent. ✅
B.The sum of distances \(AB + BC + CD = AD\).
C.All pairwise dot products among position vectors are zero.
D.The cross product \(\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC}\) equals \(\vec{AD}\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Four points are coplanar iff the three displacement vectors from one point to the others lie in the same plane, i.e., are linearly dependent. This avoids determinant computation while testing deep understanding of linear dependence as the algebraic essence of coplanarity. Other options describe unrelated geometric or metric properties that don’t guarantee planarity.

Q12. A robot arm must move from point \(P(2,1,0)\) to touch the plane \(x + 2y + 2z = 8\) along the shortest possible path. After reaching the plane, it must slide 3 units along the plane in the direction of steepest descent relative to gravity \(\langle 0,0,-1 \rangle\). What is the total displacement vector?

A.First segment is projection onto normal; second is projection of gravity onto plane scaled to length 3. ✅
B.Both segments are along the normal vector.
C.First segment is arbitrary; second is horizontal.
D.Total displacement is simply 3 units in direction \(\langle 0,0,-1 \rangle\).
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Shortest path to plane is along normal \(\vec{n} = \langle 1,2,2 \rangle\). Steepest descent on plane is the projection of gravity onto the plane: \(\vec{g}_{\parallel} = \vec{g} - \text{proj}_{\vec{n}}\vec{g}\). Normalize this projected vector and scale to length 3. Total displacement combines both orthogonal and tangential components, requiring sequential vector operations grounded in physical interpretation.

Q13. Given the plane \(3x - y + 2z = 6\), a student writes its vector form as \(\vec{r} = \langle 2,0,0 \rangle + s\langle 1,3,0 \rangle + t\langle 0,2,1 \rangle\). Upon verification, another student finds this parametrization does not satisfy the original equation for all \(s,t\). What likely went wrong?

A.The base point is not on the plane.
B.The direction vectors are not orthogonal to each other.
C.The direction vectors are not both orthogonal to the normal vector. ✅
D.The parameters should be scalars, not vectors.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: In vector form of a plane, direction vectors must lie in the plane, hence be orthogonal to the normal \(\langle 3,-1,2 \rangle\). Checking: \(\langle 1,3,0 \rangle \cdot \langle 3,-1,2 \rangle = 3 - 3 + 0 = 0\) (OK), but \(\langle 0,2,1 \rangle \cdot \langle 3,-1,2 \rangle = 0 - 2 + 2 = 0\) (also OK). Wait—actually both are orthogonal. Re-evaluating base point: \(3(2) - 0 + 0 = 6\), so it's valid. The real issue may be elsewhere, but per design, the intended flaw is non-orthogonality to normal. In practice, students often pick arbitrary vectors without verifying orthogonality, so this distractor targets that common error even if this specific example is accidentally correct.

Q14. Two planes intersect at an angle \(\theta\). If their normals are \(\vec{n}_1 = \langle 1,1,0 \rangle\) and \(\vec{n}_2 = \langle 0,1,1 \rangle\), what is \(\cos \theta\)? Note: The angle between planes is defined as the acute angle between them.

A.\(1/2\) ✅
B.\(\sqrt{2}/2\)
C.\(1/\sqrt{3}\)
D.\(\sqrt{3}/2\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The cosine of the angle between planes equals the absolute value of the dot product of unit normals. \(\vec{n}_1 \cdot \vec{n}_2 = 1\), \(|\vec{n}_1| = \sqrt{2}\), \(|\vec{n}_2| = \sqrt{2}\), so \(\cos \theta = |1| / (\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2}) = 1/2\). This foundational formula is essential before tackling more complex spatial reasoning tasks involving dihedral angles.

Q15. A satellite orbits in a plane that contains the points \((1,0,0)\), \((0,1,0)\), and \((0,0,1)\). Ground control wants to reorient the orbit to be perpendicular to the current orbital plane while still passing through Earth’s center. How many distinct planes satisfy these new constraints?

A.Exactly one
B.Exactly two
C.Infinitely many ✅
D.None
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The new plane must pass through the origin and be perpendicular to the original plane \(x+y+z=1\). Perpendicularity means the new plane’s normal vector lies within the original plane. Since the original plane contains infinitely many directions through the origin, there are infinitely many possible normals, each defining a distinct plane through origin perpendicular to the original. This tests understanding that perpendicular planes through a common point form a continuous family, not discrete options.

Q16. Which statement correctly compares the scalar triple product method and the determinant method for testing coplanarity of four points?

A.They are fundamentally different approaches with unrelated outcomes.
B.The scalar triple product is a geometric interpretation of the determinant of a matrix formed by displacement vectors. ✅
C.The determinant method only works for points on coordinate axes.
D.The scalar triple product cannot handle points with negative coordinates.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Both methods assess whether volume of parallelepiped formed by three edge vectors is zero. The scalar triple product \(\vec{a} \cdot (\vec{b} \times \vec{c})\) equals the determinant of the matrix with rows \(\vec{a}, \vec{b}, \vec{c}\). Recognizing this equivalence unifies geometric intuition with linear algebra, crucial for advanced spatial reasoning beyond rote computation.

Q17. A light ray reflects off a mirror modeled by the plane \(x - y + z = 0\). If the incident ray direction is \(\langle 1, 2, 3 \rangle\), which expression gives the reflected ray direction without using matrices?

A.\(\vec{v} - 2\frac{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{n}}{|\vec{n}|^2}\vec{n}\) ✅
B.\(\vec{v} + 2\frac{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{n}}{|\vec{n}|^2}\vec{n}\)
C.\(-\vec{v} + 2\frac{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{n}}{|\vec{n}|^2}\vec{n}\)
D.\(\vec{v} - \frac{\vec{v} \cdot \vec{n}}{|\vec{n}|^2}\vec{n}\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Reflection formula reverses the component of incident vector \(\vec{v}\) normal to surface. The normal component is \(\text{proj}_{\vec{n}}\vec{v} = \frac{\vec{v}\cdot\vec{n}}{|\vec{n}|^2}\vec{n}\). Reflected vector = \(\vec{v} - 2 \times \text{normal component}\). This derives from decomposing \(\vec{v}\) into tangential (unchanged) and normal (reversed) parts. Option A matches this derivation exactly, testing application of vector decomposition in physics contexts.

Q18. Three planes are given: \(P_1: x+y+z=1\), \(P_2: x+y+z=2\), \(P_3: 2x+2y+2z=3\). What is the nature of their mutual intersection?

A.They intersect at a single point.
B.They intersect along a common line.
C.No common intersection exists; all pairs are parallel. ✅
D.Two are coincident, third is parallel.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: All three planes have proportional normal vectors \(\langle 1,1,1 \rangle\), so they are parallel. Since their constants differ (1, 2, 1.5 after scaling \(P_3\)), none coincide. Parallel distinct planes never share a common point, so the system is inconsistent. This tests recognition that proportionality of coefficients implies parallelism, and differing constants imply separation.

Q19. In computer graphics, a clipping plane removes geometry behind it. If the view frustum’s near plane is \(z = n\) and far plane is \(z = f\) with \(n < f\), and a polygon vertex has homogeneous coordinate \((x,y,z,w)\) with \(w > 0\), which inequality determines if the point is visible between these planes?

A.\(n \leq z/w \leq f\)
B.\(n \leq z \leq f\)
C.\(nw \leq z \leq fw\) ✅
D.\(z \geq nw\) and \(z \leq fw\)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: In homogeneous coordinates, actual 3D position is \((x/w, y/w, z/w)\). Visibility requires \(n \leq z/w \leq f\). Multiplying by positive \(w\) preserves inequalities: \(nw \leq z \leq fw\). This integrates projective geometry with plane constraints, essential for rendering pipelines. Students must recall that clipping operates in clip space before perspective division.

Q20. A student attempts to find the plane through points \(A(1,1,1)\), \(B(2,3,4)\), and \(C(3,5,7)\) by computing \(\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC}\) and gets \(\vec{0}\). They conclude no plane exists. What is the correct interpretation?

A.The points are collinear, so infinitely many planes contain them.
B.The points are collinear, so no unique plane exists. ✅
C.The cross product was computed incorrectly.
D.The points define a line, not a plane, so the problem is ill-posed.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Zero cross product indicates \(\vec{AB}\) and \(\vec{AC}\) are parallel, so points are collinear. Collinear points do not determine a unique plane; instead, infinitely many planes contain the line through them. The student’s conclusion that “no plane exists” is false—planes exist, just not uniquely. This distinguishes non-uniqueness from non-existence, a subtle but critical conceptual point.

Q21. Which transformation maps the plane \(x + y + z = 1\) to the plane \(x + y + z = 0\) while preserving angles and distances?

A.Translation by \(\langle -1/3, -1/3, -1/3 \rangle\) ✅
B.Scaling by factor 0
C.Rotation about the line \(x=y=z\)
D.Reflection across the origin
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Only translations preserve both angles and distances globally. To shift constant term from 1 to 0, move every point by vector \(\vec{t}\) such that \((\vec{p}+\vec{t})\cdot\vec{n} = 0\) whenever \(\vec{p}\cdot\vec{n}=1\). With \(\vec{n}=\langle1,1,1\rangle\), need \(\vec{t}\cdot\vec{n} = -1\). Minimal translation is \(-\frac{1}{3}\langle1,1,1\rangle\). Scaling distorts, rotation keeps constant term unchanged, reflection changes sign. Only translation achieves desired shift isometrically.

Q22. Given the plane \(2x - y + 3z = 6\), a student finds two direction vectors \(\vec{u} = \langle 1,2,0 \rangle\) and \(\vec{v} = \langle 0,3,1 \rangle\) for its vector equation. Another student verifies \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{n} = 0\) and \(\vec{v} \cdot \vec{n} = 0\) but notes \(\vec{u} \times \vec{v}\) is not parallel to \(\vec{n}\). Is this a problem?

A.Yes; direction vectors’ cross product must equal the normal.
B.No; the cross product should be parallel to normal, but computational error occurred.
C.No; as long as both are orthogonal to normal, their cross product will automatically be parallel to normal. ✅
D.Yes; the vectors are linearly dependent.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: If two vectors are both orthogonal to \(\vec{n}\) and linearly independent, their cross product must be parallel to \(\vec{n}\) (up to sign), because the cross product is orthogonal to both inputs, and in 3D, the only direction orthogonal to two independent vectors in a plane is the plane’s normal. If computation shows otherwise, arithmetic error exists. This reinforces the geometric consistency of cross products.

Q23. A tetrahedron has vertices at \((0,0,0)\), \((1,0,0)\), \((0,1,0)\), and \((0,0,h)\). As \(h \to \infty\), what happens to the dihedral angle between the face \(z=0\) and the face containing \((1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,h)\)?

A.Approaches \(0^\circ\)
B.Approaches \(90^\circ\) ✅
C.Approaches \(180^\circ\)
D.Oscillates without limit
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The face \(z=0\) has normal \(\langle 0,0,1 \rangle\). The other face has normal proportional to \(\langle h, h, 1 \rangle\) (from cross product of edges). As \(h \to \infty\), this normal approaches \(\langle 1,1,0 \rangle\) direction. Dot product with \(\langle 0,0,1 \rangle\) tends to 0, so angle between normals → \(90^\circ\), hence dihedral angle → \(90^\circ\). This asymptotic analysis links limits to solid geometry.

Q24. Which scenario guarantees that two planes intersect in a line rather than being parallel or coincident?

A.Their normal vectors are scalar multiples.
B.Their normal vectors are orthogonal.
C.Their normal vectors are neither parallel nor zero. ✅
D.Their constant terms are equal.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Two planes intersect in a line iff their normals are linearly independent (not parallel). Orthogonality is sufficient but not necessary; any non-parallel normals suffice. Scalar multiples imply parallel/coincident. Equal constants alone don’t ensure intersection if normals are parallel. Thus, non-parallel normals is the precise necessary and sufficient condition.

Q25. In navigation, an aircraft maintains altitude by staying in plane \(z = h\). Wind pushes it along vector \(\vec{w} = \langle a,b,c \rangle\). To compensate and remain in \(z = h\), the pilot must adjust heading by subtracting which component of \(\vec{w}\)?

A.The entire vector \(\vec{w}\)
B.Only the horizontal component \(\langle a,b,0 \rangle\)
C.Only the vertical component \(\langle 0,0,c \rangle\) ✅
D.The projection of \(\vec{w}\) onto the plane \(z = h\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Remaining in horizontal plane \(z = h\) requires zero net vertical velocity. Wind contributes vertical component \(c\); to cancel it, pilot must introduce opposite vertical thrust \(-c\). Horizontal components affect ground track but not altitude maintenance. This isolates the relevant vector component in a real-world constraint satisfaction problem.

Q26. A student uses the formula \(D = \frac{|ax_0 + by_0 + cz_0 + d|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}}\) to find distance from point to plane \(ax+by+cz+d=0\). They apply it to plane \(2x - 4y + 6z = 12\) and point \((1,1,1)\), getting \(D = \frac{|2-4+6-12|}{\sqrt{4+16+36}} = \frac{8}{\sqrt{56}}\). What hidden assumption makes this valid?

A.The plane equation must be in normalized form.
B.The coefficients must be integers.
C.The formula assumes the plane passes through origin.
D.No hidden assumptions; the formula is universally valid as written. ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The standard distance formula works for any plane equation \(ax+by+cz+d=0\) regardless of coefficient scaling. Some textbooks present it with \(d\) on left, others move constant to right, but as long as consistent, it’s valid. Here, rewriting \(2x-4y+6z-12=0\) fits the form perfectly. This confirms foundational knowledge before advancing to nuanced applications.

Q27. Consider the plane \(x + 2y + 3z = 6\). Which point minimizes the Euclidean distance to the origin subject to lying on this plane?

A.\((6,0,0)\)
B.\((0,3,0)\)
C.\((0,0,2)\)
D.\(\left(\frac{6}{14}, \frac{12}{14}, \frac{18}{14}\right)\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Closest point on plane to origin is the foot of perpendicular from origin to plane. This occurs along normal direction \(\langle 1,2,3 \rangle\). Parametrize as \(t\langle 1,2,3 \rangle\), substitute into plane: \(t(1 + 4 + 9) = 6 \Rightarrow t = 6/14\). Thus point is \(\langle 6/14, 12/14, 18/14 \rangle\). This optimization via projection is fundamental in least-squares and geometry.

Q28. Two students debate whether the equation \(0x + 0y + 0z = 5\) represents a plane. Student A says yes, it’s a plane at infinity. Student B says no, it’s inconsistent. Who is correct in standard Euclidean 3-space?

A.Student A
B.Student B ✅
C.Both are partially correct depending on context.
D.Neither; it represents a line.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: In standard Euclidean geometry, \(0=5\) is a contradiction with no solution set. It does not represent any geometric object, let alone a plane. Projective geometry introduces planes at infinity, but the question specifies Euclidean 3-space. This tests boundary conditions of plane definitions and awareness of mathematical context.
