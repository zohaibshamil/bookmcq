What is Geometric representation of vectors?

Definition:
Geometrically, a vector is depicted as a directed line segment from an initial point (tail) to a terminal point (head), where length represents magnitude and arrow orientation represents direction.

Example:
The vector AB⃗\vec{AB}AB from A(1,2)A(1,2)A(1,2) to B(4,6)B(4,6)B(4,6) is drawn as an arrow starting at AAA and ending at BBB, with length (4−1)2+(6−2)2=5\sqrt{(4-1)^2+(6-2)^2} = 5(4−1)2+(6−2)2​=5.

Reason:
Visual representation aids intuition for operations like addition (tip-to-tail) and helps students connect abstract algebra to physical displacements or forces.

Q1. A drone flies from point AAA to BBB with displacement vector u⃗\vec{u}u, then from BBB to CCC with v⃗\vec{v}v. If the pilot mistakenly uses v⃗−u⃗\vec{v} - \vec{u}v−u instead of u⃗+v⃗\vec{u} + \vec{v}u+v to compute total displacement, what geometric error has occurred?

B.The pilot computed the vector from CCC back to AAA instead of AAA to CCC. ✅
C.The pilot found the difference in magnitudes rather than the resultant vector.
D.The pilot correctly applied the parallelogram law but labeled vertices incorrectly.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The sum u⃗+v⃗\vec{u} + \vec{v}u+v gives displacement from AAA to CCC. The expression v⃗−u⃗\vec{v} - \vec{u}v−u equals v⃗+(−u⃗)\vec{v} + (-\vec{u})v+(−u), which geometrically represents starting at the tip of u⃗\vec{u}u and going backward along u⃗\vec{u}u, then forward along v⃗\vec{v}v.
