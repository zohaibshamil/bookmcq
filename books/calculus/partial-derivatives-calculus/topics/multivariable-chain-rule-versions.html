What is Multivariable chain rule versions?

Definition:
Different tree diagram configurations handle cases like \( z=f(x,y) \) with \( x=x(s,t), y=y(s,t) \) yielding \( \frac{\partial z}{\partial s}, \frac{\partial z}{\partial t} \).

Example:
In polar coordinates \( x=r\cos\theta, y=r\sin\theta \), \( \frac{\partial f}{\partial r} = f_x \cos\theta + f_y \sin\theta \) transforms Cartesian gradients.

Reason:
Multiple versions accommodate diverse dependency structures, making coordinate transformations and parameter substitutions tractable in applied mathematics.

Q1. A temperature field \( T(x,y) \) is measured along a particle path defined by \( x(t)=t^2, y(t)=\sin t \). If \( \nabla T(1,0) = \langle 3, -2 \rangle \), which expression correctly represents the instantaneous rate of change of temperature experienced by the particle at \( t=1 \)?

A.\( \frac{\partial T}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial T}{\partial y} \)
B.\( 6 - 2\cos(1) \)
C.\( 3(2t) - 2\cos(t) \) evaluated at \( t=1 \)
D.\( \nabla T \cdot \langle 2t, \cos t \rangle \) evaluated at \( t=1 \) ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The chain rule for a scalar field along a parametric path requires the dot product of the gradient and the velocity vector. Option D correctly formulates this as \( \nabla T \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) \). Option C computes the components but fails to explicitly recognize the dot product structure, while A ignores the path's velocity entirely.

Q2. In modeling atmospheric pressure \( P(r, \theta, z) \) in cylindrical coordinates, a researcher incorrectly writes \( \frac{dP}{dt} = \frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\frac{dr}{dt} + \frac{\partial P}{\partial \theta}\frac{d\theta}{dt} + \frac{\partial P}{\partial z}\frac{dz}{dt} \) for a moving drone. What is the fundamental dimensional error in this formulation?

A.The partial derivative with respect to \( \theta \) must be multiplied by \( r \) to match linear velocity units. ✅
B.The term \( \frac{d\theta}{dt} \) should be replaced with angular acceleration.
C.The \( z \)-component is irrelevant for atmospheric modeling and should be omitted.
D.There is no error; cylindrical coordinates are orthogonal and the chain rule applies directly.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This is an error analysis question involving mixed concepts. In cylindrical coordinates, \( \theta \) has units of radians (dimensionless), so \( \frac{\partial P}{\partial \theta} \) has units of pressure. Multiplying by \( \frac{d\theta}{dt} \) (1/time) yields pressure/time, which seems correct dimensionally. However, physically, the rate of change due to angular motion depends on the tangential speed \( r\frac{d\theta}{dt} \). The correct chain rule term is \( \frac{\partial P}{\partial s_\theta} \frac{ds_\theta}{dt} \) where \( ds_\theta = r d\theta \), making the term \( \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial P}{\partial \theta} (r \frac{d\theta}{dt}) \). The student's version misses the metric factor implicitly required when interpreting physical rates.

Q3. Given \( w = f(u,v) \) where \( u = x+y \) and \( v = x-y \), a student claims that \( \frac{\partial^2 w}{\partial x^2} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial u^2} + \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial v^2} \). Analyze this claim to identify the missing component in their second-order chain rule application.

A.They forgot the mixed partial derivative terms \( 2\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial u \partial v} \). ✅
B.They should have subtracted the \( v \) term instead of adding it.
C.The second derivatives of \( u \) and \( v \) with respect to \( x \) are non-zero and were omitted.
D.No component is missing; the transformation is linear so cross terms vanish.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Applying the chain rule twice requires product rule expansion. Since \( \frac{\partial w}{\partial x} = f_u + f_v \), differentiating again yields \( (f_{uu} + f_{uv}) + (f_{vu} + f_{vv}) \). Assuming continuity, \( f_{uv}=f_{vu} \), resulting in \( f_{uu} + 2f_{uv} + f_{vv} \). The student’s error is neglecting the cross-derivative term, a common misconception when transforming coordinates even in linear cases.

Q4. Consider a contour map of \( z=f(x,y) \) where level curves are densely packed near point A and sparse near point B. A path \( \mathbf{r}(t) \) passes through both points with constant speed. Without explicit functions, how does \( \frac{dz}{dt} \) compare at A versus B assuming the path crosses contours perpendicularly?

A.\( \frac{dz}{dt} \) is larger at A because the gradient magnitude is greater where contours are dense. ✅
B.\( \frac{dz}{dt} \) is larger at B because the path covers more horizontal distance per unit time.
C.\( \frac{dz}{dt} \) is equal at both points since the path speed is constant.
D.Cannot be determined without knowing the specific elevation values of the contours.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This graph-based question tests conceptual understanding of the geometric interpretation of the chain rule \( \frac{dz}{dt} = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{v} \). Contour density is proportional to \( |\nabla f| \). With constant speed and perpendicular crossing, the dot product maximizes at A. Students often confuse spatial density with temporal rate or assume constant speed implies constant vertical rate.

Q5. In thermodynamics, enthalpy \( H(S,P) \) is often transformed to \( H(T,P) \) using \( dH = TdS + VdP \). When applying the chain rule to find \( (\frac{\partial H}{\partial T})_P \), which relationship correctly bridges the variable change from entropy \( S \) to temperature \( T \)?

A.\( (\frac{\partial H}{\partial T})_P = T(\frac{\partial S}{\partial T})_P \) ✅
B.\( (\frac{\partial H}{\partial T})_P = (\frac{\partial H}{\partial S})_P (\frac{\partial S}{\partial T})_P + V(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T})_S \)
C.\( (\frac{\partial H}{\partial T})_P = C_P \) only if volume is constant.
D.\( (\frac{\partial H}{\partial T})_P = T(\frac{\partial S}{\partial T})_P + V(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T})_H \)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This application question requires recognizing that at constant \( P \), \( dP=0 \), simplifying the total differential. The chain rule reduces to \( (\frac{\partial H}{\partial T})_P = (\frac{\partial H}{\partial S})_P (\frac{\partial S}{\partial T})_P \). Since \( (\frac{\partial H}{\partial S})_P = T \) from the fundamental relation, option A is correct. Distractors include irrelevant variables or incorrect constraint conditions.

Q6. A function \( z = g(r) \) depends only on radial distance \( r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2} \). If \( \frac{dz}{dr} = k \) (constant), what is the value of \( x\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} + y\frac{\partial z}{\partial y} \)?

A.\( k\sqrt{x^2+y^2} \) ✅
B.\( k \)
C.\( 2k(x+y) \)
D.\( 0 \)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Using the chain rule, \( \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = \frac{dz}{dr}\frac{x}{r} \) and similarly for \( y \). Substituting gives \( x(k\frac{x}{r}) + y(k\frac{y}{r}) = \frac{k}{r}(x^2+y^2) = kr \). This tests the ability to manipulate multivariable chain rule expressions algebraically rather than just computing numerical derivatives. It connects Euler’s homogeneous function theorem conceptually.

Q7. When verifying a numerical simulation of heat flow, you notice \( \frac{dT}{dt} \) computed via finite differences differs significantly from \( \nabla T \cdot \mathbf{v} \) calculated analytically at a grid point. Which source of discrepancy represents a misuse of the chain rule rather than numerical truncation error?

A.Using Eulerian spatial gradients with Lagrangian particle velocity without accounting for grid deformation. ✅
B.Insufficient grid resolution causing aliasing of high-frequency temperature modes.
C.Time step size exceeding the Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy stability limit.
D.Round-off error accumulation in floating-point arithmetic operations.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This error analysis scenario distinguishes between computational artifacts and conceptual misapplication. The chain rule \( \frac{DT}{Dt} = \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} + \mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla T \) includes a local time derivative. If the simulation tracks particles (Lagrangian) but uses fixed-grid (Eulerian) gradients without the \( \partial T/\partial t \) term or proper interpolation, the chain rule is violated. Other options describe numerical issues, not chain rule errors.

Q9. In optimizing a neural network loss \( L(w,b) \), parameters evolve via gradient descent: \( w_{new} = w - \eta \nabla_w L \). How does the multivariable chain rule justify backpropagation through a composite function \( L(f(g(w))) \)?

A.It decomposes \( \nabla_w L \) into a product of Jacobians representing local sensitivity at each layer. ✅
B.It ensures the learning rate \( \eta \) adapts to the curvature of the loss surface.
C.It guarantees convexity of the composite loss function for global convergence.
D.It replaces partial derivatives with total derivatives to account for parameter dependencies.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Backpropagation is essentially the chain rule applied recursively. The gradient of a composition is the product of derivatives (Jacobians) of constituent functions. This allows efficient computation by reusing intermediate results. Option D confuses total/partial distinction; B relates to optimization dynamics not chain rule justification; C is false as chain rule doesn't ensure convexity.

Q11. For a function \( u(x,t) \) satisfying the wave equation, consider characteristic coordinates \( \xi=x+ct, \eta=x-ct \). Transforming \( \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} \) requires careful application of the chain rule. Which term arises specifically from the second application of the operator \( \frac{\partial}{\partial t} = c\frac{\partial}{\partial \xi} - c\frac{\partial}{\partial \eta} \)?

A.\( c^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \xi^2} - 2c^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \xi \partial \eta} + c^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \eta^2} \) ✅
B.\( c \frac{\partial u}{\partial \xi} - c \frac{\partial u}{\partial \eta} \)
C.\( \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} - \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} \)
D.\( 2c \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \xi \partial \eta} \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This Olympiad-style question tests rigorous operator manipulation. Applying \( \partial_t \) twice: first gives \( c u_\xi - c u_\eta \). Second application distributes: \( c(c u_{\xi\xi} - c u_{\xi\eta}) - c(c u_{\eta\xi} - c u_{\eta\eta}) \). Combining yields \( c^2(u_{\xi\xi} - 2u_{\xi\eta} + u_{\eta\eta}) \). Students often miss the cross-term sign or coefficient, confusing it with single-variable second derivatives.

Q12. Two students compute \( \frac{dw}{dt} \) for \( w=x^2y, x=t^2, y=t^3 \). Student A gets \( 7t^6 \) by substituting first then differentiating. Student B gets \( 4t^5 \cdot t^3 + t^4 \cdot 3t^2 = 7t^8 \) using chain rule. Who is correct and what explains the discrepancy?

A.Student A; Student B made an algebraic error in exponent addition during chain rule application. ✅
B.Student B; substitution method loses information about intermediate variable dependencies.
C.Both are correct; they represent different physical quantities.
D.Student A; chain rule cannot be applied to polynomial compositions.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Direct substitution gives \( w=(t^2)^2(t^3)=t^7 \), derivative is \( 7t^6 \). Chain rule: \( w_x=2xy=2t^5, w_y=x^2=t^4, x'=2t, y'=3t^2 \). Sum: \( 2t^5(2t) + t^4(3t^2) = 4t^6 + 3t^6 = 7t^6 \). Student B incorrectly computed \( x' \) as \( 2t^2 \) or similar. This error analysis reinforces checking work through multiple methods and identifying specific computational mistakes in chain rule execution.

Q13. In economics, utility \( U(C,L) \) depends on consumption \( C(w) \) and leisure \( L(w) \), both functions of wage \( w \). If \( \frac{dU}{dw} > 0 \) but \( \frac{\partial U}{\partial C} \frac{dC}{dw} < 0 \), what must be true about the leisure-consumption trade-off?

A.The marginal utility gain from increased leisure outweighs the consumption loss, implying backward-bending labor supply possibility. ✅
B.Consumption must be an inferior good at this wage level.
C.Leisure and consumption are perfect complements in the utility function.
D.The chain rule cannot yield positive total derivative if one component is negative.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This mixed-concept question integrates economic theory with calculus. Since \( \frac{dU}{dw} = U_C C_w + U_L L_w > 0 \) and first term is negative, second term must be positive and larger in magnitude. This implies significant leisure response to wage changes, characteristic of backward-bending supply curves where income effect dominates substitution effect. Tests interpretation of chain rule signs in real-world models beyond pure computation.
