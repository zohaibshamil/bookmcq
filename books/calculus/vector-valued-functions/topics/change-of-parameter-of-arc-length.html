What is Change of Parameter of Arc Length?

Definition:
Change of parameter to arc length involves re-expressing a curve \( \vec{r}(t) \) as \( \vec{r}(s) \) where \( s \) measures distance traveled from a fixed point.

Example:
For \( \vec{r}(t) = \langle 3\cos t, 3\sin t \rangle \), arc length \( s = 3t \) leads to \( \vec{r}(s) = \langle 3\cos(s/3), 3\sin(s/3) \rangle \).

Reason:
Arc length parametrization removes dependence on arbitrary speed, yielding intrinsic geometric properties independent of traversal rate.

Q1. A particle moves along a curve defined by \( \mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t, t^2, t^3 \rangle \). If the parameter is changed to \( u = t^2 \) for \( t > 0 \), how does the speed of the particle with respect to the new parameter \( u \) compare to the original speed at corresponding points?

A.The speed with respect to \( u \) is identical to the speed with respect to \( t \).
B.The speed with respect to \( u \) is exactly half the speed with respect to \( t \) divided by \( \sqrt{u} \). ✅
C.The speed with respect to \( u \) is the original speed multiplied by \( 2\sqrt{u} \).
D.The speed with respect to \( u \) cannot be determined without re-evaluating the arc length integral.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When changing parameters from \( t \) to \( u \), the chain rule dictates that \( \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{du} = \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \frac{dt}{du} \). Since \( u=t^2 \), \( dt/du = 1/(2\sqrt{u}) \). The magnitude of velocity (speed) scales by this factor. Students often forget that speed is not invariant under non-linear reparameterization, confusing geometric path properties with kinematic quantities dependent on parametrization.

A common misconception is assuming negative derivatives yield negative lengths, confusing signed line integrals with scalar arc length.

Q24. A researcher observes that for curve \( \mathbf{r}(t) \), the ratio \( \frac{d^2s/dt^2}{ds/dt} \) is constant \( k \). What functional form must \( s(t) \) take?

B.Linear: \( s(t) = kt + c \)
C.Quadratic: \( s(t) = kt^2 \)
D.Logarithmic: \( s(t) = k\ln t \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Let \( v = ds/dt \). Then \( dv/dt / v = k \implies d(\ln v)/dt = k \implies \ln v = kt + C \implies v = Ae^{kt} \). Integrating gives \( s(t) = (A/k)e^{kt} + B \) for \( k≠0 \). This exponential growth/decay of speed characterizes curves with constant logarithmic acceleration. Recognizing this ODE structure links kinematic descriptions to parametric forms, demonstrating how differential constraints on arc length dictate allowable parameterizations beyond elementary cases.

Q25. In designing a font glyph, a Bézier curve \( \mathbf{B}(t) \) must have uniform stroke width. Stroke width perception depends on local sampling density during rasterization. Why is reparameterizing \( \mathbf{B}(t) \) by approximate arc length before rendering superior to using native parameter?

A.Native Bézier parameter correlates poorly with geometric distance, causing uneven ink deposition. ✅
B.Rasterizers inherently assume arc-length parameterization.
C.Bézier curves cannot represent true circles without reparameterization.
D.Uniform parameter causes aliasing artifacts independent of geometry.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Bézier parameter \( t \) distributes control influence non-uniformly; equal \( \Delta t \) doesn’t correspond to equal spatial displacement, especially near endpoints or high-curvature zones. Rasterizing with native \( t \) produces variable point density, leading to inconsistent stroke thickness. Approximate arc-length reparameterization equalizes spatial sampling, ensuring perceptual uniformity. This applied question bridges CAD theory and digital typography, illustrating why theoretical reparameterization matters in consumer-facing technology beyond pure mathematics.
