What is Change of parameter for curves?

Definition:
Change of parameter replaces t with a new variable u via a bijective function t=ϕ(u) to alter the curve's description without changing its geometry.

Example:
Substituting t=u3 in r(t)=⟨t,t⟩ gives r(u)=⟨u3,u3⟩.

Reason:
Reparameterization can simplify calculations, resolve singularities, or adapt to specific boundary conditions in applied problems.

Q1. A particle moves along a curve defined by r(t)=⟨t,t2⟩. If the parameter is changed to s=t3, how does the orientation and speed of traversal change for t &gt; 0 ?

A.Orientation reverses; speed decreases uniformly.
B.Orientation remains same; speed increases non-linearly with respect to s. ✅
C.Orientation remains same; speed becomes constant.
D.Orientation reverses; speed increases linearly with respect to s.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When substituting t=s1/3, the new vector function becomes R(s)=⟨s1/3,s2/3⟩. Since s=t3 is strictly increasing for positive values, orientation is preserved. However, the derivative magnitude changes because the chain rule introduces a factor of dt/ds, causing non-linear speed variation relative to the new parameter.

Q2. Consider two parametrizations of the same geometric path: r1​(t) and r2​(u). If \mathbf{r}_1&#039;(t) \times \mathbf{r}_2&#039;(u) = \mathbf{0} at corresponding points, what can be definitively concluded about the relationship between parameters t and u?

A.The parameters are identical.
B.The curve has zero curvature at that point.
C.The tangent vectors are parallel, implying a valid smooth reparametrization exists locally. ✅
D.The acceleration vectors must also be parallel.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The cross product being zero indicates that the velocity vectors are collinear or parallel. This is a necessary condition for two parametrizations to trace the same geometric curve with consistent orientation. It does not imply identical parameters or equal accelerations, as reparametrization affects higher-order derivatives through the chain rule and product rule.

Q3. A student claims that reparametrizing r(t)=⟨cost,sint⟩ using u=t2 preserves the unit speed property because the geometric shape is unchanged. Which statement best analyzes this error?

A.The student is correct; geometry dictates speed.
B.The student confuses geometric invariance with kinematic properties; ∥dr/du∥=1 due to the chain rule factor dt/du. ✅
C.The student failed to recognize that u=t2 is not one-to-one over all reals.
D.The error lies in assuming sine and cosine remain orthogonal under reparametrization.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Reparametrization preserves the image set (geometry) but alters the velocity vector magnitude unless specifically designed for arc length. Applying the chain rule gives dr/du=(dr/dt)(dt/du). Since dt/du=1/(2u​), the speed varies with position. Geometric invariance does not imply kinematic invariance; confusing these is a fundamental misconception in vector calculus.

Q4. Given a smooth curve C and a bijective differentiable function ϕ(s)=t, which condition ensures that the reparametrized curve R(s)=r(ϕ(s)) maintains the exact same orientation as r(t)?

A.ϕ(s) must be continuous.
B. \phi&#039;(s) &gt; 0 for all s in the domain. ✅
C. \phi&#039;&#039;(s) = 0 for all s.
D. \|\mathbf{r}&#039;(t)\| = \|\mathbf{R}&#039;(s)\| .
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Orientation preservation in reparametrization depends solely on the sign of the derivative of the parameter transformation function. If \phi&#039;(s) &gt; 0 , the mapping is strictly increasing, preserving direction. A negative derivative would reverse orientation. Continuity alone allows decreasing functions, and equal speed is unrelated to directional consistency along the curve.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a cubic spline component, \|\mathbf{r}&#039;(t)\| typically involves (at2+bt+c)2+...​, resulting in the square root of a degree-four polynomial. Such integrals generally yield elliptic integrals, which lack closed-form expressions in elementary functions. This necessitates numerical inversion or approximation techniques for practical arc-length parametrization in engineering simulations involving complex trajectories.

📖 Explanation: As t→∞, u=e−t→0+. The original helix extends indefinitely along the z-axis. The reparametrized version maps this infinite extension to a finite interval near u=0. While the geometric set traced is identical for corresponding domains, the limiting behavior in parameter space differs fundamentally, compressing infinite traversal into a boundary point.

Q7. Examine a graph showing speed v(t) versus time for a particle. After reparametrization by arc length s, what would the graph of speed versus s look like?

📖 Explanation: By definition, arc length parametrization means the parameter itself measures distance traveled. Therefore, the rate of change of position with respect to arc length is identically unity: ∥dr/ds∥=1. Regardless of the original speed profile v(t), plotting speed against arc length always yields a constant value of one, distinguishing it from time-based representations.

Q8. Two students reparametrize r(t)=⟨t,t⟩. Student A uses u=2t; Student B uses u=t+5. They argue about whether the unit tangent vector T changes. Who is correct and why?

A.Student A, because scaling changes direction.
B.Student B, because translation affects tangency.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The unit tangent vector \mathbf{T} = \mathbf{r}&#039;(t)/\|\mathbf{r}&#039;(t)\| is a geometric property independent of parametrization. Under any smooth bijection with positive derivative, the chain rule introduces a scalar factor that cancels during normalization. Both linear transformations preserve orientation and smoothness, so T remains identical at corresponding geometric points regardless of scaling or translation.

For arbitrary t, one must use \kappa = \|\mathbf{r}&#039; \times \mathbf{r}&#039;&#039;\| / \|\mathbf{r}&#039;\|^3 . Using the unadjusted formula with a general parameter produces erroneous values that vary with speed, misleading physical interpretations of flow dynamics.

Q10. Suppose r(t) has a cusp at t=0 where \mathbf{r}&#039;(0)=\mathbf{0} . Can a smooth reparametrization R(s) eliminate this singularity to make \mathbf{R}&#039;(s) \neq \mathbf{0} everywhere?

A.Yes, by choosing s=t3.
B.Yes, arc length parametrization always regularizes cusps.
C.No, a cusp is a geometric singularity invariant under diffeomorphisms. ✅
D.Only if the cusp is removable via coordinate rotation.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A cusp represents a genuine geometric singularity where the curve fails to have a well-defined tangent line. Reparametrization by a smooth bijection cannot create a non-zero velocity where none existed geometrically. While some algebraic singularities are artifacts of parametrization, true cusps persist because no smooth invertible transformation can map a zero-velocity point to a regular point while preserving the curve's local structure.

📖 Explanation: Kepler’s second law states that radius vectors sweep equal areas in equal times for gravitational orbits. However, for arbitrary parametrization, the area sweep rate is \frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{r}&#039;\| . To achieve uniform area parametrization, one must integrate this quantity. This differs from arc length and requires computing the magnitude of the cross product, linking geometry directly to the desired physical constraint.

📖 Explanation: For u(t)=t+sin(t) to serve as a valid reparametrization, it must be strictly monotonic (bijective) on the domain. The derivative u&#039;(t) = 1 + \cos(t) must be positive everywhere. Since cos(t)≥−1, u&#039;(t) \geq 0 , but equals zero at odd multiples of π. Strict positivity requires avoiding these points or restricting domain. Without monotonicity, the mapping folds, violating the definition of a proper parametrization.

Q15. A curve is given by r(t)=⟨t3,t2⟩. At t=0, \mathbf{r}&#039;(0)=\mathbf{0} . A student proposes s=t2 to regularize it. Does R(s) have a well-defined tangent at s=0?

A.Yes, \mathbf{R}&#039;(0) = \langle 0, 1 \rangle .
B.No, the cusp persists because s=t2 is not invertible near zero.
C.Yes, but only from the right side since s≥0.
D.No, because t3 becomes s3/2 which is non-differentiable at 0. ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Substituting t=±s​ leads to ambiguity, but even considering t≥0, x=s3/2 and y=s. Then dx/ds=(3/2)s1/2→0 and dy/ds=1. So \mathbf{R}&#039;(0) = \langle 0,1 \rangle seems defined. However, the original curve has a cusp: left and right derivatives differ in direction. The reparametrization s=t2 collapses both branches onto s≥0, losing directional information. Thus, while R(s) appears differentiable, it misrepresents the original geometry’s singularity.

C.Linear increase throughout.
D.Discontinuous jump at midpoint.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Ease-in-ease-out requires smooth acceleration and deceleration. Q17. Two curves r1​(t) and r2​(u) trace the same path. If \mathbf{r}_1&#039;&#039;(t) \cdot \mathbf{T} \neq \mathbf{r}_2&#039;&#039;(u) \cdot \mathbf{T} at corresponding points, what does this indicate?

A.The curves are geometrically distinct.
B.Tangential acceleration is parametrization-dependent, unlike normal acceleration. ✅
C.There is an error in computation since tangential components must match.
D.Normal acceleration differs due to reparametrization.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Acceleration decomposes into tangential and normal components: a=aT​T+aN​N. The normal component aN​=κv2 relates to geometry and speed squared, while tangential component aT​=dv/dt depends explicitly on how speed changes with the chosen parameter. Different parametrizations yield different dv/dt even at the same geometric point, making tangential acceleration non-invariant, whereas curvature and normal direction remain geometric.

Q18. A physicist argues that since arc length s is intrinsic, any observable derived from r(s) must be frame-independent. Identify the flaw in linking reparametrization to reference frames.

A.Reparametrization is a mathematical relabeling, not a physical Lorentz or Galilean transformation. ✅
B.Arc length is only invariant in Euclidean space.
C.Observables always depend on parametrization choice.
D.Frame independence requires tensor formulation, not just arc length.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Reparametrization changes only the label assigned to points on a curve, leaving geometric quantities invariant. Reference frame transformations involve changing coordinates in spacetime, affecting measurements like time intervals and lengths relativistically. Confusing internal parameter freedom with external observer dependence is a category error. Arc length parametrization simplifies equations but does not confer physical frame invariance; that requires proper tensorial representation under coordinate transformations.

Ignoring these higher-order transformation terms is a common pitfall; recognizing their structured cancellation confirms geometric invariance rigorously.

Reparametrization by arc length yields R(s) with \mathbf{R}&#039;(s) continuous and non-zero at corresponding points, effectively regularizing the parametrization while preserving the geometric tangent.
