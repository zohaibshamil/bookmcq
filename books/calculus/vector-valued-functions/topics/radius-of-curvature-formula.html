What is Radius of curvature formula?

Definition:
The radius of curvature is \( \rho = \frac{1}{\kappa} \), representing the radius of the osculating circle that best approximates the curve locally.

Example:
If \( \kappa = 0.5 \, m^{-1} \), then \( \rho = 2 \, m \).

Reason:
It translates abstract curvature into tangible geometric size, useful for manufacturing lenses and analyzing stress concentrations.

Q1. A particle moves along a space curve with velocity \( \mathbf{v} \) and acceleration \( \mathbf{a} \). If the speed is constant, which expression correctly gives the radius of curvature \( \rho \)?

A.\( \rho = \frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|^2}{\|\mathbf{a}\|} \)
B.\( \rho = \frac{\|\mathbf{v}\|^3}{\|\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{a}\|} \) ✅
C.\( \rho = \frac{\|\mathbf{a}\|}{\|\mathbf{v}\|^2} \)
D.\( \rho = \frac{\|\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{a}\|}{\|\mathbf{v}\|^3} \)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When speed is constant, tangential acceleration vanishes, so total acceleration equals normal acceleration. The general formula \( \rho = \|\mathbf{v}\|^3 / \|\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{a}\| \) still holds, but simplifies conceptually. Option A misses the cross product structure essential for 3D curves, while C and D invert the relationship incorrectly.

Q2. For a plane curve defined parametrically by \( x(t), y(t) \), a student computes curvature using \( \kappa = |x'y'' - y'x''| / (x'^2 + y'^2)^{3/2} \). They then claim \( \rho = 1/\kappa \) always. Under what condition does this fail?

A.When the curve has an inflection point where \( \kappa = 0 \) ✅
B.When the parameterization is not arc-length
C.When the curve is closed
D.Never; it always holds
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The reciprocal relationship \( \rho = 1/\kappa \) breaks down when curvature is zero because division by zero is undefined. At inflection points, the osculating circle degenerates to a line, making radius infinite. Students often overlook domain restrictions when applying inverse relationships mechanically without considering geometric singularities.

📖 Explanation: Radius of curvature is an intrinsic geometric property independent of parameterization. While velocity and acceleration vectors differ between parameterizations, the cross-product formula \( \|\mathbf{v}\|^3 / \|\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{a}\| \) yields identical results. This tests understanding that geometry transcends kinematics, a crucial conceptual distinction in vector calculus.

Claiming \( \rho=0 \) suggests they computed \( \kappa \) but labeled it as radius. This common misconception confuses the inverse relationship, especially under exam pressure when symbolic manipulation overshadows dimensional analysis.

Q6. A student derives \( \rho = \|\mathbf{v}\|^2 / a_N \) for circular motion and assumes it applies universally. For a general space curve, why is this insufficient without modification?

A.It ignores tangential acceleration components
B.It only works for unit-speed parameterizations
C.Normal acceleration alone doesn't capture directional change rate in 3D ✅
D.The formula is actually correct universally
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: While \( a_N = v^2 / \rho \) holds generally, computing \( a_N \) from \( \mathbf{a} \) requires projecting out tangential component: \( a_N = \|\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{a}\| / \|\mathbf{v}\| \). Simply using \( \|\mathbf{a}\| \) includes tangential effects, overestimating normal acceleration and underestimating \( \rho \). This tests decomposition skills beyond memorized circular motion formulas.

The error lies in treating \( \mathbf{a} \) as independent of \( v \), ignoring kinematic coupling inherent in constrained motion.

Q14. If \( \mathbf{r}(t) \) traces a curve and \( \tilde{\mathbf{r}}(u) = \mathbf{r}(g(u)) \) with \( g'(u)>0 \), prove \( \rho \) is invariant. Which step is most easily mishandled?

A.Chain rule application to first derivative
B.Cross product scaling under reparameterization
C.Cancellation of \( g' \) factors in numerator/denominator ✅
D.Assuming \( g''=0 \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Under reparameterization, \( \tilde{\mathbf{v}} = g'\mathbf{v} \) and \( \tilde{\mathbf{a}} = g''\mathbf{v} + (g')^2\mathbf{a} \). The cross product \( \tilde{\mathbf{v}} \times \tilde{\mathbf{a}} = (g')^3 (\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{a}) \) because \( \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{v}=0 \). Numerator gains \( (g')^3 \), denominator \( \|\tilde{\mathbf{v}}\|^3 = (g')^3 \|\mathbf{v}\|^3 \), canceling perfectly. Missing the vanishing self-cross term causes erroneous residual \( g'' \) dependence.
