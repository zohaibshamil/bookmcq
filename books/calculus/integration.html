About 6. Integration

Integration is introduced as the reverse process of differentiation, focusing on finding antiderivatives and recovering original functions from their rates of change. The indefinite integral represents a family of functions that differ only by a constant, serving as the foundational skill for all integration work. This chapter emphasizes recognizing patterns and applying basic integration rules, building the computational fluency needed before tackling definite integrals and their applications.

The definite integral connects integration to geometry by defining the exact area under a curve through the limit of Riemann sums. Rather than approximating area with rectangles, the definite integral provides a precise value that represents accumulated quantities like distance, volume, or total change. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus then brilliantly links these two concepts, showing that evaluating a definite integral can be done simply by finding an antiderivative, making area calculations both elegant and practical.
