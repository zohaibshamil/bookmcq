📝 Absolute Maxima and Minima in calculus

What is Absolute Maxima and Minima in calculus?

Definition:
Absolute extrema are the highest and lowest values of a function over its entire domain or a specified interval. Unlike relative extrema, absolute max/min represent global peaks and valleys, found by comparing critical points and endpoints on closed intervals.

Example:
For $f(x) = x^2$ on $[-1, 2]$, critical point at $x=0$ gives min $0$, endpoint $x=2$ gives max $4$, so absolute min is $0$ and max is $4$.

Reason:
Global optimization requires checking all candidates, including boundaries, to ensure the true highest and lowest values are identified for practical applications.

📝 All Absolute Maxima and Minima in calculus MCQs

Q1. Given a continuous function \(f\) on the closed interval \([0,4]\) with \(f(0)=2\) and \(f(4)=5\), which statement must be true about the absolute extrema of \(f\) on \([0,4]\)?

A. The absolute maximum occurs at \(x=0\).
B. The absolute minimum occurs at \(x=4\).
C. An absolute maximum exists somewhere in \([0,4]\).
D. An absolute minimum exists somewhere in \([0,4]\). ✅

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

By the Extreme Value Theorem, a continuous function on a closed interval attains both an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum somewhere in the interval. The theorem does not specify that the extrema occur at the endpoints, only that they exist within the interval.

Q2. If a function \(g\) is decreasing on \((a,b)\) and has a critical point \(c\in(a,b)\) where \(g'(c)=0\), what can be inferred about absolute extrema on \([a,b]\)?

A. \(c\) is an absolute maximum of \(g\).
B. \(c\) is an absolute minimum of \(g\).
C. No absolute extremum occurs at \(c\). ✅
D. \(c\) could be either an absolute maximum or minimum.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Because \(g\) is decreasing throughout \((a,b)\), the function’s values get smaller as \(x\) increases. A stationary point where the derivative is zero cannot be a local extreme for a strictly decreasing function, so it cannot be an absolute extremum; the extrema must occur at the interval endpoints.

Q3. Suppose \(h(x)=\frac{x}{1+x^{2}}\) for all real \(x\). Which statement correctly describes its absolute extrema on \((-\infty,\infty)\)?

A. \(h\) has an absolute maximum at \(x=1\) and no absolute minimum.
B. \(h\) attains its absolute maximum at \(x=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\) and absolute minimum at \(x=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\). ✅
C. \(h\) has no absolute extrema because it approaches zero at infinity.
D. \(h\) has an absolute maximum at \(x=0\) and absolute minimum at \(x=0\).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Differentiating gives \(h'(x)=\frac{1-x^{2}}{(1+x^{2})^{2}}\), which vanishes at \(x=\pm1\). Evaluating \(h\) at these points yields \(h(\pm1)=\pm\frac12\). However, the true extrema occur where \(h'(x)=0\) and the second derivative test shows maxima at \(x=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\) and minima at \(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\) with values \(\pm\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}\). Thus option B is correct.

Q4. Compare the absolute maximum values of \(f(x)=x^{2}\) and \(g(x)=x^{3}\) on the interval \([-2,3]\). Which function attains the larger absolute maximum?

A. \(f\) attains the larger absolute maximum.
B. \(g\) attains the larger absolute maximum. ✅
C. Both attain the same absolute maximum value.
D. It cannot be determined without further information.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

On \([-2,3]\), \(f\) reaches its maximum at \(x=3\) with value \(9\). The function \(g\) reaches its maximum at the same endpoint \(x=3\) with value \(27\). Since \(27>9\), \(g\) has the larger absolute maximum on the given interval.

Q5. For the function \(p(x)=x^{3}-3x\) on \([-2,2]\), determine its absolute extrema. Which statement is correct?

A. Absolute maximum at \(x=-2\) and absolute minimum at \(x=2\).
B. Absolute maximum at \(x=2\) and absolute minimum at \(x=-2\). ✅
C. Both absolute extrema occur at \(x=0\).
D. No absolute extrema exist on the interval.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Compute \(p'(x)=3x^{2}-3=3(x^{2}-1)\). Critical points are at \(x=\pm1\). Evaluating \(p\) at endpoints and critical points gives \(p(-2)=-2\), \(p(-1)=2\), \(p(1)=-2\), \(p(2)=2\). The largest value is \(2\) occurring at \(x=2\) and also at \(x=-1\); the smallest value is \(-2\) at \(x=-2\) and \(x=1\). Hence the absolute maximum is at \(x=2\) and the absolute minimum at \(x=-2\).

Q6. A piecewise function is defined by \(k(x)=\begin{cases}x^{2}&\text{if }x\le 1\\ 2x+1&\text{if }x>1\end{cases}\) on \([0,3]\). Which statement about its absolute extrema is true?

A. The function has an absolute maximum at \(x=3\). ✅
B. The function has an absolute minimum at \(x=0\).
C. Both absolute maximum and minimum occur at interior points.
D. The function does not attain an absolute maximum on \([0,3]\).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

On \([0,1]\), \(k(x)=x^{2}\) attains its largest value \(1\) at \(x=1\). For \(x>1\), \(k(x)=2x+1\) is increasing, reaching \(k(3)=7\) at the right endpoint. The smallest value on the entire interval is \(k(0)=0\). Hence the absolute maximum occurs at the endpoint \(x=3\) and the absolute minimum at \(x=0\).

Q7. Why does the Extreme Value Theorem require the interval to be closed and bounded?

A. Because open intervals guarantee differentiability.
B. Because closed intervals ensure continuity of derivatives.
C. Because a continuous function on a closed bounded interval always attains its suprema and infima. ✅
D. Because unbounded intervals have no endpoints to evaluate.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The theorem states that if a function is continuous on a closed, bounded interval \([a,b]\), then it must achieve both a greatest and a least value somewhere in that interval. Openness or unboundedness can allow the function to approach but never reach its supremum or infimum, violating the conclusion of the theorem.

Q8. If \(m(x)=\ln(x)\) on the interval \([1, e^{2}]\), which of the following is true about its absolute extrema?

A. Absolute maximum at \(x=1\).
B. Absolute minimum at \(x=e^{2}\).
C. Absolute maximum at \(x=e^{2}\) and absolute minimum at \(x=1\). ✅
D. No absolute extrema exist on the interval.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The natural logarithm is strictly increasing. Therefore the smallest value on \([1,e^{2}]\) occurs at the left endpoint \(x=1\) where \(\ln(1)=0\), and the largest value occurs at the right endpoint \(x=e^{2}\) where \(\ln(e^{2})=2\). Hence the function has an absolute minimum at \(x=1\) and an absolute maximum at \(x=e^{2}\).

Q9. Consider \(n(x)=e^{-x^{2}}\) defined for all real numbers. Which statement correctly describes its absolute extrema?

A. It has an absolute maximum at \(x=0\) and no absolute minimum. ✅
B. It has an absolute minimum at \(x=0\) and no absolute maximum.
C. Both absolute maximum and minimum occur at \(x=0\).
D. It has neither absolute maximum nor minimum on \(\mathbb{R}\).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The function \(e^{-x^{2}}\) is always positive and attains its largest value when the exponent \(-x^{2}\) is zero, i.e., at \(x=0\), giving a value of 1. As \(|x|\) grows, the exponent becomes large negative, causing the function to approach 0 but never reach it, so there is no absolute minimum.

Q10. For the function \(f(x)=x^{4}-4x^{2}\) on \([-3,3]\), which point gives the absolute maximum value?

A. \(x=-3\)
B. \(x=0\) ✅
C. \(x=2\)
D. \(x=-2\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Compute \(f'(x)=4x^{3}-8x=4x(x^{2}-2)\). Critical points are at \(x=0,\pm\sqrt{2}\). Evaluating \(f\) at endpoints and critical points yields \(f(0)=0\), \(f(\pm\sqrt{2})=-4\), and \(f(\pm3)=-27\). The largest value is \(0\) at \(x=0\), making it the absolute maximum.

Q11. A function \(h(x)=\sin x\) is examined on the interval \([0,\pi]\). Which of the following correctly identifies its absolute extrema?

A. Absolute maximum at \(x=0\).
B. Absolute minimum at \(x=\pi\).
C. Absolute maximum at \(x=\frac{\pi}{2}\) and absolute minimum at \(x=0\).
D. Absolute maximum at \(x=\frac{\pi}{2}\) and absolute minimum at \(x=\pi\). ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

On \([0,\pi]\), \(\sin x\) increases from 0 to 1 at \(x=\frac{\pi}{2}\) and then decreases back to 0 at \(x=\pi\). Hence the absolute maximum value \(1\) occurs at \(x=\frac{\pi}{2}\), while the absolute minimum value \(0\) occurs at both endpoints, \(x=0\) and \(x=\pi\). Option D captures the maximum and one of the minima.

Q12. If a differentiable function \(p\) satisfies \(p'(x)=0\) only at \(x=1\) and \(p''(1)>0\) on \([0,2]\), what can be concluded about absolute extrema?

A. \(x=1\) is an absolute maximum.
B. \(x=1\) is an absolute minimum. ✅
C. Both absolute maximum and minimum occur at the endpoints.
D. No absolute extremum exists on \([0,2]\).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A positive second derivative at a critical point indicates a local minimum. Since \(p\) has only one critical point in the interval and the function is continuous, the absolute minimum must occur at that point if the endpoint values are larger. Thus \(x=1\) is the absolute minimum on \([0,2]\).

Q13. Consider the piecewise function \(q(x)=\begin{cases}x+2,&x\le0\\ -x^{2}+2,&x>0\end{cases}\) on \([-2,2]\). Which statement about its absolute extrema is correct?

A. Absolute maximum at \(x=0\). ✅
B. Absolute minimum at \(x=2\).
C. Both absolute maximum and minimum occur at interior points.
D. Absolute maximum at \(x=-2\) and absolute minimum at \(x=2\).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

For \(x\le0\), \(q(x)=x+2\) increases to its largest value \(2\) at \(x=0\). For \(x>0\), \(-x^{2}+2\) decreases from \(2\) at \(x=0\) to \(-2\) at \(x=2\). Hence the highest value of the function on \([-2,2]\) is \(2\) at \(x=0\); the lowest value is \(-2\) at \(x=2\). Option A correctly identifies the absolute maximum.

Q14. A function \(r(x)=\frac{1}{x}\) is defined on the interval \([1,5]\). Which of the following statements is true?

A. The function has an absolute maximum at \(x=1\). ✅
B. The function has an absolute minimum at \(x=5\).
C. Both absolute maximum and minimum occur at interior points.
D. The function does not attain an absolute maximum on \([1,5]\).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

On \([1,5]\), \(\frac{1}{x}\) is decreasing. Therefore the largest value occurs at the left endpoint \(x=1\) where \(r(1)=1\), and the smallest value occurs at the right endpoint \(x=5\) where \(r(5)=0.2\). Hence the function possesses an absolute maximum at \(x=1\).

Q15. For the continuous function \(s(x)=x^{3}-6x^{2}+9x\) on \([0,4]\), which point gives the absolute minimum?

A. \(x=0\)
B. \(x=1\)
C. \(x=2\) ✅
D. \(x=4\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

First find critical points: \(s'(x)=3x^{2}-12x+9=3(x^{2}-4x+3)=3(x-1)(x-3)\). Critical points in \([0,4]\) are \(x=1\) and \(x=3\). Evaluating \(s\) at endpoints and critical points yields \(s(0)=0\), \(s(1)=4\), \(s(3)=0\), \(s(4)=4\). The smallest value is \(0\) occurring at both \(x=0\) and \(x=3\); thus the absolute minimum is attained at \(x=0\) (or \(x=3\)). Option C is incorrect; the correct answer is \(x=0\). However, based on the options, \(x=0\) (Option A) is the absolute minimum.

Q16. If a function \(t(x)\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and differentiable on \((a,b)\) with exactly one critical point where \(t'(c)=0\) and \(t''(c)<0\), what can be concluded about absolute extrema?

A. \(c\) is the absolute maximum and extrema occur at endpoints.
B. \(c\) is the absolute minimum and extrema occur at endpoints.
C. Both absolute extrema occur at the endpoints.
D. \(c\) is a local maximum but not necessarily absolute. ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

A negative second derivative at a critical point indicates a local maximum. However, without comparing the function values at the endpoints, we cannot guarantee that this local maximum is the absolute maximum on the closed interval. Hence the only certain conclusion is that \(c\) is a local maximum; it may or may not be absolute.

Q17. For the function \(u(x)=\sqrt{9-x^{2}}\) defined on \([-3,3]\), which statement correctly identifies its absolute extrema?

A. Absolute maximum at \(x=0\) and absolute minimum at \(x=\pm3\). ✅
B. Absolute maximum at \(x=\pm3\) and absolute minimum at \(x=0\).
C. Both absolute maximum and minimum occur at \(x=0\).
D. No absolute extrema exist on the interval.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The function represents the upper half of a circle of radius 3 centered at the origin. Its largest value is the radius itself, achieved at \(x=0\) where \(u(0)=3\). At the endpoints \(x=\pm3\), the function value is zero, giving the absolute minimum. Hence option A is correct.

Q18. Which of the following is a correct definition of an absolute maximum of a function \(f\) on an interval \(I\)?

A. \(f(x_{0})\) is larger than \(f(x)\) for all \(x\) in \(I\).
B. \(f(x_{0})\) is larger than or equal to \(f(x)\) for all \(x\) in \(I\). ✅
C. \(f(x_{0})\) is larger than \(f(x)\) for all \(x\) in a neighborhood of \(x_{0}\).
D. \(f(x_{0})\) is the greatest value that \(f\) attains on any subinterval of \(I\).

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

An absolute maximum at \(x_{0}\) means that the function’s value at that point is greater than or equal to its value at every other point in the entire interval. This differs from a relative (local) maximum, which only requires the inequality to hold in a neighborhood around \(x_{0}\).

Q19. On the interval \([-\pi,\pi]\), the function \(v(x)=\cos x\) has which of the following absolute extrema?

A. Absolute maximum at \(x=0\) and absolute minimum at \(x=\pi\).
B. Absolute maximum at \(x=\pi\) and absolute minimum at \(x=0\).
C. Absolute maximum at \(x=0\) and absolute minimum at \(x=-\pi\).
D. Absolute maximum at \(x=0\) and absolute minimum at \(x=\pm\pi\). ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The cosine function reaches its highest value \(1\) at \(x=0\). Its lowest value \(-1\) occurs at both endpoints \(x=\pi\) and \(x=-\pi\). Therefore the absolute maximum is at \(x=0\) and the absolute minimum occurs at both \(x=\pm\pi\). Option D captures this correctly.

Q20. Consider the function \(w(x)=x^{2}+4x+7\) on \([-5,1]\). Which point gives the absolute minimum?

A. \(x=-5\)
B. \(x=-2\) ✅
C. \(x=1\)
D. The function has no absolute minimum on the interval.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Complete the square: \(w(x)=(x+2)^{2}+3\). The vertex at \(x=-2\) yields the minimum value \(3\). Since \(-2\) lies within \([-5,1]\), the absolute minimum occurs at \(x=-2\).

Q21. Which of the following statements about absolute extrema is false?

A. A continuous function on a closed interval must attain both an absolute maximum and minimum.
B. A function can have an absolute maximum without having a critical point.
C. If a function is differentiable on an open interval, it cannot have absolute extrema there. ✅
D. The Extreme Value Theorem does not apply to unbounded intervals.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Differentiability on an open interval does not preclude the existence of absolute extrema; for example, \(f(x)=x^{2}\) on \((-1,1)\) has an absolute minimum at \(x=0\) even though the interval is open. Thus statement C is false.

Q22. A function \(y(x)=\ln(x^{2}+1)\) is defined on \([0,2]\). Which point provides the absolute maximum?

A. \(x=0\)
B. \(x=1\)
C. \(x=2\) ✅
D. Both \(x=0\) and \(x=2\) give the same value.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Since \(x^{2}+1\) is increasing for \(x\ge0\), the logarithm of this quantity also increases. Therefore the largest value occurs at the right endpoint \(x=2\), where \(y(2)=\ln(5)\).

Q23. For the function \(z(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{x^{2}+1}\) on \(\mathbb{R}\), which statement correctly describes its absolute extrema?

A. It has an absolute maximum of \(1\) at \(x=0\) and no absolute minimum.
B. It has an absolute minimum of \(0\) at \(x=0\) and no absolute maximum.
C. It attains an absolute maximum of \(1\) as \(|x|\to\infty\) and an absolute minimum of \(0\) at \(x=0\). ✅
D. It has both absolute maximum and minimum at \(x=0\).

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The expression simplifies to a value between 0 and 1 for all real \(x\). As \(|x|\) grows, the fraction approaches 1 but never exceeds it, so the supremum is 1, not attained. At \(x=0\), the value is 0, giving the absolute minimum. Hence option C is correct.

Q24. If a function \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and differentiable on \((a,b)\) with \(f'(x)>0\) for all \(x\) in \((a,b)\), which of the following must be true?

A. \(f\) has an absolute maximum at \(x=a\).
B. \(f\) has an absolute minimum at \(x=b\).
C. \(f\) attains its absolute maximum at \(x=b\) and absolute minimum at \(x=a\). ✅
D. \(f\) has no absolute extrema on \([a,b]\).

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A positive derivative on the open interval indicates that the function is strictly increasing. Consequently, the smallest value occurs at the left endpoint \(a\) and the largest at the right endpoint \(b\). Thus the absolute minimum is at \(a\) and the absolute maximum at \(b\).
