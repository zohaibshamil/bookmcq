What is Multiplicity and graph behavior?

Definition:
Multiplicity refers to the exponent of a factor in a polynomial's factored form. Even multiplicity causes the graph to touch the x-axis and turn around, while odd multiplicity causes the graph to cross the x-axis, influencing the smoothness and shape near roots.

Example:
For $f(x) = (x-1)^2(x+2)$, the root $x=1$ has multiplicity 2 (touches axis), and $x=-2$ has multiplicity 1 (crosses axis).

Reason:
Understanding multiplicity allows precise prediction of graph behavior at intercepts, distinguishing between crossing points and tangent points without plotting every coordinate.

Q1. If a polynomial has a root of even multiplicity at \(x=r\), which statement about the graph near \(x=r\) is always true?

A.The graph crosses the x‑axis and is tangent at \(x=r\).
B.The graph crosses the x‑axis without being tangent.
C.The graph does not cross the x‑axis but has an inflection point at \(x=r\).
D.The graph does not cross the x‑axis and is tangent to it at \(x=r\). ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: Because an even multiplicity forces the factor \((x-r)^m\) to be squared, the sign of the polynomial does not change on either side of the root. Consequently the curve touches the axis (tangent) but never passes through it, and no inflection occurs since concavity remains the same.

Q2. What does it mean for a root \(x=r\) of a polynomial \(p(x)\) to have multiplicity \(m\)?

A.\((x-r)^m\) divides \(p(x)\) and \((x-r)^{m+1}\) also divides \(p(x)\).
B.\((x-r)^m\) divides \(p(x)\) but \((x-r)^{m+1}\) does not. ✅
C.The root occurs \(m\) times in the factorization of the derivative.
D.The root is repeated \(m\) times in the graph.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Multiplicity \(m\) indicates that the factor \((x-r)^m\) appears in the factorization of the polynomial, while the next higher power \((x-r)^{m+1}\) is absent. This definition captures exactly how many times the factor is repeated, distinguishing simple roots (\(m=1\)) from higher‑order repetitions.

Q3. For the polynomial \(p(x)= (x-1)^2 (x+3)\), which of the following correctly describes the behavior at each real root?

A.At \(x=1\) the graph is tangent and does not cross; at \(x=-3\) the graph crosses without being tangent. ✅
B.At \(x=1\) the graph crosses with an inflection; at \(x=-3\) the graph is tangent and does not cross.
C.At both roots the graph is tangent and does not cross.
D.At both roots the graph crosses without tangency.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The factor \((x-1)^2\) gives an even multiplicity, so the curve touches the axis and bounces off, i.e., tangent without crossing. The factor \((x+3)\) is simple, giving a crossing that is not tangent. Thus the first description matches the theorem for even and odd simple roots.

Q4. Compare the local shape of the graph near a root of multiplicity 2 versus a root of multiplicity 3. Which statement is accurate?

A.Both roots produce a crossing of the x‑axis, but only multiplicity 3 gives a flat point.
B.Multiplicity 2 yields a bounce (no crossing) while multiplicity 3 yields a crossing with an inflection. ✅
C.Multiplicity 2 gives an inflection point, multiplicity 3 gives a local maximum.
D.Both produce tangency, but only multiplicity 3 changes concavity.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An even multiplicity such as 2 forces the graph to touch and bounce off the axis, so no crossing occurs. An odd multiplicity greater than 1, like 3, forces the curve to cross the axis while also being tangent, and the change in concavity creates an inflection point.

Q5. If the graph of a polynomial is tangent to the x‑axis at a root and also crosses the axis there, what can be deduced about the multiplicity of that root?

A.It must be even.
B.It must be odd and equal to 1.
C.It must be odd and greater than 1. ✅
D.It could be any integer greater than 0.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A root that is both tangent (the curve just touches) and crossing (the sign changes) can only occur when the multiplicity is odd and exceeds 1. Even multiplicities never allow crossing, while a simple odd root (\(m=1\)) is not tangent. Hence the multiplicity must be odd and at least 3.

Q6. Which of the following polynomials has a root at \(x=0\) that is simple, a root at \(x=2\) with even multiplicity, and a root at \(x=-1\) with odd multiplicity greater than 1?

A.\(x(x-2)^2(x+1)^3\) ✅

C.\(x(x-2)^4(x+1)\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The factor \(x\) supplies a simple root at 0. The factor \((x-2)^2\) gives an even multiplicity (2) at 2. The factor \((x+1)^3\) provides an odd multiplicity greater than 1 (3) at \(-1\). All conditions are satisfied only by the first choice.

Q8. A root of multiplicity 1 is also called a __________.

B.simple root ✅

D.double root
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: By definition, when a factor \((x-r)\) appears only once in the factorization of a polynomial, the root is said to be simple. This distinguishes it from roots that appear multiple times, which are described as repeated or multiple roots.

Q9. Why does an odd multiplicity greater than 1 guarantee an inflection point at the root?

A.Because the derivative changes sign while the function does not.
B.Because the second derivative is zero and changes sign, indicating a change in concavity. ✅
C.Because the graph is symmetric about the root.
D.Because the root causes the polynomial to be linear near that point.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: When the multiplicity is odd and exceeds 1, the factor \((x-r)^m\) forces the first derivative to be zero at the root, while the second derivative also vanishes but switches sign on either side. This sign change of the second derivative means the concavity changes, which is precisely the definition of an inflection point.

Q10. At a root where the graph is tangent to the x‑axis and also has an inflection point, what can be said about the signs of the first and second derivatives on either side of the root?

A.First derivative changes sign, second derivative remains positive.
B.First derivative does not change sign, second derivative changes sign. ✅
C.Both first and second derivatives change sign.
D.Neither derivative changes sign.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a tangent point the slope (first derivative) is zero, but because the graph does not change direction, the sign of the first derivative stays the same on both sides. The presence of an inflection means the second derivative switches sign, reflecting a change in concavity while the slope remains non‑changing.

Q11. How does the local behavior of \(y=x^2\) near \(x=0\) differ from that of \(y=x^3\) near \(x=0\)?

A.\(x^2\) crosses the axis while \(x^3\) does not.
B.\(x^2\) is tangent and does not cross; \(x^3\) crosses and has an inflection. ✅
C.Both are tangent, but only \(x^3\) has a maximum.
D.Both cross the axis, but only \(x^2\) has an inflection.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The factor \(x^2\) gives an even multiplicity, so the parabola touches the axis and bounces off, never crossing. The factor \(x^3\) gives an odd multiplicity greater than 1, causing the cubic to cross the axis while also being tangent at the origin, producing an inflection point.

Q12. For the polynomial \(p(x)= (x+1)^5 (x-2)^2\), which of the following correctly describes the graph at each real root?

A.At \(x=-1\) the graph is tangent, crosses, and has an inflection; at \(x=2\) the graph is tangent and does not cross. ✅
B.At \(x=-1\) the graph crosses without tangency; at \(x=2\) the graph is tangent and has an inflection.
C.Both roots produce crossing without tangency.
D.Both roots are tangent without crossing.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The factor \((x+1)^5\) gives an odd multiplicity greater than 1, so the curve is tangent, crosses, and has an inflection at \(-1\). The factor \((x-2)^2\) is even, yielding a bounce (tangency) with no crossing and no inflection at \(2\). Hence the first description is correct.

C.Negative on the left, positive on the right.

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An even multiplicity means the factor \((x-3)^4\) is always non‑negative, so the sign of the polynomial does not change when passing through the root. With a positive leading coefficient, the overall sign remains positive on both sides of the root.
