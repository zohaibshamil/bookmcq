What is Analysis of Functions II (Relative Extrema) in calculus?

Definition:
Relative extrema are local maximums or minimums where f(c)f(c)f(c) is greater or less than nearby values. These occur at critical points where f′(c)=0f'(c) = 0f′(c)=0 or f′(c)f'(c)f′(c) is undefined, marking peaks and valleys in the function's graph structure.

Example:
For f(x)=x2−4xf(x) = x^2 - 4xf(x)=x2−4x, f′(x)=2x−4f'(x) = 2x - 4f′(x)=2x−4. Setting 2x−4=02x - 4 = 02x−4=0 gives x=2x=2x=2, a relative minimum since the parabola opens upward.

Reason:
Identifying these points helps sketch accurate graphs and understand optimal values within specific local neighborhoods of the domain.
