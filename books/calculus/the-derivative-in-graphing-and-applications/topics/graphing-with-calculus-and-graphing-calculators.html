Q1. What is the derivative \(f'(x)\) of the function \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\) for \(x>0\)?

🔹 A. \( \frac{1}{x^{2}} \)
🔹 B. \( \frac{1-\ln x}{x^{2}} \)
🔹 C. \( \frac{\ln x-1}{x^{2}} \)
🔹 D. \( -\frac{1-\ln x}{x^{2}} \)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Using the quotient rule, \(f'(x)=\frac{x\cdot\frac{1}{x}-\ln x\cdot1}{x^{2}}=\frac{1-\ln x}{x^{2}}\). This matches option B, and the other choices arise from sign or term errors that are easily spotted by careful differentiation.

Q2. As \(x\to\infty\), what horizontal line does the graph of \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\) approach?

🔹 A. \(y=1\)
🔹 B. \(y=\ln x\)
🔹 C. \(y=0\)
🔹 D. \(y=\infty\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Applying L'Hôpital’s rule gives \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\ln x}{x}= \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1/x}{1}=0\). Thus the horizontal asymptote is the line \(y=0\). The correct answer is option D; the other alternatives confuse the limit with the numerator or suggest unbounded growth.

Q3. If a graph shows a relative maximum near \(x=2.7\), which sign pattern for \(f'(x)\) correctly describes the behavior around that point?

🔹 A. Positive before, positive after
🔹 B. Negative before, negative after
🔹 C. Positive before, negative after
🔹 D. Negative before, positive after

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A relative maximum occurs where the derivative changes from positive (function increasing) to negative (function decreasing). Therefore the sign pattern is positive before the point and negative after, which corresponds to option C.

Q4. Which limit confirms the vertical asymptote of \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\) at \(x=0^{+}\)?

🔹 A. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^{+}}\frac{\ln x}{x}=0\)
🔹 B. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^{+}}\frac{\ln x}{x}=+\infty\)
🔹 C. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^{+}}\frac{\ln x}{x}=-\infty\)
🔹 D. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^{+}}\frac{\ln x}{x}=1\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

As \(x\) approaches zero from the right, \(\ln x\) tends to \(-\infty\) while \(x\) tends to \(0^{+}\), making the quotient \(-\infty\). Hence the limit is \(-\infty\), indicating a vertical asymptote; option A reflects this correctly.

Q5. For \(0<x<1\) and \(x>1\), how does the sign of \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\) change?

🔹 A. Negative on both intervals
🔹 B. Positive on both intervals
🔹 C. Negative on \((0,1)\) and positive on \((1,\infty)\)
🔹 D. Positive on \((0,1)\) and negative on \((1,\infty)\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Since \(\ln x<0\) when \(0<x<1\) and \(\ln x>0\) when \(x>1\), dividing by the positive \(x\) preserves the sign. Thus \(f(x)\) is negative on \((0,1)\) and positive on \((1,\infty)\), matching option C.

Q6. Which calculus test determines that the stationary point at \(x=e\) for \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\) is a relative maximum?

🔹 A. First‑derivative test
🔹 B. Second‑derivative test
🔹 C. Limit test
🔹 D. Concavity test

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Evaluating the second derivative at \(x=e\) yields \(f''(e)=\frac{2-3e}{e^{3}}<0\), confirming concave down and thus a relative maximum. This is the second‑derivative test, making option D the correct choice.

Q7. When \(f''(x)\) changes sign at \(x=e^{3/2}\), what feature does the graph exhibit there?

🔹 A. Local maximum
🔹 B. Local minimum
🔹 C. Point of inflection
🔹 D. Vertical asymptote

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

A sign change in the second derivative indicates a transition from concave down to concave up or vice‑versa, which defines an inflection point. Therefore the graph has a point of inflection at \(x=e^{3/2}\), corresponding to option C.

Q8. Solve \(f'(x)=0\) for \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\) and classify the stationary point using \(f''(x)\).

🔹 A. \(x=1\), relative minimum
🔹 B. \(x=e\), relative maximum
🔹 C. \(x=e^{3/2}\), inflection point
🔹 D. No stationary points

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Setting \(\frac{1-\ln x}{x^{2}}=0\) gives \(\ln x=1\) so \(x=e\). Evaluating \(f''(e)=\frac{2-3e}{e^{3}}<0\) shows concave down, confirming a relative maximum. Hence option B is correct.

Q9. Using L’Hôpital’s rule, evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^{+}}\frac{\ln x}{x}\).

🔹 A. \(0\)
🔹 B. \(-\infty\)
🔹 C. \(+\infty\)
🔹 D. Does not exist

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Rewrite the limit as \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^{+}}\frac{1/x}{1}\) after differentiating numerator and denominator. The numerator \(1/x\) diverges to \(+\infty\) while the denominator stays \(1\), giving \(-\infty\) because \(\ln x\) is negative; thus option B is correct.

Q10. Which calculus argument justifies the horizontal asymptote \(y=0\) for \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\)?

🔹 A. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\ln x}{x}=0\)
🔹 B. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^{+}}\frac{\ln x}{x}=0\)
🔹 C. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{x}{\ln x}=0\)
🔹 D. \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\ln x=0\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Applying L’Hôpital’s rule to \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\ln x}{x}\) yields \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1/x}{1}=0\). This limit confirms that the function approaches the line \(y=0\) as \(x\) grows, establishing the horizontal asymptote; option A captures this reasoning.

Q11. On which interval is \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\) increasing?

🔹 A. \((0,e)\)
🔹 B. \((e,\infty)\)
🔹 C. \((0,1)\)
🔹 D. \((1,e^{3/2})\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The sign of \(f'(x)=\frac{1-\ln x}{x^{2}}\) is positive when \(1-\ln x>0\), i.e., \(\ln x<1\) or \(x<e\). Since the denominator is always positive, the function increases on \((0,e)\); option A reflects this interval.

Q12. For \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\), where is the graph concave down?

🔹 A. \((0,e^{3/2})\)
🔹 B. \((e^{3/2},\infty)\)
🔹 C. \((0,1)\)
🔹 D. \((1,e)\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The second derivative \(f''(x)=\frac{2\ln x-3}{x^{3}}\) is negative when \(2\ln x-3<0\), i.e., \(\ln x<\frac{3}{2}\) or \(x<e^{3/2}\). Hence the graph is concave down on \((0,e^{3/2})\); option C is correct.

Q13. Which equation explains the x‑intercept of \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\) at \(x=1\)?

🔹 A. \(\ln x = 1\)
🔹 B. \(\ln x = 0\)
🔹 C. \(x=0\)
🔹 D. \(\ln x = -1\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Setting \(f(x)=0\) gives \(\frac{\ln x}{x}=0\) which implies \(\ln x=0\). Solving yields \(x=e^{0}=1\). Therefore the intercept corresponds to the equation \(\ln x=0\); option B is the correct representation.

Q14. Given \(h(x)=3(x+1)(x-3)(x+2)(x-4)\) and \(h'(x)=-30(x-1)(x+2)^{2}(x-4)^{2}\), how many relative extrema does \(h\) have?

🔹 A. One
🔹 B. Two
🔹 C. Three
🔹 D. Four

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Critical points occur where \(h'(x)=0\): at \(x=1,-2,4\). The factor \((x+2)^{2}\) and \((x-4)^{2}\) indicate even multiplicity, giving no sign change, while \(x=1\) (odd multiplicity) changes sign, producing a single relative extremum. Thus there is one relative extremum, option D (the fourth choice) is correct.

Q15. Using the sign of \(h'(x)\), what is the behavior of \(h(x)\) just to the left of \(x=1\)?

🔹 A. Increasing
🔹 B. Decreasing
🔹 C. Undefined
🔹 D. Constant

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

For \(x<1\) but close, the factor \((x-1)\) is negative while the squared factors are positive, making \(h'(x)>0\). A positive derivative indicates the function is increasing just left of \(x=1\); option A captures this.

Q16. From \(f''(x)=\frac{2\ln x-3}{x^{3}}\), solve for the x‑coordinate of the inflection point.

🔹 A. \(x=e^{3/2}\)
🔹 B. \(x=e\)
🔹 C. \(x=1\)
🔹 D. \(x=0\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Setting \(f''(x)=0\) gives \(2\ln x-3=0\) → \(\ln x=3/2\) → \(x=e^{3/2}\). Therefore the inflection point occurs at \(x=e^{3/2}\); option C is the correct answer.

Q17. What is \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\ln x}{x}\) and how does it relate to the horizontal asymptote?

🔹 A. \(0\); confirms \(y=0\)
🔹 B. \(1\); confirms \(y=1\)
🔹 C. \(\infty\); no asymptote
🔹 D. Does not exist; no asymptote

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Applying L’Hôpital’s rule yields \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{1/x}{1}=0\). The limit being zero shows the function approaches the line \(y=0\) as \(x\) grows, establishing a horizontal asymptote at \(y=0\); option D states this relationship correctly.

Q18. Why might a graphing utility miss the vertical asymptote at \(x=0\) for \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\)?

🔹 A. Because the program cannot compute limits
🔹 B. Because the domain is truncated near zero
🔹 C. Because the asymptote is hidden by scaling
🔹 D. Because the function is undefined at \(x=0\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Many utilities start plotting from a small positive value (e.g., \(0.1\)) to avoid division by zero, so the dramatic blow‑up near \(x=0\) is not displayed. This truncation can hide the vertical asymptote, making option B the accurate description.

Q19. Find the exact coordinates of the relative maximum of \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\).

🔹 A. \((e,\frac{1}{e})\)
🔹 B. \((e,\frac{1-\ln e}{e^{2}})\)
🔹 C. \((e,\frac{1}{e^{2}})\)
🔹 D. \((e,\frac{1}{e^{2}})\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The stationary point occurs at \(x=e\). Substituting gives \(f(e)=\frac{\ln e}{e}=\frac{1}{e}\). Hence the maximum point is \((e,\frac{1}{e})\); option A provides the correct coordinate pair.

Q20. A graph on \([0.1,10]\) shows no inflection point for \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\). Using \(f''(x)\), prove an inflection point exists and locate it.

🔹 A. \(x=e^{3/2}\)
🔹 B. \(x=e\)
🔹 C. \(x=1\)
🔹 D. None exists

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Since \(f''(x)=\frac{2\ln x-3}{x^{3}}\), the sign changes when \(2\ln x-3=0\) → \(x=e^{3/2}\). This value lies within the plotted interval, guaranteeing an inflection point at \(x=e^{3/2}\); option C correctly identifies it.

Q21. Compare the end behavior of \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\) as \(x\to0^{+}\) and as \(x\to\infty\). Which statement best describes the influence on the graph’s shape?

🔹 A. Both limits are \(-\infty\), causing a single branch that descends indefinitely
🔹 B. The left limit is \(-\infty\) (vertical asymptote) and the right limit is \(0\) (horizontal asymptote), producing a curve that rises from \(-\infty\) and levels off
🔹 C. Both limits are \(0\), yielding a flat line
🔹 D. The function oscillates between positive and negative values at both ends

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

As \(x\to0^{+}\), \(\ln x\to-\infty\) while \(x\to0^{+}\), so the quotient tends to \(-\infty\), giving a vertical asymptote. As \(x\to\infty\), the quotient tends to \(0\), giving a horizontal asymptote. This combination creates a graph that climbs from negative infinity near the y‑axis and approaches the x‑axis far to the right; option D captures this description.

Q22. For \(h(x)=3(x+1)(x-3)(x+2)(x-4)\) with \(h''(x)=90(x^{2}-2x+4)(x+2)^{3}(x-4)^{3}\), determine the intervals of concavity and any inflection points.

🔹 A. Concave up on \((-2,4)\); inflection at \(x= -2,4\)
🔹 B. Concave down on \((-2,4)\); inflection at \(x= -2,4\)
🔹 C. Concave up everywhere except at \(x=-2,4\) where concavity changes
🔹 D. Concave down everywhere; no inflection points

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The factor \((x^{2}-2x+4)\) is always positive, while \((x+2)^{3}\) and \((x-4)^{3}\) change sign at \(-2\) and \(4\). Thus \(h''(x)\) changes sign at those points, indicating inflection points there, and the function is concave up on intervals where the product is positive, i.e., outside \((-2,4)\); option C reflects this.

Q23. A graphing utility suggests a maximum near \(x\approx2.7\) for \(f(x)=\frac{\ln x}{x}\). Calculus yields the exact maximum at \(x=e\approx2.718\). Why might the utility display a slightly different location, and how does calculus resolve the discrepancy?

🔹 A. The utility uses a coarse grid; calculus provides the exact analytical solution
🔹 B. The utility miscalculates derivatives; calculus corrects the error
🔹 C. The function has multiple maxima; calculus selects the largest
🔹 D. The graph is distorted by scaling; calculus ignores scaling

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Graphing programs sample points at discrete intervals; if the step size is larger than the distance between \(2.7\) and \(e\), the plotted peak appears at the nearest sampled point, giving an approximate location. Analytic calculus determines the exact stationary point by solving \(f'(x)=0\), yielding \(x=e\); option A explains this discrepancy.
