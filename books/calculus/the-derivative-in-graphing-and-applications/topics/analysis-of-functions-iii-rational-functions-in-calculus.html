Q1. What is the first derivative of the function \(f(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}+1\)?

🔹 A. \(3x^{2}-6x\)
🔹 B. \(x^{2}-6x\)
🔹 C. \(3x^{2}+6x\)
🔹 D. \(3x^{2}-3x\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of a polynomial is obtained by applying the power rule to each term. Differentiating \(x^{3}\) gives \(3x^{2}\) and differentiating \(-3x^{2}\) gives \(-6x\); the constant term disappears. Hence the correct derivative is \(3x^{2}-6x\).

Q2. For the function \(f(x)=xe^{-x}\), what is the x‑coordinate of its inflection point?

🔹 A. 1
🔹 B. 2
🔹 C. 0
🔹 D. 3

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The second derivative of \(f\) is \(f''(x)=(x-2)e^{-x}\). Since the exponential factor is always positive, the sign of \(f''\) changes where the linear factor \(x-2\) equals zero, i.e., at \(x=2\). Therefore the inflection point occurs at \(x=2\).

Q3. If \(f'(x)\) is negative on \((0,2)\) and positive elsewhere, which interval must contain a local minimum of \(f\)?

🔹 A. \((-\infty,0)\)
🔹 B. \((0,2)\)
🔹 C. \((2,\infty)\)
🔹 D. None of the above

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A local minimum occurs where the derivative changes from negative to positive. Since \(f'\) is negative on \((0,2)\) and becomes positive after \(x=2\), the sign change happens at the right endpoint of the interval, placing the minimum within \((0,2)\).

Q4. Given that \(f''\) changes sign at \(x=1\) and \(f'(1)=0\), what type of point is \((1,f(1))\)?

🔹 A. Local maximum
🔹 B. Local minimum
🔹 C. Inflection point
🔹 D. Saddle point

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

When the second derivative changes sign, the curvature of the graph switches from concave down to concave up or vice‑versa. If the first derivative is also zero, the point is not an extremum but a point where concavity changes, i.e., an inflection point.

Q5. For \(g(x)=x+2\sin x\) on \([0,2\pi]\), a sign change of \(g'\) from positive to negative indicates which of the following?

🔹 A. A local maximum
🔹 B. A local minimum
🔹 C. A point of inflection
🔹 D. A horizontal asymptote

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

When the first derivative passes from positive to negative, the function transitions from increasing to decreasing, which characterizes a local maximum. This reasoning holds regardless of the function’s specific form, provided the derivative exists at the point.

Q6. Consider \(h(x)=\dfrac{x^{2}-1}{x-2}\). If \(h'\) is undefined at \(x=2\) but the limit exists, what can be concluded about monotonicity near \(x=2\)?

🔹 A. Increasing on both sides
🔹 B. Decreasing on both sides
🔹 C. Increasing left and decreasing right
🔹 D. Cannot be determined

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Writing \(h(x)=x+2+\dfrac{3}{x-2}\) shows \(h'(x)=1-\dfrac{3}{(x-2)^{2}}\). The term \(\dfrac{3}{(x-2)^{2}}\) is always positive, making \(h'(x)<0\) for all \(x\neq2\). Hence the function is decreasing on both sides of the hole at \(x=2\).

Q7. If a function’s first derivative is positive everywhere except at a single point where it is zero, what can be said about its monotonicity?

🔹 A. Strictly increasing
🔹 B. Increasing but not strictly
🔹 C. Constant
🔹 D. Decreasing

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A derivative that is never negative guarantees the function never decreases. The isolated zero does not create a decrease, so the function is monotone increasing; however, because the derivative is zero at one point, the increase is not strict over the entire domain.

Q8. For \(f(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}+1\) with \(f''(x)=6(x-1)\), on which intervals does the function have an increasing rate of increase?

🔹 A. \(x>1\)
🔹 B. \(x<1\)
🔹 C. None of the above
🔹 D. Both \(x<1\) and \(x>1\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The sign of the second derivative indicates whether the first derivative is increasing. Since \(f''(x)=6(x-1)\) is positive when \(x>1\), the slope \(f'(x)\) is increasing on that interval, meaning the function’s rate of increase grows for \(x>1\).

Q9. Given that \(f\) is decreasing on \([0,2]\) and concave down on \((-\infty,1)\), what is the shape of its graph on \((0,1)\)?

🔹 A. Decreasing and concave down
🔹 B. Decreasing and concave up
🔹 C. Increasing and concave down
🔹 D. Increasing and concave up

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

On \((0,1)\) the function inherits both properties that apply to the overlapping intervals: it is part of the decreasing region \([0,2]\) and also part of the concave‑down region \((-\infty,1)\). Hence the graph is decreasing and concave down there.

Q10. Compare the intervals of increase for \(f(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}+1\) and \(g(x)=xe^{-x}\). Which statement is true?

🔹 A. Both increase on \((-\infty,0]\)
🔹 B. \(f\) increases on two intervals while \(g\) increases on one
🔹 C. Both increase on \((-\infty,1]\)
🔹 D. \(f\) increases everywhere, \(g\) decreases everywhere

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The derivative of \(f\) is \(3x(x-2)\), giving increase on \((-\infty,0]\) and \((2,\infty)\). For \(g\), \(g'(x)=(1-x)e^{-x}\) is positive only when \(x<1\). Thus \(f\) has two separate increasing intervals, whereas \(g\) has a single interval of increase.

Q11. Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 2}f'(x)\) for \(f(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}+1\).

🔹 A. 0
🔹 B. 6
🔹 C. 12
🔹 D. Undefined

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The first derivative is \(f'(x)=3x(x-2)\). Substituting \(x=2\) gives \(f'(2)=3\cdot2\cdot0=0\). Hence the limit as \(x\) approaches 2 of the derivative exists and equals 0.

Q12. For \(h(x)=\dfrac{x^{2}-1}{x-2}\), at which x‑value does the concavity change?

🔹 A. 1
🔹 B. 2
🔹 C. 3
🔹 D. None

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

After simplifying, \(h(x)=x+2+\dfrac{3}{x-2}\). Differentiating twice yields \(h''(x)=\dfrac{6}{(x-2)^{3}}\), whose sign flips when the denominator changes sign, i.e., at the vertical asymptote \(x=2\). Thus concavity changes at \(x=2\).

Q13. Which function shares the same inflection‑point x‑coordinate as \(f(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}+1\)?

🔹 A. \(g(x)=x^{3}-3x+1\)
🔹 B. \(h(x)=xe^{-x}\)
🔹 C. \(k(x)=x+2\sin x\)
🔹 D. \(m(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}+5\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The inflection point of a cubic occurs where its second derivative is zero. For \(f\), \(f''(x)=6(x-1)\) gives \(x=1\). Function \(m(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}+5\) has the same second‑derivative expression, so its inflection point also occurs at \(x=1\).

Q14. Between \(p(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}+1\) and \(q(x)=x+2\sin x\) on \([0,2\pi]\), which has greater total variation?

🔹 A. \(p\)
🔹 B. \(q\)
🔹 C. Both are equal
🔹 D. Cannot be determined without computation

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Total variation measures the accumulated absolute change. The sinusoidal term in \(q\) causes repeated rises and falls, producing more cumulative change than the smooth cubic \(p\), whose graph only climbs and falls once. Hence \(q\) exhibits greater total variation on the interval.

Q15. Is \(f(x)=xe^{-x}\) concave up at \(x=0\)?

🔹 A. Yes
🔹 B. No
🔹 C. Undefined
🔹 D. Depends on the sign of the first derivative

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The second derivative is \(f''(x)=(x-2)e^{-x}\). At \(x=0\) this equals \((-2)e^{0}=-2\), which is negative, indicating concave down—not concave up—at the origin. Therefore the correct answer is No.

Q16. How many inflection points does \(f(x)=x^{3}-3x^{2}+1\) have on \(\mathbb{R}\) compared to \(g(x)=x+2\sin x\) on \([0,2\pi]\)?

🔹 A. \(f\) has 1, \(g\) has 2
🔹 B. \(f\) has 1, \(g\) has 1
🔹 C. \(f\) has 0, \(g\) has 2
🔹 D. \(f\) has 2, \(g\) has 2

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The cubic’s second derivative \(6(x-1)\) vanishes once, giving a single inflection point at \(x=1\). For \(g\), \(g''(x)=-2\sin x\) is zero at \(x=0,\pi,2\pi\); interior to the interval is \(\pi\). Counting interior points yields two inflection points for \(g\).

Q17. For \(r(x)=\dfrac{x^{3}-3x^{2}+1}{xe^{-x}}\), what is the sign of \(r'(1)\)?

🔹 A. Positive
🔹 B. Negative
🔹 C. Zero
🔹 D. Undefined

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Using the quotient rule, \(r'(x)=\dfrac{f'(x)g(x)-f(x)g'(x)}{g(x)^{2}}\). At \(x=1\): \(f'(1)=-3\), \(g(1)=e^{-1}\), \(f(1)=-1\), \(g'(1)=0\). Numerator \((-3)e^{-1}\) is negative, denominator positive, so \(r'(1)<0\).

Q18. Why does the sign of \(f''(x)\) determine concavity?

🔹 A. Because the second derivative measures curvature
🔹 B. Because the first derivative sign determines slope
🔹 C. Because the function value sign determines height
🔹 D. Because asymptotes dictate shape

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The second derivative quantifies how the first derivative changes. When \(f''(x)>0\), the slope is increasing, producing a “cup‑shaped” graph that is concave up. Conversely, \(f''(x)<0\) means the slope is decreasing, giving a “cap‑shaped” graph that is concave down. Hence sign of \(f''\) dictates concavity.

Q19. Synthesize the relationship between critical points and inflection points for cubic functions. Which statement is correct?

🔹 A. They always coincide
🔹 B. They are always distinct
🔹 C. A cubic can have at most one of each
🔹 D. A cubic has exactly one inflection point and may have up to two critical points

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

A general cubic \(ax^{3}+bx^{2}+cx+d\) has a second derivative \(6ax+2b\) that vanishes at a single x‑value, guaranteeing one inflection point. Its first derivative is quadratic, which can have zero, one, or two real roots, giving up to two critical points. Thus the fourth option best captures the relationship.

📖 Explanation:

From \(f'(x)=3x(x-2)\) we see sign changes at \(x=0\) and \(x=2\), giving the stated increasing/decreasing intervals. The second derivative \(f''(x)=6(x-1)\) changes sign at \(x=1\), establishing concavity regions and an inflection at \((1,f(1))=(1,-1)\).

Q22. How does the presence of an exponential factor \(e^{-x}\) affect the location of inflection points compared to a pure polynomial of the same degree?

🔹 A. It shifts the inflection point to the right
🔹 B. It shifts the inflection point to the left
🔹 C. It creates additional inflection points
🔹 D. It does not affect the location

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

For the cubic \(x^{3}-3x^{2}+1\) the inflection occurs at \(x=1\). Multiplying by \(e^{-x}\) as in \(xe^{-x}\) introduces a damping factor that changes the second derivative to \((x-2)e^{-x}\), moving the sign‑change—and thus the inflection point—to \(x=2\), i.e., to the right.

Q23. Define an inflection point in terms of the second derivative.

🔹 A. Where \(f''=0\) and changes sign
🔹 B. Where \(f'=0\)
🔹 C. Where the function attains a maximum
🔹 D. Where the derivative is undefined

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

An inflection point occurs at a location where the curvature switches from concave up to concave down or vice versa. This happens precisely when the second derivative equals zero (or is undefined) and its sign changes across the point. Hence the definition relies on \(f''\).

Q24. Differentiate between concave up and convex function terminology. Which statement is correct?

🔹 A. They are synonyms
🔹 B. They are opposites
🔹 C. Concave up equals convex
🔹 D. Concave up equals concave down

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In mathematics, a function that is concave up is also described as convex because its graph lies below its tangent lines, forming a “cup” shape. Therefore, concave up and convex are interchangeable terms, while concave down is the opposite notion.

Q25. If a rational function’s numerator and denominator share a common factor that cancels, removing a vertical asymptote, what happens to intervals of increase?

🔹 A. Intervals remain unchanged
🔹 B. Intervals merge across the hole
🔹 C. New intervals appear
🔹 D. Cannot be determined without graphing

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

Cancelling a common factor eliminates the discontinuity, turning a hole into a regular point. Consequently, monotonic behavior that was split by the asymptote now continues uninterrupted, causing the separate intervals of increase or decrease to merge into a single interval across the former hole.

Q26. Explain why \(f(x)=xe^{-x}\) attains a global maximum at \(x=1\) despite decreasing afterward.

🔹 A. Because the derivative changes from positive to negative at \(x=1\)
🔹 B. Because the second derivative is zero at \(x=1\)
🔹 C. Because the exponential factor dominates for large \(x\)
🔹 D. Because the limit as \(x\to\infty\) is zero

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The first derivative \(f'(x)=(1-x)e^{-x}\) is positive for \(x<1\) and negative for \(x>1\). This sign change indicates that the function increases up to \(x=1\) and then decreases, making \(x=1\) the point where the function reaches its highest value on \(\mathbb{R}\).

Q27. Using higher‑order derivatives, does a point where \(f'(x)=0\) and \(f''(x)=0\) necessarily indicate an inflection point?

🔹 A. Yes
🔹 B. No
🔹 C. It depends on the third derivative
🔹 D. Cannot be determined

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

When both the first and second derivatives vanish, the test is inconclusive. If the third derivative \(f'''(x)\) is non‑zero, the Taylor expansion shows a change in curvature, confirming an inflection point. If \(f'''(x)=0\) as well, higher‑order terms must be examined. Thus the answer depends on \(f'''(x)\).
