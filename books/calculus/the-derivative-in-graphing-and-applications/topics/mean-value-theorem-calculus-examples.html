What is Mean value theorem calculus examples?

Definition:
The Mean Value Theorem (MVT) states if fff is continuous on [a,b][a, b][a,b] and differentiable on (a,b)(a, b)(a,b), there exists c∈(a,b)c \in (a, b)c∈(a,b) such that f′(c)=f(b)−f(a)b−af'(c) = \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}f′(c)=b−af(b)−f(a)​. It guarantees a point where instantaneous rate equals average rate.

Example:
For f(x)=x2f(x) = x^2f(x)=x2 on [1,3][1, 3][1,3], average slope is 9−13−1=4\frac{9-1}{3-1} = 43−19−1​=4. f′(c)=2c=4⇒c=2f'(c)=2c=4 \Rightarrow c=2f′(c)=2c=4⇒c=2, which lies in (1,3)(1, 3)(1,3).

Reason:
MVT connects average and instantaneous rates, proving that at some point, the tangent is parallel to the secant line, vital for analysis.
