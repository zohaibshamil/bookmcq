📝 All Logistic growth curves calculus MCQs

Q1. If the constant \(k\) in the logistic model \(y = \frac{L}{1+Ae^{-kt}}\) is increased, what happens to the time at which the inflection point occurs?

🔹 A. The inflection point occurs earlier
🔹 B. The inflection point occurs later
🔹 C. The inflection point time is unchanged
🔹 D. The inflection point disappears

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Because the inflection time is \(t_{\text{inf}}=\frac{1}{k}\ln A\). When \(k\) grows, the denominator becomes larger, making the whole expression smaller, so the inflection occurs at a smaller (earlier) time.

Q2. How does increasing the parameter \(A\) affect the location of the inflection point in the logistic curve?

🔹 A. It shifts the inflection point earlier
🔹 B. It shifts the inflection point later
🔹 C. It leaves the inflection point unchanged
🔹 D. It moves the inflection point to \(t=0\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The inflection time is \(t_{\text{inf}}=\frac{\ln A}{k}\). A larger \(A\) produces a larger numerator, so \(t_{\text{inf}}\) increases, meaning the inflection point moves to a later time.

Q3. For a logistic curve with parameters \(L>0\), \(k>0\), and \(A>1\), if at a certain time \(t\) the population satisfies \(y(t)=0.4L\), what is the concavity of the graph at that time?

🔹 A. Concave down
🔹 B. Linear
🔹 C. Concave up
🔹 D. Inflection point

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The second derivative is \(k^{2}L^{2}y(L-y)(L-2y)\). Since \(y=0.4L<L/2\), the factor \((L-2y)\) is positive, making the second derivative positive, which indicates the curve is concave up.

Q4. If the carrying capacity \(L\) is reduced while keeping \(A\) and \(k\) constant, which statement about the horizontal asymptote is true?

🔹 A. It moves higher
🔹 B. It moves lower
🔹 C. It stays unchanged
🔹 D. It disappears

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The horizontal asymptote of the logistic function is \(y=L\). Decreasing \(L\) therefore lowers the asymptote, shifting it downward on the \(y\)-axis.

Q5. Consider two logistic models with the same \(L\) and \(A\) but growth rates \(k_{1}<k_{2}\). As \(t\to\infty\), which statement correctly describes their populations?

🔹 A. The model with larger \(k\) approaches \(L\) faster
🔹 B. Both approach \(L\) at the same rate
🔹 C. The model with smaller \(k\) never reaches \(L\)
🔹 D. Both diverge to infinity

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

Both models converge to the same limiting value \(L\); however, the larger growth constant \(k_{2}\) accelerates the approach, so the population with \(k_{2}\) reaches values near \(L\) more quickly.

Q6. Given \(k>0\), \(L>0\), and \(0<y<L\), what can be inferred about the sign of \(\frac{dy}{dt}=kL\,y(L-y)\)?

🔹 A. Negative
🔹 B. Zero
🔹 C. Depends on \(y\)
🔹 D. Positive

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

All factors in the expression are positive: \(k>0\), \(L>0\), \(y>0\), and \(L-y>0\). The product of positive numbers is positive, so \(\frac{dy}{dt}>0\) for any admissible \(y\).

Q7. Which of the following best describes the difference between the logistic model \(y=\frac{L}{1+Ae^{-kt}}\) and the exponential model \(y=y_{0}e^{kt}\) for large \(t\)?

🔹 A. Logistic grows faster than exponential
🔹 B. Logistic saturates at \(L\) while exponential grows without bound
🔹 C. Both have the same horizontal asymptote
🔹 D. Logistic declines after reaching a peak

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The exponential function continues to increase indefinitely, whereas the logistic function has a horizontal asymptote at \(y=L\); thus, for large \(t\) the logistic curve levels off while the exponential keeps rising.

Q8. For a fixed \(L\) and \(A\), how does increasing \(k\) affect the steepness of the logistic curve around its inflection point?

🔹 A. It flattens the curve
🔹 B. It has no effect on steepness
🔹 C. It shifts the inflection point earlier but leaves slope unchanged
🔹 D. It makes the curve steeper

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The factor \(k\) appears in the exponent and also multiplies the derivative. A larger \(k\) accelerates the transition from low to high values, sharpening the S‑shape and increasing the slope at the inflection point.

Q9. If \(0<A<1\) instead of \(A>1\), how does the initial population \(y(0)\) compare to the carrying capacity \(L\)?

🔹 A. It is greater than \(L/2\)
🔹 B. It is less than \(L/2\)
🔹 C. It equals \(L/2\)
🔹 D. It exceeds \(L\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

At \(t=0\), \(y(0)=\frac{L}{1+A}\). With \(0<A<1\), the denominator lies between 1 and 2, so \(y(0)\) lies between \(L/2\) and \(L\), i.e., it is greater than \(L/2\).

Q10. At what population value does the second derivative \(\frac{d^{2}y}{dt^{2}}=k^{2}L^{2}y(L-y)(L-2y)\) change sign?

🔹 A. \(y=0\)
🔹 B. \(y=L\)
🔹 C. \(y=\frac{L}{2}\)
🔹 D. \(y=\frac{L}{3}\)

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

The sign change occurs when the factor \((L-2y)\) equals zero, which gives \(y=\frac{L}{2}\). For \(y<L/2\) the second derivative is positive (concave up), and for \(y>L/2\) it becomes negative (concave down).

Q11. What is \(\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{L}{1+Ae^{-kt}}\)?

🔹 A. \(0\)
🔹 B. \(L\)
🔹 C. \(\infty\)
🔹 D. \(\frac{L}{A}\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

As \(t\to\infty\), the term \(e^{-kt}\) tends to zero, so the denominator approaches \(1\). The limit therefore simplifies to \(\frac{L}{1}=L\).

Q12. For given \(A>1\) and \(k>0\), the time at which the population reaches \(\frac{3L}{4}\) is:

🔹 A. \(\frac{1}{k}\ln\frac{A}{3}\)
🔹 B. \(\frac{1}{k}\ln\frac{3}{A}\)
🔹 C. \(\frac{1}{k}\ln\frac{1}{3A}\)
🔹 D. \(\frac{1}{k}\ln(3A)\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

Setting \(\frac{L}{1+Ae^{-kt}}=\frac{3L}{4}\) gives \(Ae^{-kt}=1/3\). Solving for \(t\) yields \(t=\frac{1}{k}\ln(3A)\), which is the expression in option D.

Q13. Which statement correctly describes the effect of choosing \(A>1\) versus \(0<A<1\) on the position of the logistic curve relative to the \(t\)-axis?

🔹 A. \(A>1\) shifts the curve right
🔹 B. \(A<1\) shifts the curve right
🔹 C. Both produce the same shift
🔹 D. \(A>1\) shifts the curve left

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

When \(A<1\) the denominator \(1+Ae^{-kt}\) is smaller for early times, giving larger values of \(y\) and moving the curve leftward. Conversely, \(A>1\) yields a smaller early \(y\) and shifts the curve right.

Q14. A bacterial colony follows \(y(t)=\frac{L}{1+Ae^{-kt}}\) with \(L=10^{9}\). What does \(L\) represent?

🔹 A. The initial population size
🔹 B. The intrinsic growth rate
🔹 C. The maximum sustainable population
🔹 D. The time of the inflection point

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

In the logistic model, \(L\) is the horizontal asymptote and denotes the carrying capacity—the largest population that the environment can sustain over the long term.

Q15. Using the second derivative expression, why does the logistic curve have an inflection point when \(y=\frac{L}{2}\)?

🔹 A. Because the factor \(L-2y\) becomes zero
🔹 B. Because the first derivative is zero
🔹 C. Because the growth rate is maximal
🔹 D. Because the denominator of the original function vanishes

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The term \((L-2y)\) in \(\frac{d^{2}y}{dt^{2}}\) determines the sign of the curvature. When \(y=L/2\), this factor equals zero, causing the second derivative to change sign, which defines an inflection point.

Q16. From \(\frac{dy}{dt}=kL\,y(L-y)\), how does the term \((L-y)\) enforce the carrying capacity \(L\)?

🔹 A. It reduces growth as \(y\) approaches \(L\)
🔹 B. It increases growth as \(y\) approaches \(L\)
🔹 C. It has no effect on growth
🔹 D. It causes the population to become negative

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The factor \((L-y)\) becomes smaller as the population nears \(L\), diminishing the overall growth rate. When \(y\) equals \(L\), the factor is zero, stopping growth entirely and thus enforcing the limit.

Q17. At what population value does the logistic model achieve its maximum instantaneous growth rate?

🔹 A. \(y=0\)
🔹 B. \(y=\frac{L}{2}\)
🔹 C. \(y=L\)
🔹 D. \(y=\frac{L}{4}\)

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The derivative \(\frac{dy}{dt}=kL\,y(L-y)\) is a quadratic in \(y\) that attains its maximum at the vertex, which occurs at \(y=\frac{L}{2}\).

Q18. If \(k\) doubles, how does the time required for the population to reach \(\frac{L}{2}\) change?

🔹 A. It halves
🔹 B. It doubles
🔹 C. It stays the same
🔹 D. It becomes zero

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation:

The inflection time is \(t_{\text{inf}}=\frac{\ln A}{k}\). Doubling \(k\) divides the denominator by two, so the time to reach \(L/2\) is reduced by a factor of two.

Q19. A logistic model has \(L=1000\), \(A=4\), \(k=0.3\). What is the population after \(t=5\) units?

🔹 A. 470
🔹 B. 600
🔹 C. 529
🔹 D. 750

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation:

Compute \(y(5)=\frac{1000}{1+4e^{-0.3\cdot5}}=\frac{1000}{1+4e^{-1.5}}\approx\frac{1000}{1+0.8925}=528.6\), which rounds to 529.

Q20. Which combination of growth phases creates the characteristic S‑shape of the logistic curve?

🔹 A. Constant growth throughout
🔹 B. Initial exponential growth then deceleration
🔹 C. Decline then rise
🔹 D. Oscillatory fluctuations

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation:

The logistic curve starts with near‑exponential increase when the population is small, then environmental limits cause the growth rate to decline, producing the familiar S‑shaped pattern.

Q21. What is the standard form of the logistic growth function?

🔹 A. \(y = Le^{kt}\)
🔹 B. \(y = \frac{L}{1+Ae^{-kt}}\)
🔹 C. \(y = Ae^{kt}+L\)
🔹 D. \(y = L(1 - e^{-kt})\)

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation:

The classic logistic function is written as \(y(t)=\frac{L}{1+Ae^{-kt}}\), where \(L\) is the carrying capacity, \(A\) reflects the initial condition, and \(k\) is the growth rate constant.
