What is Vertical tangents and cusps in graphs?

Definition:
A vertical tangent occurs where $f'(x) \to \pm\infty$ but the function is continuous, while a cusp is a sharp point where left and right derivatives approach opposite infinities. Both features indicate undefined derivatives but differ in continuity and directional behavior.

Example:
For $f(x) = x^{1/3}$, $f'(x) = \frac{1}{3}x^{-2/3}$. At $x=0$, $f'(0)$ is undefined, creating a vertical tangent since limits from both sides are $+\infty$.

Reason:
Identifying these singularities helps distinguish between smooth curves, sharp turns, and infinite slopes, which are critical for analyzing differentiability and geometric properties.

📝 All Vertical tangents and cusps in graphs MCQs

Q1. What is the derivative of \(f(x)= (x-4)^{2/3}\)?

A.\( \frac{2}{3}(x-4)^{-1/3}\) ✅
B.\( \frac{2}{3}(x-4)^{1/3}\)
C.\( \frac{1}{3}(x-4)^{-2/3}\)
D.\( \frac{2}{3}(x-4)^{2/3}\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The derivative follows from the power rule: differentiate \((x-4)^{2/3}\) to obtain \(\frac{2}{3}(x-4)^{(2/3)-1}=\frac{2}{3}(x-4)^{-1/3}\). This matches option A, while the other choices either use the wrong exponent or coefficient.

Q2. Given \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to4^{+}} f'(x)=+\infty\) and \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to4^{-}} f'(x)=-\infty\), what type of singular point does the graph have at \(x=4\)?

A.Vertical tangent
B.Corner point
C.Cusp ✅
D.Discontinuity
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: When the derivative approaches opposite infinities from the two sides, the graph makes a sharp point where the slopes diverge. This configuration defines a cusp. A vertical tangent would require both one‑sided limits to be \(+\infty\) (or both \(-\infty\)).

Q3. Compare the behavior of \(f'(x)\) for \(f(x)=(x-4)^{2/3}\) and \(g(x)=(x-4)^{1/3}\) near \(x=4\). Which statement is true?

A.Both derivatives tend to \(+\infty\) from each side
B.\(f'\) tends to \(+\infty\) from the right and \(-\infty\) from the left, while \(g'\) tends to \(+\infty\) from both sides ✅
C.Both derivatives tend to \(-\infty\) from each side
D.\(f'\) is bounded while \(g'\) diverges
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For \(f(x)=(x-4)^{2/3}\), \(f'(x)=\frac{2}{3}(x-4)^{-1/3}\) which changes sign, giving \(+\infty\) from the right and \(-\infty\) from the left. For \(g(x)=(x-4)^{1/3}\), \(g'(x)=\frac{1}{3}(x-4)^{-2/3}\) is always positive, so both limits are \(+\infty\).

Q4. If a function has a vertical tangent at \(x=a\), which statements are always correct?

A.The function is continuous at \(a\) but not differentiable there
B.The function is discontinuous at \(a\)
C.The function is both continuous and differentiable at \(a\)
D.The function has a cusp at \(a\) ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A vertical tangent occurs when the slope becomes infinite while the function remains continuous. Differentiability fails because the derivative does not exist as a finite number. The presence of a cusp requires opposite infinite slopes, not the same infinite slope on both sides.

Q5. For \(h(x)=|x|^{2/3}\), what type of singularity occurs at \(x=0\)?

A.Vertical tangent
B.Cusp ✅
C.Corner point
D.Removable discontinuity
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The derivative \(h'(x)=\frac{2}{3}\operatorname{sgn}(x)\,|x|^{-1/3}\) approaches \(+\infty\) from the right and \(-\infty\) from the left, indicating opposite infinite slopes. This configuration defines a cusp at the origin.

Q6. What is the concavity of \(f(x)=(x-4)^{2/3}\) on each side of its cusp at \(x=4\)?

A.Concave up on both sides
B.Concave down on both sides
C.Concave up on the left, down on the right
D.Concave down on the left, up on the right ✅
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: The second derivative \(f''(x)=-\frac{2}{9}(x-4)^{-4/3}\) is negative for all \(x\neq4\) because the power \(-4/3\) yields a positive quantity, and the leading coefficient is negative. Hence the graph is concave down on both sides of the cusp.

Q7. Why does the graph of \(y=(x-4)^{2/3}\) have no horizontal asymptote?

A.Because the function oscillates infinitely
B.Because the limit as \(x\to\pm\infty\) is finite
C.Because the limit as \(x\to\pm\infty\) is \(+\infty\) ✅
D.Because the function is periodic
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A horizontal asymptote exists only when the function approaches a finite constant as \(x\) grows without bound. For \(y=(x-4)^{2/3}\), the expression grows like \(|x|^{2/3}\), so both limits at \(+\infty\) and \(-\infty\) diverge to \(+\infty\).

Q8. If a function's derivative approaches \(+\infty\) from both sides at \(x_{0}\), what shape does the graph exhibit there?

A.Vertical tangent ✅
B.Cusp
C.Inflection point
D.Sharp corner
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: When the slope becomes infinitely large but with the same sign on both sides, the tangent line becomes vertical, producing a vertical tangent. A cusp requires opposite infinite slopes, while an inflection point involves a change in concavity without infinite slopes.

Q9. Consider \(p(x)= (x^{2})^{1/3}\). What type of singularity occurs at \(x=0\)?

A.Vertical tangent
B.Cusp ✅
C.Corner point
D.Removable discontinuity
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Simplifying gives \(p(x)=|x|^{2/3}\). Its derivative behaves like \(\frac{2}{3}\operatorname{sgn}(x)\,|x|^{-1/3}\), which tends to \(+\infty\) from the right and \(-\infty\) from the left, characteristic of a cusp.

Q10. Which transformation preserves the existence of a vertical tangent at \(x=4\) for the base function \(y=x^{2/3}\)?

A.Vertical stretch
B.Reflection about the x‑axis
C.Horizontal shift
D.Rotation by 90° ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: A vertical tangent depends on the local behavior of the function near a specific x‑value. Translating the graph horizontally (a horizontal shift) moves the point of tangency but does not remove the vertical tangent. All other listed transformations alter the shape in ways that can eliminate the vertical tangent.

Q11. Determine the sign of \(f'(x)\) for \(f(x)=(x-4)^{2/3}\) when \(x<4\) and when \(x>4\).

A.Negative left, negative right
B.Positive left, positive right
C.Negative left, positive right ✅
D.Positive left, negative right
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The derivative \(f'(x)=\frac{2}{3}(x-4)^{-1/3}\) inherits the sign of \((x-4)^{-1/3}\). For \(x<4\), the base \((x-4)\) is negative, and an odd root preserves the sign, yielding a negative derivative. For \(x>4\), the derivative is positive.

Q12. For \(q(x)= (x-4)^{2/3} + (x-4)\), locate any critical points and classify them.

A.No critical points ✅
B.Critical at \(x=4\) – relative maximum
C.Critical at \(x=4\) – relative minimum
D.Critical at \(x=4\) – point of inflection
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: \(q'(x)=\frac{2}{3}(x-4)^{-1/3}+1\). As \(x\to4^{-}\), the first term \(\to -\infty\) so \(q'\) is negative; as \(x\to4^{+}\), it \(\to +\infty\) making \(q'\) positive. Hence the derivative changes sign from negative to positive at \(x=4\), indicating a relative minimum despite the derivative being undefined there.

Q13. How does the presence of an inflection point affect the concavity on either side of a vertical tangent?

A.Concavity does not change
B.Concavity changes sign ✅
C.Concavity becomes undefined
D.Concavity becomes positive on both sides
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: An inflection point is defined by a change in the sign of the second derivative, meaning the graph switches from concave up to concave down or vice‑versa. When a vertical tangent coincides with an inflection point, the concavity on the two sides of the tangent must be opposite.

Q14. If \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to a} f'(x)=+\infty\) and \(f\) is continuous at \(a\), which statement must be true about the tangent line at \(a\)?

A.The tangent line is horizontal ✅
B.The tangent line is vertical
C.The tangent line does not exist
D.The tangent line is slanted with slope 1
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: An infinite positive limit of the derivative indicates that the slope of the secant lines grows without bound, forcing the tangent line to become vertical. Continuity guarantees the point \((a,f(a))\) exists, so a vertical line through that point serves as the tangent.

Q15. Compare the graphs of \(y=(x-4)^{2/3}\) and \(y=|x-4|^{2/3}\). Which statement is correct?

A.They are reflections of each other across the x‑axis
B.They are identical ✅
C.The first is defined only for \(x\ge4\)
D.The second has a horizontal asymptote
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Because the exponent \(2/3\) is even in the numerator, raising a negative number to the power \(2\) eliminates the sign before taking the cube root. Consequently \((x-4)^{2/3}=|x-4|^{2/3}\) for all real \(x\); the two graphs coincide.

Q16. Why does \(y=(x-4)^{2/3}\) have a relative minimum at its cusp despite not being differentiable there?

A.Because the function value is lowest at the cusp ✅
B.Because the derivative changes from negative to positive around the cusp
C.Because the second derivative is zero at the cusp
D.Because the graph is symmetric about the y‑axis
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: The derivative sign analysis shows the function decreases for \(x<4\) and increases for \(x>4\). This sign change indicates a transition from decreasing to increasing, which defines a relative minimum. The lack of differentiability does not prevent the minimum, as the first‑derivative test relies on sign rather than finite values.

Q17. What is the limit of the secant slope \(\displaystyle\frac{f(x)-f(4)}{x-4}\) as \(x\to4^{+}\) for \(f(x)=(x-4)^{2/3}\)?

A.\(0\)
B.\(+\infty\) ✅
C.\(-\infty\)
D.\(1\)
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The secant slope approaches the right‑hand derivative, which is \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to4^{+}}\frac{2}{3}(x-4)^{-1/3}=+\infty\). Hence the limit of the quotient is \(+\infty\).

Q18. Is the graph of \(s(x)=(x-4)^{2/3}\) symmetric about any vertical line?

A.Yes, about \(x=0\)
B.Yes, about \(x=2\)
C.Yes, about \(x=4\) ✅
D.No symmetry exists
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The function can be rewritten as \(((x-4)^{1/3})^{2}\), which is an even function of the shifted variable \(u=x-4\). Therefore it is symmetric about the line \(u=0\), i.e., \(x=4\). The answer choice stating symmetry about \(x=4\) would be correct, but the options list it as D, so D is the correct answer.

Q19. If a function has a cusp at \(x_{0}\), what can be said about the existence of its second derivative at \(x_{0}\)?

A.The second derivative exists and is finite
B.The second derivative exists but is infinite
C.The second derivative does not exist ✅
D.The second derivative is zero
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A cusp implies that the first derivative approaches opposite infinities from the two sides, so the slope is unbounded. Since the second derivative involves the rate of change of this already unbounded slope, it cannot be defined at the cusp; thus it does not exist.

Q20. Which of the following statements about vertical tangents is FALSE?

A.A vertical tangent requires the function to be continuous at the point
B.A vertical tangent occurs when the derivative approaches \(+\infty\) from both sides
C.A vertical tangent can occur at a point where the function is not defined ✅
D.A vertical tangent implies the graph is locally linear
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A vertical tangent cannot exist at a point where the function is undefined because there is no point to attach a tangent line. The other statements correctly describe properties of vertical tangents.

Q21. Compute \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to4^{+}}\frac{f(x)-f(4)}{x-4}\) for \(f(x)=(x-4)^{2/3}\).

A.\(0\)
B.\(+\infty\) ✅
C.\(-\infty\)
D.\(1\)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The limit equals the right‑hand derivative, which is \(\frac{2}{3}(x-4)^{-1/3}\) as \(x\to4^{+}\). This expression diverges to \(+\infty\), so the limit is \(+\infty\).

Q22. How does adding a constant \(k\) to \(f(x)=(x-4)^{2/3}\) affect the location of its vertical tangent?

A.Shifts it left by \(k\) units
B.Shifts it right by \(k\) units
C.Leaves the location unchanged ✅
D.Eliminates the vertical tangent
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Adding a constant raises or lowers the entire graph without altering its shape or the x‑coordinates of any features. Consequently, the vertical tangent remains at the same \(x\)-value, \(x=4\), regardless of the value of \(k\).

Q23. Define \(g(x)= (x-4)^{2/3}\) for \(x\ge4\) and \(g(x)= (4-x)^{2/3}\) for \(x<4\). Which smoothness class does \(g\) belong to at \(x=4\)?

A.\(C^{0}\) only
B.\(C^{1}\) only
C.\(C^{2}\) only ✅
D.Not even continuous
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Both pieces equal \(|x-4|^{2/3}\), so the function is continuous at \(x=4\) (\(C^{0}\)). However, the derivative from the left is \(-\infty\) and from the right is \(+\infty\), so the first derivative does not exist, meaning the function is not \(C^{1}\).

Q24. Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to4^{\pm}} f''(x)\) for \(f(x)=(x-4)^{2/3}\).

A.\(0\)
B.\(+\infty\)
C.\(-\infty\) ✅
D.Does not exist
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The second derivative is \(f''(x)=-\frac{2}{9}(x-4)^{-4/3}\). As \(x\) approaches 4 from either side, \((x-4)^{-4/3}\) tends to \(+\infty\); the negative coefficient then forces the limit to \(-\infty\) on both sides.

Q25. In the First Derivative Test, how can a cusp serve as a relative extremum?

A.By having a finite derivative that changes sign
B.By being a point where the derivative is undefined but the sign of the derivative changes across it ✅
C.By making the function constant around the cusp
D.By eliminating the need for a derivative test
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Even though the derivative does not exist at a cusp, the sign of the derivative on either side can still be examined. If the derivative is negative before the cusp and positive after, the function decreases then increases, indicating a relative minimum at the cusp.
