What is Work integrals in vector fields?

Work integrals in vector fields:
Work is W=∫CF⋅drW = \int_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}W=∫C​F⋅dr, the energy transferred by a force field along a path.

Example:
Work by F=⟨−kx,−ky⟩\mathbf{F} = \langle -kx, -ky \rangleF=⟨−kx,−ky⟩ (spring force) from (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) to (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) along a straight line gives W=−kW = -kW=−k.

Reason:
Work integrals are central to physics, linking force, displacement, and energy in mechanical systems.

A.The work depends only on the length of the path.
B.The work is the same for every path connecting AAA and BBB. ✅
C.The work is zero because the starting point lies on the x-axis.

📖 Explanation: The field is F=∇(x2+y2)F=\nabla(x^2+y^2)F=∇(x2+y2), so it is conservative on the plane. Therefore the work between two fixed endpoints depends only on the endpoints, not on the particular smooth path. The geometric length, crossings, or shape of the path do not determine the work.

💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Work measures the component of the force acting in the instantaneous direction of displacement. Q3. Suppose a force field is conservative and a particle travels from AAA to BBB, first along path C1C_1C1​ and then along a much longer path C2C_2C2​. Why can the two work integrals still have the same value?

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: For a conservative force field, there is a potential function fff such that F=∇fF=\nabla fF=∇f. The work from AAA to BBB equals f(B)−f(A)f(B)-f(A)f(B)−f(A), so intermediate bends, detours, and path lengths do not change the total work. Speed is also irrelevant to this mathematical line integral.

Q4. A student claims that if a particle follows a closed curve CCC, then ∫CF⋅dr=0\int_C F\cdot dr=0∫C​F⋅dr=0 for every vector field FFF. Which response best evaluates the claim?

📖 Explanation: A closed path has the same initial and final point, but that alone does not force the work to vanish. If FFF is conservative, the potential change around the loop is zero. A nonconservative field can produce nonzero circulation and therefore nonzero work around a closed curve.

Q9. A graph shows a particle traveling around a closed loop in a field whose arrows consistently circulate counterclockwise and have positive tangential components along the loop. Which qualitative conclusion is most reasonable?

A.The work must be positive. ✅
B.The work must be negative.
C.The work must be exactly zero.
D.The work cannot be determined because the path is closed.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: If the field vectors have positive tangential components along the direction of traversal throughout the loop, then F⋅drF\cdot drF⋅dr is predominantly positive. Consequently, the accumulated work is positive. Being a closed path does not force zero work; zero circulation is guaranteed only under appropriate conservative-field conditions.

Q10. On a graph, a particle travels from AAA to BBB. Along the first half of the path, force vectors point mostly in the direction of motion; along the second half, they point mostly opposite the motion. The vectors have equal magnitudes over equal path lengths. What is the best inference?

A.The work must be positive.
B.The work must be negative.
C.The positive and negative contributions may cancel, giving zero net work. ✅
D.The work must equal the total path length.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Work depends on the dot product F⋅drF\cdot drF⋅dr, so force components parallel to the motion contribute positively or negatively according to their direction. If equal force magnitudes act over equal path lengths with exactly opposite tangential directions in the two portions, their contributions cancel. The net work is then zero, despite nonzero forces.
