What is Green's Theorem in calculus?

Green's Theorem in calculus:
Green's Theorem relates a line integral around a simple closed curve to a double integral over the region enclosed: ∮CP dx+Q dy=∬D(Qx−Py) dA\oint_C P \, dx + Q \, dy = \iint_D (Q_x - P_y) \, dA∮C​Pdx+Qdy=∬D​(Qx​−Py​)dA.

Example:
For F=⟨−y,x⟩\mathbf{F} = \langle -y, x \rangleF=⟨−y,x⟩, ∮CF⋅dr=∬D(1−(−1)) dA=2⋅area(D)\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_D (1 - (-1)) \, dA = 2 \cdot \text{area}(D)∮C​F⋅dr=∬D​(1−(−1))dA=2⋅area(D).

Reason:
Green's theorem converts boundary integrals to area integrals, simplifying computations and forming the 2D foundation for Stokes' theorem.
