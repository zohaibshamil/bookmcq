What is Green's Theorem in calculus?

Green's Theorem in calculus:
Green's Theorem relates a line integral around a simple closed curve to a double integral over the region enclosed: ∮CP dx+Q dy=∬D(Qx−Py) dA∮C​Pdx+Qdy=∬D​(Qx​−Py​)dA.

Example:
For F=⟨−y,x⟩F=⟨−y,x⟩, ∮CF⋅dr=∬D(1−(−1)) dA=2⋅area(D)∮C​F⋅dr=∬D​(1−(−1))dA=2⋅area(D).

Reason:
Green's theorem converts boundary integrals to area integrals, simplifying computations and forming the 2D foundation for Stokes' theorem.

📖 Explanation: Green's Theorem relates the positively oriented circulation integral ∮CP dx+Q dy∮C​Pdx+Qdy to the double integral ∬R(Qx−Py) dA∬R​(Qx​−Py​)dA. The key distinction is the cross-partial combination Qx−PyQx​−Py​, not the divergence Px+QyPx​+Qy​.

Q10. A graph shows a positively oriented boundary consisting of a large outer circle and a smaller circular hole inside it. The field is smooth throughout the region between the circles. Which interpretation of Green's Theorem is correct?
