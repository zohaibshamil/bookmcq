What is Line integrals with respect to x y z?

Line integrals with respect to x, y, z:
Line integrals can be expressed as \(\int_C P \, dx + Q \, dy + R \, dz\), evaluated by substituting parameterization and integrating each term separately.

Example:
\(\int_C x \, dx + y \, dy\) along \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t, t \rangle\), \(0 \le t \le 1\), becomes \(\int_0^1 (t \cdot 1 + t \cdot 1) dt = 1\).

Reason:
This differential form is useful in physics for work and in complex analysis, linking to exact differentials and path independence.

Q1. A curve \(C\) is parameterized by \(x=t^2,\ y=t,\ z=2t\), where \(0\le t\le1\). Which expression correctly represents the line integral \(\int_C f(x,y,z)\,dx\) for a scalar field \(f\)?

A.\( \int_0^1 f(t^2,t,2t)\,dt \)
B.\( \int_0^1 f(t^2,t,2t)(2t)\,dt \) ✅
C.\( \int_0^1 f(t^2,t,2t)(1)\,dt \)
D.\( \int_0^1 f(t^2,t,2t)(2)\,dt \)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a parameterized curve, \(dx\) must be replaced by \(x'(t)\,dt\). Since \(x=t^2\), we have \(dx=2t\,dt\). Substituting \(x=t^2,\ y=t,\ z=2t\) into the field gives \(f(t^2,t,2t)\), so the complete integral is \(\int_0^1 f(t^2,t,2t)(2t)\,dt\).

Q2. For a parameterized curve \(x=x(t),y=y(t),z=z(t)\), which statement best distinguishes \(\int_C f\,dx\), \(\int_C f\,dy\), and \(\int_C f\,dz\)?

A.They are always equal because \(dx,dy,dz\) describe the same curve.
B.They differ only when the scalar field changes.
C.They weight the same field by the respective coordinate changes \(x'(t),y'(t),z'(t)\). ✅
D.They are all equivalent to \(\int_C f\,ds\).
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The three integrals use the same scalar field but different differential components. Under parameterization, \(dx=x'(t)dt\), \(dy=y'(t)dt\), and \(dz=z'(t)dt\). Therefore, their values can differ substantially even on the same curve because each measures the field contribution according to a different coordinate direction.

Q3. Suppose \(f(x,y,z)=x+y+z\) along the straight-line path from \((0,0,0)\) to \((1,2,3)\). Which approach is most efficient for evaluating \(\int_C f\,dx\)?

A.Use \(ds=\sqrt{14}\,dt\) because every line integral requires arc length.
B.Parameterize the path, substitute into \(f\), and multiply by \(dx/dt\). ✅
C.Replace \(dx\) by \(dy+dz\) because the path is straight.
D.Integrate \(f\) with respect to \(x\) while treating \(y\) and \(z\) as zero.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a line integral with respect to \(x\), the differential \(dx\) determines the required weighting. A straight-line parameterization such as \(x=t,\ y=2t,\ z=3t\) makes the substitution direct. Then \(f=6t\) and \(dx=dt\), so the integral becomes an ordinary single-variable integral.

Q4. A student claims that if a curve begins and ends at the same point, then \(\int_C f\,dx=0\) for every scalar field \(f\). Which observation most directly disproves the claim?

A.The curve may have nonzero length.
B.The field \(f\) may be negative.
C.The integral depends on the entire path and on how \(x\) changes along that path, not merely on the endpoints. ✅
D.A closed curve cannot be parameterized.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A closed path has zero net change in \(x\), but \(\int_C f\,dx\) is generally a weighted accumulation of the field along the path. Unless special conditions cause cancellation, positive and negative contributions need not balance. Thus identical endpoints alone do not force a line integral with respect to \(x\) to vanish.

Q5. A curve is given by \(x=t,\ y=t^2,\ z=t^3\), \(0\le t\le2\), and \(f(x,y,z)=y\). A student evaluates \(\int_C f\,dy\) as \(\int_0^2 t^2\,dt\). What is the student's key error?

A.The field should be replaced by \(z\).
B.The limits should be reversed.
C.The factor \(dy/dt=2t\) was omitted. ✅
D.The parameterization cannot be used for line integrals.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Since \(y=t^2\), its differential is \(dy=2t\,dt\). The field becomes \(f=y=t^2\), but the integral must include the coordinate differential. Therefore, the correct setup is \(\int_0^2 t^2(2t)\,dt\). Omitting \(2t\) changes the weighting and produces an incorrect result.

Q6. A wire follows \(x=t,\ y=1-t,\ z=t^2\) for \(0\le t\le1\). If the density is \(f(x,y,z)=x+z\), which integral models the total contribution measured with respect to \(z\)?

A.\( \int_0^1 (t+t^2)\,dt \)
B.\( \int_0^1 (t+t^2)(1)\,dt \)
C.\( \int_0^1 (t+t^2)(2t)\,dt \) ✅
D.\( \int_0^1 (t+t^2)(-1)\,dt \)
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Substitution gives \(f=x+z=t+t^2\). Because the integral is taken with respect to \(z\), the differential must be included as \(dz=z'(t)dt=2t\,dt\). Hence the correct model is \(\int_0^1(t+t^2)(2t)\,dt\). This requires identifying both the field and the appropriate coordinate differential.

Q7. Consider the path \(x=t^2,\ y=2t,\ z=1-t\), \(0\le t\le1\). For \(f(x,y,z)=xz\), which expression correctly represents \(\int_C f\,dy\)?

A.\( \int_0^1 t^2(1-t)\,dt \)
B.\( \int_0^1 t^2(1-t)(2)\,dt \) ✅
C.\( \int_0^1 t^2(1-t)(2t)\,dt \)
D.\( \int_0^1 t^2(1-t)(-1)\,dt \)
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Here \(f=xz=t^2(1-t)\). Since the integral is with respect to \(y\), we calculate \(dy/dt=2\), giving \(dy=2dt\). Therefore, the correct expression is \(\int_0^1 t^2(1-t)(2)\,dt\). The distractors correspond to confusing \(dy\) with \(dx\), \(dz\), or omitting the differential factor.

Q8. Two paths connect the same endpoints. Along path \(C_1\), \(x\) increases monotonically while along \(C_2\), \(x\) first increases and then decreases. For a positive scalar field \(f\), which conclusion about \(\int_C f\,dx\) is most justified?

A.Both integrals must be equal because their endpoints agree.
B.The integral along \(C_2\) can contain cancellation because \(dx\) changes sign. ✅
C.The integral along \(C_1\) must be zero because \(x\) changes.
D.Both integrals are positive because \(f>0\).
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Although \(f\) is positive, \(dx\) carries the sign of the change in \(x\). On \(C_2\), the portion where \(x\) decreases contributes negatively, potentially canceling positive contributions from the increasing portion. This demonstrates why a line integral with respect to a coordinate differential is sensitive to path behavior and orientation.

Q9. A graph of a projected path onto the \(xy\)-plane shows a motion from \(x=0\) to \(x=4\), followed by a return from \(x=4\) to \(x=1\). Suppose \(f\) is positive everywhere. Which statement about \(\int_C f\,dx\) is necessarily true?

A.It must be positive because \(f>0\).
B.It must be negative because the path eventually moves left.
C.Its sign cannot be determined without comparing the weighted contributions of the two portions. ✅
D.It must equal \(3f\) regardless of how \(f\) varies.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The first portion has \(dx>0\), while the return portion has \(dx<0\). Since the field is positive, these portions contribute with opposite signs. The final sign depends on the magnitude of the weighted contributions, which requires information about how \(f\) behaves along the path. Positivity of \(f\) alone is insufficient.

Q10. A plotted space curve has \(z\) increasing steadily as the parameter increases, while \(x\) oscillates and \(y\) remains constant. If \(f>0\), which integral is least likely to exhibit sign cancellation caused by coordinate reversal?

A.\( \int_C f\,dx \)
B.\( \int_C f\,dy \)
C.\( \int_C f\,dz \) ✅
D.All three must have the same cancellation.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Because \(z\) increases steadily, \(dz\) retains the same sign throughout the path. Therefore, with \(f>0\), contributions to \(\int_C f\,dz\) do not cancel because of reversals in the \(z\)-direction. In contrast, the oscillation of \(x\) can produce positive and negative \(dx\) contributions.

Q11. A student parameterizes a curve correctly but reverses the parameter interval from \(t=1\) to \(t=0\) without changing the differential. What happens to \(\int_C f\,dy\) under this reversal?

A.It remains unchanged because \(f\) is scalar.
B.It changes sign because \(dy\) changes orientation with the path. ✅
C.It becomes an arc-length integral.
D.It becomes zero for every field.
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Reversing the parameter direction reverses the orientation of the curve. Since \(dy=y'(t)dt\), the differential changes sign under the reversed traversal. Consequently, a line integral with respect to \(dy\) changes sign. This is different from scalar arc-length integrals involving \(ds\), which remain unchanged under orientation reversal.

Q12. Let \(f(x,y,z)=2x-y+z\), and let \(C\) be parameterized by \(x=t,\ y=t,\ z=2t\), \(0\le t\le1\). Which pair of integrals has equal values, and why?

A.\( \int_C f\,dx\) and \( \int_C f\,dy\), because \(dx=dy\) along the path. ✅
B.\( \int_C f\,dx\) and \( \int_C f\,dz\), because \(dx=dz\).
C.\( \int_C f\,dy\) and \( \int_C f\,dz\), because \(dy=dz\).
D.None, because differentials can never be equal.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: Along the given parameterization, \(x=t\) and \(y=t\), so \(dx=dt\) and \(dy=dt\). Therefore, the same field values are multiplied by identical differentials, making \(\int_C f\,dx=\int_C f\,dy\). Meanwhile, \(dz=2dt\), so the \(z\)-integral generally has twice the weighting.

Q13. A modeling problem describes heat accumulation along a moving sensor path. The measured intensity is \(f(x,y,z)\), and the quantity of interest is accumulated only as the sensor moves vertically. Which mathematical model is most appropriate?

A.\( \int_C f\,ds \)
B.\( \int_C f\,dx \)
C.\( \int_C f\,dy \)
D.\( \int_C f\,dz \) ✅
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: If the modeled accumulation is specifically associated with vertical movement, the relevant coordinate differential is \(dz\). Thus \(\int_C f\,dz\) directly weights the measured intensity by vertical displacement. An arc-length integral would instead measure accumulation per unit distance, while \(dx\) and \(dy\) describe horizontal coordinate changes.

Q14. Suppose \(f(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+z^2\) and \(C\) is any closed curve lying entirely on the plane \(x=2\). Which conclusion about \(\int_C f\,dx\) follows immediately?

A.It is positive because \(f>0\).
B.It is negative because the curve is closed.
C.It is zero because \(dx=0\) everywhere on the curve. ✅
D.It equals the length of the curve.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Every point of the curve lies on the plane \(x=2\), so \(x\) is constant along the entire path. Therefore \(dx=0\) at every point, regardless of the behavior of \(y\), \(z\), or the value of \(f\). Hence the entire line integral \(\int_C f\,dx\) is zero.

Q15. Consider a closed curve \(C\) and the three integrals \(\int_C x\,dx\), \(\int_C y\,dy\), and \(\int_C z\,dz\). Which reasoning correctly determines all three values without explicitly parameterizing the curve?

A.Each equals zero because the curve is closed and every line integral over a closed curve vanishes.
B.Each is zero because \(x\,dx\), \(y\,dy\), and \(z\,dz\) are exact differentials of \(\frac{x^2}{2},\frac{y^2}{2},\frac{z^2}{2}\), respectively. ✅
C.They are generally nonzero because the field changes along the curve.
D.Only \(\int_C z\,dz\) is zero because \(z\) is the third coordinate.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The key is to recognize exact differentials: \(x\,dx=d(x^2/2)\), \(y\,dy=d(y^2/2)\), and \(z\,dz=d(z^2/2)\). The integral of an exact differential around a closed curve is zero. This conclusion follows from structure rather than from the particular shape or parameterization of the curve.

Q16. A researcher compares two parameterizations of the same oriented curve: one uses \(t\), and the other uses a smooth increasing parameter \(s\). Which statement best explains why both should produce the same value for \(\int_C f\,dz\)?

A.The field becomes constant under reparameterization.
B.The coordinate \(z\) disappears from the integral.
C.The chain rule transforms \(dz\) consistently, preserving the oriented accumulation along the same geometric path. ✅
D.Both parameterizations must have identical derivatives.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: A smooth increasing reparameterization changes how quickly the curve is traversed but does not change its orientation or geometric path. By the chain rule, \(dz\) transforms consistently with the new parameter, so the accumulated quantity represented by \(\int_C f\,dz\) remains unchanged. The parameter itself is therefore not part of the intrinsic result.

Q17. Let \(C\) be a closed curve for which \(x\ge0\), and suppose \(f=x\). A student argues that \(\int_C x\,dx>0\) because both \(x\) and the length of the curve are nonnegative. What is the strongest correction?

A.The argument is correct because \(dx\) is always positive.
B.The integral is zero because \(x\,dx=d(x^2/2)\), so the closed-path integral vanishes. ✅
C.The integral is negative because the curve is closed.
D.The integral equals the circumference multiplied by the average value of \(x\).
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The student's mistake is treating \(dx\) like a nonnegative length element. Unlike \(ds\), \(dx\) can be positive or negative depending on direction. More importantly, \(x\,dx\) is the exact differential \(d(x^2/2)\). Therefore, its integral around any closed curve is zero, regardless of the nonnegative values of \(x\).
