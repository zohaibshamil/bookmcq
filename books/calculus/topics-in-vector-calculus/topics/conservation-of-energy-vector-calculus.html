What is Conservation of energy vector calculus?

Conservation of energy vector calculus:
In a conservative field, the total mechanical energy E=K+UE = K + UE=K+U is conserved, where U=−fU = -fU=−f is potential energy, and work W=ΔK=−ΔUW = \Delta K = -\Delta UW=ΔK=−ΔU.

Example:
For gravity F=⟨0,−mg⟩\mathbf{F} = \langle 0, -mg \rangleF=⟨0,−mg⟩, f=−mgyf = -mgyf=−mgy, so U=mgyU = mgyU=mgy, and K+mgyK + mgyK+mgy remains constant.

Reason:
Vector calculus formalizes energy conservation, linking mathematics to fundamental physical laws.

Q1. A particle moves in a region where the net force is conservative. Its kinetic energy changes from 18 J18\text{ J}18 J to 7 J7\text{ J}7 J. Which conclusion about the change in potential energy is necessarily correct?

A.It decreases by 11 J11\text{ J}11 J
B.It increases by 11 J11\text{ J}11 J ✅
C.It increases by 25 J25\text{ J}25 J
D.It remains unchanged
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a system influenced only by conservative forces, the total mechanical energy remains constant. Therefore, any decrease in kinetic energy must appear as an equal increase in potential energy. Here the kinetic energy decreases by 18−7=11 J18-7=11\text{ J}18−7=11 J, so the potential energy increases by 11 J11\text{ J}11 J.

Q2. A student claims that conservation of energy means the kinetic energy of a particle must remain constant whenever the force field is conservative. Which evaluation is most accurate?

A.Correct, because conservative forces cannot do work
B.Correct, because potential energy is always zero
C.Incorrect, because kinetic and potential energy can exchange while their sum remains constant ✅
D.Incorrect, because conservative forces always increase kinetic energy
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Conservation of mechanical energy does not require kinetic energy to remain constant. A conservative force can convert potential energy into kinetic energy or kinetic energy into potential energy. The essential condition is that the sum K+UK+UK+U remains constant when no nonconservative work changes the mechanical energy.

Q5. A cart moves through a track with a conservative force and reaches a point where its speed is smaller than at the starting point. A student concludes that energy has been destroyed. Which explanation best corrects the reasoning?

A.The missing kinetic energy has necessarily become potential energy ✅
B.The force must have stopped acting
C.The cart has violated conservation of energy
D.A conservative force cannot change speed
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: A conservative force can transfer energy between kinetic and potential forms without changing total mechanical energy. If the cart slows down, its kinetic energy decreases, but the potential energy can increase by the same amount. Thus a decrease in speed does not imply energy destruction.
