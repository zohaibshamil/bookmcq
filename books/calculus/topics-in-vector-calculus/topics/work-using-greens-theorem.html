What is Work using Green's theorem?

Work using Green's theorem:
Work ∮CF⋅dr\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}∮C​F⋅dr can be computed as ∬D(Qx−Py) dA\iint_D (Q_x - P_y) \, dA∬D​(Qx​−Py​)dA, avoiding path parameterization.

Example:
Work by F=⟨x2,y2⟩\mathbf{F} = \langle x^2, y^2 \rangleF=⟨x2,y2⟩ around unit circle: Qx−Py=0−0=0Q_x - P_y = 0 - 0 = 0Qx​−Py​=0−0=0, so work = 0.

Reason:
This application dramatically reduces computational effort for closed paths, especially in complex regions.

Q1. A positively oriented closed curve CCC encloses a region RRR. For a vector field F=⟨P,Q⟩\mathbf F=\langle P,Q\rangleF=⟨P,Q⟩, which expression directly converts the work integral ∮CF⋅dr\oint_C \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r∮C​F⋅dr into a double integral over RRR?

A.∬R(Px−Qy) dA\iint_R(P_x-Q_y)\,dA∬R​(Px​−Qy​)dA
B.∬R(Qx−Py) dA\iint_R(Q_x-P_y)\,dA∬R​(Qx​−Py​)dA ✅
C.∬R(Py−Qx) dA\iint_R(P_y-Q_x)\,dA∬R​(Py​−Qx​)dA
D.∬R(Px+Qy) dA\iint_R(P_x+Q_y)\,dA∬R​(Px​+Qy​)dA
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For a positively oriented simple closed curve, the work integral is ∮CP dx+Q dy\oint_C P\,dx+Q\,dy∮C​Pdx+Qdy. The corresponding double integral uses the curl-like quantity Qx−PyQ_x-P_yQx​−Py​, so the correct conversion is ∬R(Qx−Py) dA\iint_R(Q_x-P_y)\,dA∬R​(Qx​−Py​)dA. The other choices either reverse the sign or represent a different differential combination.

Q2. Suppose F=⟨−y,x⟩\mathbf F=\langle -y,x\rangleF=⟨−y,x⟩ and CCC is a positively oriented closed curve enclosing area AAA. Without knowing the exact shape of CCC, what can be concluded about the work done around CCC?

A.It is always zero because the curve is closed
B.It depends on the perimeter of CCC
C.It equals 2A2A2A ✅
D.It equals A/2A/2A/2
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Here P=−yP=-yP=−y and Q=xQ=xQ=x, so Qx=1Q_x=1Qx​=1 and Py=−1P_y=-1Py​=−1. Therefore Qx−Py=2Q_x-P_y=2Qx​−Py​=2, a constant. Green's Theorem converts the work into ∬R2,dA=2A\iint_R2,dA=2A∬R​2,dA=2A. Thus the detailed boundary shape is irrelevant; only the enclosed area matters.

Q3. A student argues that because a path is closed, the work integral ∮CF⋅dr\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r∮C​F⋅dr must be zero. Which response best identifies the flaw in this reasoning?

A.Closed paths always have zero displacement, so the student is correct
B.A closed path can have nonzero circulation when Qx−PyQ_x-P_yQx​−Py​ is not identically zero inside the region ✅
C.Work can never be evaluated on closed paths
D.The work is zero only when the curve has positive orientation
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: A closed curve returns to its starting point, but that does not imply zero work for a general vector field. Green's Theorem shows that circulation depends on the accumulated quantity Qx−PyQ_x-P_yQx​−Py​ over the enclosed region. Unless that quantity integrates to zero, the closed-path work can be nonzero.

Q5. A rectangular region is bounded by 0≤x≤20\le x\le20≤x≤2 and 0≤y≤30\le y\le30≤y≤3, traversed counterclockwise. For F=⟨−2y,4x⟩\mathbf F=\langle -2y,4x\rangleF=⟨−2y,4x⟩, what is the work around the boundary?

C.36

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Here P=−2yP=-2yP=−2y and Q=4xQ=4xQ=4x, giving Qx−Py=4−(−2)=6Q_x-P_y=4-(-2)=6Qx​−Py​=4−(−2)=6. The rectangle has area 2⋅3=62\cdot3=62⋅3=6. Green's Theorem therefore gives the work as 6⋅6=366\cdot6=366⋅6=36. Thus option C is correct. The distractors arise from using perimeter, area, or an incorrect derivative combination.

Q7. A rectangular loop is traversed clockwise instead of counterclockwise. If the counterclockwise work would be 181818, what is the clockwise work for the same vector field and region?

📖 Explanation: Reversing the direction of traversal reverses the sign of a line integral. Green's Theorem is normally stated for positive, counterclockwise orientation, so a clockwise traversal introduces a negative sign. Therefore a counterclockwise value of 181818 becomes −18-18−18, not 181818 or a value based on a changed area.

Q8. A student computes ∬R(Qx−Py) dA\iint_R(Q_x-P_y)\,dA∬R​(Qx​−Py​)dA for a clockwise boundary and obtains 121212. They report the work as 121212. What is the most appropriate correction?

📖 Explanation: The standard Green's Theorem circulation form assumes counterclockwise orientation. If the actual boundary is clockwise, the line integral has the opposite sign from the double integral computed with positive orientation. Thus the student's magnitude is correct but the sign is wrong, so the corrected work is −12-12−12.

📖 Explanation: Green's Theorem converts the work into ∬R2x dA\iint_R2x\,dA∬R​2xdA. Because the region is symmetric about the yyy-axis, every point with positive xxx is paired with a point having negative xxx, producing cancellation. Q14. Suppose a positively oriented region is the union of two adjacent subregions, and Qx−PyQ_x-P_yQx​−Py​ is positive on one while negative on the other. Which strategy is most appropriate for determining the total work?

A.Use only the largest subregion because it dominates the result
B.Assume the contributions cancel automatically because the signs differ
C.Compute the signed double integral over the entire region, or split it into subregions and add their contributions ✅

💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Green's Theorem requires the signed integral of Qx−PyQ_x-P_yQx​−Py​ across the entire enclosed region. Positive and negative portions may partially cancel, but cancellation cannot be assumed without evaluating their magnitudes and areas. Splitting the region into manageable pieces and adding the resulting integrals is often the safest modelling strategy.
