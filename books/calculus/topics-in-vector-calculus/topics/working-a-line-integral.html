What is Working a Line Integral?

Working of a Line Integral:
To compute, parameterize CCC, find r′(t)\mathbf{r}'(t)r′(t), substitute into F\mathbf{F}F, compute the dot product, and integrate over ttt.

Example:
For F=⟨x2,yz⟩\mathbf{F} = \langle x^2, yz \rangleF=⟨x2,yz⟩ and C:r(t)=⟨t,t2,t3⟩C: \mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t, t^2, t^3 \rangleC:r(t)=⟨t,t2,t3⟩, 0≤t≤10 \le t \le 10≤t≤1, calculate ∫01⟨t2,t5⟩⋅⟨1,2t,3t2⟩dt=∫01(t2+2t6)dt=1/3+2/7=13/21\int_0^1 \langle t^2, t^5 \rangle \cdot \langle 1, 2t, 3t^2 \rangle dt = \int_0^1 (t^2 + 2t^6) dt = 1/3 + 2/7 = 13/21∫01​⟨t2,t5⟩⋅⟨1,2t,3t2⟩dt=∫01​(t2+2t6)dt=1/3+2/7=13/21.

Q2. For a force field F=⟨P,Q⟩\mathbf F=\langle P,Q\rangleF=⟨P,Q⟩, two students evaluate work along the same oriented curve. Student 1 computes ∫CP dx+Q dy\int_C P\,dx+Q\,dy∫C​Pdx+Qdy, while Student 2 computes ∫CP dy+Q dx\int_C P\,dy+Q\,dx∫C​Pdy+Qdx. Which statement best explains the difference?

B.Student 1 uses the standard work integral, while Student 2 generally interchanges the force components with the coordinate differentials ✅
C.Student 2 is correct only when the curve is closed
D.Student 1 is correct only for straight-line paths
💡 Difficulty: easy | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: For F=⟨P,Q⟩\mathbf F=\langle P,Q\rangleF=⟨P,Q⟩, the work integral is ∫CF⋅dr=∫CP dx+Q dy\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\int_C P\,dx+Q\,dy∫C​F⋅dr=∫C​Pdx+Qdy. Swapping dxdxdx and dydydy changes the dot product and therefore generally changes the physical quantity being calculated. The error is conceptual, not merely algebraic.

Q3. A force field is F(x,y)=⟨2x,2y⟩\mathbf F(x,y)=\langle 2x,2y\rangleF(x,y)=⟨2x,2y⟩. A particle travels from (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) to (0,2)(0,2)(0,2) along any smooth path. Without parametrizing a particular curve, what is the work?

A.-2
B.-3
C.-4 ✅
D.-5
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: The field is conservative because F=∇(x2+y2)\mathbf F=\nabla(x^2+y^2)F=∇(x2+y2). Therefore the work depends only on the endpoints. The potential changes from 12+02=11^2+0^2=112+02=1 to 02+22=40^2+2^2=402+22=4, so the work is 4−1=34-1=34−1=3. Thus the correct choice is B, not C.

Q4. A student argues: 'If a force field has positive components P>0P>0P>0 and Q>0Q>0Q>0 everywhere on a path, the work must be positive.' Which response most accurately evaluates the argument?

B.It is correct only for closed curves

Even if PPP and QQQ are positive, motion in a negative xxx- or yyy-direction can produce negative contributions. Work depends on the force component parallel to the displacement, not merely on force-component signs.

Q7. A particle moves around a closed curve in a force field. One student reverses the direction of traversal but leaves every algebraic expression unchanged. What should happen to the calculated work?

A.It remains unchanged because the curve is the same
B.It becomes zero automatically
C.Its sign reverses because drd\mathbf rdr reverses direction ✅
D.Its magnitude must double
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: Reversing orientation changes drd\mathbf rdr to −dr-d\mathbf r−dr. Since work is the line integral ∫CF⋅dr\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r∫C​F⋅dr, every contribution changes sign. Therefore the work for the reversed traversal is the negative of the original work. The geometric path is unchanged, but the direction of motion is essential.

Q8. A student evaluates the work of F=⟨3y,3x⟩\mathbf F=\langle 3y,3x\rangleF=⟨3y,3x⟩ along a path from (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) to (2,2)(2,2)(2,2) and obtains different answers using two smooth paths. Which conclusion is most justified?

A.The student must have found two different physical forces
B.The field is conservative, so different answers indicate an error in at least one calculation ✅
C.Different paths always produce different work
D.The field cannot be tested for path independence
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Here F=∇(3xy)\mathbf F=\nabla(3xy)F=∇(3xy), because the partial derivative of 3xy3xy3xy with respect to xxx is 3y3y3y, and with respect to yyy is 3x3x3x. Therefore the field is conservative and work depends only on the endpoints. Different results from two paths signal an evaluation or parametrization error.

Q9. Consider the force field F=⟨y,−x⟩\mathbf F=\langle y,-x\rangleF=⟨y,−x⟩. A particle moves counterclockwise around the unit circle. Which statement best predicts the sign of the work before doing the integral?

A.The work is positive because the motion is counterclockwise
B.The work is negative because the force opposes the counterclockwise tangent direction ✅
C.The work is zero because the path is closed
D.The sign cannot be determined from the field and orientation
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: On the unit circle, the counterclockwise tangent is ⟨−y,x⟩\langle -y,x\rangle⟨−y,x⟩, while the force is ⟨y,−x⟩=−⟨−y,x⟩\langle y,-x\rangle=-\langle -y,x\rangle⟨y,−x⟩=−⟨−y,x⟩. Thus the force points exactly opposite the direction of motion at every point. Their dot product is negative, so the total work is negative.

Q10. A graph of a planar path shows a particle moving rightward while a force vector at each point points upward and slightly rightward. At another portion, the particle moves leftward while the force still points upward and slightly rightward. Which interpretation is most accurate?

A.Both portions necessarily contribute positive work
B.The rightward motion contributes positive work from the horizontal force component, while the leftward motion contributes negative work from it ✅
C.Only the upward component determines work
D.The vertical force component always makes the work positive
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Work depends on the dot product between force and displacement. During rightward motion, a rightward force component contributes positively. During leftward motion, that same rightward component contributes negatively. The upward component contributes only when there is vertical displacement. Thus the graph must be interpreted using local alignment between force and motion.

Q11. A plotted trajectory starts at (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), moves to (2,0)(2,0)(2,0), then curves upward to (2,2)(2,2)(2,2). A force field has a horizontal component that is positive on the first segment and a vertical component that is negative on the second segment. Which qualitative conclusion follows?

A.Both segments must contribute positive work
B.The first segment contributes positive work, while the second contributes negative work ✅
C.The total work must be zero
D.Neither segment contributes work because the path changes direction
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: On the first horizontal segment, displacement is rightward and the positive horizontal force component aligns with it, producing positive work. On the second segment, displacement is upward while the vertical force component is negative, so their dot product is negative. The total work is therefore the sum of opposing contributions.

Q12. For F=⟨2x+y,x⟩\mathbf F=\langle 2x+y,x\rangleF=⟨2x+y,x⟩, a student claims the work from A=(0,0)A=(0,0)A=(0,0) to B=(1,1)B=(1,1)B=(1,1) is path-independent because the field components are both linear functions. What is the best diagnosis?

C.The reasoning is correct only for closed curves
D.The reasoning is irrelevant because line integrals cannot depend on path
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Linearity of the components does not guarantee conservativeness. Here P=2x+yP=2x+yP=2x+y gives Py=1P_y=1Py​=1, while Q=xQ=xQ=x gives Qx=1Q_x=1Qx​=1, so in fact they are equal and the field is conservative. Therefore the student's conclusion is correct, but the stated justification is incomplete. The correct test compares cross-partial derivatives, making B factually incorrect.

Q13. A force field is F=⟨P,Q⟩\mathbf F=\langle P,Q\rangleF=⟨P,Q⟩, and a particle follows a curve with xxx increasing throughout but yyy first increasing and then decreasing. Which modelling strategy is most reliable for calculating work?

A.Replace dydydy by dxdxdx everywhere
B.Split the curve into intervals where the behavior of yyy is simple, then evaluate ∫(P dx+Q dy)\int(P\,dx+Q\,dy)∫(Pdx+Qdy) on each part ✅
C.Ignore the Q dyQ\,dyQdy term because xxx is increasing
D.Use only the initial and final values of xxx and yyy, regardless of the field
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: Because yyy changes direction, its derivative changes sign and the vertical contribution to work cannot be treated as having one fixed sign. Splitting the path into convenient pieces makes the orientation and derivatives explicit. The total work is the sum of the line integrals over those pieces.
