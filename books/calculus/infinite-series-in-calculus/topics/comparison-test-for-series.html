What is Comparison test for series?

If 0≤an​≤bn​ for all large n, and ∑bn​ converges, then ∑an​ converges; if an​≥bn​≥0 and ∑bn​ diverges, then ∑an​ diverges; for example, ∑n2+11​ converges by comparing to ∑n21​.

Q1. A student analyzes the series ∑k=1∞​k​−0.51​ and claims it converges because for large k, the term behaves like k​1​, which is a known benchmark. Which statement best identifies the flaw in this reasoning regarding the Comparison Test?

B.The student assumed that asymptotic similarity guarantees convergence without verifying the inequality direction required for the Direct Comparison Test. ✅

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The core misconception here is confusing asymptotic equivalence with the strict inequality requirements of the Direct Comparison Test. Simply stating they are 'similar' is insufficient; one must rigorously establish ak​≤bk​ or ak​≥bk​. Furthermore, since ∑k​1​ diverges, comparing to it cannot prove convergence regardless of inequality direction.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

This counters the misconception that oscillating terms automatically require alternating series tests or prevent direct comparison. Dominant polynomial terms stabilize the behavior.

Q28. Which scenario renders the Limit Comparison Test completely inapplicable?

Q35. For ∑k(k+1)​1​, why is comparing to ∑1/k via DCT inconclusive for proving convergence, but useful for proving divergence?
