What is Sum of infinite series in calculus?

An infinite series \( \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \) is defined as the limit of its partial sums \( S_N = a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_N \), so the sum is \( \lim_{N \to \infty} S_N \); if this limit exists and is finite, the series converges to that sum; otherwise, it diverges.

Q65. Why is the sum of \( \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k(k+1)(k+2)} \) equal to \( \frac{1}{4} \) and not \( \frac{1}{2} \) as one might guess from similar telescoping series?

B.The series is not truly telescoping; it requires integration to evaluate, and the integral from 1 to ∞ of the corresponding function equals 1/4.
C.Students guessing 1/2 confuse this with \( \sum \frac{1}{k(k+1)} \); the extra factor in denominator halves the sum due to increased decay rate.
D.The sum is actually 1/2; the 1/4 result comes from incorrectly omitting the first surviving term in the telescoping cancellation.
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: A

📖 Explanation: This challenging problem demands precise telescoping execution. Decompose: \( \frac{1}{k(k+1)(k+2)} = \frac{A}{k} + \frac{B}{k+1} + \frac{C}{k+2} \). Solving: A=1/2, B=-1, C=1/2. Write partial sum: \( \sum [\frac{1/2}{k} - \frac{1}{k+1} + \frac{1/2}{k+2}] \). Regroup: \( \frac{1}{2}\sum \frac{1}{k} - \sum \frac{1}{k+1} + \frac{1}{2}\sum \frac{1}{k+2} \).
