What is Integral test for convergence?

For a positive, continuous, decreasing function \( f(x) \) with \( a_n = f(n) \), the series \( \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \) converges if and only if the improper integral \( \int_{1}^{\infty} f(x) \, dx \) converges; for example, \( \sum \frac{1}{n^2} \) converges because \( \int_1^\infty x^{-2} dx = 1 \) is finite.

Q1. A student attempts to apply the integral test to the series \( \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k}{k^2} \). They define \( f(x) = \frac{(-1)^x}{x^2} \) and evaluate \( \int_1^{\infty} f(x) \, dx \). Which statement best analyzes the fundamental flaw in this approach?

B.The function \( f(x) \) is not positive for all \( x \geq 1 \), violating a core hypothesis of the test. ✅
C.The function \( f(x) \) is not decreasing on the interval.
D.The integral diverges, implying the series diverges.
💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: B

📖 Explanation: The integral test strictly requires the associated function \( f(x) \) to be continuous, positive, and decreasing on the interval of integration. The term \( (-1)^x \) introduces oscillation and complex values for non-integer \( x \), making the function neither positive nor well-defined as a real-valued continuous function. This question targets error analysis by identifying that the test's preconditions are more critical than the mechanical evaluation of the integral itself.

This problem tests multi-step reasoning involving substitution and parameter analysis within the integral test framework.
