What is Taylor series nth Remainder estimation?

To estimate the error of using the \( n \)-th Taylor polynomial, use \( |R_n(x)| \le \frac{M}{(n+1)!} |x-a|^{n+1} \), where \( M \) is an upper bound for \( |f^{(n+1)}(t)| \) on the interval between \( a \) and \( x \); this lets you choose \( n \) for a desired accuracy.

Q1. A student approximates \( f(x) = e^{-x^2} \) at \( x=0.5 \) using a 4th-degree Maclaurin polynomial. They claim the error bound is determined by evaluating \( |f^{(5)}(0.5)| \). Why is this reasoning fundamentally flawed regarding the Lagrange remainder formula?

A.The derivative should be evaluated at 0, not 0.5.
B.The remainder for an even-degree polynomial of an even function requires the next non-zero derivative term.
C.The value \( M \) must be the maximum absolute value of the derivative on the entire interval between 0 and 0.5, not just at the endpoint.
D.The 5th derivative of \( e^{-x^2} \) is zero, so they should have used the 6th derivative instead. ✅
💡 Difficulty: hard | ✅ Correct: D

📖 Explanation: This question targets error analysis and conceptual understanding. The Lagrange error bound requires finding an upper bound \( M \) for \( |f^{(n+1)}(z)| \) for all \( z \) between the center \( x_0 \) and the evaluation point \( x \). Evaluating only at the endpoint is insufficient because the derivative might achieve a larger magnitude within the interval. Furthermore, since \( e^{-x^2} \) is even, its 5th derivative at 0 is zero, but the bound depends on the interval's maximum, making option C the precise correction to the student's specific procedural error.

💡 Difficulty: medium | ✅ Correct: C

📖 Explanation: This tests conceptual understanding of derivative behavior. In contrast, \( \frac{d^n}{dx^n}e^x = e^x \), so on an interval \( [0, x] \), the maximum is \( e^x \). Understanding this distinction is crucial for applying the remainder estimation theorem correctly across different function classes without overestimating error for bounded oscillatory functions.
