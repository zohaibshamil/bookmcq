What is Conditional convergence examples?

A series is conditionally convergent if ∑an \sum a_n ∑an​ converges but ∑∣an∣ \sum |a_n| ∑∣an​∣ diverges; the classic example is the alternating harmonic series ∑(−1)n+11n \sum (-1)^{n+1} \frac{1}{n} ∑(−1)n+1n1​, which converges to ln⁡2 \ln 2 ln2, but if you take absolute values, the harmonic series diverges.

📖 Explanation: Core conceptual property of conditional convergence. By definition, conditional convergence means ∑an \sum a_n ∑an​ converges but ∑∣an∣ \sum |a_n| ∑∣an​∣ diverges. Since ∣an∣=pn+qn |a_n| = p_n + q_n ∣an​∣=pn​+qn​ and an=pn−qn a_n = p_n - q_n an​=pn​−qn​, if both ∑pn \sum p_n ∑pn​ and ∑qn \sum q_n ∑qn​ converged, then ∑∣an∣ \sum |a_n| ∑∣an​∣ would converge (contradiction). If one converged and other diverged, ∑an \sum a_n ∑an​ would diverge (contradiction). Therefore, both must diverge. This decomposition reveals that conditional convergence arises precisely from cancellation between two divergent positive series. Understanding this mechanism is essential for grasping why rearrangements change sums.
